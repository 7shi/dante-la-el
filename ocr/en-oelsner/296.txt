2, 3. This obscene and insulting gesture, the origin
of which has been variously explained, was made by

12. *seme*. Pistoia was said to have been founded by

15. Referring to Capaneus, for whom see above,

25-33. Cacus was a monster inhabiting a cave in
Mount Aventine and noted for his thefts. He dragged
into his cave, by their tails, some of the oxen that
Hercules had stolen from Geryon, and was slain by
that hero. In the mode of his death Dante follows
Livy’s account (i. 7), but in other respects Virgil (*AEn*.
viii. 193-267) served as his model. Cacus was not
really a Centaur: Dante was evidently led astray by
Virgil’s ‘. . . *semihominis Caci*. Verse 27 refers, of
course, to the Centaurs guarding the Violent (see above
Canto xii).
