**The Evil Counsellors** The flame was now erect and quiet, having
ceased to speak, and now went away from us
with licence of the sweet Poet;

**Guido of Montefeltro** when another, that came behind it, made us
turn our eyes to its top, for a confused sound
that issued therefrom.

As the Sicilian bull (which bellowed first with
the lament of him—and that was right—who
had tuned it with his file)

kept bellowing with the sufferer’s voice; so that,
although it was of brass, it seemed transfixed
with pain :

thus, having at their commencement no way or
outlet from the fire, the dismal words were
changed into its language.

But after they had found their road up through
the point, giving to it the vibration which the
tongue had given in their passage,

we heard it say: “O thou, at whom I aim my
voice! and who just now wast speaking
Lombard, saying, ‘Now go, no more I urge
thee’ ;

though I have come perhaps a little late, let it
not irk thee to pause and speak with me.
thou seest it irks not me, although I burn.
