Original, and having to represent it so helplessly and inadequately in another language, has been found as painful as was anticipated, and the notes as hard to compress; but from beginning to end, all the difficulties of the task have at least been honestly fronted; and readers who are already familiar with Dante and his commentators will be able to estimate the quantity of labour required for the performance of it.

In conclusion, I have to acknowledge the kindness of one highly accomplished friend, whose name I am not allowed to mention; he read over the proofs of the first eight Cantos, and suggested some useful additions and amendments.

I have also to thank my printers for the patient diligence and dexterity with which they have gone through their difficult and complicated task, submitting to numerous alterations and corrections in the course of it. The common Italian punctuation, somewhat different from our own, has been purposely retained.

J. A. C.

Chelsea, December 1848.
