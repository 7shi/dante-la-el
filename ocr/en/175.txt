The practical meaning of the passage is very evident. Dante wishes all men to know his opinion, that Popes are not exempt from heresy, and that it deserves greater punishment in them than in other men. He finds an Emperor, a Ghibelline Cardinal, and the greatest of the Ghibelline chiefs, in the same circle. “This cry of thine will do like wind, which strikes with greatest force the highest summits.” Parad. xvii. 133.

1 “Circlets,” from the smallness of their size, compared with those above. “In gradation,” i.e. one after another, becoming smaller.
