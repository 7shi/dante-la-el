I answered him: “Ciacco,¹ thy sore distress weighs upon me so, that it bids me weep. But tell me, if thou canst, what the citizens of the divided city shall come to: if any one in it be just. And tell me the reason why such discord has assailed it.”

¹ The name Ciacco is said also to have been a familiar abbreviation of Jacopo (James) in Dante's time.

² Florence was divided by two factions, the Neri and Bianchi, or Blacks and Whites. The Whites are called the “savage party,” because it was headed by the Cerchi, a rough, purse-proud family of merchants (see Villani, viii. 39, and Boccaccio Com.), that had recently acquired great wealth and influence in Florence.
