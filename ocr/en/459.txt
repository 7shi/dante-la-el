Capraia and Gorgona move, and hedge up the Arno at its mouth, that it may drown in thee every living soul. For if Count Ugolino had the fame of having betrayed thee in thy castles, thou oughtest not to have put his sons into such torture. Their youthful age, thou modern Thebes! made innocent Uguccione and Brigata, and the other two whom my song above has named.

2 Troya in his Veltro Allegorico (Flor. 1826, p. 28, &c.) asserts, in opposition to Villani and other contemporary historians, that Ugolino's sons and grandsons were not innocent, the Archbishop not guilty, &c.; but the Veltro still seems much more like a romance than a piece of sober history. One is led to expect speedy proofs of many hazardous assertions in it, and they have now been due for twenty-two years. Such books darken every part of the subjects on which they treat, and are inexcusable among serious men.
