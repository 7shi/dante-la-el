As that river¹—which has a path of its own, first² from Monte Viso toward the east, on the left skirt of the Apennine; which is called Acquacheta above, ere it descends to its low bed, and is vacant of that name³ at Forli—resounds from the mountain, there above San Benedetto, in falling at a descent, where for a thousand there should be refuge:⁴ thus down from a steep bank we found that tainted water re-echoing, so that in little time it would have stunned the ear.

Come quel fiume, ch’ ha proprio cammino
Prima da monte Veso in vêr levante,
Dalla sinistra costa d’ Apennino,
Che si chiama Acquacheta suso, avante
Che si divalli giù nel basso letto,
E a Forlì di quel nome è vacante,
Rimbomba là sovra San Benedetto
Dall’ alpe, per cadere ad una scesa,
Dove dovria per mille esser ricetto;
Così, giù d’ una ripa discoscesa,
Trovammo risonar quell’ acqua tinta,
Sì che in poc’ ora avria l’ orecchia offesa.

1 The Montone, which passes the Abbey of St. Benedict; and there descends into the plain of Romagna, “its low bed.” It is the first of the rivers, on the left (northern) skirts of the Apennines, that has a course of its own to the sea, near Ravenna. All the rest before it, from Monte Viso eastwards, are tributaries or “attendants” of the Po.
2 Or: “Before any other river.”
3 Has lost the name of Acquacheta (cheta, still, quiet), and taken that of Montone before reaching Forli.
