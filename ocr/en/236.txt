**224 NOTES**

**106-114.** At the time of the Trojan war, all the
Greeks were absent from their country, taking part
in the siege of Troy. Before the Greeks left Aulis,
Calchas advised Agamemnon to sacrifice Iphigenia.
But Eurypylus had nothing to do with this incident,
which Dante appears to have confused with the passage
in which Virgil tells how both Eurypylus and Calchas
are consulted with reference to the departure of the
Greeks from Troy (*Æn. ii. 110, sqq.*)—For the use of
the word *Tragœdia* (v. 112) see *de Vulg. El.* ii. 4: 38—
*Per tragœdiam superiorem stilum inducimus*; [*per comœdiam
inferiorem etc.*]; see also *Epist. ad Can. Grand. x. 10.*

**116.** Michael Scott of Balwearie (*ca.* 1190-1250)
studied at Oxford, Paris and Toledo; he followed the
Emperor Frederick II. to his court, but died in
Scotland. In philosophy proper he appears to have
figured only as a translator, *e.g.* of Aristotle; his
original work deals with the occult sciences. For
further particulars see Scott's Note O to the *Lay of the
Last Minstrel.*

**118-119.** Guido Bonatti of Forlì, tiler and astrologer
