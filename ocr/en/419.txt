Li ruscelletti, che de’ verdi colli
Del Casentin discendon giuso in Arno,
Facendo i lor canali e freddi e molli,
Sempre mi stanno innanzi, e non indarno;
Chè l’ imagine lor via più m’ asciuga,
Che il male, ond’ io nel volto mi discarno.
La rigida giustizia, che mi fruga,
Tragge cagion del luogo, ov’ io peccai,
A metter più gli miei sospiri in fuga.
Ivi è Romena, là dov’ io falsai
La lega suggellata del Batista,

1 Casentino, the upper Valley of the Arno above Arezzo, is noted for its beauty and the clearness of its mountain streams. “There is Romena,” seat of the Guidos, a few miles below the sources of the Arno, and a little to the west of the Camaldoli.
2 Or: “Whereby I get lean or lose flesh in the face.”
3 The florens, with the Lily (giglio) on one side and St. John on the other, were first coined in the year 1252, and each of them contained 24 carats of pure gold (Villani, vi. 54), like the modern Zecchino. They soon circulated everywhere; and “Genuine as the yellow floren” became a proverb. “Floreins fine of gold ycoined round . . . so faire and bright,” says our own Chaucer in his Pardonere’s Tale. Guido had the Baptist’s image stamped on his base coin, which contained three carats of alloy.
