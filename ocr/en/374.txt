CANTO XXVII.

The flame was now erect and quiet, having ceased to speak, and now went away from us with license of the sweet Poet; when another, that came behind it, made us turn our eyes to its top, for a confused sound that issued therefrom. Già era dritta insù la fiamma, e queta
Per non dir più; e già da noi sen gía
Con la licenzia del dolce Poeta:
Quando un' altra, che dietro a lei venia,
Ne fece volger gli occhi alla sua cima,
Per un-confuso suon che fuor n' uscia.
Come il bue Cicilian, che mugghiò prima
Col pianto di colui, e ciò fu dritto,
Che l' avea temperato con sua lima,
Mugghiava con la voce dell' afflitto,
Sì che, con tutto ch' e' fosse di rame,

1 No longer moved its sharp point to and fro like a tongue. Canto xxvi. 85.
2 The brazen bull which Perillus invented for the Sicilian tyrant Phalaris. It was constructed with such art, that the cries of those burning within it resembled the bellowing of a real bull; and Phalaris, very justly, made the first experiment on the artist himself. The sinners here too are tortured within the flames they have prepared for themselves by applying their talents to wicked counsels.
