Siena had me burned; but what I died for does not bring me here. ’Tis true, I said to him, speaking in jest: ‘I could raise myself through the air in flight.’ And he, who had a fond desire and little wit, willed that I should shew him the art; and only because I made him not a Dædalus, he made me be burned by one who had him for a son. But to the last budget of the ten, for the alchemy that I practised in the world, Minos, who may not err, condemned me.”

And I said to the Poet: “Now was there ever people so vain as the Sienese? Certainly the French¹ not so by far.”
