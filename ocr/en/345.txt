Neither ‘O’ nor ‘I’ was ever written so quickly as he took fire, and burnt, and dropt down all changed to ashes.¹ And after he was thus dissolved upon the ground, the ashes reunited, and of themselves at once resumed the former shape.² Thus by great sages ’tis confest the Phœnix dies, and then is born again, when it approaches the five-hundredth year. In its life it eats no herb or grain, but only tears of incense and amomum: and nard and myrrh are its last swathings.³

Là dove il collo alle spalle s’ annoda.
Nè O sì tosto mai, nè I si scrisse,
Com’ ei s’ accese, e arse, e cener tutto
Convenne che cascando divenisse.
E poi che fu a terra sì distrutto,
La cener si raccolse, e per sè stessa
In quel medesmo ritornò di butto.
Così per li gran savi si confessa,
Che la Fenice muore, e poi rinasce,
Quando al cinquecentesimo anno appressa.
Erba nè biada in sua vita non pasce,
Ma sol d’ incenso lagrime e d’ amomo;
E nardo e mirra son l’ ultime fasce.
