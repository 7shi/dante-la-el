Del no per li denar vi si fa ita.
Laggiù il buttò, e per lo scoglio duro

Lombardi, Biagioli, &c. somewhat arbitrarily make convolto mean "turned with head and feet downwards;" and Lombardi says, "this posture, similar to that of one who is in fervent prayer, may be the object of the Diabolic sarcasm which follows" in verse 48. The sense of "besmeared, rolled in the pitch" has an air of platitude here, in spite of all the irrelevant examples cited by the Cruscans. The nearest and plainest meaning is obviously that of the Lat. convolutus, from which the word is immediately derived.
