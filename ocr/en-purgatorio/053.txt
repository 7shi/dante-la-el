CANTO IV 41

**Dante’s weariness** And he to me: “Let no step of thine descend,
ever up the mount behind me win thy way,
until some wise escort appear to us.”

So high was the top that it surpassed my sight,
and the slope steeper far than a line from mid-
quadrant to centre.

Weary was I when I began: “O sweet father,
turn thee and look how I remain alone, if thou
stay not.”

“My son,” said he, “so far as there drag thee,”
pointing out to me a terrace a little higher up,
which on that side circles the whole mountain.

So did his words spur me on, that I forced me,
creeping after him, so far that the ledge was
under my feet.

**The poets rest** There we both did sit us down, turned towards
the East, whence we had ascended; for to
look back is wont to cheer men.

**The position of the sun** First mine eyes I directed to the shores below;
then did raise them to the sun, and marvelled
that we were smitten by it on the left side.

Right well the Poet perceived that I was all
astonished at the chariot of the light, where
’twas entering between us and the North.

**explained by Virgil** Whereupon he to me: “If Castor and Pollux
were in company of that mirror, which purveys
of his light upward and downward,

thou wouldst see the glowing Zodiac revolve
yet closer to the Bears, unless it strayed from
its ancient path.

If thou wouldst have power to conceive how that
may be, rapt within thyself, imagine Zion and
