CANTO I 13

40. See *Inf.* xxxiv. 130.

58-60. *ultima sera*, here used in the double sense of
bodily and spiritual death (*cf. Conv.* iv. 7: 102 *sqq.*).
The verses refer, of course, to the allegory of *Inf.* i.

77. For Minos, see *Inf.* v. 4 *sqq.*

78-80. Marcia (for whom see *Inf.* iv. 128) was the
second wife of Cato, who yielded her to his friend
Q. Hortensius. On the death of the latter, she was
again married to Cato. The *Convito* (iv. 28: 97-159)
contains an elaborate allegory, in which the return of
Marcia to Cato signifies the return of the noble soul
to God.

86. *di là*. These words when used by themselves in
the *Purgatorio* always mean “in the other hemisphere.”

88. The *mal fiume* is the Acheron (see *Inf.* iii.
70 *sqq.*).
