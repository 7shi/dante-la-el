136-138. The Sadducees, having told Jesus of a woman who had married seven brothers in succession, and asked him: "Therefore in the resurrection whose wife shall she be of the seven? for they all had her. Jesus answered and said unto them, Ye do err, not knowing the scriptures, nor the power of God. For in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels of God in heaven" (Matt. xxii. 23-30; Mark xii. 18-25; Luke xx. 27-35). The passage is usually taken to refer specifically to the Pope as the spouse of the Church (cf. Inf. xix. 56, 57; Purg. xxiv. 22). But surely it may be taken with a wider reference. Marriage is regarded as the closest instance of special relations which have some legal or official sanction over and above the purely personal relations on which they are based, or which spring out of them. All such relations are abolished in the spirit world (cf. Par. vi. 10, and other passages).
