WHEN the car arrests itself, all the elders who had preceded it, turn and face round to it; and when one of them invokes the bride of Lebanon, blessed spirits rise up around it, as men shall rise at the last day. Flowers are flung in a cloud from their hands as they utter blessings, culled from Christian and Gentile scriptures; and a form clad in the colours of the three theological virtues rises like the sun in their midst (1-33). Dante without further testimony from his eyes, recognises the tokens of the ancient flame, and like a terrified child turns round to ask comfort and support from Virgil (34-48). But Virgil has gone, and not even the joys of the Earthly Paradise can prevent Dante’s cheeks, though cleansed by the mountain dew, from darkening again with tears (49-54).
