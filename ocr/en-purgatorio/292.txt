37-42. Dante frequently misunderstood the classical
Latin writers. He evidently read them with the same
ease and security and the same keen appreciation but
frequent misconception with which an Englishman,
who has made no special study of Elizabethan
English, reads Shakespeare. But if he really took
Virgil’s *quid non mortalia pectora cogis Auri sacra fames* (*Æn.*
iii. 56, 57) to mean that a moderate, and therefore
hallowed, desire for wealth ought to moderate ex-
travagance, it constitutes a more portentous blunder
in Latinity than any other that can be brought home
to him. Many ingenious attempts have been made
to escape this; but the only legitimate one is to
suppose that Dante, while understanding the sense
in which Virgil uttered the words, considered him-
self justified in supposing that his writings, like the
Scripture, had many senses, and that for purposes of
edification we must look into all the possible mean-

context in which it occurs. [For the context of
