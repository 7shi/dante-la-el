Our poet’s general conception of Cato is derived from
Lucan (*Pharsalia*, ii. 373-391); and his intense admira-
tion of the man and of his character finds expression in
several passages of the *Convito* (iv. 5 : 103; 6 : 71;
27 : 23; 28 : 92). Cato’s position as warder of the
Christian Purgatory is probably to be explained in a
similar way as the position of Ripheus in Paradise (see
*Par.* xx. 118 *sqq.*, and *note*); note especially the alle-
gorical significance of the stars in *vv.* 37-39, and the
fact that *Sole* is often synonymous with God.
