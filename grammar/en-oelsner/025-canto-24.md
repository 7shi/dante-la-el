# Inferno 24

## Lines 1–21 — Frost and Renewed Hope

> 1 In quella parte del giovinetto anno,  
> 2 che il sole i crin sotto l' Aquario tempra  
> 3 e già le notti al mezzo dì sen vanno,  

In that part of the youthful year, when the Sun tempers his locks beneath Aquarius, and the nights already wane towards half the day,

- *giovinetto* ‘very young’ → “youthful”: the diminutive force is softened.
- *che* ‘in which’ → “when”: the relative construction becomes a temporal conjunction.
- *il sole i crin sotto l' Aquario tempra* ‘the sun the locks beneath Aquarius tempers’ → “the Sun tempers his locks beneath Aquarius”: the verb moves before its object and the prepositional phrase; the article before *crin* becomes a possessive, while that before *Aquario* is omitted.
- *già le notti* ‘already the nights’ → “the nights already”: the adverb moves after the subject.
- *al mezzo dì sen vanno* ‘towards half the day go away’ → “wane towards half the day”: the pronominal verb of departure becomes a verb of diminution, and the verb precedes rather than follows the prepositional phrase.

> 4 quando la brina in su la terra assempra  
> 5 l' imagine di sua sorella bianca,  
> 6 ma poco dura alla sua penna tempra,  

when the hoar-frost copies his white sister's image on the ground, but short while lasts the temper of his pen,

- *in su la terra* ‘upon the earth’ → “on the ground”: the compound preposition becomes a single preposition, “ground” replaces ‘earth’, and the phrase moves from before the verb to after its object.
- *l' imagine di sua sorella bianca* ‘the image of her sister white’ → “his white sister's image”: the *di* construction becomes a possessive preceding “image”, and the adjective moves before “sister”; the grammatically feminine frost is treated as masculine.
- *poco dura alla sua penna tempra* ‘little lasts to her pen temper’ → “short while lasts the temper of his pen”: the adverb *poco* becomes a phrase expressing duration; the relationship expressed by *alla* becomes “of”, following “temper”; an article is supplied before “temper”, and the frost is again treated as masculine.

> 7 lo villanello, a cui la roba manca,  
> 8 si leva e guarda, e vede la campagna  
> 9 biancheggiar tutta, ond' ei si batte l' anca;  

the peasant, whose fodder fails, rises, and looks, and sees the fields all white; whereat he smites his thigh,

- *villanello* ‘little peasant’ → “peasant”: the diminutive is not separately represented.
- *a cui la roba manca* ‘to whom goods are lacking’ → “whose fodder fails”: the relative construction expressing the person lacking something becomes a possessive relative; the general *roba* is interpreted specifically as fodder, and lack is expressed as failure of supply.
- *si leva* ‘raises himself’ → “rises”: the reflexive construction becomes an intransitive verb.
- *la campagna biancheggiar tutta* ‘the countryside whiten wholly’ → “the fields all white”: the singular collective noun becomes plural; the infinitive becomes an adjective, and the expression of totality moves before “white”.
- *ond' ei* ‘whence he’ → “whereat he”: the connective is rendered as a reaction to what has been seen.
- *si batte l' anca* ‘beats himself the hip’ → “smites his thigh”: the reflexive construction and definite article become a possessive; “thigh” replaces ‘hip’.

> 10 ritorna in casa, e qua e là si lagna,  
> 11 come il tapin che non sa che si faccia,  

goes back into the house, and to and fro laments like a poor wight who knows not what to do;

- *ritorna in casa* ‘returns into house’ → “goes back into the house”: the single verb becomes “goes back”, and an article is supplied before “house”.
- *qua e là* ‘here and there’ → “to and fro”: an expression of location becomes one suggesting movement.
- *il tapin* ‘the wretched one’ → “a poor wight”: the definite article becomes indefinite, and the substantival adjective becomes an adjective modifying a noun.
- *che si faccia* ‘what he should do’ → “what to do”: the finite subjunctive clause becomes an infinitive construction; the pleonastic *si* has no separate equivalent.

> 12 poi riede e la speranza ringavagna,  
> 13 veggendo il mondo aver cangiata faccia  
> 14 in poco d' ora, e prende suo vincastro,  

then comes out again, and recovers hope, observing how the world has changed its face in little time; and takes his staff,

- *riede* ‘returns’ → “comes out again”: the verb is expanded into a phrase that specifies outward movement.
- *la speranza ringavagna* ‘the hope gathers back into a basket’ → “recovers hope”: the verb’s basket metaphor is replaced by its nonfigurative sense; the object moves after the verb, and its article is omitted.
- *veggendo* ‘seeing’ → “observing”: direct seeing is expressed as observation.
- *il mondo aver cangiata faccia* ‘the world to have changed face’ → “how the world has changed its face”: the noun-and-infinitive construction becomes a finite clause introduced by the supplied “how”; “its” is also supplied.
- *in poco d' ora* ‘in little of an hour’ → “in little time”: the partitive expression becomes a general phrase of duration, without the specific unit ‘hour’.
- *vincastro* ‘shepherd’s crook’ → “staff”: the specific implement is rendered by a broader term.

> 15 e fuor le pecorelle a pascer caccia :  

and chases forth his lambs to feed:

- *fuor … caccia* ‘forth … drives’ → “chases forth”: the verb moves from the end of the clause to precede the adverb, object, and purpose phrase; ‘drives’ becomes “chases”.
- *le pecorelle* ‘the little sheep’ → “his lambs”: the article becomes a possessive, and the diminutive is interpreted as denoting young sheep.
- *a pascer* ‘to graze’ → “to feed”: the specific activity of grazing becomes the broader activity of feeding.

> 16 così mi fece sbigottir lo mastro  
> 17 quand' io gli vidi sì turbar la fronte,  

thus the Master made me despond, when I saw his brow so troubled;

- *mi fece sbigottir lo mastro* ‘me made be dismayed the master’ → “the Master made me despond”: the postposed subject moves before the verb, and the object pronoun follows “made”; dismay is rendered as despondency.
- *gli vidi sì turbar la fronte* ‘to him I saw so grow troubled the brow’ → “I saw his brow so troubled”: the possessive dative *gli* becomes “his”; the infinitive expressing a change becomes a participial adjective expressing a condition, and “brow” moves before that description.

> 18 e così tosto al mal giunse lo impiastro.  

and thus quickly to the sore the plaster came.

- *così tosto* ‘so soon’ → “thus quickly”: *così* is rendered as manner rather than degree, while temporal promptness is expressed as quickness.
- *al mal giunse lo impiastro* ‘to the ill came the plaster’ → “to the sore the plaster came”: the general *mal* becomes the concrete “sore”, and the subject moves before the verb.

> 19 Chè, come noi venimmo al guasto ponte,  
> 20 lo duca a me si volse con quel piglio  
> 21 dolce, ch' io vidi prima a piè del monte.  

For when we reached the shattered bridge, my Guide turned to me with that sweet aspect which I saw [first] at the foot of the mountain.

- *Chè* ‘because’ → “For”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *come* ‘as’ → “when”: the temporal relationship is expressed with “when”.
- *venimmo al guasto ponte* ‘came to the ruined bridge’ → “reached the shattered bridge”: a verb of motion with a preposition becomes a transitive verb of arrival; the general damage expressed by *guasto* becomes the more specific “shattered”.
- *lo duca* ‘the leader’ → “my Guide”: the article becomes a supplied possessive, and leadership is expressed specifically as guidance.
- *a me si volse* ‘to me turned himself’ → “turned to me”: the reflexive construction becomes intransitive, and the prepositional phrase moves after the verb.
- *quel piglio dolce* ‘that aspect sweet’ → “that sweet aspect”: the adjective moves before the noun.
- *prima* ‘before’ → “[first]”: the translation selects the sense of the earliest occasion rather than simply a previous occasion.

## Lines 22–54 — Climbing and Overcoming Exhaustion

> 22 Le braccia aperse, dopo alcun consiglio  
> 23 eletto seco, riguardando prima  
> 24 ben la ruina, e diedemi di piglio.  

He opened his arms after having chosen some plan within himself, first looking well at the ruin, and took hold of me.

- *Le braccia aperse* ‘the arms he opened’ → “He opened his arms”: the object follows the verb, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *dopo alcun consiglio eletto seco* ‘after some plan chosen with himself’ → “after having chosen some plan within himself”: the participle modifying the plan becomes an active perfect gerund; *seco* is rendered as inward deliberation rather than literally being with himself.
- *riguardando prima* ‘looking first’ → “first looking”: the adverb precedes the participle.
- *diedemi di piglio* ‘gave me a grip’ → “took hold of me”: the Italian idiom becomes an English verbal phrase, with the person seized expressed after “of”.

> 25 E come quei che adopera ed estima,  
> 26 che sempre par che innanzi si provveggia:  

And as one who works, and calculates, always seeming to provide beforehand:

- *quei* ‘he’ → “one”: the demonstrative pronoun becomes an indefinite pronoun.
- *estima* ‘estimates’ → “calculates”: the translation emphasizes calculation rather than estimation.
- *che sempre par che innanzi si provveggia* ‘who always seems that beforehand he provides for himself’ → “always seeming to provide beforehand”: the relative clause becomes a participial phrase, and the second finite clause becomes an infinitive; the reflexive beneficiary is not expressed, and the adverb of anticipation moves to the end.

> 27 così, levando me su ver la cima  
> 28 d’un ronchion, avvisava un’ altra scheggia,  

so, lifting me up towards the top of one big block, he looked out another splinter,

- *d’un ronchion* ‘of a rocky projection’ → “of one big block”: the indefinite article becomes “one”; the noun is rendered as a block, with its size made explicit by “big”.
- *avvisava* ‘was marking out’ → “he looked out”: the verb is rendered by an English phrase meaning to identify or select; the imperfect becomes a simple past.

> 29 dicendo: “Sopra quella poi t’ aggrappa;  
> 30 ma tenta pria s’ è tal ch’ ella ti reggia.”  

saying: “Now clamber over that, but try first if it will carry thee.”

- *Sopra quella* ‘Onto that’ → “over that”: the prepositional phrase follows the imperative instead of preceding it, and “over” replaces the indication of movement onto a support.
- *poi* ‘then’ → “Now”: the temporal adverb changes and moves to the beginning of the instruction.
- *t’aggrappa* ‘cling’ → “clamber”: taking a firm grip is rendered as climbing; the Italian pronominal verb becomes a non-pronominal English verb.
- *s’è tal ch’ella ti reggia* ‘if it is such that it may support thee’ → “if it will carry thee”: the description of the rock’s fitness and its dependent subjunctive clause are compressed into a single clause with “will”; supporting is expressed as carrying.

> 31 Non era via da vestito di cappa,  
> 32 chè noi a pena, ei lieve ed io sospinto,  
> 33 potevan su montar di chiappa in chiappa.  

It was no way for one clad with cloak of lead: for scarcely we, he light and I pushed on, could mount up from jagg to jagg.

- *Non era via* ‘It was not a way’ → “It was no way”: negation of the verb becomes negation of the noun.
- *da vestito di cappa* ‘for [one] clad in a cloak’ → “for one clad with cloak of lead”: the substantival participle is expanded with “one”; “with” replaces ‘in’, and “of lead” is supplied.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *noi a pena* ‘we scarcely’ → “scarcely we”: the adverb precedes the subject.
- *sospinto* ‘pushed’ → “pushed on”: the directional particle is supplied.
- *di chiappa in chiappa* ‘from rock to rock’ → “from jagg to jagg”: the rocks are described more specifically as jagged projections.

> 34 E se non fosse che da quel precinto,  
> 35 più che dall’ altro, era la costa corta,  
> 36 non so di lui, ma io sarei ben vinto.  

And were it not that on that precinct the ascent was shorter than on the other, I know not about him, but I certainly had been defeated.

- *E se non fosse che* ‘And if it were not that’ → “And were it not that”: conditional inversion replaces the conjunction *se*.
- *da quel precinto, più che dall’altro, era la costa corta* ‘from that circuit, more than from the other, the slope was short’ → “on that precinct the ascent was shorter than on the other”: *da* is interpreted locatively as “on”; the slope becomes “the ascent”; the separated adjective and comparative modifier become “shorter”, and the comparison follows it.
- *sarei ben vinto* ‘I would indeed be defeated’ → “I certainly had been defeated”: the present conditional becomes an English pluperfect used counterfactually, giving the sense of a completed hypothetical defeat.

> 37 Ma perchè Malebolge in ver la porta  
> 38 del bassissimo pozzo tutta pende,  
> 39 lo sito di ciascuna valle porta  
> 40 che l’ una costa surge e l’ altra scende;  

But as Malebolge all hangs towards the entrance of the lowest well, the site of every valley imports that one side rises and the other descends;

- *perchè* ‘because’ → “as”: the causal conjunction is rendered by another conjunction that retains the causal sense.
- *Malebolge in ver la porta del bassissimo pozzo tutta pende* ‘Malebolge towards the gate of the lowest well all hangs’ → “Malebolge all hangs towards the entrance of the lowest well”: the modifier and verb move before the directional phrase; *porta* is rendered as “entrance” rather than ‘gate’.
- *porta che* ‘brings it about that’ → “imports that”: the expression of consequence becomes an expression of what the situation entails.
- *l’una costa* ‘the one slope’ → “one side”: the definite article is omitted, and the sloping bank is called a “side”.

> 41 noi pur venimmo alfine in su la punta  
> 42 onde l’ ultima pietra si scoscende.  

we, however, came at length to the point from which the last stone breaks off.

- *in su la punta* ‘up onto the point’ → “to the point”: the explicit upward direction is not separately expressed.

> 43 La lena m’ era del polmon sì munta  
> 44 quando fui su, ch’ io non potea più oltre,  
> 45 anzi mi assisi nella prima giunta.  

The breath was so exhausted from my lungs, when I was up, that I could no farther; nay, seated me at my first arrival.

- *La lena m’era del polmon sì munta* ‘The breath was to me from the lung so milked’ → “The breath was so exhausted from my lungs”: the dative *m’* becomes the possessive “my”; the singular lung becomes plural; the metaphor of milking becomes exhaustion, and the predicate moves before the phrase naming the lungs.
- *non potea più oltre* ‘could not [go] any farther’ → “could no farther”: “no” replaces ‘not any’; the omission of a verb of movement is retained.
- *anzi* ‘rather’ → “nay”: the corrective conjunction becomes an emphatic English corrective.
- *mi assisi* ‘I seated myself’ → “seated me”: the reflexive is rendered with the archaic use of “me” rather than ‘myself’.
- *nella prima giunta* ‘at the first arrival’ → “at my first arrival”: the definite article becomes a possessive.

> 46 “Omai convien che tu così ti spoltre,”  
> 47 disse il maestro; “chè, seggendo in piuma,  
> 48 in fama non si vien, nè sotto coltre;  

“Now it behooves thee thus to free thyself from sloth,” said the Master: “for sitting on down, or under coverlet, men come not into fame;

- *convien che tu così ti spoltre* ‘it is fitting that thou thus shake off sloth’ → “it behooves thee thus to free thyself from sloth”: the finite subjunctive clause becomes an object pronoun followed by an infinitive; the single verb *spoltre* is expanded into “free thyself from sloth”.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *seggendo in piuma* ‘sitting in feathers’ → “sitting on down”: the preposition becomes “on”, and the collective singular *piuma* is rendered by the mass noun “down”.
- *in fama non si vien* ‘into fame one does not come’ → “men come not into fame”: the impersonal construction becomes a clause with the plural subject “men”; the prepositional phrase follows the verb, and negation follows “come”.
- *nè sotto coltre* ‘nor under coverlet’ → “or under coverlet”: the phrase moves forward to accompany “sitting on down”; “or” replaces ‘nor’, with the negation supplied by the following main clause.

> 49 senza la qual chi sua vita consuma,  
> 50 cotal vestigio in terra di sè lascia,  
> 51 qual fummo in aer ed in acqua la schiuma;  

without which whoso consumes his life, leaves such vestige of himself on earth, as smoke in air or foam in water;

- *chi sua vita consuma* ‘whoso his life consumes’ → “whoso consumes his life”: the object follows the verb.
- *cotal vestigio in terra di sè lascia* ‘such vestige on earth of himself leaves’ → “leaves such vestige of himself on earth”: the verb precedes its object, and the phrase identifying whose vestige it is moves before the phrase of place.
- *ed* ‘and’ → “or”: the two comparisons are presented as alternatives rather than joined additively.
- *in acqua la schiuma* ‘in water the foam’ → “foam in water”: the noun precedes the phrase of place, and the definite article is omitted.

> 52 e però leva su, vinci l’ ambascia  
> 53 con l’ animo che vince ogni battaglia,  
> 54 se col suo grave corpo non s’ accascia.  

and therefore rise! conquer thy panting with the soul, that conquers every battle, if with its heavy body it sinks not down.

- *leva su* ‘get up’ → “rise”: the verb and directional adverb become a single verb.
- *l’ambascia* ‘the distress’ → “thy panting”: the definite article becomes a possessive, and the broader term for distress is interpreted specifically as breathlessness.
- *l’animo* ‘the spirit’ → “the soul”: the word is rendered as “soul” rather than the spirit or resolve through which the effort is made.
- *vince ogni battaglia* ‘wins every battle’ → “conquers every battle”: the translation uses “conquers” where English ordinarily speaks of winning a battle.
- *non s’accascia* ‘does not collapse’ → “it sinks not down”: the pronominal verb becomes a non-pronominal verbal phrase, and the negation follows the verb.

## Lines 55–87 — Climbing Toward the Serpents

> 55 Più lunga scala convien che si saglia:  

A longer ladder must be climbed:

- *Più lunga scala convien che si saglia* ‘a longer ladder it is necessary that one climb’ → “A longer ladder must be climbed”: the impersonal expression of necessity becomes a modal passive, with the ladder as its subject; an indefinite article is supplied.

> 56 non basta da costoro esser partito;  

to have quitted these is not enough;

- *non basta da costoro esser partito* ‘it does not suffice to be departed from these’ → “to have quitted these is not enough”: the infinitive phrase becomes the initial subject; ‘does not suffice’ becomes “is not enough”; and departure from someone becomes the direct, transitive action of quitting them.

> 57 se tu m' intendi, or fa sì che ti vaglia."  

if thou understandest me, now act so that it may profit thee."

- *tu m' intendi* ‘thou me understandest’ → “thou understandest me”: the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *or fa sì che ti vaglia* ‘now do so that it may avail thee’ → “now act so that it may profit thee”: ‘do’ becomes the explicitly intransitive “act,” ‘avail’ becomes “profit,” and the object pronoun follows its verb.

> 58 Leva' mi allor, mostrandomi fornito  
> 59 meglio di lena ch' io non mi sentia;  

I then rose, showing myself better furnished with breath than I felt,

- *Leva' mi allor* ‘I raised myself then’ → “I then rose”: the reflexive construction becomes intransitive, and the time adverb moves before the verb.
- *fornito meglio* ‘furnished better’ → “better furnished”: the modifying adverb moves before the participle.
- *lena* ‘strength’ → “breath”: the translation selects a more specific physical sense.
- *ch' io non mi sentia* ‘than I did not feel myself’ → “than I felt”: the comparative construction’s non-negative *non* is omitted, as is the reflexive pronoun.

> 60 e dissi: "Va, ch' io son forte ed ardito."  

and said: "Go on; for I am strong and confident."

- *Va* ‘Go’ → “Go on”: the added particle makes continuation explicit.
- *ch'* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *ardito* ‘bold’ → “confident”: self-assurance replaces the more direct expression of daring.

> 61 Su per lo scoglio prendemmo la via,  
> 62 ch' era ronchioso, stretto e malagevole,  
> 63 ed erto più assai che quel di pria.  

We took our way up the cliff, which was rugged, narrow, and difficult, and greatly steeper than the former.

- *Su per lo scoglio prendemmo la via* ‘up along the rock we took the way’ → “We took our way up the cliff”: the directional phrase moves after the verb and object; “our” replaces the definite article; “up” absorbs *su per*; and “cliff” gives a more specific rendering of the rock.
- *ed erto più assai che quel di pria* ‘and steep much more than that of before’ → “and greatly steeper than the former”: the adjective and separate comparative become “steeper,” the intensifier moves before it, and “the former” condenses the demonstrative phrase ‘that of before’.

> 64 Parlando andava per non parer fievole,  
> 65 onde una voce uscìo dall' altro fosso,  
> 66 a parole formar disconvenevole.  

Speaking I went, that I might not seem faint; whereat a voice came from the other fosse, unsuitable for forming words.

- *per non parer fievole* ‘in order not to seem feeble’ → “that I might not seem faint”: the infinitive of purpose becomes a finite clause with “might,” and “faint” replaces ‘feeble’.
- *onde* ‘whence’ → “whereat”: the connective is interpreted as marking a response to the preceding action, rather than an origin.
- *uscìo* ‘went out’ → “came”: motion outward becomes motion toward the narrator; the outward element is left implicit.
- *a parole formar disconvenevole* ‘for words to form unsuitable’ → “unsuitable for forming words”: the adjective moves before its complement, the infinitive becomes an English *-ing* form, and its object follows it.

> 67 Non so che disse, ancor che sovra il dosso  
> 68 fossi dell' arco già, che varca quivi;  
> 69 ma chi parlava ad ira parea mosso.  

I know not what it said, though I already was on the ridge of the arch which crosses there; but he who spake seemed moved to anger.

- *sovra il dosso fossi dell' arco già* ‘above the ridge I were of the arch already’ → “I already was on the ridge of the arch”: the verb precedes the location phrase, “already” moves before the verb, and the separated noun and its complement are brought together. The concessive subjunctive becomes an English indicative, and *sovra* is rendered as “on.”
- *chi parlava* ‘whoever was speaking’ → “he who spake”: the fused relative becomes an explicit antecedent with a relative clause; the ongoing past action is rendered by a simple past.
- *ad ira parea mosso* ‘to anger seemed moved’ → “seemed moved to anger”: the complement moves after the verb and participle.

> 70 Io era volto in giù; ma gli occhi vivi  
> 71 non potean ire al fondo per l' oscuro;  

I had turned myself downwards; but my living eyes could not reach the bottom for the darkness;

- *Io era volto in giù* ‘I was turned downwards’ → “I had turned myself downwards”: a past state becomes a completed, active reflexive action in the pluperfect.
- *gli occhi vivi* ‘the eyes living’ → “my living eyes”: the definite article becomes a possessive, and the adjective moves before the noun.
- *non potean ire al fondo* ‘could not go to the bottom’ → “could not reach the bottom”: “reach” replaces the verb of motion and its directional preposition.
- *l' oscuro* ‘the dark’ → “the darkness”: the substantivized adjective becomes an abstract noun.

> 72 per ch' io: "Maestro, fa che tu arrivi  
> 73 dall' altro cinghio, e dismontiam lo muro:  
> 74 chè com' i' odo quinci e non intendo,  
> 75 così giù veggio, e niente affiguro."  

wherefore I: "Master, see that thou get to the other belt, and let us dismount the wall: for as I hear from hence and do not understand, so I see down and distinguish nothing."

- *fa che tu arrivi dall' altro cinghio* ‘bring it about that thou arrive at the other belt’ → “see that thou get to the other belt”: the causative imperative becomes the idiom “see that”; ‘arrive at’ becomes “get to,” with *da* understood directionally.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *così giù veggio* ‘so down I see’ → “so I see down”: the directional adverb moves after the verb.
- *niente affiguro* ‘nothing I distinguish’ → “distinguish nothing”: the object follows the verb.

> 76 "Altra risposta," disse, "non ti rendo,  
> 77 se non lo far: chè la domanda onesta  
> 78 si dee seguir con l' opera tacendo."  

"Other answer I give thee not," he said, "than the deed: for a fit request should be followed with the work in silence."

- *Altra risposta, disse, non ti rendo* ‘Other answer, he said, I do not render thee’ → “Other answer I give thee not," he said”: the reporting clause moves after the completed clause; ‘render’ becomes “give”; and the negative and object pronoun move after the verb.
- *se non lo far* ‘except the doing’ → “than the deed”: the exception becomes a complement introduced by “than,” following “Other”; the nominalized infinitive becomes the noun “deed.”
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *la domanda onesta* ‘the request fitting’ → “a fit request”: the definite article becomes indefinite, and the adjective moves before the noun.
- *tacendo* ‘keeping silent’ → “in silence”: the verbal expression becomes a prepositional phrase.

> 79 Noi discendemmo il ponte dalla testa,  
> 80 dove s' aggiunge con l' ottava ripa,  
> 81 e poi mi fu la bolgia manifesta:  

We went down the bridge, at the head where it joins with the eighth bank; and then the chasm was manifest to me:

- *discendemmo* ‘descended’ → “went down”: a single verb becomes a verb with a directional particle.
- *dalla testa* ‘from the head’ → “at the head”: the translation expresses location rather than a starting point.
- *s' aggiunge* ‘joins itself’ → “it joins”: the reflexive construction becomes an English intransitive verb.
- *mi fu la bolgia manifesta* ‘to me was the ditch manifest’ → “the chasm was manifest to me”: the subject moves before the verb and the personal complement moves to the end; “chasm” replaces ‘ditch’.

> 82 e vidivi entro terribile stipa  
> 83 di serpenti, e di sì diversa mena,  
> 84 che la memoria il sangue ancor mi scipa.  

and I saw within it a fearful throng of serpents, and of so strange a look, that even now the recollection scares my blood.

- *vidivi entro* ‘I saw there within’ → “I saw within it”: the locative enclitic becomes the pronoun governed by “within,” rather than a separate adverb.
- *terribile stipa* ‘fearful throng’ → “a fearful throng”: an indefinite article is supplied.
- *di sì diversa mena* ‘of so different a kind’ → “of so strange a look”: difference becomes strangeness, and kind or variety becomes outward appearance.
- *la memoria il sangue ancor mi scipa* ‘the recollection the blood still for me ruins’ → “even now the recollection scares my blood”: “even now” gives an emphatic rendering of *ancor* and moves to the beginning; the object follows the verb; the personal dative becomes the possessive “my”; and ‘ruins’ or ‘damages’ becomes “scares.”

> 85 Più non si vanti Libia con sua rena;  

Let Libya boast no longer with its sand;

- *Più non si vanti Libia* ‘no longer may Libya boast itself’ → “Let Libya boast no longer”: the exhortative subjunctive becomes a construction with “Let,” the reflexive pronoun disappears, and the negative time expression moves to the end.

> 86 chè, se chelidri, iaculi e faree  
> 87 produce, e cencri con amfisibena,  

for, though it engenders chelydri, jaculi and pareæ, and cenchres with amphisbæna,

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *se* ‘if’ → “though”: the conditional is interpreted concessively.
- *chelidri, iaculi e faree produce* ‘chelydri, jaculi and pareæ it produces’ → “it engenders chelydri, jaculi and pareæ”: the verb moves before these objects, and “engenders” makes biological generation more explicit than ‘produces’.

## Lines 88–120 — Serpents, Ashes, and Rebirth

> 88 nè tante pestilenzie nè sì ree  
> 89 mostrò giammai con tutta l' Etiopia,  
> 90 nè con ciò che di sopra il mar rosso ee.  

plagues so numerous or so dire it never shewed, with all Ethiopia, nor with the land that lies by the Red Sea.

- *nè … nè … giammai* ‘neither … nor … ever’ → “or” and “never”: the paired negatives become alternatives under “never”; the temporal negative precedes the verb rather than following it.
- *tante pestilenzie* ‘so many plagues’ → “plagues so numerous”: the expression of quantity becomes an adjective phrase following the noun.
- *sì ree* ‘so wicked’ → “so dire”: moral badness is rendered as dreadful severity.
- *ciò che di sopra il mar rosso ee* ‘that which above the Red Sea is’ → “the land that lies by the Red Sea”: “the land” makes the indefinite *ciò* explicit; “lies” replaces ‘is’, and “by” interprets *di sopra* as proximity rather than position above.

> 91 Tra questa cruda e tristissima copia  
> 92 correvan genti nude e spaventate,  
> 93 senza sperar pertugio o elitropia.  

Amid this cruel and most dismal swarm were people running, naked and terrified, without hope of lurking hole or heliotrope.

- *questa cruda e tristissima copia* ‘this cruel and most sad multitude’ → “this cruel and most dismal swarm”: “dismal” replaces ‘sad’, and “swarm” gives *copia*, ‘multitude’ or ‘abundance’, a more specific collective sense.
- *correvan genti* ‘ran people’ → “were people running”: the simple imperfect becomes an English progressive, with the subject between auxiliary and participle.
- *senza sperar pertugio o elitropia* ‘without hoping for a hole or heliotrope’ → “without hope of lurking hole or heliotrope”: the infinitive becomes the noun “hope” with an “of” complement; “lurking” is supplied, and the singular “hole” has no indefinite article.

> 94 Con serpi le man dietro avean legate;  
> 95 quelle ficcavan per le ren la coda  
> 96 e il capo, ed eran dinanzi aggroppate.  

They had their hands tied behind with serpents; these through their loins fixed the tail and the head, and were coiled in knots before.

- *Con serpi le man dietro avean legate* ‘with serpents the hands behind they had bound’ → “They had their hands tied behind with serpents”: the instrumental phrase moves to the end, the verb precedes its object, and “their” replaces the definite article before *man*.
- *quelle* ‘those’ → “these”: the demonstrative changes from distant to near.
- *ficcavan per le ren la coda e il capo* ‘thrust through the loins the tail and the head’ → “through their loins fixed the tail and the head”: the locative phrase moves before the verb; “their” replaces the definite article, and “fixed” renders the action as fastening rather than thrusting.
- *ed eran dinanzi aggroppate* ‘and were in front knotted’ → “and were coiled in knots before”: “coiled in knots” expands *aggroppate*, and the locative expression follows rather than precedes the participial phrase.

> 97 Ed ecco ad un, ch' era da nostra proda,  
> 98 s'avventò un serpente, che il trafisse  
> 99 là dove il collo alle spalle s'annoda.  

And lo! at one, who was near our shore, sprang up a serpent, which transfixed him there where the neck is bound upon the shoulders.

- *ch' era da nostra proda* ‘who was on our side’ → “who was near our shore”: *proda* is given its concrete sense of ‘shore’, and *da* is interpreted as proximity.
- *s'avventò* ‘hurled itself’ → “sprang up”: the reflexive verb becomes an intransitive phrasal verb, with “up” supplied.
- *il trafisse* ‘him transfixed’ → “transfixed him”: the object pronoun follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *là dove il collo alle spalle s'annoda* ‘there where the neck to the shoulders knots itself’ → “there where the neck is bound upon the shoulders”: the reflexive construction becomes passive, “upon” replaces ‘to’, and the prepositional phrase moves after the verb.

> 100 Nè o sì tosto mai nè i si scrisse,  
> 101 com' ei s'accese ed arse, e cener tutto  
> 102 convenne che cascando divenisse;  

Neither “O” nor “I” was ever written so quickly as he took fire, and burnt, and dropt down all changed to ashes;

- *Nè o sì tosto mai nè i si scrisse* ‘neither O so quickly ever nor I was written’ → “Neither “O” nor “I” was ever written so quickly”: the two alternatives are brought together; “ever” precedes the participle, while “so quickly” moves to the end.
- *s'accese* ‘was kindled’ → “took fire”: the reflexive or middle construction becomes an active verbal idiom.
- *e cener tutto convenne che cascando divenisse* ‘and ash wholly it was necessary that, falling, he should become’ → “and dropt down all changed to ashes”: the necessity expressed by *convenne che* is omitted; the gerund ‘falling’ becomes the finite “dropt down”, while the subordinate subjunctive ‘should become’ becomes the passive participial “changed”. The fronted *cener* moves to the end as the plural “ashes”.

> 103 e poi che fu a terra sì distrutto,  
> 104 la polver si raccolse per sè stessa,  
> 105 e in quel medesmo ritornò di butto:  

and after he was thus dissolved upon the ground, the [powder] reunited of [itself and] at once resumed the former shape:

- *fu a terra sì distrutto* ‘was on the ground so destroyed’ → “was thus dissolved upon the ground”: “dissolved” replaces ‘destroyed’, “thus” gives *sì* a manner rather than degree sense, and the locative phrase moves after the participle.
- *la polver si raccolse per sè stessa, e in quel medesmo ritornò di butto* ‘the dust gathered itself by itself, and into that same one returned suddenly’ → “the [powder] reunited of [itself and] at once resumed the former shape”: the reflexive *si raccolse* becomes intransitive “reunited”, and ‘by itself’ becomes “of [itself and]”. The elliptical *in quel medesmo* is interpreted as “the former shape”; *ritornò* with a prepositional complement becomes transitive “resumed” with a direct object. “at once” precedes the verb rather than following it.

> 106 così per li gran savi si confessa,  
> 107 che la fenice more e poi rinasce,  
> 108 quando al cinquecentesimo anno appressa;  

thus by great sages 'tis confest the Phœnix dies, and then is born again, when it approaches the five-hundredth year;

- *per li gran savi* ‘by the great sages’ → “by great sages”: the definite article is omitted.
- *che la fenice more* ‘that the phoenix dies’ → “the Phœnix dies”: the conjunction introducing the reported assertion is omitted.
- *quando al cinquecentesimo anno appressa* ‘when to the five-hundredth year it draws near’ → “when it approaches the five-hundredth year”: an intransitive verb with a prepositional complement becomes transitive “approaches”; the complement moves after the verb.

> 109 erba nè biado in sua vita non pasce,  
> 110 ma sol d'incenso lagrime ed amomo,  
> 111 e nardo e mirra son l' ultime fasce.  

in its life it eats no herb or grain, but only tears of incense and amomum; and nard and myrrh are its last swathings.

- *erba nè biado in sua vita non pasce* ‘herb nor grain in its life it does not feed on’ → “in its life it eats no herb or grain”: the time phrase moves to the beginning and the objects move after the verb. The verbal negative *non*, reinforced by *nè*, becomes “no” before the first noun and “or” before the second; ‘feeds on’ becomes “eats”.
- *d'incenso lagrime* ‘of incense tears’ → “tears of incense”: the modifying phrase follows rather than precedes its noun.
- *l' ultime fasce* ‘the last bands’ → “its last swathings”: “its” replaces the definite article, and “swathings” gives *fasce* the more specific sense of wrappings.

> 112 E qual è quei che cade, e non sa como,  
> 113 per forza di demon ch' a terra il tira,  
> 114 o d' altra oppilazion che lega l' uomo,  

And as one who falls, and knows not how, through force of Demon which drags him to the ground, or of other obstruction that fetters men;

- *E qual è quei che cade* ‘and as is he who falls’ → “And as one who falls”: the copula is omitted, and the demonstrative *quei* becomes indefinite “one”.
- *non sa como* ‘not knows how’ → “knows not how”: the negative follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *ch' a terra il tira* ‘which to the ground him pulls’ → “which drags him to the ground”: both the object pronoun and the locative phrase move after the verb; “drags” gives *tira* a more forceful sense than ‘pulls’.
- *che lega l' uomo* ‘that binds man’ → “that fetters men”: “fetters” replaces the more general ‘binds’, and the generic definite singular becomes an articleless plural.

> 115 quando si leva, che intorno si mira  
> 116 tutto smarrito dalla grande angoscia  
> 117 ch' egli ha sofferta, e guardando sospira:  

who, when he rises, looks fixtly round him, all bewildered by the great anguish he has undergone, and looking sighs:

- *quando si leva* ‘when he raises himself’ → “when he rises”: the reflexive construction becomes intransitive.
- *che intorno si mira* ‘who around himself looks’ → “who” and “looks fixtly round him”: the relative pronoun moves before the intervening temporal clause; the verb precedes the expression of looking around, and “fixtly” is supplied.
- *ch' egli ha sofferta* ‘that he has suffered’ → “he has undergone”: the relative pronoun is omitted, and “undergone” replaces ‘suffered’.

> 118 tal era il peccator levato poscia.  

such was the sinner when he rose.

- *levato poscia* ‘risen afterward’ → “when he rose”: the participial phrase becomes a finite temporal clause, and *poscia*, ‘afterward’, is omitted.

> 119 O potenzia di Dio, quant' è severa,  
> 120 che cotai colpi per vendetta croscia!  

[Power] of God! o how severe, that showers such blows in vengeance!

- *O potenzia di Dio, quant' è severa* ‘O power of God, how it is severe’ → “[Power] of God! o how severe”: the opening vocative interjection moves before “how”, and the copula is omitted.
- *che cotai colpi per vendetta croscia* ‘that such blows for vengeance showers’ → “that showers such blows in vengeance”: the verb moves before its object and prepositional phrase; “in” replaces *per*, ‘for’.

## Lines 121–151 — Vanni Fucci’s Confession and Threat

> 121 Lo duca il domandò poi chi egli era;  
> 122 per ch’ ei rispose: “Io piovvi di Toscana,  
> 123 poco tempo è, in questa gola fera.  

The Guide then asked him who he was; whereupon he answered: "I rained from Tuscany, short while ago, into this fierce gullet.

- *duca* ‘leader’ → “Guide”: the noun is rendered by the role of guiding.
- *il domandò poi* ‘him asked then’ → “then asked him”: the object moves after the verb, and the temporal adverb before it.
- *per ch’* ‘for which reason’ → “whereupon”: the causal connection becomes a connection of consequence or immediate succession.
- *poco tempo è* ‘little time it is’ → “short while ago”: a finite expression of elapsed time becomes an adverbial phrase.
- *gola fera* ‘gullet fierce’ → “fierce gullet”: the adjective moves before the noun.

> 124 Vita bestial mi piacque, e non umana,  
> 125 sì come a mul ch’ io fui; son Vanni Fucci  
> 126 bestia, e Pistoia mi fu degna tana.”  

Bestial life, not human, pleased me, mule that I was; I am Vanni Fucci, savage beast; and Pistoia was a fitting den for me."

- *Vita bestial mi piacque, e non umana* ‘Life bestial was pleasing to me, and not human’ → “Bestial life, not human, pleased me”: the adjective precedes the noun; the contrasting phrase moves before the verb, and its conjunction is omitted. The Italian construction with a dative becomes the transitive “pleased me”.
- *sì come a mul ch’ io fui* ‘just as to a mule that I was’ → “mule that I was”: the comparative expression and its preposition are omitted, leaving an appositional phrase.
- *bestia* ‘beast’ → “savage beast”: “savage” is supplied.
- *mi fu degna tana* ‘to me was worthy den’ → “was a fitting den for me”: the dative moves to the end as “for me”; an indefinite article is supplied, and *degna* is rendered as “fitting”.

> 127 Ed io al duca: “Digli che non mucci,  
> 128 e domanda qual colpa quaggiù il pinse:  
> 129 ch’ io il vidi uomo di sangue e di crucci.”  

And I to the Guide: "Tell him not to budge; and ask what crime thrust him down here, for I saw him once a man of rage and blood."

- *al duca* ‘to the leader’ → “to the Guide”: the noun is again rendered by the role of guiding.
- *che non mucci* ‘that he not slip away’ → “not to budge”: a finite subordinate clause becomes an infinitive construction; slipping away becomes moving at all.
- *qual colpa quaggiù il pinse* ‘what fault down here him thrust’ → “what crime thrust him down here”: “crime” gives *colpa* a specifically criminal sense; the object and the adverbial phrase move after the verb.
- *ch’* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *io il vidi uomo di sangue e di crucci* ‘I him saw man of blood and of angers’ → “I saw him once a man of rage and blood”: the object follows the verb; “once” and an indefinite article are supplied. The two qualities are reversed, plural *crucci* becomes singular “rage”, and the repeated preposition is omitted.

> 130 E il peccator, che intese, non s’ infinse,  
> 131 ma drizzò verso me l’ animo e il volto,  
> 132 e di trista vergogna si dipinse;  

And the sinner who heard, feigned not; but directed towards me his mind and face, with a look of dismal shame;

- *non s’ infinse* ‘did not disguise himself’ → “feigned not”: the pronominal verb becomes non-reflexive, and the negative follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *drizzò verso me l’ animo e il volto* ‘directed towards me the mind and the face’ → “directed towards me his mind and face”: the two definite articles are replaced by a single shared possessive.
- *e di trista vergogna si dipinse* ‘and with dismal shame he painted himself’ → “with a look of dismal shame”: a coordinated finite clause becomes a prepositional phrase. The conjunction and the image of painting oneself disappear; “a look” is supplied.

> 133 poi disse: “Più mi duol che tu m’ hai colto  
> 134 nella miseria dove tu mi vedi,  
> 135 che quando fui dell’ altra vita tolto.  

then he said: "It pains me more that thou hast caught me in the misery wherein thou seest me, than when I was taken from the other life.

- *Più mi duol* ‘More to me is painful’ → “It pains me more”: the degree word moves to the end, the dative construction becomes transitive, and anticipatory “It” introduces the following clause.
- *tu m’ hai colto* ‘thou me hast caught’ → “thou hast caught me”: the object moves after the complete verb phrase.
- *dove tu mi vedi* ‘where thou me seest’ → “wherein thou seest me”: “wherein” explicitly expresses the relation of being in the misery; the object moves after the verb.
- *fui dell’ altra vita tolto* ‘I was from the other life taken’ → “I was taken from the other life”: the participle moves before the prepositional phrase.

> 136 Io non posso negar quel che tu chiedi:  

I cannot deny thee what thou askest:

- *Io non posso negar* ‘I not can deny’ → “I cannot deny thee”: the preceding negative is incorporated into “cannot”, and the indirect object “thee” is supplied.
- *quel che* ‘that which’ → “what”: the demonstrative and relative pronoun become a single fused relative.

> 137 in giù son messo tanto, perch’ io fui  
> 138 ladro alla sacrestia de’ belli arredi;  
> 139 e falsamente già fu apposto altrui.  

I am put down so far, because I robbed the sacristy of its goodly furniture; and falsely once it was imputed to others.

- *in giù son messo tanto* ‘downward I am put so much’ → “I am put down so far”: the directional expression moves after the verb, and “so far” makes the spatial force of *tanto* explicit.
- *io fui ladro alla sacrestia de’ belli arredi* ‘I was a thief, at the sacristy, of the beautiful furnishings’ → “I robbed the sacristy of its goodly furniture”: the construction with a predicate noun becomes an active verb. The sacristy changes from a prepositional complement to a direct object; “its” replaces the definite article before the furnishings, and plural *arredi* becomes the collective singular “furniture”.
- *già* ‘already’ → “once”: the adverb is taken in its sense of formerly, rather than of prior completion.

> 140 Ma perchè di tal vista tu non godi,  
> 141 se mai sarai di fuor de’ lochi bui,  
> 142 apri gli orecchi al mio annunzio, ed odi:  

But that thou mayest not joy in this sight, if ever thou escape the dark abodes, open thy ears and hear what I announce:

- *perchè di tal vista tu non godi* ‘so that in such a sight thou not rejoice’ → “that thou mayest not joy in this sight”: the purpose clause is expressed with “mayest”; the prepositional phrase moves after the verb, and *tal* becomes “this” rather than ‘such’.
- *se mai sarai di fuor de’ lochi bui* ‘if ever thou shalt be outside the places dark’ → “if ever thou escape the dark abodes”: a future state of being outside becomes an unmarked conditional verb expressing escape. “Abodes” substitutes for ‘places’, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *apri gli orecchi al mio annunzio, ed odi* ‘open the ears to my announcement, and hear’ → “open thy ears and hear what I announce”: the definite article becomes a possessive. The announcement, originally the complement of opening the ears, becomes the object of hearing; the possessive noun phrase becomes a relative clause with a finite verb.

> 143 Pistoia in pria di Negri si dimagra,  
> 144 poi Fiorenza rinnuova genti e modi.  

Pistoia first is thinned of Neri; then Florence renovates her people and her laws.

- *Pistoia in pria di Negri si dimagra* ‘Pistoia first of Blacks makes itself lean’ → “Pistoia first is thinned of Neri”: the reflexive construction becomes passive, and the prepositional phrase moves after the verb. The designation meaning ‘Blacks’ is retained in Italian rather than translated.
- *genti e modi* ‘peoples and ways’ → “her people and her laws”: two possessives are supplied; plural *genti* becomes collective “people”, and the broad *modi* is interpreted specifically as “laws”.

> 145 Tragge Marte vapor di Val di Magra  
> 146 ch’ è di torbidi nuvoli involuto,  
> 147 e con tempesta impetuosa ed agra  
> 148 sopra campo Picen fia combattuto;  

Mars brings from Valdimagra a fiery vapour, which is wrapt in turbid clouds, and with angry and impetuous storm [a battle] shall be [fought] on Piceno's field;

- *Tragge Marte vapor di Val di Magra* ‘Draws Mars vapour from Val di Magra’ → “Mars brings from Valdimagra a fiery vapour”: the subject moves before the verb, and the source phrase before the object. ‘Draws’ becomes “brings”; an indefinite article and “fiery” are supplied.
- *ch’ è di torbidi nuvoli involuto* ‘which is in turbid clouds wrapped’ → “which is wrapt in turbid clouds”: the participle moves before its prepositional complement.
- *con tempesta impetuosa ed agra* ‘with storm impetuous and bitter’ → “with angry and impetuous storm”: the adjectives move before the noun and exchange order; *agra* becomes “angry” rather than ‘bitter’.
- *sopra campo Picen fia combattuto* ‘upon field Picene shall be fought’ → “[a battle] shall be [fought] on Piceno's field”: the passive is taken impersonally, with “[a battle]” supplied as an explicit subject. The place phrase moves after the verb, and the adjective *Picen* becomes the possessive “Piceno's”.

> 149 ond’ ei repente spezzerà la nebbia,  
> 150 sì ch’ ogni Bianco ne sarà feruto.  

whence it suddenly shall rend the mist, so that every Bianco shall be wounded by it.

- *spezzerà* ‘will break’ → “shall rend”: a verb of breaking becomes one of tearing apart.
- *ogni Bianco* ‘every White’ → “every Bianco”: the designation is retained in Italian rather than translated.
- *ne sarà feruto* ‘by it shall be wounded’ → “shall be wounded by it”: the preceding pronominal expression becomes a following prepositional phrase, with *ne* understood as indicating the agent or cause.

> 151 E detto l’ ho, perchè doler ti debbia.”  

And I have said this [so that] it [may] grieve thee."

- *detto l’ ho* ‘said it I have’ → “I have said this”: the auxiliary precedes the participle, the object follows it, and the personal pronoun becomes the demonstrative “this”.
- *perchè doler ti debbia* ‘so that be painful to thee it may’ → “[so that] it [may] grieve thee”: *perchè* is read as introducing purpose. The modal moves before the infinitive, and the construction meaning ‘be painful to thee’ becomes the transitive “grieve thee”.

## Summary — Close Echoes and Prose Adjustments

Carlyle’s translation stays close to the Italian’s sequence of expressions and often preserves its imagery, repetitions, and unusual phrasing. Yet it regularly rearranges words into more customary English order: subjects precede verbs, objects follow them, and adjectives precede nouns. Thus *Le braccia aperse* becomes “He opened his arms”, while *d’incenso lagrime* becomes “tears of incense”. Elsewhere, marked Italian arrangements survive, as in “Speaking I went” and “such vestige of himself on earth”. The result combines ordinary prose adjustments with conspicuously literal echoes.

Changes of construction recur throughout. Reflexive verbs often become English intransitives, as *si leva* becomes “rises”; possessive datives and definite articles become possessives, as in *si batte l’anca*, “smites his thigh”. Finite clauses become infinitives, infinitives become finite clauses, and impersonal expressions acquire personal subjects or passive forms: *in fama non si vien* becomes “men come not into fame”, while *Più lunga scala convien che si saglia* becomes “A longer ladder must be climbed”. More generally, beyond the changes itemized in the comparison, English supplies subject pronouns where Italian verb endings identify the subject, and omits the articles that Italian places before possessives.

Vocabulary sometimes narrows or interprets the Italian rather than reproducing its breadth. *Roba* becomes “fodder”, *ambascia* becomes “panting”, and *modi* becomes “laws”; conversely, *vincastro*, ‘shepherd’s crook’, becomes the broader “staff”. Diminutive force is softened or recast: *villanello* becomes “peasant”, whereas *pecorelle* becomes “lambs”. Some figurative language is simplified, notably *ringavagna*, ‘gathers back into a basket’, rendered as “recovers”; other striking expressions remain close, including “I rained from Tuscany” and “tears of incense”.

Additions make implications explicit, as with “of lead” for *cappa*, “savage” for *bestia*, and “fiery” for *vapor*. Omissions can reduce emphasis or alter the relation between actions: *poscia* has no separate equivalent in “when he rose”, and the necessity expressed by *convenne che* disappears from “dropt down all changed to ashes”. Connectives also receive interpretive renderings, with *se* becoming “though” and *ed* becoming “or”. Overall, the translation preserves much of the Italian’s verbal texture, but adjusts its grammar, supplies contextual particulars, and occasionally replaces an image or a precise relationship with a more immediately intelligible English expression.
