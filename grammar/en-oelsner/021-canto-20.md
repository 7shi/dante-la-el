# Inferno 20

## Lines 1–18 — The Backward-Facing Sufferers

> 1 Di nuova pena mi convien far versi,  
> 2 e dar materia al ventesimo canto  
> 3 della prima canzon, ch' è de' sommersi.  

Of new punishment behooves me to make verses, and give matter for the twentieth canto of the first canzone, which concerns the sunken.

- *mi convien* ‘to me it behooves’ → “behooves me”: the pronoun follows the verb instead of preceding it.
- *al ventesimo canto* ‘to the twentieth canto’ → “for the twentieth canto”: the relation expressed by *a* is rendered with “for” rather than ‘to’.
- *ch' è de' sommersi* ‘which is of the sunken’ → “which concerns the sunken”: the construction ‘is of’ becomes the transitive verb “concerns”.

> 4 Io era già disposto tutto quanto  
> 5 a riguardar nello scoperto fondo,  
> 6 che si bagnava d' angoscioso pianto ;  

I now was all prepared to look into the depth discovered to me, which was bathed with tears of anguish;

- *già* ‘already’ → “now”: the temporal adverb changes and is placed before “was” rather than after it.
- *disposto tutto quanto* ‘prepared wholly’ → “all prepared”: the intensive phrase *tutto quanto* is compressed into “all” and placed before “prepared”.
- *nello scoperto fondo* ‘into the uncovered bottom’ → “into the depth discovered to me”: “depth” replaces ‘bottom’; *scoperto* becomes “discovered” rather than ‘uncovered’, moves after the noun, and receives the supplied complement “to me”.
- *si bagnava* ‘was bathing itself’ → “was bathed”: the Italian reflexive or middle construction becomes an English passive.
- *d' angoscioso pianto* ‘with anguished weeping’ → “with tears of anguish”: singular *pianto*, the act of weeping, becomes plural “tears”; the preceding adjective becomes a following noun phrase, “of anguish”.

> 7 e vidi gente per lo vallon tondo  
> 8 venir tacendo e lagrimando, al passo  
> 9 che fan le letaníe in questo mondo.  

and through the circular valley I saw a people coming silent and weeping, at the pace which the Litanies make in this world.

- *gente* ‘people’ → “a people”: an indefinite article is supplied, presenting the collective as a distinct group.
- *per lo vallon tondo* ‘through the valley round’ → “through the circular valley”: the adjective precedes the noun, and the whole prepositional phrase moves before “I saw” instead of standing after its object.
- *venir* ‘to come’ → “coming”: the infinitive dependent on a verb of perception becomes a present participle.
- *tacendo* ‘keeping silent’ → “silent”: the Italian gerund becomes an adjective.
- *che fan le letaníe* ‘which make the litanies’ → “which the Litanies make”: the subject *le letaníe*, which follows its verb in Italian, precedes it in English.

> 10 Come il viso mi scese in lor più basso,  
> 11 mirabilmente apparve esser travolto  
> 12 ciascun tra il mento e'l principio del casso :  

When my sight descended lower on them, each seemed wondrously distorted, [between] the chin [and] the commencement of the chest:

- *Come* ‘as’ → “When”: the conjunction is rendered with an explicitly temporal equivalent.
- *il viso mi scese* ‘the sight descended for me’ → “my sight descended”: the dative pronoun *mi* becomes the possessive “my” modifying “sight”.
- *in lor più basso* ‘on them lower’ → “lower on them”: the comparative expression moves before the prepositional phrase.
- *mirabilmente apparve esser travolto ciascun* ‘wondrously appeared to be twisted each’ → “each seemed wondrously distorted”: the subject moves to the beginning; ‘appeared to be’ is compressed into “seemed”; “distorted” renders ‘twisted’; and the adverb moves immediately before “distorted”.

> 13 chè dalle reni era tornato il volto,  
> 14 ed indietro venir gli convenia,  
> 15 perchè il veder dinanzi era lor tolto.  

[for] the face was turned towards the loins; and they had to come backward, for to look before them was denied.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “[for]”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *dalle reni era tornato il volto* ‘towards the loins was turned the face’ → “the face was turned towards the loins”: the subject moves before the verb, and the directional phrase moves after it.
- *ed indietro venir gli convenia* ‘and backward to come was necessary for him’ → “and they had to come backward”: the impersonal construction expressing necessity becomes the personal modal construction “they had to”; the singular reference to each individual is rendered collectively in the plural, and “backward” moves after “come”.
- *perchè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *il veder dinanzi era lor tolto* ‘the seeing in front was taken away from them’ → “to look before them was denied”: the substantival infinitive becomes an English infinitive without an article; ‘seeing’ becomes “to look”, and ‘taken away’ becomes “denied”. The dative *lor* is no longer separately expressed as the recipient of the deprivation; “them” instead completes “before”.

> 16 Forse per forza già di parlasìa  
> 17 si travolse così alcun del tutto ;  
> 18 ma io nol vidi, nè credo che sia.  

Perhaps by force of palsy some have been thus quite distorted; but I have not seen, nor do believe it to be so.

- *per forza già di parlasìa* ‘by force once of palsy’ → “by force of palsy”: *già*, marking an occurrence at some previous time, is omitted.
- *si travolse così alcun del tutto* ‘twisted itself thus someone entirely’ → “some have been thus quite distorted”: the reflexive construction becomes passive, and the simple past becomes a present perfect. Singular *alcun* becomes plural “some” and moves before the verb; ‘twisted’ becomes “distorted”; and both “thus” and “quite” precede the participle rather than following the Italian verb.
- *ma io nol vidi* ‘but I did not see it’ → “but I have not seen”: the simple past becomes a present perfect, and the object pronoun contained in *nol* is omitted.
- *nè credo che sia* ‘nor believe that it is’ → “nor do believe it to be so”: “do” is supplied; the finite subordinate clause with subjunctive *sia* becomes the infinitival construction “it to be”; and “so” makes the implied predicate explicit.

## Lines 19–51 — Pity and the Backward-Looking Diviners

> 19 Se Dio ti lasci, lettor, prender frutto  
> 20 di tua lezione, or pensa per te stesso  
> 21 com' io potea tener lo viso asciutto,  

Reader, so God grant thee to take profit of thy reading, now think for thyself how I could keep my visage dry,

- *Se Dio ti lasci, lettor* ‘if God let thee, reader’ → “Reader, so God grant thee”: the address moves from within the conditional clause to the beginning; the conditional form is rendered as a wish or invocation, and ‘let’ becomes “grant”.
- *prender frutto* ‘take fruit’ → “to take profit”: the figurative ‘fruit’ is replaced by its meaning, “profit”.
- *lo viso* ‘the visage’ → “my visage”: the definite article becomes a possessive.

> 22 quando la nostra imagine da presso  
> 23 vidi sì torta, che il pianto degli occhi  
> 24 le natiche bagnava per lo fesso.  

when near at hand I saw our image so contorted, that the weeping of the eyes bathed the hinder parts at their division?

- *la nostra imagine da presso vidi* ‘our image near at hand I saw’ → “near at hand I saw our image”: the object moves after the verb, leaving the adverbial phrase before it.
- *le natiche bagnava* ‘the buttocks bathed’ → “bathed the hinder parts”: the object moves after the verb, and the anatomical noun is replaced by a euphemistic phrase.
- *per lo fesso* ‘along the cleft’ → “at their division”: the preposition changes, ‘cleft’ becomes the less concrete “division”, and the definite article becomes a possessive.

> 25 Certo i' piangea, poggiato ad un de' rocchi  
> 26 del duro scoglio, sì che la mia scorta  
> 27 mi disse: " Ancor se' tu degli altri sciocchi?  

Certainly I wept, leaning on one of the rocks of the hard cliff, so that my Escort said to me: "Art thou, too, like the other fools?

- *poggiato ad* ‘rested against’ → “leaning on”: the past participle becomes a present participle, with a corresponding change of preposition.
- *rocchi* ‘blocks’ → “rocks”: the translation uses a more general term for the pieces of stone.
- *mi disse* ‘to me said’ → “said to me”: the indirect object follows the verb.
- *Ancor se' tu* ‘also art thou’ → “Art thou, too,”: the adverb moves after the subject.
- *degli altri sciocchi* ‘of the other fools’ → “like the other fools”: membership in a group becomes comparison with it.

> 28 Qui vive la pietà quando è ben morta.  

Here pity lives when it is altogether dead.

- *Qui vive la pietà* ‘Here lives the pity’ → “Here pity lives”: the subject moves before the verb, and English omits the article before the abstract noun.
- *ben* ‘well’ → “altogether”: the adverb’s intensive force is expressed as completeness.

> 29 Chi è più scellerato che colui  
> 30 che al giudicio divin compassion porta?  

Who more impious than he that sorrows at God's judgment?

- *Chi è più scellerato* ‘Who is more wicked’ → “Who more impious”: the copula is omitted, and general wickedness becomes specifically religious impiety.
- *che al giudicio divin compassion porta* ‘that to the divine judgment compassion brings’ → “that sorrows at God's judgment?”: the verb–noun expression becomes the single verb “sorrows”; its complement follows rather than precedes it. ‘To’ becomes “at”, and the adjective ‘divine’ becomes the possessive “God's”.

> 31 Drizza la testa, drizza, e vedi a cui  
> 32 s' aperse agli occhi de' Teban la terra,  

Raise up thy head, raise up, and see him for whom the earth opened herself before the eyes of the Thebans,

- *Drizza la testa, drizza* ‘Lift the head, lift’ → “Raise up thy head, raise up”: the simple verb becomes a phrasal verb, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *e vedi a cui* ‘and see for whom’ → “and see him for whom”: “him” is supplied as the antecedent of the relative.
- *s' aperse agli occhi de' Teban la terra* ‘opened itself to the eyes of the Thebans the earth’ → “the earth opened herself before the eyes of the Thebans”: the subject moves before the verb; the reflexive becomes the personifying “herself”; and ‘to the eyes’ becomes “before the eyes”.

> 33 per ch' ei gridavan tutti: ' Dove rui,  
> 34 Anfiarao? perchè lasci la guerra? '  

whereat they all cried, 'Whither rushest thou, Amphiaräus? Why leavest thou the war?'

- *per ch'* ‘for which’ → “whereat”: the prepositional relative expression becomes a relative adverb.
- *ei gridavan tutti* ‘they cried all’ → “they all cried”: the quantifier moves before the verb.
- *Dove rui* ‘Where dost thou fall’ → “Whither rushest thou”: the question explicitly asks about direction, while falling or plunging is rendered as rushing.

> 35 e non restò di ruinare a valle  
> 36 fino a Minòs, che ciascheduno afferra.  

And he ceased not rushing headlong down to Minos, who lays hold on every sinner.

- *non restò di ruinare* ‘did not cease to fall headlong’ → “he ceased not rushing headlong”: the preposition and infinitive become an English participial complement; falling is rendered as rushing.
- *a valle* ‘valleyward’ → “down”: the directional expression is rendered by its general sense, without the reference to a valley.
- *fino a Minòs* ‘as far as Minos’ → “to Minos”: the endpoint is expressed without a separate equivalent of *fino*.
- *che ciascheduno afferra* ‘who everyone seizes’ → “who lays hold on every sinner”: the object moves after the verb; ‘seizes’ becomes a verb phrase with a preposition; and the indefinite pronoun becomes a noun phrase specifying “sinner”.

> 37 Mira che ha fatto petto delle spalle:  

Mark how he has made a breast of his shoulders:

- *Mira che* ‘Mark that’ → “Mark how”: the conjunction introducing what is to be observed becomes an expression of manner.
- *ha fatto petto delle spalle* ‘has made breast of the shoulders’ → “he has made a breast of his shoulders”: English supplies an indefinite article before “breast” and replaces the definite article before ‘shoulders’ with a possessive.

> 38 perchè volle veder troppo davante,  
> 39 di retro guarda e fa ritroso calle.  

because he wished to see too far before him, he now looks behind and goes backward.

- *troppo davante* ‘too much ahead’ → “too far before him”: the excess is explicitly expressed as distance, and “him” supplies a point of reference.
- *di retro guarda* ‘behind he looks’ → “he now looks behind”: the adverbial expression moves after the verb, and “now” is supplied.
- *e fa ritroso calle* ‘and makes a backward path’ → “and goes backward”: a verb with a noun object becomes a verb of motion with an adverb.

> 40 Vedi Tiresia, che mutò sembiante,  
> 41 quando di maschio femmina divenne,  
> 42 cangiandosi le membra tutte quante;  

Behold Tiresias who changed his aspect, when of male he was made woman, all his limbs transforming;

- *mutò sembiante* ‘changed aspect’ → “changed his aspect”: a possessive is supplied.
- *quando di maschio femmina divenne* ‘when from male woman he became’ → “when of male he was made woman”: the intransitive ‘became’ becomes the passive “was made”, and the predicate noun moves after the verb; ‘from’ is rendered by “of”.
- *cangiandosi le membra tutte quante* ‘changing themselves the limbs, all of them’ → “all his limbs transforming”: the translation takes the limbs as the subject of the participial construction and places them before the participle. The reflexive is not separately expressed, the definite article becomes a possessive, and the emphatic *tutte quante* is reduced to “all”.

> 43 e prima poi ribatter gli convenne  
> 44 li due serpenti avvolti con la verga,  
> 45 che riavesse le maschili penne.  

and afterwards he had again to strike the two involved serpents with his rod, before he could resume his manly plumes.

- *e … poi ribatter gli convenne* ‘and … afterwards to strike again was necessary for him’ → “and afterwards he had again to strike”: the impersonal expression of necessity becomes a personal construction with “had … to”. The repetition expressed by the prefix of *ribatter* becomes the separate adverb “again”.
- *prima … che* ‘earlier … than’ → “before”: the separated elements of the temporal construction become a single conjunction placed before the subordinate clause.
- *li due serpenti avvolti* ‘the two serpents entwined’ → “the two involved serpents”: the participial adjective moves before the noun; “involved” carries its older sense of entwined.
- *con la verga* ‘with the rod’ → “with his rod”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *riavesse* ‘he should have again’ → “he could resume”: the Italian subjunctive is rendered with an explicit modal of ability, while having again becomes resuming.
- *le maschili penne* ‘the male plumes’ → “his manly plumes”: the definite article becomes a possessive, and ‘male’ is rendered by “manly”.

> 46 Aronta è quel che al ventre gli s' atterga,  
> 47 che nei monti di Luni, dove ronca  
> 48 lo Carrarese che di sotto alberga,  

That is Aruns coming back before him, who in the mountains of Luni, where hoes the Carrarese that dwells beneath,

- *Aronta è quel* ‘Aruns is that one’ → “That is Aruns”: the identifying construction is reversed, with the demonstrative first and the name after the copula.
- *che al ventre gli s' atterga* ‘who sets his back to his belly’ → “coming back before him”: the finite relative clause becomes a participial phrase. The explicit back-to-belly relationship is rendered as movement and relative position, without naming the belly.
- *ronca* ‘clears ground’ → “hoes”: the translation specifies hoeing rather than the more general cutting or clearing of vegetation.

> 49 ebbe tra i bianchi marmi la spelonca  
> 50 per sua dimora; onde a guardar le stelle  
> 51 e il mar non gli era la veduta tronca.  

amongst [the] white marbles had the cave for his abode; from which he could observe the stars and the sea with unobstructed view.

- *ebbe tra i bianchi marmi la spelonca* ‘had amongst the white marbles the cave’ → “amongst [the] white marbles had the cave”: the phrase of location moves before the verb.
- *onde a guardar le stelle e il mar non gli era la veduta tronca* ‘whence, for looking at the stars and the sea, his view was not cut off’ → “from which he could observe the stars and the sea with unobstructed view.”: the negative statement about his view becomes a positive statement of his ability to observe. The infinitive becomes the complement of “could”; the noun that was the subject becomes part of a phrase introduced by “with”; and ‘not cut off’ becomes “unobstructed”.

## Lines 52–84 — Manto’s Travels and the Mincio

> 52 E quella che ricopre le mammelle,  
> 53 che tu non vedi, con le trecce sciolte,  
> 54 e ha di là ogni pilosa pelle,  

And she that covers her bosom, which thou seest not, with her flowing tresses, and has all her hair[y skin] on the other side,

- *le mammelle* ‘the breasts’ → “her bosom”: the plural becomes a collective singular, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *non vedi* ‘not seest’ → “seest not”: the negative follows the verb instead of preceding it.
- *con le trecce sciolte* ‘with the tresses loose’ → “with her flowing tresses”: the article becomes a possessive; ‘loose’ becomes “flowing”; and the adjective precedes its noun.
- *e ha di là ogni pilosa pelle* ‘and has on the other side every hairy skin’ → “and has all her hair[y skin] on the other side”: the location moves after the object, and distributive *ogni* becomes collective “all”, with “her” supplied.

> 55 Manto fu, che cercò per terre molte,  
> 56 poscia si pose là dove nacqu’ io:  

was Manto, who searched through many lands, then settled there where I was born:

- *Manto fu* ‘Manto was’ → “was Manto”: the identifying name moves after the copula.
- *cercò per terre molte* ‘roamed through lands many’ → “searched through many lands”: *cercò* is rendered with the sense of searching rather than travelling about, and the adjective moves before its noun.
- *poscia si pose là* ‘afterward placed herself there’ → “then settled there”: the reflexive expression becomes an intransitive verb.
- *dove nacqu’ io* ‘where was born I’ → “where I was born”: the expressed subject moves before the verb.

> 57 onde un poco mi piace che m’ ascolte.  

whence it pleases me a little to have thee listen to me.

- *un poco mi piace che m’ ascolte* ‘a little it pleases me that thou listen to me’ → “it pleases me a little to have thee listen to me”: the phrase of degree moves after the main predicate; the finite subjunctive clause becomes an infinitive construction with “have thee”.

> 58 Poscia che il padre suo di vita uscìo  
> 59 e venne serva la città di Baco,  
> 60 questa gran tempo per lo mondo gìo.  

After her father went out of life, and the city of Bacchus came to be enslaved, she for a long time roamed the world.

- *il padre suo* ‘the father her’ → “her father”: the postposed possessive moves before the noun.
- *di vita uscìo* ‘out of life went’ → “went out of life”: the verb moves before the phrase expressing departure.
- *e venne serva la città di Baco* ‘and became enslaved the city of Bacchus’ → “and the city of Bacchus came to be enslaved”: the subject moves before the predicate, and *venne serva* becomes the expanded construction “came to be enslaved”.
- *questa* ‘this woman’ → “she”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun.
- *gran tempo* ‘great time’ → “for a long time”: the duration is expressed with a preposition and an article, and ‘great’ becomes “long”.
- *per lo mondo gìo* ‘through the world went’ → “roamed the world”: the verb moves before its complement; simple going becomes roaming; and the prepositional phrase becomes a direct object.

> 61 Suso in Italia bella giace un laco  
> 62 al piè dell’ alpe, che serra Lamagna  
> 63 sopra Tiralli, ch’ ha nome Benaco.  

Up in beautiful Italy there lies a lake, at the foot of the Alps which shut in Germany above the Tyrol, [which] is called Benacus.

- *in Italia bella* ‘in Italy beautiful’ → “in beautiful Italy”: the adjective moves before the noun.
- *giace un laco* ‘lies a lake’ → “there lies a lake”: existential “there” is supplied.
- *al piè dell’ alpe, che serra Lamagna* ‘at the foot of the alp, which shuts in Germany’ → “at the foot of the Alps which shut in Germany”: the collective singular becomes a plural, with corresponding plural agreement in the verb.
- *sopra Tiralli* ‘above Tyrol’ → “above the Tyrol”: the definite article is supplied.
- *ch’ ha nome Benaco* ‘which has name Benacus’ → “[which] is called Benacus”: the active expression with ‘name’ becomes a passive naming construction.

> 64 Per mille fonti, credo, e più si bagna,  
> 65 tra Garda e Val Camonica, Apennino  
> 66 dell’ acqua che nel detto lago stagna.  

Through a thousand fountains, I believe, and more, the [A]pennine, between Garda and Val Camonica, is irrigated by the water which stagnates in that lake.

- *mille fonti* ‘thousand fountains’ → “a thousand fountains”: English supplies an article before “thousand”.
- *si bagna, tra Garda e Val Camonica, Apennino* ‘bathes itself, between Garda and Val Camonica, Apennine’ → “the [A]pennine, between Garda and Val Camonica, is irrigated”: the subject moves before the verb and receives an article; the reflexive construction becomes passive, and bathing becomes irrigation.
- *dell’ acqua* ‘with the water’ → “by the water”: the water is presented as the agent of irrigation rather than the medium of bathing.
- *nel detto lago stagna* ‘in the said lake stagnates’ → “stagnates in that lake”: the verb moves before the location, and ‘the said’ becomes the demonstrative “that”.

> 67 Loco è nel mezzo là, dove il Trentino  
> 68 pastore e quel di Brescia e il Veronese  
> 69 segnar potría, se fesse quel cammino.  

At the middle there is a place where the Trentine pastor, and he of Brescia, and the Veronese might bless, if they went that way.

- *Loco è nel mezzo là* ‘A place is in the middle there’ → “At the middle there is a place”: the location moves to the beginning, and the clause becomes an existential construction; *là* is not separately retained as a locative beyond “there”.
- *il Trentino pastore* ‘the Trentine shepherd’ → “the Trentine pastor”: the translation selects the ecclesiastical sense of *pastore* rather than its literal pastoral sense.
- *e quel di Brescia* ‘and that one of Brescia’ → “and he of Brescia”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun.
- *segnar potría* ‘sign could’ → “might bless”: the modal moves before the infinitive, and making a sign is interpreted as giving a blessing.
- *se fesse quel cammino* ‘if he made that journey’ → “if they went that way”: the singular becomes plural, treating the coordinated subjects collectively; making a journey becomes going a particular way.

> 70 Siede Peschiera, bello e forte arnese  
> 71 da fronteggiar Bresciani e Bergamaschi,  
> 72 ove la riva intorno più discese.  

Peschiera, a fortress beautiful and strong to front the Brescians and the Bergamese, sits where the shore around is lowest.

- *Siede Peschiera* ‘Sits Peschiera’ → “Peschiera” … “sits”: the subject moves before the verb, with its descriptive phrases now intervening.
- *bello e forte arnese* ‘beautiful and strong defensive work’ → “a fortress beautiful and strong”: *arnese* receives the specific military rendering “fortress”; an article is supplied, and the noun moves before its adjectives.
- *da fronteggiar Bresciani e Bergamaschi* ‘for facing Brescians and Bergamese’ → “to front the Brescians and the Bergamese”: the construction expressing fitness or purpose becomes an English infinitive, and definite articles are supplied before both peoples.
- *ove la riva intorno più discese* ‘where the shore around most descended’ → “where the shore around is lowest”: a past-tense verb describing descent becomes a present-tense statement of position, and the degree of descent becomes a superlative adjective.

> 73 Ivi convien che tutto quanto caschi  
> 74 ciò che in grembo a Benaco star non può,  
> 75 e fassi fiume giù per verdi paschi.  

There all that in the bosom of Benacus cannot stay, has to descend and make itself a river, down through green pastures.

- *Ivi convien che tutto quanto caschi ciò che in grembo a Benaco star non può* ‘There it is necessary that all whatsoever fall which in the bosom of Benacus stay cannot’ → “There all that in the bosom of Benacus cannot stay, has to descend”: the impersonal construction with a subjunctive becomes a personal construction with “has to”; the postponed subject moves before that predicate; the emphatic *tutto quanto … ciò che* is compressed into “all that”; ‘fall’ becomes “descend”; and the negative modal moves before “stay”.
- *e fassi fiume* ‘and makes itself river’ → “and make itself a river”: the independent finite verb becomes an infinitive coordinated under “has to”, and an article is supplied.
- *giù per verdi paschi* ‘down through green grazing grounds’ → “down through green pastures”: *paschi* is rendered by the usual English noun for grazing grounds.

> 76 Tosto che l’ acqua a correr mette co  
> 77 non più Benaco, ma Mincio si chiama  
> 78 fino a Governo, dove cade in Po.  

Soon as the water sets head to run, it is no longer named Benacus, but Mincio,—to Governo where it falls into the Po.

- *l’ acqua a correr mette co* ‘the water to run puts head’ → “the water sets head to run”: the infinitive moves after the verb and its object, and ‘puts’ becomes “sets”.
- *non più Benaco, ma Mincio si chiama* ‘no longer Benacus, but Mincio it calls itself’ → “it is no longer named Benacus, but Mincio”: the pronominal naming construction becomes passive, and the names move after the verb.
- *fino a Governo* ‘as far as Governo’ → “to Governo”: the explicit expression of extent is reduced to “to”.
- *in Po* ‘into Po’ → “into the Po”: English supplies the definite article.

> 79 Non molto ha corso, che trova una lama,  
> 80 nella qual si distende e la impaluda,  
> 81 e suol di state talora esser grama.  

Not far has it flowed, when it finds a level, on which it spreads and makes a marsh thereof, and is wont in summer to be at times unwholesome.

- *Non molto ha corso* ‘Not much has it run’ → “Not far has it flowed”: the indefinite measure becomes explicitly spatial, and running becomes flowing.
- *una lama* ‘a low-lying hollow’ → “a level”: the rendering emphasizes flatness rather than a depression in the ground.
- *nella qual* ‘in which’ → “on which”: containment becomes position upon a surface.
- *si distende* ‘spreads itself’ → “it spreads”: the reflexive becomes an intransitive construction.
- *e la impaluda* ‘and turns it into a marsh’ → “and makes a marsh thereof”: the causative verb becomes a phrase with the noun “marsh”, and the direct-object pronoun becomes “thereof”.
- *talora esser grama* ‘sometimes to be wretched’ → “to be at times unwholesome”: the adverb moves after the infinitive, and *grama* is interpreted specifically as unhealthy rather than generally afflicted or wretched.

> 82 Quindi passando la vergine cruda  
> 83 vide terra nel mezzo del pantano,  
> 84 senza cultura e d’ abitanti nuda.  

The cruel virgin, passing that way, saw land amidst the fen, uncultivated and naked of inhabitants.

- *Quindi passando* ‘By here passing’ → “passing that way”: the expression of route moves after the participle, and ‘here’ becomes the less immediate “that way”; the whole participial phrase also moves after the subject.
- *la vergine cruda* ‘the virgin cruel’ → “The cruel virgin”: the adjective moves before the noun.
- *nel mezzo del pantano* ‘in the middle of the fen’ → “amidst the fen”: the explicit noun ‘middle’ is absorbed into a single preposition.
- *senza cultura* ‘without cultivation’ → “uncultivated”: the prepositional phrase becomes an adjective.
- *e d’ abitanti nuda* ‘and of inhabitants naked’ → “and naked of inhabitants”: the adjective moves before its complement.

## Lines 85–117 — Mantua’s Origins and Noted Diviners

> 85 Lì, per fuggire ogni consorzio umano,  
> 86 ristette co’ suoi servi a far sue arti,  
> 87 e visse, e vi lasciò suo corpo vano.  

There, to shun all human intercourse, she halted with her ministers to do her arts; and there she lived and left her body vacant.

- *consorzio umano* ‘intercourse human’ → “human intercourse”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *servi* ‘servants’ → “ministers”: the translation uses a term suggesting attendants who assist in her arts.
- *e visse, e vi lasciò* ‘and she lived, and there left’ → “and there she lived and left”: the locative *vi* is moved before “lived,” making “there” apply explicitly to both verbs.

> 88 Gli uomini poi, che intorno erano sparti,  
> 89 s’ accolsero a quel loco, ch’ era forte  
> 90 per lo pantan che avea da tutte parti.  

Afterwards the men, that were scattered round, gathered together on that spot [which] was strong by reason of the marsh it had on every side.

- *Gli uomini poi* ‘the men afterwards’ → “Afterwards the men”: the temporal adverb moves before the subject.
- *intorno erano sparti* ‘round were scattered’ → “were scattered round”: the adverb follows the verb rather than preceding it.
- *s’accolsero* ‘gathered themselves’ → “gathered together”: the pronominal construction becomes an intransitive verb with “together.”
- *a quel loco* ‘at that place’ → “on that spot”: “on” replaces ‘at,’ and “spot” renders *loco*.
- *che avea* ‘that it had’ → “it had”: the object relative pronoun is omitted.
- *da tutte parti* ‘from all sides’ → “on every side”: the spatial preposition changes, and plural ‘all sides’ becomes singular “every side.”

> 91 Fer la città sopra quell’ ossa morte;  
> 92 e per colei, che il loco prima elesse,  
> 93 Mantova l’appellar senz’ altra sorte.  

They built the city over those dead bones; and for her who first chose the place, they called it Mantua without other augury.

- *Fer* ‘made’ → “They built”: the general verb is rendered by the more specific “built.”
- *quell’ ossa morte* ‘those bones dead’ → “those dead bones”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *il loco prima elesse* ‘the place first chose’ → “first chose the place”: the object moves after the verb.
- *Mantova l’appellar* ‘Mantua they called it’ → “they called it Mantua”: the name moves after the verb and its object.
- *sorte* ‘lot’ → “augury”: the translation interprets the word as a means of divination rather than rendering its literal reference to a lot.

> 94 Già fur le genti sue dentro più spesse,  
> 95 prima che la mattìa di Casalodi  
> 96 da Pinamonte inganno ricevesse.  

Once the inhabitants were denser in it, ere the folly of Casalodi was cheated by Pinamonte.

- *Già fur le genti sue* ‘once were its peoples’ → “Once the inhabitants were”: the subject moves before the verb; ‘its peoples’ becomes “the inhabitants,” without an expressed possessive.
- *dentro più spesse* ‘inside more thick’ → “denser in it”: the predicate adjective precedes the locative expression, and “in it” makes the reference implicit in *dentro* explicit.
- *mattìa* ‘madness’ → “folly”: the translation uses a less intense term.
- *da Pinamonte inganno ricevesse* ‘from Pinamonte should receive deceit’ → “was cheated by Pinamonte”: receiving deceit becomes a passive construction, the agent moves after the verb, and the Italian subjunctive is rendered by an English indicative.

> 97 Però t’ assenno che, se tu mai odi  
> 98 originar la mia terra altrimenti,  
> 99 la verità nulla menzogna frodi.”  

Therefore I charge thee, if thou ever hearest other origin given to my city, let no falsehood defraud the truth."

- *t’assenno che* ‘I instruct thee that’ → “I charge thee”: instruction becomes an injunction, the object follows the verb, and the conjunction *che* is omitted.
- *originar la mia terra altrimenti* ‘my land originate otherwise’ → “other origin given to my city”: the infinitive becomes the noun “origin,” the adverb becomes the adjective “other,” and “given to” is supplied to express an account assigned to the city; *terra* is interpreted as ‘city.’
- *la verità nulla menzogna frodi* ‘the truth may no falsehood defraud’ → “let no falsehood defraud the truth”: the fronted object moves after the verb, and “let” expresses the force of the subjunctive.

> 100 Ed io: “Maestro, i tuoi ragionamenti  
> 101 mi son sì certi e prendon sì mia fede,  
> 102 che gli altri mi sarian carboni spenti.  

And I: "Master, thy words are to me so certain, and so take hold of my belief, that all others would be to me extinguished coals.

- *ragionamenti* ‘reasonings’ → “words”: the translation substitutes a broader term for reasoned discourse.
- *mi son sì certi* ‘to me are so certain’ → “are to me so certain”: the verb precedes the indirect object.
- *prendon sì mia fede* ‘seize so my faith’ → “so take hold of my belief”: the adverb moves before the verb, “take hold of” replaces the simple transitive verb, and “belief” renders *fede*.
- *gli altri* ‘the others’ → “all others”: “all” is supplied in place of the definite article.
- *mi sarian* ‘to me would be’ → “would be to me”: the indirect object follows the verb.
- *carboni spenti* ‘coals extinguished’ → “extinguished coals”: the participial adjective precedes the noun.

> 103 Ma dimmi della gente che procede,  
> 104 se tu ne vedi alcun degno di nota:  
> 105 chè solo a ciò la mia mente rifiede.”  

But tell me of the people that are passing, if thou seest any of them worthy of note: for to that alone my mind recurs."

- *gente che procede* ‘people that proceeds’ → “people that are passing”: the Italian singular collective takes plural agreement in English, and the simple present becomes a progressive construction.
- *ne vedi alcun* ‘of them seest any’ → “seest any of them”: the partitive pronoun moves from before the verb to after “any.”
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *solo a ciò* ‘only to that’ → “to that alone”: the focusing word follows the prepositional phrase rather than preceding it.
- *rifiede* ‘strikes again’ → “recurs”: the translation renders the figurative sense of returning to something rather than retaining the verb’s image.

> 106 Allor mi disse: “Quel, che dalla gota  
> 107 porge la barba in su le spalle brune,  

Then he said to me: "That one, who from the cheek stretches forth his beard upon his dusky shoulders,

- *mi disse* ‘to me he said’ → “he said to me”: the indirect object follows the verb.
- *Quel* ‘that’ → “That one”: “one” is supplied to support the demonstrative used as a pronoun.
- *porge la barba* ‘extends the beard’ → “stretches forth his beard”: a phrasal expression renders the simple verb, and a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *in su le spalle brune* ‘upon the shoulders dusky’ → “upon his dusky shoulders”: a possessive replaces the definite article, and the adjective precedes the noun.

> 108 fu, quando Grecia fu di maschi vota  
> 109 sì che appena rimaser per le cune,  
> 110 augure; e diede il punto con Calcanta  
> 111 in Aulide a tagliar la prima fune.  

was an augur, when Greece was so empty of males, that hardly they remained even in the cradles; and in Aulis he, with Calchas, gave the time for cutting the first cable.

- *fu … augure* ‘was … augur’ → “was an augur”: the predicate noun moves immediately after the verb, before the intervening temporal clause, and English supplies an indefinite article.
- *quando Grecia fu di maschi vota sì che* ‘when Greece was of males empty, so that’ → “when Greece was so empty of males, that”: the complement follows “empty,” and *sì* moves into the adjective phrase as “so empty.”
- *appena rimaser per le cune* ‘hardly they remained throughout the cradles’ → “hardly they remained even in the cradles”: “even” is supplied, and the distributive spatial expression with *per* becomes “in.”
- *diede il punto con Calcanta in Aulide* ‘gave the moment with Calchas in Aulis’ → “in Aulis he, with Calchas, gave the time”: both prepositional phrases move before the verb, and “time” renders *punto* as the appointed moment.
- *a tagliar la prima fune* ‘to cut the first rope’ → “for cutting the first cable”: the infinitive becomes a preposition with a gerund, and “cable” gives *fune* a specifically nautical rendering.

> 112 Euripilo ebbe nome, e così il canta  
> 113 l’alta mia Tragedía in alcun loco:  
> 114 ben lo sai tu, che la sai tutta quanta.  

Eurypylus his name; and my high Tragedy thus sings him in some place: well knowest it thou, who knowest the whole.

- *Euripilo ebbe nome* ‘Eurypylus he had as name’ → “Eurypylus his name”: the verb is omitted, and a possessive turns the naming construction into a verbless phrase.
- *e così il canta l’alta mia Tragedía* ‘and thus him sings the high my Tragedy’ → “and my high Tragedy thus sings him”: the subject moves before the verb, the object pronoun follows it, and the possessive precedes “high.”
- *la sai tutta quanta* ‘knowest it all entire’ → “knowest the whole”: the object pronoun and the emphatic expression of entirety become the single noun phrase “the whole.”

> 115 Quell’ altro, che ne’ fianchi è così poco,  
> 116 Michele Scotto fu, che veramente  
> 117 delle magiche frode seppe il gioco.  

That other who is so small about the flanks was Michael Scott; and of a truth he knew the play of magic frauds.

- *ne’ fianchi è così poco* ‘about the flanks is so little’ → “is so small about the flanks”: the prepositional phrase moves after the predicate, and “small” renders *poco* as a description of size.
- *Michele Scotto fu* ‘Michael Scott was’ → “was Michael Scott”: the identifying name follows the verb rather than preceding it.
- *che veramente* ‘who truly’ → “and of a truth he”: the relative clause becomes a coordinated clause with an explicit personal pronoun, and the adverb becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *delle magiche frode seppe il gioco* ‘of magic frauds knew the play’ → “knew the play of magic frauds”: the fronted phrase dependent on *gioco* moves after its noun.

## Lines 118–130 — The Diviners and the Setting Moon

> 118 Vedi Guido Bonatti, vedi Asdente,  
> 119 che avere inteso al cuoio ed allo spago  
> 120 ora vorrebbe, ma tardi si pente.  

See Guido Bonatti; see Asdente, who now would wish he had attended to his leather and his cord, but too late repents.

- *avere inteso al cuoio ed allo spago ora vorrebbe* ‘to have attended to the leather and to the cord now would wish’ → “now would wish he had attended to his leather and his cord”: “now would wish” moves before the expression of what he wishes; the perfect infinitive becomes a finite past-perfect clause; the definite articles become possessives, and the second preposition is omitted.
- *tardi* ‘late’ → “too late”: “too” is supplied.

> 121 Vedi le triste che lasciaron l’ ago,  
> 122 la spola e il fuso, e fecersi indovine;  
> 123 fecer malìe con erbe e con imago.  

See the wretched *women* who left the needle, the shuttle, and the spindle, and made themselves divineresses; they wrought witchcraft with herbs and images.

- *le triste* ‘the wretched [feminine plural]’ → “the wretched *women*”: the substantival adjective becomes an adjective modifying the supplied noun “women”.
- *fecer malìe* ‘made spells’ → “they wrought witchcraft”: “wrought” replaces ‘made’, and the plural count noun becomes the mass noun “witchcraft”.
- *con erbe e con imago* ‘with herbs and with an image’ → “with herbs and images”: the second preposition is omitted, and singular *imago* becomes plural “images”.

> 124 Ma vienne omai, chè già tiene il confine  
> 125 d’ ambedue gli emisperi e tocca l’ onda  
> 126 sotto Sibilia Caino e le spine;  

But now come! for Cain and his thorns already holds the confine of both hemispheres, and under Seville touches the wave;

- *vienne omai* ‘come away now’ → “now come”: the adverb moves before the verb, and the force of the attached *ne*, ‘away’, is left implicit.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the causal subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *d’ambedue gli emisperi* ‘of both the hemispheres’ → “of both hemispheres”: the definite article is omitted.
- *e tocca l’onda sotto Sibilia* ‘and touches the wave under Seville’ → “and under Seville touches the wave”: the place phrase moves before the verb.
- *Caino e le spine* ‘Cain and the thorns’ → “Cain and his thorns”: the definite article becomes a possessive, and the subject moves from after both predicates to before them.

> 127 e già iernotte fu la luna tonda;  
> 128 ben ten dee ricordar, chè non ti nocque  
> 129 alcuna volta per la selva fonda.”  

and already yesternight the Moon was round; well must thou remember: for she did not hurt thee any time in the deep wood."

- *fu la luna tonda* ‘was the moon round’ → “the Moon was round”: the subject moves before the verb.
- *ben ten dee ricordar* ‘well must remembrance of it come to thee’ → “well must thou remember”: the impersonal construction becomes a personal one, with the person experiencing remembrance made the subject; the reference conveyed by *ne*, ‘of it’, is not separately expressed.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the causal subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *per la selva fonda* ‘through the wood deep’ → “in the deep wood”: the preposition of movement or extent becomes “in”, and the adjective moves before the noun.

> 130 Sì mi parlava, ed andavamo introcque.  

Thus he spake to me, and we went on meanwhile.

- *mi parlava* ‘to me he was speaking’ → “he spake to me”: the indirect-object pronoun becomes a prepositional phrase following the verb, and the imperfect is rendered by a simple past.
- *andavamo* ‘we were going’ → “we went on”: the imperfect becomes a simple past, and “on” is supplied to express continuation.

## Summary — Close Echoes and Grammatical Reshaping

Carlyle’s prose remains close to the Italian in the sequence of its statements, its imagery, and much of its vocabulary, but repeatedly rearranges words to suit English syntax. Subjects and objects move from their Italian positions into more usual English order, while adjectives generally precede their nouns: *carboni spenti* becomes “extinguished coals”. Longer phrases also shift position, as *per lo vallon tondo* becomes “through the circular valley” before “I saw”. Yet the translation retains some conspicuously Italian turns, including “where hoes the Carrarese” and “well knowest it thou”, rather than consistently smoothing them into ordinary English prose.

Changes of construction are equally recurrent. Impersonal expressions of necessity become personal constructions with “had to” or “has to”; reflexive or pronominal verbs become passives, as *si bagnava* becomes “was bathed”, or intransitives, as *si distende* becomes “it spreads”. Infinitives and finite clauses are sometimes exchanged for participial or infinitival constructions. Noun-based expressions may become simpler verbal ones: *fa ritroso calle*, ‘makes a backward path’, becomes “goes backward”. More extensively, the statement that Aruns’s view was not obstructed is recast as an assertion that “he could observe” with “unobstructed view”. Tense also occasionally changes, notably where Italian simple pasts become English present perfects.

Vocabulary ranges from close correspondence to interpretation and softening. *Prender frutto*, ‘take fruit’, becomes “take profit”; *sorte*, ‘lot’, becomes “augury”; and *grama* is interpreted as “unwholesome”. Anatomical terms are softened in “hinder parts” and “bosom”. Elsewhere, however, the translation preserves striking expressions such as “manly plumes” and “the play of magic frauds”. Additions make implicit relations explicit: “to me” supplements “discovered”, “sinner” specifies *ciasccheduno*, and “women” supplies the noun understood in *le triste*. Conversely, *già* is sometimes omitted, *fino a* is reduced to “to”, and object or pronominal references are occasionally left unexpressed.

Underlying these individual departures are routine differences between the languages. English supplies subject pronouns where Italian gives the subject through the verb ending, and omits the Italian definite article before possessives, as *i tuoi ragionamenti* becomes “thy words”. Definite articles with parts of the body also frequently become possessives. These regular adjustments, together with shifts of word order and construction, make the prose grammatically English without removing its sustained closeness to the Italian’s phrasing and rhetorical shape.
