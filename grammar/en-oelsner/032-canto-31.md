# Inferno 31

## Lines 1–18 — A Healing Tongue and a Thunderous Horn

> 1 Una medesma lingua pria mi morse,  
> 2 sì che mi tinse l' una e l' altra guancia,  
> 3 e poi la medicina mi riporse.  

One and the same tongue first wounded me so that it tinged _with_ blushes both my cheeks, and then held forth the medicine to me.

- *Una medesma* ‘one same’ → “One and the same”: English expands the expression of identity.
- *mi morse* ‘me bit’ → “wounded me”: biting becomes wounding, and the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *mi tinse l' una e l' altra guancia* ‘dyed for me the one and the other cheek’ → “it tinged _with_ blushes both my cheeks”: “blushes” supplies what caused the colouring; the distributive singular becomes “both” with a plural noun, and the personal dative becomes possessive “my”.
- *la medicina mi riporse* ‘the medicine to me offered’ → “held forth the medicine to me”: offering is expressed as holding forth; the verb moves before its object, and the recipient follows it.

> 4 Così od' io che soleva la lancia  
> 5 d' Achille e del suo padre esser cagione  
> 6 prima di trista e poi di buona mancia.  

Thus I have heard that the lance of Achilles, and of his father, used to be occasion first of sad and then of healing gift.

- *Così od' io* ‘Thus hear I’ → “Thus I have heard”: the present becomes a present perfect, and the subject moves before the verb.
- *soleva la lancia d' Achille e del suo padre esser cagione* ‘was accustomed the lance of Achilles and of his father to be cause’ → “the lance of Achilles, and of his father, used to be occasion”: the subject moves before the habitual verb; “occasion” renders the causal sense of *cagione*.
- *buona* ‘good’ → “healing”: the translation specifies the kind of benefit rather than retaining the general adjective.

> 7 Noi demmo il dosso al misero vallone,  
> 8 su per la ripa che il cinge d' intorno  
> 9 attraversando senza alcun sermone.  

We turned our back to the wretched valley, up by the bank that girds it round, crossing without any speech.

- *demmo il dosso* ‘gave the back’ → “turned our back”: the Italian idiom becomes the corresponding English idiom, with a possessive replacing the definite article.
- *vallone* ‘ravine’ → “valley”: the translation uses a more general term for the hollow.
- *su per* ‘up along’ → “up by”: the preposition marking the route becomes one expressing position beside it.
- *il cinge* ‘it girds’ → “girds it”: the object pronoun moves after the verb.

> 10 Quivi era men che notte e men che giorno,  
> 11 sì che il viso m' andava innanzi poco;  

Here was less than night and less than day, so that my sight went little _way_ before me;

- *Quivi* ‘There’ → “Here”: the spatial reference shifts from ‘there’ to the narrator’s immediate position.
- *il viso m' andava innanzi poco* ‘the sight went ahead little for me’ → “my sight went little _way_ before me”: the personal dative is represented through “my” and “before me”; the measure of distance becomes “little _way_”, with a noun supplied, and moves before the expression of relative position.

> 12 ma io senti' sonare un alto corno,  
> 13 tanto ch' avrebbe ogni tuon fatto fioco,  

but I heard a high horn sound so _loudly_, that it would have made any thunder weak;

- *senti' sonare un alto corno* ‘heard sound a high horn’ → “heard a high horn sound”: the noun phrase moves before the infinitive whose subject it is.
- *tanto* ‘so much’ → “so _loudly_”: the general expression of degree is made specifically an expression of loudness.
- *avrebbe ogni tuon fatto fioco* ‘would have every thunder made faint’ → “it would have made any thunder weak”: the participle moves before the object; ‘every’ becomes “any” in the hypothetical comparison, and the description of muted sound becomes the more general “weak”.

> 14 che, contra sè la sua via seguitando,  
> 15 dirizzò gli occhi miei tutti ad un loco:  

which directed my eyes, [that followed its course against itself], all to one place:

- *contra sè la sua via seguitando* ‘against itself its way following’ → “[that followed its course against itself]”: the participial construction becomes a finite relative clause attached to “my eyes”, explicitly making them the followers. The clause moves after “my eyes”, while ‘against itself’ moves to its end; ‘way’ becomes “course”.
- *gli occhi miei* ‘the eyes my’ → “my eyes”: the postposed possessive moves before the noun.

> 16 dopo la dolorosa rotta, quando  
> 17 Carlo Magno perdè la santa gesta,  
> 18 non sonò sì terribilmente Orlando.  

after the dolorous rout, when Charlemain had lost the holy emprise, [Roland] did not sound _with his_ so terribly.

- *perdè* ‘lost’ → “had lost”: the simple past becomes a past perfect.
- *la santa gesta* ‘the holy host’ → “the holy emprise”: the translation takes *gesta* as an enterprise or undertaking rather than as a collective term for a company.
- *non sonò sì terribilmente Orlando* ‘did not sound so terribly Roland’ → “[Roland] did not sound _with his_ so terribly”: the subject moves from the end of the clause to before the verb, and “_with his_” supplies an instrumental phrase with the instrument itself understood.

## Lines 19–51 — Towers Revealed as Giants

> 19 Poco portai in là volta la testa,  
> 20 che mi parve veder molte alte torri ;  

Short while had I kept [my head turned] in that direction, when I seemed to see many lofty towers;

- *Poco* ‘a little’ → “Short while”: the temporal expression is expanded with “while”.
- *portai ... volta la testa* ‘I held ... turned the head’ → “had I kept [my head turned]”: the simple past becomes past perfect; “kept” expresses continued posture; the possessive replaces the definite article; and “turned” follows “head”.
- *in là* ‘that way’ → “in that direction”: the directional expression is expanded.
- *che* ‘that’ → “when”: the conjunction is rendered temporally.
- *mi parve veder* ‘it seemed to me to see’ → “I seemed to see”: the Italian impersonal construction becomes a personal one.

> 21 ond'io : “ Maestro, di', che terra è questa ?”  

whereat I: "Master! say, what town is this?"

- *ond'io* ‘whence I’ → “whereat I”: the connective is rendered as a response to the preceding perception.
- *terra* ‘land’ → “town”: the translation selects the contextual sense of an inhabited settlement.

> 22 Ed egli a me : “ Però che tu trascorri  
> 23 per le tenebre troppo dalla lungi,  
> 24 avvien che poi nel 'maginare aborri.  

And he to me: "Because thou traversest the darkness too far off, it follows that thou errest in thy imagining.

- *tu trascorri per le tenebre* ‘thou runnest through the darkness’ → “thou traversest the darkness”: the verb expresses traversal rather than running, and the prepositional phrase becomes its direct object.
- *avvien che poi* ‘it happens that then’ → “it follows that”: occurrence becomes logical consequence, absorbing the force of *poi* rather than rendering it separately.
- *nel 'maginare aborri* ‘in imagining thou strayest’ → “thou errest in thy imagining”: the verb precedes the phrase modifying it; straying becomes explicitly intellectual error; and “thy” is supplied before “imagining”.

> 25 Tu vedrai ben, se tu là ti congiungi,  
> 26 quanto il senso s'inganna di lontano :  
> 27 però alquanto più te stesso pungi.”  

Thou shalt see right well, [if] thou arrivest there, how much the sense at distance is deceived: therefore spur thee somewhat more."

- *ben* ‘well’ → “right well”: “right” supplies an explicit intensifier.
- *se tu là ti congiungi* ‘if thou there joinest thyself’ → “[if] thou arrivest there”: the reflexive construction becomes an intransitive verb of arrival, and “there” follows the verb.
- *il senso s'inganna di lontano* ‘the sense deceives itself from afar’ → “the sense at distance is deceived”: the reflexive becomes passive, and the expression of distance moves before the verb.
- *alquanto più te stesso pungi* ‘somewhat more thyself prick’ → “spur thee somewhat more”: “spur” replaces ‘prick’; the emphatic reflexive becomes “thee”; and the adverbial phrase moves after the verb and its object.

> 28 Poi caramente mi prese per mano  

Then lovingly he took me by the hand,

- *mi prese per mano* ‘me he took by hand’ → “he took me by the hand”: the object pronoun follows the verb, and English supplies the article before “hand”.

> 29 e disse : “ Pria che noi siam più avanti,  
> 30 acciocchè il fatto men ti paia strano,  

and said: "Ere we go farther, that the reality may seem less strange to thee,

- *Pria che noi siam più avanti* ‘before we be farther ahead’ → “Ere we go farther”: being farther ahead becomes going farther; the Italian subjunctive is rendered by an English indicative.
- *il fatto* ‘the fact’ → “the reality”: the translation substitutes the actual state of things for the more literal ‘fact’.
- *men ti paia strano* ‘less to thee may seem strange’ → “may seem less strange to thee”: “less” is placed immediately before the adjective, and the experiencer follows it.

> 31 sappi che non son torri, ma giganti,  
> 32 e son nel pozzo intorno dalla ripa  
> 33 dall'umbilico in giuso tutti e quanti.”  

know, they are not towers, but Giants; and are in the well, around its bank, from the navel downwards all of them."

- *sappi che* ‘know that’ → “know,”: the subordinating conjunction is omitted.
- *non son* ‘not are’ → “they are not”: the negative follows the finite verb rather than preceding it.
- *intorno dalla ripa* ‘around, along the bank’ → “around its bank”: the spatial construction is compressed, and a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *tutti e quanti* ‘all and as many’ → “all of them”: the emphatic Italian expression is replaced by a partitive construction with “of them”.

> 34 Come, quando la nebbia si dissipa,  
> 35 lo sguardo a poco a poco raffigura  
> 36 ciò che cela il vapor che l'aere stipa:  

As when a mist is vanishing, the eye by little and little reshapes that which the air-crowding vapour hides;

- *la nebbia* ‘the mist’ → “a mist”: the definite article becomes indefinite.
- *si dissipa* ‘disperses itself’ → “is vanishing”: the reflexive construction becomes intransitive, and the simple present becomes progressive.
- *lo sguardo* ‘the gaze’ → “the eye”: the act of looking is replaced by the organ of sight.
- *raffigura* ‘recognizes’ → “reshapes”: the translation takes the verb as forming the shape anew, rather than simply recognizing it.
- *cela il vapor che l'aere stipa* ‘hides the vapour that packs the air’ → “the air-crowding vapour hides”: the subject, *il vapor*, moves before “hides”; the relative clause becomes a compound adjective before “vapour”; and packing becomes crowding.

> 37 così forando l'aura grossa e scura,  
> 38 più e più appressando in ver la sponda,  
> 39 fuggìemi errore, e cresce' mi paura.  

so whilst piercing through that gross and darksome air, more and more approaching towards the brink, error [fled] from me, and [my] fear [increased].

- *forando* ‘piercing’ → “whilst piercing through”: the temporal conjunction and “through” are supplied.
- *l'aura grossa e scura* ‘the air thick and dark’ → “that gross and darksome air”: the definite article becomes demonstrative, the adjectives precede the noun, and “gross” and “darksome” render thickness and darkness.
- *fuggìemi errore* ‘fled from me error’ → “error [fled] from me”: the subject moves before the verb, and the attached pronoun is expressed by a following prepositional phrase.
- *cresce' mi paura* ‘grew to me fear’ → “[my] fear [increased]”: the dative pronoun becomes a possessive, the subject moves before the verb, and “increased” replaces ‘grew’.

> 40 Però che, come in su la cerchia tonda  
> 41 Montereggion di torri si corona :  

For as on its round wall Montereggione crowns itself with towers:

- *Però che* ‘because’ → “For”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *in su la cerchia tonda* ‘upon the circuit round’ → “on its round wall”: the circuit is rendered concretely as a wall, the article becomes possessive, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *di torri si corona* ‘with towers itself crowns’ → “crowns itself with towers”: the reflexive pronoun follows the verb, and the phrase specifying the adornment moves to the end.

> 42 così la proda che il pozzo circonda  
> 43 torreggiavan di mezza la persona  
> 44 gli orribili giganti, cui minaccia  
> 45 Giove del cielo ancora, quando tuona.  

so with half their bodies, the horrible giants, whom Jove from heaven still threatens when he thunders, turreted the [bank] which compasses the pit.

- *la proda che il pozzo circonda* ‘the bank which surrounds the well’ → “the [bank] which compasses the pit”: the main-clause object moves from before the verb to after it; within the relative clause, the object likewise follows its verb rather than preceding it; and “pit” replaces ‘well’.
- *torreggiavan ... gli orribili giganti* ‘towered ... the horrible giants’ → “the horrible giants” … “turreted”: the subject moves before the verb, and towering is rendered as furnishing something with turrets.
- *di mezza la persona* ‘by half the person’ → “with half their bodies”: the construction becomes a phrase with “with”; ‘person’ is rendered as “bodies”; and the distributive singular becomes plural with an explicit possessive. The phrase also moves before the subject and verb.
- *cui minaccia Giove del cielo ancora* ‘whom threatens Jove from heaven still’ → “whom Jove from heaven still threatens”: the subject, the phrase of origin, and the adverb all precede “threatens”, whereas they follow *minaccia*.

> 46 Ed io scorgeva già d'alcun la faccia,  
> 47 le spalle e il petto, e del ventre gran parte,  
> 48 e per le coste giù ambo le braccia.  

And already I discerned the face of one, the shoulders and the breast, and great part of the belly, and down along his sides both arms.

- *io scorgeva già* ‘I was discerning already’ → “already I discerned”: the adverb moves before the subject, and the Italian imperfect is rendered by a simple English past.
- *d'alcun la faccia* ‘of one the face’ → “the face of one”: the dependent phrase follows its noun rather than preceding it.
- *del ventre gran parte* ‘of the belly great part’ → “great part of the belly”: the dependent phrase again moves after its governing noun.
- *per le coste giù* ‘along the sides down’ → “down along his sides”: “down” moves to the beginning of the phrase, and a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *ambo le braccia* ‘both the arms’ → “both arms”: the definite article is omitted.

> 49 Natura certo, quando lasciò l'arte  
> 50 di sì fatti animali, assai fe' bene,  
> 51 per torre tali esecutori a Marte ;  

Nature certainly, when she left off the art of making animals like these, did very well, in taking away such executioners from Mars;

- *lasciò* ‘left’ → “left off”: the English phrasal verb explicitly expresses discontinuance.
- *l'arte di* ‘the art of’ → “the art of making”: “making” is supplied to state the activity implicit in the Italian.
- *sì fatti animali* ‘so fashioned animals’ → “animals like these”: the description becomes a following phrase of comparison with an explicit demonstrative.
- *assai fe' bene* ‘much did well’ → “did very well”: the intensifier moves next to “well” and is expressed as “very”.
- *per torre* ‘to take away’ → “in taking away”: the infinitive introduced by *per* becomes a gerund construction; rather than explicitly stating a purpose, the English identifies what constituted doing well.
- *tali esecutori* ‘such executors’ → “such executioners”: the broader term for agents who carry something out becomes the more specific “executioners”.
- *a Marte* ‘to Mars’ → “from Mars”: the Italian dative marking the party deprived becomes an English phrase of removal.

## Lines 52–84 — Nimrod’s Speech and the Next Giant

> 52 e s'ella d'elefanti e di balene  
> 53 non si pente, chi guarda sottilmente  
> 54 più giusta e più discreta la ne tiene:  

and if she repents her not of Elephants and Whales, whoso subtly looks, therein regards her as more just and prudent:

- *d'elefanti e di balene non si pente* ‘of elephants and of whales repents herself not’ → “repents her not of Elephants and Whales”: the prepositional phrase moves after the verb, the second ‘of’ is omitted, and the reflexive is expressed by the archaic “her”.
- *chi guarda sottilmente* ‘whoever looks subtly’ → “whoso subtly looks”: the adverb moves before the verb.
- *più giusta e più discreta la ne tiene* ‘more just and more discerning her on that account holds’ → “therein regards her as more just and prudent”: the verb and object precede their complement; “as” is supplied; *ne* becomes the introductory “therein”; the second *più* is not separately rendered; and *discreta* is expressed as “prudent”.

> 55 chè dove l'argomento della mente  
> 56 s'aggiunge al mal volere ed alla possa,  
> 57 nessun riparo vi può far la gente.  

for where [the instrument] of [the] mind is joined to evil will and potency, men can make no defence against it.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *s'aggiunge al mal volere ed alla possa* ‘adds itself to the evil will and to the power’ → “is joined to evil will and potency”: the reflexive construction becomes passive; the definite articles and the repeated preposition are omitted.
- *nessun riparo vi può far la gente* ‘no defence against it can make the people’ → “men can make no defence against it”: the subject moves before the verb and the object after it; collective singular *la gente* becomes plural “men”.

> 58 La faccia sua mi parea lunga e grossa  
> 59 come la pina di San Pietro a Roma;  
> 60 ed a sua proporzione eran l'altr'ossa;  

His face seemed to me as long and large as the pine of St Peter’s at Rome, and his other bones were in proportion to it;

- *La faccia sua* ‘the face his’ → “His face”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *mi parea* ‘to me seemed’ → “seemed to me”: the experiencer follows the verb.
- *lunga e grossa come* ‘long and large like’ → “as long and large as”: English supplies the first “as” to form the paired comparison.
- *la pina* ‘the pine cone’ → “the pine”: the specific name of the cone is shortened to “pine”.
- *San Pietro* ‘Saint Peter’ → “St Peter’s”: the English possessive makes the reference to a building explicit.
- *ed a sua proporzione eran l'altr'ossa* ‘and in its proportion were the other bones’ → “and his other bones were in proportion to it”: the subject moves before the verb, the proportional phrase follows it, *sua* becomes “to it”, and “his” is supplied before “other bones”.

> 61 sì che la ripa, ch'era perizoma  
> 62 dal mezzo in giù, ne mostrava ben tanto  
> 63 di sopra, chè di giungere alla chioma  
> 64 tre Frison s'averian dato mal vanto:  

so that the bank, which was an apron from his middle downwards, shewed us certainly so much of him above, that three Friezelanders had vainly boasted to have reached his hair:

- *perizoma* ‘loincloth’ → “an apron”: a different kind of covering is named, and an indefinite article is supplied.
- *dal mezzo in giù* ‘from the middle downwards’ → “from his middle downwards”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *ne mostrava* ‘to us showed’ → “shewed us”: the indirect object follows the verb.
- *tanto di sopra* ‘so much above’ → “so much of him above”: “of him” is supplied.
- *di giungere alla chioma* ‘of reaching the hair’ → “to have reached his hair”: the prepositional infinitive becomes a perfect infinitive, the definite article becomes a possessive, and the phrase moves from before the subject to after “boasted”.
- *s'averian dato mal vanto* ‘would have given themselves a poor boast’ → “had vainly boasted”: the reflexive verb-and-noun expression becomes a verb modified by an adverb; the conditional perfect is represented by a counterfactual “had” construction.

> 65 però ch'io ne vedea trenta gran palmi  
> 66 dal loco in giù, dov'uom s'affibbia il manto.  

for downwards from the place where a man buckles on his mantle, I saw thirty large spans of him.

- *però ch'* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating expression becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *io ne vedea trenta gran palmi* ‘I of him saw thirty large spans’ → “I saw thirty large spans of him”: the partitive pronoun becomes “of him” after the measurement.
- *dal loco in giù* ‘from the place downwards’ → “downwards from the place”: “downwards” moves before the prepositional phrase, and the whole locative expression precedes the main clause.
- *dov'uom* ‘where one’ → “where a man”: the indefinite personal subject is expressed as a noun phrase.
- *s'affibbia il manto* ‘buckles to himself the mantle’ → “buckles on his mantle”: the reflexive dative becomes a possessive, and “on” is supplied.

> 67 “Rafel mai amech zabi almi,”  
> 68 cominciò a gridar la fiera bocca,  
> 69 cui non si convenian più dolci salmi.  

“Rafel mai amech zabi almi,” began to shout the savage mouth, for which no sweeter psalmody [was] fit.

- *cui non si convenian più dolci salmi* ‘to which sweeter psalms were not fitting’ → “for which no sweeter psalmody [was] fit”: the negative modifies the noun phrase rather than the verb; plural ‘psalms’ becomes singular collective “psalmody”; and the subject precedes the predicate instead of following it.

> 70 E il duca mio ver lui: “Anima sciocca,  
> 71 tienti col corno, e con quel ti disfoga  
> 72 quand'ira o altra passion ti tocca.  

And towards him my Guide: “Stupid soul! keep to thy horn; and vent thyself with that, when rage or other passion touches thee.

- *E il duca mio ver lui* ‘And the guide my towards him’ → “And towards him my Guide”: the directional phrase moves before the noun phrase, and the possessive moves before its noun.
- *Anima sciocca* ‘soul stupid’ → “Stupid soul!”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *tienti col corno* ‘hold thyself with the horn’ → “keep to thy horn”: the reflexive expression becomes the idiom “keep to”, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *e con quel ti disfoga* ‘and with that thyself vent’ → “and vent thyself with that”: the verb precedes both its reflexive object and the instrumental phrase.
- *ti tocca* ‘thee touches’ → “touches thee”: the object follows the verb.

> 73 Cercati al collo, e troverai la soga  
> 74 che il tien legato, o anima confusa,  
> 75 e vedi lui che il gran petto ti doga.”  

Search on thy neck, and thou wilt find the belt that holds it tied, O soul confused, and see [the horn] itself that girdles thy huge breast.”

- *Cercati al collo* ‘search for thyself at the neck’ → “Search on thy neck”: the reflexive is absorbed into the possessive “thy”, and ‘at’ becomes “on”.
- *la soga* ‘the rope’ → “the belt”: the translation identifies the fastening as a belt rather than a rope.
- *che il tien legato* ‘that it holds tied’ → “that holds it tied”: the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *e vedi lui* ‘and see it’ → “and see [the horn] itself”: the pronoun is replaced by its explicit referent, with “itself” expressing its emphasis.
- *che il gran petto ti doga* ‘that the great breast for thee bands’ → “that girdles thy huge breast”: the object moves after the verb, the possessive dative becomes “thy”, and the more specific image of binding with bands becomes “girdles”.

> 76 Poi disse a me: “Egli stesso s'accusa;  
> 77 questi è Nembrotto, per lo cui mal coto  
> 78 pure un linguaggio nel mondo non s'usa.  

Then he said to me: “He accuses himself; this is Nimrod, through whose ill [thought] one language is not still used in the world.

- *Egli stesso s'accusa* ‘He himself accuses himself’ → “He accuses himself”: the emphatic *stesso* is omitted.
- *pure un linguaggio nel mondo non s'usa* ‘only one language in the world is not used’ → “one language is not still used in the world”: *pure* is interpreted temporally as “still”, rather than restrictively as ‘only’; the phrase “in the world” moves after the verb.

> 79 Lasciamlo stare, e non parliamo a voto:  
> 80 chè così è a lui ciascun linguaggio,  
> 81 come il suo ad altrui ch'a nullo è noto.”  

Let us leave him standing, and not speak in vain: for every language is to him, as to others his which no one understands.”

- *Lasciamlo stare* ‘let us leave him be’ → “Let us leave him standing”: *stare* is given the concrete sense “standing” rather than the idiomatic sense ‘be’.
- *non parliamo* ‘let us not speak’ → “not speak”: the exhortation shares the preceding “Let us” rather than receiving a separate expression.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *così è a lui ciascun linguaggio* ‘so is to him each language’ → “every language is to him”: the subject moves before the verb, and *così*, which anticipates the following comparison, is omitted.
- *come il suo ad altrui* ‘as his to others’ → “as to others his”: the phrase “to others” moves before the possessive pronoun.
- *ch'a nullo è noto* ‘which to no one is known’ → “which no one understands”: the construction with ‘is known’ becomes active, with “no one” as subject and the relative pronoun as object.

> 82 Facemmo adunque più lungo viaggio  
> 83 volti a sinistra; ed al trar d'un balestro  
> 84 trovammo l'altro assai più fiero e maggio.  

We therefore journeyed on, turning to the left; and, a crossbow-shot off, we found [the next] far more fierce and large.

- *Facemmo adunque più lungo viaggio* ‘We made therefore a longer journey’ → “We therefore journeyed on”: the verb-and-noun expression becomes the single verb “journeyed”; the comparative description of the journey becomes the continuative “on”; and “therefore” moves before the verb.
- *volti a sinistra* ‘turned to the left’ → “turning to the left”: the past participle becomes a present participle.
- *al trar d'un balestro* ‘at the shooting of a crossbow’ → “a crossbow-shot off”: the verbal noun is rendered as a unit of distance, the possessive phrase becomes a compound, and “off” expresses the spatial relation.
- *l'altro* ‘the other’ → “[the next]”: the translation interprets the other figure as the next one encountered.
- *assai più fiero e maggio* ‘much more fierce and greater’ → “far more fierce and large”: *maggio*, itself comparative, becomes “large”, with the preceding “more” understood to govern both adjectives.

## Lines 85–117 — The Bound Giants and Antæus

> 85 A cinger lui, qual che fosse il maestro  
> 86 non so io dir, ma ei tenea succinto  
> 87 dinanzi l' altro, e dietro il braccio destro  

Who and what the master could be that girt him thus, I cannot tell; but he had his right arm pinioned down behind, and the other before,

- *qual che fosse il maestro* ‘whoever the master was’ → “Who and what the master could be”: the indefinite expression becomes an indirect question, expanded into both identity and nature; the subjunctive *fosse* is rendered with “could”.
- *A cinger lui* ‘to gird him’ → “that girt him thus”: the infinitival phrase becomes a past-tense relative clause following “the master”; “thus” is supplied.
- *non so io dir* ‘I do not know how to say’ → “I cannot tell”: not knowing how to speak becomes inability to tell; the subject moves before the verb.
- *ma ei tenea succinto dinanzi l’altro, e dietro il braccio destro* ‘but he held bound close the other arm in front, and the right arm behind’ → “but he had his right arm pinioned down behind, and the other before,”: “had” replaces ‘held’, and “pinioned down” renders the condition of being tightly bound. The two arms are named in reverse order, their positions follow rather than precede them, and the definite article before ‘right arm’ becomes “his”.

> 88 d' una catena, che il teneva avvinto  
> 89 dal collo in giù, sì che in su lo scoperto  
> 90 si ravvolgeva infino al giro quinto.  

with a chain which held him clasped from the neck downwards, and on the uncovered part went round to the fifth turn.

- *d’una catena* ‘by a chain’ → “with a chain”: the means of restraint is introduced by “with” rather than ‘by’.
- *avvinto* ‘bound’ → “clasped”: the wording presents the restraint as an enclosing clasp.
- *sì che* ‘so that’ → “and”: a clause expressing consequence becomes a coordinated clause.
- *in su lo scoperto* ‘on the uncovered’ → “on the uncovered part”: “part” supplies a noun for the substantivized adjective.
- *si ravvolgeva* ‘wound itself around’ → “went round”: the reflexive verb becomes a non-reflexive verbal phrase.
- *infino al giro quinto* ‘as far as the turn fifth’ → “to the fifth turn”: the expression of extent is compressed to “to”, and the ordinal precedes its noun.

> 91 "Questo superbo voll' esser esperto  
> 92 di sua potenza contra il sommo Giove,"  
> 93 disse il mio duca, "ond' egli ha cotal merto.  

“This proud spirit willed to try his power against high Jove,” saith my Guide; “whence he has such reward.

- *Questo superbo* ‘This proud one’ → “This proud spirit”: “spirit” supplies a noun for the substantivized adjective.
- *voll’ esser esperto di sua potenza* ‘willed to be experienced in his power’ → “willed to try his power”: a construction describing acquired experience becomes an active testing of power; the prepositional complement becomes a direct object.
- *contra il sommo Giove* ‘against the highest Jove’ → “against high Jove”: the superlative becomes a positive adjective, and the definite article is omitted.
- *disse il mio duca* ‘said my leader’ → “saith my Guide”: the past tense becomes present, and ‘leader’ is rendered as “Guide”.
- *merto* ‘desert’ → “reward”: what is deserved is expressed as the recompense received.

> 94 Fialte ha nome; e fece le gran prove,  
> 95 quando i giganti fer paura ai Dei;  
> 96 le braccia ch' ei menò, giammai non move."  

Ephialtes is his name; and he made the great endeavours, when the giants made the Gods afraid; the arms he agitated then, he never moves.”

- *Fialte ha nome* ‘he has Ephialtes as name’ → “Ephialtes is his name”: the naming construction with ‘has’ becomes a copular construction with “his name”.
- *le gran prove* ‘the great feats’ → “the great endeavours”: accomplished exploits are expressed as efforts or undertakings.
- *fer paura ai Dei* ‘made fear to the Gods’ → “made the Gods afraid”: the indirect object becomes the direct object, and the noun ‘fear’ becomes the predicative adjective “afraid”.
- *ch’ei menò* ‘that he wielded’ → “he agitated then”: the relative pronoun is omitted, ‘wielded’ becomes “agitated”, and “then” is supplied.
- *giammai non* ‘never not’ → “never”: Italian negative concord is rendered by a single English negative.

> 97 Ed io a lui: "S' esser puote, io vorrei  
> 98 che dello ismisurato Briareo  
> 99 esperienza avesser gli occhi miei."  

And I to him: “If it were possible, I should wish my eyes might have experience of the immense Briareus.”

- *S’esser puote* ‘If it can be’ → “If it were possible”: the present-tense expression of possibility becomes a hypothetical construction with “were”.
- *io vorrei che* ‘I would wish that’ → “I should wish”: the conditional uses “should”, and the conjunction introducing the following clause is omitted.
- *dello ismisurato Briareo* ‘of the immeasurable Briareus’ → “of the immense Briareus”: ‘immeasurable’ becomes “immense”, and the phrase moves from before ‘experience’ to the end of the clause.
- *esperienza avesser gli occhi miei* ‘experience might have the eyes my’ → “my eyes might have experience”: the postposed subject moves before the verb, the object follows it, and the possessive precedes its noun. The imperfect subjunctive is expressed through “might have”.

> 100 Ond' ei rispose: "Tu vedrai Anteo  
> 101 presso di qui, che parla ed è disciolto,  
> 102 che ne porrà nel fondo d' ogni reo.  

Whereat he answered: “Thou shalt see Antæus near at hand, who speaks, and is unfettered, who will put us into the bottom of all guilt.

- *Ond’* ‘Whence’ → “Whereat”: the connective presents the answer as a response to the preceding words rather than as something proceeding from them.
- *presso di qui* ‘near here’ → “near at hand”: the explicit reference to ‘here’ becomes an idiomatic expression of proximity.
- *nel fondo d’ogni reo* ‘in the bottom of every evil’ → “into the bottom of all guilt”: “into” makes the movement explicit; distributive ‘every’ becomes collective “all”, and substantival *reo* is interpreted as the abstract “guilt”.

> 103 Quel che tu vuoi veder più là è molto,  
> 104 ed è legato e fatto come questo,  
> 105 salvo che più feroce par nel volto."  

He whom thou desirest to see is far beyond; and is tied and shaped like this one, save that he seems in aspect more ferocious.”

- *Quel che tu vuoi veder* ‘That one whom thou wishest to see’ → “He whom thou desirest to see”: the demonstrative becomes the personal pronoun “He”, and wishing is phrased as desiring.
- *più là è molto* ‘is much farther on’ → “is far beyond”: the comparative expression and its intensifier are recast as “far beyond”; the location follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *fatto* ‘made’ → “shaped”: the rendering specifies physical form.
- *salvo che più feroce par nel volto* ‘except that more ferocious he appears in the face’ → “save that he seems in aspect more ferocious”: the predicative adjective moves after the verb and the phrase describing appearance; the concrete ‘face’ becomes the more general “aspect”.

> 106 Non fu tremuoto già tanto rubesto,  
> 107 che scotesse una torre così forte,  
> 108 come Fialte a scotersi fu presto.  

No mighty earthquake ever shook a tower so violently, as Ephialtes forthwith shook himself.

- *Non fu tremuoto già tanto rubesto, che scotesse una torre così forte* ‘Never was there an earthquake so mighty that it should shake a tower so strongly’ → “No mighty earthquake ever shook a tower so violently,”: the negative existential construction becomes a negative subject directly governing “shook”. The degree expressed by *tanto* is not separately rendered, and the subjunctive relative clause becomes the main past-tense assertion. ‘So strongly’ becomes “so violently”.
- *come Fialte a scotersi fu presto* ‘as Ephialtes was quick to shake himself’ → “as Ephialtes forthwith shook himself.”: the construction with ‘was quick’ and an infinitive becomes a finite action qualified by the adverb “forthwith”.

> 109 Allor temett' io più che mai la morte,  
> 110 e non v' era mestier più che la dotta,  
> 111 s' io non avessi viste le ritorte.  

Then more than ever I dreaded death; and nothing else was wanted for it but the fear, had I not seen his bands.

- *Allor temett’ io più che mai la morte* ‘Then I feared more than ever the death’ → “Then more than ever I dreaded death”: the comparative phrase moves before the subject and verb; ‘feared’ becomes the stronger “dreaded”, and the article before ‘death’ is omitted.
- *e non v’era mestier più che la dotta* ‘and there was need of no more than the fear’ → “and nothing else was wanted for it but the fear”: the existential expression of need becomes a passive construction with “nothing else” as subject. “For it” is supplied, explicitly relating the need to the preceding mention of death.
- *s’io non avessi viste le ritorte* ‘if I had not seen the twisted bonds’ → “had I not seen his bands”: conditional inversion replaces ‘if’; the definite article becomes “his”, and “bands” does not retain the explicit sense of twisting in *ritorte*.

> 112 Noi procedemmo più avanti allotta,  
> 113 e venimmo ad Anteo, che ben cinqu' alle,  
> 114 senza la testa, uscía fuor della grotta.  

We then proceeded farther on, and reached Antæus, who full five ells, besides the head, forth issued from the cavern.

- *Noi procedemmo più avanti allotta* ‘We proceeded farther on then’ → “We then proceeded farther on”: the final time adverb moves between subject and verb.
- *e venimmo ad Anteo* ‘and came to Antæus’ → “and reached Antæus”: a verb followed by a prepositional complement becomes a transitive verb with a direct object.
- *senza la testa* ‘without the head’ → “besides the head”: exclusion from the stated measurement is expressed as something additional to it.
- *uscía fuor della grotta* ‘was issuing out of the cavern’ → “forth issued from the cavern”: the imperfect becomes a simple past; ‘out of’ is redistributed as “forth” and “from”, with “forth” placed before the verb.

> 115 "O tu, che nella fortunata valle,  
> 116 che fece Scipion di gloria reda  
> 117 quando Annibal co' suoi diede le spalle,  

“O thou! who in the fateful valley, which made Scipio heir of glory when Hannibal retreated with his hosts,

- *fortunata* ‘fortunate’ → “fateful”: the adjective is interpreted as marking decisive significance rather than good fortune.
- *che fece Scipion di gloria reda* ‘which made Scipio of glory heir’ → “which made Scipio heir of glory”: the complement ‘of glory’ follows rather than precedes ‘heir’.
- *co’ suoi* ‘with his own’ → “with his hosts”: “hosts” supplies an explicitly military noun for the substantivized possessive; the phrase moves after the verb.
- *diede le spalle* ‘gave the shoulders’ → “retreated”: the bodily idiom is replaced by a verb stating the action it signifies.

## Lines 118–145 — Antæus Lowers the Travellers

> 118 recasti già mille leon per preda,  

didst take of old a thousand lions for thy prey;

- *recasti* ‘didst bring’ → “didst take”: bringing is rendered as taking.
- *già* ‘already’ → “of old”: the adverb is given a more explicitly remote past reference.
- *mille* ‘thousand’ → “a thousand”: English supplies the indefinite article before the numeral.
- *per preda* ‘for prey’ → “for thy prey”: the possessive is supplied.

> 119 e che, se fossi stato all' alta guerra  
> 120 de' tuoi fratelli, ancor par ch' e' si creda  
> 121 che avrebber vinto i figli della terra;  

and through whom, hadst thou been at the high war of thy brethren, it seems yet to be believed that the sons of earth had conquered;

- *e che* ‘and who’ → “and through whom”: the relative is made explicitly instrumental, expressing the agency implied by the following hypothetical statement.
- *se fossi stato* ‘if thou hadst been’ → “hadst thou been”: inversion replaces the conjunction introducing the condition.
- *ancor par ch' e' si creda* ‘still it seems that it is believed’ → “it seems yet to be believed”: the finite subordinate clause becomes a passive infinitive, and the adverb moves after “seems”.
- *che avrebber vinto i figli della terra* ‘that would have conquered the sons of the earth’ → “that the sons of earth had conquered”: the subject moves before the verb; the conditional perfect becomes a pluperfect expressing the hypothetical result; and the article before ‘earth’ is omitted.

> 122 mettine giù, e non ten venga schifo,  
> 123 dove Cocito la freddura serra.  

set us down—and be not shy to do it—where the cold locks up Cocytus.

- *e non ten venga schifo* ‘and let disgust at it not come to thee’ → “and be not shy to do it”: an impersonal expression of aversion becomes a direct injunction against reluctance. The reference carried by *ne* is expanded into “to do it”.
- *dove Cocito la freddura serra* ‘where Cocytus the cold locks up’ → “where the cold locks up Cocytus”: the object, placed before the subject in Italian, moves after the verb.

> 124 Non ci far ire a Tizio, nè a Tifo;  

Do not make us go to Tityos nor Typhon;

- *Non ci far ire* ‘not us make go’ → “Do not make us go”: English supplies the auxiliary for the negative imperative and places the object pronoun after “make”.
- *nè a Tifo* ‘nor to Typhon’ → “nor Typhon”: the second preposition is omitted.

> 125 questi può dar di quel che qui si brama:  
> 126 però ti china, e non torcer lo grifo.  

this man can give of that which here is longed for: therefore bend thee, and curl not thy lip in scorn.

- *questi* ‘this one’ → “this man”: the demonstrative pronoun is expanded into a noun phrase.
- *ti china* ‘thyself bend’ → “bend thee”: the reflexive pronoun follows the imperative rather than preceding it.
- *non torcer lo grifo* ‘do not twist the snout’ → “curl not thy lip in scorn”: the prohibitory infinitive becomes an English imperative; “curl” replaces ‘twist’, and “thy lip” replaces ‘the snout’. “in scorn” makes the gesture’s implied attitude explicit.

> 127 Ancor ti può nel mondo render fama:  
> 128 ch' ei vive e lunga vita ancor aspetta,  
> 129 se innanzi tempo grazia a sè nol chiama."  

He can [yet] restore thy fame on earth: for he lives, and still awaits long life, so Grace before the time call him not unto herself.”

- *Ancor ti può nel mondo render fama* ‘also he can to thee in the world give fame’ → “He can [yet] restore thy fame on earth”: *Ancor* receives a temporal rendering and moves after the modal verb. Giving fame to someone becomes restoring that person’s fame, with *ti* represented by “thy”. “on earth” replaces ‘in the world’ and moves to the end of the clause.
- *ch'* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *e lunga vita ancor aspetta* ‘and long life still awaits’ → “and still awaits long life”: the object moves from before the adverb and verb to after them.
- *se innanzi tempo grazia a sè nol chiama* ‘if before time grace to herself him not calls’ → “so Grace before the time call him not unto herself”: “so” introduces the condition in the sense of ‘provided that’. The subject moves before the time phrase, which gains an article; the object pronoun follows the verb; and the reflexive prepositional phrase moves to the end. The Italian indicative becomes the English subjunctive “call”.

> 130 Così disse il maestro; e quegli in fretta  
> 131 le man distese e prese il duca mio,  
> 132 ond' Ercole sentì già grande stretta.  

Thus spake the Master; and he in haste stretched forth the hands, whence Hercules of old did feel great stress, and took my Guide.

- *disse* ‘said’ → “spake”: the verb of saying is replaced by a verb of speaking.
- *quegli* ‘that one’ → “he”: the demonstrative pronoun becomes a personal pronoun.
- *le man distese* ‘the hands stretched forth’ → “stretched forth the hands”: the object moves after the verb.
- *e prese il duca mio* ‘and took the guide my’ → “and took my Guide”: the possessive moves before the noun, and the entire clause is postponed until after the relative clause about the hands.
- *già* ‘already’ → “of old”: the adverb denotes a remote past and moves before the verb.
- *sentì* ‘felt’ → “did feel”: the simple past becomes an auxiliary construction.
- *grande stretta* ‘great squeezing’ → “great stress”: the physical pressure of a grip is rendered by a more general term for strain.

> 133 Virgilio, quando prender si sentio,  
> 134 disse a me: "Fatti in qua, sì ch' io ti prenda";  
> 135 poi fece sì che un fascio er' egli ed io.  

Virgil, when he felt their grasp, said to me: “Come here, that I may take thee”; then of himself and me he made one bundle.

- *quando prender si sentio* ‘when he felt himself being taken’ → “when he felt their grasp”: the infinitival construction becomes a noun phrase, and “their” explicitly identifies the hands as the source of the grasp.
- *Fatti in qua* ‘make thyself hither’ → “Come here”: the reflexive expression becomes a simple verb of movement, with “here” replacing the directional phrase.
- *sì ch' io ti prenda* ‘so that I thee may take’ → “that I may take thee”: the explicit ‘so’ is omitted, the object pronoun moves after the verb, and “may” expresses the Italian subjunctive.
- *poi fece sì che un fascio er' egli ed io* ‘then he did so that one bundle was he and I’ → “then of himself and me he made one bundle”: the construction expressing a resulting state becomes a direct statement of making. The subjects of the subordinate clause become complements of “of”, with *egli* becoming reflexive “himself”.

> 136 Qual pare a riguardar la Carisenda  
> 137 sotto il chinato, quando un nuvol vada  
> 138 sopr' essa sì che ella incontro penda:  

Such as the Carisenda seems to one’s view, beneath the leaning side, when a cloud is going over it so, that it hangs [in the contrary direction]:

- *Qual* ‘as’ → “Such as”: English makes the correlative expression fuller.
- *pare a riguardar la Carisenda* ‘seems, to look at, the Carisenda’ → “the Carisenda seems to one’s view”: the subject moves before the verb, and the infinitive of looking becomes a noun phrase with an explicit indefinite possessor.
- *il chinato* ‘the inclined [part]’ → “the leaning side”: the substantivized participle is expanded with the noun “side”.
- *quando un nuvol vada* ‘when a cloud should go’ → “when a cloud is going”: the subjunctive becomes an indicative progressive.
- *incontro* ‘towards [it]’ → “[in the contrary direction]”: the rendering expresses an opposing direction rather than movement towards an implied object, and the phrase moves after the verb.
- *penda* ‘should lean’ → “hangs”: a different sense of the verb is selected, and the subjunctive becomes an indicative.

> 139 tal parve Anteo a me che stava a bada  
> 140 di vederlo chinare, e fu tal ora  
> 141 ch' io avrei volut' ir per altra strada;  

such Antæus seemed to me who stood watching to see him bend; and [it was so terrible a moment, that] I should have wished to go by other road;

- *tal parve Anteo a me* ‘such seemed Antæus to me’ → “such Antæus seemed to me”: the subject moves before the verb.
- *che stava a bada di vederlo chinare* ‘who was intent on seeing him bend’ → “who stood watching to see him bend”: the expression of attentive waiting becomes an explicit act of watching, while “stood” gives *stava* a more specifically physical sense. The dependent infinitive is introduced by “to” rather than ‘of’.
- *e fu tal ora ch'* ‘and it was such a moment that’ → “and [it was so terrible a moment, that]”: “terrible” is supplied to specify the quality left implicit in *tal*, and ‘such a moment’ becomes “so terrible a moment”.

> 142 ma lievemente al fondo che divora  
> 143 Lucifero con Giuda ci sposò;  

but gently on the deep, which swallows Lucifer with Judas, he [set] us [down];

- *lievemente* ‘lightly’ → “gently”: the rendering emphasizes gentleness rather than lightness.
- *al fondo* ‘to the bottom’ → “on the deep”: the directional preposition becomes one of position, and ‘the bottom’ becomes “the deep”.
- *che divora* ‘which devours’ → “which swallows”: the verb is rendered by a more specific action of engulfing.
- *ci sposò* ‘us he deposited’ → “he [set] us [down]”: the object pronoun moves after the verb, and the single Italian verb becomes a phrasal verb.

> 144 nè sì chinato lì fece dimora,  
> 145 e come albero in nave si levò.  

nor lingered there thus bent, but raised himself as in a ship the mast.

- *nè sì chinato lì fece dimora* ‘nor thus bent there made a stay’ → “nor lingered there thus bent”: the verb-and-noun expression becomes the single verb “lingered”. The description of posture moves to the end, and the place adverb follows the verb.
- *e* ‘and’ → “but”: coordination becomes explicit contrast.
- *come albero in nave si levò* ‘like a mast in a ship himself he raised’ → “raised himself as in a ship the mast”: the comparison moves after the verb, the reflexive pronoun follows the verb, and the phrase locating the mast precedes its noun. The bare Italian nouns receive articles, with “the mast” made definite.

## Summary — Close Echoes and English Reshaping

The translation stays close to the Italian’s sequence of details, comparisons, and much of its imagery, but repeatedly rearranges them into English syntax. Subjects placed after verbs move before them, objects follow rather than precede their verbs, and possessives and descriptive adjectives generally move before their nouns. Thus *fuggìemi errore* becomes “error [fled] from me”, while *del ventre gran parte* becomes “great part of the belly”. More generally, English supplies subject pronouns where Italian gives the subject through the verb ending, and omits the articles that Italian places before possessives. Yet the translation also preserves marked constructions, including “And he to me” and “began to shout the savage mouth”, rather than consistently smoothing the prose into ordinary English.

Changes of construction often make the Italian more explicit or compact. Reflexive expressions become passives, as *s'inganna* becomes “is deceived”; personal datives become possessives, as in *cresce' mi paura*, “[my] fear [increased]”. Verb-and-noun expressions become single verbs: *facemmo … viaggio* is rendered as “journeyed on”, and *fece dimora* as “lingered”. Elsewhere, clauses replace participial or infinitival constructions, or are replaced by them. Tense and mood also shift: *od' io* becomes “I have heard”, *perdè* becomes “had lost”, and *disse* becomes “saith”. These choices sometimes clarify temporal or logical relations rather than reproduce their Italian form.

Word choice ranges from close correspondence to interpretive substitution. *Mi morse*, ‘bit me’, becomes “wounded me”; *buona*, ‘good’, becomes “healing”; and *diede le spalle*, ‘gave the shoulders’, becomes “retreated”. Some renderings alter the image or select a different sense: *perizoma* becomes “an apron”, *soga* becomes “the belt”, and *la santa gesta*, understood in the comparison as ‘the holy host’, becomes “the holy emprise”. Conversely, expressions such as “less than night and less than day” and “the air-crowding vapour” retain the Italian’s distinctive phrasing or compress its imagery without replacing it.

Additions commonly supply what the Italian leaves implicit: “blushes” explains the colouring of the cheeks, “making” specifies Nature’s art, and “in scorn” interprets the facial gesture. The revised phrase “[it was so terrible a moment, that]” likewise states a quality left unstated in *tal ora*. Omissions are usually smaller: repeated prepositions, conjunctions, and emphatic elements such as *stesso* disappear, while *così* is not separately represented in the comparison of languages. Overall, the prose remains closely tethered to the Italian, but combines that fidelity with English grammatical requirements, explanatory additions, and occasional lexical choices that reshape rather than merely reproduce the wording.
