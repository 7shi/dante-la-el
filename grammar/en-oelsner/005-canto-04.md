# Inferno 4

## Lines 1–24 — Awakening and Descent Into the Abyss

> 1 Ruppemi l' alto sonno nella testa  
> 2 un greve tuono, sì ch' io mi riscossi,  
> 3 come persona che per forza è desta;  

A heavy thunder broke the deep sleep in my head; so that I started like one who is awaked by force;

- *Ruppemi l' alto sonno nella testa / un greve tuono* ‘Broke for me the deep sleep in the head a heavy thunder’ → “A heavy thunder broke the deep sleep in my head”: the subject moves from after the verb and its complements to the beginning; the personal reference expressed by *-mi* becomes the possessive “my” before “head”.
- *mi riscossi* ‘I roused myself’ → “I started”: the reflexive construction becomes an intransitive verb expressing a sudden movement.
- *persona* ‘a person’ → “one”: an indefinite pronoun replaces the noun.
- *per forza è desta* ‘by force is awakened’ → “is awaked by force”: the phrase expressing force moves after the verb.

> 4 e l' occhio riposato intorno mossi,  
> 5 dritto levato, e fiso riguardai  
> 6 per conoscer lo loco dov' io fossi.  

and, having risen erect, I moved my rested eyes around, and looked stedfastly to know the place in which I was.

- *l' occhio riposato intorno mossi* ‘the rested eye around I moved’ → “I moved my rested eyes around”: the verb precedes its object, and “around” follows it; the singular ‘eye’ becomes plural, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *dritto levato* ‘upright, risen’ → “having risen erect”: the participle is expanded into a perfect participial construction, “erect” follows it, and the whole phrase moves before the clause about moving the eyes.
- *fiso riguardai* ‘fixedly I looked’ → “looked stedfastly”: the adverb follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *dov' io fossi* ‘where I was’ → “in which I was”: the relative adverb becomes a preposition and relative pronoun; the Italian subjunctive becomes an English indicative.

> 7 Vero è, che in su la proda mi trovai  
> 8 della valle d' abisso dolorosa,  
> 9 che tuono accoglie d' infiniti guai.  

True is it, that I found myself upon the brink of the dolorous Valley of the Abyss, which gathers thunder of endless wailings.

- *Vero è, che* ‘True is that’ → “True is it, that”: anticipatory “it” is supplied before the clause stating what is true.
- *in su la proda mi trovai* ‘upon the brink myself I found’ → “I found myself upon the brink”: the locative phrase moves after the verb, and the reflexive pronoun follows rather than precedes it.
- *della valle d' abisso dolorosa* ‘of the valley of abyss dolorous’ → “of the dolorous Valley of the Abyss”: “dolorous” moves before “Valley”, and an article is supplied before “Abyss”.
- *che tuono accoglie d' infiniti guai* ‘which thunder gathers of infinite woes’ → “which gathers thunder of endless wailings”: the object follows the verb; “endless” renders *infiniti*, and “wailings” renders *guai* as audible expressions of suffering rather than woes themselves.

> 10 Oscura, profonda era, e nebulosa,  
> 11 tanto che, per ficcar lo viso al fondo,  
> 12 io non vi discernea alcuna cosa.  

It was so dark, profound, and cloudy, that, with fixing my look upon the bottom, I there discerned nothing.

- *Oscura, profonda era, e nebulosa, / tanto che* ‘Dark, profound it was, and cloudy, so much that’ → “It was so dark, profound, and cloudy, that”: the verb moves before all three adjectives, and the following expression of degree becomes “so” before them.
- *per ficcar lo viso al fondo* ‘despite thrusting the gaze to the bottom’ → “with fixing my look upon the bottom”: the concessive force of *per* with the infinitive becomes an accompanying action introduced by “with”; forceful *ficcar* becomes “fixing”, the article becomes “my”, and direction towards the bottom becomes fixation “upon” it.
- *io non vi discernea alcuna cosa* ‘I did not there discern any thing’ → “I there discerned nothing”: the negative attached to the verb and the indefinite object are combined into “nothing”.

> 13 “Or discendiam quaggiù nel cieco mondo,”  
> 14 cominciò il poeta tutto smorto;  
> 15 “io sarò primo, e tu sarai secondo.”  

“Now let us descend into the blind world here below,” began the Poet all pale; “I will be first, and thou shalt be second.”

- *discendiam quaggiù nel cieco mondo* ‘let us descend down here into the blind world’ → “let us descend into the blind world here below”: *quaggiù* becomes “here below” and moves after the phrase naming the destination; the Italian exhortative verb is expressed through “let us” and an infinitive.

> 16 Ed io, che del color mi fui accorto,  
> 17 dissi: “Come verrò, se tu paventi,  
> 18 che suoli al mio dubbiare esser conforto?”  

And I, who had remarked his colour, said: “How shall I come, when thou fearest, who art wont to be my strength in doubt?”

- *del color mi fui accorto* ‘of the colour I had become aware’ → “had remarked his colour”: the reflexive construction with *di* becomes a transitive verb with a direct object; the object moves after the verb, and the article becomes “his”.
- *se* ‘if’ → “when”: the conditional conjunction is replaced by one that presents the fear as an existing circumstance.
- *che suoli al mio dubbiare esser conforto* ‘who art wont to my doubting to be comfort’ → “who art wont to be my strength in doubt”: the infinitive moves immediately after “art wont”; the substantival infinitive *dubbiare* becomes “doubt”, and ‘comfort’ becomes “strength”. The relation ‘comfort to my doubting’ is recast as “my strength in doubt”, transferring the possessive to “strength”.

> 19 Ed egli a me: “L' angoscia delle genti,  
> 20 che son quaggiù, nel viso mi dipinge  
> 21 quella pietà, che tu per tema senti.  

And he to me: “The anguish of the people who are here below, on my face depaints that pity, which thou takest for fear.

- *delle genti* ‘of the peoples’ → “of the people”: the Italian plural noun is rendered by the English collective “people”.
- *nel viso mi dipinge* ‘in the face paints for me’ → “on my face depaints”: ‘in’ becomes “on”, and the personal reference expressed by *mi* becomes the possessive “my”.
- *che tu per tema senti* ‘which thou as fear perceivest’ → “which thou takest for fear”: perception is expressed as interpretation through “takest for”, and the phrase identifying the supposed emotion follows rather than precedes the verb.

> 22 Andiam, chè la via lunga ne sospinge.”  

Let us go; for the length of way impels us.”

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *la via lunga* ‘the long way’ → “the length of way”: the adjective becomes the abstract noun “length”, while “way”, originally the head noun, becomes its dependent.
- *ne sospinge* ‘us impels’ → “impels us”: the object pronoun follows rather than precedes the verb.

> 23 Così si mise, e così mi fe' entrare  
> 24 nel primo cerchio che l' abisso cinge.  

Thus he entered, and made me enter, into the first circle that girds the abyss.

- *si mise* ‘he set himself in motion’ → “he entered”: the reflexive expression for setting off becomes an intransitive verb specifying entry.
- *e così mi fe' entrare* ‘and thus me he made enter’ → “and made me enter”: the second *così* is omitted, and the object pronoun moves after “made”.
- *che l' abisso cinge* ‘that the abyss girds’ → “that girds the abyss”: *l' abisso*, the object of *cinge*, moves from before the verb to after it.

## Lines 25–57 — Desire Without Hope in Limbo

> 25 Quivi, secondo che per ascoltare,  
> 26 non avea pianto, ma' che di sospiri,  
> 27 che l' aura eterna facevan tremare:  

Here there was no plaint, that could be heard, except of sighs, which caused the eternal air to tremble;

- *non avea pianto* ‘there was no weeping’ → “there was no plaint”: the impersonal use of *avere* (‘have’) becomes English “there was”; weeping is rendered as lamentation.
- *secondo che per ascoltare* ‘according as [one could judge] by listening’ → “that could be heard”: a qualification concerning what could be discerned by listening becomes a relative clause modifying “plaint”; it also follows rather than precedes that noun.
- *che l' aura eterna facevan tremare* ‘which the air eternal made tremble’ → “which caused the eternal air to tremble”: the object follows rather than precedes the causative verb, the adjective precedes its noun, and “caused” takes a *to*-infinitive rather than the bare infinitive used with ‘made’.

> 28 e ciò avvenia di duol senza martiri,  
> 29 ch' avean le turbe, ch' eran molte e grandi,  
> 30 d' infanti e di femmine e di viri.  

and this arose from the sadness, without torment, of the crowds that were many and great, both of children, and of women and men.

- *avvenia* ‘happened’ → “arose”: occurrence is expressed as arising from a cause.
- *di duol senza martiri* ‘from grief without torments’ → “from the sadness, without torment”: the definite article is supplied before “sadness”, and plural *martiri* becomes singular, uncountable “torment”.
- *ch' avean le turbe* ‘which the crowds had’ → “of the crowds”: the relative clause expressing possession becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *d' infanti e di femmine e di viri* ‘of infants and of women and of men’ → “both of children, and of women and men”: “children” is broader than ‘infants’; “both” is supplied, and the final repeated *di* is omitted.

> 31 Lo buon maestro a me: “Tu non dimandi  
> 32 che spiriti son questi, che tu vedi?  

The good Master to me : “Thou askest not what spirits are these thou seest?

- *che tu vedi* ‘that thou seest’ → “thou seest”: the object relative pronoun is omitted.

> 33 Or vo' che sappi, innanzi che più andi,  

I wish thee to know, before thou goest farther,

- *Or vo' che sappi* ‘Now I wish that thou know’ → “I wish thee to know”: *Or* is omitted, and the finite subjunctive clause becomes an object-and-infinitive construction.
- *innanzi che più andi* ‘before thou go farther’ → “before thou goest farther”: the Italian subjunctive becomes an English indicative, and the expression of distance follows rather than precedes the verb.

> 34 ch' ei non peccaro; e s' egli hanno mercedi,  
> 35 non basta, perchè non ebber battesmo,  
> 36 ch'è porte della fede che tu credi;  

that they sinned not ; and though they have merit, it suffices not : for they had not Baptism, which is the portal of the faith that thou believest

- *ei non peccaro* ‘they not sinned’ → “they sinned not”: the negative follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *e s' egli hanno mercedi* ‘and if they have merits’ → “and though they have merit”: the conditional wording is interpreted concessively, and plural *mercedi* becomes uncountable singular “merit”.
- *non basta* ‘not suffices’ → “it suffices not”: the negative follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *perchè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *non ebber battesmo* ‘not had baptism’ → “they had not Baptism”: the negative follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *ch'è porte* ‘which is gates’ → “which is the portal”: the printed *porte* is rendered as a singular noun, and a definite article is supplied.

> 37 e se furon dinanzi al Cristianesmo,  
> 38 non adorar debitamente Dio;  
> 39 e di questi cotai son io medesmo.  

; and seeing they were before Christianity, they worshipped not God aright ; and of these am I myself.

- *e se furon dinanzi al Cristianesmo* ‘and if they were before Christianity’ → “and seeing they were before Christianity”: the conditional *se* is interpreted causally as “seeing”.
- *non adorar debitamente Dio* ‘not worshipped duly God’ → “they worshipped not God aright”: the negative follows the verb, while the adverb follows rather than precedes its object.
- *di questi cotai* ‘of these such’ → “of these”: *cotai*, which characterizes them as people of this kind, is not separately rendered.

> 40 Per tai difetti, non per altro rio,  
> 41 semo perduti, e sol di tanto offesi,  
> 42 che senza speme vivemo in disio.”  

For such defects, and for no other fault, are we lost ; and only in so far afflicted, that without hope we live in desire.”

- *non per altro rio* ‘not for other wrongdoing’ → “and for no other fault”: “and” is supplied, and the negative is transferred from the prepositional phrase to its noun as “no”.
- *semo perduti* ‘we are lost’ → “are we lost”: the English subject follows the verb after the introductory prepositional phrases.
- *e sol di tanto offesi, che* ‘and only by so much harmed, that’ → “and only in so far afflicted, that”: the measure of harm becomes an expression of extent, and *offesi* is rendered as suffering affliction rather than receiving harm.

> 43 Gran duol mi prese al cuor, quando lo intesi,  
> 44 perocchè gente di molto valore  
> 45 conobbi, che in quel limbo eran sospesi.  

Great sadness took me at the heart on hearing this ; because I knew men of much worth, who in that Limbo were suspense.

- *quando lo intesi* ‘when I heard it’ → “on hearing this”: the finite temporal clause becomes a preposition with a gerund, and “this” replaces the personal pronoun.
- *gente di molto valore conobbi* ‘people of much worth I knew’ → “I knew men of much worth”: the object moves after the verb, and the collective singular *gente* becomes plural “men”.
- *che in quel limbo eran sospesi* ‘who in that limbo were suspended’ → “who in that Limbo were suspense”: the Italian participle is represented by the archaic English adjective “suspense”.

> 46 “Dimmi, Maestro mio, dimmi, Signore,”  
> 47 cominciai io, per voler esser certo  
> 48 di quella fede che vince ogni errore;  

“Tell me, Master ; tell me, Sir,” I began, desiring to be assured of that Faith which conquers every error

- *Maestro mio* ‘my Master’ → “Master”: the possessive is omitted.
- *cominciai io* ‘began I’ → “I began”: the subject moves before the verb.
- *per voler esser certo* ‘from wishing to be certain’ → “desiring to be assured”: the prepositional infinitive construction becomes a participial phrase, and being certain is expressed as being assured.

> 49 “uscicci mai alcuno, o per suo merto,  
> 50 o per altrui, che poi fosse beato?”  

; “did ever any, by his own merit, or by others’, go out from hence, that afterwards was blessed?”

- *uscicci mai alcuno* ‘went out from here ever anyone’ → “did ever any” … “go out from hence”: English introduces the interrogative auxiliary “did” and puts the main verb after the subject and the intervening phrases about merit; *alcuno* becomes the independently used “any”.
- *o per suo merto, o per altrui* ‘either by his merit, or by another’s’ → “by his own merit, or by others’,”: the first *o* is omitted, “own” is supplied, and the indefinite *altrui* is expressed by plural possessive “others’”.
- *che poi fosse beato* ‘who afterwards might be blessed’ → “that afterwards was blessed”: the Italian subjunctive becomes the indicative “was”, presenting the hypothetical qualification without an English modal.

> 51 E quei, che intese il mio parlar coverto,  
> 52 rispose: “Io era nuovo in questo stato,  
> 53 quando ci vidi venire un possente  
> 54 con segno di vittoria coronato.  

And he, understanding my covert speech, replied : “I was new in this condition, when I saw a Mighty One come to us, crowned with sign of victory.

- *quei* ‘that one’ → “he”: a demonstrative pronoun becomes a personal pronoun.
- *che intese* ‘who understood’ → “understanding”: the finite relative clause becomes a participial phrase.
- *il mio parlar coverto* ‘my speaking covert’ → “my covert speech”: the substantivized infinitive becomes the noun “speech”, and its adjective moves before it.
- *ci vidi venire un possente* ‘here I saw come a mighty one’ → “I saw a Mighty One come to us”: the locative *ci* is interpreted personally as “to us”; the person seen moves before “come”, and the substantivized adjective receives the supporting noun “One”.
- *con segno di vittoria coronato* ‘with sign of victory crowned’ → “crowned with sign of victory”: the participle moves before the phrase that complements it.

> 55 Trasseci l' ombra del primo parente,  
> 56 d' Abel suo figlio, e quella di Noè,  
> 57 di Moisè Legista, e ubbidiente;  

He took away from us the shade of our First Parent, of Abel his son, and that of Noah ; of Moses the Legislator and obedient ;

- *Trasseci* ‘drew out from here’ → “He took away from us”: drawing out becomes taking away, and *ci* is interpreted as “from us” rather than as a reference to the place.
- *del primo parente* ‘of the first parent’ → “of our First Parent”: the definite article is replaced by the supplied possessive “our”.
- *Legista* ‘legislator’ → “the Legislator”: the definite article is supplied before the title.

## Lines 58–90 — The Blessed and the Honoured Poets

> 58 Abraam patriarca, e David re,  
> 59 Israel con lo padre, e co’ suoi nati,  
> 60 e con Rachele, per cui tanto fe’,  
> 61 ed altri molti; e fecegli beati:  

Abraham the Patriarch; David the King; Israel with his father and his children, and [with] Rachel, for whom he did so much; and many others, and made them blessed;

- *Abraam patriarca, e David re* ‘Abraham patriarch, and David king’ → “Abraham the Patriarch; David the King”: articles are supplied before the titles, and the conjunction is omitted.
- *con lo padre* ‘with the father’ → “with his father”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *e co’ suoi nati* ‘and with his offspring’ → “and his children”: the repeated preposition is omitted; the substantivized participle *nati*, literally ‘those born’, becomes “children”.
- *per cui tanto fe’* ‘for whom so much he did’ → “for whom he did so much”: the object follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *ed altri molti* ‘and others many’ → “and many others”: the quantifier moves before “others”.

> 62 e vo’ che sappi che, dinanzi ad essi,  
> 63 spiriti umani non eran salvati.”  

and I wish thee to know, that, before these, no human souls were saved.”

- *e vo’ che sappi* ‘and I wish that thou know’ → “and I wish thee to know”: a subordinate clause with a subjunctive verb becomes an object-and-infinitive construction.
- *dinanzi ad essi* ‘before them’ → “before these”: a personal pronoun becomes a demonstrative.
- *spiriti umani non eran salvati* ‘spirits human were not saved’ → “no human souls were saved”: “souls” replaces ‘spirits’, the adjective precedes the noun, and the negative moves from the verb to the noun phrase.

> 64 Non lasciavam l’ andar, perch’ ei dicessi,  
> 65 ma passavam la selva tuttavia,  
> 66 la selva dico di spiriti spessi.  

We ceased not to go, though he was speaking; but passed the wood meanwhile, the wood, I say, of crowded spirits.

- *Non lasciavam l’ andar* ‘We did not leave off the going’ → “We ceased not to go”: the verbal noun becomes an infinitive complement to “ceased”.
- *perch’ ei dicessi* ‘although he spoke’ → “though he was speaking”: the concessive subjunctive becomes an English indicative progressive.
- *tuttavia* ‘continually’ → “meanwhile”: continuity is expressed instead as contemporaneous action.
- *di spiriti spessi* ‘of spirits dense’ → “of crowded spirits”: the adjective precedes the noun, and density is expressed as crowding.

> 67 Non era lunga ancor la nostra via  
> 68 di qua dal sonno, quando’ io vidi un foco,  
> 69 ch’ emisperio di tenebre vincia.  

Our way was not yet far [since my slumber], when I saw a fire, which conquered a hemisphere of the darkness.

- *Non era lunga ancor la nostra via* ‘Not was long yet our way’ → “Our way was not yet far”: the subject moves to the beginning, and ‘long’ becomes “far”, expressing distance travelled rather than the length of the way.
- *di qua dal sonno* ‘on this side of sleep’ → “[since my slumber]”: the spatial expression becomes explicitly temporal, and the definite article becomes “my”.
- *ch’ emisperio di tenebre vincia* ‘which a hemisphere of darkness was conquering’ → “which conquered a hemisphere of the darkness”: the verb moves before its object; articles are supplied before “hemisphere” and “darkness”; the imperfect is rendered by a simple past.

> 70 Di lungi v’ eravamo ancora un poco,  
> 71 ma non sì, ch’ io non discernessi in parte,  
> 72 che orrevol gente possedea quel loco.  

We were still a little distant from it; yet not so distant, that I did not in part discern what honourable people occupied that place.

- *Di lungi v’ eravamo ancora un poco* ‘At a distance there we were still a little’ → “We were still a little distant from it”: the opening adverbial becomes a predicate adjective, the locative pronoun becomes “from it”, and “a little” moves before “distant”.
- *ma non sì* ‘but not so’ → “yet not so distant”: the adversative becomes “yet”, and “distant” is supplied.
- *ch’ io non discernessi in parte* ‘that I did not discern in part’ → “that I did not in part discern”: “in part” moves before the main verb, and the subjunctive becomes indicative.
- *che* ‘that’ → “what”: the translation treats the clause as an indirect question about which people occupied the place, rather than a statement that honourable people occupied it.
- *orrevol gente possedea quel loco* ‘honourable folk possessed that place’ → “honourable people occupied that place”: the singular collective *gente* becomes plural “people”, and possession is rendered as occupation.

> 73 “O tu, che onori ogni scienza ed arte,  
> 74 questi chi son, ch’ hanno cotanto orranza,  
> 75 che dal modo degli altri li diparte?”  

“O thou, that honourest every science and art; who are these, who have such honour, that it separates them from the manner of the rest?”

- *questi chi son* ‘these who are’ → “who are these”: the demonstrative moves after the verb.
- *cotanto orranza* ‘so much honour’ → “such honour”: the explicit expression of quantity becomes one of degree.
- *che dal modo degli altri li diparte* ‘that from the manner of the others them separates’ → “that it separates them from the manner of the rest”: the verb precedes its object and prepositional complement, rather than following them; ‘the others’ becomes the collective “the rest”.

> 76 E quegli a me: “L’onrata nominanza,  
> 77 che di lor suona su nella tua vita,  
> 78 grazia acquista nel ciel che sì gli avanza.”  

And he to me: “The honoured name, which sounds of them, up in that life of thine, gains favour in heaven which thus advances them.”

- *quegli* ‘that one’ → “he”: a demonstrative pronoun becomes a personal pronoun.
- *nominanza* ‘renown’ → “name”: reputation is expressed through “name”.
- *che di lor suona* ‘which of them sounds’ → “which sounds of them”: the prepositional phrase moves after the verb.
- *su nella tua vita* ‘up in thy life’ → “up in that life of thine”: “that” replaces the definite article, and the possessive becomes a phrase following the noun.
- *grazia acquista* ‘favour acquires’ → “gains favour”: the verb moves before its object, with “gains” for ‘acquires’.
- *nel ciel* ‘in the heaven’ → “in heaven”: the definite article is omitted.
- *che sì gli avanza* ‘which thus them advances’ → “which thus advances them”: the object pronoun moves after the verb.

> 79 Intanto voce fu per me udita:  

Meanwhile a voice was heard by me:

- *voce fu per me udita* ‘a voice was by me heard’ → “a voice was heard by me”: an indefinite article is supplied, and the agent phrase moves after the participle.

> 80 “Onorate l’ altissimo poeta;  

“Honour the great Poet!

- *altissimo* ‘highest’ → “great”: the superlative becomes a positive adjective, and height is rendered as greatness.

> 81 l’ombra sua torna, ch’ era dipartita.”  

His shade returns that was departed.”

- *l’ombra sua* ‘the shade his’ → “His shade”: the possessive moves before the noun, replacing the article.
- *era dipartita* ‘had departed’ → “was departed”: the translation retains an older English perfect construction with ‘be’, corresponding to the Italian auxiliary, rather than using modern English ‘have’.

> 82 Poichè la voce fu restata e queta,  
> 83 vidi quattro grand’ ombre a noi venire:  
> 84 sembianza avevan nè trista nè lieta.  

After the voice had paused, and was silent, I saw four great shadows come to us; they had an aspect neither sad nor joyful.

- *fu restata e queta* ‘had stopped and quiet’ → “had paused, and was silent”: stopping becomes pausing, and a separate copula is supplied for the adjective; quietness is rendered specifically as silence.
- *a noi venire* ‘to us come’ → “come to us”: the infinitive moves before its prepositional complement.
- *sembianza avevan* ‘aspect they had’ → “they had an aspect”: the object moves after the verb, and an indefinite article is supplied.

> 85 Lo buon Maestro cominciò a dire:  

The good Master began to speak:

- *cominciò a dire* ‘began to say’ → “began to speak”: “speak” replaces ‘say’, expressing the act of speaking without an object.

> 86 “Mira colui con quella spada in mano,  
> 87 che vien dinanzi a’ tre sì come sire:  

“Mark him with that sword in hand, who comes before the three as their lord:

- *Mira colui* ‘Look at that one’ → “Mark him”: looking becomes attentive observation, and the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun.
- *sì come sire* ‘just as a lord’ → “as their lord”: the emphasis of *sì* is not separately expressed, and “their” is supplied.

> 88 quegli è Omero poeta sovrano  

that is Homer, the sovereign Poet;

- *poeta sovrano* ‘poet sovereign’ → “the sovereign Poet”: the adjective moves before the noun, and a definite article is supplied.

> 89 l’altro è Orazio satiro, che viene,  

the next who comes is Horace the satirist;

- *l’altro è Orazio satiro, che viene* ‘the other is Horace satirist, who comes’ → “the next who comes is Horace the satirist”: ‘the other’ becomes “the next”, making succession explicit; a definite article is supplied before “satirist”; and the relative clause moves from the end of the Italian line to immediately after “the next”.

> 90 Ovidio è il terzo, e l’ ultimo Lucano.  

Ovid is the third, and the last is Lucan.

- *e l’ ultimo Lucano* ‘and the last Lucan’ → “and the last is Lucan”: the copula, left understood in Italian, is supplied.

## Lines 91–123 — Honoured Among the Great Spirits

> 91 Però che ciascun meco si conviene  
> 92 nel nome, che sonò la voce sola,  
> 93 fannomi onore, e di ciò fanno bene.”  

Because each agrees with me in the name, which the one voice sounded, they do me honour : and therein they do well.”

- *ciascun meco si conviene* ‘each with me agrees’ → “each agrees with me”: the phrase expressing agreement follows the verb; the Italian pronominal verb is rendered by the non-reflexive “agrees”.
- *che sonò la voce sola* ‘which sounded the single voice’ → “which the one voice sounded”: the subject precedes the verb instead of following it, and the adjective follows English order before its noun.
- *di ciò* ‘in this’ → “therein”: the preposition and demonstrative become a single adverb.

> 94 Così vidi adunar la bella scuola  
> 95 di quei signor dell’ altissimo canto,  
> 96 che sopra gli altri, com’ aquila, vola.  

Thus I saw assembled the goodly school of [those] lord[s] of highest song, [which], like an eagle, soars above the rest.

- *vidi adunar* ‘I saw gather’ → “I saw assembled”: the infinitive expressing the gathering becomes a past participle describing the assembled company.
- *bella* ‘beautiful’ → “goodly”: a different adjective renders the commendation.
- *dell’ altissimo canto* ‘of the highest song’ → “of highest song”: the definite article is omitted.
- *che sopra gli altri, com’ aquila, vola* ‘which above the others, like an eagle, flies’ → “[which], like an eagle, soars above the rest”: the comparison precedes the verb, while the phrase expressing relative height follows it; “soars” replaces ‘flies’, and “the rest” replaces ‘the others’.

> 97 Da ch’ ebber ragionato insieme alquanto,  
> 98 volsersi a me con salutevol cenno:  
> 99 e il mio maestro sorrise di tanto.  

After they had talked a space together, they turned to me with sign of salutation ; and my Master smiled thereat.

- *Da ch’* ‘From when’ → “After”: the temporal expression becomes a conjunction marking subsequent action.
- *ebber ragionato insieme alquanto* ‘they had talked together a little’ → “they had talked a space together”: the indefinite amount becomes a noun phrase expressing duration, and “together” follows it.
- *volsersi a me* ‘they turned themselves to me’ → “they turned to me”: the reflexive construction becomes an intransitive one.
- *con salutevol cenno* ‘with salutatory sign’ → “with sign of salutation”: the adjective becomes a noun introduced by “of” and follows “sign”.
- *di tanto* ‘at so much’ → “thereat”: the expression referring to the preceding action becomes a single adverb, without the Italian indication of amount.

> 100 E più d’ onore ancora assai mi fenno,  
> 101 ch’ esser mi fecer della loro schiera,  
> 102 sì ch’ io fui sesto tra cotanto senno.  

And greatly more besides they honoured me ; for they made me of their number, so that I was a sixth amid such intelligences.

- *E più d’ onore ancora assai mi fenno* ‘And much more honour still they did me’ → “And greatly more besides they honoured me”: ‘did honour’ becomes the verb “honoured”, with the indirect object becoming its direct object; ‘much’ becomes “greatly”, ‘still’ becomes “besides”, and the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *ch’* ‘in that’ → “for”: the explanatory subordinate connection becomes a coordinating explanation.
- *esser mi fecer della loro schiera* ‘to be me they made of their band’ → “they made me of their number”: the infinitive ‘to be’ is omitted, the object follows the verb, and membership of a ‘band’ is expressed as belonging to their “number”.
- *io fui sesto* ‘I was sixth’ → “I was a sixth”: the article makes the ordinal explicitly a designation of one member.
- *tra cotanto senno* ‘among so much wisdom’ → “amid such intelligences”: the singular abstract noun becomes a plural designation of persons, and the expression of quantity becomes “such”.

> 103 Così n’ andammo infino alla lumiera  
> 104 parlando cose, che il tacere è bello,  
> 105 sì com’ era il parlar colà dov’ era.  

Thus we went onwards to the light, speaking things which it is well to pass in silence, as it was well to speak there where I was.

- *n’ andammo* ‘we went away’ → “we went onwards”: the pronominal expression of departure is rendered as continued forward movement.
- *infino alla lumiera* ‘as far as the light’ → “to the light”: the explicit expression of the extent reached is reduced to “to”.
- *che il tacere è bello* ‘which to keep silent is beautiful’ → “which it is well to pass in silence”: English supplies anticipatory “it”; the substantival infinitive becomes “to pass in silence”, and ‘beautiful’ becomes the evaluative “well”.
- *sì com’ era il parlar* ‘just as was the speaking’ → “as it was well to speak”: the reinforcement ‘just’ is omitted; the substantival infinitive becomes “to speak”, with anticipatory “it”; and “well”, understood from the preceding Italian clause, is supplied explicitly.

> 106 Venimmo al piè d’ un nobile castello,  
> 107 sette volte cerchiato d’ alte mura,  
> 108 difeso intorno d’ un bel fiumicello.  

We came to the foot of a Noble Castle, seven times circled with lofty Walls, defended round by a fair Rivulet.

- *d’ alte mura* ‘by lofty walls’ → “with lofty Walls”: the preposition identifying what encircles the castle is rendered by “with” rather than ‘by’.

> 109 Questo passammo come terra dura;  
> 110 per sette porte intrai con questi savi;  
> 111 giugnemmo in prato di fresca verdura.  

This we passed as solid land ; through seven gates I entered with those sages ; we reached a meadow of fresh verdure.

- *terra dura* ‘land hard’ → “solid land”: the adjective precedes its noun, and hardness is rendered as solidity.
- *questi savi* ‘these wise men’ → “those sages”: the demonstrative changes from proximity to distance, and “sages” replaces ‘wise men’.
- *giugnemmo in prato* ‘we arrived in meadow’ → “we reached a meadow”: the intransitive verb with a prepositional complement becomes a transitive verb with a direct object; an indefinite article is supplied.

> 112 Genti v’ eran con occhi tardi e gravi,  
> 113 di grande autorità ne’ lor sembianti;  
> 114 parlavan rado, con voci soavi.  

On it were people with eyes slow and grave, of great authority in their appearance ; they spoke seldom, with mild voices.

- *Genti v’ eran* ‘People there were’ → “On it were people”: the locative pronoun becomes the more explicit “On it”, referring to the meadow, and the subject moves after the verb.
- *ne’ lor sembianti* ‘in their appearances’ → “in their appearance”: the plural noun becomes singular.
- *voci soavi* ‘voices sweet’ → “mild voices”: “mild” replaces ‘sweet’, and the adjective precedes its noun.

> 115 Traemmoci così dall’ un de’ canti  
> 116 in luogo aperto, luminoso, ed alto,  
> 117 sì che veder si potean tutti quanti.  

Thus we retired on one of the sides, into a place open, luminous, and high, so that they could all be seen.

- *Traemmoci così* ‘We drew ourselves thus’ → “Thus we retired”: the reflexive expression becomes the intransitive “retired”, and the adverb moves to the beginning.
- *dall’ un de’ canti* ‘from the one of the sides’ → “on one of the sides”: the Italian expression indicating withdrawal to one side is rendered with the locative “on”; the article before ‘one’ is omitted.
- *in luogo* ‘into place’ → “into a place”: an indefinite article is supplied.
- *veder si potean tutti quanti* ‘be seen could all of them’ → “they could all be seen”: the infinitive follows the finite verb instead of preceding it; the Italian passive construction with *si* becomes an English passive with “be”; and the postposed subject is distributed between “they” and “all”.

> 118 Colà diritto, sopra il verde smalto,  
> 119 mi fur mostrati gli spiriti magni,  
> 120 che del vederli in me stesso n’ esalto.  

There direct, upon the green enamel, were shewn to me the great spirits, [so that] I glory within myself [for] having seen [them].

- *mi fur mostrati gli spiriti magni* ‘to me were shown the spirits great’ → “were shewn to me the great spirits”: the indirect object follows the passive verb, and the adjective precedes its noun.
- *che* ‘that’ → “[so that]”: the connective is interpreted as introducing a consequence.
- *del vederli* ‘from seeing them’ → “[for] having seen [them]”: the simple infinitive becomes a perfect infinitive, explicitly marking the seeing as completed; the preposition becomes “for”, and the phrase moves after the main verb and its accompanying phrase.
- *in me stesso n’ esalto* ‘within myself I exult in it’ → “I glory within myself”: the verb precedes the phrase locating the feeling within the speaker; “glory” renders ‘exult’, and the pronominal *n’* has no separate English equivalent.

> 121 Io vidi Elettra con molti compagni,  
> 122 tra’ quai conobbi Ettore, ed Enea,  
> 123 Cesare armato con gli occhi grifagni.  

I saw Electra with many companions : amongst whom I knew both Hector and Æneas ; Cæsar armed, with the falcon eyes.

- *conobbi* ‘I recognized’ → “I knew”: recognition is rendered as knowing.
- *Ettore, ed Enea* ‘Hector and Aeneas’ → “both Hector and Æneas”: “both” is supplied.
- *con gli occhi grifagni* ‘with the eyes hawk-like’ → “with the falcon eyes”: the descriptive adjective becomes the attributive noun “falcon” and moves before “eyes”.

## Lines 124–151 — Distinguished Souls and Departure From Limbo

> 124 Vidi Cammilla e la Pentesilea  
> 125 dall’ altra parte, e vidi il re Latino,  
> 126 che con Lavinia sua figlia sedea.  

I saw Camilla and Penthesilea on the other hand, [and] saw the Latian king, sitting with Lavinia his daughter.

- *la Pentesilea* ‘the Penthesilea’ → “Penthesilea”: the article before the name is omitted.
- *dall’ altra parte* ‘on the other side’ → “on the other hand”: a spatial expression is rendered by a different idiom, here retaining its spatial sense.
- *il re Latino* ‘the king Latin’ → “the Latian king”: the adjective moves before the noun.
- *che con Lavinia sua figlia sedea* ‘who with Lavinia his daughter sat’ → “sitting with Lavinia his daughter”: the finite relative clause becomes a participial phrase, and the verb moves before the phrase expressing accompaniment.

> 127 Vidi quel Bruto che cacciò Tarquino,  
> 128 Lucrezia, Julia, Marzia e Corniglia,  
> 129 e solo in parte vidi il Saladino.  

I saw that Brutus who expelled the Tarquin; Lucretia, Julia, Martia, and Cornelia; and by himself apart, I saw the Saladin.

- *Tarquino* ‘Tarquin’ → “the Tarquin”: an article is supplied.
- *solo in parte* ‘alone in a place’ → “by himself apart”: the adjective becomes a prepositional phrase, and “apart” makes explicit the separation conveyed by *in parte*.

> 130 Poi che innalzai un poco più le ciglia,  
> 131 vidi il maestro di color che sanno,  
> 132 seder tra filosofica famiglia.  

When I raised my eyelids a little higher, I saw the Master of those that know, sitting amid a philosophic family.

- *Poi che* ‘After’ → “When”: the temporal conjunction expresses coincidence rather than explicitly marking succession.
- *un poco più* ‘a little more’ → “a little higher”: “higher” specifies the direction of the increase; the phrase also moves after the object.
- *le ciglia* ‘the eyebrows’ → “my eyelids”: a possessive replaces the article, and “eyelids” interprets the bodily feature named by *ciglia*.
- *seder* ‘to sit’ → “sitting”: the infinitive dependent on the verb of seeing becomes a present participle.
- *filosofica famiglia* ‘philosophic family’ → “a philosophic family”: an indefinite article is supplied.

> 133 Tutti lo miran, tutti onor gli fanno;  

All regard him; all do him honour;

- *Tutti lo miran* ‘All him regard’ → “All regard him”: the object pronoun moves after the verb.
- *tutti onor gli fanno* ‘all honour to him do’ → “all do him honour”: the verb precedes its objects, and the indirect object precedes the direct object.

> 134 quivi vid’ io Socrate e Platone,  
> 135 che innanzi agli altri più presso gli stanno;  

here I saw Socrates and Plato, who before the rest stand nearest to him;

- *quivi vid’ io* ‘there saw I’ → “here I saw”: “here” replaces ‘there’, and the explicit subject moves before the verb.
- *innanzi agli altri* ‘before the others’ → “before the rest”: a plural expression becomes a collective singular expression.
- *più presso gli stanno* ‘nearer to him stand’ → “stand nearest to him”: the verb moves forward, the pronoun follows the expression of proximity, and the comparative is rendered as a superlative.

> 136 Democrito, che il mondo a caso pone,  

Democritus, who ascribes the world to chance;

- *che il mondo a caso pone* ‘who the world to chance puts’ → “who ascribes the world to chance”: *pone* is interpreted as an attribution rather than a literal placing, and the verb moves before its object.

> 137 Diogenes, Anassagora e Tale,  
> 138 Empedocles, Eraclito e Zenone;  

Diogenes, Anaxagoras, and Thales; Empedocles, Heraclitus, and Zeno;

- Literal.

> 139 e vidi il buono accoglitor del quale,  
> 140 Dioscoride dico: e vidi Orfeo,  
> 141 Tullio, e Lino, e Seneca morale;  

and I saw the good collector of the qualities, Dioscorides I mean; and saw Orpheus, Tully, [Linus], and Seneca the moralist;

- *del quale* ‘of the quality’ → “of the qualities”: the substantival singular *quale*, denoting quality, becomes a plural noun.
- *Dioscoride dico* ‘Dioscorides I say’ → “Dioscorides I mean”: the verb of saying is rendered as an expression clarifying the intended reference.
- *e Lino* ‘and Linus’ → “[Linus]”: the conjunction is omitted.
- *Seneca morale* ‘Seneca moral’ → “Seneca the moralist”: the adjective becomes a noun in apposition, with an article supplied.

> 142 Euclide geometra, e Tolommeo,  
> 143 Ippocrate, Avicenna e Galieno,  
> 144 Averrois che il gran comento feo.  

Euclid the geometer, and Ptolemæus; Hippocrates, Avicenna, and Galen; Averrhoës, who made the great comment.

- *Euclide geometra* ‘Euclid geometer’ → “Euclid the geometer”: an article is supplied before the appositional noun.
- *il gran comento feo* ‘the great commentary made’ → “made the great comment”: the verb moves before its object; “comment” is used in the sense of ‘commentary’.

> 145 Io non posso ritrar di tutti appieno:  
> 146 però che sì mi caccia il lungo tema,  
> 147 che molte volte al fatto il dir vien meno.  

I may not paint them all in full: for the long theme so chases me, that many times the word comes short of the reality.

- *Io non posso* ‘I cannot’ → “I may not”: “may” replaces ‘can’ as the modal expressing ability.
- *ritrar di tutti* ‘portray of all’ → “paint them all”: the construction with *di* becomes a direct object, with “them” supplied; “paint” renders the act of portrayal.
- *appieno* ‘fully’ → “in full”: an adverb becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *però che* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *sì mi caccia il lungo tema* ‘so me chases the long theme’ → “the long theme so chases me”: the subject moves before the verb, and the object pronoun moves after it.
- *al fatto il dir vien meno* ‘to the fact the saying comes less’ → “the word comes short of the reality”: the opening prepositional phrase moves to the end; the substantival infinitive becomes “the word”; *fatto* is rendered as “reality”; and the idiom *venir meno* becomes “comes short”, with “of” replacing the Italian preposition.

> 148 La sesta compagnia in due si scema;  

The company of six diminishes to two;

- *La sesta compagnia* ‘The sixth company’ → “The company of six”: the ordinal adjective is interpreted as indicating a company numbering six, and becomes a following prepositional phrase.
- *in due si scema* ‘to two diminishes itself’ → “diminishes to two”: the pronominal verb becomes an ordinary English intransitive, and the phrase expressing the resulting number moves after it.

> 149 per altra via mi mena il savio duca,  
> 150 fuor della queta, nell’ aura che trema;  

by another road the sage guide leads me, out of the quiet, into the trembling air;

- *mi mena il savio duca* ‘me leads the sage guide’ → “the sage guide leads me”: the subject moves before the verb, and the object pronoun moves after it.
- *nell’ aura che trema* ‘into the air that trembles’ → “into the trembling air”: the relative clause becomes a participial modifier before the noun.

> 151 e vengo in parte, ove non è che luca.  

and I come to a part where there is nought that shines.

- *in parte* ‘into a place’ → “to a part”: the preposition is rendered as “to”, and *parte*, used for a place or region, is rendered as “part”.
- *ove non è che luca* ‘where there is not [anything] that shines’ → “where there is nought that shines”: “nought” explicitly supplies the negative indefinite left implicit in the Italian construction, and the subjunctive *luca* becomes an English indicative.

## Summary — Close Wording, Recast Syntax

Carlyle’s translation remains close to the Italian’s vocabulary and sequence of expression, but repeatedly rearranges its syntax into English prose order. Subjects and objects placed before or after the verb for emphasis move into more customary positions: *un greve tuono* becomes the opening subject, “A heavy thunder”, while *grazia acquista* becomes “gains favour”. Adjectives generally move before their nouns, and complements follow the words they qualify. Yet some Italian arrangements survive, notably “True is it” and “And he to me”; expressions such as “without hope we live in desire” also preserve the original wording and order closely.

Changes of construction often make the English less literal without greatly changing the sense. Finite clauses become participial phrases, as *che intese* becomes “understanding”; *vo’ che sappi* becomes “I wish thee to know”; and reflexive verbs are frequently rendered as ordinary intransitives. Elsewhere, the grammatical centre shifts: *la via lunga*, ‘the long way’, becomes “the length of way”, while *cotanto senno*, ‘so much wisdom’, becomes “such intelligences”. Italian subjunctives commonly become English indicatives, and negatives are sometimes redistributed, as *non … alcuna cosa* becomes “nothing”.

Word choice ranges from close verbal correspondence to interpretative substitution. *Guai* becomes “wailings”, emphasizing audible lamentation; *conforto* becomes “strength”; and *altissimo* becomes “great”, losing the explicit superlative. Connectives can likewise alter the emphasis: *se* becomes “when”, “though”, or “seeing”, replacing conditional wording with circumstance, concession, or cause. Alongside these departures, the translation retains strikingly literal expressions such as “depaints that pity” and “the green enamel”, as well as much of the Italian’s repetition and enumerative structure.

Omissions and additions are usually local. The second *così* in *e così mi fe’ entrare* disappears, as do *Or* before *vo’ che sappi* and the possessive in *Maestro mio*. Conversely, “own”, “both”, and the possessives in “our First Parent” and “their lord” make relationships or emphasis explicit. More generally, beyond the changes individually noted in the comparison, English supplies subject pronouns where Italian verb endings identify the subject, and omits Italian definite articles before possessives. Possessives also replace articles or personal clitics in expressions concerning bodily features. These routine adjustments coexist with a sustained effort to preserve the Italian’s phrasing, even where the resulting English remains archaic or unusual.
