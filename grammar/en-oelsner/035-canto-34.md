# Inferno 34

## Lines 1–18 — The Frozen Souls and the Creature

> 1 “ *Vexilla regis prodeunt inferni*  
> 2 verso di noi : però dinanzi mira,”  
> 3 disse il maestro mio, “ se tu il discerni.”  

“*Vexilla Regis prodeunt inferni* towards us: therefore look in front of thee,” my Master said, “if thou discernest him.”

- *dinanzi mira* ‘ahead look’ → “look in front of thee”: the verb precedes the adverbial expression, and “of thee” makes its reference explicit.
- *disse il maestro mio* ‘said the master mine’ → “my Master said”: the subject precedes the verb, and the possessive precedes the noun.
- *se tu il discerni* ‘if thou him discernest’ → “if thou discernest him”: the object pronoun follows the verb.

> 4 Come quando una grossa nebbia spira,  
> 5 o quando l’ emisperio nostro annotta  
> 6 par da lungi un molin che il vento gira :  

As, when a thick mist breathes, or when the night comes on our hemisphere, a mill, [which] the wind [turns], appears at distance:

- *l’ emisperio nostro annotta* ‘the hemisphere ours grows dark’ → “the night comes on our hemisphere”: the hemisphere ceases to be the subject; night becomes the subject of an added verb of coming, and the hemisphere becomes its prepositional complement. The possessive also moves before the noun.
- *par da lungi* ‘appears from afar’ → “appears at distance”: the verb and its adverbial expression move after the subject and its relative clause; distance is expressed with “at” rather than ‘from’.

> 7 veder mi parve un tal ’dificio allotta ;  

such an edifice did I now seem to see;

- *veder mi parve un tal ’dificio allotta* ‘to see to me seemed such an edifice then’ → “such an edifice did I now seem to see”: the impersonal construction becomes a personal one with “I” as subject; the object is brought to the front, and “did” supports the past-tense verb. The infinitive follows “seem” rather than preceding it, while ‘then’ becomes “now”.

> 8 poi per lo vento mi ristrinsi retro  
> 9 al duca mio, chè non li era altra grotta.  

and, for the wind, shrunk back behind my Guide, because no other shed was there.

- *poi* ‘then’ → “and”: temporal succession becomes coordination.
- *mi ristrinsi retro al duca mio* ‘I drew myself close behind the leader mine’ → “shrunk back behind my Guide”: the reflexive becomes an intransitive verb, with “back” reinforcing the backward movement; ‘leader’ becomes “Guide”, and the possessive precedes its noun.
- *non li era altra grotta* ‘not there was another cave’ → “no other shed was there”: verbal negation becomes the negative determiner “no”; the subject moves before the verb, and “there” moves after it. ‘Cave’ is rendered as “shed”, interpreting the noun as a shelter.

> 10 Già era, e con paura il metto in metro,  
> 11 là dove l’ ombre eran tutte coperte,  
> 12 e trasparean come festuca in vetro.  

Already I had come (and with fear I put it into verse) where the souls were [wholly] covered, and shone through like straw in glass.

- *Già era* ‘already I was’ → “Already I had come”: a state expressed in the imperfect becomes a completed arrival expressed in the pluperfect.
- *il metto in metro* ‘it I put into metre’ → “I put it into verse”: the object pronoun follows the verb, and ‘metre’ becomes the broader “verse”.
- *là dove* ‘there where’ → “where”: the separate locative ‘there’ is omitted.
- *l’ ombre* ‘the shades’ → “the souls”: the figurative designation is replaced by the beings it denotes.
- *eran tutte coperte* ‘were all covered’ → “were [wholly] covered”: “wholly” interprets *tutte* as expressing complete coverage of each soul, rather than simply saying that all the souls were covered.
- *trasparean* ‘were visible through’ → “shone through”: visibility through a medium is expressed as shining through it.
- *festuca* ‘a straw’ → “straw”: an individual stalk becomes an uncounted material noun.

> 13 Altre sono a giacere, altre stanno erte,  
> 14 quella col capo e quella con le piante ;  
> 15 altra, com’ arco, il volto a’ piedi inverte.  

Some [are] lying; some stand upright, this on its head, and that upon its soles; another, like a bow, bends face to feet.

- *Altre*, *altre* ‘others’, ‘others’ → “Some”, “some”: both occurrences are rendered as indefinite portions of the group rather than as ‘others’.
- *sono a giacere* ‘are at lying’ → “[are] lying”: the construction with *a* and an infinitive becomes an English progressive.
- *quella col capo e quella con le piante* ‘that with the head and that with the soles’ → “this on its head, and that upon its soles”: the first ‘that’ becomes “this”; ‘with’ becomes “on” or “upon”, making the supporting position explicit; possessives replace the definite articles.
- *il volto a’ piedi inverte* ‘the face to the feet inverts’ → “bends face to feet”: the verb moves before its object, ‘inverts’ becomes “bends”, and the definite articles before both body parts are omitted.

> 16 Quando noi fummo fatti tanto avante,  
> 17 ch’ al mio maestro piacque di mostrarmi  
> 18 la creatura ch’ ebbe il sembiante.  

When we had proceeded on so far, that it pleased my Guide to show to me the Creature which was once so fair,

- *noi fummo fatti tanto avante* ‘we were made so far forward’ → “we had proceeded on so far”: the idiomatic construction becomes an active verb of movement, and the Italian past historic becomes an English pluperfect.
- *al mio maestro piacque* ‘to my master it pleased’ → “it pleased my Guide”: the Italian indirect object becomes the direct object of English “pleased” and follows the verb; ‘master’ becomes “Guide”.
- *di mostrarmi* ‘to show me’ → “to show to me”: the attached indirect-object pronoun becomes a separate prepositional phrase.
- *ch’ ebbe il sembiante* ‘which had the appearance’ → “which was once so fair”: possession of an appearance becomes a description with “was” and an adjective. “Fair” reflects the usual reading *ch’ebbe il bel sembiante*, rather than the supplied Italian, which lacks *bel*; “once” makes the past-time contrast explicit, and “so” adds degree.

## Lines 19–51 — The Three Faces and Wings of Dis

> 19 dinanzi mi si tolse, e fe' restarmi,  
> 20 “Ecco Dite,” dicendo, “ed ecco il loco,  
> 21 ove convien che di fortezza t' armi.”  

he took himself from before me, and made me stop, saying: “Lo Dis! and lo the place where it behoves thee arm thyself with fortitude.”

- *dinanzi mi si tolse* ‘from before me he removed himself’ → “he took himself from before me”: the phrase indicating position moves after the verb; ‘removed himself’ becomes “took himself”.
- *fe' restarmi* ‘made me remain’ → “made me stop”: *restare* is interpreted as stopping rather than remaining.
- *Ecco Dite, dicendo, ed ecco il loco* ‘Lo Dis, saying, and lo the place’ → “saying: “Lo Dis! and lo the place”: the participle moves from after the first quoted announcement to before the entire speech.
- *ove convien che di fortezza t'armi* ‘where it is fitting that with fortitude thou arm thyself’ → “where it behoves thee arm thyself with fortitude”: the clause introduced by *che*, with its subjunctive verb, becomes an infinitive construction after “behoves thee”; the phrase “with fortitude” moves to the end.

> 22 Com' io divenni allor gelato e fioco,  
> 23 nol domandar, lettor, ch' io non lo scrivo,  
> 24 però ch' ogni parlar sarebbe poco.  

How icy chill and hoarse I then became, ask not, O Reader! for I write it not, because all speech would fail to tell.

- *Com' io divenni allor gelato e fioco* ‘how I became then frozen and faint’ → “How icy chill and hoarse I then became”: the predicate adjectives move before the subject and verb, and “then” precedes “became”; ‘frozen’ becomes “icy chill”, while “hoarse” selects a specifically vocal sense of *fioco*.
- *nol domandar* ‘not it ask’ → “ask not”: the object pronoun contained in *nol* is omitted, and the negative follows the imperative.
- *lettor* ‘reader’ → “O Reader”: the vocative marker “O” is supplied.
- *io non lo scrivo* ‘I not it write’ → “I write it not”: both the object pronoun and the negative move after the verb.
- *ogni parlar sarebbe poco* ‘every speaking would be little’ → “all speech would fail to tell”: the substantival infinitive becomes “speech”, with “all” replacing distributive ‘every’; the predicate ‘would be little’ is expanded into an expression of inability, “would fail to tell”.

> 25 Io non morii, e non rimasi vivo:  

I did not die, and did not remain alive:

- *non morii, e non rimasi vivo* ‘died not, and remained not alive’ → “did not die, and did not remain alive”: English forms both past-tense negatives with auxiliary “did” and infinitives rather than directly negating inflected past-tense verbs.

> 26 pensa oramai per te, s' hai fior d' ingegno,  
> 27 qual io divenni, d' uno e d' altro privo.  

now think for thyself, if thou hast any grain of ingenuity, what I became, deprived of both *death and life*.

- *pensa oramai per te* ‘think now for thyself’ → “now think for thyself”: the temporal adverb moves before the imperative.
- *fior d'ingegno* ‘a flower of ingenuity’ → “any grain of ingenuity”: the figurative ‘flower’, expressing the slightest amount, becomes “grain”, and “any” is supplied.
- *d'uno e d'altro privo* ‘of the one and of the other deprived’ → “deprived of both *death and life*”: the adjective moves before its complement; the two pronouns are replaced by explicit nouns, and ‘the one and the other’ becomes “both”.

> 28 Lo imperador del doloroso regno  
> 29 da mezzo il petto uscia fuor della ghiaccia;  

The Emperor of the dolorous realm, from mid breast stood forth out of the ice;

- *da mezzo il petto* ‘from the middle of the breast’ → “from mid breast”: the expression identifying the middle of the breast is compressed into “mid breast”.
- *uscia fuor della ghiaccia* ‘was coming out of the ice’ → “stood forth out of the ice”: the imperfect of a verb of emergence is rendered as a static description with “stood forth”.

> 30 e più con un gigante io mi convegno  
> 31 che i giganti non fan con le sue braccia:  

and I *in size* am liker to a giant, than the giants are to his arms:

- *e più con un gigante io mi convegno* ‘and more with a giant I correspond’ → “and I *in size* am liker to a giant”: the pronominal verb becomes the copular expression “am liker”, incorporating *più* into the comparative adjective; ‘with’ becomes “to”, the complement moves after the subject and verb, and “*in size*” is supplied.
- *che i giganti non fan con le sue braccia* ‘than the giants do not with his arms’ → “than the giants are to his arms”: the pleonastic negative in the comparison is omitted; *fan*, standing for the preceding verb, becomes “are” to match the English construction, and ‘with’ becomes “to”.

> 32 vedi oramai quant' esser dee quel tutto  
> 33 ch' a così fatte parti si confaccia.  

mark now how great that whole must be, which corresponds to such a part.

- *vedi oramai* ‘see now’ → “mark now”: the visual imperative is rendered as an instruction to take notice.
- *quant' esser dee quel tutto* ‘how great must be that whole’ → “how great that whole must be”: the subject moves before the verbal group, and the modal precedes the infinitive.
- *ch'a così fatte parti si confaccia* ‘that to parts so fashioned may correspond’ → “which corresponds to such a part”: the complement moves after the verb; plural ‘parts’ becomes singular “a part”, ‘so fashioned’ is compressed to “such”, and the subjunctive becomes an English indicative.

> 34 S' ei fu sì bel com' egli è ora brutto  
> 35 e contra il suo Fattore alzò le ciglia,  
> 36 ben dee da lui procedere ogni lutto.  

If he was once as beautiful as he is ugly now, and lifted up his brows against his Maker, well may all affliction come from him.

- *fu* ‘was’ → “was once”: “once” is supplied.
- *è ora brutto* ‘is now ugly’ → “is ugly now”: the temporal adverb moves after the adjective.
- *contra il suo Fattore alzò le ciglia* ‘against his Maker lifted the brows’ → “lifted up his brows against his Maker”: the prepositional phrase moves after the verb and object; the article before *ciglia* becomes the possessive “his”.
- *ben dee da lui procedere ogni lutto* ‘well must from him proceed every grief’ → “well may all affliction come from him”: “may” softens the necessity expressed by *dee*; ‘proceed’ becomes “come”, and ‘every grief’ becomes the collective expression “all affliction”. The subject moves before the main verb, and the phrase indicating origin moves to the end.

> 37 O quanto parve a me gran maraviglia,  
> 38 quando vidi tre facce alla sua testa!  

Oh how great a marvel seemed it to me, when I saw three faces on his head!

- *quanto parve a me gran maraviglia* ‘how much it seemed to me a great marvel’ → “how great a marvel seemed it to me”: the degree expressed by *quanto* is attached directly to “great”, and the predicate noun phrase moves before the verb; the resulting English construction inverts “seemed” and “it”.
- *alla sua testa* ‘at his head’ → “on his head”: the preposition is rendered as “on” rather than ‘at’.

> 39 L' una dinanzi, e quella era vermiglia;  

The one in front, and it was fiery red;

- *quella* ‘that one’ → “it”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun.
- *vermiglia* ‘vermilion’ → “fiery red”: the colour term is paraphrased with “red” and the intensifying adjective “fiery”.

> 40 l' altre eran due, che s' aggiungieno a questa  
> 41 sopr' esso il mezzo di ciascuna spalla,  
> 42 e si giungieno al loco della cresta;  

the others were two, that were adjoined to this, above the very middle of each shoulder; and they were joined [at] his crest;

- *s'aggiungieno* ‘joined themselves’ → “were adjoined”: the pronominal construction becomes a passive.
- *si giungieno* ‘joined themselves’ → “they were joined”: the pronominal construction, here expressing mutual joining, becomes a passive.
- *al loco della cresta* ‘at the place of the crest’ → “[at] his crest”: ‘the place of’ is omitted, and the definite article before ‘crest’ becomes the possessive “his”.

> 43 e la destra parea tra bianca e gialla;  

and the right seemed between white and yellow;

- Literal.

> 44 la sinistra a vedere era tal, quali  
> 45 vengon di là onde il Nilo s' avvalla.  

the left was such to look on, as they who come from where the Nile [descends].

- *a vedere era tal* ‘to see was such’ → “was such to look on”: the infinitive phrase moves after the predicate, and ‘to see’ becomes “to look on”.
- *quali vengon* ‘such as come’ → “as they who come”: the compressed comparative-relative construction is expanded with the pronouns “they who”.
- *di là onde* ‘from there whence’ → “from where”: the separate indication ‘there’ is omitted, and the expression is compressed into a single phrase.
- *s'avvalla* ‘lowers itself’ → “[descends]”: the pronominal verb becomes a simple intransitive verb.

> 46 Sotto ciascuna uscivan due grandi ali,  
> 47 quanto si convenia a tanto uccello:  
> 48 vele di mar non vid' io mai cotali.  

Under each there issued forth two mighty wings, of size befitting such a bird: sea-sails I never saw so broad.

- *uscivan* ‘came out’ → “there issued forth”: a more formal verbal expression replaces ‘came out’, and introductory “there” is supplied.
- *grandi* ‘great’ → “mighty”: the adjective of size becomes one also suggesting power or impressiveness.
- *quanto si convenia a tanto uccello* ‘as much as befitted so great a bird’ → “of size befitting such a bird”: the degree construction becomes a phrase explicitly naming “size”; the finite impersonal verb becomes the participle “befitting”, and ‘so great a bird’ becomes “such a bird”.
- *vele di mar* ‘sails of the sea’ → “sea-sails”: the noun and its prepositional modifier become a compound noun.
- *non vid' io mai cotali* ‘never saw I such’ → “I never saw so broad”: the subject moves before the verb; ‘such’ is interpreted specifically in terms of breadth and expanded into “so broad”.

> 49 Non avean penne, ma di vipistrello  
> 50 era lor modo; e quelle svolazzava,  
> 51 sì che tre venti si movean da ello.  

No plumes had they; but were in form *and texture* like a bat’s: and he was flapping them, so that three winds went forth from him.

- *Non avean penne* ‘they had not plumes’ → “No plumes had they”: negation of the verb becomes the determiner “No” modifying the noun; the object is placed first, followed by verb and subject.
- *ma di vipistrello era lor modo* ‘but of a bat was their manner’ → “but were in form *and texture* like a bat’s”: *modo* is interpreted as physical “form”, with “*and texture*” supplied. The Italian subject ‘their manner’ becomes a prepositional complement, while the wings become the understood subject, changing singular ‘was’ to plural “were”; the possessive relationship becomes an explicit comparison with “like”, and *lor* is no longer separately expressed.
- *e quelle svolazzava* ‘and those he was fluttering’ → “and he was flapping them”: the demonstrative object becomes the personal pronoun “them” and moves after the verb; ‘fluttering’ becomes “flapping”.
- *si movean da ello* ‘moved themselves from him’ → “went forth from him”: the pronominal verb becomes the intransitive expression “went forth”, making outward movement explicit.

## Lines 52–84 — Lucifer’s Victims and the Climb Out

> 52 Quindi Cocito tutto s' aggelava;  

Thereby Cocytus all was frozen;

- *Quindi* ‘from this’ → “Thereby”: the causal connection is expressed as a means or agency.
- *s' aggelava* ‘was freezing’ → “was frozen”: the Italian imperfect of a reflexive-form intransitive verb becomes a passive construction, emphasizing the resulting condition.

> 53 con sei occhi piangeva, e per tre menti  
> 54 gocciava il pianto e sanguinosa bava.  

with six eyes he wept, and down three chins gushed tears and bloody foam.

- *per tre menti* ‘along three chins’ → “down three chins”: the direction of movement is made explicit.
- *gocciava* ‘dripped’ → “gushed”: the translation substitutes a stronger, more copious flow.
- *il pianto* ‘the weeping’ → “tears”: the action becomes its physical product, with a change from singular to plural and omission of the article.
- *sanguinosa bava* ‘bloody slaver’ → “bloody foam”: the translation emphasizes the frothy character of the saliva.

> 55 Da ogni bocca dirompea coi denti  
> 56 un peccatore, a guisa di maciulla,  
> 57 sì che tre ne facea così dolenti.  

In every mouth he champed a sinner with his teeth, like a brake; so that he thus kept three of them in torment.

- *Da ogni bocca* ‘From every mouth’ → “In every mouth”: the preposition of origin becomes one of location.
- *dirompea coi denti un peccatore* ‘he tore apart with the teeth a sinner’ → “he champed a sinner with his teeth”: tearing apart becomes chewing or crushing; the object moves before the instrumental phrase, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *a guisa di maciulla* ‘in the manner of a flax-brake’ → “like a brake”: the comparative phrase is compressed, and the implement is named without explicitly identifying its use.
- *tre ne facea così dolenti* ‘three of them he made thus suffering’ → “he thus kept three of them in torment”: making them suffer becomes maintaining them in a condition; the adjective becomes a prepositional phrase, and the object and adverb are reordered.

> 58 A quel dinanzi il mordere era nulla  
> 59 verso il graffiar, chè talvolta la schiena  
> 60 rimanea della pelle tutta brulla.  

To the one in front, the biting was nought, compared with the tearing: for at times the back of him remained quite stript of skin.

- *verso il graffiar* ‘beside the scratching’ → “compared with the tearing”: the comparison is made explicit, and scratching becomes the stronger “tearing”.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *la schiena* ‘the back’ → “the back of him”: an explicit possessor is added.
- *della pelle tutta brulla* ‘of the skin entirely bare’ → “quite stript of skin”: the complement moves after the adjective, the article is omitted, and bare becomes the participial “stript”, suggesting the action that produced the condition.

> 61 “Quell' anima lassù che ha maggior pena,”  
> 62 disse il maestro, “è Giuda Scariotto,  
> 63 che il capo ha dentro, e fuor le gambe mena.  

“That soul up there, which suffers greatest punishment,” said the Master, “is Judas Iscariot, he who has his head within, and outside plies his legs.

- *ha maggior pena* ‘has greater punishment’ → “suffers greatest punishment”: possession becomes suffering, and the comparative is interpreted as a relative superlative.
- *che il capo ha dentro* ‘who the head has within’ → “he who has his head within”: “he” is added before the relative clause, the object moves after the verb, and the article becomes a possessive.
- *e fuor le gambe mena* ‘and outside the legs moves’ → “and outside plies his legs”: the verb moves before its object, the article becomes a possessive, and “plies” gives the movement a repeated, active character.

> 64 Degli altri due ch' hanno il capo di sotto,  
> 65 quei che pende dal nero ceffo è Bruto:  
> 66 vedi come si storce, e non fa motto;  

Of the other two, who have their heads beneath, that one, who hangs from the black visage is Brutus: see how he writhes himself, and utters not a word;

- *il capo* ‘the head’ → “their heads”: the distributive singular becomes plural, and the article becomes a possessive.
- *ceffo* ‘muzzle’ → “visage”: the disparaging or animalizing noun becomes a more neutral, elevated word for a face.
- *si storce* ‘himself he twists’ → “he writhes himself”: the reflexive pronoun moves after the verb, and “writhes” emphasizes contorted movement.
- *non fa motto* ‘makes no utterance’ → “utters not a word”: the noun expressing utterance is recast as a verb with “a word” as its object.

> 67 e l' altro è Cassio, che par sì membruto.  

and the other is Cassius, who seems so stark of limb.

- *membruto* ‘large-limbed’ → “stark of limb”: the adjective becomes an adjectival phrase, emphasizing strength rather than the size of the limbs.

> 68 Ma la notte risurge; ed oramai  
> 69 è da partir, chè tutto avem veduto.”  

But night is reascending; and now must we depart: for we have seen the whole.”

- *la notte* ‘the night’ → “night”: the definite article is omitted.
- *risurge* ‘rises again’ → “is reascending”: the simple present becomes progressive.
- *ed oramai è da partir* ‘and by now it is necessary to depart’ → “and now must we depart”: the impersonal construction becomes a personal clause with “we” and the modal “must”; “now” compresses the temporal force of *oramai*.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *tutto avem veduto* ‘everything we have seen’ → “we have seen the whole”: the object moves after the verb, and the pronoun becomes a nominal phrase.

> 70 Com' a lui piacque, il collo gli avvinghiai;  

As he desired, I clasped his neck;

- *Com' a lui piacque* ‘As it pleased him’ → “As he desired”: the person pleased becomes the subject of a verb of wishing.
- *il collo gli avvinghiai* ‘the neck to him I clasped’ → “I clasped his neck”: the object moves after the verb, and the possessive dative becomes the possessive determiner “his”.

> 71 ed ei prese di tempo e loco poste,  
> 72 e, quando l' ale furo aperte assai,  
> 73 appigliò sè alle vellute coste;  

and he took opportunity of time and place; and when the wings were opened far, applied him to the shaggy sides,

- *prese di tempo e loco poste* ‘took of time and place positions’ → “took opportunity of time and place”: the plural noun is interpreted as a suitable opportunity and becomes singular; its qualifying phrase moves after it.
- *assai* ‘sufficiently’ → “far”: sufficient opening is expressed as the extent of the opening.
- *appigliò sè* ‘fastened himself’ → “applied him”: fastening becomes the more general act of placing or attaching, and the ordinary object pronoun is used reflexively.
- *coste* ‘ribs’ → “sides”: the anatomical term is rendered by the broader bodily region.

> 74 di vello in vello giù discese poscia  
> 75 tra il folto pelo e le gelate croste.  

and then from shag to shag descended down, between the tangled hair and frozen crusts.

- *di vello in vello giù discese poscia* ‘from fleece to fleece down he descended afterwards’ → “and then from shag to shag descended down”: “and” is supplied, the temporal adverb moves to the beginning, and “down” follows the verb; “shag” renders the hair as successive patches or tufts.
- *folto pelo* ‘thick hair’ → “tangled hair”: density becomes entanglement.

> 76 Quando noi fummo là dove la coscia  
> 77 si volge appunto in sul grosso dell' anche,  
> 78 lo duca con fatica e con angoscia  
> 79 volse la testa ov' egli avea le zanche,  

When we had come to where the thigh revolves just on the swelling of the haunch, my Guide with labour and with difficulty turned his head where he had had his feet before,

- *Quando noi fummo là dove* ‘When we were there where’ → “When we had come to where”: being at a place becomes arrival there, the simple past becomes pluperfect, and the location is expressed as a destination.
- *si volge* ‘turns’ → “revolves”: the bend or turn is expressed as revolving.
- *in sul grosso dell' anche* ‘on the thick part of the haunches’ → “on the swelling of the haunch”: the substantivized adjective becomes “swelling”, and the plural becomes singular.
- *lo duca* ‘the guide’ → “my Guide”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *angoscia* ‘anguish’ → “difficulty”: distress is rendered as practical or physical difficulty.
- *la testa* ‘the head’ → “his head”: the article becomes a possessive.
- *ov' egli avea le zanche* ‘where he had the legs’ → “where he had had his feet before”: the imperfect becomes pluperfect, “before” makes the temporal relationship explicit, legs become feet, and the article becomes a possessive.

> 80 ed aggrappossi al pel come uom che sale,  
> 81 sì che in inferno io credea tornar anche.  

and grappled on the hair, as one who mounts; so that I thought we were returning into Hell again.

- *aggrappossi al pel* ‘clung to the hair’ → “grappled on the hair”: the reflexive-form verb becomes an English intransitive verb, with “on” replacing the complement introduced by ‘to’.
- *come uom* ‘as a man’ → “as one”: the noun is replaced by an indefinite pronoun.
- *in inferno io credea tornar anche* ‘into hell I believed to return again’ → “I thought we were returning into Hell again”: the infinitive becomes a finite past-progressive clause, its understood singular subject becomes explicit plural “we”, and the directional phrase moves after the verb.

> 82 “Attienti ben, chè per sì fatte scale,”  
> 83 disse il maestro ansando com' uom lasso,  
> 84 “conviensi dipartir da tanto male.”  

“Hold thee fast! for by such stairs,” said my Guide, panting like a man forespent, “must we depart from so much ill.”

- *Attienti ben* ‘Hold yourself well’ → “Hold thee fast!”: “fast” gives the adverb the specific sense of holding securely, and the ordinary object pronoun is used reflexively.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *per sì fatte scale* ‘by stairs so made’ → “by such stairs”: the participial description is compressed into “such” and placed before the noun.
- *il maestro* ‘the master’ → “my Guide”: the designation changes, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *lasso* ‘weary’ → “forespent”: the translation uses an archaic adjective conveying exhaustion.
- *conviensi dipartir* ‘it is necessary to depart’ → “must we depart”: an impersonal construction becomes a personal clause with an explicit plural subject and the modal “must”.

## Lines 85–117 — Passing the Centre of the Earth

> 85 Poi uscì fuor per lo foro d' un sasso,  
> 86 e pose me in su l'orlo a sedere;  
> 87 appresso porse a me l' accorto passo.  

Thereafter through the opening of a rock he issued forth, and put me on its brim to sit; then towards me he stretched his wary step.

- *per lo foro d'un sasso* ‘through the hole of a rock’ → “through the opening of a rock”: ‘hole’ becomes “opening”, and the phrase precedes rather than follows the verb.
- *uscì fuor* ‘went out’ → “he issued forth”: the ordinary verb of going out becomes the more formal “issued forth”.
- *pose me in su l'orlo a sedere* ‘placed me on the edge to sit’ → “put me on its brim to sit”: the definite article becomes the possessive “its”, and ‘edge’ becomes “brim”.
- *porse a me l'accorto passo* ‘extended to me the cautious step’ → “towards me he stretched his wary step”: the directional phrase moves before the verb; “towards” replaces ‘to’, and “his” replaces the definite article.

> 88 Io levai gli occhi, e credetti vedere  
> 89 Lucifero com' io l' avea lasciato,  
> 90 e vidili le gambe in su tenere;  

I raised my eyes, and thought to see Lucifer as I had left him; and saw him with the legs turned upwards;

- *Io levai gli occhi* ‘I raised the eyes’ → “I raised my eyes”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *credetti vedere* ‘believed to see’ → “thought to see”: belief is expressed as a thought or impression.
- *com'io l'avea lasciato* ‘as I him had left’ → “as I had left him”: the object pronoun follows the verb instead of preceding it.
- *vidili le gambe in su tenere* ‘saw him hold the legs upwards’ → “saw him with the legs turned upwards”: the infinitive construction becomes a phrase introduced by “with”; active ‘hold’ becomes the passive participle “turned”.

> 91 e s' io divenni allora travagliato,  
> 92 la gente grossa il pensi, che non vede  
> 93 qual è quel punto ch' io avea passato.  

and the gross people who see not what [that] point [is] which I had passed, [let them] judge if I grew [perplexed] then.

- *s'io divenni allora travagliato* ‘if I became then troubled’ → “if I grew [perplexed] then”: the clause moves from the beginning to the end of the sentence, and the time adverb moves to its end. “Grew” replaces ‘became’, while “[perplexed]” interprets the trouble specifically as bewilderment.
- *la gente grossa* ‘the people coarse’ → “the gross people”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *il pensi* ‘let it think it’ → “[let them] judge”: the singular subjunctive referring to the collective *gente* becomes a plural imperative construction. The anticipatory object *il*, referring to the clause beginning *s'io*, disappears, and ‘think’ becomes “judge”.
- *che non vede* ‘that does not see’ → “who see not”: the singular verb becomes plural, and negation follows the lexical verb rather than preceding it.
- *qual è quel punto* ‘what is that point’ → “what [that] point [is]”: the copula moves after the subject, giving the English order for an indirect question.

> 94 “Levati su,” disse il maestro, “in piede:  
> 95 la via è lunga e il cammino è malvagio,  
> 96 e già il sole a mezza terza riede.”  

“Rise up!” said the Master, “upon thy feet: the way is long, and difficult the road; and [already] to middle tierce the Sun returns.”

- *Levati su* ‘Raise yourself up’ → “Rise up!”: a reflexive verb becomes an intransitive one.
- *in piede* ‘on foot’ → “upon thy feet”: the singular becomes plural, and the possessive “thy” is supplied.
- *e il cammino è malvagio* ‘and the road is bad’ → “and difficult the road”: ‘bad’ is interpreted as “difficult”; the predicate adjective moves before the subject, and the copula is omitted.
- *a mezza terza* ‘to half tierce’ → “to middle tierce”: “middle” replaces ‘half’, and the phrase moves before the subject.

> 97 Non era caminata di palagio  
> 98 là 'v' eravam, ma natural burella,  
> 99 ch' avea mal suolo e di lume disagio.  

It was no palace-hall, there where we stood, but natural dungeon with an evil floor and want of light.

- *Non era* ‘Was not’ → “It was no”: an impersonal “It” is supplied, and verbal negation becomes the determiner “no” before the noun.
- *caminata di palagio* ‘hall of a palace’ → “palace-hall”: the noun and its dependent phrase become a compound noun.
- *là 'v'eravam* ‘there where we were’ → “there where we stood”: the verb of being becomes a verb specifying posture.
- *natural burella* ‘natural underground passage’ → “natural dungeon”: “dungeon” interprets the kind of enclosed underground space denoted by *burella*.
- *ch'avea* ‘which had’ → “with”: a finite relative clause becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *mal suolo* ‘bad ground’ → “an evil floor”: an indefinite article is supplied; ‘ground’ becomes “floor”, and ‘bad’ becomes “evil”.
- *di lume disagio* ‘of light shortage’ → “want of light”: the noun precedes rather than follows its dependent phrase.

> 100 “Prima ch' io dell' abisso mi divella,  
> 101 maestro mio,” diss' io quando fui dritto,  
> 102 “a trarmi d' erro un poco mi favella.  

“Before I pluck myself from the Abyss,” said I when risen up, “O Master! speak to me a little, to draw me out of error.

- *Prima ch'io dell'abisso mi divella* ‘Before that I from the abyss myself tear away’ → “Before I pluck myself from the Abyss,”: *prima che* becomes the single conjunction “Before”; the reflexive pronoun and the phrase expressing separation move after the verb. ‘Tear away’ becomes “pluck”, and the Italian subjunctive is rendered by an English present form without a subjunctive distinction.
- *maestro mio* ‘master my’ → “O Master!”: the possessive is omitted, “O” is supplied, and the address moves after the reporting clause.
- *quando fui dritto* ‘when I was upright’ → “when risen up”: the finite clause is reduced to a participial expression, and the state of being upright becomes the result of rising.
- *a trarmi d'erro* ‘to draw me from error’ → “to draw me out of error”: “out of” replaces ‘from’, and the purpose phrase moves after the request it qualifies.
- *un poco mi favella* ‘a little to me speak’ → “speak to me a little”: the verb moves before both the indirect object and the expression of extent.

> 103 Ov' è la ghiaccia? e questi com' è fitto  
> 104 sì sottosopra? e come in sì poc' ora  
> 105 da sera a mane ha fatto il sol tragitto? ”  

Where is the ice? and this, how is he fixed thus upside down? and how, in so short a time, has the Sun from eve to morn made transit?”

- *e questi com'è fitto sì sottosopra* ‘and this one how is fixed so upside down’ → “and this, how is he fixed thus upside down?”: the personal demonstrative becomes “this” and is resumed by “he” in the question; “thus” renders *sì* as an expression of manner.
- *in sì poc'ora* ‘in so little time’ → “in so short a time”: quantity becomes duration expressed by “short”, and an indefinite article is supplied.
- *da sera a mane ha fatto il sol tragitto* ‘from evening to morning has made the sun passage’ → “has the Sun from eve to morn made transit?”: the subject moves between the auxiliary and participle, and the temporal phrase moves after the subject. ‘Passage’ becomes the more formal “transit”.

> 106 Ed egli a me: “Tu immagini ancora  
> 107 d' esser di là dal centro, ov' io m' appresi  
> 108 al pel del vermo reo, che il mondo fora.  

And he to me: “Thou imaginest that thou art still upon the other side of the centre, where I caught hold on the hair of the evil Worm which pierces through the world.

- *Tu immagini ancora d'esser* ‘Thou imaginest still to be’ → “Thou imaginest that thou art still”: the infinitive becomes a finite clause with its own subject; “still” moves from the main clause into that subordinate clause.
- *di là dal centro* ‘beyond the centre’ → “upon the other side of the centre”: the spatial expression is expanded into an explicit phrase about the “other side”.
- *ov'io m'appresi al pel* ‘where I attached myself to the hair’ → “where I caught hold on the hair”: the reflexive construction becomes the non-reflexive idiom “caught hold”, with “on” replacing ‘to’.
- *del vermo reo* ‘of the worm wicked’ → “of the evil Worm”: the adjective precedes the noun, and ‘wicked’ becomes “evil”.
- *che il mondo fora* ‘which the world pierces’ → “which pierces through the world”: the verb moves before its object, and “through” is supplied.

> 109 Di là fosti cotanto, quant' io scesi;  

Thou wast on that side, so long as I descended;

- *Di là fosti* ‘On that side thou wast’ → “Thou wast on that side”: the spatial phrase moves after the subject and verb.
- *cotanto, quant'io scesi* ‘as much as I descended’ → “so long as I descended”: the correlative expression is interpreted explicitly as duration rather than extent.

> 110 quando mi volsi, tu passasti il punto  
> 111 al qual si traggon d' ogni parte i pesi;  

when I turned myself, thou *then* didst pass the point to which all gravities from every part are drawn;

- *quando mi volsi* ‘when myself I turned’ → “when I turned myself”: the reflexive pronoun follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *tu passasti il punto* ‘thou passedst the point’ → “thou *then* didst pass the point”: the simple past becomes a construction with “didst”, and the emphatic “*then*” is supplied.
- *al qual si traggon d'ogni parte i pesi* ‘to which are drawn from every part the weights’ → “to which all gravities from every part are drawn”: the subject moves before the spatial phrase and verb; the definite article becomes “all”, and the concrete ‘weights’ are rendered by the abstract “gravities”.

> 112 e se' or sotto l' emisperio giunto,  
> 113 ch' è contrapposto a quel che la gran secca  
> 114 coperchia, e sotto il cui colmo consunto  
> 115 fu l' uom che nacque e visse senza pecca;  

and now thou art arrived beneath the hemisphere opposed to that which canopies the great dry *land*, and underneath whose summit was consumed the Man, who without sin was born and lived;

- *e se' or sotto l'emisperio giunto* ‘and art now beneath the hemisphere arrived’ → “and now thou art arrived beneath the hemisphere”: “now” moves before the subject and auxiliary, while the participle moves before the spatial phrase.
- *ch'è contrapposto a quel* ‘which is set opposite to that’ → “opposed to that”: the finite relative clause becomes a participial phrase, and ‘set opposite’ becomes “opposed”.
- *che la gran secca coperchia* ‘which the great dry expanse covers’ → “which canopies the great dry *land*”: the translation takes *che*, rather than *la gran secca*, as the subject of *coperchia*, making the dry land what is covered. ‘Covers’ becomes “canopies”, and the substantivized adjective *secca* is expanded with the explicit noun “*land*”.
- *e sotto il cui colmo* ‘and beneath the summit of which’ → “and underneath whose summit”: the possessive relative becomes “whose”, placed before the noun.
- *consunto fu l'uom* ‘consumed was the man’ → “was consumed the Man”: the auxiliary moves before the participle.
- *che nacque e visse senza pecca* ‘who was born and lived without fault’ → “who without sin was born and lived”: the final qualifying phrase moves before both verbs, and ‘fault’ is rendered specifically as “sin”. The single verb *nacque* is expressed by the English construction “was born”.

> 116 tu hai li piedi in su picciola spera,  
> 117 che l' altra faccia fa della Giudecca.  

thou hast thy feet upon a little sphere, which forms the other face of the Judecca.

- *tu hai li piedi* ‘thou hast the feet’ → “thou hast thy feet”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *in su picciola spera* ‘upon little sphere’ → “upon a little sphere”: an indefinite article is supplied.
- *che l'altra faccia fa della Giudecca* ‘which the other face makes of the Judecca’ → “which forms the other face of the Judecca”: the verb moves before the object, bringing the noun and its dependent phrase together; ‘makes’ becomes “forms”.

## Lines 118–139 — The Return to the Stars

> 118 Qui è da man, quando di là è sera;  

Here it is morn, when it is evening there;

- *è da man* ‘it is in the morning’ → “it is morn”: the prepositional expression becomes a predicate noun.
- *quando di là è sera* ‘when over there it is evening’ → “when it is evening there”: the place expression follows the verb and is reduced to “there”.

> 119 e questi, che ne fe’ scala col pelo,  
> 120 fitto è ancora, sì come prima era.  

and this Fiend, who made a ladder for us with his hair, is still fixed as he was before.

- *questi* ‘this one’ → “this Fiend”: the demonstrative pronoun becomes a demonstrative with an explicitly supplied noun.
- *ne fe’ scala* ‘for us made a ladder’ → “made a ladder for us”: the expression of benefit follows the verb and its object.
- *col pelo* ‘with the hair’ → “with his hair”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *fitto è ancora* ‘fixed is still’ → “is still fixed”: the participle follows the verb and adverb.
- *sì come* ‘so as’ → “as”: the correlative *sì* is not separately rendered.
- *prima era* ‘before he was’ → “he was before”: the temporal adverb follows the verb.

> 121 Da questa parte cadde giù dal cielo;  

On this side fell he down from Heaven;

- *Da questa parte* ‘From this side’ → “On this side”: a preposition of location replaces *da*, literally ‘from’.

> 122 e la terra, che pria di qua si sporse,  
> 123 per paura di lui fe’ del mar velo,  

and the land, which erst stood out here, through fear of him veiled itself with sea,

- *di qua* ‘on this side’ → “here”: the spatial phrase becomes a single adverb, placed after “stood out”.
- *si sporse* ‘projected itself’ → “stood out”: the reflexive construction becomes an intransitive expression.
- *fe’ del mar velo* ‘made of the sea a veil’ → “veiled itself with sea”: the noun *velo* becomes the verb “veiled”, replacing *fe’*; “itself” is supplied, and the sea becomes the instrument of veiling rather than the material of a veil.

> 124 e venne all’ emisperio nostro ; e forse  
> 125 per fuggir lui lasciò qui il loco voto  
> 126 quella che appar di qua, e su ricorse.”  

and came to our hemisphere; and perhaps, in order to escape from him, that which on this side appears left here the empty space, and upwards rushed."

- *all’ emisperio nostro* ‘to the hemisphere our’ → “to our hemisphere”: the possessive precedes the noun.
- *per fuggir lui* ‘to flee him’ → “in order to escape from him”: the purpose construction is expanded, and the direct object of *fuggir* becomes a prepositional complement.
- *lasciò qui il loco voto / quella che appar di qua* ‘left here the place empty that which appears on this side’ → “that which on this side appears left here the empty space”: the subject moves before the main verb; the place expression moves before “appears”; “space” renders *loco*, and *voto*, describing the condition in which the place was left, becomes an attributive adjective.
- *e su ricorse* ‘and upward ran back’ → “and upwards rushed”: “rushed” replaces ‘ran back’, emphasizing rapid movement without separately expressing the return implied by *ri-*.

> 127 Luogo è laggiù da Belzebù remoto  
> 128 tanto, quanto la tomba si distende,  
> 129 che non per vista, ma per suono è noto  
> 130 d’ un ruscelletto, che quivi discende  

Down there, from Beelzebub as far removed as his tomb extends, is a space, not known by sight but by the sound of a rivulet descending in it,

- *Luogo è laggiù* ‘A place is down there’ → “Down there,” … “is a space”: the place expression moves to the front, the subject follows the verb, and “space” renders *Luogo*.
- *da Belzebù remoto / tanto, quanto* ‘from Beelzebub removed so far as’ → “from Beelzebub as far removed as”: the comparative expression precedes “removed”, and the whole modifier is placed before “is a space”.
- *la tomba* ‘the tomb’ → “his tomb”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *si distende* ‘stretches itself’ → “extends”: the reflexive construction becomes intransitive.
- *che non per vista, ma per suono è noto* ‘which not by sight but by sound is known’ → “not known by sight but by the sound”: the finite relative clause becomes a participial modifier; “known” moves before the contrasting prepositional phrases, and an article is supplied before “sound”.
- *che quivi discende* ‘which there descends’ → “descending in it”: the relative clause becomes a participial phrase, while the adverb ‘there’ becomes an explicit prepositional reference to the space.

> 131 per la buca d’ un sasso, ch’ egli ha roso  
> 132 col corso ch’ egli avvolge e poco pende.  

along the hollow of a rock which it has eaten out with tortuous course and slow declivity.

- *per la buca* ‘through the hole’ → “along the hollow”: “along” replaces ‘through’, and “hollow” replaces ‘hole’.
- *ch’ egli ha roso* ‘which it has gnawed’ → “which it has eaten out”: the verb becomes a phrasal expression that explicitly conveys the formation of a hollow.
- *col corso ch’ egli avvolge e poco pende* ‘with the course that it winds, and slopes little’ → “with tortuous course and slow declivity”: the finite descriptions become the adjective “tortuous” and the noun phrase “slow declivity”; the article before *corso* disappears, and the small degree of slope is expressed by “slow” qualifying “declivity”.

> 133 Lo duca ed io per quel cammino ascoso  
> 134 entrammo a ritornar nel chiaro mondo;  

The Guide and I entered by that hidden road, to return into the bright world;

- *per quel cammino ascoso / entrammo* ‘by that road hidden we entered’ → “entered by that hidden road”: the verb moves before the expression of route, and the adjective precedes its noun.

> 135 e senza cura aver d’ alcun riposo  
> 136 salimmo suso, ei primo ed io secondo,  

and, without caring for any rest, we mounted up, he first and I second,

- *senza cura aver d’ alcun riposo* ‘without care having for any rest’ → “without caring for any rest”: the noun *cura* and infinitive *aver* become the single gerund “caring”, removing the Italian noun-before-verb order.

> 137 tanto ch’ io vidi delle cose belle  
> 138 che porta il ciel, per un pertugio tondo;  

so far that I distinguished through a round opening the beauteous things which Heaven bears;

- *tanto ch’* ‘so much that’ → “so far that”: the expression of degree is rendered explicitly as spatial extent.
- *io vidi* ‘I saw’ → “I distinguished”: simple seeing becomes the more specific act of making things out.
- *delle cose belle* ‘some of the things beautiful’ → “the beauteous things”: the partitive construction becomes a definite noun phrase, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *che porta il ciel* ‘which bears the heaven’ → “which Heaven bears”: the subject moves before the verb, and its definite article is omitted.
- *per un pertugio tondo* ‘through an opening round’ → “through a round opening”: the adjective precedes the noun, and the whole phrase moves before the object of “distinguished”.

> 139 e quindi uscimmo a riveder le stelle.  

and thence we issued out, again to see the Stars.

- *a riveder* ‘to see again’ → “again to see”: the repetition expressed by the prefix *ri-* becomes a separate adverb placed before the infinitive.

## Summary — Close Correspondence and Syntactic Reshaping

The translation generally follows the Italian phrase by phrase, preserving many images, comparisons, and verbal repetitions, but frequently rearranges their components. Objects and complements placed before Italian verbs usually follow them in English: *se tu il discerni* becomes “if thou discernest him”, and *che il mondo fora* becomes “which pierces through the world”. Adjectives and possessives commonly move before their nouns. Yet Carlyle also retains or introduces marked inversions, as in “No plumes had they”, so that the prose does not consistently adopt ordinary English word order.

Changes of construction often go beyond rearrangement. Impersonal expressions become personal clauses: *veder mi parve* becomes “did I now seem to see”, while *conviensi dipartir* becomes “must we depart”. Reflexive or pronominal verbs may become intransitive verbs or passives; finite relative clauses are sometimes compressed into participial or prepositional phrases, as *ch’ avea* becomes “with”. Elsewhere an infinitive expands into a finite clause, as *io credea tornar* becomes “I thought we were returning”. Tense and aspect also shift: *Già era*, ‘already I was’, becomes “Already I had come”, making arrival explicit rather than merely stating presence.

Vocabulary ranges from close equivalents to interpretive substitutions. *Ombre*, ‘shades’, becomes “souls”; *grotta*, ‘cave’, becomes “shed”; and *angoscia*, ‘anguish’, becomes “difficulty”. Some choices intensify the physical description: *gocciava*, ‘dripped’, becomes “gushed”, and *graffiar*, ‘scratching’, becomes “tearing”. Others specify a quality left more general in Italian, as *cotali*, ‘such’, becomes “so broad”. Nevertheless, many concrete details remain closely matched, including the straw-and-glass comparison, the colours of the faces, and the repeated distinctions between positions and directions.

Supplements clarify references or make implications explicit: “in size” identifies the basis of a comparison, “death and life” replaces *d’uno e d’altro*, and “this Fiend” supplies a noun for *questi*. Conversely, separate elements disappear when English compresses an expression: *là dove* becomes “where”, and *al loco della cresta* becomes “[at] his crest”. More generally, beyond the individual changes recorded above, English regularly supplies subject pronouns where Italian verb endings identify the subject, and omits Italian articles before possessives. Such routine adjustments coexist with freer choices, producing a translation close in sequence and detail but repeatedly reshaped in syntax, emphasis, and explicitness.
