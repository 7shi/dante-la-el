# Inferno 25

## Lines 1–21 — The Thief’s Defiance and Restraint

> 1 Al fine delle sue parole il ladro  
> 2 le mani alzò con ambedue le fiche,  

At the conclusion of his words, the thief raised up his hands with both the figs,

- *Al fine* ‘At the end’ → “At the conclusion”: “conclusion” replaces ‘end’.
- *il ladro le mani alzò* ‘the thief his hands raised’ → “the thief raised up his hands”: the object moves after the verb; the definite article becomes a possessive, and “up” supplements “raised”.

> 3 gridando : “Togli, Dio, chè a te le squadro.”  

shouting: “Take them, God, for at thee I aim them!”

- *Togli* ‘Take’ → “Take them”: the object is supplied.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *a te le squadro* ‘at thee them I square’ → “at thee I aim them”: the verb is rendered idiomatically as “aim”, and the object pronoun moves after it.

> 4 Da indi in qua mi fur le serpi amiche,  
> 5 perch’ una gli s’ avvolse allora al collo,  
> 6 come dicesse : “Io non vo’ che più diche”:  

From this time forth the serpents were my friends; for one of them then coiled itself about his neck, as if saying: “Thou shalt speak no farther!”

- *Da indi in qua* ‘From then until now’ → “From this time forth”: the Italian looks from that moment up to the narrator’s present; the English looks forward from that moment.
- *mi fur le serpi amiche* ‘to me were the serpents friends’ → “the serpents were my friends”: the subject moves before the verb, and the dative pronoun becomes a possessive.
- *perch’* ‘because’ → “for”: subordination becomes coordination.
- *una* ‘one’ → “one of them”: the partitive phrase is supplied.
- *gli s’ avvolse allora al collo* ‘to him coiled itself then at the neck’ → “then coiled itself about his neck”: the dative becomes a possessive; “about” replaces ‘at’, and the time adverb moves before the verb.
- *come dicesse* ‘as if it were saying’ → “as if saying”: the finite imperfect subjunctive becomes a participle.
- *Io non vo’ che più diche* ‘I do not wish that thou speak further’ → “Thou shalt speak no farther”: the speaker’s unwillingness, expressed through a subordinate subjunctive clause, becomes a direct prohibition with “shalt”; the negative moves into that prohibition.

> 7 ed un’ altra alle braccia, e rilegollo,  
> 8 ribadendo sè stessa sì dinanzi,  
> 9 che non potea con esse dare un crollo.  

and another about his arms; and it tied him again, rivetting itself in front so firmly, that he could not give a jog with them.

- *alle braccia* ‘at the arms’ → “about his arms”: the preposition expresses encirclement, and the article becomes a possessive.
- *rilegollo* ‘rebound him’ → “it tied him again”: the prefixed verb is expanded into a verb and the adverb “again”.
- *sì dinanzi* ‘so in front’ → “in front so firmly”: the order is reversed, and “firmly” is supplied to make the degree of fastening explicit.
- *con esse dare un crollo* ‘with them give a jog’ → “give a jog with them”: the prepositional phrase moves after the verb and its object.

> 10 Ahi Pistoia, Pistoia, che non stanzi  
> 11 d’ incenerarti, sì che più non duri,  
> 12 poi che in mal far lo seme tuo avanzi!  

Ah, Pistoia! Pistoia! why dost thou not decree to turn thyself to ashes, that thou mayest endure no longer since thou outgoest thy seed in evil-doing?

- *che non stanzi d’ incenerarti* ‘why dost thou not decree to incinerate thyself’ → “why dost thou not decree to turn thyself to ashes”: the single verb *incenerarti* is expanded into “turn thyself to ashes”.
- *sì che più non duri* ‘so that thou last no more’ → “that thou mayest endure no longer”: *sì* is not separately rendered; “mayest” expresses the subjunctive in a purpose clause, and the negative expression moves after the verb.
- *in mal far lo seme tuo avanzi* ‘in evil-doing thy seed thou surpassest’ → “thou outgoest thy seed in evil-doing”: the verb precedes its object, the possessive precedes its noun, and the initial phrase moves to the end; “outgoest” renders ‘surpassest’.

> 13 Per tutti i cerchi dell’ inferno oscuri  
> 14 non vidi spirto in Dio tanto superbo,  
> 15 non quel che cadde a Tebe giù da’ muri.  

Through all the dark circles of Hell, I saw no spirit against God so proud, not _even_ him who fell at Thebes down from the walls.

- *Per tutti i cerchi dell’ inferno oscuri* ‘Through all the circles of Hell dark’ → “Through all the dark circles of Hell”: the adjective moves before its noun.
- *non vidi spirto* ‘I did not see a spirit’ → “I saw no spirit”: negation moves from the verb to the noun phrase.
- *in Dio* ‘toward God’ → “against God”: the English makes the hostile direction of the pride explicit.
- *non quel* ‘not that one’ → “not _even_ him”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun, and “_even_” is supplied.

> 16 Ei si fuggì, che non parlò più verbo ;  

He fled, speaking not another word;

- *Ei si fuggì* ‘He took himself off’ → “He fled”: the pronominal verb becomes a simple intransitive verb.
- *che non parlò più verbo* ‘so that he spoke no more word’ → “speaking not another word”: the finite clause introduced by *che* becomes a participial phrase; ‘no more word’ is recast as “not another word”.

> 17 ed io vidi un Centauro pien di rabbia  
> 18 venir chiamando : “Ov’è, ov’è l’ acerbo ?”  

and I saw a Centaur, full of rage, come crying: “Where is, where is the surly one?”

- *chiamando* ‘calling’ → “crying”: the English chooses a more forceful verb of utterance.
- *l’ acerbo* ‘the bitter one’ → “the surly one”: the adjective is interpreted as describing disposition rather than rendered by its primary sense; “one” makes the substantival use explicit.

> 19 Maremma non cred’ io che tante n’ abbia,  
> 20 quante bisce egli avea su per la groppa,  
> 21 infin dove comincia nostra labbia.  

Maremma, I do believe, has not so many snakes as he had on his haunch, to where our human form begins.

- *Maremma non cred’ io che tante n’ abbia, quante bisce egli avea* ‘Maremma, I do not believe that it has so many of them, as snakes he had’ → “Maremma, I do believe, has not so many snakes as he had”: the negative moves from ‘believe’ to “has”, and the belief clause becomes parenthetical, with emphatic “do”; *bisce* moves into the first half of the comparison, absorbing the reference expressed by *n’*, while *quante* becomes “as”.
- *su per la groppa* ‘up along the rump’ → “on his haunch”: the directional phrase becomes a preposition of position, the article becomes a possessive, and “haunch” replaces ‘rump’.
- *infin dove comincia nostra labbia* ‘as far as where begins our appearance’ → “to where our human form begins”: the limit is expressed more compactly by “to where”; the subject moves before the verb, and *labbia* is interpreted as “form”, with “human” supplied.

## Lines 22–54 — Cacus and the Six-Footed Serpent

> 22 Sopra le spalle, dietro dalla coppa,  
> 23 con l'ale aperte gli giacea un draco;  

Over his shoulders, behind the head, a dragon lay with outstretched wings;

- *Sopra le spalle … gli* ‘over the shoulders … on him’ → “Over his shoulders”: the relation expressed by the pronoun is transferred to the possessive “his”.
- *dietro dalla coppa* ‘behind the nape’ → “behind the head”: the specific part of the head becomes the head generally.
- *con l'ale aperte* ‘with the wings open’ → “with outstretched wings”: “outstretched” interprets ‘open’; the article is omitted, the adjective precedes its noun, and the phrase moves after the subject and verb.
- *giacea un draco* ‘lay a dragon’ → “a dragon lay”: the subject moves before the verb.

> 24 e quello affoca qualunque s'intoppa.  

and it sets on fire every one he meets.

- *quello* ‘that one’ → “it”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun.
- *affoca* ‘ignites’ → “sets on fire”: a single verb becomes a verbal phrase.
- *qualunque s'intoppa* ‘whoever is encountered’ → “every one he meets”: the indefinite relative becomes an antecedent followed by a relative clause; the pronominal construction is rendered actively, with “he” identifying the person encountering others.

> 25 Lo mio maestro disse: "Quegli è Caco,  
> 26 che sotto il sasso di monte Aventino  
> 27 di sangue fece spesse volte laco.  

My Master said: "That is Cacus, who, beneath the rock of Mount Aventine, full often made a lake of blood.

- *di sangue fece spesse volte laco* ‘of blood made many times a lake’ → “full often made a lake of blood”: the material phrase moves after “lake”, and the frequency expression moves before “made”; ‘many times’ becomes the emphatic adverbial phrase “full often”.

> 28 Non va co' suoi fratei per un cammino,  
> 29 per lo furar frodolente che fece  
> 30 del grande armento, ch'egli ebbe a vicino:  

He goes not with his brethren on one same road, because of the cunning theft he made [of] the great herd that lay near him:

- *per un cammino* ‘along one road’ → “on one same road”: the preposition of passage becomes “on”, and “same” makes explicit the interpretation of *un* as a shared road.
- *per lo furar frodolente* ‘because of the fraudulent stealing’ → “because of the cunning theft”: the substantival infinitive becomes the noun “theft”; ‘fraudulent’ is rendered as “cunning”, and the adjective moves before its noun.
- *che fece* ‘that he did’ → “he made”: the relative pronoun is omitted, and *fare* is rendered with “made” rather than ‘did’.
- *ch'egli ebbe a vicino* ‘which he had as a neighbour’ → “that lay near him”: the herd becomes the subject of the relative clause, while its relation to Cacus becomes a phrase of location; *ebbe*, ‘had’, is replaced by “lay”.

> 31 onde cessàr le sue opere biece  
> 32 sotto la mazza d'Ercole, che forse  
> 33 gliene diè cento, e non sentì le diece."  

whence his crooked actions ceased beneath the club of Hercules, who gave him perhaps a hundred _blows_ with it; and he felt not the _first ten._"

- *cessàr le sue opere biece* ‘ceased his actions crooked’ → “his crooked actions ceased”: the subject moves before the verb, and the adjective before its noun.
- *che forse gliene diè cento* ‘who perhaps gave him a hundred of them’ → “who gave him perhaps a hundred _blows_ with it”: “perhaps” moves after the verb and indirect object; the pronominal *ne*, ‘of them’, is replaced by the explicit noun “_blows_”, and “with it” supplies an instrumental phrase.
- *e non sentì le diece* ‘and he did not feel the ten’ → “and he felt not the _first ten._”: “_first ten._” supplies an interpretation of which ten are meant, and the negative follows the verb rather than preceding it.

> 34 Mentre che sì parlava, ed ei trascorse,  
> 35 e tre spiriti venner sotto noi,  
> 36 de' quai nè io nè il duca mio s'accorse,  
> 37 se non quando gridar: "Chi siete voi?"  

Whilst he thus spake, the Centaur ran past, and also under us there came three spirits, whom neither I nor my Guide perceived, until they cried: "Who are ye?"

- *Mentre che sì parlava* ‘whilst thus he was speaking’ → “Whilst he thus spake”: the imperfect’s ongoing action is rendered by a simple past, and “thus” follows the expressed subject.
- *ed ei* ‘and he’ → “the Centaur”: the conjunction is omitted, and the pronoun is replaced by an identifying noun.
- *e tre spiriti venner sotto noi* ‘and three spirits came under us’ → “and also under us there came three spirits”: “also” and presentational “there” are supplied; the location moves before the verb, and the subject after it.
- *de' quai nè io nè il duca mio s'accorse* ‘of whom neither I nor the guide mine became aware’ → “whom neither I nor my Guide perceived”: *accorgersi di*, ‘become aware of’, becomes the transitive “perceived”, so the relative pronoun is a direct object rather than the complement of a preposition; the possessive moves before “Guide”.
- *se non quando gridar* ‘except when they cried’ → “until they cried”: an exception expressed by ‘except when’ becomes a temporal limit.

> 38 Per che nostra novella si ristette,  
> 39 ed intendemmo pure ad essi poi.  

Our story therefore paused, and we then gave heed to them alone.

- *Per che nostra novella si ristette* ‘wherefore our conversation stopped’ → “Our story therefore paused”: the connective moves inside the clause; *novella*, here the ongoing conversation, is rendered as “story”, and the pronominal verb becomes the intransitive “paused”, suggesting a temporary interruption.
- *ed intendemmo pure ad essi poi* ‘and we attended only to them then’ → “and we then gave heed to them alone”: the single verb becomes “gave heed”; “then” moves before the verbal phrase, and the restriction expressed by *pure* moves after “them”.

> 40 Io non gli conoscea; ma ei seguette,  
> 41 come suol seguitar per alcun caso,  
> 42 che l'un nomare un altro convenette,  

I knew them not; but it happened, as usually it happens by some chance, that one had to name another,

- *Io non gli conoscea* ‘I not them knew’ → “I knew them not”: both the object pronoun and the negative move after the verb.
- *come suol seguitar* ‘as it is accustomed to happen’ → “as usually it happens”: the habitual verb *suole* with an infinitive becomes the adverb “usually” with a finite verb.
- *che l'un nomare un altro convenette* ‘that it was necessary for the one to name another’ → “that one had to name another”: the impersonal construction of necessity becomes the personal “one had to”; the infinitive follows that verbal phrase rather than preceding *convenette*, and ‘the one’ becomes “one”.

> 43 dicendo: "Cianfa dove fia rimaso?"  

saying: "Where has Cianfa stopt?"

- *Cianfa dove fia rimaso* ‘Cianfa, where will he have remained?’ → “Where has Cianfa stopt?”: the interrogative moves to the beginning, with the subject following the auxiliary; the conjectural future perfect becomes a present perfect, and ‘remained’ becomes “stopt”.

> 44 Per ch'io, acciocchè il duca stesse attento,  
> 45 mi posi il dito su dal mento al naso.  

Whereat I, in order that my Guide might stand attentive, placed my finger upwards from the chin to the nose.

- *Per ch'* ‘wherefore’ → “Whereat”: a connective expressing consequence is rendered as a response to the preceding words.
- *il duca stesse attento* ‘the guide might remain attentive’ → “my Guide might stand attentive”: the definite article becomes the possessive “my”, and *stesse* is rendered as “stand” rather than ‘remain’.
- *mi posi il dito* ‘I placed the finger to myself’ → “placed my finger”: the reflexive dative marking the finger’s relation to the speaker becomes the possessive “my”.

> 46 Se tu sei or, lettore, a creder lento  
> 47 ciò ch'io dirò, non sarà maraviglia,  
> 48 chè io, che il vidi, appena il mi consento.  

If thou art now, O Reader, slow to credit what I have to tell, it will be no wonder: for I who saw it, scarce allow it to myself.

- *lettore* ‘reader’ → “O Reader”: the vocative particle “O” is supplied.
- *a creder lento* ‘to believe slow’ → “slow to credit”: the adjective moves before its infinitival complement, and ‘believe’ becomes “credit”.
- *ciò ch'io dirò* ‘that which I shall say’ → “what I have to tell”: ‘that which’ contracts to “what”; the future becomes “have to tell”, presenting the forthcoming statement as something the speaker has to relate.
- *non sarà maraviglia* ‘it will not be a wonder’ → “it will be no wonder”: negation moves from the verb to the noun phrase.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating explanatory conjunction.
- *che il vidi* ‘who it saw’ → “who saw it”: the object pronoun moves after the verb.
- *appena il mi consento* ‘scarcely it to myself I allow’ → “scarce allow it to myself”: the object pronouns move after the verb, and the indirect object becomes the prepositional phrase “to myself”.

> 49 Com'io tenea levate in lor le ciglia,  
> 50 ed un serpente con sei piè si lancia  
> 51 dinanzi all'uno, e tutto a lui s'appiglia.  

Whilst I kept gazing on them, lo! a serpent with six feet darts up in front of one, and fastens itself all upon him.

- *Com'io tenea levate in lor le ciglia* ‘as I held my eyebrows raised towards them’ → “Whilst I kept gazing on them”: the physical expression involving raised eyebrows becomes the verbal expression “kept gazing”; maintained position becomes continued action, and the eyebrows are no longer explicitly mentioned.
- *ed* ‘and’ → “lo!”: the conjunction introducing the sudden event becomes an exclamation directing attention to it.
- *si lancia* ‘throws itself’ → “darts up”: the reflexive verb becomes an intransitive verb, and “up” supplies a direction.
- *dinanzi all'uno* ‘in front of the one’ → “in front of one”: the definite expression becomes indefinite.
- *e tutto a lui s'appiglia* ‘and wholly to him attaches itself’ → “and fastens itself all upon him”: “all” and the phrase referring to the man move after the verb; attachment ‘to’ him is expressed as fastening “upon” him.

> 52 Coi piè di mezzo gli avvinse la pancia,  
> 53 e con gli anterior le braccia prese;  

With its middle feet it clasped his belly, with the anterior it seized his arms;

- *Coi piè di mezzo* ‘with the feet of the middle’ → “With its middle feet”: the article becomes the possessive “its”, and the postposed prepositional modifier becomes the adjective “middle” before the noun.
- *gli avvinse la pancia* ‘to him clasped the belly’ → “it clasped his belly”: the dative pronoun becomes the possessive “his”.
- *e con gli anterior* ‘and with the anterior ones’ → “with the anterior”: the conjunction is omitted, and “the anterior” retains the Italian’s elliptical reference to the feet.
- *le braccia prese* ‘the arms it seized’ → “it seized his arms”: the object moves after the verb, and the article becomes the possessive “his”.

> 54 poi gli addentò e l'una e l'altra guancia.  

then fixed its teeth in both his cheeks.

- *gli addentò e l'una e l'altra guancia* ‘to him bit both the one and the other cheek’ → “fixed its teeth in both his cheeks”: the single verb meaning ‘bit’ is expanded into a phrase explicitly naming the teeth and expressing their lodgement; the dative becomes “his”, and the correlated singular expressions become “both” with a plural noun.

## Lines 55–87 — Two Bodies Merge, a Serpent Strikes

> 55 Gli diretani alle cosce distese,  
> 56 e miseli la coda tr’ambe e due,  
> 57 e dietro per le ren su la ritese.  

The hinder *feet* it stretched along his thighs; and put its tail between the two, and bent it upwards on his loins behind.

- *Gli diretani* ‘the hind ones’ → “The hinder *feet*”: the substantivized adjective is expanded by supplying the noun “feet”.
- *alle cosce* ‘to the thighs’ → “along his thighs”: the preposition expresses extension along rather than direction towards; the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *e miseli la coda* ‘and put the tail to him’ → “and put its tail”: the personal dative *-li* is not separately rendered, and the definite article becomes a possessive referring to the creature.
- *tr’ambe e due* ‘between both two’ → “between the two”: the emphatic expression for both is reduced to the ordinary definite numeral.
- *e dietro per le ren su la ritese* ‘and behind along the loins upward it stretched again’ → “and bent it upwards on his loins behind”: “bent” replaces stretching or extending again; the object pronoun follows the verb, the adverbial expressions are reordered, and “on his loins” replaces ‘along the loins’, supplying a possessive.

> 58 Ellera abbarbicata mai non fue  
> 59 ad arbor sì, come l’orribil fiera  
> 60 per l’altrui membra avviticchiò le sue;  

Ivy was never so rooted to a tree, as round the other's limbs the hideous monster entwined its own;

- *Ellera abbarbicata mai non fue / ad arbor sì* ‘Ivy rooted never was to a tree so’ → “Ivy was never so rooted to a tree”: the verb precedes its participial complement, and “so” moves from the end of the clause to immediately before “rooted”.
- *l’orribil fiera / per l’altrui membra avviticchiò le sue* ‘the horrible beast round the other's limbs entwined its own’ → “round the other's limbs the hideous monster entwined its own”: both the adjective and the noun describing the creature are replaced by stronger descriptions of monstrous appearance; the phrase “round the other's limbs” moves from after the subject to before it.

> 61 poi s’appiccar, come di calda cera  
> 62 fossero stati, e mischiâr lor colore;  
> 63 nè l’un nè l’altro già parea quel ch’era:  

then they stuck together, as if they had been of heated wax, and mingled their colours; neither the one, nor the other, now seemed what it *was at first*:

- *poi s’appiccar* ‘then they attached themselves’ → “then they stuck together”: the pronominal construction becomes an intransitive verb with “together”, making the reciprocal attachment explicit.
- *come di calda cera / fossero stati* ‘as if of hot wax they had been’ → “as if they had been of heated wax”: the material phrase moves after the verb, and “heated” presents the wax as having undergone heating rather than simply being hot.
- *lor colore* ‘their colour’ → “their colours”: the singular noun becomes plural.
- *già* ‘already’ → “now”: the translation gives present result rather than explicit anteriority.
- *quel ch’era* ‘that which it was’ → “what it *was at first*”: the demonstrative and relative become “what”, and “at first” is supplied.

> 64 come procede innanzi dall’ardore  
> 65 per lo papiro suso un color bruno,  
> 66 che non è nero ancora, e il bianco more.  

as up before the flame on paper, goes a brown colour which is not yet black, and the white dies away.

- *come procede innanzi dall’ardore / per lo papiro suso* ‘as advances ahead of the heat along the paper upward’ → “as up before the flame on paper, goes”: the final directional adverb moves to the front; “goes” replaces ‘advances’, “flame” replaces ‘heat’, and ‘along the paper’ becomes “on paper”, without the definite article.
- *un color bruno* ‘a colour brown’ → “a brown colour”: the adjective moves before the noun.
- *che non è nero ancora* ‘which is not black yet’ → “which is not yet black”: the adverb moves before the adjective.
- *more* ‘dies’ → “dies away”: the added particle emphasizes gradual disappearance.

> 67 Gli altri due riguardavano, e ciascuno  
> 68 gridava: “O me, Agnèl, come ti muti!  
> 69 vedi che già non sei nè due nè uno.”  

The other two looked on, and each cried: "O me! Agnello, how thou changest! lo, thou art already neither two nor one!"

- *Gli altri due riguardavano, e ciascuno / gridava* ‘The other two were watching, and each was crying’ → “The other two looked on, and each cried”: the Italian imperfects become English simple pasts, without expressly marking their continuing aspect; watching is rendered by the phrasal verb “looked on”.
- *come ti muti* ‘how you change yourself’ → “how thou changest”: the reflexive construction becomes an intransitive verb.
- *vedi che* ‘see that’ → “lo”: the imperative verb and its subordinate-clause conjunction become an exclamation.
- *già non sei nè due nè uno* ‘already you are neither two nor one’ → “thou art already neither two nor one”: “already” moves from before the verb to after it.

> 70 Già eran li due capi un divenuti,  
> 71 quando n’apparver due figure miste  
> 72 in una faccia, ov’eran due perduti.  

The two heads had now become one, when two shapes appeared to us mixed in one face, where both were lost.

- *Già eran li due capi un divenuti* ‘Already were the two heads one become’ → “The two heads had now become one”: the subject moves before the auxiliary, the complement “one” follows the participle, and the Italian pluperfect with *essere* becomes an English pluperfect with “had”; *già* is rendered as “now” rather than ‘already’.
- *quando n’apparver due figure* ‘when to us appeared two figures’ → “when two shapes appeared to us”: the subject moves before the verb and the personal pronoun after it; “shapes” renders *figure* as bodily forms.
- *ov’eran due perduti* ‘where two were lost’ → “where both were lost”: the numeral becomes the inclusive pronoun “both”.

> 73 Fêrsi le braccia due di quattro liste;  

Two arms were made of the four lists;

- *Fêrsi le braccia due di quattro liste* ‘The arms made themselves two from four strips’ → “Two arms were made of the four lists”: the pronominal verb becomes an English passive; the predicate numeral becomes an adjective before “arms”; and the definite article moves from the resulting arms to the four constituent strips.

> 74 le cosce con le gambe, il ventre e il casso  
> 75 divenner membra che non fur mai viste.  

the thighs with the legs, the belly, and the chest, became such members as were never seen.

- *membra che* ‘members that’ → “such members as”: “such” is supplied, and the relative construction is correspondingly changed from ‘that’ to “as”.

> 76 Ogni primaio aspetto ivi era casso:  

The former shape was all extinct in them:

- *Ogni primaio aspetto ivi era casso* ‘Every original appearance there was effaced’ → “The former shape was all extinct in them”: the distributive ‘every’ becomes “all”, qualifying the extinction rather than the appearance; “former shape” replaces ‘original appearance’; “extinct” replaces ‘effaced’; and the locative ‘there’ is interpreted as “in them” and moved to the end.

> 77 due e nessun l’imagine perversa  
> 78 parea, e tal sen gía con lento passo.  

both, and neither, the perverse image seemed; and such it went away with languid step.

- *due e nessun* ‘two and none’ → “both, and neither”: the numeral and negative pronoun become a paired expression referring to the two constituents.
- *sen gía* ‘went itself thence’ → “it went away”: the pronominal expression is rendered by an ordinary verb with “away”; the reflexive element has no separate equivalent.
- *con lento passo* ‘with slow step’ → “with languid step”: “languid” substitutes an implication of listlessness or weakness for the direct statement of slowness.

> 79 Come il ramarro, sotto la gran fersa  
> 80 de’ dì canicular cangiando siepe,  
> 81 folgore par, se la via attraversa:  

As the lizard, beneath the mighty scourge of the canicular days, going from hedge to hedge, appears a flash of lightning, if it cross the way:

- *ramarro* ‘green lizard’ → “lizard”: the more specific animal name becomes a general one.
- *gran* ‘great’ → “mighty”: the adjective is rendered with an emphasis on force.
- *de’ dì canicular* ‘of the days canicular’ → “of the canicular days”: the adjective moves before the noun.
- *cangiando siepe* ‘changing hedge’ → “going from hedge to hedge”: the transitive participial construction becomes a verb of motion with phrases expressing departure and destination.
- *folgore par* ‘a lightning-flash appears’ → “appears a flash of lightning”: the predicative noun moves after the verb, and a single Italian noun becomes a noun with a prepositional complement.
- *se la via attraversa* ‘if the way it crosses’ → “if it cross the way”: the object moves after the verb, and the Italian indicative becomes the English subjunctive “cross”.

> 82 così parea, venendo verso l’epe  
> 83 degli altri due, un serpentello acceso,  
> 84 livido e nero come gran di pepe.  

so, coming towards the bowels of the other two, appeared a little reptile burning *with rage*, livid and black as pepper corn.

- *così parea* ‘so appeared’ → “so” and “appeared”: the participial phrase intervenes between the adverb and the verb, instead of following both.
- *venendo verso l’epe / degli altri due* ‘coming towards the bellies of the other two’ → “coming towards the bowels of the other two”: “bowels” replaces the anatomical designation ‘bellies’.
- *un serpentello acceso* ‘a little serpent kindled’ → “a little reptile burning *with rage*”: the diminutive suffix becomes the separate adjective “little”; ‘serpent’ becomes the broader “reptile”; and the participle is interpreted as burning with anger, with “with rage” supplied.
- *gran di pepe* ‘a grain of pepper’ → “pepper corn”: the noun with its prepositional complement becomes a compound noun, without an indefinite article.

> 85 E quella parte, donde prima è preso  
> 86 nostro alimento, all’un di lor trafisse;  

And it pierced that part, in one of them, at which we first receive our nourishment;

- *E quella parte, donde prima è preso / nostro alimento, all’un di lor trafisse* ‘And that part, whence first is taken our nourishment, in one of them it pierced’ → “And it pierced that part, in one of them, at which we first receive our nourishment”: the main verb moves before its object, and “in one of them” moves before the relative clause; ‘whence’ becomes the locative “at which”; and the passive ‘is taken our nourishment’ becomes the active “we first receive our nourishment”, with “we” supplied as the recipient.

> 87 poi cadde giuso innanzi lui disteso.  

then fell down stretched out before him.

- *innanzi lui disteso* ‘before him stretched out’ → “stretched out before him”: the participial expression moves before the phrase of position.

## Lines 88–120 — The Exchange of Forms

> 88 Lo trafitto il mirò, ma nulla disse;  

The pierced *thief* gazed on it but said nothing;

- *Lo trafitto* ‘the pierced one’ → “The pierced *thief*”: the substantival participle is expanded with the supplied noun “thief”.
- *il mirò* ‘it he looked at’ → “gazed on it”: the object follows the verb, and “gazed on” gives a more sustained sense than ‘looked at’.
- *nulla disse* ‘nothing said’ → “said nothing”: the object moves after the verb.

> 89 anzi co' piè fermati sbadigliava,  
> 90 pur come sonno o febbre l' assalisse.  

nay, with his feet motionless, yawned only as if sleep or fever had come upon him.

- *co' piè fermati* ‘with the feet stopped’ → “with his feet motionless”: the article becomes a possessive, and the participle becomes an adjective describing a state.
- *pur come* ‘just as’ → “only as if”: “only” is placed with “yawned”, while “as if” makes the hypothetical comparison explicit.
- *l'assalisse* ‘should assail him’ → “had come upon him”: the imperfect subjunctive becomes an English past perfect within the hypothetical comparison, and the verb of assault is softened to “come upon”.

> 91 Egli il serpente, e quei lui riguardava;  

He eyed the reptile, the reptile him;

- *Egli il serpente, e quei lui riguardava* ‘He the serpent, and that one him regarded’ → “He eyed the reptile, the reptile him”: the shared verb moves into the first clause and is understood in the second; ‘and’ is omitted; ‘serpent’ becomes the broader “reptile”; and the demonstrative ‘that one’ becomes a repeated noun.

> 92 l' un per la piaga, e l' altro per la bocca  
> 93 fumavan forte, e il fummo si scontrava.  

the one from his wound, the other from its mouth, smoked violently, and their smoke met.

- *per la piaga* ‘through the wound’ → “from his wound”: passage through becomes origin from, and the article becomes a possessive.
- *e l'altro* ‘and the other’ → “the other”: the conjunction is omitted.
- *per la bocca* ‘through the mouth’ → “from its mouth”: passage through becomes origin from, and the article becomes a possessive.
- *fumavan forte* ‘smoked strongly’ → “smoked violently”: the adverb of intensity is rendered as one suggesting force or violence.
- *il fummo si scontrava* ‘the smoke encountered itself’ → “their smoke met”: the article becomes a possessive, and the reciprocal pronominal construction becomes an intransitive English verb.

> 94 Taccia Lucano omai, là dove tocca  
> 95 del misero Sabello e di Nassidio,  
> 96 ed attenda ad udir quel ch' or si scocca.  

Let Lucan now be silent, where he tells of poor Sabellus and Nasidius; and wait to hear that which is now sent forth.

- *Taccia Lucano omai* ‘Be silent Lucan now’ → “Let Lucan now be silent”: the exhortative subjunctive becomes a construction with “Let”; the subject precedes “be silent”, and “now” moves before it.
- *là dove* ‘there where’ → “where”: ‘there’ is omitted.
- *tocca del misero Sabello e di Nassidio* ‘touches on the wretched Sabellus and on Nasidius’ → “tells of poor Sabellus and Nasidius”: ‘touches on’ becomes “tells of”, ‘wretched’ becomes “poor”, and the article before the epithet and the repeated preposition are omitted.
- *ed attenda ad udir* ‘and attend to hearing’ → “and wait to hear”: *attenda* is interpreted as waiting rather than attending, and the infinitive gives the purpose of that waiting.
- *quel ch'or si scocca* ‘that which now is shot forth’ → “that which is now sent forth”: “now” follows the auxiliary, and the specific sense of shooting or discharge becomes the more general “sent forth”.

> 97 Taccia di Cadmo e d' Aretusa Ovidio:  
> 98 chè se quello in serpente, e quella in fonte  
> 99 converte poetando, io non l' invidio:  

Of Cadmus and of Arethusa be Ovid silent: for if he, poetizing, converts the one into a serpent and the other into a fount, I envy him not:

- *Taccia di Cadmo e d'Aretusa Ovidio* ‘Be silent about Cadmus and about Arethusa Ovid’ → “Of Cadmus and of Arethusa be Ovid silent”: the prepositional phrases move to the beginning, “of” renders ‘about’, and the subject stands between “be” and “silent”.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *quello … quella* ‘that one … that one’ → “the one” and “the other”: the gendered demonstratives become an explicitly contrasting pair without grammatical gender.
- *in serpente … in fonte* ‘into serpent … into fountain’ → “into a serpent” and “into a fount”: English indefinite articles are supplied.
- *converte poetando* ‘converts composing poetry’ → “poetizing, converts”: the gerund becomes an English participial expression and moves before the finite verb; the verb itself precedes the objects that stand before it in Italian.
- *io non l'invidio* ‘I not him envy’ → “I envy him not”: the object pronoun and the negative move after the verb.

> 100 chè due nature mai a fronte a fronte  
> 101 non trasmutò, sì ch' ambo e due le forme  
> 102 a cambiar lor materia fosser pronte.  

for never did he so transmute two natures front to front, that both forms were ready to exchange their substance.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *due nature mai a fronte a fronte non trasmutò, sì* ‘two natures never front to front he did not transmute, so’ → “never did he so transmute two natures front to front”: Italian negative concord becomes the single “never”; its initial position requires auxiliary inversion; the object and “front to front” follow the verb; and “so” moves into the main clause.
- *ch'ambo e due le forme* ‘that both and two the forms’ → “that both forms”: the emphatic doubling in *ambo e due* is compressed into “both”, and the article is omitted.
- *a cambiar lor materia fosser pronte* ‘to change their matter were ready’ → “were ready to exchange their substance”: the infinitive phrase moves after “were ready”; the imperfect subjunctive becomes an indicative; ‘change’ is interpreted reciprocally as “exchange”; and ‘matter’ becomes “substance”.

> 103 Insieme si risposero a tai norme,  
> 104 che il serpente la coda in forca fesse,  
> 105 e il feruto ristrinse insieme l' orme.  

They mutually responded in such a way, that the reptile cleft its tail into a fork, and the wounded *spirit* drew his steps together.

- *Insieme si risposero* ‘Together they answered one another’ → “They mutually responded”: “mutually” incorporates the reciprocal construction and the accompanying ‘together’.
- *a tai norme* ‘according to such rules’ → “in such a way”: the plural expression of conformity becomes a singular expression of manner.
- *il serpente* ‘the serpent’ → “the reptile”: a broader noun replaces the specific one.
- *la coda in forca fesse* ‘the tail into a fork cleft’ → “cleft its tail into a fork”: the verb moves before its object, and the article becomes a possessive.
- *il feruto* ‘the wounded one’ → “the wounded *spirit*”: the substantival participle is expanded with the supplied noun “spirit”.
- *ristrinse insieme l'orme* ‘pressed together the footprints’ → “drew his steps together”: “drew” renders the compression expressed by *ristrinse*; “steps” renders *orme*; the article becomes a possessive; and “together” follows the object.

> 106 Le gambe con le cosce seco stesse  
> 107 s' appiccar sì, che in poco la giuntura  
> 108 non facea segno alcun che si paresse.  

The legs and the thighs along with them so stuck to one another, that soon their juncture left no mark that was discernible.

- *con le cosce seco stesse* ‘with the thighs with themselves’ → “and the thighs along with them”: the first preposition becomes a coordinating conjunction, while the reflexive phrase becomes “along with them”.
- *s'appiccar sì* ‘stuck themselves together so’ → “so stuck to one another”: the pronominal construction is explicitly reciprocal, and “so” moves before the verb.
- *in poco* ‘in a little’ → “soon”: the elliptical temporal phrase becomes an adverb.
- *la giuntura* ‘the juncture’ → “their juncture”: the article becomes a possessive.
- *non facea segno alcun* ‘made not any mark’ → “left no mark”: ‘made’ becomes “left”, and verbal negation with ‘any’ becomes the determiner “no”.
- *che si paresse* ‘that appeared’ → “that was discernible”: the subjunctive verb becomes an indicative copular construction with an adjective expressing perceptibility.

> 109 Togliea la coda fessa la figura,  
> 110 che si perdeva là, e la sua pelle  
> 111 si facea molle, e quella di là dura.  

The cloven tail assumed the figure that was lost in the other; and its skin grew soft, the other's hard.

- *Togliea la coda fessa la figura* ‘Took the tail cloven the figure’ → “The cloven tail assumed the figure”: the subject moves before the verb, the adjective precedes its noun, and “assumed” renders ‘took’.
- *che si perdeva là* ‘that was being lost there’ → “that was lost in the other”: the ongoing imperfect is rendered by a simple past passive, and the spatial adverb is expanded into an explicit reference to “the other”.
- *si facea molle* ‘made itself soft’ → “grew soft”: the pronominal expression of becoming becomes an intransitive English construction.
- *e quella di là dura* ‘and that over there hard’ → “the other's hard”: the conjunction is omitted, and the demonstrative and spatial expression become a possessive with “skin” understood.

> 112 Io vidi entrar le braccia per l' ascelle,  
> 113 e i duo piè della fiera, ch' eran corti,  
> 114 tanto allungar, quanto accorciavan quelle.  

I saw the arms enter at the armpits, and the two feet of the brute, which were short, lengthen themselves as much as those *arms* were shortened.

- *entrar le braccia per l'ascelle* ‘enter the arms through the armpits’ → “the arms enter at the armpits”: the subject of the infinitive moves before it, and “at” replaces ‘through’.
- *fiera* ‘wild beast’ → “brute”: a different noun renders the designation of the animal.
- *tanto allungar, quanto accorciavan quelle* ‘as much lengthen as those shortened’ → “lengthen themselves as much as those *arms* were shortened”: “themselves” makes the reflexive sense explicit; the correlative comparison follows the infinitive; “arms” is supplied after the demonstrative; and the intransitive shortening becomes a passive construction, with its subject before the verb.

> 115 Poscia li piè dirietro, insieme attorti,  
> 116 diventaron lo membro che l' uom cela,  
> 117 e il misero del suo n' avea duo porti.  

Then the *two* hinder feet, twisted together, became the member which man conceals; and the wretch from his had two thrust forth.

- *li piè dirietro* ‘the feet behind’ → “the *two* hinder feet”: “two” is supplied, and the following adverb becomes an adjective before the noun.
- *insieme attorti* ‘together twisted’ → “twisted together”: the adverb follows the participle.
- *che l'uom cela* ‘which the man conceals’ → “which man conceals”: the article marking a generic singular is omitted.
- *il misero* ‘the wretched one’ → “the wretch”: the substantival adjective becomes a noun.
- *del suo n'avea duo porti* ‘from his he had two of them extended’ → “from his had two thrust forth”: the pronominal *ne* is not separately represented, and “thrust forth” gives a more forceful rendering of ‘extended’.

> 118 Mentre che il fummo l' uno e l' altro vela  
> 119 di color nuovo, e genera il pel suso  
> 120 per l' una parte, e dall' altra il dipela,  

Whilst the smoke with a new colour *veils them* both, and generates on one part *hair*, and strips it from another,

- *l'uno e l'altro vela di color nuovo* ‘the one and the other veils with colour new’ → “with a new colour *veils them* both”: the colour phrase moves before the verb, the object follows it, and ‘the one and the other’ becomes “them both”; an indefinite article is supplied, and the adjective precedes its noun.
- *e genera il pel suso per l'una parte* ‘and generates the hair above on the one part’ → “and generates on one part *hair*”: the prepositional phrase moves before the object; ‘above’ and both definite articles are omitted.
- *e dall'altra il dipela* ‘and from the other it depilates’ → “and strips it from another”: the verb precedes the prepositional phrase; depilation is expressed as stripping away hair, with “it” referring to that hair; and ‘the other’ becomes “another”.

## Lines 121–151 — The Final Transformation and Recognition

> 121 l' un si levò, e l' altro cadde giuso,  
> 122 non torcendo però le lucerne empie,  
> 123 sotto le quai ciascun cambiava muso.  

the one rose upright, and prostrate the other fell, not therefore turning the impious lights, under which they mutually exchanged visages.

- *l' un si levò* ‘the one raised himself’ → “the one rose upright”: the reflexive construction becomes intransitive, and “upright” makes the resulting posture explicit.
- *e l' altro cadde giuso* ‘and the other fell down’ → “and prostrate the other fell”: the directional adverb becomes a description of posture, placed before the subject.
- *però* ‘however’ → “therefore”: the adversative expression is rendered consequentially, under the preceding negation, and placed before “turning”.
- *le lucerne empie* ‘the impious lamps’ → “the impious lights”: “lights” replaces the more concrete ‘lamps’, and the adjective precedes rather than follows the noun.
- *ciascun cambiava muso* ‘each was changing snout’ → “they mutually exchanged visages”: the singular distributive construction becomes plural and explicitly reciprocal; changing becomes exchanging, and the singular ‘snout’ becomes the plural “visages”.

> 124 Quel ch' era dritto il trasse ver le tempie,  
> 125 e di troppa materia che in là venne,  
> 126 uscir gli orecchi delle gote scempie;  

He that was erect, drew his towards the temples; and from the too much matter that went thither, ears came out of the smooth cheeks;

- *Quel ch' era dritto* ‘the one who was upright’ → “He that was erect”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative expression.
- *il trasse ver le tempie* ‘it drew towards the temples’ → “drew his towards the temples”: the object pronoun becomes a possessive pronoun and follows the verb.
- *di troppa materia* ‘from too much matter’ → “from the too much matter”: the definite article is supplied.
- *che in là venne* ‘that thither came’ → “that went thither”: ‘came’ becomes “went”, and the directional expression follows the verb.
- *uscir gli orecchi delle gote scempie* ‘came out the ears from the bare cheeks’ → “ears came out of the smooth cheeks”: the subject moves before the verb, its definite article disappears, and ‘bare’ is interpreted as “smooth”.

> 127 ciò che non corse in dietro e si ritenne,  
> 128 di quel soperchio fe' naso alla faccia,  
> 129 e le labbra ingrossò quanto convenne.  

that which went not back, but was retained, of its superfluity formed a nose, and enlarged the lips to a fit size.

- *non corse in dietro* ‘did not run back’ → “went not back”: ‘run’ is generalized to “went”.
- *e si ritenne* ‘and held itself back’ → “but was retained”: the conjunction becomes adversative, and the reflexive construction becomes passive.
- *di quel soperchio fe' naso alla faccia* ‘of that surplus made a nose for the face’ → “of its superfluity formed a nose”: the demonstrative becomes possessive, ‘made’ becomes “formed”, and ‘for the face’ is omitted.
- *le labbra ingrossò* ‘the lips thickened’ → “enlarged the lips”: the object moves after the verb, and increasing thickness is rendered more generally as enlargement.
- *quanto convenne* ‘as much as was fitting’ → “to a fit size”: the finite clause becomes a prepositional phrase, with “size” supplied.

> 130 Quel che giacea il muso innanzi caccia,  
> 131 e gli orecchi ritira per la testa,  
> 132 come face le corna la lumaccia;  

He that lay prone, thrusts forward his sharpened visage, and draws back his ears into the head, as the snail does its horns;

- *Quel che giacea* ‘the one who lay’ → “He that lay prone”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative expression, and “prone” is supplied.
- *il muso innanzi caccia* ‘the snout forward thrusts’ → “thrusts forward his sharpened visage”: the verb precedes its object; the article becomes possessive; and ‘snout’ is expanded into “sharpened visage”.
- *gli orecchi ritira per la testa* ‘the ears draws back through the head’ → “draws back his ears into the head”: the object follows the verb, the article becomes possessive, and *per* is interpreted as movement “into”.
- *come face le corna la lumaccia* ‘as does the horns the snail’ → “as the snail does its horns”: the subject moves before the verb and object, and the article before ‘horns’ becomes possessive.

> 133 e la lingua, che avea unita e presta  
> 134 prima a parlar, si fende, e la forcuta  
> 135 nell' altro si richiude, e il fummo resta.  

and his tongue, which was before united and apt for speech, cleaves itself; and in the other the forked *tongue* recloses; and the smoke *now rests*.

- *la lingua* ‘the tongue’ → “his tongue”: the definite article becomes possessive.
- *che avea unita e presta prima a parlar* ‘which he previously had united and ready to speak’ → “which was before united and apt for speech”: the construction with ‘had’ and an object becomes one with “was” and a subject; “before” moves ahead of the adjectives; and the infinitive ‘to speak’ becomes the noun phrase “for speech”.
- *e la forcuta nell' altro si richiude* ‘and the forked in the other closes itself again’ → “and in the other the forked *tongue* recloses”: the locative phrase moves before the subject; the substantivized adjective is expanded with “tongue”; and the reflexive becomes an intransitive verb.
- *e il fummo resta* ‘and the smoke ceases’ → “and the smoke *now rests*”: cessation is rendered as resting, and “now” is supplied.

> 136 L' anima, ch' era fiera divenuta,  
> 137 si fuggì sufolando per la valle,  
> 138 e l' altro dietro a lui parlando sputa.  

The soul that had become a brute, [fled] hissing along the valley, and after it the other talking and sputtering.

- *ch' era fiera divenuta* ‘that had a wild beast become’ → “that had become a brute”: the complement moves after the participle, and ‘wild beast’ is rendered more generally as “brute”.
- *si fuggì* ‘fled away’ → “[fled]”: the pronominal reinforcement of the verb is not separately represented.
- *per la valle* ‘through the valley’ → “along the valley”: the preposition is rendered as movement along rather than through.
- *dietro a lui* ‘behind him’ → “after it”: the personal pronoun becomes neuter, and the phrase moves before the subject.
- *parlando sputa* ‘speaking spits’ → “talking and sputtering”: the gerund and finite present verb become coordinated participles, with “and” supplied; ‘spits’ becomes “sputtering”.

> 139 Poscia gli volse le novelle spalle,  
> 140 e disse all' altro: "Io vo' che Buoso corra,  
> 141 com' ho fatt' io, carpon, per questo calle."  

Then he turned his novel shoulders towards it, and said to the other : “Buoso shall run crawling, as I have done, along this road

- *gli* ‘to him’ → “towards it”: the indirect-object pronoun becomes a directional prepositional phrase, changes to neuter, and moves after the direct object.
- *le novelle spalle* ‘the new shoulders’ → “his novel shoulders”: the definite article becomes possessive.
- *Io vo' che Buoso corra* ‘I will that Buoso run’ → “Buoso shall run”: the statement of the speaker’s will and its subordinate subjunctive clause become a single clause with “shall”; the explicit first-person subject disappears.
- *com' ho fatt' io* ‘as have done I’ → “as I have done”: the expressed, postposed subject moves before the auxiliary.
- *carpon* ‘on all fours’ → “crawling”: an adverbial expression becomes a participle and moves before the comparison.

> 142 Così vid' io la settima zavorra  
> 143 mutare e trasmutare; e qui mi scusi  
> 144 la novità, se fior la penna abborra.  

!” Thus I beheld the seventh ballast change and rechange ; and here let the novelty excuse me, if my tongue [pen] goes aught astray.

- *vid' io* ‘saw I’ → “I beheld”: the expressed subject moves before the verb.
- *trasmutare* ‘transmute’ → “rechange”: the translation gives the verb an explicitly repetitive force rather than retaining the sense of transformation.
- *mi scusi la novità* ‘me may excuse the novelty’ → “let the novelty excuse me”: the independent subjunctive becomes a construction with “let”, and the object moves after the verb.
- *se fior la penna abborra* ‘if at all the pen blunders’ → “if my tongue [pen] goes aught astray”: the article becomes possessive; “tongue” substitutes for ‘pen’, while “[pen]” restores it; ‘blunders’ becomes the phrase “goes aught astray”; and the expression meaning ‘at all’ moves from before the subject to within that phrase.

> 145 Ed avvegna che gli occhi miei confusi  
> 146 fossero alquanto, e l' animo smagato,  
> 147 non poter quei fuggirsi tanto chiusi,  
> 148 ch' io non scorgessi ben Puccio Sciancato.  

And though my eyes were somewhat perplexed, and my mind dismayed, [those] could not flee so covertly, but that I well distinguished Puccio Sciancato:

- *gli occhi miei* ‘the eyes mine’ → “my eyes”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *confusi fossero alquanto* ‘confused were somewhat’ → “were somewhat perplexed”: the adjective moves after the verb and degree adverb; the Italian concessive subjunctive is represented by an ordinary English past-tense construction.
- *l' animo* ‘the mind’ → “my mind”: the definite article becomes possessive.
- *non poter quei* ‘not could those’ → “[those] could not”: the demonstrative subject moves before the verb, and the negation follows the modal.
- *fuggirsi* ‘flee away’ → “flee”: the pronominal reinforcement is not separately represented.
- *tanto chiusi* ‘so concealed’ → “so covertly”: an adjective describing the subjects becomes an adverb modifying their flight.
- *ch' io non scorgessi ben Puccio Sciancato* ‘that I should not clearly discern Puccio Sciancato’ → “but that I well distinguished Puccio Sciancato”: the negative result clause becomes an affirmative clause introduced by “but that”; the subjunctive becomes indicative; and the adverb moves before the verb.

> 149 ed era quei che sol, de' tre compagni  
> 150 che venner prima, non era mutato;  

and it was he alone, of the three companions that first came, who was not changed

- *ed era quei che sol, de' tre compagni che venner prima, non era mutato* ‘and it was he who alone, of the three companions who came first, was not changed’ → “and it was he alone, of the three companions that first came, who was not changed”: “alone” is placed directly with “he”, outside the relative clause; the relative clause begins only after the intervening phrase identifying the three companions; and “first” moves before “came”.

> 151 l' altro era quel che tu, Gaville, piagni.  

; the other was he whom thou, Gaville, lamentest.

- *quel* ‘the one’ → “he”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.

## Summary — Close Detail, Recast Syntax

Carlyle’s translation remains close to the Italian’s sequence of details, comparisons, and concrete bodily vocabulary, but regularly rearranges their syntax. Subjects move before verbs, objects follow them, and adjectives precede their nouns: *giacea un draco* becomes “a dragon lay”, and *un color bruno* becomes “a brown colour”. Adverbial and prepositional phrases also move into more customary English positions. Yet some marked arrangements survive, as in “both, and neither, the perverse image seemed”; expressions such as “seventh ballast” likewise preserve the Italian’s unusual wording rather than explain it.

Changes of construction often make relations more explicit. Datives associated with parts of the body become possessives: *gli avvinse la pancia* becomes “it clasped his belly”. Pronominal verbs become ordinary English intransitives, passives, or phrases expressing reciprocity. Finite clauses sometimes become participial phrases, while substantivized adjectives and participles acquire nouns, as in “The pierced *thief*” and “the wounded *spirit*”. More substantial recasting occurs when *Io non vo’ che più diche*, ‘I do not wish thee to speak further’, becomes “Thou shalt speak no farther!”: the speaker’s will and a subordinate clause are compressed into a direct prohibition. Negation also shifts its grammatical attachment, as when *non vidi spirto* becomes “I saw no spirit”.

Word choice ranges from close correspondence to interpretation and expansion. *Incenerarti* is unpacked as “turn thyself to ashes”, whereas *serpente* sometimes becomes the broader “reptile”, and *lento* becomes “languid”, adding a suggestion beyond simple slowness. Supplied words clarify reference, manner, or an inferred meaning: “human” qualifies “form”, “firmly” specifies the fastening, and “with rage” interprets *acceso*. Conversely, conjunctions, repeated prepositions, and some local details disappear: *suso* is not separately represented in the description of hair growing, and *alla faccia* is omitted where the nose is formed. Such choices generally streamline the prose, though they can narrow or alter the Italian’s emphasis.

Throughout, English also supplies subject pronouns where Italian verb endings alone identify the person, and omits the Italian articles before possessives, as *Lo mio maestro* becomes “My Master”. These routine grammatical adjustments accompany the more conspicuous departures recorded in the comparison. The result is neither strictly word-for-word nor freely paraphrastic: it preserves much of the original’s vocabulary and intricate descriptive progression while reshaping order, reference, and clause structure for English prose.
