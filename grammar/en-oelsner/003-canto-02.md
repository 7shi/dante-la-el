# Inferno 2

## Lines 1–24 — Dante Questions His Fitness for the Journey

> 1 Lo giorno se n’ andava, e l’ aer bruno  
> 2 toglieva gli animai, che sono in terra,  
> 3 dalle fatiche loro; ed io sol uno  

The day was departing, and the brown air taking the animals, that are on earth, from their toils; and I, one alone,

- *se n’ andava* ‘was going away’ → “was departing”: the Italian pronominal expression is rendered by a single verb.
- *l’ aer bruno* ‘the air brown’ → “the brown air”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *toglieva* ‘was taking away’ → “taking”: the repeated auxiliary is omitted, and the sense of removal is expressed by “taking” together with the following “from”.
- *dalle fatiche loro* ‘from the toils their’ → “from their toils”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *ed io sol uno* ‘and I alone one’ → “and I, one alone”: “one” and “alone” exchange positions.

> 4 m’ apparecchiava a sostener la guerra  
> 5 sì del cammino, e sì della pietate,  
> 6 che ritrarrà la mente, che non erra.  

was preparing myself to bear the war both of the journey and the pity, which memory, that errs not, shall relate.

- *m’ apparecchiava* ‘myself was preparing’ → “was preparing myself”: the reflexive pronoun follows the verb.
- *sostener* ‘sustain’ → “bear”: the verb is rendered in the sense of enduring.
- *sì del cammino, e sì della pietate* ‘as much of the journey, and as much of the pity’ → “both of the journey and the pity”: the repeated Italian correlative becomes “both” and “and”; the second “of” is left implicit.
- *che ritrarrà* ‘which will portray’ → “which” and “shall relate”: portrayal becomes narration, and the verb moves after its subject and the relative clause qualifying that subject.
- *la mente* ‘the mind’ → “memory”: a particular faculty replaces the broader term, and the definite article is omitted.
- *che non erra* ‘that not errs’ → “that errs not”: the negative follows rather than precedes the verb.

> 7 O Muse, o alto ingegno, or m’ aiutate!  

O Muses, o high Genius, now help me!

- *alto ingegno* ‘high intellect’ → “high Genius”: intellectual power is rendered as exceptional creative ability.
- *or m’ aiutate* ‘now me help’ → “now help me”: the object pronoun follows the imperative.

> 8 O mente, che scrivesti ciò ch’ io vidi,  
> 9 qui si parrà la tua nobilitate.  

O Memory, that hast inscribed what I saw, here will be shewn thy nobleness.

- *mente* ‘mind’ → “Memory”: the broader faculty is again specified as recollection.
- *che scrivesti* ‘that wrote’ → “that hast inscribed”: the simple past becomes a present perfect, and writing becomes inscription.
- *ciò ch’ io vidi* ‘that which I saw’ → “what I saw”: the demonstrative and relative pronoun become a single fused relative.
- *si parrà* ‘will appear’ → “will be shewn”: the pronominal verb of appearing becomes a passive construction.

> 10 Io cominciai: “Poeta che mi guidi,  
> 11 guarda la mia virtù, s’ ella è possente,  
> 12 prima che all’ alto passo tu mi fidi.  

I began: "Poet, who guidest me, look if there be worth in me sufficient, before thou trust me to the arduous passage.

- *che mi guidi* ‘who me guidest’ → “who guidest me”: the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *guarda la mia virtù, s’ ella è possente* ‘look at my strength, whether it is powerful’ → “look if there be worth in me sufficient”: the direct object and the clause resuming it become one existential clause. “worth” replaces strength or capacity; the possessive becomes “in me”; “sufficient” replaces ‘powerful’; and indicative *è* becomes subjunctive “be”.
- *all’ alto passo tu mi fidi* ‘to the high passage thou me entrust’ → “thou trust me to the arduous passage”: the prepositional phrase moves after the verb and object, the object pronoun follows the verb, and “arduous” interprets *alto* as difficulty rather than height.

> 13 Tu dici, che di Silvio lo parente,  
> 14 corruttibile ancora, ad immortale  
> 15 secolo andò, e fu sensibilmente.  

Thou sayest that the father of Sylvius, while subject to corruption, went to the immortal world, and was there in body.

- *di Silvio lo parente* ‘of Sylvius the parent’ → “the father of Sylvius”: the possessive phrase follows its noun, and “father” specifies the parental relationship.
- *corruttibile ancora* ‘corruptible still’ → “while subject to corruption”: the adjective becomes a phrase, “while” explicitly marks the temporal relationship, and ‘still’ is not separately represented.
- *ad immortale secolo andò* ‘to immortal age went’ → “went to the immortal world”: the verb precedes its destination, an article is supplied, and *secolo* is interpreted as a world or realm rather than an age.
- *e fu sensibilmente* ‘and was perceptibly’ → “and was there in body”: “there” is supplied, and the adverb describing perceptible existence becomes a phrase specifying bodily presence.

> 16 Però se l’ avversario d’ ogni male  
> 17 cortese i fu, pensando l’ alto effetto,  
> 18 che uscir dovea di lui, ed il chi, ed il quale,  

But if the Adversary of all evil was propitious [to him], considering the high effect, and who and what should come from him,

- *Però* ‘therefore’ → “But”: an inferential connective becomes an adversative one.
- *d’ ogni male* ‘of every evil’ → “of all evil”: individually distributive ‘every’ becomes collective “all”.
- *cortese i fu* ‘courteous to him was’ → “was propitious [to him]”: the verb moves first, and courtesy becomes favour or favourable disposition. The bracketed words render the Italian dative *i*.
- *pensando* ‘thinking’ → “considering”: the verb is rendered specifically as reflective consideration.
- *che uscir dovea di lui, ed il chi, ed il quale* ‘which come forth was to from him, and the who, and the what sort’ → “and who and what should come from him”: the relative clause qualifying *effetto* is recast as a clause whose subjects are “who and what”. The substantivized interrogatives lose their articles, *quale* becomes “what” rather than ‘what sort’, and the auxiliary precedes “come”; “should” expresses the prospective sense of *dovea*.

> 19 non pare indegno ad uomo d’ intelletto:  
> 20 ch’ ei fu dell’ alma Roma e di suo impero  
> 21 nell’ empireo ciel per padre eletto;  

it seems not unfitting to an understanding mind: for in the empyreal heaven, he was chosen to be the father of generous Rome, and of her Empire;

- *non pare indegno* ‘not seems unworthy’ → “it seems not unfitting”: an anticipatory “it” is supplied, the negative follows the verb, and unworthiness becomes unfittingness.
- *ad uomo d’ intelletto* ‘to a man of intellect’ → “to an understanding mind”: the person becomes the intellectual faculty, and the qualifying noun phrase becomes an adjective.
- *ch’ ei fu … nell’ empireo ciel* ‘because he was … in the empyreal heaven’ → “for in the empyreal heaven, he was”: the causal conjunction becomes coordinating “for”, and the locative phrase moves from after the subject and verb to the beginning of the causal clause.
- *dell’ alma Roma e di suo impero* ‘of nurturing Rome and of her empire’ → “of generous Rome, and of her Empire”: “generous” renders *alma* through bounty rather than nurture, and the whole phrase moves after the noun “father” that it qualifies.
- *per padre eletto* ‘as father chosen’ → “chosen to be the father”: the participle moves first, and the phrase expressing a role becomes an infinitive construction with “be” and a definite article supplied.

> 22 la quale, e il quale, a voler dir lo vero,  
> 23 fur stabiliti per lo loco santo,  
> 24 u’ siede il successor del maggior Piero.  

both these, to say the truth, were established for the holy place, where the Successor of [the greatest] Peter sits.

- *la quale, e il quale* ‘which, and which’ → “both these”: two gender-distinguished relative pronouns become a plural demonstrative subject, with “both” replacing their coordination.
- *a voler dir lo vero* ‘to wish to say the true’ → “to say the truth”: *voler* is omitted, and the substantivized adjective becomes the noun “truth”.
- *per lo loco santo* ‘for the place holy’ → “for the holy place”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *u’ siede* ‘where sits’ → “where” and “sits”: the verb moves after its subject and the phrase qualifying that subject.
- *del maggior Piero* ‘of the greater Peter’ → “of [the greatest] Peter”: the comparative form is interpreted and rendered as a superlative.

## Lines 25–57 — Dante’s Doubt and Virgil’s Reassurance

> 25 Per questa andata, onde gli dai tu vanto,  
> 26 intese cose che furon cagione  
> 27 di sua vittoria e del papale ammanto.  

By this journey, for which thou honourest him, he learned things that were the causes of his victory, and of the Papal Mantle.

- *Per questa andata* ‘Through this going’ → “By this journey”: the act of going becomes a journey, and the preposition presents it as a means.
- *onde gli dai tu vanto* ‘for which to him givest thou praise’ → “for which thou honourest him”: giving praise becomes honouring; the indirect object becomes a direct object, and the subject precedes the verb.
- *intese* ‘understood’ → “he learned”: the translation emphasizes acquiring knowledge rather than understanding it.
- *furon cagione* ‘were cause’ → “were the causes”: the singular predicate noun becomes plural, and a definite article is supplied.

> 28 Andovvi poi lo Vas d' elezione,  
> 29 per recarne conforto a quella fede,  
> 30 ch'è principio alla via di salvazione.  

Afterwards, the Chosen Vessel went thither, to bring confirmation of that Faith which is the entrance to the way of salvation.

- *Andovvi poi* ‘Went thither afterwards’ → “Afterwards,” … “went thither”: the temporal adverb moves to the front, and the verb and its directional adverb follow the subject.
- *lo Vas d' elezione* ‘the Vessel of election’ → “the Chosen Vessel”: the modifying prepositional phrase becomes a participial adjective before the noun.
- *per recarne conforto a quella fede* ‘to bring back from there comfort to that faith’ → “to bring confirmation of that Faith”: *ne*, marking the place from which something is brought, is omitted; comfort becomes confirmation, and faith becomes what is confirmed rather than what receives comfort.
- *ch'è principio alla via di salvazione* ‘which is beginning to the way of salvation’ → “which is the entrance to the way of salvation”: beginning becomes entrance, with a definite article supplied.

> 31 Ma io, perchè venirvi? o chi 'l concede?  

But I, why go? or who permits it?

- *venirvi* ‘come there’ → “go”: coming becomes going, and the directional *vi* is omitted.

> 32 Io non Enea, io non Paolo sono;  

I am not Æneas, am not Paul;

- *Io non Enea, io non Paolo sono* ‘I not Aeneas, I not Paul am’ → “I am not Æneas, am not Paul”: the single final verb is placed before each predicate name; the second explicit subject pronoun is omitted.

> 33 me degno a ciò nè io nè altri 'l crede.  

neither myself nor others deem me worthy of it.

- *me degno a ciò nè io nè altri 'l crede* ‘me worthy of this neither I nor others believe it’ → “neither myself nor others deem me worthy of it”: the fronted object and its adjective move after the verb; the resumptive *'l* is absorbed into the English object-and-complement construction. *Io* becomes the emphatic “myself”, *ciò* becomes “it”, and believing becomes deeming.

> 34 Per che se del venire io m' abbandono,  
> 35 temo che la venuta non sia folle;  
> 36 se' savio, intendi me' ch' io non ragiono.”  

Wherefore, if I resign myself to go, I fear my going may prove foolish; thou art wise, and understandest better than I speak.”

- *se del venire io m' abbandono* ‘if to coming I give myself up’ → “if I resign myself to go”: the fronted prepositional phrase follows the verb and becomes an infinitive; giving oneself up becomes resigning oneself, and coming becomes going.
- *che la venuta non sia folle* ‘that the coming be not foolish’ → “my going may prove foolish”: the conjunction is omitted, the definite article becomes a possessive, and coming becomes going. The apprehensive *non* is not translated as a negation; the subjunctive of being becomes “may prove”.
- *intendi* ‘understandest’ → “and understandest”: a coordinating conjunction is supplied.
- *me' ch' io non ragiono* ‘better than I do not speak’ → “better than I speak”: the pleonastic *non* in the comparison has no separate English equivalent.

> 37 E quale è quei, che disvuol ciò che volle,  
> 38 e per novi pensier cangia proposta,  
> 39 sì che dal cominciar tutto si tolle,  
> 40 tal mi fec' io in quella oscura costa:  

And as one who unwills what he willed, and with new thoughts changes his purpose, so that he wholly quits the thing commenced, such I made myself on that dim coast:

- *E quale è quei* ‘And such as is that one’ → “And as one”: the comparative construction is shortened, omitting the copula and the demonstrative force of *quei*.
- *per novi pensier* ‘through new thoughts’ → “with new thoughts”: the explicitly causal preposition becomes “with”.
- *cangia proposta* ‘changes purpose’ → “changes his purpose”: a possessive is supplied.
- *dal cominciar tutto si tolle* ‘from beginning wholly removes himself’ → “he wholly quits the thing commenced”: withdrawing oneself becomes quitting a direct object; the substantivized infinitive becomes a phrase naming the undertaking already begun.
- *tal mi fec' io* ‘such myself made I’ → “such I made myself”: the subject moves before the verb and the reflexive pronoun after it.
- *in quella oscura costa* ‘on that dark slope’ → “on that dim coast”: dark becomes dim, while “coast” uses the older topographical sense corresponding to *costa*, a slope or hillside.

> 41 perchè pensando consumai la impresa,  
> 42 che fu nel cominciar cotanto tosta.  

for with thinking I wasted the enterprise, that had been so quick in its commencement.

- *perchè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *pensando* ‘thinking’ → “with thinking”: the gerund becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *consumai* ‘consumed’ → “I wasted”: the image of consuming is rendered as wasting.
- *che fu nel cominciar cotanto tosta* ‘which was at the beginning so very prompt’ → “that had been so quick in its commencement”: the simple past becomes past perfect, and the predicate adjective moves before the temporal phrase. The substantivized infinitive becomes “commencement”, with a possessive replacing the definite article. “Quick” takes *tosta* as prompt or swift rather than firm or resolute; *cotanto* is rendered simply as “so”.

> 43 “Se io ho ben la tua parola intesa,”  
> 44 rispose del magnanimo quell' ombra,  
> 45 “l' anima tua è da viltade offesa,  

“If I have rightly understood thy words,” replied that shade of the Magnanimous, “thy soul is smit with coward fear,

- *ho ben la tua parola intesa* ‘have well thy word understood’ → “have rightly understood thy words”: the participle moves before its object, “rightly” specifies the sense of *ben*, and singular *parola* becomes plural.
- *del magnanimo quell' ombra* ‘of the magnanimous one that shade’ → “that shade of the Magnanimous”: the modifying prepositional phrase moves after the noun.
- *l' anima tua* ‘the soul thine’ → “thy soul”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *è da viltade offesa* ‘is by cowardice assailed’ → “is smit with coward fear”: the participle moves before the phrase identifying the cause; *da* becomes “with”, and the single noun *viltade* is expanded into an adjective and noun.

> 46 la qual molte fiate l' uomo ingombra,  
> 47 sì che d' onrata impresa lo rivolve,  
> 48 come falso veder bestia quand' ombra.  

which oftentimes encumbers men, so that it turns them back from honoured enterprise; as false seeing does a startled beast.

- *molte fiate* ‘many times’ → “oftentimes”: the noun phrase becomes a single adverb.
- *l' uomo ingombra* ‘the man encumbers’ → “encumbers men”: the object moves after the verb, and the generic definite singular becomes an indefinite plural.
- *d' onrata impresa lo rivolve* ‘from honoured enterprise turns him back’ → “it turns them back from honoured enterprise”: the prepositional phrase moves after the verb and object, and singular *lo* becomes plural “them”, agreeing with the preceding “men”.
- *falso veder bestia quand' ombra* ‘false seeing a beast when it shies’ → “false seeing does a startled beast”: “does” supplies the verb understood from the preceding clause; the temporal clause describing the beast’s shying becomes the adjective “startled” before the noun.

> 49 Da questa tema acciocchè tu ti solve,  
> 50 dirotti, perch' io venni, e quel che intesi,  
> 51 nel primo punto che di te mi dolve.  

To free thee from this dread, I will tell thee why I came, and what I heard in the first moment when I took pity of thee.

- *Da questa tema acciocchè tu ti solve* ‘From this fear so that thou free thyself’ → “To free thee from this dread”: the purpose clause becomes an infinitive phrase, and the fronted phrase expressing release follows the verb. Dante’s freeing himself becomes the speaker’s freeing him; fear is rendered as dread.
- *di te mi dolve* ‘for thee it grieved me’ → “I took pity of thee”: the construction expressing pain experienced by the speaker becomes a personal construction expressing pity; the phrase referring to Dante moves after the verb.

> 52 Io era tra color, che son sospesi,  
> 53 e donna mi chiamò beata e bella,  
> 54 tal che di comandare io la richiesi.  

I was amongst them who are in suspense; and a Lady, so fair and blessed that I prayed her to command, called me.

- *son sospesi* ‘are suspended’ → “are in suspense”: the participle becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *e donna mi chiamò* ‘and a lady me called’ → “and a Lady,” … “called me”: the object follows the verb, and the verb is delayed until after the description and its dependent clause.
- *beata e bella tal che* ‘blessed and beautiful, such that’ → “so fair and blessed that”: the adjectives exchange order, beautiful becomes fair, and *tal* becomes the degree adverb “so” before them.
- *di comandare io la richiesi* ‘to command I her requested’ → “I prayed her to command”: the infinitive phrase moves after the main verb and its object; requesting becomes praying.

> 55 Lucevan gli occhi suoi più che la stella;  

Her eyes shone brighter than the star[s];

- *Lucevan gli occhi suoi* ‘Shone the eyes hers’ → “Her eyes shone”: the subject moves before the verb and the possessive before the noun.
- *più che la stella* ‘more than the star’ → “brighter than the star[s]”: the comparison makes brightness explicit; the bracketed revision changes the singular star to plural stars.

> 56 e cominciommi a dir soave e piana  
> 57 con angelica voce, in sua favella:  

and she began soft and gentle to tell me with angelic voice, in her language:

- *e cominciommi a dir* ‘and began to me to say’ → “and she began” … “to tell me”: the pronoun attached to the verb of beginning becomes the object of “tell”, and saying becomes telling.
- *soave e piana* ‘sweet and plain’ → “soft and gentle”: the adjectives are interpreted as qualities of gentleness rather than sweetness and plainness.

## Lines 58–90 — Beatrice’s Request and Reassurance

> 58 ‘O anima cortese Mantovana,  
> 59 di cui la fama ancor nel mondo dura,  
> 60 e durerà quanto il moto lontana!  

‘O courteous Mantuan Spirit, whose fame still lasts in the world, and will last as long as Time!

- *anima cortese Mantovana* ‘soul courteous Mantuan’ → “courteous Mantuan Spirit”: ‘soul’ becomes “Spirit”, and both adjectives move before the noun.
- *di cui la fama* ‘of whom the fame’ → “whose fame”: the prepositional relative becomes a possessive relative preceding the noun.
- *ancor nel mondo dura* ‘still in the world lasts’ → “still lasts in the world”: the verb moves before the phrase of place.
- *quanto il moto lontana* ‘as long as motion extends’ → “as long as Time”: the reference to motion and its continuance is condensed into “Time”; *lontana* has no separate verbal equivalent.

> 61 l’ amico mio, e non della ventura,  
> 62 nella diserta piaggia è impedito  
> 63 sì nel cammin, che volto è per paura;  

my friend, and not the friend of fortune, is so impeded in his way upon the desert shore, that he has turned back for terror;

- *l’amico mio* ‘the friend mine’ → “my friend”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *e non della ventura* ‘and not of fortune’ → “and not the friend of fortune”: “the friend” is supplied from the preceding phrase.
- *nella diserta piaggia* ‘on the deserted slope’ → “upon the desert shore”: *piaggia* is taken as ‘shore’ rather than ‘slope’; the phrase moves after the description of the impediment.
- *è impedito sì nel cammin* ‘is impeded so in the way’ → “is so impeded in his way”: “so” moves before the participle, and the definite article becomes the possessive “his”.
- *volto è per paura* ‘turned is for fear’ → “he has turned back for terror”: the construction with *essere* becomes an active present perfect, “back” is supplied, and ‘fear’ is strengthened to “terror”.

> 64 e temo che non sia già sì smarrito,  
> 65 ch’ io mi sia tardi al soccorso levata,  
> 66 per quel ch’ io ho di lui nel Cielo udito.  

and I fear he may already be so far astray, that I have risen too late for his relief, from what I heard of him in Heaven.

- *e temo che non sia già sì smarrito* ‘and I fear that he may not already be so lost’ → “and I fear he may already be so far astray”: *che* is not separately expressed; *non*, expletive after a verb of fearing, is correctly not translated as a negative. The subjunctive is represented by “may”, and ‘so lost’ becomes “so far astray”, with “far” supplied.
- *ch’io mi sia tardi al soccorso levata* ‘that I may have raised myself late to the aid’ → “that I have risen too late for his relief”: the reflexive becomes intransitive “have risen”, and the subjunctive becomes indicative. “Too” and “his” are supplied; the verb moves before the phrases describing lateness and purpose.
- *per quel ch’io ho di lui nel Cielo udito* ‘from that which I have of him in Heaven heard’ → “from what I heard of him in Heaven”: ‘that which’ becomes “what”, the compound perfect becomes a simple past, and “heard” moves before its accompanying prepositional phrases.

> 67 Or muovi, e con la tua parola ornata,  
> 68 e con ciò, ch’ è mestieri al suo campare,  
> 69 l’ aiuta sì, ch’ io ne sia consolata.  

Now go, and with thy ornate speech, and with what is necessary for his escape, help him so, that I may be consoled thereby.

- *muovi* ‘move’ → “go”: the command to move is rendered as a command to go.
- *con la tua parola ornata* ‘with thy word ornate’ → “with thy ornate speech”: singular *parola* is rendered collectively as “speech”, and the adjective moves before the noun.
- *con ciò, ch’è mestieri al suo campare* ‘with that which is needful for his surviving’ → “with what is necessary for his escape”: ‘that which’ becomes “what”; the substantivized infinitive *campare* becomes the noun “escape”, choosing escape rather than continued survival as its English expression.
- *l’aiuta sì* ‘him help so’ → “help him so”: the object pronoun moves after the imperative.
- *ne* ‘by it’ → “thereby”: the pronoun becomes an adverb and moves from before the verb to the end of the clause.

> 70 Io son Beatrice, che ti faccio andare;  

I am Beatrice who send thee;

- *che ti faccio andare* ‘who make thee go’ → “who send thee”: the causative construction becomes the single verb “send”, and the object pronoun follows that verb.

> 71 vegno di loco, ove tornar disio;  

I come from a place where I desire to return;

- *vegno di loco* ‘I come from place’ → “I come from a place”: the indefinite article is supplied.
- *ove tornar disio* ‘where to return I desire’ → “where I desire to return”: the infinitive moves after the finite verb.

> 72 amor mi mosse, che mi fa parlare.  

love moved me, that makes me speak.

- *amor mi mosse* ‘love me moved’ → “love moved me”: the object pronoun moves after the verb.
- *che mi fa parlare* ‘that me makes speak’ → “that makes me speak”: the object pronoun moves after “makes”.

> 73 Quando sarò dinanzi al Signor mio,  
> 74 di te mi loderò sovente a lui.’  

When I shall be before my Lord, I oft will praise thee to him.’

- *dinanzi al Signor mio* ‘before the Lord mine’ → “before my Lord”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *di te mi loderò sovente a lui* ‘of thee I shall praise myself often to him’ → “I oft will praise thee to him”: the idiomatic reflexive construction *lodarsi di* becomes transitive “praise”, with *di te* recast as the direct object “thee”. The object moves after the verb, while the adverb of frequency moves before the auxiliary.

> 75 Tacette allora, e poi cominciai io:  

She was silent then, and I began:

- *Tacette allora* ‘fell silent then’ → “She was silent then”: the cessation of speech is expressed as a state of silence.
- *e poi cominciai io* ‘and then began I’ → “and I began”: *poi* is omitted, and the explicit subject moves before the verb.

> 76 ‘O donna di virtù, sola per cui  
> 77 l’ umana spezie eccede ogni contento  
> 78 da quel ciel, che ha minori li cerchi sui!  

‘O Lady [of virtue], through whom [alone] mankind excels all that is contained within the heaven which has the smallest circles!

- *sola per cui* ‘alone through whom’ → “through whom [alone]”: *sola*, an adjective agreeing with *donna*, moves after the relative pronoun and is expressed there by “alone”.
- *l’umana spezie* ‘the human species’ → “mankind”: the noun phrase is condensed into a single collective noun.
- *eccede ogni contento da quel ciel* ‘exceeds everything contained by that heaven’ → “excels all that is contained within the heaven”: “excels” selects the sense of superiority in *eccede*. The substantivized *contento* becomes an explicit relative clause; ‘by’ becomes “within”, and the demonstrative ‘that’ becomes “the”.
- *che ha minori li cerchi sui* ‘which has smaller the circles its’ → “which has the smallest circles”: comparative *minori* is rendered as a superlative and placed attributively before “circles”; the possessive *sui* is omitted.

> 79 tanto m’ aggrada il tuo comandamento,  
> 80 che l’ ubbidir, se già fosse, m’ è tardi;  

so grateful to me is thy command, that my obeying, were it done already, seems tardy;

- *tanto m’aggrada il tuo comandamento* ‘so much pleases me thy command’ → “so grateful to me is thy command”: the verb meaning ‘pleases’ becomes “is” with the adjective “grateful”, used in the sense ‘pleasing’. The object pronoun becomes “to me”.
- *che l’ubbidir, se già fosse, m’è tardi* ‘that to obey, if already it were, is late to me’ → “that my obeying, were it done already, seems tardy”: the substantivized infinitive becomes the possessive gerund phrase “my obeying”. The conditional conjunction is replaced by inversion, “done” is supplied, and “already” moves after it. ‘Is late to me’ becomes “seems tardy”, with the first-person reference expressed instead in “my obeying”.

> 81 più non t’ è uopo aprirmi il tuo talento.  

it needs not that thou more explain to me thy wish.

- *più non t’è uopo aprirmi il tuo talento* ‘more it is not needful to thee to open to me thy wish’ → “it needs not that thou more explain to me thy wish”: the expression *è uopo* becomes impersonal “it needs”, with the negative following the verb. The infinitive becomes a finite clause introduced by “that”, and *t’*, ‘to thee’, becomes its subject “thou”. “More” moves into that clause; figurative ‘open’ becomes explicit “explain”, and the attached pronoun *mi* becomes “to me”.

> 82 Ma dimmi la cagion, che non ti guardi  
> 83 dello scender quaggiuso in questo centro  
> 84 dall’ ampio loco, ove tornar tu ardi.’  

But tell me the cause, why thou forbearest not to descend into this centre here below from the spacious place, to which thou burnest to return.’

- *la cagion, che* ‘the cause that’ → “the cause, why”: the relation introduced by *che* is made explicit with “why”.
- *non ti guardi dello scender* ‘thou dost not guard thyself against descending’ → “thou forbearest not to descend”: the reflexive construction becomes non-reflexive “forbearest”, with an infinitive complement instead of the prepositional construction. The negative follows the finite verb.
- *quaggiuso in questo centro* ‘here below into this centre’ → “into this centre here below”: the two expressions of place exchange positions.
- *ove tornar tu ardi* ‘where to return thou burnest’ → “to which thou burnest to return”: the relative adverb becomes a preposition and relative pronoun, explicitly expressing direction; the infinitive moves after the subject and finite verb.

> 85 ‘Da che tu vuoi saper cotanto addentro,  
> 86 dirotti brevemente,’ mi rispose,  
> 87 ‘perch’ io non temo di venir qua entro.  

‘Since thou desirest to know thus far, I will tell thee briefly,’ she replied, ‘why I fear not to come within this place.

- *cotanto addentro* ‘so far within’ → “thus far”: the explicit suggestion of inward depth in *addentro* is not separately retained.
- *mi rispose* ‘to me she replied’ → “she replied”: *mi*, ‘to me’, is omitted.
- *perch’io non temo di venir qua entro* ‘why I do not fear to come here within’ → “why I fear not to come within this place”: the negative moves after the finite verb. The Italian infinitive’s introductory *di* has no separate English equivalent; *qua entro* becomes “within this place”, replacing the adverb ‘here’ with an explicit noun phrase.

> 88 Temer si dee di sole quelle cose  
> 89 ch’ hanno potenza di fare altrui male;  
> 90 dell’ altre no, che non son paurose.  

Those things alone are to be feared that have the power of hurting; the others not, which are not fearful.

- *Temer si dee di sole quelle cose* ‘one ought to fear only those things’ → “Those things alone are to be feared”: the impersonal active construction becomes passive, with the things feared promoted to the subject and moved to the beginning. Obligation is expressed by “are to be”; *sole* becomes postposed “alone”, and *di* has no separate equivalent.
- *ch’hanno potenza di fare altrui male* ‘that have power to do harm to others’ → “that have the power of hurting”: the definite article is supplied before “power”; ‘to do harm’ becomes “of hurting”, changing the infinitival expression into a gerund construction. *Altrui*, ‘to others’, is omitted.
- *dell’altre no* ‘of the others not’ → “the others not”: the preposition is omitted, matching the preceding recasting of the things feared as the subject of a passive construction.

## Lines 91–123 — Heavenly Help and Renewed Courage

> 91 Io son fatta da Dio, sua mercè, tale,  
> 92 che la vostra miseria non mi tange,  
> 93 nè fiamma d’esto incendio non m’ assale.  

I am made such by God, in his grace, that your misery does not touch me; nor the flame of this burning assail me.

- *Io son fatta da Dio, sua mercè, tale* ‘I am made by God, through his grace, such’ → “I am made such by God, in his grace”: “such” moves immediately after “made”; the expression of gracious agency or favour becomes “in his grace”.
- *non mi tange* ‘not me touches’ → “does not touch me”: English uses an auxiliary for negation and places the object after the verb.
- *nè fiamma d’esto incendio non m’ assale* ‘nor flame of this fire not me assails’ → “nor the flame of this burning assail me”: “the” is supplied before “flame”; *incendio* becomes “burning”; Italian negative concord is reduced to “nor”; the object follows the verb. The English verb shares the preceding auxiliary “does”.

> 94 Donna è gentil nel Ciel, che si compiange  
> 95 di questo impedimento, ov’ io ti mando,  
> 96 sì che duro giudizio lassù frange.  

There is a noble Lady in Heaven who has such pity of this hindrance, for which I send thee, that she breaks the sharp judgment there on high.

- *Donna è gentil nel Ciel* ‘Lady is noble in the Heaven’ → “There is a noble Lady in Heaven”: the clause becomes existential, with “There” and the indefinite article supplied; *gentil* becomes an attributive adjective before the noun. The article contained in *nel* is omitted.
- *che si compiange … sì che* ‘who feels compassion … so that’ → “who has such pity” … “that”: the pronominal verb becomes a construction with “has” and a noun; the degree expressed later by *sì* is anticipated by “such” before “pity”.
- *ov’ io ti mando* ‘where I thee send’ → “for which I send thee”: the locative relative is interpreted as referring to the occasion or purpose of the sending; the object follows the verb.
- *duro giudizio lassù frange* ‘harsh judgment up there she breaks’ → “she breaks the sharp judgment there on high”: the verb precedes its object; “the” is supplied; *duro* becomes “sharp”, and *lassù* is expanded to “there on high”.

> 97 Questa chiese Lucia in suo dimando,  
> 98 e disse: “Or ha bisogno il tuo fedele  
> 99 di te, ed io a te lo raccomando.”  

She called Lucia, in her request, and said: “Now thy faithful one has need of thee; and I commend him to thee.”

- *Questa* ‘This one’ → “She”: a demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun.
- *chiese Lucia* ‘asked for Lucia’ → “called Lucia”: the request for someone’s presence is rendered as calling that person.
- *ha bisogno il tuo fedele di te* ‘has need thy faithful one of thee’ → “thy faithful one has need of thee”: the subject moves before the verb; the substantival adjective *fedele* is expressed with the added noun “one”.
- *ed io a te lo raccomando* ‘and I to thee him commend’ → “and I commend him to thee”: both objects move after the verb, and their relative order is reversed.

> 100 Lucia, nimica di ciascun crudele,  
> 101 si mosse, e venne al loco dov’ io era,  
> 102 che mi sedea con l’antica Rachele.  

Lucia, enemy of all cruelty, arose and came to the place where I was sitting with the ancient Rachel.

- *nimica di ciascun crudele* ‘enemy of every cruel person’ → “enemy of all cruelty”: the substantival adjective referring to cruel individuals becomes an abstract noun.
- *si mosse* ‘moved herself’ → “arose”: the pronominal expression for setting oneself in motion becomes a verb specifying rising.
- *dov’ io era, che mi sedea* ‘where I was, who was sitting’ → “where I was sitting”: the clause introduced by *che* is absorbed into the preceding clause, producing one past-progressive construction. The pronominal *mi* has no separate English equivalent.

> 103 Disse: “Beatrice, loda di Dio vera,  
> 104 che non soccorri quei che t’ amò tanto,  
> 105 che uscìo per te della volgare schiera?  

She said: “Beatrice, true praise of God; why helpest thou not him who loved thee so, that for thee he left the vulgar crowd?

- *loda di Dio vera* ‘praise of God true’ → “true praise of God”: the adjective moves before the noun and its dependent phrase.
- *quei che t’ amò tanto* ‘that one who thee loved so much’ → “him who loved thee so”: the demonstrative becomes an object personal pronoun; “thee” follows the verb; *tanto* is rendered by “so” rather than ‘so much’.
- *che uscìo per te della volgare schiera* ‘that he went out for thee from the common company’ → “that for thee he left the vulgar crowd”: “for thee” moves before the verb; the intransitive construction ‘went out from’ becomes transitive “left”; *schiera*, a company or body of people, becomes “crowd”.

> 106 Non odi tu la pieta del suo pianto?  

Hearest not thou the misery of his plaint?

- *Non odi tu* ‘Not hearest thou’ → “Hearest not thou”: the negative moves after the verb.
- *la pieta del suo pianto* ‘the piteousness of his weeping’ → “the misery of his plaint”: *pieta* is interpreted as distress rather than pity felt by someone else; *pianto* becomes “plaint”, emphasizing lamentation rather than weeping.

> 107 Non vedi tu la morte che il combatte  
> 108 su la fiumana, ove il mar non ha vanto?”  

Seest thou not the death which combats him upon the river [over] which the sea has no boast?”

- *Non vedi tu* ‘Not seest thou’ → “Seest thou not”: the negative moves after both verb and subject.
- *che il combatte* ‘which him combats’ → “which combats him”: the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *fiumana* ‘torrent’ → “river”: the implication of a swollen or rushing stream is not explicitly conveyed.
- *ove* ‘where’ → “[over] which”: the locative relative becomes a prepositional relative expressing comparison; the bracketed revision makes the relation one of boasting over something rather than boasting at a place.

> 109 Al mondo non fur mai persone ratte  
> 110 a far lor pro, nè a fuggir lor danno,  
> 111 com’ io, dopo cotai parole fatte,  
> 112 venni quaggiù dal mio beato scanno,  

None on earth were ever swift to seek their good, or flee their hurt, as I, after these words were uttered, to come from my blessed seat;

- *Al mondo non fur mai persone ratte* ‘In the world never were persons swift’ → “None on earth were ever swift”: *persone* and the negation are combined in “None”; *mai* consequently becomes “ever”. The opening place phrase moves after the subject, and ‘in the world’ becomes “on earth”.
- *a far lor pro* ‘to do their good’ → “to seek their good”: accomplishing or procuring benefit is rendered as seeking it.
- *nè a fuggir lor danno* ‘nor to flee their hurt’ → “or flee their hurt”: the continuing Italian negation becomes “or” under the scope of “None”; the repeated infinitive marker is omitted.
- *dopo cotai parole fatte* ‘after such words made’ → “after these words were uttered”: ‘such’ becomes “these”; the participial construction becomes a finite passive clause; ‘made’ becomes “uttered”.
- *venni quaggiù* ‘I came down here’ → “to come”: the finite past-tense verb becomes an infinitive dependent on the comparison with being swift; *quaggiù* is omitted.

> 113 fidandomi del tuo parlare onesto,  
> 114 che onora te, e quei che udito l’ hanno.’  

confiding in thy noble speech, which honours thee, and them who have heard it.’

- *fidandomi del tuo parlare onesto* ‘trusting in thy honourable speaking’ → “confiding in thy noble speech”: the pronominal construction *fidarsi di* becomes non-pronominal “confiding in”; the substantival infinitive *parlare* becomes the noun “speech”; *onesto* becomes “noble” and moves before the noun.
- *e quei che udito l’ hanno* ‘and those who heard it have’ → “and them who have heard it”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun; the auxiliary moves before the participle, and the object follows the completed verb phrase.

> 115 Poscia che m’ ebbe ragionato questo,  
> 116 gli occhi lucenti lagrimando volse:  
> 117 per che mi fece del venir più presto;  

After saying this to me, she turned away her bright eyes weeping; by which she made me hasten more to come;

- *Poscia che m’ ebbe ragionato questo* ‘After she had related this to me’ → “After saying this to me”: a finite past-perfect clause becomes a non-finite construction, without an explicit perfect tense; *ragionato* becomes “saying”, and the object pronoun moves from before the verb to the final prepositional phrase.
- *gli occhi lucenti lagrimando volse* ‘the shining eyes, weeping, she turned’ → “she turned away her bright eyes weeping”: the verb moves before its object, and “weeping” moves to the end; the definite article becomes a possessive; “away” is supplied. *Lucenti* becomes “bright” and moves before the noun.
- *per che* ‘for which reason’ → “by which”: the causal connection is expressed as a means or agency.
- *mi fece del venir più presto* ‘me she made, as to coming, quicker’ → “she made me hasten more to come”: the object follows “made”; the adjective expressing increased readiness or speed becomes the verb “hasten” with “more”; the construction specifying the respect in which he became quicker becomes the infinitive “to come”.

> 118 e venni a te così, com’ ella volse;  
> 119 dinanzi a quella fiera ti levai,  
> 120 che del bel monte il corto andar ti tolse.  

and thus I came to thee, as she desired; took thee from before that savage beast, which bereft thee of the short way to the beautiful mountain.

- *e venni a te così* ‘and I came to thee thus’ → “and thus I came to thee”: “thus” moves before the subject and verb.
- *dinanzi a quella fiera ti levai* ‘from before that wild beast thee I lifted’ → “took thee from before that savage beast”: the verb and its object precede the place phrase; *levai* is rendered as removal by “took”; the noun *fiera* is expressed by “savage beast”.
- *che del bel monte il corto andar ti tolse* ‘which of the beautiful mountain the short going from thee took away’ → “which bereft thee of the short way to the beautiful mountain”: the construction ‘take something away from someone’ becomes ‘bereave someone of something’. The substantival infinitive *andar* becomes “way”; the mountain phrase moves after it, and its preposition becomes “to”, explicitly expressing destination.

> 121 Dunque che è? perchè, perchè ristai?  

What is it then? why, why haltest thou?

- *Dunque che è* ‘Then what is it’ → “What is it then”: the opening connective moves to the end of the question.

> 122 perchè tanta viltà nel cuore allette?  

why lodgest in thy heart such coward fear?

- *perchè tanta viltà nel cuore allette* ‘why so much cowardice in the heart dost thou lodge’ → “why lodgest in thy heart such coward fear”: the verb moves before the place phrase and object, reversing their Italian arrangement. Quantitative *tanta* becomes qualitative “such”; *viltà* becomes the adjective-and-noun expression “coward fear”; the definite article before *cuore* becomes the possessive “thy”.

> 123 perchè ardire e franchezza non hai,  

why art thou not bold and free,

- *perchè ardire e franchezza non hai* ‘why daring and confidence hast thou not’ → “why art thou not bold and free”: possession of two qualities becomes a construction with “art” and two predicative adjectives; the verb and negative move before those complements. *Ardire* becomes “bold”, while *franchezza*, here confidence or fearlessness, is rendered as “free”.

## Lines 124–142 — Courage Restored and the Journey Begun

> 124 poscia che tai tre Donne benedette  
> 125 curan di te nella corte del cielo,  
> 126 e il mio parlar tanto ben t’ impromette ?”  

when three such blessed Ladies care for thee in the court of Heaven, and my words promise thee so much good?”

- *poscia che* ‘since’ → “when”: the causal conjunction is rendered by a temporal one.
- *tai tre Donne benedette* ‘such three Ladies blessed’ → “three such blessed Ladies”: “such” follows the numeral, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *il mio parlar tanto ben t’impromette* ‘my speaking so much good to thee promises’ → “my words promise thee so much good”: the substantivized infinitive becomes the plural noun “words”; the verb consequently becomes plural, and both objects follow it rather than precede it.

> 127 Quali i fioretti dal notturno gelo  
> 128 chinati e chiusi, poi che il sol gl’ imbianca,  
> 129 si drizzan tutti aperti in loro stelo:  

As flowerets, by the nightly chillness bended down and closed, erect themselves all open on their stems when the sun whitens them

- *i fioretti* ‘the flowerets’ → “flowerets”: the definite article is omitted.
- *dal notturno gelo chinati e chiusi* ‘by the nightly frost bent and closed’ → “by the nightly chillness bended down and closed”: “chillness” replaces ‘frost’, and “down” makes the direction of bending explicit.
- *poi che il sol gl’imbianca* ‘after the sun whitens them’ → “when the sun whitens them”: ‘after’ becomes “when”, and the temporal clause moves from before the main verb to the end of the sentence.
- *si drizzan* ‘themselves they straighten’ → “erect themselves”: “erect” renders ‘straighten’, and the reflexive pronoun follows the verb.
- *in loro stelo* ‘in their stem’ → “on their stems”: the preposition changes, and the distributive singular becomes plural.

> 130 tal mi fec’ io, di mia virtute stanca;  
> 131 e tanto buono ardire al cuor mi corse,  
> 132 ch’ io cominciai come persona franca:  

: thus I did, with my fainting courage; and so much good daring ran into my heart, that I began as one set free:

- *tal mi fec’ io* ‘such I made myself’ → “thus I did”: the reflexive construction becomes a non-reflexive verb referring back to the preceding action; ‘such’ becomes the adverb “thus”, and the expressed subject moves before the verb.
- *di mia virtute stanca* ‘as regards my strength weary’ → “with my fainting courage”: the phrase specifying the respect in which the comparison applies is introduced by “with”; ‘strength’ is interpreted as “courage”, ‘weary’ becomes “fainting”, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *al cuor mi corse* ‘to the heart to me ran’ → “ran into my heart”: the verb moves before the prepositional phrase, the personal dative becomes a possessive, and “into” replaces ‘to’.
- *persona franca* ‘a person free’ → “one set free”: “one” replaces ‘a person’, and the adjective describing freedom becomes a participial phrase suggesting release.

> 133 “O pietosa colei che mi soccorse,  
> 134 e tu cortese, ch’ ubbidisti tosto  
> 135 alle vere parole che ti porse!  

“O compassionate she, who succoured me! and courteous thou, who quickly didst obey the true words that she gave thee!

- *colei* ‘that woman’ → “she”: the demonstrative pronoun becomes a personal pronoun.
- *che mi soccorse* ‘who me succoured’ → “who succoured me”: the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *e tu cortese* ‘and thou courteous’ → “and courteous thou”: the adjective and pronoun exchange places.
- *ubbidisti tosto* ‘obeyed promptly’ → “quickly didst obey”: the adverb precedes the verb, and the simple past is expressed with the auxiliary “didst”.
- *alle vere parole* ‘to the true words’ → “the true words”: the Italian prepositional complement becomes the direct object required by English “obey”.
- *che ti porse* ‘that to thee she offered’ → “that she gave thee”: ‘offered’ becomes “gave”, and the indirect-object pronoun follows the verb.

> 136 Tu m’ hai con desiderio il cuor disposto  
> 137 sì al venir, con le parole tue,  
> 138 ch’ io son tornato nel primo proposto.  

Thou hast disposed my heart with such desire to go, by what thou sayest, that I have returned to my first purpose.

- *Tu m’hai con desiderio il cuor disposto sì al venir* ‘Thou to me hast with desire the heart disposed so to coming’ → “Thou hast disposed my heart with such desire to go”: the personal dative becomes “my”; the auxiliary and participle are brought together before the object; and the phrase expressing desire follows that object. The degree expressed by *sì*, qualifying the disposition, is transferred to “such desire”; the substantivized infinitive becomes “to go”, with ‘coming’ rendered as motion away rather than towards.
- *con le parole tue* ‘with the words thine’ → “by what thou sayest”: “by” replaces ‘with’, and the noun with its possessive becomes a relative clause containing a present-tense verb.
- *ch’ io son tornato* ‘that I am returned’ → “that I have returned”: English uses “have” where Italian forms the compound tense with ‘be’.
- *nel primo proposto* ‘in the first purpose’ → “to my first purpose”: “to” replaces ‘in’, and the definite article becomes a possessive.

> 139 Or va, chè un sol volere è d’ ambo due;  
> 140 tu duca, tu signore, e tu maestro.”  

Now go, for both have one will; thou guide, thou lord and master.”

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *un sol volere è d’ ambo due* ‘a single will is of both two’ → “both have one will”: possession expressed through ‘be of’ becomes a construction with “have”, making the possessors the subject; ‘a single’ is compressed into “one”, and the doubled expression ‘both two’ becomes “both”.
- *e tu maestro* ‘and thou master’ → “and master”: the repeated pronoun is omitted.

> 141 Così gli dissi; e poichè mosso fue,  
> 142 entrai per lo cammino alto e silvestro.  

Thus I spake to him; and he moving, I entered on the arduous and savage way.

- *Così gli dissi* ‘Thus to him I said’ → “Thus I spake to him”: ‘said’ becomes “spake”, and the indirect object moves after the verb.
- *e poichè mosso fue* ‘and after he had set off’ → “and he moving”: the finite temporal clause becomes an absolute participial construction; the explicit ‘after’ disappears, and completed prior motion becomes motion presented as ongoing.
- *entrai per* ‘I entered through’ → “I entered on”: the preposition expressing passage is replaced by “on”.
- *lo cammino alto e silvestro* ‘the way high and wooded’ → “the arduous and savage way”: *alto* is interpreted as difficulty rather than literal height, “savage” replaces ‘wooded’, and both adjectives move before the noun.

## Summary — Close Echoes and Prose Reshaping

Carlyle’s translation stays close to the Italian vocabulary and sequence of thought, often preserving its images and unusual expressions: *la guerra* remains “the war”, *disvuol ciò che volle* becomes “unwills what he willed”, and *il sol gl’ imbianca* becomes “the sun whitens them”. Yet English prose regularly reshapes the word order. Objects and their pronouns follow verbs, adjectives and possessives generally precede nouns, and qualifying phrases move beside the words they qualify. Thus *di Silvio lo parente* becomes “the father of Sylvius”, while *del magnanimo quell’ ombra* becomes “that shade of the Magnanimous”. More generally, subjects implicit in Italian verb endings are supplied, and the Italian articles accompanying possessives are left out.

Word choice frequently makes an interpretation explicit. *Mente* becomes “memory”, *virtute* becomes “courage”, and *alto* becomes “arduous” when it qualifies the passage or way. Such choices narrow broader terms; elsewhere the translation changes the emphasis, as when *conforto* becomes “confirmation”, or *franchezza*, ‘confidence’ or ‘fearlessness’, becomes “free”. Some concrete features are softened or displaced: *fiumana*, ‘torrent’, becomes “river”, and *silvestro*, ‘wooded’ or ‘wild’, becomes “savage”. Alongside these departures, close verbal echoes such as “high effect”, “Papal Mantle”, and “good daring” retain the Italian’s distinctive phrasing rather than replacing it with more ordinary English.

Changes of construction are equally recurrent. Italian substantivized infinitives become nouns, as *parlare* becomes “speech” and *andar* becomes “way”; reflexive and impersonal expressions become direct or passive constructions. *Ti faccio andare*, ‘make thee go’, is condensed into “send thee”, while *Temer si dee* is recast through “are to be feared”. Possession of qualities becomes a description with adjectives when *ardire e franchezza non hai* becomes “art thou not bold and free”. Finite clauses can also become participial phrases: *Poscia che m’ ebbe ragionato questo* becomes “After saying this to me”, and *poichè mosso fue* becomes “he moving”, reducing the explicit marking of tense and temporal sequence.

Omission and supplementation help these adjustments. Repeated pronouns, auxiliaries, and prepositions are sometimes left implicit; *ne* in *recarne* and *quaggiù* after *venni* receive no separate equivalent. Italian expletive negatives are also omitted without reversing the intended meaning. Conversely, “there” and “in body” expand *sensibilmente*, while “the friend” supplies the noun understood in *e non della ventura*. Connectives occasionally shift the logical emphasis, notably *Però* becoming “But” and *poscia che*, ‘since’, becoming “when”. The result is a translation closely tethered to Italian diction and imagery, but repeatedly reorganized, expanded, or compressed to make its grammatical relationships explicit in English.
