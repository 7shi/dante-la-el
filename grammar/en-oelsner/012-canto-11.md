# Inferno 11

## Lines 1–24 — The Pause Before the Descent

> 1 In su l' estremità d' un' alta ripa,  
> 2 che facevan gran pietre rotte in cerchio,  
> 3 venimmo sopra più crudele stipa ;  

Upon the edge of a high bank, formed by large broken stones in a circle, we came above a still more cruel throng;

- *che facevan* ‘which … made’ → “formed by”: the active relative clause becomes a passive participial phrase; the stones that were its subject become the agent introduced by “by”.
- *pietre rotte* ‘stones broken’ → “broken stones”: the adjective moves before the noun.
- *più crudele stipa* ‘more cruel packed mass’ → “a still more cruel throng”: “a” and “still” are supplied, and “throng” renders the collective noun without its explicit suggestion of tight packing.

> 4 e quivi per l' orribile soperchio  
> 5 del puzzo, che il profondo abisso gitta,  
> 6 ci raccostammo dietro ad un coperchio  
> 7 d' un grande avello, ov' io vidi una scritta  

and here, because of the horrible excess of stench which the deep abyss throws out, we approached it under cover of a great monument, whereon I saw a writing

- *quivi* ‘there’ → “here”: the spatial demonstrative changes.
- *gitta* ‘throws’ → “throws out”: the directional particle is supplied.
- *ci raccostammo* ‘we drew ourselves close’ → “we approached it”: the reflexive construction becomes transitive, with “it” supplied as its object.
- *dietro ad un coperchio* ‘behind a lid’ → “under cover”: a concrete object and the position behind it become an idiomatic expression of shelter.
- *avello* ‘tomb’ → “monument”: a broader term replaces the specific word for a tomb.
- *ov'* ‘where’ → “whereon”: the translation specifies a surface relationship that the Italian relative adverb leaves unstated.

> 8 che diceva: “Anastasio papa guardo,  
> 9 lo qual trasse Fotin della via dritta.”  

that said: “I hold Pope Anastasius, whom Photinus drew from the straight way.”

- *Anastasio papa guardo* ‘Anastasius pope I guard’ → “I hold Pope Anastasius”: the object moves after the verb, the title moves before the name, and “hold” replaces ‘guard’.
- *lo qual trasse Fotin* ‘whom drew Photinus’ → “whom Photinus drew”: the subject moves before the verb.
- *della via dritta* ‘from the way straight’ → “from the straight way”: the adjective moves before the noun.

> 10 “Lo nostro scender convien esser tardo,  
> 11 sì che s' ausi prima un poco il senso  
> 12 al tristo fiato, e poi non fia riguardo.”  

“Our descent we must delay, till sense be somewhat used to the dismal blast, and then we shall not heed it.”

- *Lo nostro scender convien esser tardo* ‘our descending must be slow’ → “Our descent we must delay”: the substantival infinitive becomes the noun “descent”; the impersonal construction becomes a personal one with “we”; and ‘be slow’ becomes the transitive verb “delay”, with “Our descent” as its fronted object.
- *sì che* ‘so that’ → “till”: a conjunction expressing purpose or consequence becomes temporal.
- *s'ausi … il senso* ‘may accustom itself … the sense’ → “sense be somewhat used”: the postposed subject moves before the verb and loses its article; the reflexive verb becomes a construction expressing an accustomed state.
- *prima un poco* ‘first a little’ → “somewhat”: “somewhat” condenses ‘a little’, while ‘first’ is omitted.
- *al tristo fiato* ‘to the sad breath’ → “to the dismal blast”: “dismal” replaces ‘sad’, and “blast” gives a stronger rendering of ‘breath’.
- *e poi non fia riguardo* ‘and then there will be no concern’ → “and then we shall not heed it”: the existential construction becomes a personal statement; the noun becomes the verb “heed”, and “it” is supplied as its object.

> 13 Così il maestro ; ed io : “Alcun compenso,”  
> 14 dissi lui, “trova, che il tempo non passi  
> 15 perduto.” Ed egli: “Vedi ch' a ciò penso.  

Thus the Master; and I said to him: “Find some compensation, that the time may not be lost.” And he: “Thou seest that I intend it.

- *ed io : Alcun compenso, dissi lui, trova* ‘and I: some compensation, said to him, find’ → “and I said to him: “Find some compensation,”: the reporting clause is brought together before the speech instead of interrupting it, and the imperative moves before its object.
- *passi perduto* ‘pass lost’ → “be lost”: the verb expressing the passage of time becomes the auxiliary of a passive construction.
- *a ciò penso* ‘of this I think’ → “I intend it”: thinking about something is rendered as intending it; the prepositional complement becomes a direct object and moves after the verb.

> 16 Figliuol mio, dentro da cotesti sassi,”  
> 17 cominciò poi a dir, “son tre cerchietti  
> 18 di grado in grado, come quei che lassi.  

My Son, within these stones,” he then began to say, “are three circlets in gradation, like those thou leavest.

- *Figliuol mio* ‘son my’ → “My Son”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *cotesti* ‘those’ → “these”: the demonstrative associated with the person addressed becomes the English proximal demonstrative.
- *cominciò poi a dir* ‘began then to say’ → “he then began to say”: “then” moves before the finite verb.
- *di grado in grado* ‘from step to step’ → “in gradation”: the repeated, distributive phrase becomes a prepositional phrase with an abstract noun.
- *quei che* ‘those that’ → “those”: the relative pronoun is omitted.

> 19 Tutti son pien di spirti maledetti ;  
> 20 ma perchè poi ti basti pur la vista,  
> 21 intendi come, e perchè son costretti.  

They all are filled with spirits accurst; but, that the sight of these hereafter may of itself suffice thee, hearken how and wherefore they are pent up.

- *Tutti* ‘all’ → “They all”: the substantive ‘all’ is expanded with an explicit personal pronoun.
- *pien di* ‘full of’ → “filled with”: an adjective becomes a participle, with a corresponding change of preposition.
- *poi ti basti pur la vista* ‘afterwards to thee may suffice only the sight’ → “the sight of these hereafter may of itself suffice thee”: the subject moves before the verb, and the indirect object moves after it; “of these” is supplied; the temporal adverb moves after the expanded subject; and ‘only’ becomes “of itself”.
- *intendi* ‘understand’ → “hearken”: the imperative is interpreted as an instruction to listen rather than to understand.
- *son costretti* ‘are constrained’ → “are pent up”: the broader verb of constraint is rendered specifically as confinement.

> 22 D' ogni malizia ch' odio in cielo acquista,  
> 23 ingiuria è il fine; ed ogni fin cotale  
> 24 o con forza, o con frode altrui contrista.  

Of all malice, which gains hatred in Heaven, the end is injury; and every such end, either by force or by fraud, aggrieveth others.

- *D' ogni malizia* ‘of every malice’ → “Of all malice”: the distributive “every” becomes “all”, treating malice collectively.
- *ch' odio in cielo acquista* ‘which hatred in heaven gains’ → “which gains hatred in Heaven”: the verb moves before its object and the phrase specifying place.
- *ingiuria è il fine* ‘injury is the end’ → “the end is injury”: the two sides of the copular construction exchange positions.
- *ogni fin cotale* ‘every end such’ → “every such end”: the demonstrative adjective moves before the noun.
- *o con forza, o con frode* ‘either with force or with fraud’ → “either by force or by fraud”: “by” replaces ‘with’ in both expressions of means.
- *altrui contrista* ‘others saddens’ → “aggrieveth others”: the object moves after the verb, and “aggrieveth” replaces ‘saddens’.

## Lines 25–57 — Violence and Fraud

> 25 Ma perchè frode è dell’ uom proprio male  
> 26 più spiace a Dio; e però stan di sutto  
> 27 gli frodolenti, e più dolor gli assale.  

But because fraud is a vice peculiar to man, it more displeases God; and therefore the fraudulent are placed beneath, and more pain assails them.

- *dell’ uom proprio male* ‘man's peculiar evil’ → “a vice peculiar to man”: “vice” narrows ‘evil’; the possessive construction becomes “to man”, and the qualification follows the noun. An indefinite article is supplied.
- *più spiace a Dio* ‘is more displeasing to God’ → “it more displeases God”: the Italian construction with an indirect object becomes an English transitive construction.
- *stan di sutto gli frodolenti* ‘stand below the fraudulent’ → “the fraudulent are placed beneath”: the subject moves before the verb, and ‘stand’ becomes the passive “are placed”.

> 28 De’ violenti il primo cerchio è tutto;  
> 29 ma perchè si fa forza a tre persone,  
> 30 in tre gironi è distinto e costrutto.  

All the first circle is for the violent; but as violence may be done to three persons, it is formed and distinguished into three rounds.

- *De’ violenti il primo cerchio è tutto* ‘of the violent the first circle is wholly’ → “All the first circle is for the violent”: the opening possessessive phrase becomes a phrase with “for” at the end; *tutto*, expressing the circle's entire allocation, becomes “All” before the subject.
- *ma perchè* ‘but because’ → “but as”: the causal conjunction is rendered by “as”.
- *si fa forza a tre persone* ‘violence is done to three persons’ → “violence may be done to three persons”: “may” makes possibility explicit where the Italian uses the present indicative.
- *in tre gironi è distinto e costrutto* ‘into three rounds it is distinguished and constructed’ → “it is formed and distinguished into three rounds”: the prepositional phrase moves to the end; the two participles reverse order, and ‘constructed’ becomes “formed”.

> 31 A Dio, a sè, al prossimo si puone  
> 32 far forza; dico in loro, e in lor cose,  
> 33 come udirai con aperta ragione.  

To God, to one's self, and to one's neighbour, may violence be done; I say in them and in their things, as thou shalt hear with evident discourse.

- *A Dio, a sè, al prossimo* ‘To God, to oneself, to the neighbour’ → “To God, to one's self, and to one's neighbour,”: “and” is supplied, and the generic definite article before *prossimo* becomes a possessive.
- *si puone far forza* ‘one can do violence’ → “may violence be done”: the impersonal active construction becomes passive, with violence as its subject.
- *aperta ragione* ‘open reasoning’ → “evident discourse”: ‘open’ is interpreted as ‘clear’ or ‘evident’, and the reasoning is rendered as discourse.

> 34 Morte per forza, e ferute dogliose  
> 35 nel prossimo si danno, e nel suo avere  
> 36 ruine, incendi e tollette dannose:  

By force, death and painful wounds may be inflicted upon one's neighbour; and upon his substance, devastations, burnings, and injurious extortions:

- *Morte per forza* ‘death by force’ → “By force, death”: the modifying phrase is moved before the noun.
- *ferute dogliose* ‘wounds painful’ → “painful wounds”: the adjective moves before the noun.
- *nel prossimo* ‘in the neighbour’ → “upon one's neighbour”: “upon” replaces ‘in’, and a possessive replaces the generic definite article. The phrase moves after the verb.
- *si danno* ‘are given’ → “may be inflicted”: “may” is supplied, and ‘given’ becomes the idiomatic “inflicted”.
- *nel suo avere* ‘in his possessions’ → “upon his substance”: the preposition changes, and the collective noun is rendered by “substance”.
- *ruine* ‘ruins’ → “devastations”: the translation uses a noun explicitly denoting destructive acts or effects.
- *tollette dannose* ‘harmful takings’ → “injurious extortions”: the takings are interpreted specifically as extortions, and the adjective moves before the noun.

> 37 onde omicidi, e ciascun che mal fiere,  
> 38 guastatori e predon, tutti tormenta  
> 39 lo giron primo per diverse schiere.  

wherefore the first round torments all homicides and every one who strikes maliciously, all plunderers and robbers, in different bands.

- *mal fiere* ‘ill strikes’ → “strikes maliciously”: the adverb moves after the verb, and *mal* is interpreted as expressing malicious intent.
- *guastatori* ‘ravagers’ → “plunderers”: a term emphasizing destruction is rendered by one emphasizing pillage.
- *tutti tormenta lo giron primo* ‘all [of them] torments the round first’ → “the first round torments all”, “all”: the subject moves before the verb, and its adjective moves before the noun. The resumptive *tutti*, which follows the listed objects, becomes a determiner before them and is repeated before “plunderers”.
- *per diverse schiere* ‘by different bands’ → “in different bands”: “in” replaces ‘by’ in expressing the grouping.

> 40 Puote uomo avere in sè man violenta  
> 41 e ne’ suoi beni: e però nel secondo  
> 42 giron convien che senza pro si penta  
> 43 qualunque priva sè del vostro mondo,  

A man may lay violent hand upon himself, and upon his property: and therefore in the second round must every one repent in vain who deprives himself of your world,

- *Puote uomo* ‘Can man’ → “A man may”: the subject moves before the modal, an indefinite article is supplied, and ‘can’ becomes “may”.
- *avere in sè man violenta* ‘have in himself hand violent’ → “lay violent hand upon himself”: ‘have’ becomes “lay”, and ‘in’ becomes “upon”. The object moves before the prepositional phrase, and the adjective moves before its noun.
- *ne’ suoi beni* ‘in his goods’ → “upon his property”: the preposition changes, and the plural noun becomes a singular collective.
- *convien che* ‘it is necessary that’ → “must”: an impersonal main clause introducing a subordinate clause becomes a modal verb.
- *senza pro si penta* ‘without benefit he repent’ → “repent in vain”: ‘without benefit’ becomes the idiom “in vain” and moves after the verb; the Italian subjunctive becomes an infinitive governed by “must”.
- *qualunque* ‘whoever’ → “every one”, “who”: the indefinite relative is expanded into an antecedent and a relative pronoun. The antecedent moves before “repent”, while its relative clause follows.

> 44 biscazza e fonde la suo facultade,  

gambles away and dissipates his wealth,

- *biscazza* ‘gambles’ → “gambles away”: “away” explicitly expresses the loss resulting from the gambling.
- *fonde* ‘melts’ → “dissipates”: the figurative ‘melting’ is rendered directly as dissipation.

> 45 e piange là dove esser dee giocondo.  

and weeps there where he should be joyous.

- *esser dee giocondo* ‘be he ought joyous’ → “he should be joyous”: the modal precedes rather than follows the infinitive, and ‘ought’ is rendered by “should”.

> 46 Puossi far forza nella Deitade,  
> 47 col cor negando e bestemmiando quella,  
> 48 e spregiando Natura, e sua bontade:  

Violence may be done against the Deity, in the heart denying and blaspheming Him; and disdaining Nature and her bounty:

- *Puossi far forza* ‘one can do violence’ → “Violence may be done”: the impersonal active construction becomes passive, with violence as its subject.
- *nella Deitade* ‘in the Deity’ → “against the Deity”: “against” replaces ‘in’.
- *col cor* ‘with the heart’ → “in the heart”: an instrumental phrase becomes a locative one.
- *quella* ‘that one’ → “Him”: the feminine demonstrative referring to *Deitade* becomes a masculine personal pronoun.
- *bontade* ‘goodness’ → “bounty”: the translation selects the sense of beneficence or generosity rather than goodness generally.

> 49 e però lo minor giron suggella  
> 50 del segno suo e Sodoma e Caorsa,  
> 51 e chi spregiando Dio col cor favella.  

and hence the smallest round seals with its mark both Sodom and Cahors, and all who speak with disparagement of God in their hearts.

- *però* ‘therefore’ → “hence”: the causal connection is expressed by a different adverb.
- *lo minor giron* ‘the smaller round’ → “the smallest round”: the Italian comparative form is rendered as an English superlative.
- *del segno suo* ‘with the mark its’ → “with its mark”: the postposed possessive moves before the noun.
- *e Sodoma e Caorsa* ‘and Sodom and Cahors’ → “both Sodom and Cahors”: the repeated conjunction becomes the English correlative construction “both” … “and”.
- *chi* ‘whoever’ → “all who”: the singular indefinite relative becomes an explicitly plural antecedent and relative pronoun.
- *spregiando Dio* ‘disdaining God’ → “with disparagement of God”: the gerund becomes a prepositional phrase containing a noun, and its direct object becomes an “of” phrase. The phrase moves after the verb of speaking.
- *col cor* ‘with the heart’ → “in their hearts”: the preposition changes, the definite article becomes a possessive, and the singular noun becomes plural.
- *favella* ‘speaks’ → “speak”: the verb becomes plural to agree with “all who” and moves before its modifying phrases.

> 52 La frode, ond’ ogni coscïenza è morsa,  
> 53 può l’ uomo usare in colui, ch’ in lui fida,  
> 54 e in quei che fidanza non imborsa.  

Fraud, which gnaws every conscience, a man may practise upon [one] who confide[s] in him; and upon [him] who repose[s] no confidence.

- *La frode* ‘the fraud’ → “Fraud”: the definite article before the abstract noun is omitted.
- *ond’ ogni coscïenza è morsa* ‘by which every conscience is bitten’ → “which gnaws every conscience”: the passive becomes active, with fraud rather than conscience as the subject; ‘biting’ becomes ‘gnawing’.
- *può l’ uomo usare* ‘can the man use’ → “a man may practise”: the subject moves before the modal, the generic definite article becomes an indefinite article, ‘can’ becomes “may”, and ‘use’ becomes “practise”.
- *in colui, ch’ in lui fida* ‘in that person who in him trusts’ → “upon [one] who confide[s] in him”: “upon” replaces ‘in’; the demonstrative becomes the indefinite “[one]”; and the phrase expressing the person trusted moves after the verb.
- *in quei* ‘in him’ → “upon [him]”: “upon” replaces ‘in’.
- *che fidanza non imborsa* ‘who confidence does not pocket’ → “who repose[s] no confidence”: the metaphor of pocketing becomes the idiom of reposing confidence. The object moves after the verb, and verbal negation becomes the determiner “no”.

> 55 Questo modo di retro par che uccida  
> 56 pur lo vinco d’ amor che fa natura:  
> 57 onde nel cerchio secondo s’ annida  

This latter mode seems only to cut off the bond of love which Nature makes: hence in the second circle nests

- *Questo modo di retro* ‘This mode behind’ → “This latter mode”: the postposed expression referring to the last-mentioned mode becomes the adjective “latter” before the noun.
- *par che uccida pur* ‘seems that it kills only’ → “seems only to cut off”: the subordinate clause with a subjunctive becomes an infinitive; ‘kills’ becomes “cut off”; and “only” moves before the infinitive.
- *che fa natura* ‘which makes Nature’ → “which Nature makes”: the subject moves before the verb.
- *nel cerchio secondo* ‘in the circle second’ → “in the second circle”: the adjective moves before the noun.
- *s’ annida* ‘nests itself’ → “nests”: the Italian reflexive construction becomes an English intransitive verb.

## Lines 58–90 — Fraud, Treachery, and Incontinence

> 58 ipocrisia, lusinghe e chi affattura,  
> 59 falsità, ladroneccio e simonia,  
> 60 ruffian, baratti, e simile lordura.  

hypocrisy, flattery, sorcerers, cheating, theft and simony, pandars, barrators, and like filth.

- *lusinghe* ‘flatteries’ → “flattery”: the plural becomes a singular collective noun.
- *e chi affattura* ‘and whoever practises sorcery’ → “sorcerers”: the singular relative construction becomes a plural noun, and the conjunction is omitted.
- *falsità* ‘falsehood’ → “cheating”: falseness is rendered as deceptive conduct.
- *baratti* ‘corrupt dealings’ → “barrators”: the translation names the offenders rather than their offences.

> 61 Per l’ altro modo quell’ amor s’ obblia  
> 62 che fa natura, e quel ch’ è poi aggiunto,  
> 63 di che la fede spezïal si cria :  

In the other mode is forgotten that love which Nature makes, and also that which afterwards is added, giving birth to special trust:

- *Per l’ altro modo* ‘By the other mode’ → “In the other mode”: the preposition of means becomes “In”.
- *quell’ amor s’ obblia* ‘that love is forgotten’ → “is forgotten that love”: the passive verb precedes its subject.
- *fa natura* ‘makes Nature’ → “Nature makes”: the subject moves before the verb.
- *e quel ch’ è poi aggiunto* ‘and that which is afterwards added’ → “and also that which afterwards is added”: “also” is supplied, and the adverb moves before the auxiliary.
- *di che la fede spezïal si cria* ‘from which special trust is created’ → “giving birth to special trust”: a relative clause with a passive verb becomes an active participial phrase; trust changes from the subject of creation to the object of “giving birth to”.

> 64 onde nel cerchio minore, ov’ è il punto  
> 65 dell’ universo, in su che Dite siede,  
> 66 qualunque trade in eterno è consunto.”  

hence in the smallest circle, at the centre of the universe and seat of Dis, every traitor is eternally consumed."

- *nel cerchio minore* ‘in the smaller circle’ → “in the smallest circle”: the comparative is rendered as a superlative.
- *ov’ è il punto dell’ universo* ‘where is the point of the universe’ → “at the centre of the universe”: the relative clause becomes a prepositional phrase, and “centre” specifies the sense of *punto*.
- *in su che Dite siede* ‘upon which Dis sits’ → “and seat of Dis”: the relative clause becomes a coordinated noun phrase; the action of sitting becomes the noun “seat”, and “and” is supplied.
- *qualunque trade* ‘whoever betrays’ → “every traitor”: the relative pronoun and verb become a determiner and noun.
- *in eterno* ‘for eternity’ → “eternally”: the prepositional phrase becomes an adverb.

> 67 Ed io : “ Maestro, assai chiaro procede  
> 68 la tua ragione, ed assai ben distingue  
> 69 questo baratro, e il popol che il possiede.  

And I: "Master, thy discourse proceeds most clearly, and excellently distinguishes this gulf, and the people that possess it.

- *assai chiaro procede la tua ragione* ‘very clearly proceeds thy reasoning’ → “thy discourse proceeds most clearly”: the subject moves before the verb and the adverbial expression after it; *ragione* becomes “discourse”, and *assai* is rendered by the emphatic “most”.
- *assai ben* ‘very well’ → “excellently”: the two-word expression becomes a single, stronger adverb.
- *il popol che il possiede* ‘the people that possesses it’ → “the people that possess it”: the Italian singular collective takes plural agreement in English, and the object pronoun follows rather than precedes the verb.

> 70 Ma dimmi : Quei della palude pingue,  
> 71 che mena il vento, e che batte la pioggia,  
> 72 e che s’ incontran con sì aspre lingue,  

But tell me: Those of the fat marsh; those whom the wind leads, and whom the rain beats; and those who meet with tongues so sharp,

- *Quei della palude pingue* ‘Those of the marsh fat’ → “Those of the fat marsh”: the adjective moves before its noun.
- *che mena il vento* ‘whom leads the wind’ → “those whom the wind leads”: “those” is supplied, and the subject moves before the verb.
- *e che batte la pioggia* ‘and whom beats the rain’ → “and whom the rain beats”: the subject moves before the verb.
- *e che s’ incontran con sì aspre lingue* ‘and who meet one another with so harsh tongues’ → “and those who meet with tongues so sharp”: “those” is supplied; the reciprocal pronoun is left implicit in “meet”; and the adjective phrase moves after the noun, with “sharp” rendering *aspre*.

> 73 perchè non dentro della città roggia  
> 74 son ei puniti, se Dio gli ha in ira ?  
> 75 e se non gli ha, perchè sono a tal foggia ?”  

— why are they not punished in the red city, if God's anger be upon them? and if not, why are they in such plight?"

- *perchè non dentro della città roggia son ei puniti* ‘why not within the city red are they punished’ → “why are they not punished in the red city”: the auxiliary and subject move before the negative, the place phrase moves after the participle, and the adjective precedes its noun; “in” renders *dentro della*.
- *se Dio gli ha in ira* ‘if God holds them in anger’ → “if God's anger be upon them”: God’s anger becomes the subject instead of God, and the indicative construction becomes one with subjunctive “be”.
- *e se non gli ha* ‘and if he does not hold them so’ → “and if not”: the verb and its object are omitted, leaving the condition elliptical.
- *a tal foggia* ‘in such a manner’ → “in such plight”: an expression of manner becomes an expression of condition.

> 76 Ed egli a me : “ Perchè tanto delira,”  
> 77 disse, “lo ingegno tuo da quel ch’ ei suole ?  
> 78 ovver la mente dove altrove mira ?  

And he [said] to me: "Wherefore errs thy mind so much beyond its wont? or are thy thoughts turned somewhere else?

- *Ed egli a me … disse* ‘And he to me … said’ → “And he [said] to me”: *disse*, parenthetical within the Italian speech, is moved into the introductory clause.
- *Perchè tanto delira … lo ingegno tuo* ‘Why so much strays thy understanding’ → “Wherefore errs thy mind so much”: “mind” renders *ingegno*, and “errs” renders *delira*; the possessive precedes its noun, while the expression of degree moves after the subject.
- *da quel ch’ ei suole* ‘from what it is accustomed to do’ → “beyond its wont”: “beyond” replaces ‘from’, and the relative clause becomes a possessive noun phrase.
- *ovver la mente dove altrove mira* ‘or where else does the mind look’ → “or are thy thoughts turned somewhere else”: singular *mente* becomes plural “thoughts”, with “thy” supplied; active looking becomes passive turning, and the question asking where becomes a question asking whether the thoughts are elsewhere.

> 79 Non ti rimembra di quelle parole,  
> 80 con le quai la tua Etica pertratta  
> 81 le tre disposizion, che il ciel non vuole ;  

Rememberest thou not the words wherewith thy Ethics treat of the three dispositions which Heaven wills not,

- *Non ti rimembra di quelle parole* ‘Does it not come to mind for thee concerning those words’ → “Rememberest thou not the words”: the impersonal construction becomes a personal, transitive verb; the prepositional complement becomes its direct object, “the” replaces the demonstrative, and the negative follows the verb and subject.
- *la tua Etica pertratta* ‘thy Ethics treats of’ → “thy Ethics treat of”: the Italian title and verb are grammatically singular, whereas the English title takes plural agreement.
- *che il ciel non vuole* ‘which Heaven not wills’ → “which Heaven wills not”: the negative moves after the verb.

> 82 incontinenza, malizia e la matta  
> 83 bestialitade? e come incontinenza  
> 84 men Dio offende, e men biasimo accatta ?  

incontinence, malice, and mad bestiality? and how incontinence less offends God, and receives less blame?

- *la matta bestialitade* ‘the mad bestiality’ → “mad bestiality”: the definite article is omitted.
- *men Dio offende* ‘less God offends’ → “less offends God”: the object, God, moves after the verb.
- *e men biasimo accatta* ‘and less blame earns’ → “and receives less blame”: the object moves after the verb, and “receives” replaces ‘earns’.

> 85 Se tu riguardi ben questa sentenza,  
> 86 e rechiti alla mente chi son quelli,  
> 87 che su di fuor sostengon penitenza,  

If thou rightly considerest this doctrine, and recallest to thy memory who they are that suffer punishment above, without,

- *riguardi ben* ‘considerest well’ → “rightly considerest”: the adverb moves before the verb, and ‘well’ is interpreted as ‘correctly’.
- *questa sentenza* ‘this statement’ → “this doctrine”: the particular statement is described as a doctrine.
- *e rechiti alla mente* ‘and bringest to thy mind’ → “and recallest to thy memory”: bringing to mind becomes recalling to memory; the reflexive dative is represented by the possessive “thy”.
- *chi son quelli* ‘who are those’ → “who they are”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun, and the subject precedes the verb.
- *che su di fuor sostengon penitenza* ‘that above outside endure penance’ → “that suffer punishment above, without”: the place expressions move after the verb and object; *penitenza* is rendered as “punishment”, and *sostengon* as “suffer”.

> 88 tu vedrai ben perchè da questi felli  
> 89 sien dipartiti, e perchè men crucciata  
> 90 la divina giustizia gli martelli.”  

thou easily wilt see why they are separated from these fell spirits, and why, with less anger, Divine Justice strikes them."

- *tu vedrai ben* ‘thou wilt see well’ → “thou easily wilt see”: *ben* is interpreted as “easily”, and the adverb moves before the auxiliary.
- *perchè da questi felli sien dipartiti* ‘why from these wicked ones they be separated’ → “why they are separated from these fell spirits”: the prepositional phrase moves after the passive verb, and the subjunctive becomes indicative; the substantivized adjective *felli* becomes “fell spirits”, with the noun supplied.
- *men crucciata* ‘less angered’ → “with less anger”: a participial adjective agreeing with justice becomes an adverbial prepositional phrase.
- *la divina giustizia* ‘the divine justice’ → “Divine Justice”: the definite article is omitted.
- *gli martelli* ‘them it hammer’ → “strikes them”: the object pronoun moves after the verb, the subjunctive becomes indicative, and the specific action of hammering becomes the more general “strikes”.

## Lines 91–115 — Nature, Art, and Usury

> 91 “O Sol, che sani ogni vista turbata,  
> 92 tu mi contenti sì, quando tu solvi,  
> 93 che, non men che saver, dubbiar m’ aggrata.  

“O Sun! who healest all troubled vision, thou makest so glad when thou resolvest me, that to doubt is not less grateful than to know.

- *ogni vista turbata* ‘every vision troubled’ → “all troubled vision”: “all” replaces ‘every’, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *tu mi contenti sì, quando tu solvi* ‘thou satisfiest me so, when thou dost resolve’ → “thou makest so glad when thou resolvest me”: *contenti* becomes “makest … glad”; the expressed object “me” accompanies “resolvest” rather than “makest”, whereas Italian attaches *mi* to *contenti* and leaves the object of *solvi* unexpressed.
- *non men che saver, dubbiar m’ aggrata* ‘not less than knowing, doubting pleases me’ → “to doubt is not less grateful than to know”: the subject moves before the predicate, and the comparison follows it. ‘Pleases me’ becomes “is … grateful”, with no expressed equivalent of *m’*.

> 94 Ancora un poco indietro ti rivolvi,”  
> 95 diss’ io, “là dove di’ che usura offende  
> 96 la divina bontade, e il groppo svolvi.”  

Turn thee yet a little back” [I said], “to where thou sayest that usury offends the Divine Goodness, and unravel the knot.”

- *Ancora un poco indietro ti rivolvi* ‘yet a little back turn thyself’ → “Turn thee yet a little back”: the verb and reflexive pronoun move before the adverbial expressions.
- *diss’ io* ‘said I’ → “[I said]”: subject and verb change places.
- *là dove* ‘there where’ → “to where”: the expression of place becomes explicitly directional.
- *il groppo svolvi* ‘the knot unravel’ → “unravel the knot”: the object follows the verb.

> 97 “Filosofia,” mi disse, “a chi l’ attende,  
> 98 nota non pure in una sola parte,  
> 99 come natura lo suo corso prende  
> 100 dal divino intelletto e da sua arte;  

He said to me: “Philosophy, to him who hears it, points out, not in one place alone, how Nature takes her course from the Divine Intellect, and from its art;

- *mi disse* ‘to me he said’ → “He said to me”: the indirect object follows the verb, and the reporting clause moves before the speech instead of interrupting it after *Filosofia*.
- *a chi l’ attende* ‘to whoever attends to it’ → “to him who hears it”: the indefinite relative becomes an explicit antecedent and relative clause; attending to something is rendered as hearing it.
- *nota* ‘notes’ → “points out”: a phrasal verb replaces the single verb.
- *non pure in una sola parte* ‘not only in one single part’ → “not in one place alone”: ‘part’ becomes “place”, and the restrictive expressions *pure* and *sola* are combined in the final “alone”.
- *natura lo suo corso prende* ‘nature her course takes’ → “Nature takes her course”: the verb moves before its object.

> 101 e se tu ben la tua Fisica note,  
> 102 tu troverai non dopo molte carte,  

and if thou note well thy Physics, thou wilt find, not many pages from the first,

- *tu ben la tua Fisica note* ‘thou well thy Physics note’ → “thou note well thy Physics”: the verb moves before the adverb and object.
- *non dopo molte carte* ‘not after many leaves’ → “not many pages from the first”: ‘leaves’ becomes “pages”; the construction with ‘after’ becomes a measure of distance from a starting point, with “from the first” supplied.

> 103 che l’ arte vostra quella, quanto puote,  
> 104 segue, come il maestro fa il discente,  
> 105 sì che vostr’ arte a Dio quasi è nipote.  

that your art, as far as it can, follows her, as the scholar does his master; so that your art is, as it were, the grandchild of the Deity.

- *l’ arte vostra quella, quanto puote, segue* ‘the art of yours that one, as much as it can, follows’ → “your art, as far as it can, follows her”: the possessive precedes its noun, and the object follows the verb. The demonstrative *quella* becomes the personal pronoun “her”; ‘as much as’ becomes “as far as”.
- *come il maestro fa il discente* ‘as the master does the learner’ → “as the scholar does his master”: Italian places the object, *il maestro*, before the verb and the subject, *il discente*, after it; English reverses that order. ‘Learner’ becomes “scholar”, and the definite article before *maestro* becomes “his”.
- *vostr’ arte a Dio quasi è nipote* ‘your art to God almost is grandchild’ → “your art is, as it were, the grandchild of the Deity”: the copula moves before its complement; ‘to God’ becomes “of the Deity” and follows “grandchild”. *Quasi* is rendered as the qualifying parenthesis “as it were”, rather than ‘almost’, and “the” is supplied before the predicate noun.

> 106 Da queste due, se tu ti rechi a mente  
> 107 lo Genesi dal principio, conviene  
> 108 prender sua vita ed avanzar la gente.  

By these two, if thou recallest to thy memory Genesis at the beginning, it behoves man to gain his bread and [to prosper].

- *Da queste due* ‘from these two’ → “By these two”: the preposition of source becomes one of means.
- *tu ti rechi a mente* ‘thou bringest to mind for thyself’ → “thou recallest to thy memory”: ‘bringest’ becomes “recallest”; the reflexive indirect object is represented by the possessive “thy”, and ‘mind’ becomes “memory”.
- *lo Genesi dal principio* ‘the Genesis from the beginning’ → “Genesis at the beginning”: the article before the title is omitted, and ‘from’ becomes “at”.
- *conviene prender sua vita ed avanzar la gente* ‘it behoves the people to take their livelihood and advance’ → “it behoves man to gain his bread and [to prosper]”: the postposed *la gente* moves before the infinitives and becomes the generic singular “man”. ‘Take their livelihood’ becomes “gain his bread”, and the infinitive marker is repeated before “[to prosper]”. That rendering takes *avanzar* intransitively, with *la gente* as the subject of both infinitives, rather than taking *la gente* as its object.

> 109 E perchè l’ usuriere altra via tiene,  
> 110 per sè natura, e per la sua seguace  
> 111 dispregia, poichè in altro pon la spene.  

And because the usurer takes another way, he contemns Nature in herself and in her follower, placing elsewhere his hope.

- *l’ usuriere altra via tiene* ‘the usurer another way holds’ → “the usurer takes another way”: the verb moves before its object, and ‘holds’ becomes “takes”.
- *per sè natura, e per la sua seguace dispregia* ‘in herself nature, and in her follower, he contemns’ → “he contemns Nature in herself and in her follower”: the verb moves before its object, and “in herself” follows “Nature” rather than preceding it.
- *poichè in altro pon la spene* ‘since in something else he places his hope’ → “placing elsewhere his hope”: the finite causal clause becomes a participial phrase, leaving its causal connection implicit. ‘In something else’ becomes the adverb “elsewhere”, and the verb moves before it.

> 112 Ma seguimi oramai, chè il gir mi piace :  

But follow me now, as it pleases me to go:

- *chè il gir mi piace* ‘because going pleases me’ → “as it pleases me to go”: the nominal infinitive serving as subject becomes a postponed infinitive, with anticipatory “it” occupying the subject position.

> 113 chè i Pesci guizzan su per l’ orizzonta  
> 114 e il Carro tutto sovra il Coro giace,  
> 115 e il balzo via là oltre si dismonta.”  

for the Fishes [are quivering] on the horizon, and all the Wain lies over Caurus, and yonder far onwards we go down the cliff.”

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *guizzan* ‘quiver’ → “[are quivering]”: the simple present becomes the present progressive.
- *su per l’ orizzonta* ‘up along the horizon’ → “on the horizon”: the upward and extended movement expressed by *su per* is reduced to position.
- *il Carro tutto* ‘the Wain all’ → “all the Wain”: the quantifier moves before the noun phrase.
- *il balzo via là oltre si dismonta* ‘the cliff far there beyond is descended’ → “yonder far onwards we go down the cliff”: the *si* construction becomes an active clause with “we” as its subject. “The cliff” moves to the end, while the spatial expressions move to the beginning and are rearranged; *oltre* is rendered directionally as “onwards”.

## Summary — Close Correspondence and Prose Reshaping

The translation generally follows the Italian closely in the sequence of its clauses, its repeated connections, and much of its vocabulary, but regularly rearranges words into more customary English prose order. Subjects move before verbs, objects after them, and adjectives and possessives before their nouns. Thus *che fa natura* becomes “which Nature makes”, while *Figliuol mio* becomes “My Son”. Reporting clauses are sometimes moved out of interrupted speech and placed before it. Yet Carlyle also preserves some marked arrangements, including the fronted object “Our descent” and the delayed subject in “hence in the second circle nests”.

Changes of construction often go beyond rearrangement. Active clauses become passive, as *che facevan* becomes “formed by”; elsewhere the passive *ond’ ogni coscïenza è morsa* becomes the active “which gnaws every conscience”. Impersonal expressions are recast with personal subjects or modal verbs: *convien che* becomes “must”, and *si dismonta* becomes “we go down”. Relative clauses and phrases are compressed into nouns or other constructions: *qualunque trade* becomes “every traitor”, *in su che Dite siede* becomes “and seat of Dis”, and *di grado in grado* becomes “in gradation”. Conversely, a compact Italian expression may be expanded, as *chi* becomes “all who”.

Word choices range from close equivalents to broader or more interpretative renderings. *Avello*, ‘tomb’, becomes “monument”; *guastatori*, ‘ravagers’, becomes “plunderers”; and *martelli*, ‘hammers’, becomes “strikes”. Some figurative expressions are replaced by English idioms: *fidanza non imborsa*, ‘does not pocket confidence’, becomes “repose[s] no confidence”. Others retain their imagery closely, including “the straight way”, “the bond of love”, and “unravel the knot”. The translation also preserves much of the Italian’s formal register through such words as “wherefore”, “accurst”, and “contemns”.

Small additions and omissions help make the prose explicit or fluent. “Still” strengthens the comparison in “a still more cruel throng”; “of these” identifies what is to be seen; and “may” makes possibility explicit in “may be inflicted”. By contrast, *prima*, ‘first’, disappears from the instruction about becoming accustomed to the air, while *e se non gli ha* is reduced to “and if not”. Alongside these individually noted departures are two pervasive grammatical adjustments: English supplies subject pronouns where Italian verb endings already identify the subject, and omits Italian articles before possessives, as in *la tua ragione* becoming “thy discourse”. These routine changes, together with the frequent shifts of word order, allow the translation to remain recognizably close without reproducing Italian syntax word for word.
