# Inferno 27

## Lines 1–24 — Another Flame Finds Its Voice

> 1 Già era dritta in su la fiamma e queta  
> 2 per non dir più, e già da noi sen gía  
> 3 con la licenza del dolce poeta,  

The flame was now erect and quiet, having ceased to speak, and now went away from us with licence of the sweet Poet;

- *Già era dritta in su la fiamma e queta* ‘Already was upright the flame and quiet’ → “The flame was now erect and quiet”: the subject moves before the verb; *già* becomes “now” rather than ‘already’, and “erect” condenses *dritta in su*, ‘straight upwards’.
- *per non dir più* ‘because of speaking no more’ → “having ceased to speak”: the causal infinitive becomes a perfect participial construction, expressing cessation rather than continued silence.
- *e già da noi sen gía* ‘and already from us it went away’ → “and now went away from us”: *già* again becomes “now”; the phrase expressing departure from the speakers moves after the verb.
- *con la licenza* ‘with the licence’ → “with licence”: the definite article is omitted.

> 4 quando un' altra, che dietro a lei venia,  
> 5 ne fece volger gli occhi alla sua cima  
> 6 per un confuso suon che fuor n' uscia.  

when another, that came behind it, made us turn our eyes to its top, for a confused sound that issued therefrom.

- *che dietro a lei venia* ‘that behind it was coming’ → “that came behind it”: the verb moves before the phrase of position, and the imperfect is rendered by a simple past rather than a progressive.
- *gli occhi* ‘the eyes’ → “our eyes”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *che fuor n’ uscia* ‘that out of it was issuing’ → “that issued therefrom”: “therefrom” gathers up the expression of emergence and follows the verb; the imperfect becomes a simple past.

> 7 Come il bue Cicilian, che mugghiò prima  
> 8 col pianto di colui (e ciò fu dritto)  
> 9 che l' avea temperato con sua lima,  

As the Sicilian bull (which bellowed first with the lament of him—and that was right—who had tuned it with his file)

- *il bue Cicilian* ‘the bull Sicilian’ → “the Sicilian bull”: the adjective moves before the noun.
- *col pianto* ‘with the weeping’ → “with the lament”: the act of weeping is rendered as a lament.
- *che l’ avea temperato* ‘who it had fashioned’ → “who had tuned it”: *temperato* is interpreted as acoustic tuning rather than the fashioning or finishing of the object; the object pronoun moves after the verb.

> 10 mugghiava con la voce dell' afflitto,  
> 11 sì che, con tutto ch' ei fosse di rame,  
> 12 pure e' pareva dal dolor trafitto:  

kept bellowing with the sufferer’s voice; so that, although it was of brass, it seemed transfixed with pain

- *mugghiava* ‘was bellowing’ → “kept bellowing”: the imperfect’s continuing action is made explicit through “kept”.
- *con la voce dell’ afflitto* ‘with the voice of the afflicted one’ → “with the sufferer’s voice”: the substantivized participle becomes the noun “sufferer”, and the possessive construction moves before “voice”.
- *con tutto ch’ ei fosse* ‘with all that it were’ → “although it was”: the concessive phrase becomes a single conjunction, and the subjunctive becomes indicative.
- *di rame* ‘of copper’ → “of brass”: a different metal is named.
- *pure e’ pareva dal dolor trafitto* ‘yet it seemed by the pain transfixed’ → “it seemed transfixed with pain”: *pure*, ‘yet’ or ‘nevertheless’, is omitted; the participle moves before the phrase expressing what pierces it, “with” replaces ‘by’, and the article before ‘pain’ disappears.

> 13 così per non aver via nè forame  
> 14 dal principio del foco, in suo linguaggio  
> 15 si convertivan le parole grame.  

: thus, having at their commencement no way or outlet from the fire, the dismal words were changed into its language.

- *per non aver via nè forame* ‘because of not having a way nor a hole’ → “having” and “no way or outlet”: the causal infinitive becomes a participle without an explicit causal connective; verbal negation becomes “no” before the nouns, ‘nor’ becomes “or”, and the physical ‘hole’ becomes the functional “outlet”.
- *dal principio* ‘at first’ → “at their commencement”: the temporal expression is expanded with a possessive referring to the words, and moves before “no way or outlet”.
- *in suo linguaggio si convertivan le parole grame* ‘into its language were turning the sorrowful words’ → “the dismal words were changed into its language”: the postverbal subject moves to the front, its adjective moves before the noun, and the phrase expressing the resulting language moves to the end. The pronominal verb of transformation becomes an English passive.

> 16 Ma poscia ch' ebber colto lor viaggio  
> 17 su per la punta, dandole quel guizzo  
> 18 che dato avea la lingua in lor passaggio,  

But after they had found their road up through the point, giving to it the vibration which the tongue had given in their passage,

- *ebber colto lor viaggio* ‘they had caught their journey’ → “they had found their road”: the Italian expression for finding or taking a course is recast with “found”, while ‘journey’ becomes “road”.
- *dandole quel guizzo* ‘giving it that flicker’ → “giving to it the vibration”: the attached indirect-object pronoun becomes “to it”; the demonstrative becomes a definite article, and the sudden movement expressed by *guizzo* becomes “vibration”.
- *che dato avea la lingua* ‘which given had the tongue’ → “which the tongue had given”: the subject moves before the verb, and the participle follows its auxiliary.

> 19 udimmo dire: “O tu, a cui io drizzo  
> 20 la voce, e che parlavi mo Lombardo,  
> 21 dicendo: ‘Issa ten va, più non t' adizzo’;  

we heard it say: “O thou, at whom I aim my voice! and who just now wast speaking Lombard, saying, ‘Now go, no more I urge thee’

- *udimmo dire* ‘we heard say’ → “we heard it say”: “it” is supplied as the speaker.
- *a cui io drizzo la voce* ‘to whom I direct the voice’ → “at whom I aim my voice”: ‘direct to’ becomes “aim” with “at”, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *e che parlavi mo Lombardo* ‘and who wast speaking now Lombard’ → “and who just now wast speaking Lombard”: the time expression moves before the verb, and “just now” specifies the immediate past.
- *ten va* ‘go yourself away’ → “go”: the pronominal expression of departure is reduced to a simple verb, without an explicit equivalent of ‘away’.
- *più non t’ adizzo* ‘any more not thee I urge’ → “no more I urge thee”: the negation and temporal adverb are combined as “no more”, and the object pronoun moves after the verb.

> 22 perch' io sia giunto forse alquanto tardo,  
> 23 non t' incresca restare a parlar meco:  
> 24 vedi che non incresce a me, ed ardo.  

; though I have come perhaps a little late, let it not irk thee to pause and speak with me: thou seest it irks not me, although I burn.

- *sia giunto* ‘be arrived’ → “have come”: the perfect subjunctive becomes an indicative present perfect, with “come” replacing ‘arrived’.
- *non t’ incresca* ‘may it not irk thee’ → “let it not irk thee”: the independent subjunctive expressing a request becomes a construction with “let”; the object pronoun follows the verb rather than preceding it.
- *restare a parlar meco* ‘to stay to speak with me’ → “to pause and speak with me”: “pause” specifies a temporary stopping, and the dependent infinitive introduced by *a* becomes a coordinated verb.
- *vedi che non incresce a me* ‘thou seest that it does not irk to me’ → “thou seest it irks not me”: the conjunction ‘that’ is omitted; the negative follows the verb, and the Italian indirect object becomes the direct object of “irks”.
- *ed* ‘and’ → “although”: coordination becomes explicit concession.

## Lines 25–57 — Romagna’s Rulers and a Request

> 25 Se tu pur mo in questo mondo cieco  
> 26 caduto sei di quella dolce terra  
> 27 Latina, ond' io mia colpa tutta reco,  

If thou art but now fallen into this blind world from that sweet Latian land, whence I bring all my guilt,

- *pur mo ... caduto sei* ‘only now ... fallen art’ → “art but now fallen”: the auxiliary precedes the participle, with the time expression between them; the verb also moves before the phrase describing the destination.
- *in questo mondo cieco* ‘in this world blind’ → “into this blind world”: “into” expressly marks motion, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *di quella dolce terra Latina* ‘from that sweet land Latin’ → “from that sweet Latian land”: the adjective corresponding to *Latina* moves before the noun.
- *ond’ io mia colpa tutta reco* ‘whence I my guilt all bring’ → “whence I bring all my guilt”: the verb precedes its object, and “all” precedes the possessive and noun.

> 28 dimmi se i Romagnoli han pace o guerra:  
> 29 ch' io fui de' monti là intra Urbino  
> 30 e il giogo di che 'l Tever si disserra.”  

tell me if the Romagnuols have peace or war : for I was of the mountains there, between Urbino and the yoke from which the Tiber springs.”

- *ch’* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *si disserra* ‘unbars itself’ → “springs”: the reflexive expression becomes an intransitive verb describing emergence, without the image of unbarring.

> 31 Io era in giuso ancora attento e chino,  
> 32 quando il mio duca mi tentò di costa,  
> 33 dicendo : “Parla tu, questi è Latino.”  

I still was eager downwards and bent, when my Leader touched me on the side, saying : “Speak thou; this is a Latian.”

- *era in giuso ancora attento e chino* ‘was downwards still attentive and bent’ → “still was eager downwards and bent”: “still” moves before the verb, “downwards” follows the first adjective, and *attento* is rendered as “eager” rather than ‘attentive’.
- *mi tentò di costa* ‘me touched at the side’ → “touched me on the side”: the object pronoun follows the verb, and the location is expressed with “on”.
- *è Latino* ‘is Latin’ → “is a Latian”: the adjective becomes a noun identifying a person, with an indefinite article.

> 34 Ed io ch’ avea già pronta la risposta,  
> 35 senza indugio a parlare incominciai :  
> 36 “O anima, che se’ laggiù nascosta,  

And I, who had my answer ready then, began without delay to speak : “O soul, that there below art hidden!

- *avea già pronta la risposta* ‘had already ready the answer’ → “had my answer ready then”: “my” replaces the definite article, “ready” follows the object, and *già* becomes “then” rather than ‘already’.
- *senza indugio a parlare incominciai* ‘without delay to speak I began’ → “began without delay to speak”: the main verb moves before both the adverbial phrase and the infinitive.
- *se’ laggiù nascosta* ‘art there below hidden’ → “there below art hidden”: the expression of place moves before the verb.

> 37 Romagna tua non è, e non fu mai,  
> 38 senza guerra ne’ cor de’ suoi tiranni ;  
> 39 ma ’n palese nessuna or vi lasciai.  

thy Romagna is not, and never was, without war in the hearts of her tyrants ; but open[ly] just now I there left none.

- *Romagna tua* ‘Romagna thy’ → “thy Romagna”: the possessive precedes the noun.
- *non fu mai* ‘not was ever’ → “never was”: the negative and temporal expressions are combined in “never”, which precedes the verb.
- *suoi* ‘its’ → “her”: English uses a feminine personal possessive for the place; the Italian possessive does not itself distinguish the possessor’s gender.
- *’n palese* ‘in the open’ → “open[ly]”: a prepositional expression becomes an adverb.
- *nessuna or vi lasciai* ‘none now there I left’ → “just now I there left none”: the object moves after the verb, and “just” makes the recent-time sense of *or* explicit.

> 40 Ravenna sta, come stata è molti anni:  
> 41 l’ aquila da Polenta la si cova,  
> 42 sì che Cervia ricopre co’ suoi vanni.  

Ravenna stands, as it has stood for many years : the Eagle of Polenta broods over it, so that he covers Cervia with his pinions.

- *come stata è molti anni* ‘as stood is many years’ → “as it has stood for many years”: the perfect auxiliary changes from ‘be’ to “has” and precedes the participle; “for” is supplied before the duration.
- *da Polenta* ‘from Polenta’ → “of Polenta”: origin or association is expressed with “of” rather than ‘from’.
- *la si cova* ‘it for itself broods’ → “broods over it”: the direct object becomes the object of “over” and follows the verb; the reflexive, self-involving *si* is not separately expressed.
- *Cervia ricopre co’ suoi vanni* ‘Cervia covers with its pinions’ → “he covers Cervia with his pinions”: the object follows the verb, and the supplied subject and possessive are masculine personal forms, despite the grammatically feminine antecedent.

> 43 La terra, che fe’ già la lunga prova,  
> 44 e de’ Franceschi sanguinoso mucchio,  
> 45 sotto le branche verdi si ritrova.  

The city, which made erewhile the long probation, and sanguinary heap of [the] Frenchmen, finds itself again under the Green Clutches.

- *terra* ‘land’ → “city”: the translation selects the word’s urban sense.
- *e de’ Franceschi sanguinoso mucchio* ‘and of the Frenchmen bloody heap’ → “and sanguinary heap of [the] Frenchmen”: the phrase identifying those composing the heap moves after the noun.
- *sotto le branche verdi* ‘under the claws green’ → “under the Green Clutches”: the adjective precedes the noun, “Clutches” replaces ‘claws’, and the whole phrase moves after the verb.
- *si ritrova* ‘finds itself’ → “finds itself again”: the reflexive pronoun follows the verb, and “again” gives the prefix *ri-* an explicit iterative force rather than treating *ritrovarsi* simply as ‘find oneself’ or ‘be situated’.

> 46 Il Mastin vecchio e il nuovo da Verrucchio,  
> 47 che fecer di Montagna il mal governo,  
> 48 là dove soglion fan de’ denti succhio.  

The old Mastiff of Verrucchio and the young, who of Montagna made evil governance, there, where they are wont, ply their teeth.

- *Il Mastin vecchio* ‘the mastiff old’ → “The old Mastiff”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *nuovo* ‘new’ → “young”: relative newness is expressed as relative youth.
- *da Verrucchio* ‘from Verrucchio’ → “of Verrucchio”: “of” replaces ‘from’, and the phrase moves before the reference to the second mastiff.
- *fecer di Montagna il mal governo* ‘made of Montagna the bad treatment’ → “of Montagna made evil governance”: the prepositional phrase precedes the verb, the definite article is omitted, and *governo*, here denoting treatment or handling, is rendered as “governance”.
- *fan de’ denti succhio* ‘make of the teeth a drill’ → “ply their teeth”: the construction and drill image are replaced by a simple verb with “teeth” as its direct object; a possessive replaces the definite article.

> 49 Le città di Lamone e di Santerno  
> 50 conduce il leoncel dal nido bianco,  
> 51 che muta parte dalla state al verno;  

The cities of Lamone and Santerno guides [the] Lioncel of the white lair, who changes faction from the summer to the winter

- *di Lamone e di Santerno* ‘of Lamone and of Santerno’ → “of Lamone and Santerno”: the second preposition is omitted.
- *dal nido bianco* ‘from the nest white’ → “of the white lair”: “of” replaces ‘from’, “lair” replaces ‘nest’, and the adjective precedes the noun.

> 52 e quella a cui il Savio bagna il fianco,  
> 53 così com’ ella sie’ tra il piano e il monte,  
> 54 tra tirannia si vive e stato franco.  

; and that [city] whose flank the Savio bathes, as it lies between the plain and mount, so lives it between tyranny and freedom.

- *quella* ‘that one’ → “that [city]”: the noun understood with the demonstrative is supplied explicitly.
- *a cui il Savio bagna il fianco* ‘to which the Savio bathes the flank’ → “whose flank the Savio bathes”: the dative expression of possession becomes possessive “whose”, bringing the object before the subject and verb.
- *così com’* ‘so as’ → “as” and “so”: the paired expressions are separated, with “so” deferred until the following clause.
- *ella sie’* ‘it sits’ → “it lies”: the verb of position changes.
- *tra tirannia si vive e stato franco* ‘between tyranny it lives for itself and a free state’ → “lives it between tyranny and freedom”: the verb moves before the entire prepositional phrase, the subject follows the verb, and the reflexive *si* is not separately expressed; ‘a free state’ becomes the abstract noun “freedom”.

> 55 Ora chi sei ti prego che ne conte;  
> 56 non esser duro più ch’ altri sia stato,  
> 57 se il nome tuo nel mondo tegna fronte.”  

Now I pray thee, tell us who thou art ; be not more hard than one has been to thee, so may thy name on earth maintain its front.”

- *chi sei ti prego che ne conte* ‘who thou art I pray thee that thou tell us’ → “I pray thee, tell us who thou art”: the request moves first, the subordinate subjunctive becomes a direct imperative, and the indirect question moves after “tell us”.
- *non esser duro più* ‘not be hard more’ → “be not more hard”: the negative follows the verb, and the comparative adverb precedes the adjective.
- *ch’ altri sia stato* ‘than another has been’ → “than one has been to thee”: the indefinite personal pronoun becomes “one”, the subjunctive becomes indicative, and “to thee” is supplied.
- *se il nome tuo nel mondo tegna fronte* ‘if the name thy in the world may hold front’ → “so may thy name on earth maintain its front”: the conditional-looking *se* construction is rendered as an explicit wish with “so may”; the possessive precedes “name”, ‘in the world’ becomes “on earth”, and “its” is supplied before “front”.

## Lines 58–90 — Guido’s Repentance and Relapse

> 58 Poscia che il foco alquanto ebbe rugghiato  
> 59 al modo suo, l' aguta punta mosse  
> 60 di qua, di là, e poi diè cotal fiato :  

After the flame had roared awhile as usual, it moved the sharp point to and fro, and then gave forth this breath

- *il foco* ‘the fire’ → “the flame”: the noun is changed.
- *alquanto ebbe rugghiato* ‘awhile had roared’ → “had roared awhile”: the expression of duration follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *al modo suo* ‘in its own manner’ → “as usual”: a phrase describing manner becomes one describing customary behaviour.
- *l' aguta punta mosse* ‘the sharp point it moved’ → “it moved the sharp point”: the object follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *di qua, di là* ‘hither, thither’ → “to and fro”: the two directions become an idiomatic expression of alternating movement.
- *diè cotal fiato* ‘gave such breath’ → “gave forth this breath”: “forth” is supplied, and *cotal* becomes the demonstrative “this”.

> 61 " S' io credessi che mia risposta fosse  
> 62 a persona che mai tornasse al mondo,  
> 63 questa fiamma staria senza più scosse ;  

: "If I thought my answer were to one who ever could return to the world, this flame should shake no more;

- *S' io credessi che* ‘If I believed that’ → “If I thought”: belief becomes thought, and the conjunction introducing the dependent statement is omitted.
- *a persona* ‘to a person’ → “to one”: a pronoun replaces the noun.
- *che mai tornasse* ‘who ever returned’ → “who ever could return”: the Italian subjunctive is rendered with an explicit modal of possibility.
- *staria senza più scosse* ‘would remain without further shakings’ → “should shake no more”: remaining without movement becomes a negated verb of movement; the plural noun becomes the verb “shake”.

> 64 ma per ciò che giammai di questo fondo  
> 65 non tornò vivo alcun, s' i' odo il vero,  
> 66 senza tema d' infamia ti rispondo.  

but since none ever did return alive from this depth, if what I hear be true, without fear of infamy I answer thee.

- *per ciò che* ‘for the reason that’ → “since”: the connective phrase is compressed into a conjunction.
- *giammai … non tornò vivo alcun* ‘never … returned alive anyone’ → “none ever did return alive”: the Italian negative construction becomes “none ever”; the subject moves before the verb, and the simple past is expressed with “did”.
- *di questo fondo* ‘from this bottom’ → “from this depth”: “depth” replaces ‘bottom’, and the phrase moves from before the verb to after “alive”.
- *s' i' odo il vero* ‘if I hear the truth’ → “if what I hear be true”: the object ‘the truth’ becomes a predicate adjective applied to a new subject, “what I hear”.
- *ti rispondo* ‘to thee I answer’ → “I answer thee”: the preverbal indirect-object pronoun becomes an object following the verb, without a preposition.

> 67 Io fui uom d' arme, e poi fui cordelliero,  
> 68 credendomi, sì cinto, fare ammenda ;  

I was a man of arms; and then became a Cordelier, hoping, thus girt, to make amends;

- *fui cordelliero* ‘was a Cordelier’ → “became a Cordelier”: a statement of status becomes a statement of entering that status.
- *credendomi* ‘believing myself’ → “hoping”: belief becomes hope, and the reflexive pronoun is not separately represented.
- *fare ammenda* ‘to make reparation’ → “to make amends”: the singular noun is rendered by the idiomatic plural “amends”.

> 69 e certo il creder mio veniva intero,  
> 70 se non fosse il gran prete, a cui mal prenda,  
> 71 che mi rimise nelle prime colpe ;  

and certainly my hope were come in full, but for the Great Priest, may ill befall him! who brought me back to my first sins;

- *il creder mio* ‘the believing of mine’ → “my hope”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun denoting hope rather than belief, and the possessive precedes it.
- *veniva intero* ‘came whole’ → “were come in full”: the imperfect indicative becomes a counterfactual construction; the adjective becomes an adverbial phrase.
- *se non fosse* ‘if it were not’ → “but for”: the finite conditional clause becomes a prepositional construction.
- *a cui mal prenda* ‘to whom may ill befall’ → “may ill befall him!”: a relative clause expressing a wish becomes an independent wish, with “him” following the verb.
- *mi rimise nelle prime colpe* ‘put me back into the first faults’ → “brought me back to my first sins”: “brought” replaces ‘put’; the object pronoun follows the verb; ‘into’ becomes “to”; “sins” gives *colpe* its moral sense, and “my” replaces the definite article.

> 72 e come e quare voglio che m' intenda.  

and how and why, I wish thee to hear from me.

- *voglio che m' intenda* ‘I wish that thou understand me’ → “I wish thee to hear from me”: the finite subjunctive clause becomes an object-and-infinitive construction; understanding becomes hearing, and the object ‘me’ becomes the source “from me”.

> 73 Mentre ch' io forma fui d' ossa e di polpe,  
> 74 che la madre mi diè, l' opere mie  
> 75 non furon leonine, ma di volpe.  

Whilst I was the form of bones and pulp, which my mother gave me, my deeds were not those of the lion, but of the fox.

- *io forma fui* ‘I form was’ → “I was the form”: the predicate noun follows the verb, and the definite article is supplied.
- *d' ossa e di polpe* ‘of bones and of fleshy parts’ → “of bones and pulp”: the repeated preposition is omitted, and the plural *polpe* becomes the mass noun “pulp”.
- *la madre mi diè* ‘the mother to me gave’ → “my mother gave me”: the definite article becomes a possessive, and the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *l' opere mie* ‘the deeds of mine’ → “my deeds”: the postposed possessive moves before the noun.
- *non furon leonine* ‘were not leonine’ → “were not those of the lion”: the adjective becomes a phrase with the substitute pronoun “those” and a noun.
- *di volpe* ‘of fox’ → “of the fox”: the definite article is supplied.

> 76 Gli accorgimenti e le coperte vie  
> 77 io seppi tutte, e sì menai lor arte,  
> 78 ch' al fine della terra il suono uscie.  

All wiles and covert ways I knew; and used the art of them so well, that to the ends of the earth the sound went forth.

- *Gli accorgimenti e le coperte vie io seppi tutte* ‘the wiles and the covert ways I knew all’ → “All wiles and covert ways I knew”: “All” moves from after the verb to the beginning of the object phrase, and the definite articles are omitted.
- *e sì menai lor arte* ‘and so practised their art’ → “and used the art of them so well”: practising becomes using; the possessive becomes the postposed phrase “of them”; *sì* moves after the object and is expanded to “so well”.
- *al fine della terra* ‘to the end of the earth’ → “to the ends of the earth”: the singular becomes plural.
- *uscie* ‘went out’ → “went forth”: the direction is expressed with “forth” rather than ‘out’.

> 79 Quando mi vidi giunto in quella parte  
> 80 di mia etade, ove ciascun dovrebbe  
> 81 calar le vele e raccoglier le sarte,  

When I saw myself come to that period of my age at which every one should lower sails and gather in his ropes,

- *mi vidi* ‘myself I saw’ → “I saw myself”: the reflexive pronoun follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *giunto in quella parte* ‘arrived in that part’ → “come to that period”: arrival is expressed as coming, ‘in’ becomes “to”, and “period” makes the temporal sense of *parte* explicit.
- *ove* ‘where’ → “at which”: the relative adverb becomes a preposition and relative pronoun referring to the period.
- *calar le vele* ‘lower the sails’ → “lower sails”: the definite article is omitted.
- *raccoglier le sarte* ‘gather the rigging’ → “gather in his ropes”: “in” is supplied, the nautical term becomes the more general “ropes”, and the definite article becomes a possessive.

> 82 ciò che pria mi piaceva allor m' increbbe,  
> 83 e pentuto e confesso mi rendei ;  

that which before had pleased me, grieved me then; and with repentance and confession I [became a monk];

- *mi piaceva* ‘to me was pleasing’ → “had pleased me”: the imperfect becomes a pluperfect, and the Italian indirect-object pronoun becomes a direct object following the verb.
- *allor m' increbbe* ‘then me grieved’ → “grieved me then”: both the object pronoun and the adverb move after the verb.
- *e pentuto e confesso* ‘and repentant and confessed’ → “and with repentance and confession”: adjectives describing the speaker become nouns governed by “with”.
- *mi rendei* ‘I gave myself up’ → “I [became a monk]”: the reflexive expression is rendered according to its sense of entering religious life, rather than by its literal construction.

> 84 ahi miser lasso ! e giovato sarebbe.  

ah woe alas! and it would have availed me.

- *miser* ‘wretched’ → “woe”: an adjective describing the speaker becomes an exclamatory noun.
- *giovato sarebbe* ‘availed would have’ → “would have availed me”: the participle moves after its auxiliary, and “me” is supplied as the beneficiary.

> 85 Lo principe de' nuovi Farisei,  
> 86 avendo guerra presso a Laterano,  
> 87 e non con Saracin, nè con Giudei,  

The Prince of the new Pharisees—waging war near to the Lateran, and not with Saracens or Jews;

- *avendo guerra* ‘having war’ → “waging war”: the general verb of possession becomes the English idiomatic verb for conducting war.
- *presso a Laterano* ‘near to Lateran’ → “near to the Lateran”: the definite article is supplied.
- *e non con Saracin, nè con Giudei* ‘and not with Saracens, nor with Jews’ → “and not with Saracens or Jews”: the second negative conjunction becomes “or” under the shared “not”, and the repeated preposition is omitted.

> 88 chè ciascun suo nimico era Cristiano,  
> 89 e nessuno era stato a vincer Acri,  
> 90 nè mercatante in terra di Soldano ;  

for every enemy of his was Christian, and none had been to conquer Acre, nor been a merchant in the Soldan's land—

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *ciascun suo nimico* ‘each his enemy’ → “every enemy of his”: the possessive preceding the noun becomes the postposed phrase “of his”.
- *nè mercatante* ‘nor merchant’ → “nor been a merchant”: “been”, understood from *era stato*, is repeated explicitly, and an indefinite article is supplied.
- *in terra di Soldano* ‘in land of Soldan’ → “in the Soldan's land”: the following *di* phrase becomes a preceding possessive, and the definite article is supplied.

## Lines 91–123 — Fraudulent Counsel and Failed Absolution

> 91 nè sommo ufficio, nè ordini sacri  
> 92 guardò in sè, nè in me quel capestro  
> 93 che solea far li suoi cinti più macri.  

regarded not the Highest Office nor Holy Orders in himself, nor in me that Cord which used to make those whom it girded leaner.

- *nè … guardò* ‘neither … regarded’ → “regarded not”: the verb moves before its objects, and the first correlative negative becomes a negative attached to the verb.
- *sommo ufficio* ‘highest office’ → “the Highest Office”: the definite article is supplied.
- *ordini sacri* ‘orders holy’ → “Holy Orders”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *capestro* ‘halter’ → “Cord”: a different, less specific noun is used.
- *li suoi cinti* ‘its girded ones’ → “those whom it girded”: the substantival participle becomes a relative clause, and the possessive becomes the subject of a finite verb.

> 94 Ma come Costantin chiese Silvestro  
> 95 dentro Siratti a guarir della lebbre,  
> 96 così mi chiese questi per maestro  
> 97 a guarir della sua superba febbre;  

But as Constantine [sought] Silvestro [within] Soracte to cure his leprosy, so this man called me as an adept to cure the fever of his pride;

- *chiese* ‘asked for’ → “[sought]”: asking for someone is rendered as seeking him.
- *a guarir della lebbre* ‘to heal of the leprosy’ → “to cure his leprosy”: the illness becomes the direct object of “cure,” and a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *così mi chiese questi* ‘so me asked for this one’ → “so this man called me”: the subject precedes the verb and the object follows it; the demonstrative is expanded with “man,” and asking for becomes calling.
- *per maestro* ‘as a master’ → “as an adept”: “adept” emphasizes expertise rather than the status expressed by ‘master.’
- *a guarir della sua superba febbre* ‘to heal of his proud fever’ → “to cure the fever of his pride”: the illness becomes the direct object; the adjective describing the fever becomes the abstract noun “pride” in a possessive phrase.

> 98 domandommi consiglio, ed io tacetti,  
> 99 perché le sue parole parver ebbre.  

he demanded counsel of me; and I kept silent, for his words seemed drunken.

- *domandommi consiglio* ‘he asked me counsel’ → “he demanded counsel of me”: “demanded” strengthens ‘asked,’ and the personal object becomes an “of” phrase placed after “counsel.”
- *ed io tacetti* ‘and I was silent’ → “and I kept silent”: the simple verb is rendered by a phrase emphasizing continued silence.
- *perché* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.

> 100 E poi mi disse: ‘Tuo cor non sospetti;  
> 101 finor t’ assolvo, e tu m’ insegna fare  
> 102 sì come Penestrino in terra getti.  

And then he said to me: ‘Let not thy heart misdoubt; even now I do absolve thee, and do thou teach me so to act, that I may cast Penestrino to the ground.

- *mi disse* ‘to me he said’ → “he said to me”: the indirect object follows the verb.
- *Tuo cor non sospetti* ‘let thy heart not suspect’ → “Let not thy heart misdoubt”: the subjunctive injunction becomes a construction with “Let”; the negative precedes the subject, and “misdoubt” renders suspecting or feeling apprehension.
- *finor* ‘already’ → “even now”: the adverb becomes a phrase stressing present, anticipatory force.
- *t’assolvo* ‘thee I absolve’ → “I do absolve thee”: the object follows the verb, and emphatic “do” is supplied.
- *e tu m’insegna fare sì come Penestrino in terra getti* ‘and thou teach me to do in such a way that I may cast Penestrino to earth’ → “and do thou teach me so to act, that I may cast Penestrino to the ground.”: the imperative receives “do”; *fare* becomes “act”; the manner construction is recast as “so … that”; the verb precedes its object and destination, and “ground” receives a definite article.

> 103 Lo ciel poss’ io serrare e disserrare,  
> 104 come tu sai: però son due le chiavi,  
> 105 che il mio antecessor non ebbe care.’  

Heaven I can shut and open, as thou knowest: for two are the keys that my predecessor held not dear.’

- *Lo ciel poss’io serrare e disserrare* ‘the heaven can I shut and open’ → “Heaven I can shut and open”: the article is omitted, and the subject precedes the modal verb.
- *però son due le chiavi* ‘therefore are two the keys’ → “for two are the keys”: the inferential connective becomes a causal one, and “two” moves before the copula.
- *non ebbe care* ‘did not have dear’ → “held not dear”: the idiom is rendered with “held,” and the negative follows the finite verb.

> 106 Allor mi pinser gli argomenti gravi  
> 107 là ‘ve il tacer mi fu avviso il peggio,  
> 108 e dissi: ‘Padre, da che tu mi lavi  
> 109 di quel peccato, ov’ io mo cader deggio,  

Then the weighty arguments impelled me to think silence worst; and I said: ‘Father! since thou cleansest me from that guilt into which I now must fall,

- *Allor mi pinser gli argomenti gravi* ‘then me pushed the arguments weighty’ → “Then the weighty arguments impelled me”: the subject moves before the verb, the object follows it, and the adjective precedes its noun; figurative pushing becomes “impelled.”
- *là ‘ve il tacer mi fu avviso il peggio* ‘there where keeping silent seemed to me the worst’ → “to think silence worst”: the spatially expressed clause becomes an infinitive of thought dependent on “impelled”; the substantival infinitive becomes “silence,” and the article before the superlative is omitted.
- *mi lavi di quel peccato* ‘me thou washest of that sin’ → “thou cleansest me from that guilt”: the object follows the verb; washing becomes cleansing, ‘of’ becomes “from,” and ‘sin’ becomes “guilt.”
- *ov’io mo cader deggio* ‘where I now fall must’ → “into which I now must fall”: the relative adverb becomes a preposition and relative pronoun, and the modal precedes the infinitive.

> 110 lunga promessa con l’ attender corto  
> 111 ti farà trionfar nell’ alto seggio.’  

large promise, with small observance of it, will make thee triumph in thy High Seat.’

- *lunga promessa con l’attender corto* ‘long promise with the short fulfilling’ → “large promise, with small observance of it”: length becomes magnitude in “large” and “small”; the substantival infinitive becomes “observance”; its article is omitted, its adjective moves before it, and “of it” is supplied.
- *ti farà trionfar nell’alto seggio* ‘thee will make triumph in the high seat’ → “will make thee triumph in thy High Seat”: the personal object follows the causative verb, and a possessive replaces the definite article.

> 112 Francesco venne poi, com’ io fui morto,  
> 113 per me, ma un de’ neri cherubini  
> 114 gli disse: ‘Nol portar, non mi far torto.  

Saint Francis afterwards, when I was dead, came for me; but one of the Black Cherubim said to him: ‘Do not take him; wrong me not.

- *Francesco venne poi, com’io fui morto, per me* ‘Francis came afterwards, when I was dead, for me’ → “Saint Francis afterwards, when I was dead, came for me”: “Saint” is supplied, and “came” moves after the temporal clause.
- *gli disse* ‘to him said’ → “said to him”: the indirect object follows the verb.
- *Nol portar* ‘do not carry him’ → “Do not take him”: carrying is rendered more generally as taking.
- *non mi far torto* ‘do not do me wrong’ → “wrong me not”: the verb-and-noun construction becomes the single verb “wrong,” and the negative follows the verb and object.

> 115 Venir sen dee laggiù tra’ miei meschini,  
> 116 perchè diede il consiglio frodolente,  
> 117 dal quale in qua stato gli sono a’ crini:  

He must come down amongst my menials; because he gave the fraudulent counsel, since which I have kept fast by his hair:

- *Venir sen dee laggiù* ‘come away he must down there’ → “He must come down”: the modal precedes the infinitive; the pronominal motion construction is reduced to “come,” and the explicit distal element in ‘down there’ is omitted.
- *meschini* ‘wretches’ → “menials”: the translation selects the word’s sense of servants or dependants rather than wretched persons.
- *il consiglio frodolente* ‘the counsel fraudulent’ → “the fraudulent counsel”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *dal quale in qua stato gli sono a’ crini* ‘from which until now I have been at his hairs’ → “since which I have kept fast by his hair”: “since” condenses the temporal phrase; being at becomes keeping fast by; the personal dative becomes a possessive, and the plural noun becomes collective singular “hair.”

> 118 ch’ assolver non si può, chi non si pente,  
> 119 nè pentere e volere insieme puossi,  
> 120 per la contraddizion che nol consente.’  

for he who repents not, cannot be absolved; nor is it possible to repent and will a *thing* at the same time, the contradiction not permitting it.’

- *ch’* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *assolver non si può, chi non si pente* ‘one cannot absolve whoever does not repent’ → “he who repents not, cannot be absolved”: the impersonal active construction becomes passive, and the person absolved moves from object to subject; the negative in the relative clause follows its verb.
- *nè pentere e volere insieme puossi* ‘nor repent and will together can one’ → “nor is it possible to repent and will a *thing* at the same time,”: the impersonal modal becomes “is it possible,” placed before the infinitives; an object, “a *thing*,” is supplied, and ‘together’ becomes “at the same time.”
- *per la contraddizion che nol consente* ‘because of the contradiction that does not permit it’ → “the contradiction not permitting it.”: the causal prepositional phrase and its relative clause become an absolute participial construction.

> 121 O me dolente! come mi riscossi,  
> 122 quando mi prese, dicendomi: ‘Forse  
> 123 tu non pensavi ch’io loico fossi.’  

O wretched me! how I started when he seized me, saying to me: ‘May be thou didst not think that I was a logician!’

- *O me dolente!* ‘O me sorrowful!’ → “O wretched me!”: the adjective precedes the pronoun, and sorrow becomes wretchedness.
- *come mi riscossi* ‘how I roused myself’ → “how I started”: the reflexive verb becomes an intransitive verb expressing a sudden reaction.
- *quando mi prese* ‘when me he seized’ → “when he seized me”: the object follows the verb.
- *tu non pensavi* ‘thou wast not thinking’ → “thou didst not think”: the imperfect is rendered by the English simple past.
- *ch’io loico fossi* ‘that I logician were’ → “that I was a logician”: the subjunctive becomes indicative, the copula precedes its complement, and an indefinite article is supplied.

## Lines 124–136 — Minos’s Judgment and the Next Ditch

> 124 A Minos mi portò; e quegli attorse  
> 125 otto volte la coda al dosso duro,  
> 126 e, poi che per gran rabbia la si morse,  
> 127 disse: ‘Questi è de’ rei del fuoco furo’;  

To Minos he bore me, who twined his tail eight times round his fearful back, and then biting it in great rage, said : 'This is a sinner for the thievish fire'

- *mi portò* ‘me he bore’ → “he bore me”: the object pronoun moves after the verb.
- *e quegli* ‘and that one’ → “who”: a coordinated clause becomes a relative clause.
- *otto volte la coda* ‘eight times the tail’ → “his tail eight times”: the object moves before the expression of frequency, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *al dosso duro* ‘at the hard back’ → “round his fearful back”: the preposition explicitly expresses encirclement; the article becomes a possessive; ‘hard’ becomes “fearful”; and the adjective moves before the noun.
- *e, poi che per gran rabbia la si morse* ‘and, after he bit it out of great rage’ → “and then biting it in great rage”: the finite temporal clause becomes a participial construction with “then”; the expression of rage moves after the verb and expresses an accompanying state rather than explicitly a cause. The reflexive *si*, marking the action as affecting the subject himself, has no separate equivalent.
- *Questi è de’ rei del fuoco furo* ‘This one is of the guilty of the thievish fire’ → “This is a sinner for the thievish fire”: the demonstrative is shortened; the partitive expression ‘of the guilty’ becomes the singular “a sinner”; and “for” interprets the second *de’/del* relationship as assignment to the fire rather than simple belonging.

> 128 per ch’io là dove vedi son perduto,  
> 129 e sì vestito andando mi rancuro.”  

; therefore I, where thou seest, am lost ; and going thus clothed, in heart I grieve."

- *per ch’* ‘for which reason’ → “therefore”: the connective phrase becomes an adverb.
- *là dove vedi* ‘there where thou seest’ → “where thou seest”: *là* is omitted.
- *sì vestito andando* ‘thus clothed going’ → “going thus clothed”: the participle of motion moves before the description of clothing.
- *mi rancuro* ‘I grieve’ → “in heart I grieve”: “in heart” is supplied, and the Italian reflexive verb is represented by an English intransitive verb.

> 130 Quand’ egli ebbe il suo dir così compiuto,  
> 131 la fiamma dolorando si partio,  
> 132 torcendo e dibattendo il corno acuto.  

When he his words had ended thus, the flame, sorrowing, departed, writhing and tossing its sharp horn.

- *ebbe il suo dir così compiuto* ‘had his saying thus completed’ → “his words had ended thus”: the substantivized infinitive *dir* becomes the plural noun “words”; the object moves before the auxiliary, and “thus” moves after the participle. ‘Completed’ becomes “ended,” and the Italian past anterior is rendered by the English past perfect.
- *torcendo* ‘twisting’ → “writhing”: the verb of twisting is rendered by a verb emphasizing contorted movement.
- *dibattendo* ‘beating about’ → “tossing”: the repeated shaking or beating movement is expressed as tossing.
- *il corno acuto* ‘the horn sharp’ → “its sharp horn”: the definite article becomes a possessive, and the adjective moves before the noun.

> 133 Noi passammo oltre, ed io e il duca mio,  
> 134 su per lo scoglio infino in su l’altr’ arco  
> 135 che copre il fosso, in che si paga il fio  
> 136 a quei che scommettendo acquistan carco.  

We passed on, I and my Guide, along the cliff up to the other arch that covers the foss, in which their fee is paid to those who, sowing discord, gather guilt.

- *Noi passammo oltre* ‘We passed beyond’ → “We passed on”: movement beyond a point is expressed as continued movement.
- *ed io e il duca mio* ‘both I and the guide my’ → “I and my Guide”: the first member of the paired conjunction is omitted, and the possessive moves before its noun.
- *su per lo scoglio* ‘up along the rock’ → “along the cliff”: the first explicit indication of upward movement is omitted, and ‘rock’ is rendered more specifically as “cliff.”
- *infino in su l’altr’ arco* ‘as far as up onto the other arch’ → “up to the other arch”: the accumulated expressions of extent and direction are compressed into “up to.”
- *in che si paga il fio* ‘in which the penalty is paid’ → “in which their fee is paid”: the Italian *si* construction becomes an explicit English passive; the definite article becomes “their,” anticipating “those”; and *fio* is rendered by its monetary sense, “fee,” rather than ‘penalty.’
- *scommettendo* ‘sundering’ → “sowing discord”: a single verb expressing separation is expanded into an idiom naming discord explicitly.
- *acquistan carco* ‘acquire a burden’ → “gather guilt”: acquisition becomes gathering, and the figurative ‘burden’ is interpreted explicitly as “guilt.”

## Summary — Close Correspondence and English Reshaping

Carlyle’s prose follows the Italian closely in sequence, imagery, and much of its vocabulary, but repeatedly reshapes its syntax. Subjects and objects move into more customary English positions, adjectives generally precede their nouns, and auxiliaries precede participles. Thus *gli argomenti gravi* becomes “the weighty arguments,” placed before “impelled me.” Yet the translation also preserves conspicuous inversions, as in “The cities of Lamone and Santerno guides [the] Lioncel” and “Heaven I can shut and open.” Its closeness is therefore not simply lexical: some of the Italian’s marked arrangement survives in the English.

Changes of construction often replace compact Italian expressions with more explicit English ones. Substantivized forms become nouns or clauses: *il creder mio* becomes “my hope,” while *li suoi cinti*, ‘its girded ones’, becomes “those whom it girded.” Requests expressed by subjunctives receive “let,” and impersonal constructions become passives or phrases of possibility: *assolver non si può* is recast as “cannot be absolved.” Finite clauses sometimes become participial constructions, and the Italian imperfect is variously rendered by a simple past, a progressive expression, or an explicitly continuing action such as “kept bellowing.”

Word choice ranges from close correspondence to interpretive substitution. “the thievish fire” preserves the unusual image of *fuoco furo*, whereas *fan de’ denti succhio*, ‘make of the teeth a drill’, becomes “ply their teeth,” losing the drill image. Similarly, *scommettendo* becomes “sowing discord,” and *acquistan carco*, ‘acquire a burden’, becomes “gather guilt”: the English names what the Italian expresses figuratively. Elsewhere the shift is more local: *rame*, ‘copper’, becomes “brass”; *duro*, ‘hard’, becomes “fearful”; and *credendomi*, ‘believing myself’, becomes “hoping.”

Omissions and additions further adjust emphasis and explicitness. *Pure*, ‘yet’, and the *là* in *là dove vedi* receive no separate equivalent, while reflexive particles are often absorbed into ordinary English verbs. Conversely, “in heart” expands *mi rancuro*, and “a *thing*” supplies an object for *volere*. Logical relationships can also become more explicit, notably when *ed*, ‘and’, becomes “although.” Throughout, English supplies subject pronouns where Italian verb endings identify the subject, and normally omits the articles that Italian places before possessives. These routine grammatical adjustments accompany the more noticeable departures without substantially disturbing the translation’s close progression through the original.
