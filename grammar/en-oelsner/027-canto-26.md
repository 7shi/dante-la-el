# Inferno 26

## Lines 1–18 — Florence’s Shame and the Rocky Ascent

> 1 Godi, Fiorenza, poi che sei sì grande,  
> 2 che per mare e per terra batti l’ ali,  
> 3 e per l’ inferno il tuo nome si spande.  

Joy, Florence, since thou art so great that over land and sea thou beatest thy wings, and thy name through Hell expands itself!

- *per mare e per terra* ‘by sea and by land’ → “over land and sea”: the geographical terms exchange places, “over” replaces *per*, and the repeated preposition is omitted.
- *batti l’ ali* ‘thou beatest the wings’ → “thou beatest thy wings”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *per l’ inferno il tuo nome* ‘through Hell thy name’ → “thy name through Hell”: the subject moves before the prepositional phrase.
- *si spande* ‘spreads’ → “expands itself”: the pronominal verb is rendered with an explicitly reflexive construction and a verb of expansion rather than spreading.

> 4 Tra li ladron trovai cinque cotali  
> 5 tuoi cittadini, onde mi vien vergogna,  
> 6 e tu in grande onranza non ne sali.  

Among the thieves I found five such, thy citizens; whereat shame comes on me, and thou to great honour mountest not thereby.

- *onde* ‘whence’ → “whereat”: the connective expresses occasion or cause rather than source.
- *mi vien vergogna* ‘to me comes shame’ → “shame comes on me”: the subject moves before the verb, and the indirect-object pronoun becomes a following phrase with “on”.
- *in grande onranza non ne sali* ‘in great honour thou dost not rise from it’ → “to great honour mountest not thereby”: “to” makes honour the destination of the figurative ascent; the negative follows the verb, and *ne* becomes the final adverb “thereby”, expressing means or cause.

> 7 Ma se presso al mattin il ver si sogna,  
> 8 tu sentirai di qua da picciol tempo  
> 9 di quel che Prato, non ch’ altri, t’ agogna.  

But if the truth is dreamed of near the morning, thou shalt feel ere long what Prato, not to speak of others, craves for thee.

- *presso al mattin il ver si sogna* ‘near the morning one dreams the true’ → “the truth is dreamed of near the morning”: the substantivized adjective *il ver* becomes the noun “truth”; the impersonal construction becomes passive, “of” is supplied after “dreamed”, and the temporal phrase moves to the end.
- *di qua da picciol tempo* ‘on this side of a short time’ → “ere long”: the spatially phrased expression of proximity in time is compressed into an English temporal idiom.
- *di quel che* ‘of that which’ → “what”: the partitive preposition is omitted, and the demonstrative and relative pronoun are combined.
- *non ch’ altri* ‘not that others’ → “not to speak of others”: the elliptical Italian connective is expanded into an English infinitive phrase.
- *t’ agogna* ‘for thee craves’ → “craves for thee”: the indirect-object pronoun becomes a prepositional phrase following the verb.

> 10 E se già fosse, non saria per tempo;  

And if it were already _come_, it would not be too early;

- *se già fosse* ‘if already it were’ → “if it were already _come_”: “already” moves after the verb, and “_come_” is supplied, making explicit the occurrence understood with *fosse*.
- *per tempo* ‘early’ → “too early”: “too” supplies a degree not separately expressed in the Italian.

> 11 così foss’ ei da che pure esser dee :  

so were it! since indeed it must be:

- *esser dee* ‘be must’ → “it must be”: the infinitive moves after the finite modal verb.

> 12 chè più mi graverà, com’ più m’ attempo.  

for it will weigh the heavier on me as I grow older.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *più mi graverà* ‘more on me it will weigh’ → “it will weigh the heavier on me”: the initial comparative becomes the following phrase “the heavier”, and the affected person is expressed by a prepositional phrase after the verb.
- *com’ più m’ attempo* ‘as more I age’ → “as I grow older”: the pronominal verb and its comparative adverb become “grow” followed by a comparative adjective.

> 13 Noi ci partimmo, e su per le scalee,  
> 14 che n’avean fatte i borni a scender pria,  
> 15 rimontò il duca mio, e trasse mee ;  

We departed thence; and, by the stairs which the curbstones had [made for] us to descend before, my Guide remounted and drew me up;

- *Noi ci partimmo* ‘we departed’ → “We departed thence”: *ci*, which can belong to the pronominal verb *partirsi*, is given a locative interpretation in “thence”.
- *e su per le scalee … e trasse mee* ‘and up along the stairs … and drew me’ → “and, by the stairs” … “and drew me up”: “by” replaces ‘along’, and the explicit upward direction moves from the phrase describing the stairs to the final verb phrase.
- *n’avean fatte i borni* ‘had made for us the projecting stones’ → “the curbstones had [made for] us”: the postposed subject moves before the verb, and the preceding indirect-object pronoun becomes “for” with “us” after the verb. *Borni* is interpreted as “curbstones” rather than rendered as natural projecting stones.
- *a scender pria* ‘in descending before’ → “to descend before”: the Italian infinitive describing the circumstances of the earlier descent becomes an English infinitive describing the stairs’ purpose or use.
- *rimontò il duca mio* ‘remounted the leader mine’ → “my Guide remounted”: the subject moves before the verb, the possessive moves before the noun, and *duca* is rendered by the functional designation “Guide”.

> 16 e proseguendo la solinga via  
> 17 tra le schegge e tra’ rocchi dello scoglio,  
> 18 lo piè senza la man non si spedia.  

and pursuing our solitary way among the jaggs and branches of the cliff, the foot without the hand sped not.

- *la solinga via* ‘the solitary way’ → “our solitary way”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *schegge* ‘splinters’ → “jaggs”: the translation describes sharp, irregular projections rather than splinter-like fragments.
- *e tra’ rocchi* ‘and among the blocks’ → “and branches”: the repeated preposition and article are omitted, and the rocky blocks or chunks are represented as branch-like formations.
- *dello scoglio* ‘of the rock’ → “of the cliff”: the more general designation of rock is rendered as a particular landform.
- *non si spedia* ‘did not get free’ → “sped not”: the pronominal verb becomes the plain intransitive “sped”, emphasizing progress rather than getting clear, and the negative follows the verb.

## Lines 19–51 — Flames in the Eighth Chasm

> 19 Allor mi dolsi, ed ora mi ridoglio,  
> 20 quand' io drizzo la mente a ciò ch' io vidi;  

I sorrowed then, and sorrow now again when I direct my memory to what I saw;

- *Allor mi dolsi, ed ora mi ridoglio* ‘Then I sorrowed, and now I sorrow again’ → “I sorrowed then, and sorrow now again”: both temporal adverbs move after their verbs; the Italian pronominal verbs become plain intransitives, and the prefix *ri-* is expressed separately by “again”.
- *la mente* ‘the mind’ → “my memory”: the faculty is specified as memory, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *ciò ch'* ‘that which’ → “what”: the demonstrative and relative pronoun become a single fused relative.

> 21 e più lo ingegno affreno ch' io non soglio,  
> 22 perchè non corra, che virtù nol guidi;  

and curb my genius more than I am wont, lest it run where Virtue guides it not;

- *e più lo ingegno affreno ch' io non soglio* ‘and more the intellect I curb than I am not wont’ → “and curb my genius more than I am wont”: the verb precedes its object, and “more” follows it; *ingegno* becomes “genius”, and its article becomes “my”. The comparative clause’s expletive *non*, which does not negate *soglio*, is omitted.
- *perchè non corra* ‘so that it may not run’ → “lest it run”: the negative purpose construction is compressed into “lest” with a subjunctive.
- *che virtù nol guidi* ‘without virtue guiding it’ → “where Virtue guides it not”: the limiting construction becomes a clause of place; Italian subjunctive *guidi* becomes indicative “guides”, and the negative follows the verb.

> 23 sì che se stella buona o miglior cosa  
> 24 m' ha dato il ben, ch' io stesso nol m' invidi.  

so that, if kindly star or something better have given to me the good, I may not grudge myself that gift.

- *stella buona* ‘star good’ → “kindly star”: the adjective precedes the noun, and goodness is expressed as benevolence.
- *miglior cosa* ‘better thing’ → “something better”: the noun becomes an indefinite pronoun, with its modifier following it.
- *m' ha dato* ‘to me has given’ → “have given to me”: the indirect object follows the verb, and Italian singular indicative *ha* becomes the English conditional clause’s subjunctive-style “have”.
- *ch' io stesso nol m' invidi* ‘that I myself may not grudge it to myself’ → “I may not grudge myself that gift”: the resumptive *che* and emphatic *stesso* are omitted; the subjunctive is expressed with “may”. The pronoun *lo* becomes the explicit noun phrase “that gift”, and the two objects follow the verb.

> 25 Quante il villan, ch' al poggio si riposa,  
> 26 nel tempo che colui che il mondo schiara  
> 27 la faccia sua a noi tien meno ascosa,  
> 28 come la mosca cede alla zanzara,  

As many fireflies as the peasant who is resting on the hill—at the time that he who lights the world least hides his face from us, when the fly yields to the gnat—

- *Quante … lucciole* ‘As many … fireflies’ → “As many fireflies as”: the noun from line 29 is brought forward before the intervening clauses, and the comparison is expressed through the English paired construction “As many … as”.
- *ch' al poggio si riposa* ‘who on the hill rests’ → “who is resting on the hill”: the place phrase follows the verb; the simple present becomes progressive, and the pronominal verb becomes an English intransitive.
- *nel tempo che* ‘in the time when’ → “at the time that”: the preposition and relative connective change.
- *colui che il mondo schiara* ‘he who the world lights’ → “he who lights the world”: the object follows its verb.
- *la faccia sua a noi tien meno ascosa* ‘his face to us holds less hidden’ → “least hides his face from us”: ‘holds hidden’ becomes the single verb “hides”, with its object following it; comparative *meno* becomes superlative “least”, and ‘to us’ becomes “from us”.
- *come* ‘as’ → “when”: the temporal connection is made explicit.
- *zanzara* ‘mosquito’ → “gnat”: a different, broader English insect name is used.

> 29 vede lucciole giù per la vallea,  
> 30 forse colà dove vendemmia ed ara:  

sees down along the valley, there perchance where he gathers grapes and tills:

- *giù per la vallea* ‘down through the valley’ → “down along the valley”: *per* is rendered as extension along the valley rather than passage through it.
- *forse colà* ‘perhaps over there’ → “there perchance”: the adverbs exchange order, and *colà*’s indication of distance is not separately expressed.
- *vendemmia ed ara* ‘harvests grapes and ploughs’ → “gathers grapes and tills”: the specialized verb *vendemmia* is expanded into a verb and object; “tills” is less specific than ‘ploughs’.

> 31 di tante fiamme tutta risplendea  
> 32 l' ottava bolgia, sì com' io m' accorsi,  
> 33 tosto ch' io fui là 've il fondo parea.  

with flames thus numerous the eighth chasm was all gleaming, as I perceived, so soon as I came to where the bottom shewed itself.

- *di tante fiamme* ‘with so many flames’ → “with flames thus numerous”: the quantity precedes the noun in Italian but follows it as an adjectival phrase in English.
- *tutta risplendea l' ottava bolgia* ‘all shone the eighth pouch’ → “the eighth chasm was all gleaming”: the subject moves before the verb; the imperfect becomes a past progressive. *Tutta*, agreeing with *bolgia*, is placed with the English verb, and *bolgia* is rendered as “chasm” rather than ‘pouch’.
- *sì com' io m' accorsi* ‘just as I became aware’ → “as I perceived”: the emphasis of *sì* is omitted, and the pronominal expression becomes a plain active verb.
- *io fui là 've* ‘I was there where’ → “I came to where”: being at a place is interpreted as arriving there; the separate *là* is absorbed into the destination construction.
- *il fondo parea* ‘the bottom appeared’ → “the bottom shewed itself”: the intransitive verb becomes a reflexive expression.

> 34 E qual colui che si vengiò con gli orsi  
> 35 vide il carro d' Elia al dipartire,  
> 36 quando i cavalli al cielo erti levorsi,  

And as he, who was avenged by the bears, saw Elijah’s chariot at its departure, when the horses rose erect to heaven,—

- *si vengiò con gli orsi* ‘avenged himself with the bears’ → “was avenged by the bears”: the reflexive active becomes passive, and the instrumental phrase becomes a phrase of agency.
- *il carro d' Elia* ‘the chariot of Elijah’ → “Elijah’s chariot”: the possessive construction replaces the following *di*-phrase and reverses the order of the nouns.
- *al dipartire* ‘at the departing’ → “at its departure”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun, and “its” is supplied.
- *i cavalli al cielo erti levorsi* ‘the horses to heaven upright lifted themselves’ → “the horses rose erect to heaven”: the reflexive verb becomes intransitive; the verb precedes both the adjective and the destination, whose order is also reversed.

> 37 chè nol potea sì con gli occhi seguire  
> 38 ch' ei vedesse altro che la fiamma sola,  
> 39 sì come nuvoletta, in su salire:  

for he could not so follow it with his eyes as to see other than the flame alone, like a little cloud, ascending up:

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *nol potea sì con gli occhi seguire* ‘he could not so with the eyes follow it’ → “he could not so follow it with his eyes”: the infinitive precedes the instrumental phrase, the object pronoun follows the infinitive, and the definite article becomes “his”.
- *ch' ei vedesse* ‘that he might see’ → “as to see”: the finite subjunctive clause of result becomes an infinitive construction.
- *sì come nuvoletta* ‘just like a little cloud’ → “like a little cloud”: the emphasis of *sì* is omitted; the diminutive suffix is expressed by the separate adjective “little”.
- *in su salire* ‘upwards to rise’ → “ascending up”: the infinitive becomes a present participle, and the directional expression follows it.

> 40 tal si movea ciascuna per la gola  
> 41 del fosso, chè nessuna mostra il furto,  
> 42 ed ogni fiamma un peccatore invola.  

thus moved each of *those flames* along the gullet of the foss, for none of them shews the theft, and every flame steals a sinner.

- *tal si movea ciascuna* ‘thus moved each one’ → “thus moved each of *those flames*”: the pronominal verb becomes an English intransitive, and the understood reference of *ciascuna* is supplied as an explicit partitive phrase.
- *per* ‘through’ → “along”: the preposition expresses movement along rather than through.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *nessuna* ‘none’ → “none of them”: the partitive complement is supplied.
- *ed ogni fiamma un peccatore invola* ‘and every flame a sinner steals’ → “and every flame steals a sinner”: the object moves after the verb.

> 43 Io stava sopra il ponte a veder surto,  
> 44 sì che, s' io non avessi un ronchion preso,  
> 45 caduto sarei giù senza esser urto.  

I stood upon the bridge, *having* risen so to look, that, if I had not caught a rock, I should have fallen down without being pushed.

- *a veder surto, sì che* ‘to look, risen, so that’ → “*having* risen so to look, that,”: “having” makes the participial construction explicitly perfect, interpreting *surto* as a completed rising rather than simply an upright posture. “Risen” precedes the purpose infinitive, and “so” moves into the phrase describing that action.
- *s' io non avessi un ronchion preso* ‘if I had not a rocky projection grasped’ → “if I had not caught a rock”: the participle precedes its object; “caught” replaces ‘grasped’, and “rock” is less specific than *ronchion*.
- *caduto sarei giù* ‘fallen I should be down’ → “I should have fallen down”: the subject and auxiliary precede the participle; the Italian perfect auxiliary ‘be’ becomes English “have”, and the conditional is expressed with “should”.
- *senza esser urto* ‘without being struck’ → “without being pushed”: the verb is interpreted specifically as a push.

> 46 E il duca, che mi vide tanto atteso,  
> 47 disse: "Dentro da' fochi son gli spirti;  
> 48 ciascun si fascia di quel ch' egli è inceso."  

And the Guide, who saw me thus attent, said: “Within those fires are the spirits; each swathes himself with that which burns him.”

- *duca* ‘leader’ → “Guide”: the designation is rendered in terms of guidance rather than leadership.
- *tanto* ‘so’ → “thus”: an expression of degree becomes one of manner.
- *Dentro da' fochi* ‘Within the fires’ → “Within those fires”: the definite article becomes a demonstrative.
- *di quel ch' egli è inceso* ‘in that with which he is burnt’ → “with that which burns him”: the material enveloping him is introduced by “with” rather than ‘in’; the passive relative construction becomes active, making its antecedent the subject and the sinner the object.

> 49 "Maestro mio," rispos' io, "per udirti  
> 50 son io più certo; ma già m' era avviso  
> 51 che così fusse, e già voleva dirti:  

“Master,” I replied, “from hearing thee I feel more certain; but had already discerned it to be so, and already wished to say to thee:

- *Maestro mio* ‘Master mine’ → “Master”: the possessive is omitted.
- *rispos' io* ‘replied I’ → “I replied”: the subject moves before the verb.
- *per udirti* ‘through hearing thee’ → “from hearing thee”: the causal preposition changes, and the Italian infinitive is rendered by an English gerund.
- *son io più certo* ‘am I more certain’ → “I feel more certain”: the subject moves before the verb, and being certain becomes feeling certain.
- *ma già m' era avviso che così fusse* ‘but already it was apparent to me that it was so’ → “but had already discerned it to be so”: the impersonal expression becomes an active first-person pluperfect; the finite subjunctive clause becomes an object followed by an infinitive, and “already” moves after the auxiliary.

## Lines 52–84 — The Two In One Flame

> 52 Chi è in quel foco, che vien sì diviso  
> 53 di sopra, che par surger della pira  
> 54 ov' Eteòcle col fratel fu miso?”  

Who is in that fire, which comes so parted at the top, as if it rose from the pyre where Eteocles with his brother was placed?”

- *di sopra* ‘above’ → “at the top”: the translation specifies the upper part of the flame.
- *che par surger* ‘that it seems to rise’ → “as if it rose”: a clause expressing appearance through *par* becomes an *as if* clause, with a finite verb replacing the infinitive.
- *col fratel* ‘with the brother’ → “with his brother”: the definite article becomes a possessive.

> 55 Risposemi : “ Là entro si martira  
> 56 Ulisse e Diomede, e così insieme  
> 57 alla vendetta vanno come all' ira;  

He answered me: “Within it there Ulysses is tortured, and Diomed; and thus they run together in punishment, as *erst* in wrath;

- *Là entro* ‘there within’ → “Within it there”: the spatial expressions change order, and “it” explicitly supplies the object of “Within”.
- *si martira / Ulisse e Diomede* ‘is tormented Ulysses and Diomed’ → “Ulysses is tortured, and Diomed”: the first member of the compound subject moves before the verb; the Italian passive construction with *si* becomes an English passive with “is”.
- *e così insieme / alla vendetta vanno* ‘and thus together to vengeance they go’ → “and thus they run together in punishment”: “run” replaces ‘go’, *insieme* moves after the verb, and movement ‘to vengeance’ becomes participation “in punishment”.
- *come all' ira* ‘as to wrath’ → “as *erst* in wrath”: “in” replaces ‘to’, and “erst” supplies an explicit contrast with their former condition.

> 58 e dentro dalla lor fiamma si geme  
> 59 l'aguato del caval, che fe' la porta  
> 60 ond' uscì de' Romani il gentil seme;  

and in their flame they groan for the ambush of the horse, that made the door by which the noble seed of the Romans came forth;

- *si geme / l'aguato del caval* ‘is lamented the ambush of the horse’ → “they groan for the ambush of the horse”: the passive construction becomes active; the ambush becomes the object of the supplied preposition “for”, rather than the subject of the lamentation.
- *ond' uscì de' Romani il gentil seme* ‘whence came forth of the Romans the noble seed’ → “by which the noble seed of the Romans came forth”: “by which” presents the door as a passage rather than a point of departure; the subject moves before the verb, and *de' Romani* moves after the noun phrase it qualifies.

> 61 piangevisi entro l' arte, per che morta  
> 62 Deidamìa ancor si duol d' Achille,  

within it they lament the artifice, whereby Deidamia in death still sorrows for Achilles;

- *piangevisi entro l' arte* ‘is wept for therein within the art’ → “within it they lament the artifice”: the passive becomes active, with “they” supplied as the agents; *l'arte* becomes the object and is rendered more specifically as “the artifice”. The overlapping locative expressions *vi* and *entro* become the single opening phrase “within it”.
- *morta / Deidamìa* ‘dead Deidamia’ → “Deidamia in death”: the adjective becomes a prepositional phrase and moves after the name.

> 63 e del Palladio pena vi si porta.”  

and there for the Palladium they suffer punishment.”

- *e del Palladio pena vi si porta* ‘and for the Palladium punishment there is borne’ → “and there for the Palladium they suffer punishment”: the passive construction becomes active, with “they” supplied as the sufferers; ‘bear’ becomes “suffer”, punishment becomes the object, and the locative *vi* moves before the phrase concerning the Palladium.

> 64 “ S' ei posson dentro da quelle faville  
> 65 parlar,” diss' io, “ maestro, assai ten prego,  
> 66 e riprego che il prego vaglia mille,  

“If they within those sparks can speak,” said I, “Master! I pray thee much, and repray that my prayer may equal a thousand,

- *posson dentro da quelle faville / parlar* ‘can within those sparks speak’ → “within those sparks can speak”: the spatial phrase moves before the modal verb, bringing “can speak” together.
- *assai ten prego* ‘much I pray thee for it’ → “I pray thee much”: *assai* moves to the end, the object pronoun follows the verb, and *ne*, incorporated in *ten*, has no explicit equivalent.
- *il prego* ‘the prayer’ → “my prayer”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *vaglia mille* ‘may be worth a thousand’ → “may equal a thousand”: worth or efficacy is expressed as equality.

> 67 che non mi facci dell' attender niego,  
> 68 finchè la fiamma cornuta qua vegna;  
> 69 vedi che del disio ver lei mi piego.”  

deny me not to wait until the horned flame comes hither; thou seest how with desire I bend me towards it.”

- *che non mi facci dell' attender niego* ‘that thou not make me a refusal of waiting’ → “deny me not to wait”: the subordinate clause dependent on the preceding prayer becomes a direct imperative. The expression ‘make a refusal’ becomes the verb “deny”, and the substantival infinitive *attender* becomes “to wait”; the negative and object pronoun follow the verb.
- *la fiamma cornuta qua vegna* ‘the flame horned hither come’ → “the horned flame comes hither”: the adjective moves before its noun and the directional adverb after the verb; the Italian subjunctive becomes the ordinary English present used after “until”.
- *che* ‘that’ → “how”: the conjunction introduces the manner or degree of the action rather than simply its occurrence.
- *del disio* ‘from desire’ → “with desire”: the causal expression becomes one of accompanying feeling.
- *ver lei mi piego* ‘towards her myself I bend’ → “I bend me towards it”: the directional phrase moves after the verb and reflexive object; the feminine pronoun referring to the flame becomes neuter “it”.

> 70 Ed egli a me: “ La tua preghiera è degna  
> 71 di molta lode, ed io però l' accetto;  
> 72 ma fa che la tua lingua si sostegna.  

And he to me: “Thy request is worthy of much praise, and therefore I accept it; but do thou refrain thy tongue.

- *preghiera* ‘prayer’ → “request”: a less supplicatory noun is used.
- *ed io però l' accetto* ‘and I therefore it accept’ → “and therefore I accept it”: “therefore” moves before the subject, and the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *ma fa che la tua lingua si sostegna* ‘but make that thy tongue restrain itself’ → “but do thou refrain thy tongue”: the imperative governing a subordinate clause becomes an emphatic direct imperative. The tongue changes from the subject of a reflexive verb to the object of a transitive verb.

> 73 Lascia parlare a me: ch' io ho concetto  
> 74 ciò che tu vuoi; ch' ei sarebbero schivi,  
> 75 perch' ei fur Greci, forse del tuo detto.”  

Let me speak: for I have conceived what thou wishest; and they, perhaps, because they were Greeks, might disdain thy words.”

- *Lascia parlare a me* ‘Leave speaking to me’ → “Let me speak”: the construction with *a me* becomes an English construction with “me” before the bare infinitive.
- *ciò che* ‘that which’ → “what”: the demonstrative and relative pronoun become a single fused relative.
- *ch'* ‘for’ → “and”: the explanatory conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction without explicit causal force.
- *ei sarebbero schivi, / perch' ei fur Greci, forse del tuo detto* ‘they would be averse, because they were Greeks, perhaps to thy speech’ → “they, perhaps, because they were Greeks, might disdain thy words”: the conditional copula and adjective become a modal verb and the transitive “disdain”. “Perhaps” moves immediately after the subject, and the causal clause precedes the main predicate. The prepositional complement becomes a direct object, with singular *detto* rendered as plural “words”.

> 76 Poi che la fiamma fu venuta quivi,  
> 77 dove parve al mio duca tempo e loco,  
> 78 in questa forma lui parlare audivi:  

After the flame had come where time and place seemed fitting to my Guide, I heard him speak in this manner:

- *quivi, / dove* ‘there where’ → “where”: the separate locative *quivi* is omitted.
- *parve al mio duca tempo e loco* ‘seemed to my guide time and place’ → “time and place seemed fitting to my Guide”: the subject moves before the verb and the phrase referring to the guide moves after it; “fitting” explicitly supplies the suitability implicit in the Italian expression.
- *in questa forma lui parlare audivi* ‘in this form him speak I heard’ → “I heard him speak in this manner”: the governing verb moves before the object and infinitive, while the opening adverbial phrase moves to the end; *forma* is rendered as “manner”.

> 79 “ O voi, che siete due dentro ad un foco,  

“O ye, two in one fire!

- *O voi, che siete due dentro ad un foco* ‘O you, who are two within one fire’ → “O ye, two in one fire!”: the relative clause becomes an appositional phrase, with no equivalent for *che siete*.

> 80 s' io meritai di voi mentre ch' io vissi,  
> 81 s' io meritai di voi assai o poco,  
> 82 quando nel mondo gli alti versi scrissi,  

if I merited of you whilst I lived, if I merited of you much or little, when on earth I wrote the High Verses,

- *nel mondo* ‘in the world’ → “on earth”: both the noun and its accompanying preposition change.
- *gli alti versi scrissi* ‘the high verses I wrote’ → “I wrote the High Verses”: the object moves from before the verb to after it.

> 83 non vi movete; ma l' un di voi dica  
> 84 dove per lui perduto a morir gissi.”  

move ye not; but let the one of you tell where he, having lost himself, went to die.”

- *non vi movete* ‘do not move yourselves’ → “move ye not”: the reflexive object is omitted, and the negative follows the verb rather than preceding it.
- *ma l' un di voi dica* ‘but the one of you say’ → “but let the one of you tell”: the third-person subjunctive expressing a command becomes a construction with “let”; ‘say’ becomes “tell”.
- *per lui perduto* ‘lost through himself’ → “having lost himself”: the passive participial expression is interpreted as self-caused loss and recast as an active perfect participial clause, explicitly marking that loss as anterior.
- *a morir gissi* ‘to die he went himself’ → “went to die”: the purpose infinitive moves after the main verb, and the pronominal form *gissi* becomes the simple “went”.

## Lines 85–117 — Ulysses’ Voyage and Appeal

> 85 Lo maggior corno della fiamma antica  
> 86 cominciò a crollarsi mormorando,  
> 87 pur come quella cui vento affatica.  

The greater horn of the ancient flame began to shake itself, murmuring, just like a flame that struggles with the wind.

- *quella* ‘that one’ → “a flame”: the demonstrative pronoun becomes an indefinite noun phrase, repeating the noun understood in Italian.
- *cui vento affatica* ‘which wind wearies’ → “that struggles with the wind”: the flame becomes the subject rather than the object, and the wind becomes the complement of “struggles”.

> 88 Indi la cima qua e là menando,  
> 89 come fosse la lingua che parlasse,  

Then carrying to and fro the top, as if it were the tongue that spake,

- *la cima qua e là menando* ‘the top here and there moving’ → “carrying to and fro the top”: the participle moves before its object, and ‘here and there’ becomes “to and fro”.
- *come fosse la lingua che parlasse* ‘as though it were the tongue that should speak’ → “as if it were the tongue that spake”: the subjunctive in the relative clause becomes an indicative past tense.

> 90 gittò voce di fuori e disse : “Quando  
> 91 mi diparti’ da Circe, che sottrasse  
> 92 me più d’ un anno là presso a Gaeta,  
> 93 prima che sì Enea la nominasse,  

threw forth a voice, and said : " When I departed from Circe, who beyond a year detained me there near Gaeta, ere Æneas thus had named it,

- *gittò voce di fuori* ‘cast voice outside’ → “threw forth a voice”: the directional phrase becomes “forth”, placed before the object, and an indefinite article is supplied.
- *mi diparti’* ‘I parted myself’ → “I departed”: the Italian pronominal construction becomes a simple intransitive verb.
- *che sottrasse me più d’ un anno* ‘who took me away more than a year’ → “who beyond a year detained me”: taking away is rendered as detention; the duration moves before the verb, and ‘more than’ becomes “beyond”.
- *prima che sì Enea la nominasse* ‘before thus Æneas named it’ → “ere Æneas thus had named it”: the subject moves before the adverb, and the Italian imperfect subjunctive becomes an English past perfect indicative.

> 94 nè dolcezza di figlio, nè la pieta  
> 95 del vecchio padre, nè il debito amore  
> 96 lo qual dovea Penelope far lieta,  

neither fondness for my son, nor reverence for my aged father, nor the due love that should have cheered Penelope,

- *dolcezza di figlio* ‘sweetness of a son’ → “fondness for my son”: the noun becomes an explicit feeling directed toward the son, and “my” is supplied.
- *la pieta del vecchio padre* ‘the pity for the old father’ → “reverence for my aged father”: *pieta* is interpreted as filial reverence; the definite article is replaced by the supplied possessive “my”.
- *lo qual dovea Penelope far lieta* ‘which ought to make Penelope glad’ → “that should have cheered Penelope”: the imperfect obligation becomes “should have” with a perfect infinitive; ‘make glad’ becomes “cheered”, and Penelope moves after the verb.

> 97 vincer poter dentro da me l’ardore  
> 98 ch’i’ ebbi a divenir del mondo esperto,  
> 99 e degli vizii umani e del valore ;  

could conquer in me the ardour that I had to gain experience of the world, and of human vice and worth

- *vincer poter* ‘conquer could’ → “could conquer”: the modal verb precedes its infinitive.
- *a divenir del mondo esperto* ‘to become of the world experienced’ → “to gain experience of the world”: becoming experienced is recast as gaining a noun, “experience”, and the complement follows it.
- *e degli vizii umani e del valore* ‘and of the vices human and of worth’ → “and of human vice and worth”: plural *vizii* becomes the singular mass noun “vice”, the adjective moves before it, and the articles and repeated preposition are omitted.

> 100 ma misi me per l’ alto mare aperto  
> 101 sol con un legno e con quella compagna  
> 102 picciola, dalla qual non fui deserto.  

; I put forth on the deep open sea, with but one ship, and with that small company, which had not deserted me.

- *ma misi me per* ‘but I put myself onto’ → “I put forth on”: the adversative conjunction is omitted, the reflexive construction becomes the intransitive phrase “put forth”, and the directional preposition becomes “on”.
- *l’ alto mare aperto* ‘the high sea open’ → “the deep open sea”: *alto* is taken in its sense of depth rather than height, and *aperto* moves before the noun.
- *sol con un legno* ‘only with one wood’ → “with but one ship”: “but” follows the preposition, and the material noun *legno* is rendered by the vessel it denotes.
- *e con quella compagna picciola* ‘and with that company small’ → “and with that small company”: the adjective moves before the noun.
- *dalla qual non fui deserto* ‘by which I was not deserted’ → “which had not deserted me”: the passive becomes active, and the simple past becomes a past perfect.

> 103 L’ un lito e l’ altro vidi infin la Spagna,  
> 104 fin nel Morrocco, e l’ isola de’ Sardi,  
> 105 e l’ altre che quel mare intorno bagna.  

Both the shores I saw as far as Spain, far as Morocco ; and saw Sardinia and the other isles which that sea bathes round.

- *L’ un lito e l’ altro* ‘the one shore and the other’ → “Both the shores”: the paired singular expressions become one plural phrase.
- *fin nel Morrocco* ‘as far as into Morocco’ → “far as Morocco”: the separate preposition expressing entry into or extent within Morocco is not retained.
- *e l’ isola de’ Sardi* ‘and the island of the Sardinians’ → “and saw Sardinia”: the descriptive noun phrase becomes a geographical name, and “saw” is repeated.
- *e l’ altre* ‘and the others’ → “and the other isles”: the noun understood in Italian is supplied.
- *intorno bagna* ‘round bathes’ → “bathes round”: the adverb moves after the verb.

> 106 Io e i compagni eravam vecchi e tardi,  
> 107 quando venimmo a quella foce stretta,  
> 108 ov’ Ercole segnò li suoi riguardi,  
> 109 acciocchè l’uom più oltre non si metta ;  

I and my companions were old and tardy, when we came to that narrow pass, where Hercules assigned his landmarks to hinder man from venturing farther

- *i compagni* ‘the companions’ → “my companions”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *a quella foce stretta* ‘to that mouth narrow’ → “to that narrow pass”: *foce* becomes “pass”, and the adjective moves before the noun.
- *segnò li suoi riguardi* ‘marked his bounds’ → “assigned his landmarks”: marking becomes assigning, and *riguardi* is interpreted as physical landmarks rather than bounds or warning limits.
- *acciocchè l’uom più oltre non si metta* ‘so that man may not put himself farther beyond’ → “to hinder man from venturing farther”: the purpose clause becomes an infinitive phrase; its subject becomes the object of “hinder”, its negation is absorbed into that verb, and the pronominal verb becomes “venturing”.

> 110 dalla man destra mi lasciai Sibilia,  
> 111 dall’ altra già m’ avea lasciata Setta.  

; on the right hand, I left Seville ; on the other, had already left Ceuta.

- *mi lasciai* ‘I left for myself’ → “I left”: the reflexive pronoun has no separate English expression.
- *già m’ avea lasciata* ‘already I had left for myself’ → “had already left”: the reflexive pronoun is again unexpressed, and the adverb moves between auxiliary and participle.

> 112 ‘O frati,’ dissi, ‘che per cento milia  
> 113 perigli siete giunti all’ occidente,  

‘O brothers !’ I said, ‘who through a hundred thousand dangers have reached the West,

- *siete giunti all’ occidente* ‘you have arrived at the west’ → “have reached the West”: ‘arrive at’ becomes the transitive verb “reached”, with the destination as its direct object.

> 114 a questa tanto picciola vigilia  
> 115 de’ vostri sensi, ch’è del rimanente,  
> 116 non vogliate negar l’ esperienza,  
> 117 di retro al sol, del mondo senza gente.  

deny not, to this the brief vigil of your senses that remains, experience of the unpeopled world behind the Sun.

- *a questa tanto picciola vigilia* ‘to this so small vigil’ → “to this the brief vigil”: *tanto* is omitted, smallness becomes explicitly temporal brevity, and “the” is supplied after “this”.
- *ch’è del rimanente* ‘which is of the remainder’ → “that remains”: the construction with ‘be’ and a nominal expression becomes a single verb.
- *non vogliate negar* ‘do not wish to deny’ → “deny not”: the verb of willing is omitted, leaving a direct imperative; the phrase moves before the preceding indirect object.
- *l’ esperienza* ‘the experience’ → “experience”: the definite article is omitted.
- *di retro al sol, del mondo senza gente* ‘behind the sun, of the world without people’ → “of the unpeopled world behind the Sun”: the phrase ‘behind the sun’ moves after the description of the world, and ‘without people’ becomes an adjective placed before the noun.

## Lines 118–142 — The Voyage and Shipwreck

> 118 Considerate la vostra semenza:  
> 119 fatti non foste a viver come bruti,  
> 120 ma per seguir virtute e conoscenza.'  

Consider your origin : ye were not formed to live like brutes, but to follow virtue and knowledge.'

- *semenza* ‘seed’ → “origin”: the concrete noun is rendered by its figurative meaning.
- *fatti non foste* ‘made not were you’ → “ye were not formed”: the participle moves after the auxiliary and negative, and ‘made’ becomes “formed”.

> 121 Li miei compagni fec' io sì acuti,  
> 122 con questa orazion picciola, al cammino,  
> 123 che appena poscia gli avrei ritenuti;  

With this brief speech I made my companions so eager for the voyage, that I could hardly then have checked them;

- *Li miei compagni fec' io* ‘My companions made I’ → “I made my companions”: object–verb–subject order becomes subject–verb–object order.
- *con questa orazion picciola* ‘with this speech little’ → “With this brief speech”: the phrase moves to the beginning of the sentence; the adjective precedes its noun, and ‘little’ is interpreted as brevity.
- *sì acuti … al cammino* ‘so keen … for the journey’ → “so eager for the voyage”: the metaphorical ‘keen’ becomes “eager”, and the general ‘journey’ becomes the specifically nautical “voyage”.
- *che appena poscia gli avrei ritenuti* ‘that scarcely afterwards them I would have restrained’ → “that I could hardly then have checked them”: the conditional is rendered with “could”, expressing ability; ‘afterwards’ becomes “then”, ‘restrained’ becomes “checked”, and the object pronoun moves after the verb.

> 124 e, volta nostra poppa nel mattino,  
> 125 de' remi facemmo ale al folle volo,  
> 126 sempre acquistando dal lato mancino.  

and, turning the poop towards morning, we of our oars made wings for the foolish flight, always gaining on the left.

- *volta nostra poppa* ‘our stern having been turned’ → “turning the poop”: the absolute participial construction becomes an active participial phrase referring to the voyagers; the possessive becomes a definite article.
- *nel mattino* ‘into the morning’ → “towards morning”: the preposition is rendered as direction towards rather than movement into.
- *de' remi* ‘of the oars’ → “of our oars”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *dal lato mancino* ‘from the left-hand side’ → “on the left”: “on” replaces ‘from’, and the noun ‘side’ is omitted, leaving “the left” as a noun phrase.

> 127 Tutte le stelle già dell' altro polo  
> 128 vedea la notte, e il nostro tanto basso,  
> 129 che non surgeva fuor del marin suolo.  

Night already saw the other pole, with all its stars; and ours so low, that it rose not from the ocean floor.

- *Tutte le stelle già dell' altro polo vedea la notte* ‘All the stars already of the other pole I saw at night’ → “Night already saw the other pole, with all its stars”: the translation takes *la notte* as the subject rather than as a temporal expression, and *vedea* as third-person rather than first-person singular. The pole replaces its stars as the direct object; the stars become an accompanying phrase introduced by the supplied “with”, and “its” is also supplied. The subject moves to the beginning.
- *non surgeva* ‘not rose’ → “rose not”: the negative follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *fuor del marin suolo* ‘out of the marine floor’ → “from the ocean floor”: ‘out of’ becomes “from”, and the adjective ‘marine’ becomes the attributive noun “ocean”.

> 130 Cinque volte racceso, e tante casso  
> 131 lo lume era di sotto dalla luna,  
> 132 poi ch' entrati eravam nell' alto passo,  

Five times the light beneath the Moon had been rekindled and quenched as oft, since we had entered on the arduous passage,

- *Cinque volte racceso, e tante casso lo lume era di sotto dalla luna* ‘Five times rekindled, and as many times effaced, the light was beneath the Moon’ → “Five times the light beneath the Moon had been rekindled and quenched as oft”: the subject moves before the participles, and the phrase locating the light follows the subject directly. *Era* is rendered with the pluperfect “had been” rather than ‘was’; *casso*, literally ‘effaced’, becomes “quenched”. ‘As many times’ becomes the adverbial “as oft” and moves after the second participle.
- *poi ch'* ‘after that’ → “since”: the compound temporal expression becomes a single conjunction.
- *entrati eravam nell' alto passo* ‘entered we were into the high passage’ → “we had entered on the arduous passage”: the Italian pluperfect, formed with ‘be’, becomes an English pluperfect with ‘have’, and the participle follows its auxiliary. ‘Into’ becomes “on” in the construction “entered on”; *alto* is interpreted as “arduous” rather than literally ‘high’.

> 133 quando n' apparve una montagna bruna  
> 134 per la distanza, e parvemi alta tanto,  
> 135 quanto veduta non n' aveva alcuna.  

when there appeared to us a Mountain, dim with distance ; and to me it seemed the highest I had ever seen.

- *n' apparve* ‘to us appeared’ → “there appeared to us”: introductory “there” is supplied, and the pronoun preceding the Italian verb becomes a prepositional phrase following it.
- *bruna per la distanza* ‘dark because of the distance’ → “dim with distance”: ‘dark’ becomes “dim”; the causal phrase is expressed with “with”, and the definite article before ‘distance’ is omitted.
- *e parvemi alta tanto, quanto veduta non n' aveva alcuna* ‘and it seemed to me so high as I had not seen any’ → “and to me it seemed the highest I had ever seen”: the comparison of degree becomes a superlative construction. The negative statement about not having seen any equally high becomes an affirmative statement with the supplied “ever”; the partitive *n'* and *alcuna* have no separate equivalents. The suffixed indirect object in *parvemi* becomes “to me” before the verb, and the earlier-seen participle *veduta* moves after its auxiliary.

> 136 Noi ci allegrammo, e tosto tornò in pianto:  

We joyed, and soon our joy was turned to grief:

- *Noi ci allegrammo* ‘We gladdened ourselves’ → “We joyed”: the Italian reflexive construction becomes an English intransitive verb.
- *e tosto tornò in pianto* ‘and soon it turned into weeping’ → “and soon our joy was turned to grief”: the unexpressed subject is explicitly identified as “our joy”; the intransitive ‘turned’ becomes passive “was turned”. ‘Weeping’ is replaced by the emotion “grief”, and ‘into’ becomes “to”.

> 137 chè dalla nuova terra un turbo nacque,  
> 138 e percosse del legno il primo canto.  

for a tempest rose from the new land, and struck the forepart of our ship.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *dalla nuova terra un turbo nacque* ‘from the new land a whirlwind was born’ → “a tempest rose from the new land”: the source phrase moves after the verb; the specific ‘whirlwind’ becomes the broader “tempest”, and ‘was born’ becomes “rose”.
- *e percosse del legno il primo canto* ‘and struck of the wood the first corner’ → “and struck the forepart of our ship”: the possessive phrase moves after the noun it qualifies. *Legno*, literally ‘wood’, is rendered by the vessel it denotes, with “our” supplied; ‘the first corner’ becomes “the forepart”.

> 139 Tre volte il fe' girar con tutte l' acque,  

Three times it made her whirl round with all the waters

- *il fe' girar* ‘made it turn’ → “it made her whirl round”: the object pronoun moves from before the causative verb to after it. Italian masculine *il*, referring to the vessel, becomes the English nautical feminine “her”; ‘turn’ is expanded to the more forceful “whirl round”.

> 140 alla quarta levar la poppa in suso,  
> 141 e la prora ire in giù, com' altrui piacque,  

; at the fourth, *made* the poop rise up and prow go down, as pleased *Another*,

- *levar la poppa in suso* ‘rise the stern upwards’ → “*made* the poop rise up”: the causative verb understood from the preceding line is repeated explicitly as “*made*”, and the subject of the infinitive moves before “rise”.
- *e la prora ire in giù* ‘and the prow go down’ → “and prow go down”: the definite article before ‘prow’ is omitted.

> 142 infin che il mar fu sopra noi richiuso."  

till the sea was closed above us."

- *fu sopra noi richiuso* ‘was above us closed again’ → “was closed above us”: the prepositional phrase moves after the participle, and the sense ‘again’ in *richiuso* is not separately expressed.

## Summary — Close Echoes and Prose Reshaping

Carlyle’s translation stays close to much of the Italian vocabulary and imagery, but frequently rearranges its syntax into English prose order. Subjects and objects move from before or after their verbs into more customary English positions, adjectives generally precede their nouns, and possessive phrases follow the words they qualify. Longer constructions are also regrouped: *Quante … lucciole* becomes “As many fireflies as”, bringing the noun forward across several intervening clauses. Yet the translation retains conspicuously Italian turns, including “to great honour mountest not thereby” and “the foot without the hand sped not”, rather than consistently smoothing them into ordinary English.

Changes of construction recur alongside these rearrangements. Italian pronominal verbs often become plain English intransitives, while passive or impersonal expressions become active clauses with explicit agents: *si geme / l’aguato del caval* becomes “they groan for the ambush of the horse”. Conversely, *si vengiò con gli orsi* becomes the passive “was avenged by the bears”. Finite subordinate clauses sometimes become infinitive phrases, as *acciocchè l’uom più oltre non si metta* becomes “to hinder man from venturing farther”. Elsewhere, a comparison becomes a superlative—“the highest I had ever seen”—or an adjective becomes a phrase, as *morta* becomes “in death”.

Word choice ranges from close verbal echoes to interpretation and expansion. “steals a sinner” preserves the striking action of *un peccatore invola*, and “we of our oars made wings” closely follows the Italian image and arrangement. By contrast, *mente* becomes “memory”, *semenza* becomes “origin”, and *alto* in *alto passo* becomes “arduous”. Such choices specify a faculty, replace a concrete expression with its figurative meaning, or select a contextual sense. A more substantial interpretive departure occurs when *la notte* becomes the subject of “Night already saw”, rather than being understood as ‘at night’.

Small omissions and additions help accommodate the Italian to English. Repeated prepositions, emphatic elements, and some pronouns disappear; understood nouns, connections, and actions are supplied. Thus *ciascuna* becomes “each of *those flames*”, *lo* becomes “that gift”, and *tornò in pianto* receives the explicit subject “our joy”. Alongside the individually noted changes, English regularly supplies subject pronouns where Italian verb endings alone identify the person, and drops the definite article before possessives. These routine adjustments coexist with less necessary shifts from articles to possessives, such as *la solinga via* becoming “our solitary way”. Overall, the translation combines close verbal correspondence and deliberately archaic phrasing with recurrent grammatical reshaping and occasional interpretive departures.
