# Inferno 6

## Lines 1–21 — The Torments of the Third Circle

> 1 Al tornar della mente, che si chiuse  
> 2 dinanzi alla pietà de’ duo cognati,  
> 3 che di tristizia tutto mi confuse,  

On sense returning, which closed itself before the misery of the two kinsfolk that stunned me all with sadness,

- *Al tornar della mente* ‘at the return of the mind’ → “On sense returning”: the nominal infinitive becomes a participial construction, with “sense” as the subject of “returning”; *mente* is interpreted as consciousness rather than mind.
- *alla pietà* ‘the pity’ → “the misery”: the translation takes *pietà* objectively, as the pitiable suffering rather than the feeling of pity.
- *de’ duo cognati* ‘of the two relatives by marriage’ → “of the two kinsfolk”: “kinsfolk” does not specify the relationship by marriage expressed by *cognati*.
- *che di tristizia tutto mi confuse* ‘that with sadness wholly confounded me’ → “that stunned me all with sadness”: “stunned” replaces ‘confounded’; the phrase expressing the cause moves after the verb and object, and *tutto* is rendered by the postposed “all”.

> 4 nuovi tormenti e nuovi tormentati  
> 5 mi veggio intorno, come ch’ io mi muova,  
> 6 e ch’ io mi volga, e come ch’ io mi guati.  

I discern new torments, and new tormented souls, whithersoever I move, and turn, and gaze.

- *nuovi tormenti e nuovi tormentati* ‘new torments and new tormented ones’ → “new torments, and new tormented souls”: the objects move from before the verb to after it; “souls” supplies a noun for the substantival participle *tormentati*.
- *mi veggio intorno* ‘I see around me’ → “I discern”: “discern” replaces ‘see’, and the surrounding location expressed by *mi … intorno* is omitted.
- *come ch’ io mi muova, e ch’ io mi volga, e come ch’ io mi guati* ‘however I move myself, and however I turn myself, and however I look about myself’ → “whithersoever I move, and turn, and gaze”: the repeated concessive constructions are consolidated into one directional “whithersoever”; the reflexive verbs become ordinary English intransitives, the repeated explicit subjects disappear, and the Italian subjunctives have no distinct subjunctive form in English.

> 7 Io sono al terzo cerchio della piova  
> 8 eterna, maledetta, fredda e greve;  
> 9 regola e qualità mai non l’è nova.  

I am in the Third Circle, that of the eternal, accursed, cold, and heavy rain; its [law] and quality is never new.

- *Io sono al terzo cerchio* ‘I am at the third circle’ → “I am in the Third Circle”: the preposition of location changes from ‘at’ to “in”.
- *della piova eterna, maledetta, fredda e greve* ‘of the rain eternal, accursed, cold and heavy’ → “that of the eternal, accursed, cold, and heavy rain”: “that” is supplied to introduce an explanatory apposition, and the adjectives move before their noun.
- *regola e qualità mai non l’è nova* ‘rule and quality never is new to it’ → “its [law] and quality is never new”: the dative *l’* becomes the possessive “its”; *regola* is rendered as “[law]”; “never” moves after the verb, and the Italian negative combination *mai non* becomes a single English negative.

> 10 Grandine grossa, e acqua tinta, e neve  
> 11 per l’ aer tenebroso si riversa;  

Large hail, and turbid water, and snow, [pour down] through the darksome air;

- *Grandine grossa* ‘hail large’ → “Large hail”: the adjective moves before the noun.
- *e acqua tinta* ‘and water tinted’ → “and turbid water”: “turbid” interprets the water’s discoloration as murkiness, and the adjective moves before the noun.
- *per l’ aer tenebroso si riversa* ‘through the air dark pours itself out’ → “[pour down] through the darksome air”: the verb moves before the prepositional phrase, and the adjective before its noun; the pronominal verb becomes an English intransitive, singular agreement becomes plural agreement with the coordinated subjects, and “down” specifies the direction.

> 12 pute la terra che questo riceve.  

the ground, on which it falls, emits a putrid smell.

- *pute la terra* ‘stinks the earth’ → “the ground” … “emits a putrid smell”: the subject moves before the verb; ‘earth’ becomes “ground”, and the single verb is expanded into a verb and noun phrase.
- *che questo riceve* ‘which receives this’ → “on which it falls”: the relative clause changes its grammatical viewpoint. The ground is no longer the subject receiving something, but the location on which “it” falls; the demonstrative *questo* becomes a personal pronoun.

> 13 Cerbero, fiera crudele e diversa,  
> 14 con tre gole caninamente latra  
> 15 sovra la gente che quivi è sommersa.  

Cerberus, a monster fierce and strange, with three throats, barks dog-like over those that are immersed in it.

- *fiera crudele e diversa* ‘beast cruel and different’ → “a monster fierce and strange”: an indefinite article is supplied; ‘beast’ becomes “monster”, cruelty becomes fierceness, and *diversa* is interpreted as “strange”.
- *caninamente latra* ‘dog-wise barks’ → “barks dog-like”: the adverbial expression moves after the verb.
- *sovra la gente* ‘over the people’ → “over those”: the collective noun becomes a plural demonstrative pronoun.
- *che quivi è sommersa* ‘that there is submerged’ → “that are immersed in it”: singular agreement with *gente* becomes plural agreement with “those”; *quivi*, ‘there’, becomes “in it”, and ‘submerged’ is rendered as “immersed”.

> 16 Gli occhi ha vermigli, e la barba unta ed atra,  
> 17 e il ventre largo, e unghiate le mani;  

His eyes are red, his beard [greasy] and black, his belly wide, and clawed his hands;

- *Gli occhi ha vermigli* ‘the eyes he has vermilion’ → “His eyes are red”: possession expressed by ‘has’ becomes a possessive determiner with “are”; the object becomes the subject, and the more specific colour term becomes the general “red”.
- *e la barba unta ed atra* ‘and the beard greasy and black’ → “his beard [greasy] and black”: the initial conjunction is omitted, the definite article becomes a possessive determiner, and the object of the continuing Italian ‘has’ becomes the subject of an understood English ‘is’.
- *e il ventre largo* ‘and the belly wide’ → “his belly wide”: the conjunction is omitted, the article becomes a possessive determiner, and the object becomes the subject of an understood ‘is’.
- *e unghiate le mani* ‘and clawed the hands’ → “and clawed his hands”: the article becomes a possessive determiner, and the object becomes the subject of an understood ‘are’; the preceding position of “clawed” is retained.

> 18 graffia gli spirti, gli scuoia, ed isquatra.  

he clutches the spirits, flays, and piecemeal rends them.

- *graffia gli spirti* ‘scratches the spirits’ → “he clutches the spirits”: scratching is replaced by clutching.
- *gli scuoia, ed isquatra* ‘them he flays and quarters’ → “flays, and piecemeal rends them”: the object pronoun moves from before the verbs to after both; the specific action of quartering becomes the more general “piecemeal rends”.

> 19 Urlar gli fa la pioggia come cani;  

The rain makes them howl like dogs;

- *Urlar gli fa la pioggia* ‘to howl them makes the rain’ → “The rain makes them howl”: the postposed subject moves to the beginning, while the infinitive moves from the beginning to follow the causative verb and its object.

> 20 dell’ un de’ lati fanno all’ altro schermo;  

with one side they screen the other;

- *dell’ un de’ lati* ‘of the one of the sides’ → “with one side”: ‘of’ becomes instrumental “with”, and the partitive construction is compressed into “one side”.
- *fanno all’ altro schermo* ‘they make a screen for the other’ → “they screen the other”: the verb-and-noun construction becomes the single verb “screen”, and the indirect object becomes a direct object.

> 21 volgonsi spesso i miseri profani.  

they often turn themselves, the impious wretches.

- *volgonsi spesso i miseri profani* ‘turn themselves often the wretched profane ones’ → “they often turn themselves, the impious wretches”: “often” moves before the verb; the postposed Italian subject becomes an apposition to the supplied “they”. The adjective *miseri* becomes the noun “wretches”, while the substantival *profani* becomes the adjective “impious”.

## Lines 22–54 — Cerberus Quieted and Ciacco Encountered

> 22 Quando ci scorse Cerbero, il gran vermo,  
> 23 le bocche aperse, e mostrocci le sanne;  
> 24 non avea membro che tenesse fermo.  

When Cerberus, the great Worm, perceived us, he opened his mouths and shewed his tusks: no limb of him kept still.

- *ci scorse Cerbero, il gran vermo* ‘us perceived Cerberus, the great Worm’ → “Cerberus, the great Worm, perceived us”: the subject and its apposition move before the verb, and the object follows it.
- *le bocche aperse* ‘the mouths he opened’ → “he opened his mouths”: the object moves after the verb, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *mostrocci le sanne* ‘shewed us the tusks’ → “shewed his tusks”: the indirect object ‘us’ is omitted, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *non avea membro che tenesse fermo* ‘he had no limb that he held still’ → “no limb of him kept still”: the possessive construction and relative clause become a single clause with “no limb” as subject; ‘held still’ becomes the intransitive “kept still”.

> 25 E il duca mio distese le sue spanne,  
> 26 prese la terra, e con piene le pugna  
> 27 la gittò dentro alle bramose canne.  

My Guide, spreading his palms, took up earth; and, with full fists, cast it into his ravening gullets.

- *E il duca mio* ‘And the guide my’ → “My Guide”: the opening conjunction is omitted, and the possessive moves before the noun.
- *distese le sue spanne* ‘spread his spans’ → “spreading his palms”: the finite verb becomes a participle subordinate to “took”; the hand-spans are rendered as palms.
- *prese la terra* ‘took the earth’ → “took up earth”: “up” is supplied, and the definite article is omitted.
- *con piene le pugna* ‘with full the fists’ → “with full fists”: the definite article between adjective and noun is omitted.
- *la gittò* ‘it cast’ → “cast it”: the object pronoun moves after the verb.
- *dentro alle bramose canne* ‘inside the ravenous pipes’ → “into his ravening gullets”: the prepositional expression becomes “into”, the article becomes a possessive, and the figurative ‘pipes’ are explicitly identified as “gullets”; ‘ravenous’ becomes the participial “ravening”.

> 28 Qual è quel cane che abbaiando agugna,  
> 29 e si racqueta poi che il pasto morde,  
> 30 chè solo a divorarlo intende e pugna:  

As the dog, that barking craves, and grows quiet when he bites his food, for he strains and battles only to devour it:

- *Qual è quel cane* ‘Such as is that dog’ → “As the dog”: the comparative opening is compressed, the verb is omitted, and the demonstrative becomes a definite article.
- *si racqueta* ‘quiets himself’ → “grows quiet”: the reflexive verb becomes an intransitive construction with an adjective.
- *poi che* ‘after’ → “when”: the explicit indication of succession becomes a more general temporal conjunction.
- *il pasto morde* ‘the meal he bites’ → “he bites his food”: the object moves after the verb, the definite article becomes a possessive, and ‘meal’ becomes “food”.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *solo a divorarlo intende e pugna* ‘only to devour it he applies himself and fights’ → “he strains and battles only to devour it”: the infinitive phrase moves after the verbs, and application or concentration is rendered as physical effort in “strains”.

> 31 cotai se fecer quelle facce lorde  
> 32 dello demonio Cerbero che introna  
> 33 l'anime sì, ch'esser vorrebber sorde.  

so did those squalid visages of Cerberus the Demon, who thunders on the spirits so, that they would fain be deaf.

- *cotai se fecer* ‘such they made themselves’ → “so did”: the reflexive expression of becoming is replaced by an auxiliary referring back to the dog's behaviour.
- *quelle facce lorde* ‘those visages squalid’ → “those squalid visages”: the adjective moves before the noun.
- *dello demonio Cerbero* ‘of the demon Cerberus’ → “of Cerberus the Demon”: the name and its descriptive title exchange positions.
- *introna l'anime sì* ‘deafens the souls so’ → “thunders on the spirits so”: a transitive verb describing the effect of noise becomes an intransitive verb with “on”, describing the noise directed at its objects.
- *esser vorrebber sorde* ‘to be they would wish deaf’ → “they would fain be deaf”: the conditional verb of wishing becomes the modal “would” with the adverb “fain”, and the infinitive follows it.

> 34 Noi passavam su per l'ombre, che adona  
> 35 la greve pioggia, e ponevam le piante  
> 36 sopra lor vanità, che par persona.  

We passed over the shadows whom the heavy rain subdues; and placed our soles upon their emptiness, which seems a body.

- *passavam su per* ‘were passing up over’ → “passed over”: the imperfect is rendered by a simple past, leaving its ongoing aspect implicit, and the compound prepositional expression is reduced to “over”.
- *che adona la greve pioggia* ‘whom subdues the heavy rain’ → “whom the heavy rain subdues”: the subject moves before the verb.
- *ponevam le piante* ‘were placing the soles’ → “placed our soles”: the imperfect becomes a simple past, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *par persona* ‘seems person’ → “seems a body”: an indefinite article is supplied, and “body” selects the bodily-form sense of *persona*, rather than ‘person’.

> 37 Elle giacean per terra tutte quante,  
> 38 fuor d'una che a seder si levò, ratto  
> 39 ch'ella ci vide passarsi davante.  

They all were lying on the ground save one, who sat up forthwith when he saw us pass before him.

- *Elle … tutte quante* ‘They … all of them’ → “They all”: the comprehensive expression at the end of the clause is compressed and moved beside the subject.
- *per terra* ‘on ground’ → “on the ground”: English supplies a definite article.
- *fuor d'una* ‘outside of one’ → “save one”: the Italian expression is rendered by an explicitly exceptive preposition.
- *a seder si levò* ‘to sit raised itself’ → “sat up”: the reflexive verb and infinitive of purpose are compressed into a phrasal verb.
- *ratto … ch'* ‘as soon as’ → “forthwith when”: the expression of immediate succession is divided into an adverb modifying the action and a temporal conjunction.
- *ella ci vide passarsi davante* ‘she us saw pass before herself’ → “he saw us pass before him”: the feminine pronoun referring to the shade becomes masculine; “us” moves after “saw”, and the reflexive clitic attached to the infinitive becomes the non-reflexive prepositional complement “before him”.

> 40 “O tu, che se' per questo inferno tratto,”  
> 41 mi disse, “riconoscimi, se sai;  
> 42 tu fosti, prima ch'io disfatto, fatto.”  

“O thou, who through this Hell art led,” he said to me, “recognise me if thou mayest; thou wast made before I was unmade.”

- *se' per questo inferno tratto* ‘art through this hell drawn’ → “through this Hell art led”: the prepositional phrase moves before the auxiliary, and ‘drawn’ is interpreted as “led”.
- *mi disse* ‘to me he said’ → “he said to me”: the indirect object moves after the verb.
- *se sai* ‘if thou knowest how’ → “if thou mayest”: knowing how is rendered by a modal expressing ability.
- *tu fosti, prima ch'io disfatto, fatto* ‘thou wast, before I unmade, made’ → “thou wast made before I was unmade”: the main verb and participle are brought together, and “was” is supplied in the subordinate clause, where Italian leaves it understood.

> 43 Ed io a lei: “L'angoscia che tu hai  
> 44 forse ti tira fuor della mia mente,  
> 45 sì che non par ch'io ti vedessi mai.  

And I to him: “The anguish which thou hast, perhaps withdraws thee from my memory, so that it seems not as if I ever saw thee.

- *lei* ‘her’ → “him”: grammatical agreement with the feminine shade is replaced by masculine reference to the speaker.
- *ti tira fuor della mia mente* ‘thee pulls out of my mind’ → “withdraws thee from my memory”: the object moves after the verb, ‘pulls out of’ becomes “withdraws … from”, and ‘mind’ is narrowed to “memory”.
- *non par che* ‘not seems that’ → “it seems not as if”: the negative follows the verb, and the content clause introduced by ‘that’ becomes an “as if” construction.
- *io ti vedessi mai* ‘I thee saw ever’ → “I ever saw thee”: the object moves after the verb and the adverb before it; the Italian imperfect subjunctive is represented by an English simple-past form.

> 46 Ma dimmi chi tu se', che in sì dolente  
> 47 luogo se' messa, ed a sì fatta pena,  
> 48 che s'altra è maggio, nulla è sì spiacente.”  

But tell me who art thou, that art put in such a doleful place, and in such punishment; that, though other may be greater, none is so displeasing.”

- *chi tu se'* ‘who thou art’ → “who art thou”: English uses direct-question inversion within the dependent question.
- *in sì dolente luogo se' messa* ‘in so doleful a place art put’ → “art put in such a doleful place”: the passive verb moves before the prepositional phrase, and ‘so doleful’ becomes “such a doleful”, with an indefinite article supplied.
- *ed a sì fatta pena* ‘and to a punishment so fashioned’ → “and in such punishment”: “in” replaces ‘to’, and the descriptive expression *sì fatta* is compressed into “such”.
- *che s'altra è maggio* ‘that if another is greater’ → “that, though other may be greater”: the conditional conjunction is interpreted concessively as “though”; the indicative ‘is’ becomes the modal “may be”, and ‘another’ becomes “other”.

> 49 Ed egli a me: “La tua città, ch'è piena  
> 50 d'invidia sì, che già trabocca il sacco,  
> 51 seco mi tenne in la vita serena.  

And he to me: “Thy city, which is so full of envy that the sack already overflows, contained me in the clear life.

- *ch'è piena d'invidia sì* ‘which is full of envy so’ → “which is so full of envy”: the degree adverb moves before the adjective.
- *già trabocca il sacco* ‘already overflows the sack’ → “the sack already overflows”: the subject moves before the adverb and verb.
- *seco mi tenne* ‘with itself me held’ → “contained me”: ‘held with itself’ is compressed into “contained”, and the object pronoun moves after the verb.

> 52 Voi, cittadini, mi chiamaste Ciacco:  
> 53 per la dannosa colpa della gola,  
> 54 come tu vedi, alla pioggia mi fiacco;  

You, citizens, called me Ciacco: for the baneful crime of gluttony, as thou seest, I languish in the rain;

- *mi chiamaste Ciacco* ‘me called Ciacco’ → “called me Ciacco”: the object pronoun moves after the verb.
- *colpa della gola* ‘fault of the throat’ → “crime of gluttony”: ‘fault’ becomes the stronger “crime”, and the bodily term is rendered explicitly as the vice it denotes.
- *alla pioggia mi fiacco* ‘at the rain I weaken myself’ → “I languish in the rain”: the prepositional phrase moves after the verb and takes “in”; the reflexive expression of weakening becomes the intransitive “languish”.

## Lines 55–87 — Ciacco Foretells Discord and Names the Damned

> 55 ed io anima trista non son sola,  
> 56 chè tutte queste a simil pena stanno  
> 57 per simil colpa”; e più non fe’ parola.  

and I, wretched spirit, am not alone, since all these for like crime are in like punishment”; and more he said not.

- *anima trista* ‘soul sad’ → “wretched spirit”: the adjective precedes the noun; ‘sad’ becomes “wretched”, and ‘soul’ becomes “spirit”.
- *non son sola* ‘not am alone’ → “am not alone”: the negative follows the verb.
- *a simil pena stanno per simil colpa* ‘in like punishment stand for like fault’ → “for like crime are in like punishment”: the phrase giving the cause moves before the verb; ‘stand’ becomes “are”, and ‘fault’ becomes “crime”.
- *più non fe’ parola* ‘further he made no word’ → “more he said not”: the noun-and-verb expression becomes the single verb “said”, with the negative following it.

> 58 Io gli risposi: “Ciacco, il tuo affanno  
> 59 mi pesa sì, ch’ a lagrimar m’ invita;  

I answered him : “ Ciacco, thy sore distress weighs upon me so, that it bids me weep;

- *Io gli risposi* ‘I to him answered’ → “I answered him”: the indirect pronoun becomes a direct object and follows the verb.
- *il tuo affanno* ‘thy distress’ → “thy sore distress”: “sore” is supplied.
- *mi pesa sì* ‘upon me weighs so’ → “weighs upon me so”: the phrase representing the pronoun follows the verb.
- *ch’ a lagrimar m’ invita* ‘that to weep it invites me’ → “that it bids me weep”: the invitation becomes a bidding; the infinitive moves after the object and loses its introductory preposition.

> 60 ma dimmi, se tu sai, a che verranno  
> 61 li cittadin della città partita?  

but tell me, if thou canst, what the citizens of the divided city shall come to?

- *se tu sai* ‘if thou knowest’ → “if thou canst”: knowledge becomes ability.
- *a che verranno / li cittadin della città partita* ‘to what shall come the citizens of the city divided’ → “what the citizens of the divided city shall come to?”: the subject moves before the verb, the preposition moves to the end, and the adjective precedes “city”.

> 62 s’ alcun v’ è giusto; e dimmi la cagione,  
> 63 perchè l’ ha tanta discordia assalita.”  

if any one in it be just; and tell me the reason why such discord has assailed it.”

- *s’ alcun v’ è giusto* ‘if anyone there is just’ → “if any one in it be just;”: the locative pronoun is expanded into “in it”, and the indicative ‘is’ becomes the subjunctive “be”.
- *l’ ha tanta discordia assalita* ‘it has so much discord assailed’ → “such discord has assailed it.”: the subject moves before the auxiliary and the object after the participle; the quantitative ‘so much’ becomes “such”.

> 64 Ed egli a me: “Dopo lunga tenzone  
> 65 verranno al sangue, e la parte selvaggia  
> 66 caccerà l’ altra con molta offensione.  

And he to me : “ After long contention, they shall come to blood, and the party of the woods shall expel the other with much offence.

- *al sangue* ‘to the blood’ → “to blood”: the definite article is omitted.
- *la parte selvaggia* ‘the party wild’ → “the party of the woods”: the adjective becomes a prepositional phrase, taking *selvaggia* in its woodland sense rather than simply as ‘wild’.

> 67 Poi appresso convien che questa caggia  
> 68 infra tre soli, e che l’ altra sormonti  
> 69 con la forza di tal, che testè piaggia.  

Then it behoves this to fall within three suns, and the other to prevail through the force of one who now keeps tacking.

- *Poi appresso* ‘Then afterwards’ → “Then”: the two expressions of succession are reduced to one.
- *convien che questa caggia* ‘it is necessary that this fall’ → “it behoves this to fall”: the finite subjunctive clause becomes an object-and-infinitive construction.
- *e che l’ altra sormonti* ‘and that the other rise above’ → “and the other to prevail”: the second subjunctive clause likewise becomes an infinitive construction; the spatial expression ‘rise above’ becomes “prevail”.
- *con la forza di tal* ‘with the force of such a one’ → “through the force of one”: “through” expresses the means, and *tal* loses its demonstrative qualification.
- *piaggia* ‘hugs the shore’ → “keeps tacking”: the translation follows a nautical interpretation but substitutes a different nautical action; “keeps” also makes continued action explicit.

> 70 Alto terrà lungo tempo le fronti,  
> 71 tenendo l’ altra sotto gravi pesi,  
> 72 come che di ciò pianga, e che ne adonti.  

It shall carry its front high for a long time, keeping the other under heavy burdens, however it may weep thereat and be ashamed.

- *Alto terrà lungo tempo le fronti* ‘High it shall hold a long time the foreheads’ → “It shall carry its front high for a long time,”: ‘hold’ becomes “carry”; the plural ‘foreheads’ becomes singular “front”, and the definite article becomes possessive “its”. The adjective moves after the object, and the expression of duration moves to the end, with “for a” supplied.
- *come che di ciò pianga* ‘however at this it weep’ → “however it may weep thereat”: the phrase ‘at this’ becomes the adverb “thereat” and follows the verb; “may” explicitly renders the subjunctive.
- *e che ne adonti* ‘and that at it it take offence’ → “and be ashamed.”: the repeated conjunction is omitted, and *ne* receives no separate rendering. The verb is taken in the sense of feeling shame rather than taking offence.

> 73 Giusti son duo, ma non vi sono intesi;  

Two are just; but are not listened to there;

- *Giusti son duo* ‘Just are two’ → “Two are just”: the subject and predicate exchange positions.
- *ma non vi sono intesi* ‘but are not there understood’ → “but are not listened to there;”: ‘understood’ becomes “listened to”, and the locative moves to the end.

> 74 superbia, invidia ed avarizia sono  
> 75 le tre faville ch’ hanno i cuori accesi.”  

Pride, Envy, and Avarice are the three sparks which have set the hearts of all on fire.”

- *ch’ hanno i cuori accesi* ‘which have the hearts kindled’ → “which have set the hearts of all on fire.”: the participle follows its object in Italian, whereas English places “set” before the object. ‘Kindled’ is expanded into the expression using “set” and “on fire”, and “of all” is supplied.

> 76 Qui pose fine al lacrimabil suono.  

Here he ended the lamentable sound.

- *pose fine al lacrimabil suono* ‘he put an end to the lamentable sound’ → “he ended the lamentable sound.”: the verb-and-noun expression becomes the single verb “ended”, and the prepositional complement becomes its direct object.

> 77 Ed io a lui: “Ancor vo’ che m’ insegni,  
> 78 e che di più parlar mi facci dono.  

And I to him : “ Still I wish thee to instruct me, and to bestow a little farther speech on me.

- *Ancor vo’ che m’ insegni* ‘Still I wish that thou instruct me’ → “Still I wish thee to instruct me,”: the finite subjunctive clause becomes an object-and-infinitive construction, and the pronoun representing the person instructed moves after the verb.
- *e che di più parlar mi facci dono* ‘and that of further speaking thou make me a gift’ → “and to bestow a little farther speech on me.”: the subjunctive clause becomes another infinitive construction. ‘Make a gift’ becomes “bestow”; the substantivized infinitive ‘speaking’ becomes “speech”; “a little” is supplied; and the indirect pronoun becomes the final phrase “on me”.

> 79 Farinata e il Tegghiaio, che fur sì degni,  
> 80 Jacopo Rusticucci, Arrigo e il Mosca,  
> 81 e gli altri, che a ben far poser gl’ ingegni,  

Farinata and Tegghiaio, who were so worthy ; Jacopo Rusticucci, Arrigo and Mosca, and the rest who set their minds on doing good

- *il Tegghiaio* ‘the Tegghiaio’ → “Tegghiaio,”: the article before the name is omitted.
- *il Mosca* ‘the Mosca’ → “Mosca,”: the article before the name is omitted.
- *gli altri* ‘the others’ → “the rest”: the plural substantivized adjective becomes a collective noun phrase.
- *che a ben far poser gl’ ingegni* ‘who to doing well put the intellects’ → “who set their minds on doing good ;”: the opening prepositional phrase moves after the verb and its object, and ‘to’ becomes “on”. ‘Doing well’ becomes “doing good”, with the adverb replaced by a noun; ‘the intellects’ becomes “their minds”, making possession explicit.

> 82 dimmi ove sono, e fa ch’ io li conosca:  

; tell me where they are, and give me to know them:

- *e fa ch’ io li conosca* ‘and make that I know them’ → “and give me to know them:”: the causative verb followed by a subjunctive clause becomes “give” with an indirect object and an infinitive; the object of ‘know’ moves after its verb.

> 83 chè gran desio mi stringe di sapere,  
> 84 se il ciel gli addolcia o l’ inferno gli attosca.”  

for great desire urges me to learn whether Heaven soothes or Hell empoisons them.”

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *gran desio mi stringe di sapere* ‘great desire grips me to know’ → “great desire urges me to learn”: ‘grips’ becomes “urges”, replacing the physical image with an expression of prompting; ‘know’ becomes “learn”, shifting from knowledge to its acquisition. The object pronoun follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *se il ciel gli addolcia o l’ inferno gli attosca* ‘whether the heaven them sweetens or the hell them poisons’ → “whether Heaven soothes or Hell empoisons them.”: ‘sweetens’ becomes “soothes”. The definite articles are omitted, and the two preverbal object pronouns become one shared object after the second verb.

> 85 E quegli: “Ei son tra le anime più nere;  
> 86 diversa colpa giù gli aggrava al fondo:  

And he to me : “ They are amongst the blackest spirits; a different crime weighs them downwards to the bottom;

- *E quegli* ‘And that one’ → “And he to me”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun, and “to me” is supplied.
- *le anime più nere* ‘the souls most black’ → “the blackest spirits;”: ‘souls’ becomes “spirits”, and the superlative adjective precedes the noun.
- *diversa colpa* ‘a different fault’ → “a different crime”: the broader ‘fault’ becomes “crime”.
- *giù gli aggrava al fondo* ‘downwards them burdens to the bottom’ → “weighs them downwards to the bottom;”: both the directional adverb and the object move from before the verb to after it; ‘burdens’ is rendered through the language of weighing.

> 87 se tanto scendi, gli potrai vedere.  

shouldst thou descend so far, thou mayest see them.

- *se tanto scendi* ‘if so far thou descendest’ → “shouldst thou descend so far,”: the conditional conjunction and present indicative become an inverted construction with “shouldst”; the expression of extent moves after the verb.
- *gli potrai vedere* ‘them thou wilt be able to see’ → “thou mayest see them.”: future ability becomes the modal “mayest”, without an explicit future tense, and the object moves after the infinitive.

## Lines 88–115 — Remembrance and Future Torments

> 88 Ma quando tu sarai nel dolce mondo,  
> 89 pregoti che alla mente altrui mi rechi ;  
> 90 più non ti dico, e più non ti rispondo.”  

But [when thou shalt be in] the sweet world, I pray thee recall me to the memory of men; more I tell thee not, and more I answer not.”

- *pregoti che alla mente altrui mi rechi* ‘I pray thee that to the mind of others thou bring me’ → “I pray thee recall me to the memory of men”: the clause introduced by *che*, with its subjunctive verb, becomes a bare verb construction after “I pray thee”; the verb and object precede the prepositional phrase. Bringing someone to mind becomes recalling them to memory, and the indefinite ‘others’ becomes “men”.
- *più non ti dico* ‘more I do not say to thee’ → “more I tell thee not”: ‘say to thee’ becomes “tell thee”; the negative and the object pronoun follow the verb.
- *e più non ti rispondo* ‘and more I do not answer thee’ → “and more I answer not”: “not” follows the verb, and *ti*, ‘thee’, is omitted.

> 91 Gli diritti occhi torse allora in biechi ;  
> 92 guardommi un poco ; e poi chinò la testa ;  
> 93 cadde con essa a par degli altri ciechi.  

Therewith he writhed his straight eyes asquint; looked at me a little; then bent his head, and fell down with it like his blind companions.

- *Gli diritti occhi torse allora in biechi* ‘the straight eyes he turned then into squinting ones’ → “Therewith he writhed his straight eyes asquint”: the object moves after the verb, and the temporal expression moves to the beginning. The definite article becomes “his”; ‘turned’ becomes the more forceful “writhed”; ‘then’ becomes “Therewith”; and the adjectival construction *in biechi* becomes the adverb “asquint”.
- *e poi* ‘and then’ → “then”: the conjunction is omitted.
- *la testa* ‘the head’ → “his head”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *cadde* ‘fell’ → “and fell down”: “and” supplies coordination, and “down” makes the direction explicit.
- *a par degli altri ciechi* ‘on a level with the other blind ones’ → “like his blind companions”: the expression of equal position becomes a comparison. ‘The other blind ones’ becomes “his blind companions”, supplying possession and companionship and turning the substantival adjective into a modifier.

> 94 E il duca disse a me : “ Più non si desta  
> 95 di qua dal suon dell' angelica tromba ;  

And my Guide said to me: “He wakes no more until the angel’s trumpet sounds;

- *il duca* ‘the leader’ → “my Guide”: the definite article becomes a possessive, and ‘leader’ is rendered as “Guide”.
- *Più non si desta* ‘no more does he awaken’ → “He wakes no more”: the negative expression moves after the verb.
- *di qua dal suon dell' angelica tromba* ‘on this side of the sound of the angelic trumpet’ → “until the angel’s trumpet sounds”: the prepositional expression becomes a temporal clause. The noun *suon* becomes the verb “sounds”, while the adjective *angelica* becomes the possessive “angel’s”.

> 96 quando verrà la nimica podesta,  
> 97 ciascun ritroverà la trista tomba,  
> 98 ripiglierà sua carne e sua figura,  
> 99 udirà quel che in eterno rimbomba.”  

when the adverse Power shall come, each shall revisit his sad grave; shall resume his flesh and form; shall hear that which resounds to all eternity.”

- *quando verrà la nimica podesta* ‘when shall come the hostile power’ → “when the adverse Power shall come”: the subject moves before the verb.
- *ritroverà* ‘shall find again’ → “shall revisit”: finding again is rendered as visiting again.
- *la trista tomba* ‘the sad grave’ → “his sad grave”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *ripiglierà* ‘shall take back’ → “shall resume”: taking back is expressed through a single verb meaning taking up again.
- *sua carne e sua figura* ‘his flesh and his form’ → “his flesh and form”: the second possessive is omitted.
- *in eterno rimbomba* ‘for ever resounds’ → “resounds to all eternity”: the temporal expression follows the verb and is recast as “to all eternity”.

> 100 Sì trapassammo per sozza mistura  
> 101 dell' ombre e della pioggia, a passi lenti,  
> 102 toccando un poco la vita futura.  

Thus passed we through the filthy mixture of the shadows and the rain, with paces slow, touching a little on the future life.

- *per sozza mistura* ‘through filthy mixture’ → “through the filthy mixture”: the definite article is supplied.
- *a passi lenti* ‘at paces slow’ → “with paces slow”: the preposition is changed.
- *toccando un poco la vita futura* ‘touching a little the life future’ → “touching a little on the future life”: the direct-object construction becomes “touching … on”, marking the sense of briefly discussing something; the adjective moves before its noun.

> 103 Per ch' io dissi : “ Maestro, esti tormenti  
> 104 cresceranno ei dopo la gran sentenza,  
> 105 o fien minori, o saran sì cocenti ? ”  

Wherefore I said: “Master, shall these torments increase after the great Sentence, or grow less, or remain as burning?”

- *esti tormenti cresceranno ei* ‘these torments, will they increase’ → “shall these torments increase”: the resumptive pronoun *ei*, ‘they’, is omitted, and “these torments” becomes the subject within an English question, following the auxiliary.
- *o fien minori* ‘or will they be less’ → “or grow less”: ‘be’ becomes “grow”, expressing change rather than a state; the future auxiliary is shared with the preceding clause.
- *o saran sì cocenti* ‘or will they be so burning’ → “or remain as burning”: ‘be’ becomes “remain”, making continuance explicit, and ‘so’ becomes the comparative “as”. The future auxiliary is again shared rather than repeated.

> 106 Ed egli a me : “ Ritorna a tua scienza,  
> 107 che vuol, quanto la cosa è più perfetta,  
> 108 più senta il bene, e così la doglienza.  

And he to me: “Return to thy science, which has it, that the more a thing is perfect, the more it feels pleasure and likewise pain.

- *che vuol* ‘which wills’ → “which has it, that”: the verb is interpreted as asserting or maintaining something, and “that” explicitly introduces what is asserted.
- *quanto la cosa è più perfetta* ‘by how much the thing is more perfect’ → “the more a thing is perfect”: the comparative construction is recast with “the more” at the beginning, separating the degree expression from “perfect”; the generic definite article becomes indefinite.
- *più senta* ‘the more it feel’ → “the more it feels”: the Italian subjunctive becomes an English indicative.
- *il bene* ‘the good’ → “pleasure”: a substantivized adjective becomes a noun naming the pleasurable experience, and the article is omitted.
- *la doglienza* ‘the pain’ → “pain”: the definite article is omitted.

> 109 Tuttochè questa gente maledetta  
> 110 in vera perfezion giammai non vada,  
> 111 di là, più che di qua, essere aspetta.”  

Though these accursed people never attain to true perfection, yet they [look to] be nearer it after than before.”

- *questa gente maledetta* ‘this people accursed’ → “these accursed people”: the singular collective becomes grammatically plural, and the adjective moves before the noun.
- *in vera perfezion giammai non vada* ‘into true perfection never go’ → “never attain to true perfection”: going into something becomes attaining to it. The subjunctive becomes indicative, the negative expression moves before the verb, and the Italian negative concord of *giammai non* is represented by the single “never”.
- *di là, più che di qua, essere aspetta* ‘on that side, more than on this side, it expects to be’ → “yet they [look to] be nearer it after than before”: “yet” is supplied to reinforce the concession. The spatial expressions become temporal “after” and “before”; ‘be more’ is interpreted as “be nearer it”, supplying a reference back to perfection. ‘Expects’ becomes “[look to]”; the governing verb precedes its infinitive, and the comparison follows it.

> 112 Noi aggirammo a tondo quella strada,  
> 113 parlando più assai ch' io non ridico ;  

We went round along that road, speaking much more than I repeat;

- *aggirammo a tondo quella strada* ‘circled round that road’ → “went round along that road”: the verb and its reinforcing adverbial expression become “went round”; “along” changes the direct object into a prepositional expression of route.
- *parlando più assai* ‘speaking more much’ → “speaking much more”: the intensifier moves before the comparative.
- *ch' io non ridico* ‘than I do not repeat’ → “than I repeat”: the pleonastic *non* used in the Italian comparison is omitted.

> 114 venimmo al punto dove si digrada ;  

we reached the point where the descent begins;

- *venimmo al punto* ‘we came to the point’ → “we reached the point”: an intransitive verb with a prepositional complement becomes a transitive verb with a direct object.
- *dove si digrada* ‘where one goes down by degrees’ → “where the descent begins”: the impersonal verbal construction becomes a clause with “the descent” as its subject; “begins” makes the starting point explicit.

> 115 quivi trovammo Pluto il gran nemico.  

here found we Plutus, the great enemy.

- *quivi* ‘there’ → “here”: the spatial reference shifts from ‘there’ to “here”.

## Summary — Close Echoes and Prose Reshaping

The translation remains close to the Italian in its sequence of statements, repeated terms, and many distinctive images, but regularly rearranges them into English prose order. Subjects and objects move from emphatic or inverted positions into their usual places before and after the verb: *Urlar gli fa la pioggia* becomes “The rain makes them howl”. Adjectives generally precede their nouns, though such phrases as “a monster fierce and strange” and “with paces slow” preserve the Italian arrangement. Literal expressions such as “within three suns” and “their emptiness, which seems a body” likewise retain the source’s unusual wording rather than paraphrasing it.

Choices of words sometimes interpret, narrow, or alter the Italian. *Mente* becomes “sense” when it denotes returning consciousness, but “memory” in the exchange about recognition; *pietà* becomes “misery”, locating the meaning in suffering rather than the response to it. *Gola*, literally ‘throat’, is rendered explicitly as “gluttony”, whereas *graffia*, ‘scratches’, becomes “clutches”, changing the action. Elsewhere the translation preserves a figurative expression but modifies its terms: *piaggia*, understood as ‘hugs the shore’, becomes “keeps tacking”. Such choices coexist with close verbal correspondences, notably the accumulated qualities of the rain and the sequence “Pride, Envy, and Avarice”.

Changes of construction often make the English more compact or explicit. *Fanno all’ altro schermo*, ‘make a screen for the other’, becomes “screen the other”; *pose fine*, ‘put an end’, becomes “ended”. Conversely, *pute*, ‘stinks’, expands into “emits a putrid smell”. Finite clauses become participles or infinitives, as when *distese* becomes “spreading” and *vo’ che m’ insegni* becomes “I wish thee to instruct me”. Reflexive verbs frequently become ordinary intransitives, while Italian subjunctives and imperfects often lose their distinct grammatical marking. Changes of viewpoint can be more substantial: *che questo riceve*, ‘which receives this’, becomes “on which it falls”, making the ground a location rather than the receiving subject.

Omissions and additions also recur. Repeated conjunctions, pronouns, and parallel constructions are sometimes reduced; *mi … intorno* receives no separate rendering in “I discern”, and the recipient expressed by *-cci* disappears from “shewed his tusks”. Added words identify or clarify what Italian leaves implicit: “souls” supplies a noun for *tormentati*, “of all” extends “the hearts”, and “nearer it” interprets the compressed comparison concerning perfection. Throughout, English also supplies subject pronouns where Italian verb endings alone identify the subject, and omits the Italian articles before possessives, as in *la tua città* becoming “Thy city”. Alongside these routine adjustments, the frequent replacement of definite articles with possessives in references to body parts helps produce idiomatic English without abandoning the translation’s generally close verbal texture.
