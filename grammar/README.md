# grammar

Grammar commentaries in English on the texts of the Divine Comedy, generated by an LLM.

> [!NOTE]
> The commentaries are machine-generated and have not been reviewed by translators or scholars.

`<code>-<source>/<canticle>/<NN>.md` holds a grammar commentary in English on every line of a source text, quoting the translation of `<code>` from [`commentary/`](../commentary/) (see [scripts/grammar.py](#scriptsgrammarpy)). The source is `it` (the Italian of dante-corpus, with the translations of `../commentary/<code>/`) or `grc` (Musurus's Greek, with the translations of `../commentary/<code>-grc/`). `en/inferno/<NN>.md` explains the Italian printed in Carlyle's Inferno ([`texts/en/`](../texts/en/), whose lines are those of dante-corpus, with the readings and the spelling of that edition) against Carlyle's own English prose translation, printed with it.

The scripts and the Makefile are run from this directory. `scripts/grammar.py` uses the scripts of [`commentary/scripts/`](../commentary/scripts/) (`source.py`, `generate.py`, `fix_quote_blocks.py`).

The commentaries still to be made, and their order, are planned in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md); those done, with their review, are recorded in [DONE.md](DONE.md).

## scripts/grammar.py

```bash
uv run scripts/grammar.py <canticle> -m MODEL -l CODE -d DIR -o OUT [-c SPEC] [-s SOURCE] [--size N] [-t TRIES] [--no-think] [--save-usage]
uv run scripts/grammar.py <canticle> -n -o OUT [-c SPEC] [-s SOURCE]
```

argument | description | default
---|---|---
`canticle` | `inferno`, `purgatorio`, or `paradiso` | required
`-m`, `--model` | model name with an optional vendor prefix | required unless `-n`
`-l`, `--lang-code` | language code of the translation | required unless `-n` or `-s en`
`-d`, `--dir` | directory holding the translations `<canticle>/<NN>.txt` | required unless `-n` or `-s en`
`-o`, `--out` | output directory for `<canticle>/<NN>.md` | required
`-c`, `--canto` | cantos to process (SPEC syntax) | all
`-s`, `--source` | source text to explain: `it`, `grc`, or `en` | `it`
`--size` | lines per section, roughly (not with `-s en`, cut in blocks) | `30`
`-t`, `--tries` | answers asked for one section (or block) before giving up | `3`
`-n`, `--notes` | only list the translation notes of the saved commentaries | -
`--no-think` | disable thinking output | enabled
`--save-usage` | record usage for any model | -

From the Makefile of this directory (the output goes to `<code>-<source>/`, or `en/`):

```bash
make grammar CANTICLE=inferno CANTO=1 SOURCE=en
make grammar CANTICLE=inferno CANTO=1 LANG=en
make grammar-notes CANTICLE=inferno SOURCE=grc
make fix-grammar-quotes SOURCE=grc
```

The commentary explains the grammar and usage of every line of the source, in English: the forms of the words, the syntax, the particles, the usage of the language (Old Italian forms, or the Attic and poetic forms of Musurus's Greek), and how the translation renders the constructions. The content (story, allegory, background) and the meter are left out.

Each canto is cut into sections of about `--size` lines that end where a sentence of the source ends (`.`, `!`, `?`, and the Greek `;`, before any closing quote marks), or a clause (`;`, `:`, `·`) if no sentence ends between half and one and a half times `--size` lines on; the last section takes the rest of the canto when it is at most one and a half times `--size` lines. Each section is one call: the whole canto, every line followed by its translation in parentheses, then the prompt for the section's lines. The model writes one heading `## Lines a–b — <short title>`, then quotes the lines in order, one sentence or clause (one to four lines) at a time, as `> N original` / `> (translation)`, each followed by its explanation.

An answer is kept only if its heading names the section's lines, it quotes every line of the section exactly once and in order, and every quoted original and translation matches the source and the `.txt` (as `generate.py` matches quotes: up to the style of the quote marks and three other characters). The model must not rewrite the translation, so a quoted translation that does not match rejects the answer. A rejected answer is asked again, up to `--tries` times; if none passes, the canto is left unfinished and the run goes on with the next one (the exit status is then 1). The quote blocks of a kept answer are rebuilt from the source and the `.txt` (as `fix_quote_blocks.py` does) and the section is appended to `<NN>.md`, under the heading `# <Canticle> <N>`, so an interrupted run resumes after the last section saved. After the last section, a closing section `## Summary — <short title>` is asked for, with the canto and the commentary so far; a canto with a summary is complete and skipped. At the end of the run, the calls that had answers rejected are listed (`--- Rejections ---`), each with the first problem of every rejected answer, and the count of all the rejected answers and calls.

With `-s en`, the Italian is compared with Carlyle's English instead, block by block: `../commentary/scripts/source.py` cuts `texts/en/` into blocks of Italian lines and the English that renders exactly them (`../editions/en-blocks.txt`, see [scripts/align_carlyle.py](../editions/README.md#scriptsalign_carlylepy)), without the argument, the notes, and the footnote references, and `--size` is not used. Each block is one call, with the block alone, the lines in `<italian>` and the English in `<english>`. The model writes a short title on the first line, then goes through the lines a sentence or clause at a time, each as a range `[a–b]` followed by the English that renders it, copied from the block, and after it bullets on every place where the English departs from a word-for-word rendering (word order, wording, words left out or supplied, changes of construction, another reading), as `- *Italian* ‘word-for-word rendering’ → “Carlyle's words”: what has changed`, or `- Literal.`:

```
The Dark Wood at the Midpoint of Life
[1–3] In the middle of the journey of our life, I found myself in a dark wood; for the straight way was lost.
- *Mi ritrovai* ‘myself I found’ → “I found myself”: the subject, given only by the ending of *ritrovai*, is supplied, and the pronoun follows the verb.
```

An answer is kept only if the ranges cover the block's lines once and in order, their English, joined, is the block's English word for word (up to punctuation and case), every phrase in double quotation marks is found in the English of its range (the model's own renderings go in single quotation marks), and no bullet gives Carlyle's own words as the word-for-word rendering (a bullet on nothing, or on capitals only). The prompt also asks for one bullet per Italian phrase and none on capitals, punctuation, or the spelling of names, nor ones that only say that a subject given by the verb ending is supplied or that the article before a possessive is left out: these general changes are summed up in the summary. A rejected answer is sent back with its problems to be corrected (the rest kept as it is), up to `--tries` answers in all. The script then writes the section: the heading `## Lines a–b — <title>`, and for each range the quote block of its lines (`> N original`), the English as printed (cut where the ranges part), and the bullets. The summary sums up the kinds of departure that recur in the canto; its phrases in double quotation marks are checked against the English of the canto in the same way, and a summary that fails is sent back to be corrected. These comparisons have no translation notes: Carlyle's English is a printed text, not one to be corrected.

Where the model finds that the translation gets the grammar of a line wrong, it writes a paragraph opening with `Translation note:` instead of changing the translation. `--notes` lists these paragraphs with the lines quoted before them; after the `.txt` is corrected, `make fix-grammar-quotes` copies the corrected lines into the quote blocks.
