# Paradiso 16

## Lines 1–12 — Pride in Nobility of Blood

> 1 O poca nostra nobiltà di sangue!  

Ah puny blood-nobility of ours!

- *O* ‘O’ → “Ah”: the sign of direct address becomes an exclamation.
- *poca nostra nobiltà di sangue* ‘little our nobility of blood’ → “puny blood-nobility of ours”: ‘little’ becomes the more disparaging “puny”; ‘of blood’ becomes the first element of a compound, and the possessive moves after the noun as “of ours”.

> 2 se gloriar di te la gente fai  
> 3 quaggiù, dove l' affetto nostro langue,  
> 4 mirabil cosa non mi sarà mai;  

If thou makest folk glory in thee here below, where our affections sicken, it shall be marvel to me never more;

- *gloriar di te la gente fai* ‘glory in thee the people thou makest’ → “thou makest folk glory in thee”: the causative verb moves to the front, followed by the people made to glory and then the infinitive with its complement; the definite collective noun becomes “folk” without an article.
- *l' affetto nostro langue* ‘our affection languishes’ → “our affections sicken”: the possessive moves before the noun, the singular becomes plural, and ‘languishes’ is rendered by a verb of illness.
- *mirabil cosa non mi sarà mai* ‘a marvellous thing it will not be to me ever’ → “it shall be marvel to me never more”: the predicate moves after the verb, adjective and noun are compressed into the single noun “marvel”, and the Italian negative is gathered into “never”, with “more” added.

> 5 chè là, dove appetito non si torce,  
> 6 dico nel cielo, io me ne gloriai.  

for there, where appetite is unwarped, I mean in heaven, I gloried me therein.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *non si torce* ‘does not twist aside’ → “is unwarped”: the negative pronominal verb becomes a description of a state, with the negation carried by the prefix.
- *dico* ‘I say’ → “I mean”: the verb of speaking is rendered as an expression of clarification.
- *io me ne gloriai* ‘I in that gloried myself’ → “I gloried me therein”: the Italian pronouns precede the verb; the English reflexive follows it, and “therein” carries the reference of *ne* at the end.

> 7 Ben sei tu manto che tosto raccorce,  

Yet verily thou art a mantle that soon shrinketh,

- *Ben sei tu* ‘indeed art thou’ → “Yet verily thou art”: “Yet” makes the contrast explicit, “verily” gives the emphasis of the adverb, and the subject moves before the verb.
- *manto* ‘mantle’ → “a mantle”: English supplies an indefinite article for the predicate noun.
- *raccorce* ‘grows shorter’ → “shrinketh”: a verb specifically of loss of length becomes a more general verb of diminishing size.

> 8 sì che, se non s' appon di dìe in dìe,  
> 9 lo tempo va dintorno con le force.  

so that, if day by day there be nought added, time goeth round with the shears.

- *se non s' appon di dìe in dìe* ‘if there is not added from day to day’ → “if day by day there be nought added”: the time phrase moves before the verb and loses its prepositions in favour of “by”; the negative becomes the pronoun “nought”, and the indicative pronominal construction becomes an English subjunctive passive.
- *lo tempo* ‘the time’ → “time”: English uses the noun without the definite article.

> 10 Dal *voi* che prima Roma sofferie,  
> 11 in che la sua famiglia men persevra,  
> 12 ricominciaron le parole mie;  

With that *ye* that Rome was first to allow wherein her household doth least persevere, my words began again;

- *Dal voi* ‘from the you’ → “With that *ye*”: English marks what the words begin with rather than what they begin from; the article becomes demonstrative, and “ye” distinguishes the plural form of address.
- *che prima Roma sofferie* ‘that first Rome allowed’ → “that Rome was first to allow”: “first” is taken as marking Rome’s priority; the simple past verb becomes an infinitive dependent on “was first”.
- *la sua famiglia* ‘her family’ → “her household”: the collective is rendered as a household rather than a family.
- *men persevra* ‘less perseveres’ → “doth least persevere”: the comparative is taken in a superlative sense, and the simple verb is expanded with an auxiliary.
- *ricominciaron le parole mie* ‘began anew the words of mine’ → “my words began again”: the subject moves before the verb, the possessive before the noun, and a separate adverb renders the sense of repetition in the verb.

## Lines 13–45 — Dante Asks Cacciaguida About His Forebears and Florence

> 13 onde Beatrice, ch' era un poco scevra,  
> 14 ridendo, parve quella che tossio  
> 15 al primo fallo scritto di Ginevra.  

whereon Beatrice, who was a little sundered from us, smiled, and seemed to me like her who coughed at the first trespass writ of Guinivere.

- *un poco scevra* ‘a little apart’ → “a little sundered from us”: the adjective becomes a participle, and those from whom she was apart are specified.
- *ridendo* ‘smiling’ → “smiled, and”: the gerund becomes a finite verb coordinated with the next.
- *parve quella* ‘seemed that woman’ → “seemed to me like her”: the observer is supplied, and identification becomes an explicit comparison.

> 16 Io cominciai: “Voi siete il padre mio,  
> 17 voi mi date a parlar tutta baldezza,  
> 18 voi mi levate sì ch' io son più ch' io.  

I began: “Ye are my father, ye give me full boldness to speak, ye so uplift me, that I am more than I.

- *il padre mio* ‘the father of mine’ → “my father”: the possessive precedes the noun instead of following it.
- *a parlar tutta baldezza* ‘to speak all boldness’ → “full boldness to speak”: degree replaces totality, and the infinitive follows the noun.
- *mi levate sì* ‘me ye lift so’ → “ye so uplift me”: the adverb precedes the verb and the object follows it.

> 19 Per tanti rivi s' empie d' allegrezza  
> 20 la mente mia, che di sè fa letizia,  
> 21 perchè può sostener che non si spezza.  

By so many streams my mind is filled with gladness, it giveth itself joy that it can bear it and yet not be rent.

- *Per tanti rivi* ‘through so many streams’ → “By so many streams”: the preposition expresses means rather than passage.
- *s’ empie d’ allegrezza la mente mia* ‘fills itself with gladness the mind of mine’ → “my mind is filled with gladness”: a passive replaces the reflexive form; the subject moves before the verb and the possessive before the noun.
- *che di sè fa letizia* ‘that of itself it makes joy’ → “it giveth itself joy”: the result clause becomes an independent clause; the prepositional phrase becomes the recipient of joy, and giving replaces making.
- *perchè può sostener* ‘because it can bear’ → “that it can bear it”: the reason is expressed as the object of the rejoicing, and an object for ‘bear’ is supplied.
- *che non si spezza* ‘so that it does not break’ → “and yet not be rent”: coordination with an added adversative adverb replaces subordination; the finite intransitive becomes a passive infinitive governed by “can”.

> 22 Ditemi dunque, cara mia primizia,  
> 23 quai fur li vostri antichi, e quai fur gli anni  
> 24 che si segnaro in vostra puerizia.  

Tell me, then, dear stock from which I spring, what was your ancestry, and what the years recorded in your boyhood.

- *cara mia primizia* ‘dear firstfruit of mine’ → “dear stock from which I spring”: the image of a firstfruit becomes that of parent stock, and the possessive relation is expanded into a relative clause.
- *quai fur li vostri antichi* ‘which were your ancestors’ → “what was your ancestry”: a singular abstract noun replaces the persons in the plural, with a corresponding change in the verb.
- *e quai fur gli anni* ‘and which were the years’ → “and what the years”: the repeated verb is left understood.
- *che si segnaro* ‘that were marked’ → “recorded”: the finite relative clause is reduced to a participle, and marking becomes recording.

> 25 Ditemi dell' ovil di San Giovanni  
> 26 quanto era allora, e chi eran le genti  
> 27 tra esso degne di più alti scanni.”  

Tell me of the sheepfold of St John, how great it then was, and who were the folk worthy of loftiest seats in it.”

- *quanto era allora* ‘how great it was then’ → “how great it then was”: the adverb moves before the verb.
- *tra esso* ‘within it’ → “in it”: the phrase moves from before the description of worthiness to the end of it.
- *di più alti scanni* ‘of higher seats’ → “of loftiest seats”: the comparative becomes a superlative.

> 28 Come s' avviva allo spirar dei venti  
> 29 carbone in fiamma, così vidi quella  
> 30 luce risplendere ai miei blandimenti;  

As a coal quickeneth into flame at the wind’s breathing, so did I see that light glow forth at my caressing words;

- *s’ avviva … carbone in fiamma* ‘quickens … coal into flame’ → “a coal quickeneth into flame”: the subject moves before the verb and takes an indefinite article.
- *allo spirar dei venti* ‘at the breathing of the winds’ → “at the wind’s breathing”: the plural becomes singular, the possessor precedes the verbal noun, and the phrase moves after the description of the coal.
- *così vidi* ‘so I saw’ → “so did I see”: the simple past becomes an inverted construction with an auxiliary.
- *risplendere* ‘shine brightly’ → “glow forth”: a verb with a particle replaces the single verb.
- *ai miei blandimenti* ‘at my blandishments’ → “at my caressing words”: the noun becomes a description of words and their quality.

> 31 e come agli occhi miei si fe' più bella,  
> 32 così con voce più dolce e soave,  
> 33 ma non con questa moderna favella,  

and even as to my sight it grew more beauteous, so with a voice more sweet and gentle, but not in this our modern dialect,

- *e come* ‘and as’ → “and even as”: an intensifier is supplied.
- *agli occhi miei* ‘to the eyes of mine’ → “to my sight”: the faculty replaces the organs, and the possessive moves before the noun.
- *si fe’ più bella* ‘made itself more beautiful’ → “it grew more beauteous”: a verb of becoming replaces the reflexive construction.
- *con voce* ‘with voice’ → “with a voice”: an indefinite article is supplied.
- *con questa moderna favella* ‘with this modern speech’ → “in this our modern dialect”: the preposition changes, “our” is supplied, and the more specific “dialect” replaces ‘speech’.

> 34 dissemi: “Da quel dì che fu detto *Ave*  
> 35 al parto in che mia madre, ch' è or santa,  
> 36 s' alleviò di me ond' era grave,  

he said: “From the day on which *Ave* was uttered, to the birth wherein my mother, now sainted, unburdened her of me with whom she was laden,

- *dissemi* ‘said to me’ → “he said”: the recipient is omitted.
- *Da quel dì* ‘from that day’ → “From the day”: the demonstrative becomes a definite article.
- *che fu detto Ave* ‘that was said Ave’ → “on which *Ave* was uttered”: the temporal relative takes a preposition, the subject moves before the passive verb, and saying becomes uttering.
- *ch’ è or santa* ‘who is now holy’ → “now sainted”: the relative clause becomes a participial description.
- *s’ alleviò* ‘herself she relieved’ → “unburdened her”: the object follows the verb, and the wording makes the image of a burden explicit.

> 37 al suo Leon cinquecento cinquanta  
> 38 e trenta fiate venne questo foco  
> 39 a rinfiammarsi sotto la sua pianta.  

five hundred, fifty, and thirty times did this flame return to his own Lion to rekindle him beneath his feet.

- *al suo Leon* ‘to his Lion’ → “to his own Lion”: “own” is supplied, and the phrase moves from before the count to after the verb of motion.
- *venne questo foco* ‘came this fire’ → “did this flame return”: coming is interpreted as returning, ‘fire’ becomes “flame”, and an auxiliary precedes the subject.
- *a rinfiammarsi* ‘to rekindle itself’ → “to rekindle him”: Wicksteed takes the Lion as the object rather than the flame as the reflexive object.
- *sotto la sua pianta* ‘under his sole’ → “beneath his feet”: the whole foot replaces the sole, and the singular becomes plural.

> 40 Gli antichi miei ed io nacqui nel loco,  
> 41 dove si trova pria l' ultimo sesto  
> 42 da quel che corre il vostro annual gioco.  

My forebears and myself were born in the spot where he who runneth in your annual games doth first encounter the last sesto.

- *Gli antichi miei ed io nacqui* ‘the ancestors of mine and I was born’ → “My forebears and myself were born”: the possessive moves before the noun, emphatic “myself” replaces the plain subject pronoun, and the verb agrees with both subjects rather than with the first-person singular alone.
- *nel loco* ‘in the place’ → “in the spot”: a more particular noun is used.
- *dove si trova pria l’ ultimo sesto* ‘where is found first the last sesto’ → “doth first encounter the last sesto”: the passive construction becomes active, with the finder as subject and the thing found as object; the adverb stands between auxiliary and verb. The relative “where” still opens the clause.
- *da quel che corre il vostro annual gioco* ‘by him who runs your annual game’ → “he who runneth in your annual games”: the agent phrase moves forward as subject; running takes “in” rather than a direct object, and the game becomes plural.

> 43 Basti dei miei maggiori udirne questo;  

About my ancestors let it suffice so much to hear;

- *Basti* ‘may it suffice’ → “let it suffice”: a construction with “let” renders the independent subjunctive.
- *dei miei maggiori* ‘of my forebears’ → “About my ancestors”: the phrase is placed first and marked explicitly as the topic.
- *udirne questo* ‘to hear of them this’ → “so much to hear”: the resumptive pronoun is omitted, “so much” expresses a limit rather than pointing with ‘this’, and the object precedes the infinitive.

> 44 chi ei si furo, ed onde venner quivi,  
> 45 più è tacer, che ragionare, onesto.  

of who they were and whence they hither came silence were comelier than discourse.

- *chi ei si furo* ‘who they were’ → “of who they were”: “of” is supplied to mark the topic, and the pronominal *si* has no separate rendering.
- *onde venner quivi* ‘whence they came thither’ → “whence they hither came”: the direction changes from ‘to there’ to ‘to here’, and the directional adverb precedes the verb.
- *più è tacer, che ragionare, onesto* ‘more is keeping silent, than speaking, seemly’ → “silence were comelier than discourse”: nouns replace the infinitives; the indicative becomes a modal “were”, and the separated comparative adverb and adjective become a single comparative before the second term.

## Lines 46–78 — Florence’s Growth and the Passing of Families

> 46 Tutti color ch' a quel tempo eran ivi  
> 47 da poter arme, tra Marte e il Batista,  
> 48 erano il quinto di quei che son vivi.  

At that time all who were there, between Mars and the Baptist, capable of arms, were but the fifth of the now living ones.

- *Tutti color ch' a quel tempo eran ivi* ‘all those who at that time were there’ → “At that time all who were there”: the time phrase moves out of the relative clause to the head of the sentence; ‘those’ is absorbed into “all who”.
- *da poter arme, tra Marte e il Batista* ‘able to bear arms, between Mars and the Baptist’ → “between Mars and the Baptist, capable of arms”: the place phrase comes first, next to “there”; the infinitive of ability becomes an adjective governing “of arms”.
- *erano il quinto* ‘were the fifth’ → “were but the fifth”: “but” is supplied to emphasize the smallness of the fraction.
- *di quei che son vivi* ‘of those who are alive’ → “of the now living ones”: the relative clause becomes an attributive participle with “ones”; “now” makes the contrast of tenses explicit.

> 49 Ma la cittadinanza, ch' è or mista  
> 50 di Campi, di Certaldo e di Fighine,  
> 51 pura vedeasi nell' ultimo artista.  

But the citizenship, contaminated now from Campi, from Certaldo and from Fighine, saw itself pure down to the humblest artizan.

- *ch' è or mista* ‘which is now mixed’ → “contaminated now”: the finite relative clause becomes a participial phrase, “now” follows the participle, and “contaminated” gives ‘mixed’ a specifically adverse sense.
- *di Campi, di Certaldo e di Fighine* ‘of Campi, of Certaldo and of Fighine’ → “from Campi, from Certaldo and from Fighine”: the preposition of composition is rendered as one of origin.
- *pura vedeasi* ‘pure was seen’ → “saw itself pure”: Wicksteed takes the pronominal verb reflexively rather than passively, and places the predicative adjective last.
- *nell' ultimo artista* ‘in the lowest artisan’ → “down to the humblest artizan”: ‘in’ becomes an expression of extent, and ‘last’ in order of rank becomes “humblest”.

> 52 O quanto fora meglio esser vicine  
> 53 quelle genti ch' io dico, ed al Galluzzo  
> 54 ed a Trespiano aver vostro confine,  

Oh, how much better were it for these folk of whom I speak to be your neighbours, and to have your boundary at Galluzzo and at Trespiano,

- *fora meglio esser vicine quelle genti ch' io dico* ‘better would it be that those folk whom I mention be neighbours’ → “better were it for these folk of whom I speak to be your neighbours”: “were it” expresses the conditional; the subject of the infinitive is introduced by “for” and moved before it. ‘Those’ becomes “these”, a direct object becomes “of whom”, and “your” is supplied.
- *ed al Galluzzo ed a Trespiano aver vostro confine* ‘and at Galluzzo and at Trespiano to have your boundary’ → “and to have your boundary at Galluzzo and at Trespiano”: the infinitive and its object move ahead of the two place phrases; the article in *al* has no counterpart.

> 55 che averle dentro, e sostener lo puzzo  
> 56 del villan d' Aguglion, di quel da Signa,  
> 57 che già per barattare ha l' occhio aguzzo!  

than to have them within, and bear the stench of the hind of Aguglion, and of him of Signa, who still for jobbery hath his eye alert!

- *sostener* ‘to endure’ → “bear”: the sense is retained, but the infinitive shares the earlier “to”.
- *di quel da Signa* ‘of that one from Signa’ → “and of him of Signa”: “and” is supplied between the parallel phrases, the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun, and origin is expressed by “of”.
- *già* ‘already’ → “still”: the adverb is taken to indicate continuance rather than something already the case.
- *per barattare* ‘in order to practise jobbery’ → “for jobbery”: a purpose phrase with an infinitive becomes a preposition with an abstract noun.
- *ha l' occhio aguzzo* ‘has the eye sharp’ → “hath his eye alert”: the definite article becomes a possessive, and sharpness is rendered as alertness.

> 58 Se la gente, ch' al mondo più traligna,  
> 59 non fosse stata a Cesare noverca,  
> 60 ma, come madre a suo figliuol, benigna,  

Had the race, which goeth most degenerate on earth, not been to Cæsar as a step-mother, but, as a mother to her son, benign,

- *Se la gente … non fosse stata* ‘if the race … had not been’ → “Had the race, which goeth most degenerate on earth, not been”: inversion replaces the conditional conjunction, putting the auxiliary before the subject and leaving the negative after the intervening relative clause.
- *al mondo più traligna* ‘in the world most degenerates’ → “goeth most degenerate on earth”: the single verb becomes a verb with a predicative adjective; the place phrase is recast and moved to the end.
- *a Cesare noverca* ‘to Caesar a stepmother’ → “to Cæsar as a step-mother”: “as” makes the predicate noun an explicit comparison.
- *a suo figliuol* ‘to its own son’ → “to her son”: the possessive, which in Italian does not mark the possessor’s gender, is referred explicitly to the mother.

> 61 tal fatto è Fiorentino, e cambia e merca,  
> 62 che si sarebbe volto a Simifonti,  
> 63 là dove andava l' avolo alla cerca.  

one who is now a Florentine and changeth coin and wares, had been dispatched to Simifonte, where his own grandfather went round a-begging.

- *tal fatto è Fiorentino, e cambia e merca* ‘such a one has become a Florentine, and exchanges and trades’ → “one who is now a Florentine and changeth coin and wares”: the indefinite subject is expanded with a relative clause; becoming is expressed as a present state with “now”. Two distinct verbs become one verb with two supplied objects, so that the conjunction now links nouns.
- *che si sarebbe volto* ‘who would have turned himself’ → “had been dispatched”: where the Italian introduces a relative clause with *che*, the English resumes “one” as the subject of the main clause. A past conditional of a verb of turning becomes a passive pluperfect with counterfactual force; being sent replaces turning of one’s own accord.
- *là dove* ‘there where’ → “where”: the separate adverb of place drops out.
- *andava l' avolo alla cerca* ‘went the grandfather in quest of alms’ → “his own grandfather went round a-begging”: the subject precedes the verb, the article becomes the emphatic possessive “his own”, and the prepositional phrase becomes “a-begging”, with “round” supplied.

> 64 Sariasi Montemurlo ancor dei Conti,  
> 65 sariansi i Cerchi nel pivier d' Acone,  
> 66 e forse in Val di Greve i Buondelmonti.  

Still would Montemurlo pertain unto the Conti, still were the Cerchi in Acone parish, and perchance in Valdigreve were still the Buondelmonti.

- *Sariasi Montemurlo ancor dei Conti* ‘Montemurlo would still be the Conti’s’ → “Still would Montemurlo pertain unto the Conti”: “Still” moves to the front; being someone’s is expressed by a verb of belonging with “unto”.
- *sariansi i Cerchi* ‘the Cerchi would be’ → “still were the Cerchi”: irrealis “were” replaces the conditional, and “still” is carried over from the preceding clause.
- *nel pivier d' Acone* ‘in the parish of Acone’ → “in Acone parish”: the place name becomes an attributive modifier, and the article disappears.
- *e forse in Val di Greve i Buondelmonti* ‘and perhaps in Val di Greve the Buondelmonti’ → “and perchance in Valdigreve were still the Buondelmonti”: the verb understood in Italian is expressed, and “still” is supplied here too.

> 67 Sempre la confusion delle persone  
> 68 principio fu del mal della cittade,  
> 69 come del corpo il cibo che s' appone.  

Ever was mingling of persons the source of the city’s woes, as piled on food is of the body’s.

- *la confusion delle persone* ‘the mixture of the persons’ → “mingling of persons”: a verbal noun suggests the process of mixing, and both definite articles drop out.
- *principio fu del mal della cittade* ‘beginning was of the ill of the city’ → “was mingling of persons the source of the city’s woes”: the copula moves ahead of the subject instead of following the predicate noun. ‘Beginning’ becomes “the source”, singular ‘ill’ becomes plural “woes”, and the city is expressed by a preceding possessive.
- *come del corpo il cibo che s' appone* ‘as, of the body, the food that is added’ → “as piled on food is of the body’s”: the relative clause becomes a participial modifier before “food”, with “piled on” for ‘added’ and no article. The copula is supplied, and “of the body’s” moves to the end, leaving “woes” understood.

> 70 E cieco toro più avaccio cade  
> 71 che 'l cieco agnello, e molte volte taglia  
> 72 più e meglio una che le cinque spade.  

And a blind bull falleth more presently than a blind lamb, and many a time cutteth one sword better and more than five.

- *cieco toro* ‘blind bull’ → “a blind bull”: English supplies the indefinite article for the generic singular.
- *più avaccio cade* ‘sooner falls’ → “falleth more presently”: the comparative adverb follows the verb and is expressed by “more” with “presently” in its sense of ‘quickly’.
- *'l cieco agnello* ‘the blind lamb’ → “a blind lamb”: the generic definite article becomes indefinite.
- *molte volte* ‘many times’ → “many a time”: a plural noun becomes singular in the English idiom.
- *taglia più e meglio una che le cinque spade* ‘cuts more and better one than the five swords’ → “cutteth one sword better and more than five”: the subject moves directly after the verb, the two comparatives exchange places, and “sword” is expressed with “one” instead of with “five”; the definite article before ‘five’ drops out.

> 73 Se tu riguardi Luni ed Urbisaglia  
> 74 come son ite, e come se ne vanno  
> 75 di retro ad esse Chiusi e Sinigaglia;  

If thou regard Luni and Urbisaglia, how they have perished, and how are following them Chiusi and Sinigaglia;

- *Se tu riguardi Luni ed Urbisaglia come son ite* ‘if thou consider Luni and Urbisaglia, how they are gone’ → “If thou regard Luni and Urbisaglia, how they have perished”: “regard” has the uninflected form of an English subjunctive after “If”; ‘are gone’ is interpreted as destruction and rendered with a *have*-perfect.
- *come se ne vanno di retro ad esse* ‘how they go away behind them’ → “how are following them”: the pronominal verb of going and the phrase ‘behind them’ become a single progressive verb with a direct object; ‘away’ is not separately expressed.

> 76 udir come le schiatte si disfanno,  
> 77 non ti parrà nuova cosa nè forte,  
> 78 poscia che le cittadi termine hanno.  

it shall not seem a novel or hard thing to hear how families undo themselves, since even cities have their term.

- *udir come le schiatte si disfanno* ‘to hear how the lineages come apart’ → “to hear how families undo themselves”: the infinitive subject moves after the predicate, with anticipatory “it” in its place. “Families” renders ‘lineages’ without the generic article, and the pronominal verb is taken explicitly as reflexive.
- *non ti parrà nuova cosa nè forte* ‘will not seem to thee a new thing nor a hard one’ → “it shall not seem a novel or hard thing”: the person to whom it seems so is omitted, “novel” renders ‘new’, and “or” coordinates the two adjectives before their common noun instead of adding the second adjective after it.
- *le cittadi termine hanno* ‘the cities an end have’ → “even cities have their term”: the generic article disappears, “even” is supplied, and the object follows the verb, with the possessive “their” supplied before “term”.

## Lines 79–111 — The Rise and Fall of Florentine Houses

> 79 Le vostre cose tutte hanno lor morte,  
> 80 sì come voi; ma celasi in alcuna  
> 81 che dura molto, e le vite son corte.  

Your affairs all have their death, even as have ye; but in such an one as long endureth, it escapeth note because your lives are short.

- *cose* ‘things’ → “affairs”: a more specific noun.
- *sì come voi* ‘just as you’ → “even as have ye”: the verb understood in the Italian comparison is expressed.
- *celasi* ‘it hides itself’ → “it escapeth note”: the reflexive becomes a verb with an object, and follows the phrase saying where death goes unnoticed.
- *in alcuna che dura molto* ‘in one that endures long’ → “in such an one as long endureth”: the simple indefinite and relative become a correlative construction; the adverb precedes the verb.
- *e le vite son corte* ‘and the lives are short’ → “because your lives are short”: coordination becomes an explicit statement of cause, and a possessive replaces the definite article.

> 82 E come il volger del ciel della luna  
> 83 copre ed iscopre i liti senza posa,  
> 84 così fa di Fiorenza la fortuna;  

And as the rolling of the lunar heaven covereth and layeth bare the shores incessantly, so fortune doth to Florence;

- *il volger* ‘the turning’ → “the rolling”: a different word for the motion.
- *del ciel della luna* ‘of the heaven of the moon’ → “of the lunar heaven”: the second prepositional phrase becomes an adjective before the noun.
- *iscopre* ‘uncovers’ → “layeth bare”: a verb and complement replace the single verb.
- *senza posa* ‘without pause’ → “incessantly”: the prepositional phrase becomes an adverb.
- *così fa di Fiorenza la fortuna* ‘so does with Florence fortune’ → “so fortune doth to Florence”: the subject moves before the verb and loses its article; Florence is introduced as the object of the action by “to”.

> 85 per che non dee parer mirabil cosa  
> 86 ciò ch' io dirò degli alti Fiorentini,  
> 87 onde la fama nel tempo è nascosa.  

wherefore it should appear no wondrous thing which I shall tell of the exalted Florentines whose fame lieth concealed by time.

- *non dee parer mirabil cosa ciò ch' io dirò* ‘ought not to seem a wondrous thing that which I shall tell’ → “it should appear no wondrous thing which I shall tell”: English opens the clause with anticipatory “it” rather than making the postponed ‘that which I shall tell’ its subject; the negative passes from the verb to the noun phrase.
- *alti* ‘high’ → “exalted”: the adjective is rendered in its figurative sense.
- *onde la fama* ‘of whom the fame’ → “whose fame”: a possessive relative replaces the relative adverb and article.
- *nel tempo è nascosa* ‘in time is hidden’ → “lieth concealed by time”: the prepositional phrase moves to the end and makes time the agent rather than the setting; “lieth” replaces ‘is’.

> 88 Io vidi gli Ughi, e vidi i Catellini,  
> 89 Filippi, Greci, Ormanni ed Alberichi,  
> 90 già nel calare, illustri cittadini;  

I have seen the Ughi, seen the Catellini, Filippi, Greci, Ormanni, and Alberichi, illustrious citizens, already in decline;

- *Io vidi* ‘I saw’ → “I have seen”: the simple past becomes a present perfect.
- *e vidi* ‘and I saw’ → “seen”: the conjunction drops out, and the second participle shares the first auxiliary.
- *già nel calare* ‘already in the declining’ → “already in decline”: a noun replaces the substantivized infinitive, and the phrase moves after the description of the citizens.

> 91 e vidi così grandi come antichi,  
> 92 con quel della Sannella, quel dell' Arca,  
> 93 e Soldanieri, ed Ardinghi, e Bostichi.  

I have seen, even as great as ancient, with him of the Sannella, him of the Arca, and Soldanieri and Ardinghi and Bostichi.

- *e vidi* ‘and I saw’ → “I have seen”: the conjunction is omitted and the present perfect replaces the simple past.
- *così grandi come antichi* ‘as great as ancient’ → “even as great as ancient”: “even” adds emphasis.

> 94 Sopra la porta, che al presente è carca  
> 95 di nuova fellonia di tanto peso  
> 96 che tosto fia jattura della barca,  

Over the gate which is now laden with new felony of so great weight, that soon ’twill be the wrecking of the barque,

- *al presente è carca* ‘at present is laden’ → “is now laden”: the temporal phrase becomes an adverb placed after the finite verb.
- *di nuova fellonia* ‘of new felony’ → “with new felony”: English uses “with” for what constitutes the load.
- *di tanto peso* ‘of so much weight’ → “of so great weight”: amount is expressed as magnitude.
- *jattura della barca* ‘loss of the barque’ → “the wrecking of the barque”: the more specific action noun presents the loss as a wrecking and takes a definite article.

> 97 erano i Ravignani, ond' è disceso  
> 98 il conte Guido, e qualunque del nome  
> 99 dell' alto Bellincion ha poscia preso.  

were the Ravignani, whence descendeth the County Guy, and whoso since hath taken lofty Bellincione’s name.

- *è disceso* ‘has descended’ → “descendeth”: the compound past becomes a simple present.
- *qualunque del nome dell' alto Bellincion ha poscia preso* ‘whoever of the name of the lofty Bellincione has afterwards taken’ → “whoso since hath taken lofty Bellincione’s name”: the partitive phrase becomes a direct object after the verb; a possessive replaces ‘of’ and the epithet loses its article. The temporal adverb moves before the auxiliary.

> 100 Quel della Pressa sapeva già come  
> 101 regger si vuole, ed avea Galigaio  
> 102 dorata in casa sua già l' elsa e il pome.  

The Della Pressa knew already how to govern, and Galigaio in his mansion already had the hilt and pummel gilt.

- *Quel della Pressa* ‘he of the Pressa’ → “The Della Pressa”: the family designation stands directly for its member.
- *come regger si vuole* ‘how one ought to govern’ → “how to govern”: the impersonal expression of what is requisite is reduced to an infinitive.
- *ed avea Galigaio* ‘and had Galigaio’ → “and Galigaio” … “had”: the subject moves before the verb, which now follows the expressions of place and time.
- *dorata* ‘gilded’ → “gilt”: the participle moves from before the locative phrase and the objects to after both objects.
- *in casa sua* ‘in his house’ → “in his mansion”: the noun becomes more specific and the possessive moves before it.
- *l' elsa e il pome* ‘the hilt and the pummel’ → “the hilt and pummel”: one article serves both nouns.

> 103 Grande era già la colonna del Vaio,  
> 104 Sacchetti, Giuochi, Fifanti e Barucci,  
> 105 e Galli, e quei che arrossan per lo staio.  

Great already were the Vair column, Sacchetti, Giuochi, Fifanti, and Barucci; and Galli, and they who blush red for the bushel.

- *era già* ‘was already’ → “already were”: the adverb precedes the verb, and the verb agrees with the whole list rather than its first member.
- *la colonna del Vaio* ‘the column of the vair’ → “the Vair column”: the prepositional phrase becomes an attributive noun.
- *arrossan* ‘turn red’ → “blush red”: English expresses the change of colour with a verb of blushing and an explicit colour adjective.

> 106 Lo ceppo, di che nacquero i Calfucci,  
> 107 era già grande, e già erano tratti  
> 108 alle curule Sizii ed Arrigucci.  

The stock whence the Calfucci sprang was great already, and already drawn to curule office were Sizii and Arrigucci.

- *di che nacquero i Calfucci* ‘from which were born the Calfucci’ → “whence the Calfucci sprang”: a relative adverb replaces preposition and relative pronoun; the subject precedes the verb, and springing replaces birth.
- *era già grande* ‘was already great’ → “was great already”: the adverb moves after the adjective.
- *erano tratti alle curule* ‘were drawn to the curule chairs’ → “drawn to curule office were”: the auxiliary moves after the complement; the substantivized adjective, understood as plural seats, is rendered by an adjective modifying singular “office”.

> 109 O quali io vidi quei che son disfatti  
> 110 per lor superbia! e le palle dell' oro  

Oh, how great have I seen those now undone by their pride!

- *quali* ‘of what sort’ → “how great”: the exclamation of quality becomes one of degree, with greatness specified.
- *io vidi* ‘I saw’ → “have I seen”: the present perfect replaces the simple past, with subject–auxiliary inversion.
- *quei che son disfatti* ‘those who are undone’ → “those now undone”: the finite relative clause becomes a participial modifier, and “now” is supplied.
- *per lor superbia* ‘through their pride’ → “by their pride”: “by” marks pride as the agent of their undoing.

> 111 fiorian Fiorenza in tutti suoi gran fatti.  

And the balls of gold adorned Florence in all her mighty feats.

- *e le palle dell' oro* ‘and the balls of the gold’ → “And the balls of gold”: the article before the material is dropped. This phrase begins in line 110 and belongs syntactically to the verb in 111.
- *fiorian* ‘were flowering’ → “adorned”: the floral verb is rendered by its transitive sense of ornamenting; the English simple past does not mark the imperfect aspect.
- *gran fatti* ‘great deeds’ → “mighty feats”: the adjective and noun are both rendered more emphatically.

## Lines 112–144 — Florentine Houses and Buondelmonte’s Broken Betrothal

> 112 Così facean li padri di coloro  
> 113 che, sempre che la vostra chiesa vaca,  
> 114 si fanno grassi stando a consistoro.  

So did their fathers who, whene’er your church is vacant, stand guzzling in consistory.

- *li padri di coloro che* ‘the fathers of those who’ → “their fathers who”: a preceding possessive replaces the phrase following ‘fathers’; ‘those’ is no longer expressed separately as the antecedent of “who”.
- *sempre che* ‘every time that’ → “whene’er”: one conjunction expresses the recurrence.
- *vaca* ‘falls vacant’ → “is vacant”: a single verb is rendered by a copula and an adjective.
- *si fanno grassi stando* ‘make themselves fat while remaining’ → “stand guzzling”: the gerund supplies the finite “stand”, while the finite expression of fattening becomes a participle naming the activity rather than its result.

> 115 L' oltracotata schiatta, che s' indraca  
> 116 retro a chi fugge, ed a chi mostra il dente  
> 117 o ver la borsa com' agnel si placa,  

The outrageous tribe that playeth dragon after whoso fleeth, and to whoso showeth tooth—or purse—is quiet as a lamb,

- *s’ indraca* ‘turns itself into a dragon’ → “playeth dragon”: the reflexive verb of becoming is rendered as a verb of acting a part, with a noun.
- *il dente o ver la borsa* ‘the tooth or else the purse’ → “tooth—or purse”: the articles drop out, and the alternative is marked by a simple “or”.
- *com’ agnel si placa* ‘like a lamb grows calm’ → “is quiet as a lamb”: a change of state becomes a state; the comparison moves after the predicate and acquires an article.

> 118 già venia su, ma di picciola gente,  
> 119 sì che non piacque ad Ubertin Donato  
> 120 che poi il suocero il fe' lor parente.  

was coming up already, but from humble folk, so that it pleased not Ubertin Donato when his father-in-law made him their relative.

- *già venia su* ‘already was coming up’ → “was coming up already”: the adverb moves after the verb phrase.
- *di picciola gente* ‘of little people’ → “from humble folk”: the preposition explicitly marks origin, and ‘little’ is rendered in the sense of social standing.
- *non piacque ad Ubertin Donato* ‘did not please Ubertin Donato’ → “it pleased not Ubertin Donato”: an anticipatory “it” is supplied, the negative follows the verb, and the person affected is a direct object rather than a complement with *ad*.
- *che poi* ‘that afterwards’ → “when”: the clause stating what displeased him becomes a clause of time, without a separate equivalent for ‘afterwards’.
- *il suocero* ‘the father-in-law’ → “his father-in-law”: a possessive replaces the article.
- *il fe’* ‘him made’ → “made him”: the object pronoun follows the verb.

> 121 Già era il Caponsacco nel mercato  
> 122 disceso giù da Fiesole, e già era  

Already Caponsacco had come down from Fiesole into the market-place;

- *Già era il Caponsacco nel mercato disceso giù da Fiesole* ‘already was the Caponsacco into the market descended down from Fiesole’ → “Already Caponsacco had come down from Fiesole into the market-place”: the name loses its article and precedes the auxiliary; English forms the pluperfect with “had” rather than ‘was’. “Come down” gathers the two Italian indications of descent into one expression, and the destination moves from before the participle to after the place of origin.

> 123 buon cittadino Giuda ed Infangato.  

and good citizens already were Giuda and Infangato.

- *e già era buon cittadino Giuda ed Infangato* ‘and already was a good citizen Giuda and Infangato’ → “and good citizens already were Giuda and Infangato”: the predicate moves ahead of the adverb and verb; both predicate and verb become plural to agree with the two names. The conjunction and adverb are in the preceding Italian line.

> 124 Io dirò cosa incredibile e vera;  

I will tell a thing incredible but true:

- *e vera* ‘and true’ → “but true”: coordination becomes explicit contrast.

> 125 nel picciol cerchio s' entrava per porta,  
> 126 che si nomava da quei della Pera.  

the little circuit was entered by a gate named after them of Pera.

- *nel picciol cerchio s’ entrava* ‘into the little circuit one entered’ → “the little circuit was entered”: the imperfect impersonal construction becomes a simple past passive, with the destination as subject.
- *per porta* ‘through a gate’ → “by a gate”: the English preposition marks the means of entry.
- *che si nomava* ‘which was called’ → “named”: the finite relative clause is reduced to a participle.
- *da quei della Pera* ‘from those of the Pera’ → “after them of Pera”: “after” is the English idiom for the source of a name; a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative, and the article in *della* drops out.

> 127 Ciascun che della bella insegna porta  
> 128 del gran barone, il cui nome e il cui pregio  
> 129 la festa di Tommaso riconforta,  

Each one who beareth aught of the fair arms of the great baron whose name and worth the festival of Thomas keepeth living,

- *della bella insegna porta* ‘of the fair ensign bears’ → “beareth aught of the fair arms”: the verb moves before its complement, “aught” makes the partitive explicit, and the singular emblem becomes the English heraldic plural.
- *il cui nome e il cui pregio* ‘whose name and whose worth’ → “whose name and worth”: one possessive relative serves for both nouns.
- *la festa di Tommaso* ‘the feast of Thomas’ → “the festival of Thomas”: “festival” is used for ‘feast’.
- *riconforta* ‘revives’ → “keepeth living”: a verb of renewal becomes a phrase expressing continued life.

> 130 da esso ebbe milizia e privilegio;  

from him derived knighthood and privilege;

- *ebbe* ‘had’ → “derived”: a verb of having or receiving becomes one expressly denoting derivation.
- *milizia* ‘military estate’ → “knighthood”: the particular dignity is named.

> 131 avvenga che col popol si raduni  
> 132 oggi colui che la fascia col fregio.  

though he who fringeth it around hath joined him now unto the people.

- *avvenga che* ‘albeit that’ → “though”: one conjunction replaces the concessive phrase.
- *col popol si raduni oggi* ‘with the people joins himself today’ → “hath joined him now unto the people”: the present subjunctive becomes a present perfect; the reflexive is expressed by the older “him”. The complement moves after the verb and takes “unto” rather than ‘with’; ‘today’ becomes “now”.
- *colui che la fascia col fregio* ‘he who bands it with the fringe’ → “he who fringeth it around”: the subject moves before its predicate; the noun for the edging supplies the English verb, while “around” conveys the encircling expressed by the Italian verb.

> 133 Già eran Gualterotti ed Importuni;  

Already there were Gualterotti and Importuni;

- *eran* ‘were’ → “there were”: existential “there” is supplied.

> 134 ed ancor saria Borgo più quieto,  
> 135 se di nuovi vicin fosser digiuni.  

and still were Borgo a more quiet spot, if from new neighbours they were still afasting.

- *ancor saria Borgo più quieto* ‘still would Borgo be quieter’ → “still were Borgo a more quiet spot”: hypothetical “were” renders the conditional; “a … spot” is supplied, changing the predicative adjective into a modifier of a noun.
- *se di nuovi vicin fosser digiuni* ‘if of new neighbours they were fasting’ → “if from new neighbours they were still afasting”: “from” answers to the English expression for abstaining; the predicate adjective becomes “afasting”, and “still” is supplied a second time.

> 136 La casa di che nacque il vostro fleto,  
> 137 per lo giusto disdegno che v' ha morti  
> 138 e posto fine al vostro viver lieto,  

The house from which your wailing sprang, because of the just anger which hath slain you and placed a term upon your joyous life,

- *di che nacque il vostro fleto* ‘from which was born your lamentation’ → “from which your wailing sprang”: the subject moves before the verb; the image of birth becomes one of springing from a source, and the noun is rendered by an English verbal noun.
- *per lo giusto disdegno* ‘through the just indignation’ → “because of the just anger”: the causal relation is explicit, and “anger” is less specific than ‘indignation’.
- *che v’ ha morti* ‘which you has killed’ → “which hath slain you”: the object follows the participle, which in English does not mark agreement with it.
- *posto fine al vostro viver lieto* ‘put an end to your living joyously’ → “placed a term upon your joyous life”: the expression for ending becomes one of setting a limit, with “upon” for ‘to’; the substantival infinitive becomes “life”, with its qualifier before it.

> 139 era onorata ed essa e suoi consorti.  

was honoured, it and its associates.

- *ed essa e suoi consorti* ‘both it and its associates’ → “it and its associates”: the first member of the paired conjunction is unexpressed.

> 140 O Buondelmonte, quanto mal fuggisti  
> 141 le nozze sue per gli altrui conforti!  

Oh Buondelmonte, how ill didst thou flee its nuptials at the prompting of another!

- *fuggisti* ‘thou fleddest’ → “didst thou flee”: the simple past is expressed with an inverted auxiliary and an infinitive.
- *le nozze sue* ‘the nuptials of it’ → “its nuptials”: the postposed possessive moves before the noun.
- *per gli altrui conforti* ‘through another’s urgings’ → “at the prompting of another”: plural urgings become a singular action noun, the possessor follows it, and the preposition changes with the expression.

> 142 Molti sarebbon lieti, che son tristi,  
> 143 se Dio t' avesse conceduto ad Ema  
> 144 la prima volta che a città venisti.  

Joyous had many been who now are sad, had God committed thee unto the Ema the first time that thou camest to the city.

- *Molti sarebbon lieti* ‘many would be joyous’ → “Joyous had many been”: the predicate is fronted, and the present conditional becomes an inverted past counterfactual.
- *che son tristi* ‘who are sad’ → “who now are sad”: “now” is supplied to mark the contrast.
- *se Dio t’ avesse conceduto ad Ema* ‘if God thee had granted to Ema’ → “had God committed thee unto the Ema”: inversion replaces ‘if’, the object pronoun moves after the participle, “committed” renders granting as giving over, and the river name takes an article.
- *a città venisti* ‘to city thou camest’ → “thou camest to the city”: the destination follows the verb and acquires an article.

## Lines 145–154 — Florence’s Last Peace and Its Lily

> 145 Ma conveniasi a quella pietra scema  
> 146 che guarda il ponte, che Fiorenza fesse  
> 147 vittima nella sua pace postrema.  

But to that mutilated stone which guardeth the bridge ’twas meet that Florence should give a victim in her last time of peace.

- *conveniasi* ‘it was fitting’ → “’twas meet”: the impersonal verb becomes a copula and an adjective, and moves from before the phrase naming the stone to after its relative clause.
- *a quella pietra scema* ‘to that stone shorn’ → “to that mutilated stone”: the adjective comes before the noun.
- *fesse vittima* ‘should make a victim’ → “should give a victim”: the imperfect subjunctive is expressed with “should”, and making a victim becomes giving one.
- *nella sua pace postrema* ‘in her peace last’ → “in her last time of peace”: “time” is supplied as the noun qualified by “last”, and “peace” becomes its complement.

> 148 Con queste genti, e con altre con esse,  
> 149 vid' io Fiorenza in sì fatto riposo,  
> 150 che non avea cagion onde piangesse;  

With these folk, and with others with them, did I see Florence in such full repose, she had not cause for wailing;

- *queste genti* ‘these peoples’ → “these folk”: the plural noun is rendered by the collective “folk”.
- *vid’ io* ‘saw I’ → “did I see”: the Italian verb precedes its subject; English puts a past-tense auxiliary before the subject and the main verb after it.
- *in sì fatto riposo* ‘in repose of such a kind’ → “in such full repose”: the phrase of kind is rendered as one of degree, with “full” supplied.
- *che non avea cagion* ‘that she had no cause’ → “she had not cause”: the conjunction introducing the result is omitted, and the negative follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *onde piangesse* ‘for which she should weep’ → “for wailing”: the relative construction with a finite subjunctive becomes a preposition with a gerund; “wailing” is stronger than ‘weeping’.

> 151 con queste genti vid' io glorioso  
> 152 e giusto il popol suo, tanto che il giglio  
> 153 non era ad asta mai posto a ritroso,  
> 154 nè per division fatto vermiglio.”  

with these folk I saw her people so glorious and so just, ne’er was the lily on the shaft reversed, nor yet by faction dyed vermilion.”

- *queste genti* ‘these peoples’ → “these folk”: the plural noun is again rendered by the collective “folk”.
- *vid’ io* ‘saw I’ → “I saw”: the subject precedes the verb.
- *glorioso e giusto il popol suo, tanto che* ‘glorious and just the people of hers, to such a degree that’ → “her people so glorious and so just,”: the possessive comes before the noun, and the two adjectives follow the object they describe. The degree expressed afterwards by *tanto* is marked by “so” before each adjective; the conjunction introducing the result is omitted.
- *il giglio non era … mai* ‘the lily was not … ever’ → “ne’er was the lily”: the two parts of the negative become a single adverb, placed first, with the verb then preceding its subject.
- *ad asta* ‘at a shaft’ → “on the shaft”: the preposition changes, and the article, absent in Italian, is supplied as definite.
- *posto a ritroso* ‘placed backwards’ → “reversed”: the participle and its adverbial phrase become a single participle.
- *nè* ‘nor’ → “nor yet”: “yet” is supplied.
- *per division* ‘through division’ → “by faction”: division is rendered more specifically as faction, with “by” marking the cause.
- *fatto vermiglio* ‘made vermilion’ → “dyed vermilion”: the general participle is replaced by one specific to colouring.

## Summary — Close in Imagery, Freer in Syntax

Wicksteed often keeps Dante’s sequence of images while rearranging the words within a clause. The subject of *s’ empie*, for instance, comes after the verb and its complement in Italian, but “my mind” precedes “is filled with gladness”. Elsewhere he preserves a marked order by different means: “did I see” answers to *vid’ io* by placing the auxiliary, rather than the main verb, before the subject. Two routine adjustments generally pass unremarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where Italian gives the subject by the verb ending alone, and drops the article before a possessive, as in *la mente mia*, “my mind”.

Changes of construction often redistribute the sense rather than alter it. The gerund *ridendo* becomes a finite verb in “smiled, and”, while the finite relative *che si segnaro* contracts to “recorded”. The negative in *non si torce* is carried by a prefix in “is unwarped”. Wicksteed sometimes supplies a relation left implicit, as when “from us” specifies the sense of *scevra*, ‘apart’; sometimes he leaves one unexpressed, as when *dissemi*, ‘he said to me’, becomes “he said”.

The choice of words can sharpen a judgment: *poca*, ‘little’, becomes “puny”, and *mista*, ‘mixed’, becomes “contaminated”. It can also change an image, as when *primizia*, ‘firstfruit’, becomes “stock from which I spring”. Yet the mantle and shears, the coal and flame, and the lily and its colour all remain close to the Italian. So do emphatic repetitions: the threefold *voi* is answered by “Ye”, “ye”, “ye”, and *con queste genti* returns in both its places as “with these folk”.
