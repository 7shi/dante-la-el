# Paradiso 24

## Lines 1–15 — Beatrice’s Appeal and the Wheeling Souls

> 1 “O sodalizio eletto alla gran cena  
> 2 del benedetto agnello, il qual vi ciba  
> 3 sì che la vostra voglia è sempre piena;  

“O fellowship elect to the great supper of the blessed Lamb, who feedeth you in such fashion that your desire ever is fulfilled;

- *vi ciba* ‘you he feeds’ → “feedeth you”: the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *sì che* ‘so that’ → “in such fashion that”: the adverb is expanded into a phrase of manner.
- *è sempre piena* ‘is always full’ → “ever is fulfilled”: the adverb moves before the verb, and the adjective of fullness becomes a participle of fulfilment.

> 4 se per grazia di Dio questi preliba  
> 5 di quel che cade della vostra mensa,  
> 6 prima che morte tempo gli prescriba,  

if by the grace of God this man foretasteth of that which falleth from your table ere death prescribe the time to him,

- *per grazia di Dio* ‘by grace of God’ → “by the grace of God”: the definite article is supplied before “grace”.
- *questi* ‘this one’ → “this man”: the masculine demonstrative pronoun becomes a demonstrative with an explicit noun.
- *tempo gli prescriba* ‘time to him prescribe’ → “prescribe the time to him”: both objects move after the verb, and “time” takes a definite article.

> 7 ponete mente all' affezione immensa,  
> 8 e roratelo alquanto: voi bevete  
> 9 sempre del fonte onde vien quel ch' ei pensa.”  

give heed to his unmeasured yearning and bedew him somewhat: ye drink ever of the fountain whence floweth that on which his thought is fixed.”

- *ponete mente* ‘set your mind’ → “give heed”: one idiom of attention replaces another.
- *all' affezione immensa* ‘to the affection immense’ → “to his unmeasured yearning”: the article becomes a possessive; the feeling is specified as yearning, and the adjective is recast and placed before the noun.
- *vien* ‘comes’ → “floweth”: the general verb of motion is made specific to the fountain.
- *quel ch' ei pensa* ‘that which he thinks’ → “that on which his thought is fixed”: the object of thinking becomes the object of a preposition; the subject and active verb become a possessive with a noun, and a passive predicate is supplied.

> 10 Così Beatrice: e quelle anime liete  
> 11 si fero spere sopra fissi poli,  
> 12 fiammando forte a guisa di comete.  

Thus Beatrice: and those glad souls made themselves spheres upon fixed poles, outflaming mightily like unto comets.

- *anime liete* ‘souls glad’ → “glad souls”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *si fero* ‘themselves they made’ → “made themselves”: the reflexive pronoun follows the verb.
- *fiammando* ‘flaming’ → “outflaming”: the English adds a prefix suggesting flame streaming out.
- *a guisa di* ‘in the manner of’ → “like unto”: the noun phrase becomes a direct expression of likeness.

> 13 E come cerchi in tempra d' oriuoli  
> 14 si giran sì che il primo, a chi pon mente,  
> 15 quieto pare, e l' ultimo che voli,  

And even as wheels in harmony of clock-work so turn that the first, to whoso noteth it, seemeth still, and the last to fly,

- *come* ‘as’ → “even as”: “even” is supplied to emphasize the comparison.
- *cerchi* ‘circles’ → “wheels”: the circular parts are named by their mechanical function.
- *in tempra d' oriuoli* ‘in the adjustment of clocks’ → “in harmony of clock-work”: adjustment is expressed as harmony, and the plural name for the instruments becomes a collective name for their mechanism.
- *si giran sì* ‘turn themselves so’ → “so turn”: the pronominal verb is rendered by an intransitive verb, and the adverb moves before it.
- *a chi pon mente* ‘to whoever gives heed’ → “to whoso noteth it”: the idiom becomes a transitive verb with an explicit object.
- *quieto pare* ‘still seems’ → “seemeth still”: the predicate adjective moves after the verb.
- *che voli* ‘that it fly’ → “to fly”: the finite subjunctive clause becomes an infinitive, with the verb of seeming understood.

## Lines 16–48 — A Flame Circles Beatrice, Who Asks It to Examine Dante on Faith

> 16 così quelle carole differente-  
> 17 mente danzando, della sua ricchezza  
> 18 mi si facean stimar, veloci e lente.  

so did these carols with their differing whirl, or swift or slow, make me deem of their riches.

- *così quelle carole* ‘so those carols’ → “so did these carols”: the demonstrative changes from distant to near, and an auxiliary precedes the subject.
- *differentemente danzando* ‘differently dancing’ → “with their differing whirl”: the participle becomes a noun governed by a preposition, the adverb becomes its modifier, and a possessive is supplied.
- *veloci e lente* ‘swift and slow’ → “or swift or slow”: the final adjectives are brought forward to follow “whirl”, and the conjunction becomes a pair of alternatives.
- *della sua ricchezza mi si facean stimar* ‘of its richness made themselves to be appraised by me’ → “make me deem of their riches”: the reflexive construction is recast with “me” as the one made to judge; the phrase for what is judged moves to the end. The singular possessive, referring to each in turn, and the singular noun become plural.

> 19 Di quella ch' io notai di più bellezza  
> 20 vid' io uscire un foco sì felice,  
> 21 che nullo vi lasciò di più chiarezza;  

From the one I noted of most beauty, I saw issue a so blissful flame it left none there of greater brightness;

- *Di quella ch' io notai di più bellezza* ‘from that which I noted of more beauty’ → “From the one I noted of most beauty”: “the one” stands for the demonstrative, the object relative is unexpressed, and the comparative is rendered as a superlative.
- *vid' io* ‘saw I’ → “I saw”: the subject moves before the verb.
- *un foco sì felice* ‘a fire so blissful’ → “a so blissful flame”: “flame” replaces ‘fire’, and the qualified adjective precedes the noun.
- *che nullo vi lasciò* ‘that none there it left’ → “it left none there”: the result conjunction is omitted, and the verb precedes its object and the adverb.
- *di più chiarezza* ‘of more brightness’ → “of greater brightness”: degree is expressed by the comparative of ‘great’.

> 22 e tre fiate intorno di Beatrice  
> 23 si volse con un canto tanto divo,  
> 24 che la mia fantasia nol mi ridice;  

and thrice round Beatrice did it sweep with so divine a song, my fantasy repeateth it not to me;

- *tre fiate* ‘three times’ → “thrice”: numeral and noun become a single adverb.
- *si volse* ‘it turned itself’ → “did it sweep”: a verb of sweeping motion replaces the reflexive verb of turning, and the auxiliary precedes the subject.
- *un canto tanto divo* ‘a song so divine’ → “so divine a song”: the qualified adjective moves before the article and noun.
- *che la mia fantasia nol mi ridice* ‘that my fantasy does not tell it again to me’ → “my fantasy repeateth it not to me”: the result conjunction is omitted, ‘tell again’ becomes “repeateth”, and the object, negative, and indirect object follow the verb.

> 25 però salta la penna, e non lo scrivo,  

wherefore my pen leapeth, and I write it not:

- *salta la penna* ‘leaps the pen’ → “my pen leapeth”: the subject moves before the verb and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *non lo scrivo* ‘not it I write’ → “I write it not”: the object and negative follow the verb, in the reverse of their Italian order.

> 26 chè l' imagine nostra a cotai pieghe,  
> 27 non che il parlare, è troppo color vivo.  

for such folds our imagination, not only our speech, is too vivid colouring.

- *chè l' imagine nostra a cotai pieghe* ‘because the imagination of ours for such folds’ → “for such folds our imagination”: the phrase for the folds moves before the subject, and the possessive moves before the noun. The causal conjunction has no separate equivalent before “for such folds”.
- *non che il parlare* ‘not to say speaking’ → “not only our speech”: the elliptical comparison becomes an additive phrase, and the substantive infinitive becomes a noun with a supplied possessive.
- *troppo color vivo* ‘too vivid a colour’ → “too vivid colouring”: “colouring” replaces the count noun, and the adjective moves before it.

> 28 “O santa suora mia, che sì ne preghe  
> 29 devota, per lo tuo ardente affetto  
> 30 da quella bella spera mi disleghe.”  

“O holy sister mine, who thus dost pray to us devoutly, by thy glowing love thou dost unloosen me from this fair sphere.”

- *santa suora mia* ‘holy sister of mine’ → “holy sister mine”: English retains the postposed possessive without a linking preposition.
- *sì ne preghe* ‘thus us thou entreatest’ → “thus dost pray to us”: the verb takes a prepositional complement instead of a direct object, which moves after the verb; “dost” is supplied.
- *devota* ‘devout’ → “devoutly”: the adjective describing the person addressed becomes an adverb modifying her prayer.
- *per lo tuo ardente affetto* ‘through thy burning affection’ → “by thy glowing love”: the preposition changes, and both the image of burning and the noun for the feeling are rephrased.
- *da quella bella spera mi disleghe* ‘from that fair sphere me thou unbindest’ → “thou dost unloosen me from this fair sphere”: the verb precedes the object, the source phrase moves to the end, and the distant demonstrative becomes a near one.

> 31 Poscia, fermato il foco benedetto,  
> 32 alla mia donna dirizzò lo spiro,  
> 33 che favellò così, com' io ho detto.  

The breath that thus discoursed, as I have written down, was turned unto my Lady by that blessed flame so soon as it had stayed.

- *Poscia, fermato il foco benedetto* ‘then, the blessed fire having stopped’ → “by that blessed flame so soon as it had stayed”: the opening absolute construction moves to the end. Its noun becomes the agent of the English passive, with a demonstrative for the article and “flame” for ‘fire’; the participle becomes a finite past perfect, and simple succession becomes immediate succession.
- *alla mia donna dirizzò lo spiro* ‘unto my lady it directed the breath’ → “The breath” … “was turned unto my Lady”: the direct object becomes the subject of a passive, ‘directed’ becomes “turned”, and the phrase of direction follows the verb.
- *che favellò così* ‘which spoke thus’ → “that thus discoursed”: the adverb precedes the verb, and “discoursed” replaces ‘spoke’.
- *com' io ho detto* ‘as I have said’ → “as I have written down”: saying becomes writing.

> 34 Ed ella: “O luce eterna del gran viro,  
> 35 a cui nostro Signor lasciò le chiavi,  
> 36 ch' ei portò giù, di questo gaudio miro,  

And she: “O light eternal of that great man to whom our Lord gave up the keys he brought down of this wondrous joy,

- *del gran viro* ‘of the great man’ → “of that great man”: a demonstrative replaces the definite article.
- *lasciò* ‘left’ → “gave up”: leaving is rendered as a handing over.
- *ch' ei portò giù* ‘which he brought down’ → “he brought down”: the object relative is unexpressed.
- *di questo gaudio miro* ‘of this joy wondrous’ → “of this wondrous joy”: the adjective moves before the noun.

> 37 tenta costui dei punti lievi e gravi,  
> 38 come ti piace, intorno della fede,  
> 39 per la qual tu su per lo mare andavi.  

test this man here on the points both light and grave, as it doth please thee, anent the faith whereby thou once didst walk upon the sea.

- *costui* ‘this one’ → “this man here”: the personal demonstrative is expanded into a noun phrase with an adverb of place.
- *dei punti lievi e gravi* ‘of the points light and grave’ → “on the points both light and grave”: the preposition is adapted to “test”, and “both” is supplied.
- *come ti piace* ‘as pleases thee’ → “as it doth please thee”: a dummy subject and auxiliary are supplied, and the personal pronoun follows the verb.
- *intorno della fede* ‘concerning the faith’ → “anent the faith”: a single preposition replaces the Italian prepositional expression.
- *per la qual tu su per lo mare andavi* ‘by which thou upon the sea wert going’ → “whereby thou once didst walk upon the sea”: a relative adverb replaces preposition and pronoun; the verb moves ahead of the place phrase, going becomes walking, and the imperfect is rendered by a simple past with “once” supplied.

> 40 S' egli ama bene, e bene spera, e crede,  
> 41 non t' è occulto, perchè il viso hai quivi,  
> 42 dov' ogni cosa dipinta si vede.  

Whether he loveth well and well hopeth and believeth is not hidden from thee, for thou hast thy vision there where everything is seen depicted.

- *S' egli* ‘if he’ → “Whether he”: English marks the clause explicitly as an indirect question.
- *non t' è occulto* ‘not to thee is hidden’ → “is not hidden from thee”: the negative follows the copula, and the dative becomes a phrase with “from” after the predicate.
- *perchè* ‘because’ → “for”: a coordinating conjunction replaces the subordinating one.
- *il viso hai quivi* ‘the sight thou hast there’ → “thou hast thy vision there”: the object moves after the verb, ‘sight’ becomes “vision”, and a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *ogni cosa dipinta si vede* ‘every thing depicted is seen’ → “everything is seen depicted”: the distributive phrase becomes one pronoun, and the participle describing what is seen moves after the verb.

> 43 Ma perchè questo regno ha fatto civi  
> 44 per la verace fede, a gloriarla,  
> 45 di lei parlare è buon ch' a lui arrivi.”  

But since this realm hath made its citizens by the true faith, ’tis well that, for the glorifying of it, it should chance him to speak thereof.”

- *ha fatto civi* ‘has made citizens’ → “hath made its citizens”: the possessive is supplied.
- *a gloriarla* ‘to glorify it’ → “for the glorifying of it”: the purpose infinitive becomes a prepositional phrase with a verbal noun; its object becomes an “of” complement. The phrase moves inside the English ‘that’ clause.
- *di lei parlare è buon ch' a lui arrivi* ‘to speak of it, it is good that to him it should fall’ → “’tis well that” … “it should chance him to speak thereof”: the impersonal judgment comes first, with “well” for ‘good’; the infinitive is postponed until after “should chance him”, and its complement moves after “speak” as the pronominal adverb “thereof”.

> 46 Sì come il baccellier s' arma, e non parla,  
> 47 fin che il maestro la question propone,  
> 48 per approvarla, e non per terminarla;  

Even as the bachelor armeth himself and speaketh not until the master setteth forth the question, to sanction it, but not determine it;

- *Sì come* ‘just as’ → “Even as”: “Even” gives the emphasis of the opening adverb.
- *s' arma* ‘himself arms’ → “armeth himself”: the reflexive pronoun follows the verb.
- *non parla* ‘not speaks’ → “speaketh not”: the negative follows the verb.
- *la question propone* ‘the question sets forth’ → “setteth forth the question”: the object follows the verb.
- *per approvarla* ‘to approve it’ → “to sanction it”: approval is expressed as sanctioning.
- *e non per terminarla* ‘and not to determine it’ → “but not determine it”: “but” makes the contrast explicit, and the infinitive marker is not repeated.

## Lines 49–81 — Faith as Substance and Argument

> 49 così m' armava io d' ogni ragione,  
> 50 mentre ch' ella dicea, per esser presto  
> 51 a tal querente ed a tal professione.  

so did I arm myself with every reason whilst she was speaking, that I might be ready for such examiner and such profession.

- *così m' armava io* ‘so myself armed I’ → “so did I arm myself”: English introduces the auxiliary and puts the subject before the main verb and the reflexive after it.
- *d' ogni ragione* ‘of every reason’ → “with every reason”: English uses the preposition of instrument with “arm”.
- *mentre ch' ella dicea* ‘while she spoke’ → “whilst she was speaking”: the two-word conjunction becomes one word, and the progressive brings out the continuing action of the imperfect.
- *per esser presto* ‘in order to be ready’ → “that I might be ready”: the infinitive of purpose becomes a finite clause with a modal verb.
- *a tal querente ed a tal professione* ‘for such a questioner and for such a profession’ → “for such examiner and such profession”: “examiner” makes the questioning more specific; the second preposition is not repeated.

> 52 “Di', buon Cristiano, fatti manifesto:  
> 53 fede che è?” Ond' io levai la fronte  
> 54 in quella luce onde spirava questo;  

“Good Christian, speak, and manifest thyself; what thing is faith?” Whereat I lifted up my brow upon that light whence breathed forth this word;

- *Di', buon Cristiano* ‘say, good Christian’ → “Good Christian, speak”: the address comes first, and the command to say something becomes the intransitive “speak”.
- *fatti manifesto* ‘make thyself manifest’ → “and manifest thyself”: the adjective becomes a verb governing the reflexive, and “and” is supplied.
- *fede che è?* ‘faith, what is it?’ → “what thing is faith?”: the interrogative comes first, “thing” is supplied, and the subject follows the verb.
- *levai la fronte* ‘I raised the brow’ → “I lifted up my brow”: English uses a verb with a particle and a possessive in place of the article.
- *in quella luce* ‘into that light’ → “upon that light”: the preposition expresses the direction of the gaze differently.
- *onde spirava questo* ‘whence breathed this’ → “whence breathed forth this word”: “forth” makes the outward motion explicit, and “word” gives the demonstrative a noun.

> 55 poi mi volsi a Beatrice, ed essa pronte  
> 56 sembianze femmi, perch' io spandessi  
> 57 l' acqua di fuor del mio interno fonte.  

then turned me to Beatrice, and she made eager indication to me that I should pour the water forth from my inward fountain.

- *mi volsi* ‘myself I turned’ → “turned me”: the reflexive follows the verb.
- *pronte sembianze femmi* ‘eager signs made she to me’ → “made eager indication to me”: the verb precedes its object, the plural becomes a singular, and the attached indirect pronoun becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *perch' io spandessi* ‘in order that I might pour’ → “that I should pour”: the purpose clause with an imperfect subjunctive is cast as what the indication bids him do.
- *di fuor del mio interno fonte* ‘out of my internal fountain’ → “forth from my inward fountain”: “forth” goes with the verb of pouring, while “from” separately marks the source; “inward” renders the adjective.

> 58 “La grazia che mi dà ch' io mi confessi,  
> 59 comincia' io, dall' alto primipilo,  
> 60 faccia li miei concetti esser espressi.”  

“May the grace that granteth me to confess me,” I began, “to the veteran fore-fighter, make my thoughts find expression!”

- *La grazia … faccia* ‘the grace … may it make’ → “May the grace” … “make”: the wish expressed by the subjunctive is marked at the outset by a modal auxiliary.
- *che mi dà ch' io mi confessi* ‘which gives to me that I confess myself’ → “that granteth me to confess me”: the second finite clause becomes an infinitive dependent on “granteth”, with the reflexive placed after it.
- *comincia' io* ‘began I’ → “I began”: the subject precedes the verb.
- *dall' alto primipilo* ‘before the high-ranking first centurion’ → “to the veteran fore-fighter”: the preposition marks the person addressed; the title and its epithet are recast in terms of experience and fighting in front rather than rank.
- *li miei concetti esser espressi* ‘my conceptions to be expressed’ → “my thoughts find expression”: “thoughts” renders the noun, and the passive infinitive becomes an active bare infinitive with a noun as its object.

> 61 E seguitai: “Come il verace stilo  
> 62 ne scrisse, patre, del tuo caro frate,  
> 63 che mise Roma teco nel buon filo,  

And I followed on: “As wrote for us, O father, the veracious pen of thy dear brother, who, with thee, set Rome on the good track;

- *seguitai* ‘I continued’ → “I followed on”: a verb with a particle replaces the single verb.
- *Come il verace stilo ne scrisse … del tuo caro frate* ‘as the truthful pen wrote of it … of thy dear brother’ → “As wrote for us” … “the veracious pen of thy dear brother”: Wicksteed takes the clitic as ‘to us’ rather than ‘of it’ and puts the verb first, bringing “pen” together with the phrase that depends on it.
- *patre* ‘father’ → “O father”: the vocative particle is supplied.
- *mise Roma teco nel buon filo* ‘put Rome with thee in the good course’ → “with thee, set Rome on the good track”: the phrase of accompaniment moves before the verb, and the figure of a course or line becomes that of a track one is set on.

> 64 fede è sustanzia di cose sperate,  
> 65 ed argomento delle non parventi;  
> 66 e questo pare a me sua quiditate.”  

faith is the substance of things hoped for, and argument of things which are not seen; and this I take to be its quidity.”

- *è sustanzia* ‘is substance’ → “is the substance”: the definite article is supplied.
- *cose sperate* ‘things hoped’ → “things hoped for”: English requires the preposition with “hoped” in this construction.
- *delle non parventi* ‘of the non-appearing ones’ → “of things which are not seen”: the noun understood from the preceding line is repeated, and the negated participle becomes a passive relative clause.
- *questo pare a me sua quiditate* ‘this seems to me its quidity’ → “this I take to be its quidity”: the person judging becomes the subject, “this” becomes the fronted object, and an infinitive links it to “its quidity”.

> 67 Allora udii: “Dirittamente senti,  
> 68 se bene intendi, perchè la ripose  
> 69 tra le sustanzie, e poi tra gli argomenti.”  

Then heard I: “Rightly dost thou deem, if well thou understandest wherefore he placed it amongst the substances, and then amongst the arguments.”

- *Dirittamente senti* ‘rightly thou judgest’ → “Rightly dost thou deem”: the judgment is expressed by “deem” with an inverted auxiliary.
- *la ripose* ‘it he placed’ → “he placed it”: the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *e poi tra gli argomenti* ‘and then among the arguments’ → “and then amongst the arguments”: unlike the Italian as printed, which has no second *tra*, English repeats the preposition.

> 70 Ed io appresso: “Le profonde cose,  
> 71 che mi largiscon qui la lor parvenza,  
> 72 agli occhi di laggiù son sì ascose,  

And I thereon: “The deep things which grant me here the largess to appear before me, are from the eyes of them below so hidden

- *appresso* ‘next’ → “thereon”: succession is phrased as a response to what precedes.
- *che mi largiscon qui la lor parvenza* ‘which bestow on me here their appearance’ → “which grant me here the largess to appear before me”: the idea of generous giving is distributed between “grant” and “largess”; the possessed noun becomes an infinitive, with “before me” specifying to whom the things appear.
- *agli occhi di laggiù son sì ascose* ‘to the eyes of down there are so hidden’ → “are from the eyes of them below so hidden”: the verb moves ahead of the prepositional phrase; English hides something “from” eyes, and supplies “them” as the possessors of the eyes.

> 73 che l' esser loro v' è in sola credenza,  
> 74 sopra la qual si fonda l' alta spene,  
> 75 e però di sustanzia prende intenza;  

that their existence is there only in belief, whereon is built the lofty hope; and so of *substance* it embraceth the intention;

- *l' esser loro* ‘the being of them’ → “their existence”: a noun replaces the substantivized infinitive, with the possessive before it.
- *v' è in sola credenza* ‘there is in belief alone’ → “is there only in belief”: the locative follows the verb, and the adjective restricting “belief” becomes an adverb before the preposition.
- *sopra la qual* ‘upon which’ → “whereon”: preposition and relative are combined.
- *si fonda* ‘is founded’ → “is built”: the passive sense of the Italian pronominal verb is expressed with a different building verb.
- *però* ‘therefore’ → “so”: the consequence is expressed less formally.
- *di sustanzia prende intenza* ‘of substance it takes the sense’ → “of *substance* it embraceth the intention”: “embraceth” replaces ‘takes’, and “intention”, with an article supplied, gives the technical rather than the everyday sense of the noun.

> 76 e da questa credenza ci conviene  
> 77 sillogizzar, senza avere altra vista;  
> 78 però intenza di argomento tiene.”  

and from this belief needs must we syllogise without further sight; therefore it includes the intention of *argument*.”

- *ci conviene sillogizzar* ‘it is needful for us to syllogise’ → “needs must we syllogise”: the impersonal construction becomes a personal one with an adverb and a modal verb.
- *senza avere altra vista* ‘without having other sight’ → “without further sight”: the infinitive of possession disappears, and “further” renders the adjective as indicating additional sight.
- *intenza di argomento tiene* ‘the sense of argument it holds’ → “it includes the intention of *argument*”: the verb moves before its object and becomes “includes”; the technical noun again receives an article.

> 79 Allora udii: “Se quantunque s' acquista  
> 80 giù per dottrina fosse così inteso,  
> 81 non gli avria loco ingegno di sofista.”  

Then heard I: “If all that is acquired down below by teaching were so understood, there were no room left for the wit of sophist.”

- *quantunque s' acquista* ‘whatever one acquires’ → “all that is acquired”: the indefinite relative is expanded, and the pronominal construction is rendered by an English passive.
- *non gli avria loco ingegno di sofista* ‘the wit of a sophist would have no place there’ → “there were no room left for the wit of sophist”: the postposed subject becomes the complement of “for” in an existential construction; “there” introduces that construction rather than separately rendering the locative, “room left” expands ‘place’, and the hypothetical “were” replaces the conditional of ‘have’.

## Lines 82–114 — The Proof of Faith

> 82 Così spirò da quell' amore acceso;  

Thus was breathed forth from that enkindled love;

- *spirò* ‘it breathed’ → “was breathed forth”: the active verb becomes passive, with “forth” making the outward motion explicit.
- *amore acceso* ‘love enkindled’ → “enkindled love”: the participle comes before the noun.

> 83 indi soggiunse: “Assai bene è trascorsa  
> 84 d' esta moneta già la lega e il peso;  

then did it add: “Right well hath now been traversed this coin’s alloy and weight;

- *soggiunse* ‘it added’ → “did it add”: the simple past is expanded with “did”, which precedes the subject after “then”.
- *Assai bene* ‘very well’ → “Right well”: “Right” serves as the English intensifier.
- *è trascorsa … già* ‘has passed … already’ → “hath now been traversed”: the Italian presents the examination as having run its course; the English makes the verb passive and brings the adverb, rendered “now”, inside the verb phrase.
- *d' esta moneta … la lega* ‘of this coin … the alloy’ → “this coin’s alloy”: the prepositional phrase becomes a possessive attached directly to “alloy”, displacing the article.
- *e il peso* ‘and the weight’ → “and weight”: the second article drops out.

> 85 ma dimmi se tu l' hai nella tua borsa.”  

but tell me if thou hast it in thy purse.”

- *tu l' hai* ‘thou it hast’ → “thou hast it”: the object pronoun follows the verb.

> 86 Ond' io: “Sì ho, sì lucida e sì tonda,  
> 87 che nel suo conio nulla mi s' inforsa.”  

Whereupon I: “Yea, so bright and round I have it that for me is no *perhaps* in its impression.”

- *Sì ho, sì lucida e sì tonda* ‘yes, I have, so bright and so round’ → “Yea, so bright and round I have it”: the adjectives move before the verb, “so” is not repeated, and the unexpressed object reappears as “it”.
- *nel suo conio* ‘in its stamp’ → “in its impression”: the phrase moves from the beginning of the subordinate clause to its end; “impression” denotes the mark left by the stamp.
- *nulla mi s' inforsa* ‘nothing puts itself in doubt for me’ → “for me is no *perhaps*”: the dative moves to the front, and the reflexive verb formed on *forse* becomes “is” with a negative noun phrase formed from the English adverb.

> 88 Appresso uscì della luce profonda,  
> 89 che lì splendeva: “Questa cara gioia,  
> 90 sopra la quale ogni virtù si fonda,  
> 91 onde ti venne?” Ed io: “La larga ploia  

Then issued from the deep light that was glowing there: “This dear gem on which all virtue is up-built, whence came it to thee?” And I: “The ample shower

- *luce profonda* ‘light deep’ → “deep light”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *che lì splendeva* ‘that there shone’ → “that was glowing there”: the imperfect becomes a past progressive, “glowing” renders ‘shining’, and the adverb moves to the end.
- *gioia* ‘jewel’ → “gem”: the word is taken in its sense of a precious stone.
- *ogni virtù* ‘every virtue’ → “all virtue”: the distributive expression becomes collective.
- *si fonda* ‘founds itself’ → “is up-built”: the pronominal construction becomes passive, and being founded is rendered as being built up.
- *ti venne* ‘to thee came’ → “came it to thee”: the dative follows the verb, and “it” resumes the preceding noun phrase as subject of the question.

> 92 dello Spirito Santo, ch' è diffusa  
> 93 in sulle vecchie e in sulle nuove cuoia,  

of the Holy Spirit which is poured over the old and over the new parchments,

- *Spirito Santo* ‘Spirit Holy’ → “Holy Spirit”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *è diffusa* ‘is spread abroad’ → “is poured”: the verb takes its English sense from the shower.
- *in sulle vecchie e in sulle nuove cuoia* ‘upon the old and upon the new hides’ → “over the old and over the new parchments”: each compound preposition becomes “over”, and the material is named in English as prepared for writing.

> 94 è sillogismo, che la m' ha conchiusa  
> 95 acutamente sì che in verso d' ella  
> 96 ogni dimostrazion mi pare ottusa.”  

is syllogism that hath brought it to so sharp conclusion for me, that, compared to it, all demonstration seemeth blunt to me.”

- *che la m' ha conchiusa acutamente sì* ‘that it for me has concluded sharply so’ → “that hath brought it to so sharp conclusion for me”: the object follows the verb and the dative moves to the end; the participle becomes a phrase with the noun “conclusion”, so the adverb becomes an adjective with the degree word before it.
- *in verso d' ella* ‘in relation to it’ → “compared to it”: a participial phrase makes the comparison explicit.
- *ogni dimostrazion* ‘every demonstration’ → “all demonstration”: the distributive expression becomes collective.
- *mi pare ottusa* ‘to me seems blunt’ → “seemeth blunt to me”: the dative follows the predicate adjective.

> 97 Io udii poi: “L' antica e la novella  
> 98 proposizion che così ti conchiude,  
> 99 perchè l' hai tu per divina favella?”  

Then heard I: “That old and that new proposition which bringeth thee to such conclusion, wherefore dost hold it for divine discourse?”

- *Io udii poi* ‘I heard then’ → “Then heard I”: the adverb comes first and the subject last.
- *L' antica e la novella proposizion* ‘the old and the new proposition’ → “That old and that new proposition”: both definite articles become demonstratives.
- *che così ti conchiude* ‘which thus concludes for thee’ → “which bringeth thee to such conclusion”: the person for whom the inference is drawn becomes the object of “bringeth”; the verb of inference becomes a noun, with “such” taking up the force of the adverb.
- *perchè l' hai tu per* ‘why it hast thou for’ → “wherefore dost hold it for”: ‘have for’ becomes “hold … for”; the question takes the auxiliary “dost”, the object follows the main verb, and the explicit subject is omitted.

> 100 Ed io: “La prova che il ver mi dischiude  
> 101 son l' opere seguite, a che natura  
> 102 non scaldò ferro mai, nè battè incude.”  

And I: “The proof which doth unfold the truth to me lieth in the works that followed, for which nature ne’er heated iron yet, nor hammered anvil.”

- *il ver mi dischiude* ‘the true to me discloses’ → “doth unfold the truth to me”: the substantivized adjective becomes the noun “truth”; the verb takes an auxiliary, and both objects move after it.
- *son l' opere seguite* ‘are the works that followed’ → “lieth in the works that followed”: instead of identifying the proof with the works by a plural copula, the English locates it in them with a singular verb agreeing with “proof”; the participle becomes a relative clause.
- *a che* ‘to which’ → “for which”: English uses a preposition of purpose.
- *non scaldò ferro mai* ‘did not heat iron ever’ → “ne’er heated iron yet”: the negative and its reinforcement are recast as “ne’er” before the verb and “yet” after the object.
- *battè* ‘beat’ → “hammered”: the English verb specifies the means of striking.

> 103 Risposto fummi: “Di', chi t' assicura  
> 104 che quell' opere fosser? Quel medesmo  

The answer came to me: “Say, who assureth thee that these works were?

- *Risposto fummi* ‘answered was it to me’ → “The answer came to me”: the impersonal passive becomes an active clause with “The answer” as subject; the dative is detached from the verb.
- *t' assicura* ‘thee assures’ → “assureth thee”: the object follows the verb.
- *quell' opere fosser* ‘those works were’ → “these works were”: the demonstrative changes from distant to near; the Italian imperfect subjunctive has no distinct mood marking in the English.

> 105 che vuol provarsi, non altri, il ti giura.”  

The very script that would attest itself, no other, sweareth it to thee.”

- *Quel medesmo* ‘that very thing’ → “The very script”: the demonstrative becomes an article, and “script” supplies a noun identifying the referent.
- *che vuol provarsi* ‘that wills to prove itself’ → “that would attest itself”: volition is expressed by the modal “would”, and proving becomes attesting.
- *il ti giura* ‘it to thee swears’ → “sweareth it to thee”: both pronouns move after the verb.

> 106 “Se il mondo si rivolse al Cristianesmo,  
> 107 diss' io, senza miracoli, quest' uno  
> 108 è tal che gli altri non sono il centesmo;  

“If the world turned to Christianity, I said, without miracles, this one is such that the others are not the hundredth of it;

- *si rivolse* ‘turned itself’ → “turned”: the English verb is intransitive rather than reflexive.
- *al Cristianesmo* ‘to the Christianity’ → “to Christianity”: the name takes no article in English.
- *diss' io* ‘said I’ → “I said”: the subject precedes the verb.
- *gli altri non sono il centesmo* ‘the others are not the hundredth’ → “the others are not the hundredth of it”: “of it” supplies the standard of comparison.

> 109 chè tu entrasti povero e digiuno  
> 110 in campo, a seminar la buona pianta,  
> 111 che fu già vite, ed ora è fatta pruno.”  

for thou didst enter poor and hungry upon the battle-field to sow the good plant which was erst a vine, but now has grown a thorn.”

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *entrasti* ‘entered’ → “didst enter”: the simple past is expanded with an auxiliary.
- *digiuno* ‘fasting’ → “hungry”: the English gives the condition rather than the abstinence from food.
- *in campo* ‘into the field’ → “upon the battle-field”: the preposition changes, and “battle-field” specifies the sense of the noun.
- *fu già vite* ‘was formerly vine’ → “was erst a vine”: English supplies an article before the predicate noun.
- *ed ora è fatta pruno* ‘and now is made thorn’ → “but now has grown a thorn”: the conjunction makes the contrast explicit, the change of state is expressed by the present perfect “has grown”, and the predicate noun gains an article.

> 112 Finito questo, l' alta corte santa  
> 113 risonò per le spere un *Dio laudamo*,  
> 114 nella melode che lassù si canta.  

This ended, the high holy court made *God we praise* ring through the spheres, in melody such as up there is sung.

- *Finito questo* ‘ended this’ → “This ended”: the subject of the absolute construction precedes its participle.
- *l' alta corte santa* ‘the high court holy’ → “the high holy court”: the second adjective joins the first before the noun.
- *risonò per le spere un Dio laudamo* ‘resounded through the spheres a God we praise’ → “made *God we praise* ring through the spheres”: the single verb is unpacked into a causative with “ring”; the name of the song loses its article and precedes both the infinitive and the phrase of place.
- *nella melode che* ‘in the melody that’ → “in melody such as”: the article drops out, and a relative identifying the melody becomes an expression of likeness.
- *lassù si canta* ‘up there one sings’ → “up there is sung”: the Italian pronominal construction becomes an English passive.

## Lines 115–147 — Dante Professes His Faith

> 115 E quel Baron, che sì di ramo in ramo,  
> 116 esaminando, già tratto m' avea  
> 117 che all' ultime fronde appressavamo,  

And that Baron who so from branch to branch, examining, had drawn me now, that we were nigh unto the utmost leaves,

- *già tratto m' avea* ‘already drawn me had’ → “had drawn me now”: the auxiliary comes first, the object follows the participle, and the adverb moves to the end and marks the point reached rather than saying ‘already’.
- *all' ultime fronde appressavamo* ‘to the last leaves we were drawing near’ → “we were nigh unto the utmost leaves”: the verb of approach becomes an expression of nearness, followed by its complement; ‘last’ is rendered spatially as “utmost”.

> 118 ricominciò: “La grazia, che donnea  
> 119 con la tua mente, la bocca t' aperse  
> 120 infino a qui, com' aprir si dovea;  

began again: “The grace which holdeth amorous converse with thy mind hath oped thy mouth till now as it behoved to open;

- *ricominciò* ‘recommenced’ → “began again”: the force of the prefix is given by a separate adverb.
- *donnea* ‘courts’ → “holdeth amorous converse”: a single verb becomes a phrase that brings out the suggestion of speech.
- *la bocca t' aperse* ‘the mouth to thee opened’ → “hath oped thy mouth”: the Italian dative becomes a possessive, the object follows the verb, and the simple past becomes a present perfect.
- *infino a qui* ‘up to here’ → “till now”: the limit is expressed in terms of time rather than place.
- *com' aprir si dovea* ‘as it ought to open’ → “as it behoved to open”: the Italian infinitive precedes the verb of obligation and has a reflexive pronoun; English puts the impersonal expression of obligation first and uses ‘open’ intransitively.

> 121 sì ch' io approvo ciò che fuori emerse:  
> 122 ma or conviene esprimer quel che credi,  
> 123 ed onde alla credenza tua s' offerse.”  

so that I sanction that which forth emerged; but now behoveth thee to utter what it is thou dost believe, and whence it offered it to thy believing.”

- *approvo* ‘approve’ → “sanction”: assent is expressed as an act of sanction.
- *conviene esprimer* ‘it is needful to express’ → “behoveth thee to utter”: the person on whom the obligation falls is made explicit, and ‘express’ is rendered by a verb of speaking.
- *quel che credi* ‘that which thou believest’ → “what it is thou dost believe”: a relative phrase becomes an expanded indirect question, with “it is” and the auxiliary “dost”.
- *alla credenza tua s' offerse* ‘to the belief of thine it offered itself’ → “it offered it to thy believing”: the prepositional complement moves to the end, the possessive precedes its noun, and ‘belief’ becomes the verbal noun “believing”. The second “it” does duty for the Italian reflexive.

> 124 “O santo padre, spirito che vedi  
> 125 ciò che credesti sì che tu vincesti  
> 126 ver lo sepolcro i più giovani piedi,  

“O holy father, thou spirit who now seest that which of old thou didst so believe that thou didst overcome more youthful feet drawing anigh the sepulchre,”

- *spirito* ‘spirit’ → “thou spirit”: the apposition gains a pronoun of address.
- *vedi* ‘seest’ → “now seest”: the present time is made explicit.
- *credesti sì* ‘thou believedst so’ → “of old thou didst so believe”: the past is marked explicitly by “of old” as well as by the auxiliary, and the degree adverb precedes the main verb.
- *vincesti* ‘thou overcamest’ → “thou didst overcome”: an auxiliary bears the past tense.
- *ver lo sepolcro i più giovani piedi* ‘toward the sepulchre the younger feet’ → “more youthful feet drawing anigh the sepulchre”: the object moves before the expression of direction and loses its article; the comparative becomes “more youthful”, and the preposition of direction is expanded into a participial phrase of approach.

> 127 comincia' io, tu vuoi ch' io manifesti  
> 128 la forma qui del pronto creder mio,  
> 129 ed anco la cagion di lui chiedesti;  

I began, “thou wouldst have me here make plain the form of my eager belief, and dost also ask the cause of it;

- *comincia' io* ‘began I’ → “I began”: the subject moves before the verb.
- *tu vuoi ch' io manifesti* ‘thou willest that I make manifest’ → “thou wouldst have me here make plain”: the present verb of willing and its subjunctive clause become a modal expression with “have”, an object pronoun, and a bare infinitive.
- *la forma qui* ‘the form here’ → “here make plain the form”: the adverb is brought forward from its position after the object to precede the act of making plain.
- *del pronto creder mio* ‘of the ready believing of mine’ → “of my eager belief”: the possessive moves forward, ‘ready’ becomes “eager”, and the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun.
- *ed anco la cagion di lui chiedesti* ‘and also the cause of it thou askedst’ → “and dost also ask the cause of it”: the question is treated as a present rather than a past request; the object follows the verb, and the adverb stands between auxiliary and verb.

> 130 ed io rispondo: Io credo in uno Iddio  
> 131 solo ed eterno, che tutto il ciel move,  
> 132 non moto, con amore e con disio.  

whereto I answer: I believe in one God, sole and eternal, who moveth all the heaven, himself unmoved, with love and with desire.

- *ed io rispondo* ‘and I answer’ → “whereto I answer”: a relative connective referring back to the request replaces simple coordination.
- *non moto* ‘not moved’ → “himself unmoved”: the negation is incorporated into the participle, and an emphatic pronoun is supplied.

> 133 Ed a tal creder non ho io pur prove  
> 134 fisice e metafisice, ma dalmi  
> 135 anco la verità che quinci piove  

And for such belief I have not only proofs physic and metaphysic, but it is given me likewise by the truth which hence doth rain

- *a tal creder* ‘for such believing’ → “for such belief”: the substantivized infinitive is rendered by a noun.
- *non ho io pur* ‘not have I only’ → “I have not only”: the expressed subject moves before the verb and the negative follows it.
- *dalmi anco la verità* ‘gives it me also the truth’ → “it is given me likewise by the truth”: the object becomes the subject of a passive, and the postposed Italian subject becomes the agent introduced by “by”.
- *piove* ‘rains’ → “doth rain”: the present tense is expressed with an auxiliary.

> 136 per Moisè, per Profeti e per Salmi,  
> 137 per l' Evangelio, e per voi che scriveste,  
> 138 poichè l' ardente Spirto vi fece almi.  

through Moses, through the Prophets and through the Psalms, through the Gospel and through you who wrote when the glowing Spirit had made you fosterers.

- *per Profeti e per Salmi* ‘through Prophets and through Psalms’ → “through the Prophets and through the Psalms”: both nouns gain definite articles.
- *poichè* ‘after’ → “when”: the conjunction is taken temporally, with anteriority conveyed by the tense that follows.
- *ardente* ‘burning’ → “glowing”: the image of burning is rendered as that of giving off a glow.
- *vi fece almi* ‘you made fostering’ → “had made you fosterers”: the object follows the verb, the simple past becomes a past perfect, and the predicate adjective is interpreted actively and rendered by an agent noun.

> 139 E credo in tre persone eterne, e queste  
> 140 credo una essenza sì una e sì trina,  
> 141 che soffera congiunto sono ed *este*.  

And I believe in three eternal Persons, and I believe them one Essence, so One and so Trine as to comport at once with *are* and *is*.

- *persone eterne* ‘Persons eternal’ → “eternal Persons”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *queste credo* ‘these I believe’ → “I believe them”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun and follows the verb.
- *sì trina, che soffera* ‘so trine that it admits’ → “so Trine as to comport at once with”: the finite subjunctive clause of result becomes an infinitive construction, and a transitive verb becomes “comport” with a preposition.
- *congiunto sono ed este* ‘conjoined, are and is’ → “at once with *are* and *is*”: the idea of the two forms being conjoined is expressed adverbially by “at once”.

> 142 Della profonda condizion divina  
> 143 ch' io tocco, nella mente mi sigilla  
> 144 più volte l' evangelica dottrina.  

With the profound divine state whereof I speak, my mind is stamped more times than once by evangelic teaching.

- *Della profonda condizion divina* ‘of the profound condition divine’ → “With the profound divine state”: “With” gives the content of the impression, ‘condition’ becomes “state”, and the second adjective moves before the noun.
- *ch' io tocco* ‘which I touch upon’ → “whereof I speak”: the metaphor of touching becomes an explicit verb of speaking, with a prepositional relative instead of a direct object.
- *nella mente mi sigilla* ‘in the mind it seals me’ → “my mind is stamped”: the place of the impression becomes the subject of a passive, the personal pronoun becomes a possessive, and sealing is rendered as stamping.
- *più volte* ‘several times’ → “more times than once”: repetition is expressed by an explicit comparison.
- *l' evangelica dottrina* ‘the evangelic doctrine’ → “by evangelic teaching”: the subject of the active verb becomes the agent of the passive, and ‘doctrine’ becomes the articleless “teaching”.

> 145 Quest' è il principio, quest' è la favilla  
> 146 che si dilata in fiamma poi vivace,  
> 147 e, come stella in cielo, in me scintilla.”  

This the beginning is; this is the spark which then dilates into a living flame, and like a star in heaven shineth in me.”

- *Quest' è il principio* ‘this is the beginning’ → “This the beginning is”: the verb is moved to the end.
- *si dilata* ‘expands itself’ → “dilates”: the reflexive is rendered by an intransitive verb.
- *in fiamma poi vivace* ‘into flame then living’ → “then dilates into a living flame”: the adverb moves before the verb, the adjective moves before the noun, and an indefinite article is supplied.
- *come stella* ‘like star’ → “like a star”: English supplies an indefinite article.
- *in me scintilla* ‘in me it sparkles’ → “shineth in me”: the verb precedes its complement, and the more general “shineth” replaces ‘sparkles’.

## Lines 148–154 — The Apostolic Light Encircles Dante

> 148 Come il signor, ch' ascolta quel che i piace,  
> 149 da indi abbraccia il servo, gratulando  
> 150 per la novella, tosto ch' ei si tace;  

Like as the master who heareth what doth please him, and thereupon embraceth the servant, rejoicing at the news, so soon as he is silent;

- *Come* ‘As’ → “Like as”: English uses a two-word expression for the comparison.
- *ascolta* ‘listens to’ → “heareth”: hearing replaces listening.
- *quel che i piace* ‘that which to him pleases’ → “what doth please him”: “what” combines the antecedent and the relative; the verb takes the auxiliary “doth”, and the pronoun follows it as a direct object.
- *da indi* ‘from then’ → “and thereupon”: the temporal phrase becomes a single adverb, and “and” coordinates the two verbs governed by “who”.
- *gratulando per la novella* ‘congratulating on account of the news’ → “rejoicing at the news”: the participle expresses the master’s joy rather than his congratulation, and “at” introduces the occasion of that joy.
- *tosto ch’ ei si tace* ‘as soon as he falls silent’ → “so soon as he is silent”: the reflexive verb for ceasing to speak becomes a statement of being silent.

> 151 così, benedicendomi cantando,  
> 152 tre volte cinse me, sì com' io tacqui,  
> 153 l' apostolico lume, al cui comando  
> 154 io avea detto; sì nel dir gli piacqui.  

so, blessing me as it sang, three times circled me, so soon as I was silent, the apostolic light at whose command I had discoursed; so did I please him in my utterance.

- *cantando* ‘singing’ → “as it sang”: the gerund becomes a finite temporal clause.
- *sì com’ io tacqui* ‘just as I fell silent’ → “so soon as I was silent”: the conjunction is rendered explicitly in terms of immediacy, and the act of falling silent becomes a state.
- *al cui comando* ‘at the command of whom’ → “at whose command”: the possessive relative stands before the noun in English, without a separate article.
- *io avea detto* ‘I had spoken’ → “I had discoursed”: the more specific verb suggests extended speech.
- *sì nel dir gli piacqui* ‘so in the speaking to him I pleased’ → “so did I please him in my utterance”: initial “so” is followed by subject–auxiliary inversion; the dative becomes a direct object following “please”, and the nominal infinitive becomes a noun with a supplied possessive in a phrase moved to the end.

## Summary — English Syntax and Italian Emphasis

Wicksteed often changes word order without changing emphasis. He moves the object pronoun after the verb in *vi ciba* (‘you he feeds’), “feedeth you”, and the adjective before the noun in *anime liete*, “glad souls”. Yet he keeps the adjective after the noun in *luce eterna*, “light eternal”. Sometimes the inversion is his own: *Io udii poi* (‘I heard then’) becomes “Then heard I”. Two routine adjustments generally go unmarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending identifies the subject, and drops the article before a possessive, as in *la tua mente*, “thy mind”.

A change of wording often makes explicit what the Italian leaves to context. The *cerchi* (‘circles’) of the clock become “wheels”, and, in the image of the fountain, *vien* (‘comes’) becomes “floweth”. An addition need not introduce a new idea: “this man” supplies a noun for the demonstrative *questi*, and “my brow” makes possession explicit where *la fronte* has the article alone. Elsewhere Wicksteed leaves a connective unexpressed, notably the result conjunction *che* after “so divine a song”, or lets one degree word serve twice, rendering *sì lucida e sì tonda* as “so bright and round”.

Changes of construction range from the slight to the extensive. The purpose infinitive *per esser presto* becomes the finite clause “that I might be ready”; conversely, *cantando* (‘singing’) becomes “as it sang”. In *la verità* … *dalmi* (‘the truth gives it to me’), the English passive “it is given me likewise by the truth” makes the gift rather than the giver the subject. Yet the technical pair *sustanzia* and *argomento* remains “substance” and “argument”, and *di ramo in ramo* keeps both its image and its repetition in “from branch to branch”. Wicksteed is often closest to the Italian in these governing terms and figures, even when he rearranges the sentence that carries them.
