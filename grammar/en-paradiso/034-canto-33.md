# Paradiso 33

## Lines 1–3 — The Virgin Mother’s Humility and Exaltation

> 1 “Vergine madre, figlia del tuo figlio,  

“Virgin mother, daughter of thy son,

- Literal.

> 2 umile ed alta più che creatura,  

lowly and uplifted more than any creature,

- *umile* ‘humble’ → “lowly”: the translation chooses a word that contrasts with height.
- *alta* ‘high’ → “uplifted”: a participle takes the place of the adjective.
- *che creatura* ‘than creature’ → “than any creature”: “any” makes explicit the general force of the bare singular noun.

> 3 termine fisso d' eterno consiglio,  

fixed goal of the eternal counsel,

- *termine fisso* ‘end fixed’ → “fixed goal”: “goal” gives ‘end’ the sense of an aim, and the adjective moves before the noun.
- *d' eterno consiglio* ‘of eternal counsel’ → “of the eternal counsel”: the definite article is supplied.

## Lines 4–36 — Bernard’s Prayer to Mary

> 4 tu se' colei, che l'umana natura  
> 5 nobilitasti sì che il suo Fattore  
> 6 non disdegnò di farsi sua fattura.  

thou art she who didst human nature so ennoble that its own Maker scorned not to become its making.

- *che l'umana natura nobilitasti sì* ‘who human nature didst ennoble so’ → “who didst human nature so ennoble”: the finite verb is divided into auxiliary and infinitive, with the object between them; the degree adverb moves before the infinitive, and the generic definite article is dropped.
- *il suo Fattore* ‘its Maker’ → “its own Maker”: “own” adds emphasis.
- *non disdegnò* ‘did not disdain’ → “scorned not”: the negative follows the verb.
- *di farsi sua fattura* ‘to make himself its creature’ → “to become its making”: the reflexive verb becomes a verb of becoming; “making” denotes the thing made and preserves the Italian play between maker and made.

> 7 Nel ventre tuo si raccese l' amore,  
> 8 per lo cui caldo nell' eterna pace  
> 9 così è germinato questo fiore.  

In thy womb was lit again the love under whose warmth in the eternal peace this flower hath thus unfolded.

- *Nel ventre tuo* ‘in the womb of thine’ → “In thy womb”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *si raccese* ‘rekindled’ → “was lit again”: the pronominal verb becomes an English passive, and the prefix of repetition becomes a separate adverb.
- *per lo cui caldo* ‘through whose warmth’ → “under whose warmth”: the instrumental or causal preposition becomes “under”.
- *così è germinato questo fiore* ‘thus has sprouted this flower’ → “this flower hath thus unfolded”: the postposed subject comes first, the adverb moves between auxiliary and participle, and sprouting becomes unfolding.

> 10 Qui sei a noi meridiana face  
> 11 di caritate, e giuso, intra i mortali,  
> 12 sei di speranza fontana vivace.  

Here art thou unto us the meridian torch of love and there below with mortals art a living spring of hope.

- *meridiana face* ‘meridian torch’ → “the meridian torch”: a definite article is supplied to the predicate noun.
- *di caritate* ‘of charity’ → “of love”: the broader English noun renders the more specific Italian one.
- *giuso* ‘below’ → “there below”: “there” makes the contrast with “Here” explicit.
- *intra i mortali* ‘among the mortals’ → “with mortals”: “with” replaces ‘among’, and the generic article is dropped.
- *di speranza fontana vivace* ‘of hope spring living’ → “a living spring of hope”: an indefinite article is supplied, the adjective precedes the noun, and the complement of the noun follows it.

> 13 Donna, sei tanto grande e tanto vali,  
> 14 che qual vuol grazia ed a te non ricorre,  
> 15 sua disianza vuol volar senz'ali.  

Lady thou art so great and hast such worth, that if there be who would have grace yet betaketh not himself to thee, his longing seeketh to fly without wings.

- *tanto vali* ‘so much art thou worth’ → “hast such worth”: the verb of worth becomes a noun with “hast”, and the degree is expressed by a noun modifier.
- *qual vuol grazia* ‘whoever wants grace’ → “if there be who would have grace”: the indefinite relative becomes an existential condition with a relative clause; wanting is expressed by “would have”.
- *ed a te non ricorre* ‘and to thee has not recourse’ → “yet betaketh not himself to thee”: the conjunction becomes adversative, the verb of recourse becomes a reflexive expression, and the destination moves to the end.
- *vuol volar* ‘wants to fly’ → “seeketh to fly”: seeking replaces willing or wishing.

> 16 La tua benignità non pur soccorre  
> 17 a chi domanda, ma molte fiate  
> 18 liberamente al domandar precorre.  

Thy kindliness not only succoureth whoso requesteth, but doth oftentimes freely forerun request.

- *soccorre a chi domanda* ‘gives succour to whoever asks’ → “succoureth whoso requesteth”: the English verb takes a direct object rather than a complement with ‘to’.
- *molte fiate* ‘many times’ → “oftentimes”: the noun phrase becomes an adverb.
- *al domandar precorre* ‘the asking it precedes’ → “forerun request”: the nominalized infinitive becomes a bare noun placed after a transitive verb; the Italian finite verb is divided between “doth”, placed before the adverbs, and “forerun”.

> 19 In te misericordia, in te pietate,  
> 20 in te magnificenza, in te s'aduna  
> 21 quantunque in creatura è di bontate.  

In thee is tenderness, in thee is pity, in thee munificence, in thee united whatever in created being is of excellence.

- *In te misericordia* ‘in thee mercy’ → “In thee is tenderness”: the copula is supplied, and mercy is rendered as tenderness.
- *in te pietate* ‘in thee pity’ → “in thee is pity”: the copula is again supplied.
- *magnificenza* ‘magnificence’ → “munificence”: the translation selects the sense of lavish generosity.
- *s'aduna* ‘gathers together’ → “united”: the finite pronominal verb becomes a participle with the copula understood.
- *in creatura* ‘in a creature’ → “in created being”: the generic singular noun becomes an expression without an article, with the idea of creation expressed by a participial adjective.
- *di bontate* ‘of goodness’ → “of excellence”: excellence replaces goodness.

> 22 Or questi, che dall' infima lacuna  
> 23 dell' universo infin qui ha vedute  
> 24 le vite spiritali ad una ad una,  

Now he who from the deepest pool of the universe even to here hath seen the spirit lives one after one

- *questi* ‘this man’ → “he”: the personal pronoun loses the demonstrative force.
- *infima* ‘lowest’ → “deepest”: depth replaces position on a scale of height.
- *le vite spiritali* ‘the lives spiritual’ → “the spirit lives”: the adjective becomes an attributive noun and precedes the noun it qualifies.
- *ad una ad una* ‘one by one’ → “one after one”: “after” expresses the succession conveyed by the repeated Italian preposition.

> 25 supplica a te, per grazia, di virtute  
> 26 tanto che possa con gli occhi levarsi  
> 27 più alto verso l' ultima salute;  

imploreth thee, of grace, for so much power as to be able to uplift his eyes more high towards final bliss;

- *supplica a te* ‘makes supplication to thee’ → “imploreth thee”: the person addressed becomes the direct object.
- *di virtute tanto* ‘for power so much’ → “for so much power”: the expression of quantity precedes the noun.
- *che possa* ‘that he may be able’ → “as to be able”: the finite subjunctive result clause becomes an infinitival complement of “so much”.
- *con gli occhi levarsi* ‘with the eyes to raise himself’ → “to uplift his eyes”: the eyes, expressed in Italian by a prepositional phrase with the definite article, become the direct object with a possessive; the reflexive object disappears.
- *l' ultima salute* ‘the ultimate salvation’ → “final bliss”: salvation is rendered as bliss, and the definite article is dropped.

> 28 ed io, che mai per mio veder non arsi  
> 29 più ch' io fo per lo suo, tutti i miei preghi  
> 30 ti porgo, e prego che non sieno scarsi,  

and I, who never burned for my own vision more than I do for his, proffer thee all my prayers and pray they be not scant

- *mai per mio veder non arsi* ‘never for my seeing did I burn’ → “never burned for my own vision”: the verb precedes its complement, the nominalized infinitive becomes a noun, “own” adds emphasis, and the Italian negative concord is rendered by “never” alone.
- *tutti i miei preghi ti porgo* ‘all my prayers to thee I proffer’ → “proffer thee all my prayers”: the verb comes first, followed by its indirect and direct objects.
- *che non sieno scarsi* ‘that they be not scant’ → “they be not scant”: the conjunction introducing the subjunctive clause is omitted.

> 31 perchè tu ogni nube gli disleghi  
> 32 di sua mortalità coi preghi tuoi,  
> 33 sì che il sommo piacer gli si dispieghi.  

that thou do scatter for him every cloud of his mortality with prayers of thine, so that the joy supreme may be unfolded to him.

- *tu ogni nube gli disleghi* ‘thou every cloud for him unbind’ → “thou do scatter for him every cloud”: the subjunctive is rendered with “do”; scattering replaces unbinding, and the verb and indirect object both precede the direct object.
- *coi preghi tuoi* ‘with thy prayers’ → “with prayers of thine”: the definite phrase with a possessive becomes an indefinite phrase with an independent possessive after “of”.
- *il sommo piacer* ‘the supreme delight’ → “the joy supreme”: joy replaces delight, and the adjective follows the noun.
- *gli si dispieghi* ‘to him unfold itself’ → “may be unfolded to him”: the pronominal subjunctive becomes a modal passive, with the recipient last.

> 34 Ancor ti prego, Regina che puoi  
> 35 ciò che tu vuoli, che conservi sani,  
> 36 dopo tanto veder, gli affetti suoi.  

And further do I pray thee, Queen who can’st all that thou wilt, that thou keep sound for him, after so great a vision, his affections.

- *Ancor ti prego* ‘further thee I pray’ → “And further do I pray thee”: a conjunction is supplied, the verb is expanded with “do” before the subject, and the object follows “pray”.
- *ciò che* ‘that which’ → “all that”: “all” gives the relative expression an explicitly comprehensive sense.
- *conservi sani* ‘keep sound’ → “keep sound for him”: a phrase marking the beneficiary is supplied.
- *dopo tanto veder* ‘after so much seeing’ → “after so great a vision”: the nominalized infinitive becomes a count noun with an article, and the degree of seeing becomes the greatness of a vision.
- *gli affetti suoi* ‘the affections of his’ → “his affections”: the possessive comes before the noun.

## Lines 37–69 — Sight Beyond Speech and Memory

> 37 Vinca tua guardia i movimenti umani;  

Let thy protection vanquish human ferments;

- *Vinca tua guardia* ‘may thy guardianship prevail over’ → “Let thy protection vanquish”: the initial subjunctive becomes a construction with “Let”; the subject comes before “vanquish”, and the act of keeping guard is rendered as “protection”.
- *i movimenti umani* ‘the impulses human’ → “human ferments”: the article drops out, the adjective comes first, and “ferments” suggests agitation rather than movement alone.

> 38 vedi Beatrice con quanti beati  
> 39 per li miei preghi ti chiudon le mani.”  

see Beatrice, with how many Saints, for my prayers folding hands.”

- *beati* ‘blessed ones’ → “Saints”: a substantive adjective becomes a noun.
- *per li miei preghi* ‘for the sake of my prayers’ → “for my prayers”: “for” compresses the sense of acting in support of the prayers.
- *ti chiudon le mani* ‘to thee they close the hands’ → “folding hands”: the finite plural verb becomes a participle dependent on “see”; “folding” replaces ‘close’, and both the addressee and the article before ‘hands’ disappear.

> 40 Gli occhi da Dio diletti e venerati,  
> 41 fissi nell' orator, ne dimostraro  
> 42 quanto i devoti preghi le son grati.  

Those eyes, of God beloved and venerated, fixed upon him who prayed, showed us how greatly devout prayers please her.

- *Gli occhi* ‘the eyes’ → “Those eyes”: the article becomes a demonstrative.
- *nell' orator* ‘on the suppliant’ → “upon him who prayed”: the agent noun is expanded into a pronoun and a relative clause.
- *ne dimostraro* ‘to us showed’ → “showed us”: the recipient follows the verb.
- *quanto i devoti preghi le son grati* ‘how much the devout prayers to her are pleasing’ → “how greatly devout prayers please her”: the article drops out; the copula and adjective become the active verb “please”, with “her” following as its object.

> 43 Indi all' eterno lume si drizzaro,  
> 44 nel qual non si de' creder che s' invii  
> 45 per creatura l' occhio tanto chiaro.  

Then to the eternal light they bent themselves, wherein we may not ween that any creature’s eye findeth its way so clear.

- *si drizzaro* ‘themselves they directed’ → “they bent themselves”: “bent” replaces the verb of directing, and the reflexive follows the verb.
- *non si de' creder* ‘one ought not to believe’ → “we may not ween”: the impersonal construction becomes first-person plural, and obligation is expressed as lack of leave to suppose.
- *che s' invii per creatura l' occhio* ‘that there be directed by a creature the eye’ → “that any creature’s eye findeth its way”: the agent becomes a possessor, with “any” supplied under the preceding negative; the eye comes before its verb, and the subjunctive construction of direction becomes an indicative idiom of finding a way.

> 46 Ed io ch' al fine di tutti i disii  
> 47 m'appropinquava, sì com' io dovea,  
> 48 l'ardor del desiderio in me finii.  

And I, who to the goal of all my longings was drawing nigh, even as was meet the ardour of the yearning quenched within me.

- *al fine* ‘to the end’ → “to the goal”: “goal” selects the sense of an end aimed at.
- *di tutti i disii* ‘of all the desires’ → “of all my longings”: “longings” intensifies ‘desires’, and “my” replaces the article.
- *m'appropinquava* ‘was bringing myself near’ → “was drawing nigh”: an intransitive idiom replaces the reflexive.
- *sì com' io dovea* ‘just as I ought’ → “even as was meet”: a personal obligation becomes an impersonal expression of fitness.
- *del desiderio in me finii* ‘of desire in me I ended’ → “of the yearning quenched within me”: “yearning” has an article in English; “quenched” gives the ending a sense fitted to “ardour”, and “within me” moves after the verb.

> 49 Bernardo m' accennava, e sorridea,  
> 50 perch' io guardassi suso: ma io era  
> 51 già per me stesso tal qual ei volea;  

Bernard gave me the sign and smiled to me that I should look on high, but I already of myself was such as he would have me;

- *m' accennava* ‘to me was making a sign’ → “gave me the sign”: the imperfect becomes simple past, the act is expressed by a verb and definite noun, and the recipient follows the verb.
- *sorridea* ‘was smiling’ → “smiled to me”: the imperfect becomes simple past, and the recipient is made explicit here too.
- *perch' io guardassi* ‘so that I might look’ → “that I should look”: the purpose conjunction is reduced to “that”, with “should” conveying the subjunctive.
- *io era già per me stesso* ‘I was already of myself’ → “I already of myself was”: the adverb and prepositional phrase move before the verb.
- *tal qual ei volea* ‘such as he wished’ → “such as he would have me”: “have me” makes explicit the object and the desired state implicit after ‘wished’.

> 52 chè la mia vista, venendo sincera,  
> 53 e più e più entrava per lo raggio  
> 54 dell' alta luce, che da sè è vera.  

because my sight, becoming purged, now more and more was entering through the ray of the deep light which in itself is true.

- *venendo sincera* ‘becoming pure’ → “becoming purged”: a past participle suggesting purification replaces the adjective of condition.
- *e più e più entrava* ‘and more and more entered’ → “now more and more was entering”: “now” takes the place of the connective, and the progressive brings out the imperfect.
- *dell' alta luce* ‘of the high light’ → “of the deep light”: “deep” substitutes a different spatial term for ‘high’ or ‘lofty’.
- *da sè è vera* ‘of itself is true’ → “in itself is true”: the preposition of source or independence becomes “in”.

> 55 Da quinci innanzi il mio veder fu maggio  
> 56 che il parlar nostro ch' a tal vista cede,  
> 57 e cede la memoria a tanto oltraggio.  

Thence forward was my vision mightier than our discourse, which faileth at such sight, and faileth memory at so great outrage.

- *il mio veder fu maggio* ‘my seeing was greater’ → “was my vision mightier”: the copula moves before the subject, the substantival infinitive becomes “vision”, and the comparative of greatness becomes one of power.
- *che il parlar nostro* ‘than the speaking of ours’ → “than our discourse”: a substantival infinitive becomes a noun, with the possessive before it.
- *ch' a tal vista cede* ‘which to such sight yields’ → “which faileth at such sight”: yielding to something becomes failing in its presence, and the verb moves before the prepositional phrase.
- *cede la memoria a tanto oltraggio* ‘yields the memory to so great an excess’ → “faileth memory at so great outrage”: the repeated verb and its preposition undergo the same shift; the article before ‘memory’ drops out, and “outrage” renders *oltraggio* in the sense of what exceeds bounds.

> 58 Qual è colui che somniando vede,  
> 59 che dopo il sogno la passione impressa  
> 60 rimane, e l'altro alla mente non riede;  

As is he who dreaming seeth, and when the dream is gone the passion stamped remaineth, and nought else cometh to the mind again;

- *che dopo il sogno* ‘such that after the dream’ → “and when the dream is gone”: coordination replaces the relative link, and a temporal clause replaces the prepositional phrase.
- *la passione impressa* ‘the feeling imprinted’ → “the passion stamped”: “passion” renders the felt emotion, and “stamped” gives the impression a more concrete verb.
- *e l'altro alla mente non riede* ‘and the rest to the mind does not return’ → “and nought else cometh to the mind again”: the negative moves from the verb into the subject; ‘return’ is distributed between “cometh” and “again”, with the destination between them.

> 61 cotal son io, chè quasi tutta cessa  
> 62 mia visione, ed ancor mi distilla  
> 63 nel cor lo dolce che nacque da essa.  

even such am I; for almost wholly faileth me my vision, yet doth the sweetness that was born of it still drop within my heart.

- *cotal* ‘such’ → “even such”: “even” reinforces the comparison.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *quasi tutta cessa mia visione* ‘almost all ceases, my vision’ → “almost wholly faileth me my vision”: the feminine adjective agreeing with ‘vision’ becomes an adverb modifying the verb; ceasing becomes failing a person, with “me” supplied.
- *ed ancor mi distilla nel cor* ‘and still to me drips into the heart’ → “yet doth” … “still drop within my heart”: “yet” makes the contrast explicit; “doth” carries the tense before the postponed subject, “still” moves next to “drop”, and the dative becomes the possessive “my”.
- *lo dolce che nacque da essa* ‘the sweet that was born from it’ → “the sweetness that was born of it”: the substantive adjective becomes an abstract noun, and the whole subject moves from after the phrase about the heart to before “still drop”.

> 64 Così la neve al sol si disigilla,  

So doth the snow unstamp it to the sun,

- *la neve al sol si disigilla* ‘the snow at the sun unseals itself’ → “doth the snow unstamp it to the sun”: the auxiliary precedes the subject, “unstamp” replaces ‘unseal’, and “it” serves for the reflexive; the phrase identifying the sun is placed last with “to” for the Italian preposition.

> 65 così al vento nelle foglie lievi  
> 66 si perdea la sentenza di Sibilla.  

so to the wind on the light leaves was lost the Sybil’s wisdom.

- *nelle foglie lievi* ‘in the leaves light’ → “on the light leaves”: the preposition changes, and the adjective precedes its noun.
- *si perdea* ‘was being lost’ → “was lost”: the imperfect middle or passive is rendered by a simple past passive.
- *la sentenza di Sibilla* ‘the utterance of the Sibyl’ → “the Sybil’s wisdom”: the possessor comes first, and “wisdom” gives the import of the utterance rather than naming the utterance itself.

> 67 O somma luce, che tanto ti levi  
> 68 dai concetti mortali, alla mia mente  
> 69 ripresta un poco di quel che parevi,  

O light supreme who so far dost uplift thee o’er mortal thoughts, re-lend unto my mind a little of what then thou didst seem,

- *somma luce* ‘highest light’ → “light supreme”: the adjective follows the noun.
- *che tanto ti levi dai concetti mortali* ‘that so much thyself raisest above the conceptions of mortals’ → “who so far dost uplift thee o’er mortal thoughts”: “who” treats the light as a personal addressee; degree becomes distance, the reflexive follows the verb with its auxiliary, and “mortal” precedes “thoughts”.
- *alla mia mente ripresta* ‘unto my mind lend again’ → “re-lend unto my mind”: the imperative moves before the recipient.
- *di quel che parevi* ‘of that which thou didst seem’ → “of what then thou didst seem”: “what” combines antecedent and relative, and “then” makes the past reference explicit.

## Lines 70–102 — The Vision of Eternal Light

> 70 e fa la lingua mia tanto possente,  
> 71 ch' una favilla sol della tua gloria  
> 72 possa lasciare alla futura gente;  

and give my tongue such power that it may leave only a single sparkle of thy glory unto the folk to come;

- *fa la lingua mia tanto possente* ‘make the tongue of mine so powerful’ → “give my tongue such power”: the tongue becomes the recipient rather than the object of the action; the possessive comes before the noun, and the adjective of degree becomes a noun qualified by “such”.
- *ch' una favilla sol della tua gloria possa lasciare* ‘that a spark alone of thy glory it may leave’ → “that it may leave only a single sparkle of thy glory”: the verb comes before its object; “only” comes before the noun phrase, and “a single” makes the numerical force of *una* explicit.
- *alla futura gente* ‘to the future people’ → “unto the folk to come”: the adjective before the noun becomes an infinitive phrase after it.

> 73 chè, per tornare alquanto a mia memoria,  
> 74 e per sonare un poco in questi versi,  
> 75 più si conceperà di tua vittoria.  

for by returning to my memory somewhat, and by a little sounding in these verses, more of thy victory will be conceived.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *per tornare alquanto a mia memoria* ‘through returning somewhat to my memory’ → “by returning to my memory somewhat”: the infinitive after a preposition becomes a gerund, and the adverb moves after the phrase of destination.
- *per sonare un poco* ‘through sounding a little’ → “by a little sounding”: the infinitive again becomes a gerund, with the expression of degree moved before it.
- *più si conceperà di tua vittoria* ‘more will be conceived of thy victory’ → “more of thy victory will be conceived”: the *si* construction is rendered by an English passive, and the partitive phrase is brought forward to stand with “more”.

> 76 Io credo, per l'acume ch' io soffersi  
> 77 del vivo raggio, ch' io sarei smarrito,  
> 78 se gli occhi miei da lui fossero aversi.  

I hold that by the keenness of the living ray which I endured I had been lost, had mine eyes turned aside from it.

- *Io credo … ch' io sarei smarrito* ‘I believe … that I should be lost’ → “I hold that” … “I had been lost”: the verb of belief becomes “hold”; its “that” is brought forward before the intervening phrase, and the hypothetical result is expressed as a past counterfactual.
- *per l'acume ch' io soffersi del vivo raggio* ‘by the keenness that I endured of the living ray’ → “by the keenness of the living ray which I endured”: the phrase specifying whose keenness it was comes before the relative clause rather than after it, so that “which” now stands next to “ray”.
- *se gli occhi miei da lui fossero aversi* ‘if the eyes of mine from it were turned away’ → “had mine eyes turned aside from it”: inversion replaces the conditional conjunction; the possessive precedes the noun, and the eyes are represented as having turned rather than as being turned away. The phrase of separation follows the verb.

> 79 E mi ricorda ch' io fui più ardito  
> 80 per questo a sostener tanto ch' io giunsi  
> 81 l'aspetto mio col valor infinito.  

And so I was the bolder, as I mind me, so long to sustain it as to unite my glance with the Worth infinite.

- *E mi ricorda ch'* ‘and it comes back to me that’ → “And” … “as I mind me”: the impersonal construction governing a clause becomes a personal, parenthetic expression of recollection, placed after the assertion recalled.
- *io fui più ardito per questo* ‘I was more daring on this account’ → “so I was the bolder”: the expression of cause is reduced to an initial adverb, and the comparative takes “the”.
- *a sostener tanto ch' io giunsi* ‘to sustain so much that I joined’ → “so long to sustain it as to unite”: the expression of degree is read as duration and brought before the infinitive; the object of “sustain” is made explicit, and a second infinitive replaces the finite result clause.
- *l'aspetto mio* ‘the gaze of mine’ → “my glance”: the possessive comes first, and “glance” renders the noun for the act of looking.
- *col valor infinito* ‘with the infinite worth’ → “with the Worth infinite”: the translation retains the Italian noun–adjective order rather than the usual English order.

> 82 O abbondante grazia, ond' io presunsi  
> 83 ficcar lo viso per la luce eterna  
> 84 tanto che la veduta vi consunsi!  

Oh grace abounding, wherein I presumed to fix my look on the eternal light so long that I consumed my sight thereon!

- *abbondante grazia* ‘abounding grace’ → “grace abounding”: the participial adjective follows the noun instead of preceding it.
- *ond'* ‘by which’ → “wherein”: the relative is rendered as expressing circumstance rather than means.
- *ficcar lo viso per la luce eterna* ‘to drive the gaze through the light eternal’ → “to fix my look on the eternal light”: forceful penetration becomes fixation on an object; the definite article becomes a possessive, and the adjective precedes its noun.
- *tanto che* ‘so far that’ → “so long that”: the degree to which the gaze penetrates is rendered as duration.
- *la veduta vi consunsi* ‘the sight there I consumed’ → “I consumed my sight thereon”: the object follows the verb and takes a possessive; the locative pronoun becomes a final adverb specifying what the sight was spent on.

> 85 Nel suo profondo vidi che s' interna,  
> 86 legato con amore in un volume,  
> 87 ciò che per l'universo si squaderna;  

Within its depths I saw ingathered, bound by love in one volume, the scattered leaves of all the universe;

- *Nel suo profondo* ‘in its depth’ → “Within its depths”: the substantivized singular adjective becomes a plural noun.
- *vidi che s' interna* ‘I saw that there lies within’ → “I saw ingathered”: a finite clause in the present becomes a participle dependent on “saw”; being contained within is rendered as being gathered in.
- *con amore* ‘with love’ → “by love”: accompaniment or means becomes the agency of the binding.
- *ciò che per l'universo si squaderna* ‘that which throughout the universe is spread out in sheets’ → “the scattered leaves of all the universe”: the relative construction becomes a noun phrase; the sheets implicit in the verb become “leaves”, the action becomes a participial adjective, and distribution throughout becomes an “of” relation with “all” added.

> 88 sustanzia ed accidenti, e lor costume,  
> 89 quasi conflati insieme per tal modo,  
> 90 che ciò ch' io dico è un semplice lume.  

substance and accidents and their relations, as though together fused, after such fashion that what I tell of is one simple flame.

- *lor costume* ‘their manner’ → “their relations”: a singular noun for manner or customary behaviour is interpreted as plural relations.
- *quasi conflati insieme* ‘as if fused together’ → “as though together fused”: the adverb precedes the participle instead of following it.
- *per tal modo* ‘in such a way’ → “after such fashion”: a different prepositional idiom expresses manner.
- *ciò ch' io dico* ‘that which I say’ → “what I tell of”: the direct object of saying becomes the subject matter told of.
- *un semplice lume* ‘a simple light’ → “one simple flame”: “one” gives the article numerical force, and “flame” is more specific than ‘light’.

> 91 La forma universal di questo nodo  
> 92 credo ch' io vidi, perchè più di largo,  
> 93 dicendo questo, mi sento ch' io godo.  

The universal form of this complex I think that I beheld, because more largely, as I say this, I feel that I rejoice.

- *La forma universal* ‘the form universal’ → “The universal form”: the adjective comes before the noun.
- *di questo nodo* ‘of this knot’ → “of this complex”: an abstract noun replaces the image of a knot.
- *credo ch' io vidi* ‘I believe that I saw’ → “I think that I beheld”: belief is phrased as thinking, and “beheld” replaces the ordinary verb of seeing.
- *più di largo* ‘more at large’ → “more largely”: a prepositional expression becomes an adverb.
- *dicendo questo* ‘saying this’ → “as I say this”: the gerund becomes a finite temporal clause.
- *mi sento ch' io godo* ‘I feel in myself that I rejoice’ → “I feel that I rejoice”: the reflexive element has no separate equivalent.

> 94 Un punto solo m' è maggior letargo,  
> 95 che venticinque secoli alla impresa,  
> 96 che fe' Nettuno ammirar l' ombra d' Argo.  

A single moment maketh a deeper lethargy for me than twenty and five centuries have wrought on the emprise that erst threw Neptune in amaze at Argo’s shadow.

- *Un punto solo* ‘a point alone’ → “A single moment”: the point is interpreted as a point of time, and its qualifier precedes the noun.
- *m' è maggior letargo* ‘is to me greater oblivion’ → “maketh a deeper lethargy for me”: identity becomes causation; “deeper” expresses degree, an article is supplied, and the pronoun becomes a final prepositional phrase.
- *che venticinque secoli alla impresa* ‘than twenty-five centuries to the enterprise’ → “than twenty and five centuries have wrought on the emprise”: the numeral is expanded, and the elliptical comparison is completed with a perfect verb and a preposition marking what was acted upon.
- *che fe' Nettuno ammirar l' ombra d' Argo* ‘that made Neptune marvel at the shadow of Argo’ → “that erst threw Neptune in amaze at Argo’s shadow”: “erst” supplies a time adverb; the causative verb and infinitive become a verb and a phrase of resulting state, and the possessor moves before “shadow”.

> 97 Così la mente mia, tutta sospesa,  
> 98 mirava fissa, immobile ed attenta,  
> 99 e sempre del mirar faceasi accesa.  

Thus all suspended did my mind gaze fixed, immovable, intent, ever enkindled by its gazing.

- *la mente mia, tutta sospesa, mirava* ‘the mind of mine, wholly suspended, was gazing’ → “all suspended did my mind gaze”: the description moves before the subject, the possessive before the noun; inversion with “did” renders the imperfect.
- *immobile ed attenta* ‘motionless and attentive’ → “immovable, intent”: absence of movement becomes incapacity to move, “intent” renders attentiveness, and the conjunction drops out.
- *e sempre del mirar faceasi accesa* ‘and always from looking it grew enkindled’ → “ever enkindled by its gazing”: the coordinated finite clause becomes a participial phrase without the conjunction or an explicit verb of becoming; the source of the kindling follows the participle, and the substantivized infinitive takes a possessive.

> 100 A quella luce cotal si diventa,  
> 101 che volgersi da lei per altro aspetto  
> 102 è impossibil che mai si consenta.  

Such at that light doth man become that to turn thence to any other sight could not by possibility be ever yielded.

- *A quella luce cotal si diventa* ‘at that light such does one become’ → “Such at that light doth man become”: the predicate is brought to the front, and impersonal *si* is rendered with the generic subject “man”.
- *volgersi da lei per altro aspetto* ‘to turn oneself from it for another sight’ → “to turn thence to any other sight”: the verb becomes intransitive without a reflexive pronoun; the reference back to the light becomes a directional adverb, and the alternative sight is made the destination, with “any” supplied.
- *è impossibil che mai si consenta* ‘it is impossible that one should ever consent’ → “could not by possibility be ever yielded”: the impersonal predicate and subjunctive clause become a single negative modal passive; consenting to turn is expressed as the turning being yielded.

## Lines 103–135 — The Three Circles and the Human Likeness

> 103 Però che il ben, ch' è del volere obbietto,  
> 104 tutto s'accoglie in lei, e fuor di quella  
> 105 è difettivo ciò che lì è perfetto.  

For the good, which is the object of the will, is therein wholly gathered, and outside it that same thing is defective which therein is perfect.

- *Però che* ‘because’ → “For”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *è del volere obbietto* ‘is of the will the object’ → “is the object of the will”: the noun precedes its complement.
- *tutto s'accoglie in lei* ‘all gathers itself in her’ → “is therein wholly gathered”: the reflexive becomes passive, “wholly” expresses the totality adverbially, and the final prepositional phrase becomes an adverb placed within the verb phrase.
- *fuor di quella* ‘outside of that’ → “outside it”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun.
- *è difettivo ciò che* ‘is defective that which’ → “that same thing is defective which”: the antecedent moves before the verb, “same” is supplied, and the relative is separated from its antecedent.
- *lì è perfetto* ‘there is perfect’ → “therein is perfect”: “therein” makes the sense of ‘in it’ explicit.

> 106 Omai sarà più corta mia favella,  
> 107 pure a quel ch' io ricordo, che di un fante  
> 108 che bagni ancor la lingua alla mammella.  

Now shall my speech fall farther short even of what I can remember than an infant’s who still bathes his tongue at breast.

- *sarà più corta mia favella* ‘will be shorter my speech’ → “shall my speech fall farther short”: being shorter becomes falling farther short, with the subject between the auxiliary and the main verb.
- *pure a quel ch' io ricordo* ‘even in relation to that which I remember’ → “even of what I can remember”: “of” completes “fall farther short”; “what” combines antecedent and relative, and “can” is supplied.
- *che di un fante* ‘than of an infant’ → “than an infant’s”: the possessive ending replaces the preposition, leaving ‘speech’ understood.
- *che bagni ancor la lingua* ‘who should bathe still the tongue’ → “who still bathes his tongue”: the subjunctive describing an unspecified infant becomes indicative, the adverb moves before the verb, and the possessive replaces the definite article.
- *alla mammella* ‘at the breast’ → “at breast”: the article is omitted.

> 109 Non perchè più ch' un semplice sembiante  
> 110 fosse nel vivo lume ch' io mirava,  
> 111 che tal è sempre qual era davante;  

Not that more than a single semblance was in the living light whereon I looked, which ever is such as it was before;

- *Non perchè* ‘not because’ → “Not that”: English disclaims a supposition rather than introducing a rejected cause with ‘because’.
- *un semplice sembiante* ‘a simple semblance’ → “a single semblance”: the adjective is rendered in the sense of oneness.
- *fosse* ‘should be’ → “was”: the Italian imperfect subjunctive becomes an English past indicative.
- *ch' io mirava* ‘which I gazed at’ → “whereon I looked”: a direct object becomes a relative expression of direction with “looked”.
- *tal è sempre qual* ‘such is always as’ → “ever is such as”: the adverb moves before the verb and the predicate ‘such’ after it.

> 112 ma per la vista che s' avvalorava  
> 113 in me, guardando, una sola parvenza,  
> 114 mutandom' io, a me si travagliava.  

but by the sight that gathered strength in me one sole appearance even as I changed worked on my gaze.

- *per la vista* ‘because of the sight’ → “by the sight”: the causal phrase is rendered as one of means.
- *s' avvalorava in me, guardando* ‘was growing stronger in me, in looking’ → “gathered strength in me”: the imperfect reflexive verb of becoming is rendered by a simple past verb and noun, and the gerund *guardando* has no separate English rendering.
- *mutandom' io* ‘I changing’ → “even as I changed”: the reflexive gerund with its expressed subject becomes a finite temporal clause, with “even” supplied.
- *a me si travagliava* ‘to me transformed itself’ → “worked on my gaze”: instead of the appearance itself undergoing change for the beholder, the English makes it act on his gaze, specifying ‘gaze’ for the personal pronoun.

> 115 Nella profonda e chiara sussistenza  
> 116 dell' alto lume parvemi tre giri  
> 117 di tre colori e d'una continenza;  

In the profound and shining being of the deep light appeared to me three circles, of three colours and one magnitude;

- *chiara sussistenza* ‘clear subsistence’ → “shining being”: the adjective is taken in its luminous sense, and the more general “being” replaces ‘subsistence’.
- *alto* ‘high’ → “deep”: the adjective is taken in the sense of depth rather than height.
- *parvemi* ‘there appeared to me’ → “appeared to me”: the singular Italian verb precedes the plural subject; the English past form does not mark that number.
- *d'una continenza* ‘of one extent’ → “one magnitude”: the second ‘of’ is left understood from “of three colours”, and extent is expressed as magnitude.

> 118 e l'un dall' altro, come Iri da Iri,  
> 119 parea riflesso, e il terzo parea foco  
> 120 che quinci e quindi egualmente si spiri.  

one by the second as Iris by Iris seemed reflected, and the third seemed a fire breathed equally from one and from the other.

- *e l'un dall' altro* ‘and the one from the other’ → “one by the second”: the opening conjunction and article are omitted, ‘from’ becomes the “by” of reflection, and ‘the other’ is identified by ordinal number.
- *come Iri da Iri* ‘as Iris from Iris’ → “as Iris by Iris”: the relation of source is again expressed with “by”.
- *foco* ‘fire’ → “a fire”: an indefinite article is supplied.
- *che quinci e quindi egualmente si spiri* ‘which from here and from there equally is breathed’ → “breathed equally from one and from the other”: the relative clause with a subjunctive passive becomes a participial phrase; the adverb follows the participle, and the two adverbs of place become explicit references to the two sources.

> 121 O quanto è corto il dire, e come fioco  
> 122 al mio concetto! e questo, a quel ch' io vidi,  
> 123 è tanto che non basta a dicer poco.  

Oh but how scant the utterance, and how faint, to my conceit! and it, to what I saw, is such that it sufficeth not to call it little.

- *O quanto è corto il dire* ‘O how short is the saying’ → “Oh but how scant the utterance”: “but” is supplied, the copula is omitted, “scant” replaces ‘short’, and a noun replaces the substantival infinitive.
- *al mio concetto* ‘to my conception’ → “to my conceit”: “conceit” is used in the sense of something conceived.
- *questo* ‘this’ → “it”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun.
- *a quel ch' io vidi* ‘to that which I saw’ → “to what I saw”: “what” combines antecedent and relative.
- *è tanto che* ‘is so much that’ → “is such that”: degree is expressed by “such” rather than a word of quantity.
- *non basta a dicer poco* ‘it does not suffice to say little’ → “it sufficeth not to call it little”: ‘say’ is construed as naming or characterizing, and “call” takes an explicit object with “little” as its complement.

> 124 O luce eterna, che sola in te sidi,  
> 125 sola t' intendi, e, da te intelletta  
> 126 ed intendente te, ami ed arridi!  

O Light eternal who only in thyself abidest, only thyself dost understand, and to thyself, self-understood self-understanding, turnest love and smiling!

- *che sola in te sidi* ‘who alone in thyself sittest’ → “who only in thyself abidest”: the adjective agreeing with the addressee becomes an adverb, and sitting is rendered as abiding.
- *sola t' intendi* ‘alone thyself thou understandest’ → “only thyself dost understand”: “only” again replaces the adjective, and “dost” carries the finite inflection.
- *da te intelletta ed intendente te* ‘by thyself understood and understanding thyself’ → “self-understood self-understanding”: the agent of the passive and the object of the active participle both become “self-” in compounds; the conjunction is omitted.
- *ami ed arridi* ‘thou lovest and smilest upon’ → “to thyself,” … “turnest love and smiling”: the two finite verbs become a noun and a verbal noun dependent on the supplied “turnest”, with a common recipient made explicit and placed before the participles.

> 127 Quella circulazion, che sì concetta  
> 128 pareva in te come lume riflesso,  
> 129 dagli occhi miei alquanto circonspetta,  

That circling which appeared in thee to be conceived as a reflected light, by mine eyes scanned some little,

- *circulazion* ‘circulation’ → “circling”: a verbal noun in ‘-ing’ renders the Italian noun.
- *sì concetta pareva in te* ‘so conceived appeared in thee’ → “appeared in thee to be conceived”: the participle moves after the verb of seeming and becomes a passive infinitive; ‘so’, anticipating the comparison, has no separate equivalent.
- *come lume riflesso* ‘as light reflected’ → “as a reflected light”: an indefinite article is supplied and the participle precedes the noun.
- *alquanto circonspetta* ‘somewhat looked round’ → “scanned some little”: “scanned” renders the participle without a distinct word for ‘round’, and the expression of degree follows it.

> 130 dentro da sè del suo colore stesso  
> 131 mi parve pinta della nostra effige,  
> 132 per che il mio viso in lei tutto era messo.  

in itself, of its own colour, seemed to be painted with our effigy, and thereat my sight was all committed to it.

- *dentro da sè* ‘within itself’ → “in itself”: the compound expression of interior position becomes a simple preposition.
- *del suo colore stesso* ‘of its colour itself’ → “of its own colour”: the emphatic adjective after the noun becomes “own” before it.
- *mi parve pinta* ‘to me it appeared painted’ → “seemed to be painted”: the perceiver is not separately expressed and the participle becomes a passive infinitive.
- *della nostra effige* ‘of our effigy’ → “with our effigy”: the preposition changes after “painted”.
- *per che* ‘for which reason’ → “and thereat”: a connective relative expression becomes a conjunction and an adverb.
- *il mio viso in lei tutto era messo* ‘my sight in it wholly was put’ → “my sight was all committed to it”: ‘put in’ becomes “committed to”, with the auxiliary before “all” and the prepositional complement last.

> 133 Qual è 'l geometra che tutto s'affige  
> 134 per misurar lo cerchio, e non ritrova,  
> 135 pensando, quel principio ond' egli indige;  

As the geometer who all sets himself to measure the circle and who findeth not, think as he may, the principle he lacketh;

- *Qual è 'l geometra* ‘as is the geometer’ → “As the geometer”: the verb of the comparison is omitted.
- *tutto s'affige* ‘wholly fixes himself’ → “all sets himself”: ‘fixes’ becomes “sets” in the expression for applying oneself to a task.
- *per misurar* ‘in order to measure’ → “to measure”: the infinitive alone expresses purpose.
- *e non ritrova* ‘and does not find’ → “and who findeth not”: the relative pronoun is repeated, and the negative follows the finite verb.
- *pensando* ‘thinking’ → “think as he may”: the gerund becomes an explicitly concessive clause.
- *quel principio ond' egli indige* ‘that principle of which he has need’ → “the principle he lacketh”: the demonstrative becomes a definite article; “lacketh” takes a direct object, allowing the relative to stand unexpressed.

## Lines 136–145 — The Flash and the Turning Will

> 136 tale era io a quella vista nuova:  

such was I at this new seen spectacle;

- *quella* ‘that’ → “this”: the demonstrative changes from distant to near.
- *vista nuova* ‘sight new’ → “new seen spectacle”: the modifier comes before the noun, and “seen” is supplied, making the spectacle newly seen rather than simply new.

> 137 veder voleva, come si convenne  
> 138 l' imago al cerchio, e come vi s' indova;  

I would perceive how the image consorteth with the circle, and how it settleth there;

- *veder voleva* ‘to see I wished’ → “I would perceive”: the infinitive, placed first in Italian, follows the English verb of volition; ‘see’ becomes “perceive”.
- *come si convenne l' imago al cerchio* ‘how fitted the image to the circle’ → “how the image consorteth with the circle”: the subject moves before the verb; the Italian pronominal verb in the past becomes a simple verb in the present, and its complement takes “with” rather than ‘to’.
- *vi s' indova* ‘there it in-wheres itself’ → “it settleth there”: the coined reflexive verb formed on *dove* ‘where’ becomes an ordinary intransitive verb, and the locative follows it.

> 139 ma non eran da ciò le proprie penne;  

but not for this were my proper wings,

- *non eran da ciò* ‘were not equal to this’ → “not for this were”: the Italian construction expresses fitness for a task; the English puts the negated prepositional phrase before the verb.
- *le proprie penne* ‘one’s own feathers’ → “my proper wings”: the ownership is made explicitly first-person, “proper” retains the sense ‘own’, and the feathers are rendered as the wings.

> 140 se non che la mia mente fu percossa  
> 141 da un fulgore, in che sua voglia venne.  

save that my mind was smitten by a flash wherein its will came to it.

- *se non che* ‘if not that’ → “save that”: an English exceptive conjunction replaces the Italian conditional turn.
- *in che* ‘in which’ → “wherein”: the preposition and relative pronoun become a single relative adverb.
- *sua voglia venne* ‘its desire came’ → “its will came to it”: “will” renders the desire, and “to it” supplies a recipient not expressed with the Italian verb.

> 142 All'alta fantasia qui mancò possa;  

To the high fantasy here power failed;

- *mancò possa* ‘failed power’ → “power failed”: the subject moves before the verb.

> 143 ma già volgeva il mio disiro e il *velle*,  
> 144 sì come rota ch' egualmente è mossa,  
> 145 l' amor che move il sole e l' altre stelle.  

but already my desire and will were rolled—even as a wheel that moveth equally—by the Love that moves the sun and the other stars.

- *volgeva* ‘was turning’ → “were rolled”: the singular active imperfect becomes a plural passive; the two Italian objects become the English subject and thus precede the verb.
- *e il velle* ‘and the willing’ → “and will”: the substantivized Latin infinitive becomes an English noun coordinated under the preceding possessive.
- *sì come rota* ‘just as a wheel’ → “even as a wheel”: “even” gives the emphasis of *sì*, and English supplies the indefinite article before the bare singular noun.
- *ch' egualmente è mossa* ‘that evenly is moved’ → “that moveth equally”: the passive becomes an intransitive active verb, and the adverb moves after it.
- *l' amor* ‘the love’ → “by the Love”: the delayed Italian subject becomes the agent of the English passive, introduced by “by”.

## Summary — Close in Phrasing, Freer in Construction

Wicksteed often keeps the Italian phrasing even where its order is unusual in English: *valor infinito* remains “Worth infinite”, and the repeated *in te* is answered by “In thee” and then “in thee”. Elsewhere he moves adjectives and possessives before their nouns and verbs before their objects. Thus *tutti i miei preghi ti porgo* ‘all my prayers to thee I proffer’ becomes “proffer thee all my prayers”. Two routine changes generally go unmarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending identifies the subject, and omits the Italian article before a possessive, as in *la mia mente*, “my mind”.

The choice of words can preserve an Italian echo without following each word literally: *Fattore* and *fattura* are paired as “Maker” and “making”. At other times the image itself changes: *alta* ‘high’ becomes “deep” when it qualifies the light, and *nodo* ‘knot’ becomes “complex”. Small additions often make an implication explicit: *giuso* ‘below’ becomes “there below” in contrast with “Here”, and *il suo Fattore* ‘its Maker’ gains emphasis as “its own Maker”. Conversely, *guardando* ‘in looking’ has no separate English equivalent.

Changes of construction can affect a phrase or a whole sentence. The nominalized infinitive *veder* ‘seeing’ becomes “vision”, while the gerund *pensando* ‘thinking’ expands into the concessive “think as he may”. At the close, Wicksteed keeps the order in which desire and will, the wheel, and love are named, but changes the grammar that holds them together: the objects of *volgeva* ‘was turning’ become the subject of “were rolled”, and the delayed Italian subject returns as “by the Love”. Here the departure from word-for-word syntax is precisely what allows the prose to follow the Italian sequence.
