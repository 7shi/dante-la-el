# Paradiso 1

## Lines 1–21 — Glory, Memory, and the Invocation to Apollo

> 1 La gloria di colui che tutto move  
> 2 per l' universo penetra, e risplende  
> 3 in una parte più, e meno altrove.  

The All-mover’s glory penetrates through the universe, and regloweth in one region more, and less in another.

- *La gloria di colui che tutto move* ‘the glory of him who moves all’ → “The All-mover’s glory”: the pronoun and relative clause become a compound noun, and the possessor precedes what is possessed.
- *per l' universo penetra* ‘through the universe penetrates’ → “penetrates through the universe”: the verb comes before the prepositional phrase.
- *risplende* ‘shines’ → “regloweth”: Wicksteed uses a verb formed on ‘glow’ rather than ‘shine’.
- *in una parte* ‘in one part’ → “in one region”: ‘part’ is made specifically a place.
- *altrove* ‘elsewhere’ → “in another”: the adverb becomes a prepositional phrase, with “region” understood after “another”.

> 4 Nel ciel che più della sua luce prende  
> 5 fu' io; e vidi cose che ridire  
> 6 nè sa nè può qual di lassù discende;  

In that heaven which most receiveth of his light, have I been; and have seen things which whoso descendeth from up there hath nor knowledge nor power to re-tell;

- *Nel ciel* ‘in the heaven’ → “In that heaven”: a demonstrative replaces the definite article.
- *che più della sua luce prende* ‘which most of his light takes’ → “which most receiveth of his light”: ‘takes’ becomes ‘receives’, and the verb moves before the partitive phrase.
- *fu' io* ‘was I’ → “have I been”: the simple past becomes a present perfect, with the subject between auxiliary and participle.
- *vidi* ‘I saw’ → “have seen”: the simple past again becomes a present perfect, now sharing the preceding subject.
- *ridire nè sa nè può* ‘to tell again neither knows how nor is able’ → “hath nor knowledge nor power to re-tell”: the two finite verbs become nouns governed by a single verb of possession, and the infinitive moves to the end.
- *qual di lassù discende* ‘whoever from up there descends’ → “whoso descendeth from up there”: the subject clause moves before its predicate, and within the clause the verb moves before the phrase of origin.

> 7 perchè, appressando sè al suo disire,  
> 8 nostro intelletto si profonda tanto,  
> 9 che retro la memoria non può ire.  

because, as it draweth nigh to its desire, our intellect sinketh so deep, that memory cannot go back upon the track.

- *appressando sè al suo disire* ‘bringing itself near to its desire’ → “as it draweth nigh to its desire”: the reflexive gerund becomes a finite temporal clause with an intransitive verb.
- *si profonda tanto* ‘deepens itself so much’ → “sinketh so deep”: the reflexive verb becomes an intransitive verb of motion, with depth expressed separately.
- *retro la memoria non può ire* ‘back the memory cannot go’ → “memory cannot go back upon the track”: ‘back’ moves after ‘go’, the abstract noun loses its article, and the image of a track is supplied.

> 10 Veramente quant' io del regno santo  
> 11 nella mia mente potei far tesoro,  
> 12 sarà ora materia del mio canto.  

Nathless, whatever of the holy realm I had the power to treasure in my memory, shall now be matter of my song.

- *Veramente* ‘truly’ → “Nathless”: Wicksteed takes the adverb in its adversative sense, ‘nevertheless’.
- *quant' io del regno santo* ‘as much as I of the realm holy’ → “whatever of the holy realm I”: the expression of quantity becomes an indefinite relative, the subject moves after the phrase naming the realm, and the adjective precedes its noun.
- *nella mia mente* ‘in my mind’ → “in my memory”: ‘mind’ is narrowed to ‘memory’, and the phrase moves after the verb of treasuring.
- *potei far tesoro* ‘I could make treasure’ → “had the power to treasure”: ability is expressed by a noun phrase, and the verb with its noun complement becomes a single verb.

> 13 O buono Apollo, all' ultimo lavoro  
> 14 fammi del tuo valor sì fatto vaso,  
> 15 come dimandi a dar l' amato alloro.  

O good Apollo, for the crowning task, make me a so-fashioned vessel of thy worth, as thou demandest for the grant of thy beloved laurel.

- *all' ultimo lavoro* ‘for the last work’ → “for the crowning task”: ‘work’ becomes ‘task’, and ‘last’ is rendered as culminating rather than simply final.
- *del tuo valor sì fatto vaso* ‘of thy worth so fashioned a vessel’ → “a so-fashioned vessel of thy worth”: the unordered-in-English complement is placed after ‘vessel’, an indefinite article is supplied, and the words of degree and fashion become a compound modifier.
- *a dar* ‘to give’ → “for the grant of”: the infinitive becomes a prepositional phrase with a noun of action.
- *l' amato alloro* ‘the beloved laurel’ → “thy beloved laurel”: a possessive replaces the definite article.

> 16 Infino a qui l' un giogo di Parnaso  
> 17 assai mi fu, ma or con ambo e due  
> 18 m' è uopo entrar nell' aringo rimaso.  

Up till here one peak of Parnassus hath sufficed me; but now, with both the two, needs must I enter this last wrestling-ground.

- *l' un giogo* ‘the one ridge’ → “one peak”: the article drops out, and the ridge is rendered as a peak.
- *assai mi fu* ‘enough for me it was’ → “hath sufficed me”: the expression with ‘enough’ becomes a verb of sufficiency in the present perfect, followed by its object.
- *con ambo e due* ‘with both and two’ → “with both the two”: the emphatic doubling is retained, but an article replaces the conjunction.
- *m' è uopo entrar* ‘it is needful for me to enter’ → “needs must I enter”: the impersonal expression becomes a personal modal construction, with the person experiencing the need as subject.
- *nell' aringo rimaso* ‘into the arena remaining’ → “this last wrestling-ground”: ‘enter’ takes a direct object rather than a directional phrase; a demonstrative replaces the article, ‘arena’ is specified as a wrestling-ground, and the following participle becomes a preceding adjective.

> 19 Entra nel petto mio, e spira tue  
> 20 sì come quando Marsia traesti  
> 21 della vagina delle membra sue.  

Into my bosom enter thou, and so breathe as when thou drewest Marsyas from out what sheathed his limbs.

- *Entra nel petto mio* ‘enter into my breast’ → “Into my bosom enter thou”: the phrase of direction is fronted, ‘breast’ becomes ‘bosom’, and the possessive precedes its noun. The explicit pronoun of the following Italian imperative is attached to this first imperative instead.
- *spira tue sì* ‘breathe thou so’ → “so breathe”: the adverb precedes the imperative, which here has no expressed pronoun.
- *Marsia traesti* ‘Marsyas thou drewest’ → “thou drewest Marsyas”: the object follows the verb.
- *della vagina delle membra sue* ‘from the sheath of the limbs of his’ → “from out what sheathed his limbs”: ‘from out’ conveys emergence, the noun ‘sheath’ becomes a verb in a relative clause, and the limbs become its direct object, with the possessive before the noun.

## Lines 22–54 — The Prayer for the Laurel and the Gaze at the Sun

> 22 O divina virtù, se mi ti presti  
> 23 tanto che l' ombra del beato regno  
> 24 segnata nel mio capo io manifesti,  

O divine Virtue, if thou dost so far lend thyself to me, that I make manifest the shadow of the blessed realm imprinted on my brain,

- *mi ti presti tanto* ‘to me thyself thou lendest so much’ → “dost so far lend thyself to me”: the verb takes an auxiliary, the expression of degree moves before “lend”, and the two pronouns follow it in reverse order.
- *l' ombra del beato regno segnata nel mio capo* ‘the shadow of the blessed realm marked in my head’ → “the shadow of the blessed realm imprinted on my brain”: the whole object moves after the verb; the mark in the head becomes an imprint on the brain.
- *io manifesti* ‘I may manifest’ → “I make manifest”: the single verb becomes a verb and adjective, and the Italian subjunctive after the expression of degree has no distinct English form here.

> 25 venir vedra' mi al tuo diletto legno,  
> 26 e coronarmi allor di quelle foglie,  
> 27 che la materia e tu mi farai degno.  

thou shalt see me come to thy chosen tree and crown me, then, with the leaves of which the matter and thou shalt make me worthy.

- *venir vedra' mi* ‘come thou wilt see me’ → “thou shalt see me come”: the infinitive moves after the verb of seeing and its object.
- *al tuo diletto legno* ‘to thy beloved wood’ → “to thy chosen tree”: being loved becomes being chosen, and the wood is named as the living tree.
- *coronarmi allor* ‘to crown myself then’ → “crown me, then”: the coordinated infinitive takes the bare English form, and the reflexive is expressed by “me” rather than ‘myself’.
- *di quelle foglie* ‘of those leaves’ → “with the leaves”: English uses the preposition of material appropriate to “crown”, and the demonstrative becomes an article.
- *che* ‘which’ → “of which”: English makes explicit the relation to “worthy”.
- *mi farai degno* ‘me thou wilt make worthy’ → “shalt make me worthy”: the object moves after the verb.

> 28 Sì rade volte, padre, se ne coglie,  
> 29 per trionfare o Cesare o poeta,  
> 30 colpa e vergogna dell' umane voglie,  

So few times, Father, is there gathered of it, for triumph or of Caesar or of poet,—fault and shame of human wills,—

- *Sì rade volte* ‘so rare times’ → “So few times”: rarity is expressed as smallness of number.
- *se ne coglie* ‘one gathers of it’ → “is there gathered of it”: the impersonal construction becomes a passive with introductory “there”, and the partitive pronoun follows the verb as a prepositional phrase.
- *per trionfare o Cesare o poeta* ‘for either Caesar or poet to triumph’ → “for triumph or of Caesar or of poet”: the infinitive becomes a noun, its alternative subjects become phrases with “of”, and the first member of the pair is introduced by “or”.
- *dell' umane voglie* ‘of the human desires’ → “of human wills”: the article drops out, and desire is expressed as will.

> 31 che partorir letizia in sulla lieta  
> 32 delfica deità dovria la fronda  
> 33 Peneia, quando alcun di sè asseta.  

that the Peneian frond should bring forth gladness in the joyous Delphic deity, when it sets any athirst for itself.

- *partorir letizia … dovria* ‘bring forth gladness … it should’ → “should bring forth gladness”: the modal moves before the infinitive instead of being separated from it by the object and the following phrase.
- *in sulla lieta delfica deità* ‘upon the joyous Delphic deity’ → “in the joyous Delphic deity”: English locates the gladness within the deity.
- *la fronda Peneia* ‘the frond Peneian’ → “the Peneian frond”: the subject moves from after the verb to before it, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *alcun di sè asseta* ‘anyone for itself it makes thirsty’ → “it sets any athirst for itself”: the single causative verb becomes a verb and predicate adjective; the object follows “sets”, and the phrase naming what is thirsted for comes last.

> 34 Poca favilla gran fiamma seconda:  

A mighty flame followeth a tiny spark;

- *Poca favilla gran fiamma seconda* ‘a little spark a great flame follows’ → “A mighty flame followeth a tiny spark”: the Italian order is object, subject, verb; English puts the subject first and the object last, supplies both articles, and renders smallness and greatness as tininess and might.

> 35 forse retro da me con miglior voci  
> 36 si pregherà perchè Cirra risponda.  

perchance, after me, shall prayer with better voices be so offered that Cirrha may respond.

- *retro da me* ‘behind me’ → “after me”: the relation is expressed in temporal rather than spatial terms.
- *con miglior voci si pregherà* ‘with better voices one will pray’ → “shall prayer with better voices be so offered”: the impersonal future becomes a passive with a noun for the act of praying; “with better voices” now follows “prayer”, and “so” is supplied to lead into the following clause.
- *perchè* ‘in order that’ → “that”: the purpose conjunction is rendered by “that” in conjunction with the preceding “so”.

> 37 Surge ai mortali per diverse foci  
> 38 la lucerna del mondo; ma da quella,  
> 39 che quattro cerchi giunge con tre croci,  

The lantern of the universe riseth unto mortals through divers straits; but from that which joineth four circles in three crosses

- *Surge ai mortali per diverse foci* ‘rises to the mortals through diverse mouths’ → “riseth unto mortals through divers straits”: the predicate moves after the subject, the generic article drops out, and the openings are called “straits”.
- *la lucerna del mondo* ‘the lamp of the world’ → “The lantern of the universe”: the subject moves to the head of the sentence; “lantern” and “universe” replace ‘lamp’ and ‘world’.
- *quattro cerchi giunge* ‘four circles it joins’ → “joineth four circles”: the object moves after the verb.
- *con tre croci* ‘with three crosses’ → “in three crosses”: the preposition changes.

> 40 con miglior corso e con migliore stella  
> 41 esce congiunta, e la mondana cera  
> 42 più a suo modo tempera e suggella.  

he issueth with more propitious course, and united with a more propitious star, and doth temper and stamp the mundane wax more after his own mood.

- *con miglior corso* ‘with better course’ → “with more propitious course”: the comparative is given the more specific sense of favorableness, and the phrase moves after the verb of issuing.
- *con migliore stella* ‘with better star’ → “with a more propitious star”: the comparative is again narrowed in sense, an article is supplied, and the phrase moves after the participle it complements.
- *esce congiunta* ‘she issues conjoined’ → “he issueth” … “united”: the feminine agreement with *lucerna* gives way to a masculine pronoun; the verb comes before the course phrase, while the participle follows it.
- *la mondana cera più a suo modo tempera e suggella* ‘the mundane wax more in its own manner it tempers and seals’ → “doth temper and stamp the mundane wax more after his own mood”: the object moves from before both verbs to after them, and the phrase of manner moves to the end. Manner becomes “mood”, the possessive is masculine, the verbs take a shared auxiliary, and sealing is expressed as stamping.

> 43 Fatto avea di là mane e di qua sera  
> 44 tal foce quasi, e tutto era là bianco  
> 45 quello emisperio, e l' altra parte nera,  

Almost this strait had made morning on that side and evening on this; and there that hemisphere all was aglow, and the other region darkling;

- *Fatto avea* ‘made it had’ → “had made”: the auxiliary precedes the participle, and the verb follows its subject.
- *di là mane e di qua sera* ‘on that side morning and on this side evening’ → “morning on that side and evening on this”: each expression of place moves after the noun.
- *tal foce quasi* ‘such a mouth almost’ → “Almost this strait”: the delayed subject moves before the verb, “Almost” comes first, and ‘such a mouth’ becomes the demonstrative “this strait”.
- *tutto era là bianco quello emisperio* ‘all was there white that hemisphere’ → “there that hemisphere all was aglow”: the place and subject move to the front, “all” follows the subject, and whiteness becomes a glow.
- *l' altra parte nera* ‘the other part black’ → “the other region darkling”: “region” specifies the part as an area, and blackness is expressed as darkness.

> 46 quando Beatrice in sul sinistro fianco  
> 47 vidi rivolta, e riguardar nel sole.  

when I beheld Beatrice turned on her left side and gazing on the sun.

- *Beatrice … vidi* ‘Beatrice I saw’ → “I beheld Beatrice”: the verb moves before its object and is rendered by “beheld”.
- *in sul sinistro fianco … rivolta* ‘on the left flank … turned’ → “turned on her left side”: the participle precedes the phrase of position, the article becomes a possessive, and ‘flank’ becomes “side”.
- *riguardar nel sole* ‘to gaze into the sun’ → “gazing on the sun”: the infinitive dependent on the verb of seeing becomes a participle, and the preposition changes.

> 48 Aquila sì non gli s' affisse unquanco.  

Never did eagle so fix himself thereon.

- *Aquila sì non gli s' affisse unquanco* ‘eagle so did not fix itself there ever yet’ → “Never did eagle so fix himself thereon”: the separated negative and temporal expressions become initial “Never”, with no separate ‘yet’; this brings auxiliary inversion, and the locative and reflexive pronouns move after “fix”, in reverse order.

> 49 E sì come secondo raggio suole  
> 50 uscir del primo, e risalire in suso,  
> 51 pur come peregrin che tornar vuole;  

And even as the second ray doth ever issue from the first, and rise back upward, (like as a pilgrim whose will is to return);

- *secondo raggio* ‘a second ray’ → “the second ray”: English supplies a definite article for the unarticled noun.
- *suole uscir del primo* ‘is accustomed to issue from the first’ → “doth ever issue from the first”: habitual action is expressed by an adverb and an auxiliary rather than by a verb governing an infinitive.
- *pur come peregrin* ‘just as a pilgrim’ → “like as a pilgrim”: the emphasis on the comparison becomes the double comparative expression “like as”; English supplies the article.
- *che tornar vuole* ‘who to return wishes’ → “whose will is to return”: the relative subject becomes possessive, the finite verb of wishing becomes a noun with a copula, and the infinitive comes last.

> 52 così dell' atto suo, per gli occhi infuso  
> 53 nell' imagine mia, il mio si fece,  
> 54 e fissi gli occhi al sole oltre a nostr' uso.  

so from her gesture, poured through the eyes into my imagination, did mine own take shape; and I fixed mine eyes upon the sun, transcending our wont.

- *dell' atto suo* ‘from the act of hers’ → “from her gesture”: ‘act’ becomes “gesture”, and the possessive precedes the noun.
- *per gli occhi infuso* ‘through the eyes poured’ → “poured through the eyes”: the participle moves before the phrase of passage.
- *nell' imagine mia* ‘into the image of mine’ → “into my imagination”: the image is rendered as the faculty of imagination, and the possessive precedes the noun.
- *il mio si fece* ‘mine was formed’ → “did mine own take shape”: the substantival possessive is strengthened by “own”; the reflexive construction becomes “take shape”, with auxiliary inversion after the introductory phrase.
- *fissi gli occhi al sole* ‘I fixed the eyes to the sun’ → “I fixed mine eyes upon the sun”: the article becomes a possessive, and English uses “upon” for the direction of the gaze.
- *oltre a nostr' uso* ‘beyond our wont’ → “transcending our wont”: the prepositional expression becomes a participial clause.

## Lines 55–87 — Passing Beyond Humanity

> 55 Molto è licito là, che qui non lece  
> 56 alle nostre virtù, mercè del loco  
> 57 fatto per proprio dell' umana spece.  

Much is granted there which is not granted here to our powers, in virtue of the place made as proper to the human race.

- *è licito là, che qui non lece* ‘is lawful there which here is not permitted’ → “is granted there which is not granted here”: the adjective and the verb are rendered by the same passive construction; “here” is moved after it.
- *virtù* ‘faculties’ → “powers”: the word is taken in the sense of capacities.
- *mercè del loco* ‘thanks to the place’ → “in virtue of the place”: English substitutes a phrase expressing the ground on which something is granted.
- *fatto per proprio dell' umana spece* ‘made for the human species’ own’ → “made as proper to the human race”: what is the species’ own becomes what is “proper to” it; *per* becomes “as”, and “race” replaces ‘species’.

> 58 Io nol soffersi molto, nè sì poco  
> 59 ch' io nol vedessi sfavillar dintorno,  
> 60 qual ferro che bogliente esce del foco.  

I not long endured him, nor yet so little but that I saw him sparkle all around, like iron issuing molten from the furnace.

- *Io nol soffersi molto* ‘I did not endure him much’ → “I not long endured him”: the measure is made explicitly one of time and moved before the verb; the object follows the verb.
- *nè sì poco ch' io nol vedessi* ‘nor so little that I did not see him’ → “nor yet so little but that I saw him”: “yet” is supplied; “but that” takes the place of the negative result clause, and the imperfect subjunctive becomes an affirmative past indicative with the object after the verb.
- *dintorno* ‘round about’ → “all around”: “all” makes the extent explicit.
- *che bogliente esce del foco* ‘which boiling comes out of the fire’ → “issuing molten from the furnace”: the finite relative becomes a participial phrase; the description of the iron moves after “issuing” and changes from ‘boiling’ to “molten”; the fire becomes the furnace.

> 61 E di subito parve giorno a giorno  
> 62 essere aggiunto, come quei che puote  
> 63 avesse il ciel d' un altro sole adorno.  

And, of a sudden, meseemed that day was added unto day, as though he who hath the power, had adorned heaven with a second sun.

- *parve giorno a giorno essere aggiunto* ‘it seemed day to day to be added’ → “meseemed that day was added unto day”: “meseemed” makes the experiencer explicit; the passive infinitive becomes a finite *that*-clause, and the second ‘day’ moves after the verb.
- *come quei che puote* ‘as he who can’ → “as though he who hath the power”: “though” makes the hypothetical comparison explicit, and the modal verb becomes a verb with a noun object.
- *avesse il ciel d' un altro sole adorno* ‘had the heaven with another sun adorned’ → “had adorned heaven with a second sun”: the participle moves next to its auxiliary, the article before ‘heaven’ disappears, and ‘another’ becomes the ordinal “a second”. The Italian pluperfect subjunctive is represented by the English past perfect after “as though”.

> 64 Beatrice tutta nell' eterne rote  
> 65 fissa con gli occhi stava: ed io in lei  
> 66 le luci fissi, di lassù remote.  

Beatrice was standing with her eyes all fixed upon the eternal wheels, and I fixed my sight, removed from there above, on her.

- *tutta nell' eterne rote fissa con gli occhi stava* ‘wholly upon the eternal wheels intent with her eyes she stood’ → “was standing with her eyes all fixed upon the eternal wheels”: the final imperfect moves to the front and becomes a progressive; *tutta* and *fissa*, feminine singular and applied to Beatrice, are recast as “all fixed” describing her eyes. The phrase naming the wheels now follows “fixed”.
- *in lei le luci fissi, di lassù remote* ‘on her my eyes I fixed, from up there withdrawn’ → “fixed my sight, removed from there above, on her”: the verb precedes its object; the plural ‘eyes’ becomes the singular “sight”, and the phrase naming its new direction moves to the end.

> 67 Nel suo aspetto tal dentro mi fei,  
> 68 qual si fe' Glauco nel gustar dell' erba,  
> 69 che il fe' consorto in mar degli altri dei.  

Gazing on her such I became within, as was Glaucus, tasting of the grass that made him the sea-fellow of the other gods.

- *Nel suo aspetto* ‘in the sight of her’ → “Gazing on her”: a prepositional phrase with a noun becomes an active participial phrase; she is explicitly the object of the gaze.
- *tal dentro mi fei* ‘such within I made myself’ → “such I became within”: the reflexive verb becomes an intransitive verb of change, and ‘within’ moves after it.
- *qual si fe' Glauco* ‘as Glaucus made himself’ → “as was Glaucus”: the second reflexive verb of change becomes a verb of state.
- *nel gustar dell' erba* ‘in the tasting of the grass’ → “tasting of the grass”: the preposition and article introducing the substantivized infinitive give way to a participle.
- *che il fe' consorto in mar degli altri dei* ‘that him made a fellow in the sea of the other gods’ → “that made him the sea-fellow of the other gods”: the object pronoun moves after the verb; the phrase of place becomes the first element of a compound noun, and “the” is supplied before that noun.

> 70 Trasumanar significar *per verba*  
> 71 non si poria; però l' esemplo basti  
> 72 a cui esperienza grazia serba.  

To pass beyond humanity may not be told in words, wherefore let the example satisfy him for whom grace reserveth the experience.

- *Trasumanar* ‘to transhumanize’ → “To pass beyond humanity”: the single coined verb is unfolded into a phrase.
- *significar per verba non si poria* ‘to signify through words one could not’ → “may not be told in words”: the impersonal conditional becomes a modal passive with the preceding infinitive as subject; ‘signify’ becomes “be told”, and the Latin phrase of means follows the verb rather than preceding the modal.
- *l' esemplo basti a cui* ‘the example may suffice for whom’ → “let the example satisfy him for whom”: “let” expresses the independent subjunctive; sufficing becomes satisfying a direct object, and “him” gives the relative an explicit antecedent.
- *esperienza grazia serba* ‘experience grace reserves’ → “grace reserveth the experience”: the object moves from before the subject to after the verb and acquires a definite article.

> 73 S' io era sol di me quel che creasti  
> 74 novellamente, Amor che il ciel governi,  
> 75 tu il sai, che col tuo lume mi levasti.  

If I was only that of me which thou didst new-create, O Love who rulest heaven, thou knowest, who with thy light didst lift me up.

- *sol di me quel che* ‘only of me that which’ → “only that of me which”: “that” moves before its partitive qualification.
- *creasti novellamente* ‘thou createdst anew’ → “thou didst new-create”: the following adverb is incorporated into a compound verb, and the past tense is carried by “didst”.
- *Amor che il ciel governi* ‘Love who heaven governest’ → “O Love who rulest heaven”: the vocative marker is supplied, the object moves after the verb and loses its article, and ‘governest’ becomes “rulest”.
- *tu il sai* ‘thou it knowest’ → “thou knowest”: the object pronoun is omitted.
- *mi levasti* ‘me thou liftedst’ → “didst lift me up”: English uses the auxiliary, puts the object after “lift”, and expresses the upward direction with a particle.

> 76 Quando la rota, che tu sempiterni  
> 77 desiderato, a sè mi fece atteso,  
> 78 con l' armonia che temperi e discerni,  

When the wheel which thou, by being longed for, makest eternal, drew unto itself my mind with the harmony which thou dost temper and distinguish,

- *sempiterni desiderato* ‘thou dost perpetuate, desired’ → “by being longed for, makest eternal”: the single verb becomes a verb and adjective; the passive participle moves before them and is explicitly construed as expressing means.
- *a sè mi fece atteso* ‘to itself made me attentive’ → “drew unto itself my mind”: making someone attentive becomes drawing his mind; ‘me’ becomes a possessive with a supplied noun, and the verb precedes the phrase of direction.
- *temperi e discerni* ‘thou temperest and distinguishest’ → “thou dost temper and distinguish”: a shared auxiliary carries the inflection of the two finite verbs.

> 79 parvemi tanto allor del cielo acceso  
> 80 dalla fiamma del sol, che pioggia o fiume  
> 81 lago non fece mai tanto disteso.  

so much of heaven then seemed to me enkindled with the sun’s flame, that rain nor river ever made a lake so wide distended.

- *parvemi tanto allor del cielo acceso* ‘seemed to me so much then of the heaven kindled’ → “so much of heaven then seemed to me enkindled”: the quantity and its partitive complement are brought together before the verb, “then” also moves before the verb, and ‘heaven’ loses its article.
- *dalla fiamma del sol* ‘by the flame of the sun’ → “with the sun’s flame”: “with” replaces ‘by’, and a preceding possessive replaces the following *of*-phrase.
- *pioggia o fiume lago non fece mai* ‘rain or river a lake never made’ → “rain nor river ever made a lake”: the negative is carried by “nor” rather than by the verb with *mai*; the object moves after the verb and takes an indefinite article.
- *tanto disteso* ‘so extended’ → “so wide distended”: “wide” is supplied to specify the dimension of the extension.

> 82 La novità del suono e il grande lume  
> 83 di lor cagion m' accesero un disio  
> 84 mai non sentito di cotanto acume.  

The newness of the sound and the great light kindled in me a longing for their cause, ne’er felt before so keenly.

- *di lor cagion m' accesero un disio* ‘for their cause kindled in me a desire’ → “kindled in me a longing for their cause”: the pronominal complement follows the verb, ‘desire’ becomes “longing”, and its complement moves from the front of the clause to follow the noun.
- *mai non sentito di cotanto acume* ‘never felt of such great sharpness’ → “ne’er felt before so keenly”: “ne’er” alone carries the negative, “before” is supplied, and the phrase with the noun ‘sharpness’ becomes an adverbial modifying “felt”.

> 85 Ond' ella, che vedea me, sì com' io,  
> 86 a quietarmi l' animo commosso,  
> 87 pria ch' io a domandar, la bocca aprio;  

Whence she who saw me even as I saw myself, to still my agitated mind, opened her lips, e’er I mine to ask;

- *sì com' io* ‘just as I’ → “even as I saw myself”: the omitted predicate is supplied, with an explicit reflexive object.
- *a quietarmi l' animo commosso* ‘to quiet for me the mind stirred’ → “to still my agitated mind”: the personal pronoun attached to the infinitive becomes a possessive modifying “mind”; the descriptive participle moves before the noun.
- *pria ch' io a domandar, la bocca aprio* ‘before I to ask, the mouth she opened’ → “opened her lips, e’er I mine to ask”: the main predicate moves before the time clause, with its object after the verb; singular ‘mouth’ becomes plural “lips” and the article becomes a possessive. In the elliptical time clause “mine” supplies the corresponding object, while the verb of opening remains understood.

## Lines 88–120 — The Order That Moves All Things

> 88 e cominciò: “Tu stesso ti fai grosso  
> 89 col falso imaginar, sì che non vedi  
> 90 ciò che vedresti, se l'avessi scosso.  

and she began: “Thou thyself makest thyself dense with false imagining, and so thou seest not what thou wouldst see, if thou hadst cast it off.

- *ti fai grosso* ‘thyself thou makest thick’ → “makest thyself dense”: the reflexive follows the verb, and “dense” renders *grosso* in its intellectual sense.
- *col falso imaginar* ‘with the false imagining’ → “with false imagining”: the article before the substantivized infinitive is dropped.
- *sì che non vedi* ‘so that thou seest not’ → “and so thou seest not”: a subordinate clause of result becomes a coordinate clause.
- *ciò che* ‘that which’ → “what”: one relative word does the work of antecedent and relative together.
- *se l'avessi scosso* ‘if it thou hadst shaken off’ → “if thou hadst cast it off”: “cast” replaces ‘shaken’, and the object moves from before the auxiliary to between the verb and its particle.

> 91 Tu non se' in terra, sì come tu credi;  

Thou art not upon earth, as thou believest;

- *non se' in terra* ‘not art on earth’ → “art not upon earth”: the negative follows the verb.
- *sì come tu credi* ‘just as thou believest’ → “as thou believest”: the reinforcing *sì* has no separate equivalent.

> 92 ma folgore, fuggendo il proprio sito,  
> 93 non corse, come tu ch' ad esso riedi.”  

but lightning, fleeing its proper site, ne’er darted as dost thou who art returning thither.”

- *folgore … non corse* ‘lightning … did not run’ → “lightning” … “ne’er darted”: the simple denial becomes a denial at any time, and “darted” gives a more specific kind of motion.
- *come tu* ‘as thou’ → “as dost thou”: an auxiliary stands for the verb understood in the comparison.
- *ch' ad esso riedi* ‘who to it returnest’ → “who art returning thither”: the simple present becomes progressive, and the prepositional phrase becomes an adverb placed after the verb.

> 94 S' io fui del primo dubbio disvestito  
> 95 per le sorrise parolette brevi,  
> 96 dentro ad un novo più fui irretito;  

If I was stripped of my first perplexity by the brief smile-enwrapped discourse, I was the more enmeshed within another;

- *del primo dubbio disvestito* ‘of the first doubt stripped’ → “stripped of my first perplexity”: the participle precedes its complement, the article becomes a possessive, and “perplexity” denotes the state of doubt.
- *per le sorrise parolette brevi* ‘by the smiled little words brief’ → “by the brief smile-enwrapped discourse”: the plural diminutive becomes a singular collective without an explicit diminutive; “smile-enwrapped” expands the unusual participle, and “brief” moves before the noun.
- *dentro ad un novo* ‘within a new one’ → “within another”: the substantivized adjective becomes a pronoun, without explicit mention of newness, and the phrase moves to the end of the clause.
- *più fui irretito* ‘more was I enmeshed’ → “I was the more enmeshed”: the comparative follows the auxiliary and gains “the”.

> 97 e dissi: “Già contento requievi  
> 98 di grande ammirazion; ma ora ammiro  
> 99 com' io trascenda questi corpi lievi.”  

and I said: “Content already and at rest from a great marvelling, now am I in amaze how I transcend these lightsome bodies.”

- *Già contento requievi* ‘already content I came to rest’ → “Content already and at rest”: “already” follows “Content”; the finite past verb becomes a phrase of state, joined to “Content” by a supplied “and”.
- *di grande ammirazion* ‘from great wonder’ → “from a great marvelling”: an article is supplied, and the noun of feeling becomes a verbal noun.
- *ma ora ammiro* ‘but now I marvel’ → “now am I in amaze”: the adversative is dropped, and a verb of wondering becomes an inverted copular construction with a noun.
- *questi corpi lievi* ‘these bodies light in weight’ → “these lightsome bodies”: “lightsome” renders *lievi*, and the adjective precedes the noun.

> 100 Ond' ella, appresso d'un pio sospiro,  
> 101 gli occhi drizzò ver me con quel sembiante  
> 102 che madre fa sopra figliuol deliro;  

Whereon she, after a sigh of pity, turned her eyes toward me with that look a mother casts on her delirious child;

- *d'un pio sospiro* ‘of a pitying sigh’ → “a sigh of pity”: English “after” takes its complement directly, and the adjective becomes a phrase following the noun.
- *gli occhi drizzò* ‘the eyes she directed’ → “turned her eyes”: the verb precedes its object, and the article becomes a possessive.
- *con quel sembiante che madre fa sopra figliuol deliro* ‘with that look which a mother makes over a delirious child’ → “with that look a mother casts on her delirious child”: the relative is unexpressed, “casts on” replaces ‘makes over’, the bare nouns acquire “a” and “her”, and the adjective precedes “child”.

> 103 e cominciò: “Le cose tutte e quante  
> 104 hann' ordine tra loro; e questo è forma  
> 105 che l'universo a Dio fa simigliante.  

and began: “All things whatsoever observe a mutual order; and this the form that maketh the universe like unto God.

- *Le cose tutte e quante* ‘the things all and every one’ → “All things whatsoever”: the article drops out, “All” precedes the noun, and “whatsoever” conveys the emphatic second expression of totality.
- *hann' ordine tra loro* ‘have order among themselves’ → “observe a mutual order”: possession becomes observance, an article is supplied, and the reciprocal phrase becomes an adjective.
- *questo è forma* ‘this is form’ → “this the form”: the copula is omitted and a definite article supplied.
- *che l'universo a Dio fa simigliante* ‘that the universe to God makes like’ → “that maketh the universe like unto God”: the verb moves before its object and the complement of ‘like’ follows the adjective.

> 106 Qui veggion l' alte creature l' orma  
> 107 dell' eterno valore, il quale è fine,  
> 108 al quale è fatta la toccata norma.  

Herein the exalted creatures trace the impress of the Eternal Worth, which is the goal whereto was made the norm now spoken of.

- *Qui* ‘here’ → “Herein”: the adverb is made explicitly one of inclusion.
- *veggion l' alte creature* ‘see the high creatures’ → “the exalted creatures trace”: the subject moves before the verb, “exalted” renders ‘high’, and seeing becomes tracing.
- *l' orma* ‘the footprint’ → “the impress”: a more general word for a mark replaces the specific image.
- *il quale è fine* ‘which is end’ → “which is the goal”: “goal” selects the sense of an end aimed at, and the article is supplied.
- *al quale* ‘to which’ → “whereto”: the preposition and relative pronoun become a single relative adverb.
- *è fatta* ‘is made’ → “was made”: the passive changes from present to past.
- *la toccata norma* ‘the touched-on norm’ → “the norm now spoken of”: the participle is rendered by a phrase after the noun, with “now” supplied.

> 109 Nell' ordine ch' io dico sono accline  
> 110 tutte nature, per diverse sorti,  
> 111 più al principio loro e men vicine;  

In the order of which I speak all things incline, by diverse lots, more near and less unto their principle;

- *ch' io dico* ‘which I tell of’ → “of which I speak”: the verb takes a prepositional complement in English, with the preposition before the relative.
- *sono accline tutte nature* ‘are inclined all natures’ → “all things incline”: the subject moves before the predicate, “things” replaces ‘natures’, and copula and adjective become an active intransitive verb.
- *più al principio loro e men vicine* ‘more to their principle and less near’ → “more near and less unto their principle”: “near” is placed with the first comparative rather than after the second; its complement follows both, and the possessive precedes its noun.

> 112 onde si movono a diversi porti  
> 113 per lo gran mar dell'essere, e ciascuna  
> 114 con istinto a lei dato che la porti.  

wherefore they move to diverse ports o’er the great sea of being, and each one with instinct given it to bear it on.

- *si movono* ‘move themselves’ → “they move”: the Italian pronominal verb is rendered intransitively without a reflexive.
- *per lo gran mar* ‘through the great sea’ → “o’er the great sea”: motion through becomes motion over.
- *a lei dato* ‘to it given’ → “given it”: the recipient follows the participle without a preposition.
- *che la porti* ‘that may bear it’ → “to bear it on”: the relative clause with a subjunctive becomes an infinitive of purpose; the object follows the verb, and “on” is supplied.

> 115 Questi ne porta il foco in ver la luna,  
> 116 questi nei cor mortali è permotore,  
> 117 questi la terra in sè stringe ed aduna.  

This beareth the fire toward the moon; this is the mover in the hearts of things that die; this doth draw the earth together and unite it.

- *Questi ne porta il foco* ‘this bears the fire along’ → “This beareth the fire”: the adverbial particle *ne* has no separate equivalent.
- *nei cor mortali* ‘in mortal hearts’ → “in the hearts of things that die”: the phrase moves after the predicate, and the adjective is expanded into a description of the possessors of the hearts.
- *è permotore* ‘is impeller’ → “is the mover”: “mover” renders the agent noun, with a definite article supplied.
- *la terra in sè stringe ed aduna* ‘the earth into itself draws tight and gathers’ → “doth draw the earth together and unite it”: the first verb, with auxiliary “doth”, precedes its object; “together” conveys the drawing inward without the explicit reflexive, and “unite” takes a repeated object, “it”.

> 118 Nè pur le creature, che son fuore  
> 119 d'intelligenza, quest' arco saetta,  
> 120 ma quelle ch' hanno intelletto ed amore.  

Nor only the creatures that lack intelligence doth this bow shoot, but those that have both intellect and love.

- *son fuore d'intelligenza* ‘are outside of intelligence’ → “lack intelligence”: a copula with a prepositional phrase becomes a transitive verb with a direct object.
- *quest' arco saetta* ‘this bow shoots’ → “doth this bow shoot”: after the fronted negative phrase, English introduces an auxiliary and inverts it with the subject.
- *hanno intelletto ed amore* ‘have intellect and love’ → “have both intellect and love”: “both” is supplied.

## Lines 121–142 — Providence and the Naturalness of Ascent

> 121 La provvidenza, che cotanto assetta,  
> 122 del suo lume fa il ciel sempre quieto,  
> 123 nel qual si volge quel ch' ha maggior fretta;  

The Providence that doth assort all this, doth with its light make ever still the heaven wherein whirleth that one that hath the greatest speed;

- *cotanto assetta* ‘so much sets in order’ → “doth assort all this”: the verb comes before its object, with auxiliary “doth”; the expression of quantity becomes a demonstrative phrase.
- *del suo lume fa il ciel sempre quieto* ‘with its light makes the heaven ever still’ → “doth with its light make ever still the heaven”: the instrumental phrase comes between the supplied auxiliary and the main verb, and the description of the resulting state comes before the object.
- *nel qual si volge* ‘in which turns itself’ → “wherein whirleth”: the preposition and relative pronoun become a relative adverb; the pronominal verb becomes a more vivid intransitive verb.
- *quel ch' ha maggior fretta* ‘that which has greater haste’ → “that one that hath the greatest speed”: “one” makes the antecedent explicit, the comparative becomes a definite superlative, and haste is expressed as speed.

> 124 ed ora lì, com' a sito decreto,  
> 125 cen porta la virtù di quella corda,  
> 126 che ciò che scocca drizza in segno lieto.  

and thither now, as to the appointed site, the power of that bowstring beareth us which directeth to a joyful mark whatso it doth discharge.

- *ora lì* ‘now there’ → “thither now”: the adverbs change places, and “thither” expresses direction rather than position.
- *a sito decreto* ‘to site appointed’ → “to the appointed site”: the definite article is supplied and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *cen porta la virtù di quella corda* ‘us thither bears the power of that string’ → “the power of that bowstring beareth us”: the subject moves before the verb and the object after it; the particle of destination in *cen* is not rendered a second time, and “bowstring” specifies the kind of string.
- *che ciò che scocca drizza in segno lieto* ‘which what it shoots directs toward a mark joyful’ → “which directeth to a joyful mark whatso it doth discharge”: the relative is now separated from its antecedent by “beareth us”; the verb of direction and the phrase of destination precede the object clause. The adjective precedes its noun, and “whatso” gives the object an explicitly unrestricted sense.

> 127 Ver' è che come forma non s' accorda  
> 128 molte fiate alla intenzion dell' arte,  
> 129 perch' a risponder la materia è sorda;  

True is it, that as the form often accordeth not with the intention of the art, because that the material is dull to answer;

- *Ver' è che* ‘true is that’ → “True is it, that”: anticipatory “it” is inserted before the subject clause.
- *come forma non s' accorda molte fiate alla intenzion dell' arte* ‘as form does not accord many times with the intention of art’ → “as the form often accordeth not with the intention of the art”: articles are supplied before “form” and “art”; the pronominal verb becomes intransitive, with the negative after it, and the following phrase of frequency becomes an adverb before the verb.
- *perch' a risponder la materia è sorda* ‘because to answer the matter is deaf’ → “because that the material is dull to answer”: the conjunction is expanded; the infinitive moves to the end, “material” replaces ‘matter’, and the metaphor of deafness becomes one of dullness.

> 130 così da questo corso si diparte  
> 131 talor la creatura, ch' ha potere  
> 132 di piegar, così pinta, in altra parte  

so from this course sometimes departeth the creature that hath power, thus thrust, to swerve to-ward some other part,

- *si diparte talor* ‘departs sometimes’ → “sometimes departeth”: the adverb precedes the verb, and the pronominal form has no separate English particle.
- *di piegar, così pinta, in altra parte* ‘to bend, thus impelled, in another direction’ → “thus thrust, to swerve to-ward some other part”: the participial phrase moves before the infinitive; “swerve” gives the bending a specifically directional sense, and “some” makes the destination expressly indefinite.

> 133 (e sì come veder si può cadere  
> 134 foco di nube), se l' impeto primo  
> 135 a terra è torto da falso piacere.  

(even as fire may be seen to dart down from the cloud) if its first rush be wrenched aside to earth by false seeming pleasure.

- *e sì come* ‘and just as’ → “even as”: the conjunction drops out and “even” conveys the emphasis of the comparison.
- *veder si può cadere foco di nube* ‘one can see fire fall from a cloud’ → “fire may be seen to dart down from the cloud”: the impersonal construction becomes passive, with “fire” as subject; ‘fall’ becomes the more forceful “dart down”, and the cloud receives a definite article.
- *l' impeto primo* ‘the impulse first’ → “its first rush”: the article becomes a possessive, the adjective precedes the noun, and “rush” expresses the impulse as motion.
- *a terra è torto* ‘toward earth is turned awry’ → “be wrenched aside to earth”: the phrase of direction moves after the verb; the indicative becomes an English conditional subjunctive, and the participle is expanded into a more forcible verb with an adverb of deflection.
- *falso piacere* ‘false pleasure’ → “false seeming pleasure”: “seeming” is supplied to express falsity as a matter of appearance.

> 136 Non dei più ammirar, se bene estimo,  
> 137 lo tuo salir, se non come d' un rivo  
> 138 se d' alto monte scende giuso ad imo.  

Thou shouldst no more wonder, if I deem aright, at thine uprising, than at a river dropping down from a lofty mountain to the base.

- *Non dei più ammirar … se non come* ‘you ought not to marvel more … except as’ → “Thou shouldst no more wonder” … “than”: the negative and comparative are drawn together as “no more”, and the exceptive comparison becomes its English correlative “than”.
- *se bene estimo* ‘if rightly I judge’ → “if I deem aright”: the adverb moves after the verb.
- *lo tuo salir* ‘thy rising’ → “at thine uprising”: the infinitive used as a noun becomes a verbal noun governed by the preposition required by “wonder”.
- *d' un rivo se d' alto monte scende giuso ad imo* ‘at a stream if from a high mountain it descends down to the bottom’ → “at a river dropping down from a lofty mountain to the base”: “river” broadens ‘stream’; the conditional clause becomes a participial phrase, with the motion before its point of departure. An article is supplied before “lofty mountain”, and “base” specifies the bottom in relation to the mountain.

> 139 Maraviglia sarebbe in te, se privo  
> 140 d'impedimento giù ti fossi assiso,  
> 141 come a terra quiete in foco vivo.”  

Marvel were it in thee if, bereft of all impediment, thou hadst settled down below; even as were stillness on the earth in a living flame.”

- *Maraviglia sarebbe in te* ‘a marvel would be in thee’ → “Marvel were it in thee”: the conditional becomes subjunctive “were”, with anticipatory “it” for the following clause.
- *privo d'impedimento* ‘devoid of impediment’ → “bereft of all impediment”: “all” makes the absence explicitly total.
- *giù ti fossi assiso* ‘down below thou hadst seated thyself’ → “thou hadst settled down below”: the reflexive expression of sitting becomes intransitive “settled”, and the expression of place follows the verb.
- *come a terra quiete in foco vivo* ‘as on earth rest in fire living’ → “even as were stillness on the earth in a living flame”: “even” reinforces the comparison and “were” supplies its understood verb; “stillness” precedes the phrase of place. English supplies articles before “earth” and “living flame”, with ‘fire’ particularized as “flame” and the adjective placed first.

> 142 Quinci rivolse in ver lo cielo il viso.  

Thereon toward Heaven she turned back her gaze.

- *Quinci* ‘from this’ → “Thereon”: the connective is rendered as temporal succession.
- *rivolse in ver lo cielo il viso* ‘turned back toward the sky the face’ → “toward Heaven she turned back her gaze”: the directional phrase moves before the verb and loses its article; the definite article before the object becomes a possessive, and “gaze” takes *viso* in the sense of the look rather than the face.

## Summary — Reordering the Phrase, Retaining the Image

Wicksteed often changes the order of a phrase without changing its emphasis. In *Marsia traesti*, the object follows the verb in English, “thou drewest Marsyas”; in *la fronda Peneia*, the adjective moves before the noun, “the Peneian frond”, and the whole subject moves from after the verb to before it. Yet the prose does not consistently prefer ordinary English order: “Into my bosom enter thou” gives the phrase of direction a prominence it does not have in *Entra nel petto mio*.

A change of construction can either compress or expand the Italian. *Colui che tutto move*, ‘him who moves all’, becomes the compound “All-mover’s”, while *Trasumanar* opens out into “To pass beyond humanity”. In *nè sa nè può*, two verbs become nouns under a single verb, “hath nor knowledge nor power”; elsewhere a noun becomes a verb, as the ‘sheath’ in *della vagina delle membra sue* becomes “what sheathed his limbs”. Word choice can also alter the precision of an image: “the impress” is less specific than *l’orma*, ‘the footprint’, whereas “the furnace” gives *il foco*, ‘the fire’, a more definite setting.

Some additions bring out what the Italian leaves implicit: *retro* gives “back”, but Wicksteed adds “upon the track”; *sì com’ io*, ‘just as I’, becomes “even as I saw myself”. Conversely, the object *il* in *tu il sai* has no separate equivalent in “thou knowest”. Two routine differences run throughout: English supplies subject pronouns where an Italian verb ending suffices, and omits the article that Italian uses before a possessive, as in *del mio canto*, “of my song”.

Wicksteed stays particularly close where repetition or a comparison gives the phrasing its shape. The repeated *questi* is answered three times by “This” or “this”; *con ambo e due* keeps its emphatic doubling in “with both the two”. Even where the words change places, as in *Poca favilla gran fiamma seconda*, the spark, the flame, and the verb of following all survive.
