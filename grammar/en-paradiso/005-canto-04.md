# Paradiso 4

## Lines 1–15 — Two Doubts and an Unspoken Question

> 1 Intra due cibi, distanti e moventi  
> 2 d' un modo, prima si morria di fame,  
> 3 che liber uomo l' un recasse ai denti.  

Between two foods, distant and appetising in like measure, death by starvation would ensue ere a free man put either to his teeth.

- *moventi* ‘moving’ → “appetising”: the participle becomes an adjective specifying how the foods move a man to choose them.
- *d' un modo* ‘in one manner’ → “in like measure”: sameness of manner is expressed as equality of degree.
- *prima si morria di fame, che* ‘one would die of hunger before … than’ → “death by starvation would ensue ere”: the impersonal verb becomes a noun subject with a new verb, “would ensue”; hunger becomes the cause expressed by “by starvation”. The comparative construction split between *prima* and *che* gives way to a single temporal conjunction, “ere”.
- *liber uomo* ‘free man’ → “a free man”: English supplies the indefinite article.
- *l' un recasse ai denti* ‘the one he should bring to the teeth’ → “put either to his teeth”: the object follows the verb and becomes “either”; “put” replaces ‘bring’, with no distinct subjunctive form, and the definite article becomes a possessive.

> 4 Sì si starebbe un agno intra due brame  
> 5 di fieri lupi, egualmente temendo;  

So would a lamb stand still between two cravings of fierce wolves, in equipoise of dread;

- *si starebbe un agno* ‘would stay a lamb’ → “would a lamb stand still”: the pronominal verb becomes “stand still”, and the subject comes between the auxiliary and the main verb rather than after the whole verb.
- *egualmente temendo* ‘equally fearing’ → “in equipoise of dread”: the participial construction becomes a prepositional phrase; the adverb is expressed by the noun “equipoise”, and the act of fearing by the noun “dread”.

> 6 sì si starebbe un cane intra due dame.  

so would a dog stand still between two hinds.

- *si starebbe un cane* ‘would stay a dog’ → “would a dog stand still”: again the pronominal verb becomes “stand still”, with the subject between auxiliary and main verb.

> 7 Per che, s' io mi tacea, me non riprendo,  
> 8 dalli miei dubbi d' un modo sospinto,  
> 9 poich' era necessario, nè commendo.  

Wherefore, if I held my peace I blame me not, (thrust in like measure either way by my perplexities) since ’twas necessity, nor yet commend me.

- *mi tacea* ‘was silent’ → “held my peace”: a pronominal imperfect becomes an English idiom with a possessive object and a simple past verb.
- *me non riprendo* ‘myself I do not rebuke’ → “I blame me not”: “blame” replaces ‘rebuke’, the emphatically placed object follows the verb, and the negative follows the object.
- *dalli miei dubbi d' un modo sospinto* ‘by my doubts in one manner impelled’ → “thrust in like measure either way by my perplexities”: the participle comes first and its agent last; ‘doubts’ becomes “perplexities”, sameness of manner becomes equality of degree, and “either way” makes the two directions explicit.
- *poich' era necessario* ‘since it was necessary’ → “since ’twas necessity”: the predicate adjective becomes a noun.
- *nè commendo* ‘nor do I commend’ → “nor yet commend me”: “yet” is supplied, and the object understood from the earlier clause is repeated.

> 10 Io mi tacea; ma il mio disir dipinto  
> 11 m' era nel viso, e il domandar con ello  
> 12 più caldo assai, che per parlar distinto.  

I held my peace, but my desire was painted on my face, and my questioning with it, in warmer colours far than if set out by speech.

- *Io mi tacea* ‘I was silent’ → “I held my peace”: the pronominal imperfect again becomes an idiom in the simple past.
- *dipinto m' era nel viso* ‘painted was to me in the face’ → “was painted on my face”: the auxiliary precedes the participle; the personal dative becomes a possessive modifying “face”, and ‘in’ becomes “on”.
- *il domandar* ‘the asking’ → “my questioning”: the substantivized infinitive becomes an *-ing* noun, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *più caldo assai* ‘much warmer’ → “in warmer colours far”: the comparative adjective describing the questioning becomes a prepositional phrase describing its depiction; “colours” is supplied from the idea of painting, and the intensifier “far” follows the noun.
- *che per parlar distinto* ‘than by distinct speech’ → “than if set out by speech”: a comparison using a phrase of means becomes a conditional comparison; the notion of distinct articulation is transferred from the modifier of speech to the passive “set out”.

> 13 Fe' sì Beatrice, qual fe' Daniello,  
> 14 Nabuccodonosor levando d' ira,  
> 15 che l' avea fatto ingiustamente fello;  

And Beatrice took the part that Daniel took when he lifted Nebuchadnezzar out of the wrath that had made him unjustly cruel,

- *Fe' sì Beatrice, qual fe' Daniello* ‘did so Beatrice, as did Daniel’ → “And Beatrice took the part that Daniel took”: “And” is supplied, both subjects precede their verbs, and the comparison of ways of acting becomes a shared “part”, introduced as the object of the first verb and taken up by a relative clause.
- *Nabuccodonosor levando d' ira* ‘Nebuchadnezzar lifting out of wrath’ → “when he lifted Nebuchadnezzar out of the wrath”: the gerund becomes a finite temporal clause, the object follows the verb, and “wrath” gains a definite article.
- *l' avea fatto* ‘him had made’ → “had made him”: the object pronoun follows the verb instead of preceding the auxiliary.
- *fello* ‘fell’ → “cruel”: the adjective is rendered specifically in its sense of cruelty.

## Lines 16–48 — Two Doubts and the Appearance of Souls

> 16 e disse: “Io veggio ben come ti tira  
> 17 uno ed altro disio, sì che tua cura  
> 18 sè stessa lega sì che fuor non spira.  

and she said: “Yea, but I see how this desire and that so draweth thee, that thy eagerness entangleth its own self, and therefore breathes not forth.

- *Io veggio ben* ‘I see well’ → “Yea, but I see”: the assurance conveyed by the adverb becomes an opening affirmation, and a contrast is supplied.
- *ti tira uno ed altro disio* ‘thee draws one and another desire’ → “this desire and that so draweth thee”: the paired subjects are put before the verb and the object after it; demonstratives replace the indefinites, the noun goes with the first rather than the second, and “so” anticipates the result clause.
- *sì che tua cura* ‘so that thy concern’ → “that thy eagerness”: the result clause begins with “that” alone after the preceding “so”; the noun is rendered as eagerness rather than concern.
- *sè stessa lega* ‘itself binds’ → “entangleth its own self”: the reflexive follows the verb and is expanded with “own”; binding becomes entangling.
- *sì che fuor non spira* ‘so that forth it breathes not’ → “and therefore breathes not forth”: coordination with a consequential adverb replaces subordination, and the directional adverb moves to the end.

> 19 Tu argomenti: ‘Se il buon voler dura,  
> 20 la violenza altrui per qual ragione  
> 21 di meritar mi scema la misura?’  

Thou arguest: *If the right will endureth, by what justice can another’s violence sheer me the measure of desert?*

- *il buon voler* ‘the good willing’ → “the right will”: the substantivized infinitive is rendered by a noun, and goodness by rightness.
- *la violenza altrui* ‘the violence of another’ → “another’s violence”: the possessor comes first, and the article drops out.
- *per qual ragione* ‘for what reason’ → “by what justice”: the question is put in terms of justice rather than reason, and the phrase is brought before the subject.
- *di meritar mi scema la misura* ‘of meriting diminishes for me the measure’ → “can another’s violence sheer me the measure of desert”: the complement of ‘measure’ follows its noun, and the infinitive becomes the noun “desert”; the simple present becomes a question with “can”, and diminution is expressed as shearing.

> 22 Ancor di dubitar ti dà cagione,  
> 23 parer tornarsi l' anime alle stelle,  
> 24 secondo la sentenza di Platone.  

And further matter of perplexity is given thee by the semblance of the souls returning to the stars, as Plato’s doctrine hath it.

- *Ancor di dubitar ti dà cagione* ‘further, of doubting, to thee gives cause’ → “And further matter of perplexity is given thee”: a conjunction is supplied; the object becomes the subject of a passive, with the recipient after the verb. Cause for doubting becomes matter of perplexity, a noun replacing the infinitive.
- *parer tornarsi l' anime alle stelle* ‘the souls’ seeming to return to the stars’ → “by the semblance of the souls returning to the stars”: the infinitival subject becomes a noun introduced by “by” in the passive construction; the souls precede “returning” rather than following the infinitive, whose pronominal form has no separate equivalent.
- *secondo la sentenza di Platone* ‘according to the opinion of Plato’ → “as Plato’s doctrine hath it”: a prepositional phrase becomes a clause with a supplied verb and object; opinion becomes doctrine, and the possessor precedes the noun.

> 25 Queste son le question che nel tuo velle  
> 26 pontano egualemente; e però pria  
> 27 tratterò quella che più ha di felle.  

These are the questions which weigh equally upon thy will; and therefore I will first treat that which hath the most of gall.

- *nel tuo velle pontano egualemente* ‘in thy willing press equally’ → “weigh equally upon thy will”: pressing in becomes weighing upon; the prepositional phrase moves after the verb and adverb, and the substantivized infinitive becomes “will”.
- *pria tratterò* ‘first I shall treat’ → “I will first treat”: the adverb stands between the future auxiliary and the verb.
- *più ha di felle* ‘more has of gall’ → “hath the most of gall”: the comparative is rendered by a superlative, placed after the verb.

> 28 Dei serafin colui che più s' india,  
> 29 Moisè, Samuel, e quel Giovanni,  
> 30 qual prender vuoli, io dico, non Maria,  

He of the Seraphim who most doth sink himself in God, Moses, Samuel, and that John whichso thou choose to take, not Mary’s self,

- *Dei serafin colui* ‘of the Seraphim he’ → “He of the Seraphim”: the pronoun precedes its partitive complement.
- *s' india* ‘in-Gods himself’ → “doth sink himself in God”: the single reflexive verb formed on the name of God is unpacked into a verb of immersion and a prepositional phrase.
- *qual prender vuoli* ‘whichever to take thou wishest’ → “whichso thou choose to take”: choosing replaces wishing, and the infinitive follows the verb governing it.
- *io dico* ‘I say’ → no English equivalent: the parenthetical qualification is omitted.
- *non Maria* ‘not Mary’ → “not Mary’s self”: “self” supplies emphasis.

> 31 non hanno in altro cielo i loro scanni,  
> 32 che quegli spirti che mo t' appariro,  
> 33 nè hanno all' esser lor più o meno anni.  

in any other heaven hold their seats than these spirits who but now appeared to thee, nor have they to their being more nor fewer years.

- *non hanno in altro cielo i loro scanni* ‘do not have in another heaven their seats’ → “in any other heaven hold their seats”: the verb does not repeat the preceding negative; “any” marks its scope. The phrase of place precedes the verb, and having becomes holding.
- *quegli spirti* ‘those spirits’ → “these spirits”: the demonstrative changes from distant to near.
- *mo t' appariro* ‘just now to thee appeared’ → “but now appeared to thee”: the recipient follows the verb, and “but now” renders the time adverb.
- *più o meno anni* ‘more or fewer years’ → “more nor fewer years”: the alternative is joined by “nor” under the preceding negation.

> 34 Ma tutti fanno bello il primo giro,  
> 35 e differentemente han dolce vita,  
> 36 per sentir più e men l' eterno spiro.  

But all make beauteous the first circle, and share sweet life, with difference, by feeling more and less the eternal breath.

- *differentemente han dolce vita* ‘differently have sweet life’ → “share sweet life, with difference”: having becomes sharing, and the initial adverb becomes a prepositional phrase following the object.
- *per sentir* ‘through feeling’ → “by feeling”: the preposition and infinitive are rendered by “by” and a gerund.

> 37 Qui si mostraron, non perchè sortita  
> 38 sia questa spera lor; ma per far segno  
> 39 della celestial ch' ha men salita.  

They have here revealed themselves, not that this sphere is given them, but to make sign of the celestial one that hath the least ascent.

- *Qui si mostraron* ‘here they showed themselves’ → “They have here revealed themselves”: the simple past becomes a present perfect, showing becomes revealing, and the adverb moves inside the verb phrase.
- *non perchè sortita sia questa spera lor* ‘not because allotted be this sphere to them’ → “not that this sphere is given them”: “that” introduces the rejected explanation; the subject precedes the passive, the subjunctive becomes indicative, and allotment is rendered as giving.
- *della celestial* ‘of the celestial’ → “of the celestial one”: “one” supplies a nominal head for the adjective.
- *men salita* ‘less ascent’ → “the least ascent”: the comparative becomes a superlative with an article.

> 40 Così parlar conviensi al vostro ingegno,  
> 41 però che solo da sensato apprende  
> 42 ciò che fa poscia d' intelletto degno.  

Needs must such speech address your faculty, which only from the sense-reported thing doth apprehend what it then proceedeth to make fit matter for the intellect.

- *Così parlar conviensi al vostro ingegno* ‘thus to speak is fitting to your understanding’ → “Needs must such speech address your faculty”: fitness becomes necessity; the adverb and infinitive become the subject “such speech”, and the understanding to which the speaking is suited becomes the faculty that the speech must “address”.
- *però che* ‘because’ → “which”: a causal clause becomes a relative clause modifying “faculty”.
- *da sensato apprende* ‘from what is sensed apprehends’ → “from the sense-reported thing doth apprehend”: the substantivized participle is expanded into a qualified noun, and the simple verb is rendered with an auxiliary.
- *ciò che fa poscia d' intelletto degno* ‘that which it makes afterwards of intellect worthy’ → “what it then proceedeth to make fit matter for the intellect”: “what” combines antecedent and relative; the adverb moves before the verb, and proceeding is supplied. Being made worthy of intellect becomes being made fit matter for it, with the complement moved to the end.

> 43 Per questo la Scrittura condiscende  
> 44 a vostra facultate, e piedi e mano  
> 45 attribuisce a Dio, ed altro intende;  

And therefore doth the Scripture condescend to your capacity, assigning foot and hand to God, with other meaning;

- *Per questo* ‘for this’ → “And therefore”: an adverb replaces the prepositional phrase, and a conjunction is supplied.
- *la Scrittura condiscende* ‘the Scripture condescends’ → “doth the Scripture condescend”: an auxiliary is supplied and placed before the subject.
- *a vostra facultate* ‘to your faculty’ → “to your capacity”: capacity replaces faculty.
- *e piedi e mano attribuisce a Dio* ‘and feet and hand it attributes to God’ → “assigning foot and hand to God”: the coordinated finite verb becomes a participle preceding its objects, attribution becomes assignment, and plural feet become singular “foot”.
- *ed altro intende* ‘and it means something else’ → “with other meaning”: a prepositional phrase with a noun replaces the coordinated clause.

> 46 e santa Chiesa con aspetto umano  
> 47 Gabriel e Michel vi rappresenta,  
> 48 e l' altro che Tobia rifece sano.  

and Holy Church doth represent to you with human aspect Gabriel and Michael, and him too who made Tobit sound again.

- *con aspetto umano* ‘with aspect human’ → “with human aspect”: the adjective precedes the noun, and the phrase moves after the verb and its recipient.
- *Gabriel e Michel vi rappresenta* ‘Gabriel and Michael to you represents’ → “doth represent to you with human aspect Gabriel and Michael”: the verb takes an auxiliary and precedes both the recipient and the named objects.
- *l' altro* ‘the other’ → “him too”: a pronoun and an additive adverb replace the expression distinguishing the other figure.
- *Tobia rifece sano* ‘Tobit he made again sound’ → “made Tobit sound again”: the object follows the verb, and the sense of the verbal prefix is expressed by a separate adverb after “sound”.

## Lines 49–81 — The Stars and the Unbroken Will

> 49 Quel che Timeo dell' anime argomenta  
> 50 non è simile a ciò che qui si vede,  
> 51 però che, come dice, par che senta.  

That which Timæus argueth of the souls is not the like of what may here be seen, for seemingly he thinketh as he saith.

- *dell' anime argomenta* ‘of the souls argueth’ → “argueth of the souls”: the verb comes before its prepositional complement.
- *simile a* ‘similar to’ → “the like of”: an adjective becomes a noun phrase, with a different preposition.
- *ciò che qui si vede* ‘that which one sees here’ → “what may here be seen”: a single relative does duty for demonstrative and relative; the impersonal construction becomes passive, with a modal verb added.
- *però che* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *come dice, par che senta* ‘as he says, it seems that he thinks’ → “seemingly he thinketh as he saith”: the verb of seeming becomes an adverb, its subjunctive complement becomes the main assertion, and the comparison moves to the end.

> 52 Dice che l' alma alla sua stella riede,  
> 53 credendo quella quindi esser decisa,  
> 54 quando natura per forma la diede.  

He saith the soul returneth to its star, believing it cleft thence when nature gave it as a form.

- *che l' alma alla sua stella riede* ‘that the soul to its star returns’ → “the soul returneth to its star”: the conjunction is dropped, and the verb precedes the phrase of destination.
- *quella quindi esser decisa* ‘that one thence to be severed’ → “it cleft thence”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative; the passive infinitive loses its auxiliary, and the adverb follows the participle.
- *per forma la diede* ‘for form it gave’ → “gave it as a form”: the object follows the verb; the phrase expressing capacity moves to the end, with an indefinite article.

> 55 E forse sua sentenza è d' altra guisa  
> 56 che la voce non suona; ed esser puote  
> 57 con intenzion da non esser derisa.  

Although perchance his meaning is of other guise than the word soundeth, and may have a not-to-be-derided purport.

- *E* ‘and’ → “Although”: coordination gives way to a concessive opening.
- *sua sentenza* ‘his opinion’ → “his meaning”: the noun is taken in the sense of what he means.
- *che la voce non suona* ‘than the voice sounds’ → “than the word soundeth”: the spoken expression is rendered as “the word”; the expletive negative in the comparison has no English counterpart.
- *esser puote con intenzion da non esser derisa* ‘be it may with an intention not to be derided’ → “may have a not-to-be-derided purport”: being with an intention becomes having a purport; the modal precedes its infinitive, and the passive infinitival modifier becomes a compound adjective before the noun.

> 58 S' egl' intende tornare a queste rote  
> 59 l' onor dell' influenza e il biasmo, forse  
> 60 in alcun vero suo arco percote.  

If he meaneth that the honour and the blame of their influence return unto these wheels, perchance his bow smiteth a certain truth.

- *intende tornare a queste rote l' onor dell' influenza e il biasmo* ‘means to return to these wheels the honour of the influence and the blame’ → “meaneth that the honour and the blame of their influence return unto these wheels”: the infinitive becomes a finite clause with honour and blame as its subjects. The destination follows the verb; the phrase about influence follows both nouns, with a possessive in place of the article.
- *in alcun vero suo arco percote* ‘upon some truth his bow strikes’ → “his bow smiteth a certain truth”: the initial prepositional phrase becomes a direct object after the verb, and the indefinite adjective is expanded to “a certain”.

> 61 Questo principio male inteso torse  
> 62 già tutto il mondo quasi, sì che Giove,  
> 63 Mercurio e Marte a nominar trascorse.  

This principle misunderstood erst wrenched aside the whole world almost, so that it rushed astray to call upon the names of Jove and Mercury and Mars.

- *male inteso* ‘badly understood’ → “misunderstood”: adverb and participle become one word.
- *torse già* ‘twisted formerly’ → “erst wrenched aside”: the time adverb moves before the verb, and the verb of turning is rendered by a verb and a directional adverb.
- *Giove, Mercurio e Marte a nominar trascorse* ‘to name Jove, Mercury and Mars it ran to excess’ → “it rushed astray to call upon the names of Jove and Mercury and Mars”: the finite verb comes first and expresses deviation rather than excess; it is followed by the infinitive. Naming is interpreted as calling upon names, and a conjunction is supplied before the second name.

> 64 L' altra dubitazion che ti commove  
> 65 ha men velen, però che sua malizia  
> 66 non ti poria menar da me altrove.  

The other perplexity which troubleth thee hath less of poison, because its malice could not lead thee away from me elsewhere.

- *dubitazion* ‘doubt’ → “perplexity”: the noun is rendered by a term for the resulting uncertainty.
- *ti commove* ‘thee stirs’ → “troubleth thee”: the verb is given a more specifically troubling sense, and the object follows it.
- *men velen* ‘less poison’ → “less of poison”: the measure is expressed with a preposition.
- *non ti poria menar da me altrove* ‘not thee could lead from me elsewhere’ → “could not lead thee away from me elsewhere”: the negative follows the modal, the object follows the infinitive, and “away” makes the direction explicit.

> 67 Parere ingiusta la nostra giustizia  
> 68 negli occhi dei mortali, è argomento  
> 69 di fede, e non d' eretica nequizia.  

For our justice to appear unjust in mortal eyes is argument of faith, and not of heretic iniquity.

- *Parere ingiusta la nostra giustizia* ‘to appear unjust our justice’ → “For our justice to appear unjust”: the subject of the infinitive comes first and is introduced by “For”.
- *negli occhi dei mortali* ‘in the eyes of mortals’ → “in mortal eyes”: the possessors become an attributive adjective, and the article disappears.
- *eretica nequizia* ‘heretical wickedness’ → “heretic iniquity”: the adjective takes the attributive form “heretic”, and the noun is rendered by “iniquity”.

> 70 Ma, perchè puote vostro accorgimento  
> 71 ben penetrare a questa veritate,  
> 72 come disiri, ti farò contento.  

But since your wit hath power to pierce unto this truth, e’en as thou wishest I will satisfy thee.

- *puote vostro accorgimento ben penetrare a questa veritate* ‘can your discernment well penetrate to this truth’ → “your wit hath power to pierce unto this truth”: the subject precedes the expression of ability; discernment becomes “wit”, and the modal becomes a phrase with a noun. Penetrating becomes piercing; the emphatic adverb has no separate rendering.
- *come disiri* ‘as thou desirest’ → “e’en as thou wishest”: the comparison is strengthened with “e’en”, and wishing renders desiring.
- *ti farò contento* ‘thee I shall make content’ → “I will satisfy thee”: the causative verb and adjective become one transitive verb, followed by its object.

> 73 Se violenza è quando quel che pate,  
> 74 niente conferisce a quel che isforza,  
> 75 non fur quest' alme per essa scusate;  

If *violence* is when he who suffereth doth naught contribute to what forceth him, then these souls had not the excuse of it;

- *quel che pate* ‘that which suffers’ → “he who suffereth”: the relative expression is made explicitly personal.
- *niente conferisce* ‘nothing contributes’ → “doth naught contribute”: English supplies the auxiliary, retaining the negative object before the main verb.
- *a quel che isforza* ‘to that which forces’ → “to what forceth him”: a single relative replaces demonstrative and relative, and an object is supplied.
- *non fur quest' alme per essa scusate* ‘were not these souls by it excused’ → “then these souls had not the excuse of it”: “then” marks the consequence, the subject comes before the verb, and the passive becomes an active construction with a noun; what excuses becomes the excuse one has.

> 76 chè volontà, se non vuol, non s' ammorza,  
> 77 ma fa come natura face in foco,  
> 78 se mille volte violenza il torza.  

for if the will willeth not, it cannot be crushed, but doth as nature doeth in the flame, though violence wrench it aside a thousand times.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the causal connection is made with a coordinating conjunction.
- *volontà, se non vuol* ‘will, if it wills not’ → “if the will willeth not”: the noun moves inside the condition and takes a definite article; its place in the main clause is taken by a pronoun.
- *non s' ammorza* ‘is not quenched’ → “it cannot be crushed”: the verb of extinction becomes one of crushing, and the negative assertion becomes a statement of impossibility.
- *come natura face in foco* ‘as nature does in fire’ → “as nature doeth in the flame”: fire becomes a definite flame.
- *se mille volte violenza il torza* ‘if a thousand times violence twist it’ → “though violence wrench it aside a thousand times”: the condition is read concessively; the object follows the verb, twisting becomes wrenching aside, and the count moves to the end.

> 79 Perchè, s' ella si piega assai o poco,  
> 80 segue la forza; e così queste fero,  
> 81 possendo ritornare al santo loco.  

For should it bend itself, or much or little, it doth abet the force; and so did these, since they had power to return to the sacred place.

- *Perchè* ‘because’ → “For”: the causal conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *s' ella si piega* ‘if it bends itself’ → “should it bend itself”: an inverted conditional with a modal replaces the conjunction and present indicative.
- *assai o poco* ‘much or little’ → “or much or little”: a first “or” is supplied.
- *segue la forza* ‘follows the force’ → “it doth abet the force”: following is interpreted as assisting, and the verb takes an auxiliary.
- *possendo ritornare* ‘being able to return’ → “since they had power to return”: the gerund becomes a finite causal clause, and ability is expressed by a noun.

## Lines 82–114 — Absolute Will and Consent Under Force

> 82 Se fosse stato lor volere intero,  
> 83 come tenne Lorenzo in su la grada  
> 84 e fece Muzio alla sua man severo,  

If their will had remained intact, like that which held Lawrence upon the grid, and made Mucius stern against his own right hand,

- *fosse stato lor volere intero* ‘had been their will whole’ → “their will had remained intact”: the subject comes before the verb, and being whole becomes remaining intact.
- *come tenne* ‘as held’ → “like that which held”: the translation supplies “that which”, making the will the subject of “held” and Lawrence its object.
- *fece Muzio alla sua man severo* ‘made Mucius toward his hand stern’ → “made Mucius stern against his own right hand”: the adjective moves next to “Mucius”; “against” gives the relation more force than ‘toward’, and “own” and “right” are supplied.

> 85 così le avria ripinte per la strada  
> 86 ond' eran tratte, come furo sciolte;  
> 87 ma così salda voglia è troppo rada.  

it would have thrust them back upon the path whence they were drawn, so soon as they were loose; but such sound will is all too rare.

- *così le avria ripinte* ‘so it would have pushed them back’ → “it would have thrust them back”: the answering ‘so’ after the comparison drops out, and the object pronoun moves after the verb.
- *per la strada* ‘along the road’ → “upon the path”: movement along a road is rendered as movement back onto a path.
- *ond' eran tratte* ‘whence they had been drawn’ → “whence they were drawn”: the English uses a simple past passive rather than marking the drawing away as prior.
- *come furo sciolte* ‘as they were released’ → “so soon as they were loose”: the conjunction is expanded to mark immediacy, and the act of release becomes the state of being loose.
- *così salda voglia* ‘so firm a will’ → “such sound will”: the degree of firmness becomes a description of the kind of will, and “sound” replaces ‘firm’.
- *troppo rada* ‘too rare’ → “all too rare”: “all” adds emphasis.

> 88 E per queste parole, se ricolte  
> 89 l' hai come devi, è l' argomento casso,  
> 90 che t' avria fatto noia ancor più volte.  

Now by these words, if thou hast gleaned them as thou should’st, the argument which would have troubled thee more times than this, is rendered void.

- *E* ‘And’ → “Now”: a connective marking the next step replaces the conjunction.
- *se ricolte l' hai* ‘if gathered them thou hast’ → “if thou hast gleaned them”: the auxiliary precedes the participle and the object follows it; “gleaned” is more specific than ‘gathered’.
- *come devi* ‘as thou oughtest’ → “as thou should’st”: a present verb of obligation is rendered by the modal “should’st”.
- *è l' argomento casso* ‘is the argument void’ → “the argument” … “is rendered void”: the subject comes first, the relative clause now intervenes before the predicate, and “rendered” makes the result explicit.
- *t' avria fatto noia* ‘would have caused thee trouble’ → “would have troubled thee”: verb and noun become a single verb, with the pronoun following as its direct object.
- *ancor più volte* ‘yet more times’ → “more times than this”: the translation supplies an explicit term of comparison in place of ‘yet’.

> 91 Ma or ti s' attraversa un altro passo  
> 92 dinanzi agli occhi tal, che per te stesso  
> 93 non usciresti, pria saresti lasso.  

But now across thy path another strait confronts thine eyes, through which ere thou should’st win thy way alone, thou should’st be weary.

- *ti s' attraversa un altro passo dinanzi agli occhi* ‘another passage sets itself athwart thee before the eyes’ → “across thy path another strait confronts thine eyes”: the reflexive verb becomes the opening phrase of position, with “path” supplied; the passage becomes a “strait”, and what was before the eyes now actively “confronts” them.
- *tal, che per te stesso non usciresti, pria saresti lasso* ‘such that by thyself thou wouldst not get out; sooner wouldst thou be weary’ → “through which ere thou should’st win thy way alone, thou should’st be weary”: a relative of passage replaces ‘such that’; the negative verb of exit becomes the affirmative “win thy way”, with ‘by thyself’ moved after it as “alone”. The priority expressed by ‘sooner’ is conveyed by “ere” introducing the first clause rather than by an adverb in the second.

> 94 Io t' ho per certo nella mente messo,  
> 95 ch' alma beata non poria mentire  
> 96 però ch' è sempre al primo vero appresso:  

I have set it in thy mind for sure, that no blessed soul may lie because hard by the Primal Truth it ever doth abide;

- *Io t' ho per certo nella mente messo* ‘I have for certain put into the mind for thee’ → “I have set it in thy mind for sure”: “it” anticipates the following clause, the personal pronoun becomes the possessive “thy”, the participle moves next to the auxiliary, and the expression of certainty moves to the end.
- *alma beata non poria mentire* ‘a soul blessed could not lie’ → “no blessed soul may lie”: the adjective precedes the noun, the negation moves from the verb to the subject, and the conditional ‘could’ becomes “may”.
- *è sempre al primo vero appresso* ‘it is always to the first truth close’ → “hard by the Primal Truth it ever doth abide”: the whole expression of proximity moves to the front, “Primal” replaces ‘first’, and simple being becomes abiding, with the adverb before the expanded verb.

> 97 e poi potesti da Piccarda udire  
> 98 che l' affezion del vel Costanza tenne,  
> 99 sì ch' ella par qui meco contradire.  

and then thou mightest hear from Piccarda that her devotion to the veil Constance still held, so that here she seemeth me to contradict.

- *potesti da Piccarda udire* ‘thou wast able from Piccarda to hear’ → “thou mightest hear from Piccarda”: the past of the verb of ability becomes a modal, and the source follows “hear”.
- *l' affezion del vel* ‘the affection of the veil’ → “her devotion to the veil”: the article becomes a possessive, “devotion” specifies the kind of attachment, and “to” marks the veil as its object.
- *tenne* ‘held’ → “still held”: “still” makes continuance explicit.
- *ella par qui meco contradire* ‘she seems here with me to contradict’ → “here she seemeth me to contradict”: “here” moves to the front, and ‘with me’ becomes the direct object “me”.

> 100 Molte fiate già, frate, addivenne  
> 101 che per fuggir periglio, contro a grato  
> 102 sì fe' di quel che far non si convenne;  

Many a time ere now, my brother, hath it come to pass that to flee peril things were done, against the grain, that were unmeet to do;

- *Molte fiate già* ‘many times already’ → “Many a time ere now”: the plural becomes a distributive singular, and ‘already’ becomes a phrase of time.
- *frate* ‘brother’ → “my brother”: the possessive is supplied.
- *addivenne* ‘it happened’ → “hath it come to pass”: a simple past becomes a present perfect expressed by a verbal phrase, with the auxiliary before “it”.
- *per fuggir periglio* ‘in order to flee peril’ → “to flee peril”: the bare infinitive of purpose does the work of the Italian preposition and infinitive.
- *contro a grato* ‘contrary to one's liking’ → “against the grain”: an idiom replaces the reference to liking, and the phrase moves after the action it qualifies.
- *sì fe' di quel che far non si convenne* ‘one did some of that which it was not fitting to do’ → “things were done” … “that were unmeet to do”: the indefinite singular construction becomes a plural passive, and the partitive gives way to the noun “things”; the impersonal judgement of fitness becomes an adjective predicated of those things.

> 103 come Almeone, che, di ciò pregato  
> 104 dal padre suo, la propria madre spense,  
> 105 per non perder pietà si fe' spietato.  

so did Alcmæon, moved by his father’s prayer, slay his own mother, and not to sacrifice his filial piety became an impious son.

- *come Almeone, che* ‘as Alcmæon, who’ → “so did Alcmæon”: the comparison and relative clause become an independent clause, with the auxiliary before the subject.
- *di ciò pregato dal padre suo* ‘of this entreated by his father’ → “moved by his father’s prayer”: the object of the entreaty drops out; the father's entreating becomes a possessive and a noun, and the participle describes its effect.
- *la propria madre spense* ‘his own mother he extinguished’ → “slay his own mother”: the figurative verb becomes a direct verb of killing, in the base form after “did”, and the object follows it.
- *per non perder pietà* ‘in order not to lose piety’ → “and not to sacrifice his filial piety”: “and” connects the clauses, losing becomes sacrificing, and “his filial” specifies the otherwise unqualified noun.
- *si fe' spietato* ‘he made himself pitiless’ → “became an impious son”: the reflexive verb becomes a verb of becoming; the adjective is read in terms of impiety rather than pitilessness, and “son” is supplied.

> 106 A questo punto voglio che tu pense  
> 107 che la forza al voler si mischia, e fanno  
> 108 sì che scusar non si posson l' offense.  

At this point, I would have thee think, violence receiveth mixture from the will, and they so work that the offences may not plead excuse.

- *voglio che tu pense che* ‘I wish that thou think that’ → “I would have thee think,”: the present verb of willing and its subjunctive clause become a modal construction with “thee” and an infinitive; the following assertion is set beside “think” without a second ‘that’.
- *la forza al voler si mischia* ‘force with the will mingles’ → “violence receiveth mixture from the will”: “violence” replaces ‘force’; the reflexive verb becomes a verb and noun, and the will is presented as the source of the admixture.
- *fanno sì* ‘they bring it about so’ → “they so work”: the adverb precedes the verb, and the causative expression becomes “work”.
- *scusar non si posson l' offense* ‘be excused cannot the offences’ → “the offences may not plead excuse”: the subject moves to the front, inability is expressed by “may not”, and the passive construction becomes an active one in which the offences themselves plead.

> 109 Voglia assoluta non consente al danno,  
> 110 ma consentevi in tanto in quanto teme,  
> 111 se si ritrae, cadere in più affanno.  

The absolute will consenteth not to the ill, but yet consenteth in so far as it doth fear, should it draw back, to fall into a worse annoy.

- *Voglia assoluta* ‘will absolute’ → “The absolute will”: the article is supplied and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *non consente al danno* ‘not consents to the harm’ → “consenteth not to the ill”: the negative follows the verb, and “ill” replaces ‘harm’.
- *ma consentevi* ‘but it consents thereto’ → “but yet consenteth”: “yet” is supplied, while the attached pronoun referring back to the harm is not separately rendered.
- *in tanto in quanto teme* ‘to such an extent as it fears’ → “in so far as it doth fear”: the paired expressions of degree become one English locution, and the simple verb takes the auxiliary “doth”.
- *se si ritrae* ‘if it draws back’ → “should it draw back”: an inverted modal conditional replaces ‘if’ and the present indicative; “draw back” needs no reflexive pronoun.
- *in più affanno* ‘into greater distress’ → “into a worse annoy”: the comparative of degree becomes “worse”, and distress is expressed as a countable “annoy”.

> 112 Però, quando Piccarda quello espreme,  
> 113 della voglia assoluta intende, ed io  
> 114 dell' altra, sì che ver diciamo insieme.”  

Wherefore, when Piccarda expresseth this, she meaneth it of the absolute will, and I of the other; so that we both speak truth together.”

- *quello espreme* ‘that she expresses’ → “expresseth this”: the demonstrative changes from ‘that’ to “this” and follows the verb.
- *della voglia assoluta intende* ‘of the will absolute she means’ → “she meaneth it of the absolute will”: the verb moves before the prepositional phrase and takes an explicit object, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *ver diciamo insieme* ‘truth we speak together’ → “we both speak truth together”: the object moves after the verb, and “both” is supplied alongside “together”.

## Lines 115–142 — The Intellect’s Rest and the Question of Broken Vows

> 115 Cotal fu l' ondeggiar del santo rio,  
> 116 ch' uscì del fonte ond' ogni ver deriva;  

Such the rippling of the sacred stream which issued from the Spring whence all truth down-floweth;

- *Cotal fu* ‘such was’ → “Such”: the copula is left understood.
- *l' ondeggiar* ‘the undulating’ → “the rippling”: the infinitive used as a noun becomes an English verbal noun, with a more particular word for the motion of the water.
- *ogni ver deriva* ‘every truth flows’ → “all truth down-floweth”: the distributive singular becomes collective, and the direction of flow is made explicit.

> 117 tal pose in pace uno ed altro disio.  

and being such, it set at peace one and the other longing.

- *tal* ‘such’ → “and being such”: a conjunction and a participle are supplied to connect the description to the next verb.

> 118 “O amanza del primo amante, o diva,  
> 119 diss' io appresso, il cui parlar m' inonda,  
> 120 e scalda sì, che più e più m' avviva,  

“O love of the primal Lover, O divine one,” said I then, “whose speech o’erfloweth me and warmeth, so that more and more it quickeneth me,

- *primo* ‘first’ → “primal”: the ordinal is rendered by an adjective of origin or primacy.
- *diva* ‘goddess’ → “divine one”: an adjective with “one” replaces the feminine noun of address.
- *appresso* ‘afterwards’ → “then”: succession is expressed less explicitly.
- *il cui parlar m' inonda* ‘whose speaking me inundates’ → “whose speech o’erfloweth me”: the substantival infinitive becomes a noun, and the object follows the verb rather than preceding it.
- *sì, che* ‘so greatly that’ → “so that”: the degree word modifying the preceding verb is drawn into an English conjunction of result.
- *m' avviva* ‘me it quickens’ → “it quickeneth me”: the object follows the verb.

> 121 non è l' affezion mia tanto profonda,  
> 122 che basti a render voi grazia per grazia;  
> 123 ma quei che vede e puote a ciò risponda.  

my love hath no such depth as to suffice to render grace for grace; but may he who seeth it, and hath the power, answer thereto.

- *non è l' affezion mia tanto profonda* ‘is not the affection of mine so deep’ → “my love hath no such depth”: the subject comes first, the possessive precedes the noun, and ‘affection’ becomes “love”; a negated copula and adjective become a verb of possession with a negated noun phrase.
- *che basti* ‘that it should suffice’ → “as to suffice”: the finite subjunctive clause becomes an infinitive construction dependent on “such”.
- *a render voi* ‘to render to you’ → “to render”: the recipient is omitted.
- *quei che vede* ‘he who sees’ → “he who seeth it”: an object is supplied.
- *puote* ‘can’ → “hath the power”: a verb of capacity becomes a verb with a noun as its object.
- *a ciò risponda* ‘to that let him answer’ → “may he” … “answer thereto”: the optative subjunctive is expressed by an auxiliary placed before the subject, and the complement follows the verb.

> 124 Io veggio ben che giammai non si sazia  
> 125 nostro intelletto, se il ver non lo illustra,  
> 126 di fuor dal qual nessun vero si spazia.  

Now do I see that never can our intellect be sated, unless that Truth shine on it, beyond which no truth hath range.

- *Io veggio ben* ‘I see well’ → “Now do I see”: the adverb of assurance gives way to a temporal adverb, with auxiliary inversion.
- *giammai non si sazia nostro intelletto* ‘never is sated our intellect’ → “never can our intellect be sated”: one English negative does the work of the Italian negative pair; the reflexive becomes a passive, with an added modal before the subject.
- *se il ver non lo illustra* ‘if the truth does not illumine it’ → “unless that Truth shine on it”: the negative condition becomes an “unless” clause, the article becomes demonstrative, and a transitive indicative becomes a subjunctive with a prepositional complement.
- *di fuor dal qual* ‘outside of which’ → “beyond which”: a single preposition replaces the phrase.
- *si spazia* ‘ranges freely’ → “hath range”: the pronominal verb becomes a verb of possession with a noun.

> 127 Posasi in esso, come fera in lustra,  
> 128 tosto che giunto l' ha: e giugner puollo;  
> 129 se non, ciascun disio sarebbe *frustra*.  

Therein it resteth as a wild beast in his den so soon as it hath reached it; and reach it may; else were all longing futile.

- *Posasi in esso* ‘it rests itself in it’ → “Therein it resteth”: the locative phrase becomes an adverb placed first, and the reflexive gives way to an intransitive verb.
- *come fera in lustra* ‘as wild beast in den’ → “as a wild beast in his den”: English supplies an indefinite article and makes the beast’s possession of the den explicit.
- *tosto che* ‘as soon as’ → “so soon as”: a different comparative turn introduces the time clause.
- *giunto l' ha* ‘reached it has’ → “it hath reached it”: the auxiliary precedes the participle, and the object follows it.
- *giugner puollo* ‘reach it-can [it]’ → “reach it may”: the infinitive stays first; the object attached to the modal in Italian is understood rather than repeated, and the English “it” before “may” is the subject.
- *se non* ‘if not’ → “else”: a single adverb replaces the abbreviated condition.
- *ciascun disio sarebbe* ‘each desire would be’ → “were all longing”: the distributive becomes collective, and the conditional becomes a subjunctive placed before its subject.
- *frustra* ‘in vain’ → “futile”: the Latin adverb becomes a predicate adjective.

> 130 Nasce per quello, a guisa di rampollo,  
> 131 a piè del vero il dubbio: ed è natura,  
> 132 che al sommo pinge noi di collo in collo.  

Wherefore there springeth, like a shoot, questioning at the foot of truth; which is a thing that thrusteth us to-wards the summit, on from ridge to ridge.

- *Nasce per quello* ‘springs up on account of that’ → “Wherefore there springeth”: the causal phrase becomes a connective placed first, and presentative “there” introduces the postponed subject.
- *a guisa di rampollo* ‘in the manner of a shoot’ → “like a shoot”: a preposition replaces the phrase of comparison.
- *a piè del vero il dubbio* ‘at the foot of the truth the doubt’ → “questioning at the foot of truth”: the subject moves before the locative phrase; the definite noun for a doubt becomes an articleless verbal noun for the act of questioning, and ‘truth’ also loses its article.
- *ed è natura, che* ‘and it is nature that’ → “which is a thing that”: coordination becomes a relative clause, and the translation treats the preceding process as something that propels us rather than naming nature as the agent.
- *al sommo pinge noi* ‘to the summit pushes us’ → “thrusteth us to-wards the summit”: the directional phrase moves after verb and object, and “to-wards” expresses direction rather than an endpoint.
- *di collo in collo* ‘from hill to hill’ → “on from ridge to ridge”: “on” is supplied to mark progression, and the successive heights are called ridges.

> 133 Questo m' invita, questo m' assicura,  
> 134 con riverenza, donna, a domandarvi  
> 135 d' un' altra verità che m' è oscura.  

This doth invite me and giveth me assurance, with reverence, lady, to make question to thee as to another truth which is dark to me.

- *Questo m' invita* ‘this me invites’ → “This doth invite me”: the object moves after the verb, and the affirmative auxiliary is supplied.
- *questo m' assicura* ‘this me assures’ → “and giveth me assurance”: a conjunction replaces the repeated subject, and the single verb becomes a verb with a noun object.
- *a domandarvi* ‘to ask you’ → “to make question to thee”: the verb becomes a phrase, and the attached courteous plural pronoun is rendered by a singular pronoun after a preposition.
- *d' un' altra verità* ‘of another truth’ → “as to another truth”: the topic of the question is introduced by a different prepositional turn.
- *m' è oscura* ‘to me is obscure’ → “is dark to me”: the personal complement follows the adjective, which is rendered by “dark”.

> 136 Io vo' saper se l' uom può satisfarvi  
> 137 ai voti manchi sì con altri beni,  
> 138 ch' alla vostra statera non sien parvi.”  

I would know if man can satisfy you so for broken vows, with other goods, as not to weigh too short upon your balance.”

- *vo' saper* ‘wish to know’ → “would know”: volition is expressed by a modal with a bare infinitive.
- *l' uom* ‘the man’ → “man”: English drops the article in the generic singular.
- *ai voti manchi sì* ‘for the vows unfulfilled so’ → “so for broken vows”: the degree adverb moves forward, the article disappears, and the adjective moves before the noun; “broken” characterizes the vows more strongly than ‘unfulfilled’.
- *ch' alla vostra statera non sien parvi* ‘that on your balance they be not small’ → “as not to weigh too short upon your balance”: the result clause with a finite subjunctive becomes an infinitive construction; being small becomes weighing too little, and the balance phrase moves to the end.

> 139 Beatrice mi guardò con gli occhi pieni  
> 140 di faville d' amor così divini,  
> 141 che, vinta, mia virtù diede le reni,  
> 142 e quasi mi perdei con gli occhi chini.  

Beatrice looked on me with eyes filled so divine with sparks of love, that my vanquished power turned away, and I became as lost with eyes downcast.

- *mi guardò* ‘me she regarded’ → “looked on me”: a transitive verb takes a prepositional turn, with the pronoun after it.
- *con gli occhi pieni* ‘with the eyes full’ → “with eyes filled”: the article is omitted, and the adjective becomes a participle.
- *di faville d' amor così divini* ‘of sparks of love so divine’ → “so divine with sparks of love”: the complement of fullness is introduced by “with”; the masculine plural adjective, agreeing with ‘eyes’, is brought forward from its delayed position.
- *vinta, mia virtù* ‘overcome, my power’ → “my vanquished power”: the detached participle becomes an attributive modifier inside the subject phrase.
- *diede le reni* ‘gave the back’ → “turned away”: the bodily idiom is replaced by a verb of turning with a directional adverb.
- *quasi mi perdei* ‘I almost lost myself’ → “I became as lost”: the reflexive event becomes a change of state, and approximation is expressed by “as” rather than ‘almost’.
- *con gli occhi chini* ‘with the eyes lowered’ → “with eyes downcast”: the article is omitted, and the lowered position is expressed by “downcast”.

## Summary — Changes of Construction, Continuities of Image

Wicksteed often changes the order of a phrase while keeping its parts. A subject placed after its verb in Italian commonly comes before it in English; an object pronoun before the verb moves after it, as in *m’ avviva*, “it quickeneth me”. Even the inverted “would a lamb stand still” places the subject between the auxiliary and the main verb rather than after the whole verb, as in *si starebbe un agno*. Two routine changes generally go unmarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending identifies the subject, and drops the article before a possessive, as in *il mio disir*, “my desire”.

A change of construction often brings a change of emphasis. In *egualmente temendo*, ‘fearing equally’, the adverb measures the act of fearing; “in equipoise of dread” instead names the balance itself. Conversely, *ti farò contento*, ‘I shall make thee content’, contracts to “I will satisfy thee”. Wicksteed also makes relations between clauses explicit: the gerund *possendo*, ‘being able’, becomes “since they had power”, marking ability as the reason for what precedes.

Some departures turn on a single word. For *segue*, ‘follows’, Wicksteed gives “doth abet”, which implies active assistance. Elsewhere he draws a word from a neighbouring image: “in warmer colours far” supplies “colours” from the earlier *dipinto*, ‘painted’. Small qualifications can also disappear, as the parenthetic *io dico*, ‘I say’, does. Yet the imagery and phrasing often remain close: *grazia per grazia* keeps its repetition in “grace for grace”, and *a piè del vero* remains “at the foot of truth”. In the latter phrase the image survives exactly where the adjoining noun changes: *il dubbio*, ‘the doubt’, becomes the activity of “questioning”.
