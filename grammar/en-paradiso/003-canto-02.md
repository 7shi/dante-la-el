# Paradiso 2

## Lines 1–18 — A Warning to Those Who Follow

> 1 O voi, che siete in piccioletta barca,  
> 2 desiderosi d'ascoltar, seguiti  
> 3 retro al mio legno che cantando varca,  

O ye who in your little skiff, longing to hear, have followed on my keel that singeth on its way,

- *siete … seguiti* ‘are … followed’ → “have followed”: the Italian perfect of the verb of motion uses ‘be’ as its auxiliary; English uses ‘have’ and puts the auxiliary beside the participle.
- *in piccioletta barca* ‘in a very little boat’ → “in your little skiff”: “your” is supplied; the diminutive is conveyed by “little” together with the more specific “skiff”.
- *desiderosi d'ascoltar* ‘desirous of listening’ → “longing to hear”: a participle replaces the adjective, and listening is expressed as hearing.
- *retro al mio legno* ‘behind my timber’ → “on my keel”: “on” takes its sense from the preceding “followed”; one figure for the vessel, its timber, is replaced by another, its keel.
- *cantando varca* ‘singing passes on’ → “singeth on its way”: the singing becomes the finite verb, and the onward movement becomes a phrase modifying it.

> 4 tornate a riveder li vostri liti:  

turn to revisit your own shores;

- *tornate a riveder* ‘return to see again’ → “turn to revisit”: “turn” expresses the change of direction, while “revisit” carries the sense of going back as well as seeing again.
- *li vostri liti* ‘your shores’ → “your own shores”: “own” adds emphasis.

> 5 non vi mettete in pelago; chè forse,  
> 6 perdendo me, rimarreste smarriti.  

commit you not to the open sea; for perchance, losing me, ye would be left astray.

- *non vi mettete in pelago* ‘do not put yourselves out into open sea’ → “commit you not to the open sea”: the verb of placing becomes one of entrusting, with “to” rather than ‘into’; “you” does duty for the reflexive, the negative follows it, and the sea takes a definite article.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a coordinating conjunction replaces a subordinating one.
- *rimarreste smarriti* ‘you would remain lost’ → “ye would be left astray”: the verb of remaining becomes a passive, and the participle describing the resulting state becomes “astray”.

> 7 L'acqua ch' io prendo giammai non si corse:  

The water which I take was never coursed before;

- *giammai non si corse* ‘never was it traversed’ → “was never coursed before”: the *si* construction becomes a passive with an auxiliary; the two Italian marks of negation become the single “never”, placed after the auxiliary, and “before” is supplied.

> 8 Minerva spira, e conducemi Apollo,  
> 9 e nove Muse mi dimostran l' Orse.  

Minerva bloweth, Apollo guideth me, and the nine Muses point me to the Bears.

- *e conducemi Apollo* ‘and leads me Apollo’ → “Apollo guideth me”: the conjunction is dropped, the subject comes before the verb, and leading is rendered as guiding.
- *nove Muse* ‘nine Muses’ → “the nine Muses”: the definite article is supplied.
- *mi dimostran l' Orse* ‘show me the Bears’ → “point me to the Bears”: what is shown becomes the object of “to”, and the person to whom it is shown becomes the direct object of “point”.

> 10 Voi altri pochi, che drizzaste il collo  
> 11 per tempo al pan degli angeli, del quale  
> 12 vivesi qui, ma non sen vien satollo,  

Ye other few, who timely have lift up your necks for bread of angels whereby life is here sustained but wherefrom none cometh away sated,

- *drizzaste il collo per tempo* ‘you raised the neck in good time’ → “timely have lift up your necks”: the time phrase becomes an adverb placed before the verb, the simple past becomes a perfect, and the singular body part with its definite article becomes a plural with a possessive.
- *al pan degli angeli* ‘toward the bread of the angels’ → “for bread of angels”: direction becomes purpose, and neither noun retains its definite article.
- *del quale vivesi qui* ‘on which one lives here’ → “whereby life is here sustained”: the relative phrase becomes an adverb of means; impersonal living becomes a passive clause with “life” as subject and “here” before the participle.
- *non sen vien satollo* ‘one does not come away from it sated’ → “wherefrom none cometh away sated”: the sense of ‘from it’ is brought forward as a second relative, and the negative is transferred from the verb to its subject.

> 13 metter potete ben per l' alto sale  
> 14 vostro navigio, servando mio solco  
> 15 dinanzi all' acqua che ritorna equale.  

ye may indeed commit your vessel to the deep keeping my furrow, in advance of the water that is falling back to the level.

- *metter potete ben* ‘put you can indeed’ → “ye may indeed commit”: the modal precedes the infinitive, ability is phrased as permission, and putting the vessel to sea is again rendered as committing it.
- *per l' alto sale vostro navigio* ‘upon the high salt your vessel’ → “your vessel to the deep”: the object is moved ahead of the sea phrase; “to” marks a destination, and “the deep” replaces the adjective and the figurative noun for the sea.
- *dinanzi all' acqua* ‘ahead of the water’ → “in advance of the water”: an adverb of position is expanded into a prepositional phrase.
- *ritorna equale* ‘returns level’ → “is falling back to the level”: the simple present becomes progressive, returning is expressed as falling back, and the adjective of resulting state becomes a prepositional phrase with a noun.

> 16 Quei gloriosi che passaro a Colco  
> 17 non s' ammiraron, come voi farete,  
> 18 quando Jason vider fatto bifolco.  

The glorious ones who fared to Colchis not so marvelled as shall ye, when Jason turned ox-plough-man in their sight.

- *Quei gloriosi* ‘those glorious ones’ → “The glorious ones”: the demonstrative becomes a definite article.
- *passaro* ‘passed’ → “fared”: the verb is rendered as one of journeying.
- *non s' ammiraron* ‘did not marvel’ → “not so marvelled”: the Italian pronominal verb becomes a simple intransitive, and “so” is supplied in anticipation of the comparison.
- *come voi farete* ‘as you will do’ → “as shall ye”: the future auxiliary stands alone for the repeated action, and the subject follows it.
- *quando Jason vider fatto bifolco* ‘when Jason they saw made a ploughman’ → “when Jason turned ox-plough-man in their sight”: Jason changes from the object of seeing to the subject of “turned”; the participle becomes a finite verb of becoming, the occupational noun specifies the ox, and the act of seeing becomes the closing phrase “in their sight”.

## Lines 19–51 — Ascent to the Moon

> 19 La concreata e perpetua sete  
> 20 del deiforme regno cen portava  
> 21 veloci, quasi come il ciel vedete.  

The thirst, born with us and ne’er failing, for the god-like realm bore us swift almost as ye see the heaven.

- *La concreata e perpetua sete* ‘the co-created and perpetual thirst’ → “The thirst, born with us and ne’er failing”: the modifiers follow the noun; the first is expanded to say with whom the thirst was created, and the second becomes a negative participial phrase.
- *del* ‘of the’ → “for the”: English marks the object of thirst with a different preposition.
- *cen portava* ‘us thither was bearing’ → “bore us”: the object follows the verb, the directional force of *ne* is not separately rendered, and the imperfect becomes a simple past.
- *come il ciel vedete* ‘as the heaven ye see’ → “as ye see the heaven”: the object moves after the verb.

> 22 Beatrice in suso, ed io in lei guardava;  

Beatrice was gazing upward, and I on her;

- *Beatrice in suso, ed io in lei guardava* ‘Beatrice upward, and I on her gazed’ → “Beatrice was gazing upward, and I on her”: the verb, expressed only in the second clause in Italian, is expressed only in the first in English; the progressive brings out the continuing action of the imperfect.

> 23 e forse in tanto, in quanto un quadrel posa  
> 24 e vola e dalla noce si dischiava,  

and perchance in such space as an arrow stays and flies and is discharged from the nocking point

- *in tanto, in quanto* ‘in so much, in as much as’ → “in such space as”: the correlative expressions become a noun phrase of duration followed by “as”, without a second preposition.
- *un quadrel* ‘a bolt’ → “an arrow”: the less specific projectile name replaces the term for a crossbow bolt.
- *posa* ‘comes to rest’ → “stays”: the verb of coming to rest is rendered by one that can also denote remaining still.
- *dalla noce si dischiava* ‘from the nut unfastens itself’ → “is discharged from the nocking point”: the pronominal verb becomes passive, the point of release is described by its function rather than named as the nut, and the prepositional phrase follows the verb.

> 25 giunto mi vidi ove mirabil cosa  
> 26 mi torse il viso a sè; e però quella,  
> 27 cui non potea mia opra essere ascosa,  

I saw me arrived where a wondrous thing drew my sight to it; and therefore she from whom my doing might not be hidden

- *giunto mi vidi* ‘arrived myself I saw’ → “I saw me arrived”: both the reflexive object and the participle describing it move after the verb.
- *mirabil cosa* ‘wondrous thing’ → “a wondrous thing”: the indefinite article is supplied.
- *mi torse il viso a sè* ‘turned me the sight toward itself’ → “drew my sight to it”: the dative becomes a possessive, turning becomes drawing, and the reflexive becomes a personal pronoun.
- *quella* ‘that one’ → “she”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *cui non potea mia opra essere ascosa* ‘to whom could not my action be hidden’ → “from whom my doing might not be hidden”: English construes concealment with “from”; the subject precedes the modal, the negative follows it, and ability is phrased as possibility.

> 28 volta ver me sì lieta come bella:  

turning to me as much in joy as beauty,

- *volta ver me* ‘turned toward me’ → “turning to me”: the past participle becomes an active present participle.
- *sì lieta come bella* ‘as joyful as beautiful’ → “as much in joy as beauty”: comparison of two adjectives becomes comparison of degree with two nouns.

> 29 “Drizza la mente in Dio grata, mi disse,  
> 30 che n' ha congiunti con la prima stella.”  

“Direct thy mind to God in gratitude,” she said, “who hath united us with the first star.”

- *la mente* ‘the mind’ → “thy mind”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *in Dio grata* ‘on God, grateful’ → “to God in gratitude”: the direction of thought is expressed with “to”, and the adjective agreeing with the mind becomes an adverbial phrase.
- *mi disse* ‘said to me’ → “she said”: the addressee is left unexpressed.
- *n' ha congiunti* ‘us hath united’ → “hath united us”: the object moves after the verb.

> 31 Pareva a me che nube ne coprisse  
> 32 lucida, spessa, solida e polita,  
> 33 quasi adamante che lo sol ferisse.  

Meseemed a cloud enveloped us, shining, dense, firm and polished, like diamond smitten by the sun.

- *Pareva a me* ‘it seemed to me’ → “Meseemed”: verb and experiencer are compressed into one impersonal form.
- *che nube ne coprisse* ‘that a cloud us should cover’ → “a cloud enveloped us”: the complementizer drops out, the imperfect subjunctive becomes a simple past, the object follows the verb, and covering is rendered as enveloping.
- *spessa, solida* ‘thick, solid’ → “dense, firm”: density and firmness replace thickness and solidity.
- *quasi* ‘as if’ → “like”: the comparison is introduced by a preposition.
- *che lo sol ferisse* ‘that the sun should strike’ → “smitten by the sun”: the active relative clause with a subjunctive verb becomes a passive participial phrase.

> 34 Per entro sè l' eterna margarita  
> 35 ne recepette, com' acqua recepe  
> 36 raggio di luce, permanendo unita.  

Within itself the eternal pearl received us, as water doth receive a ray of light, though still itself uncleft.

- *Per entro sè* ‘through into itself’ → “Within itself”: the compound expression of penetration becomes a single locative preposition.
- *ne recepette* ‘us received’ → “received us”: the object follows the verb.
- *recepe* ‘receives’ → “doth receive”: the simple present is expressed with an auxiliary.
- *raggio di luce* ‘ray of light’ → “a ray of light”: the indefinite article is supplied.
- *permanendo unita* ‘remaining united’ → “though still itself uncleft”: the gerund gives way to a verbless concession; “still” conveys continuance, “itself” is supplied, and unity is expressed negatively as absence of division.

> 37 S' io era corpo, e qui non si concepe  
> 38 com' una dimension altra patio  
> 39 ch' esser convien se corpo in corpo repe,  

If I was body,—and if here we conceive not how one dimension could support another, which must be, if body into body creep,—

- *e qui non si concepe* ‘and here one does not conceive’ → “and if here we conceive not”: a second “if” is supplied, and the impersonal construction becomes first-person plural.
- *una dimension altra patio* ‘one dimension another endured’ → “one dimension could support another”: the object moves after the verb, and the simple past of enduring or admitting becomes a modal construction with “support”.
- *ch' esser convien* ‘which to be is necessary’ → “which must be”: impersonal necessity governing an infinitive becomes a modal preceding “be”.

> 40 accender ne dovria più il disio  
> 41 di veder quella essenza, in che si vede  
> 42 come nostra natura e Dio s' unio.  

the more should longing enkindle us to see that Essence wherein we behold how our own nature and God unified themselves.

- *accender ne dovria più il disio* ‘it ought to kindle in us more the desire’ → “the more should longing enkindle us”: the comparative comes first; the desire to be kindled becomes the subject doing the kindling, and those whose desire it is become the direct object.
- *di veder* ‘of seeing’ → “to see”: an English infinitive replaces the preposition and infinitive dependent on the noun of desire.
- *in che si vede* ‘in which one sees’ → “wherein we behold”: the preposition and relative pronoun are combined, the impersonal becomes first-person plural, and seeing is rendered as beholding.
- *nostra natura* ‘our nature’ → “our own nature”: the intensive “own” is supplied.
- *s' unio* ‘united itself’ → “unified themselves”: the Italian singular verb with a reflexive clitic is rendered with “unified” and an explicitly plural reflexive referring to both members of the subject.

> 43 Lì si vedrà ciò che tenem per fede,  
> 44 non dimostrato, ma fia per sè noto,  
> 45 a guisa del ver primo che l'uom crede.  

There what we hold by faith shall be beheld, not demonstrated, but self-known in fashion of the initial truth which man believeth.

- *si vedrà ciò che tenem* ‘will be seen that which we hold’ → “what we hold by faith shall be beheld”: the subject of the passive moves before the verb, the demonstrative and relative become “what”, and seeing becomes beholding.
- *per fede* ‘through faith’ → “by faith”: English uses “by” for the means.
- *ma fia per sè noto* ‘but will be known of itself’ → “but self-known”: the repeated future copula is omitted, and the prepositional expression is compressed into a compound.
- *a guisa del ver primo* ‘in the fashion of the truth first’ → “in fashion of the initial truth”: the article before “fashion” is absent, and “initial” precedes its noun.
- *l'uom* ‘the man’ → “man”: English uses the bare noun generically.

> 46 Io risposi: “Madonna, sì devoto,  
> 47 quant' esser posso più, ringrazio lui  
> 48 lo qual dal mortal mondo m' ha remoto.  

I answered: “Lady, devoutly as I most may, do I thank him who hath removed me from the mortal world.

- *Madonna* ‘my lady’ → “Lady”: the possessive force of the title is not expressed.
- *sì devoto, quant' esser posso più* ‘as devout as I can most be’ → “devoutly as I most may”: the adjective describing the speaker becomes an adverb modifying his thanks; the first degree marker and the infinitive of being disappear, and the superlative adverb precedes the modal.
- *ringrazio* ‘I thank’ → “do I thank”: English adds an auxiliary and inverts it with the subject after the introductory expression of degree.
- *lo qual* ‘the which’ → “who”: English uses a personal relative pronoun.
- *dal mortal mondo m' ha remoto* ‘from the mortal world me hath removed’ → “hath removed me from the mortal world”: both the object and the phrase of separation move after the verb.

> 49 Ma ditemi, che son li segni bui  
> 50 di questo corpo, che laggiuso in terra  
> 51 fan di Cain favoleggiare altrui?”  

But tell me what those dusky marks upon this body, which down there on earth make folk to tell the tale of Cain?”

- *che son li segni bui* ‘what are the marks dark’ → “what those dusky marks”: the copula is omitted, the article becomes a demonstrative, and the adjective is rendered by “dusky” before its noun.
- *di questo corpo* ‘of this body’ → “upon this body”: the relation is expressed as one of position.
- *fan di Cain favoleggiare altrui* ‘make about Cain tell tales other people’ → “make folk to tell the tale of Cain”: the people made to speak come immediately after the causative verb; the bare infinitive becomes a *to*-infinitive with a definite singular object, and the reference to Cain follows that object.

## Lines 52–84 — Rare and Dense Put to the Test

> 52 Ella sorrise alquanto, e poi: “S' egli erra  
> 53 l' opinion, mi disse, dei mortali,  
> 54 dove chiave di senso non disserra,  

She smiled a little, and then: “And if,” she said, “the opinion of mortals goeth wrong, where the key of sense doth not unlock,

- *S' egli erra l' opinion … dei mortali* ‘if it errs, the opinion … of mortals’ → “And if,” … “the opinion of mortals goeth wrong”: “And” is supplied; the anticipatory *egli* drops out, the full subject is brought together before the verb, and ‘errs’ becomes “goeth wrong”.
- *mi disse* ‘to me she said’ → “she said”: the addressee is omitted, and the reporting clause is moved forward to follow “if”.
- *chiave* ‘key’ → “the key”: the definite article is supplied.

> 55 certo non ti dovrien punger gli strali  
> 56 d' ammirazione omai; poi retro ai sensi  
> 57 vedi che la ragione ha corte l' ali.  

truly the shafts of wonder should no longer pierce thee; since even when the senses give the lead thou see’st reason hath wings too short.

- *non ti dovrien punger gli strali d' ammirazione omai* ‘should not prick thee, the shafts of wonder, by now’ → “the shafts of wonder should no longer pierce thee”: the subject moves before the verb and the object after it; “pierce” renders ‘prick’, and the negation and final adverb are combined as “no longer”.
- *retro ai sensi* ‘behind the senses’ → “even when the senses give the lead”: a prepositional phrase becomes a temporal clause with the senses as subject; “even” makes the contrast explicit.
- *vedi che la ragione* ‘thou seest that reason’ → “thou see’st reason”: the conjunction and the Italian article before the abstract noun are omitted.
- *ha corte l' ali* ‘hath short the wings’ → “hath wings too short”: the article drops out, the adjective follows the noun, and “too” makes the insufficiency explicit.

> 58 Ma dimmi quel che tu da te ne pensi.”  

But tell me what thou, of thyself, thinkest concerning it?”

- *quel che … ne pensi* ‘that which … of it thou thinkest’ → “what” … “thinkest concerning it”: the demonstrative and relative are fused in “what”, and the preverbal *ne* becomes a prepositional phrase after the verb.

> 59 Ed io: “Ciò che n' appar quassù diverso,  
> 60 credo che il fanno i corpi rari e densi.”  

And I: “That which to us appeareth diverse in this high region, I hold to be produced by bodies rare and dense.”

- *quassù diverso* ‘up here diverse’ → “diverse in this high region”: the adjective moves forward, and a local adverb becomes a phrase naming a “region”.
- *credo che il fanno i corpi rari e densi* ‘I believe that the rare and dense bodies make it’ → “I hold to be produced by bodies rare and dense”: ‘believe that’ becomes “hold” with an infinitive; the active becomes passive, the bodies become the agent of “produced” and lose their article, and the fronted “That which …” serves directly as the object of “hold” without a resumptive ‘it’.

> 61 Ed ella: “Certo assai vedrai sommerso  
> 62 nel falso il creder tuo, se bene ascolti  
> 63 l' argomentar ch' io gli farò avverso.  

And she: “Verily, thou shalt see thy thought plunged deep in falsity, if well thou hearken to the argument which I shall make against it.

- *assai vedrai sommerso nel falso il creder tuo* ‘much wilt thou see submerged in the false thy belief’ → “thou shalt see thy thought plunged deep in falsity”: “deep” renders the degree of submersion beside “plunged”; the object moves before the participle, the possessive before its noun, and the substantivized infinitive and adjective become the nouns “thought” and “falsity”.
- *ascolti l' argomentar* ‘thou hear the arguing’ → “thou hearken to the argument”: the English verb takes “to”, and a noun for the argument replaces the infinitive naming the act of arguing.
- *ch' io gli farò avverso* ‘which I shall make adverse to it’ → “which I shall make against it”: the adjective and its preverbal dative become a single prepositional phrase after the verb.

> 64 La spera ottava vi dimostra molti  
> 65 lumi, li quali nel quale e nel quanto  
> 66 notar si posson di diversi volti.  

The eighth sphere revealeth many lights to you, the which in quality, as eke in quantity, may be observed of diverse countenance.

- *La spera ottava* ‘the sphere eighth’ → “The eighth sphere”: the ordinal precedes the noun.
- *vi dimostra molti lumi* ‘to you shows many lights’ → “revealeth many lights to you”: “revealeth” renders ‘shows’, and the recipient moves after the direct object.
- *nel quale e nel quanto* ‘in the what-kind and in the how-much’ → “in quality, as eke in quantity”: the substantivized forms become abstract nouns without articles, and simple coordination becomes the more emphatic “as eke”.
- *notar si posson* ‘note themselves one can’ → “may be observed”: the Italian *si* construction has passive force; English uses a modal followed by an explicit passive infinitive, with “observed” for ‘noted’.
- *di diversi volti* ‘of diverse faces’ → “of diverse countenance”: the plural becomes a collective singular.

> 67 Se raro e denso ciò facesser tanto,  
> 68 una sola virtù sarebbe in tutti,  
> 69 più e men distributa, ed altrettanto.  

If rare and dense alone produced this thing, one only virtue, more or less or equally distributed, were in them all.

- *ciò facesser tanto* ‘this did so much’ → “alone produced this thing”: “alone” takes *tanto* restrictively and places its force on “rare and dense”; ‘did’ becomes “produced”, and *ciò* is expanded and moved after the verb.
- *sarebbe in tutti* ‘would be in all’ → “were in them all”: a hypothetical “were” replaces the conditional, “them” is supplied, and the predicate is delayed until after the participial phrase.
- *più e men distributa, ed altrettanto* ‘more and less distributed, and equally so’ → “more or less or equally distributed”: the conjunctions become alternatives, and the last adverb is brought before the participle so that all three modifiers share it.

> 70 Virtù diverse esser convengon frutti  
> 71 di principi formali, e quei, fuor ch' uno,  
> 72 seguiterieno a tua ragion distrutti.  

Diverse virtues must needs be fruits of formal principles, the which, save only one, would have no leave to be, upon thy reasoning.

- *Virtù diverse esser convengon frutti* ‘virtues diverse to be must befit as fruits’ → “Diverse virtues must needs be fruits”: the adjective precedes the noun, and the necessity expressed by *convengon* comes before, rather than after, the infinitive it governs.
- *principi formali* ‘principles formal’ → “formal principles”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *e quei* ‘and those’ → “the which”: a relative continues the preceding clause in place of a conjunction and demonstrative.
- *fuor ch' uno* ‘except one’ → “save only one”: “only” reinforces the exception.
- *seguiterieno … distrutti* ‘would follow … destroyed’ → “would have no leave to be”: the construction expressing destruction as a logical consequence becomes a negative idiom of permission to exist.
- *a tua ragion* ‘according to thy reasoning’ → “upon thy reasoning”: the preposition changes, and the qualification moves to the end.

> 73 Ancor, se raro fosse di quel bruno  
> 74 cagion che tu domandi, od oltre in parte  

Again, were rarity cause of that duskiness whereof thou makest question, either in some certain part, right through,

- *se raro fosse … cagion* ‘if the rare were … cause’ → “were rarity cause”: inversion replaces the conditional conjunction, the substantivized adjective becomes an abstract noun, and “cause” is brought next to “rarity”.
- *di quel bruno* ‘of that dark’ → “of that duskiness”: the substantivized adjective becomes an abstract noun and follows “cause” rather than preceding it.
- *che tu domandi* ‘that thou askest about’ → “whereof thou makest question”: the relation to the antecedent is expressed in “whereof”, and a verb becomes a verb–noun phrase.
- *od oltre in parte* ‘either through in part’ → “either in some certain part, right through”: the two qualifications exchange places; “some certain” specifies ‘part’, and “right” emphasizes ‘through’.

> 75 fora di sua materia sì digiuno  
> 76 esto pianeta, o, sì come comparte  
> 77 lo grasso e il magro un corpo, così questo  
> 78 nel suo volume cangerebbe carte.  

thus stinted of its matter were this planet; or, like as a body doth dispose the fat and lean, would it alternate pages in its volume.

- *fora di sua materia sì digiuno esto pianeta* ‘would be of its matter so starved this planet’ → “thus stinted of its matter were this planet”: hypothetical “were” replaces the conditional and follows the whole predicate phrase; “thus stinted” replaces the image of being ‘so starved’ and precedes its complement.
- *sì come comparte lo grasso e il magro un corpo* ‘just as apportions the fat and the lean a body’ → “like as a body doth dispose the fat and lean”: the subject moves before the verb, “doth dispose” renders the simple verb, and one article serves for both ‘fat’ and ‘lean’.
- *così questo* ‘so this one’ → “it”: the answering ‘so’ of the comparison is omitted, and a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *nel suo volume cangerebbe carte* ‘in its volume would change leaves’ → “would it alternate pages in its volume”: the local phrase moves to the end, “alternate” specifies the kind of change, and “pages” renders the leaves of a book.

> 79 Se il primo fosse, fora manifesto  
> 80 nell' eclissi del sol, per trasparere  
> 81 lo lume, come in altro raro ingesto.  

Were the first true, ’twould be revealed in the eclipses of the sun, by the light shining through it, as it doth when hurled on aught else rare.

- *Se il primo fosse* ‘if the first were’ → “Were the first true”: inversion replaces ‘if’, and “true” completes the predicate.
- *fora manifesto* ‘it would be manifest’ → “’twould be revealed”: a passive of disclosure replaces the predicate adjective.
- *per trasparere lo lume* ‘through shining through the light’ → “by the light shining through it”: English puts “the light”, the subject of the Italian infinitive, before the participle and supplies “it” for what the light traverses.
- *come in altro raro ingesto* ‘as into another rare thing cast’ → “as it doth when hurled on aught else rare”: “it doth” supplies the verb of comparison, “when” introduces the participle, and “hurled on” precedes its object in place of the final ‘cast’ with ‘into’.

> 82 Questo non è; però è da vedere  
> 83 dell' altro, e s' egli avvien ch' io l' altro cassi,  
> 84 falsificato fia lo tuo parere.  

This is not; wherefore we have to see what of the other case, and if it chance that I make vain this also, thy thought will be refuted.

- *è da vedere dell' altro* ‘it is to be seen concerning the other’ → “we have to see what of the other case”: the impersonal construction becomes personal and active; “what of” frames an indirect question, and “case” supplies a noun for ‘the other’.
- *s' egli avvien* ‘if it happens’ → “if it chance”: “chance” expresses the contingency, with an English subjunctive in place of the Italian indicative.
- *ch' io l' altro cassi* ‘that I the other cancel’ → “that I make vain this also”: the simple verb becomes a verb and adjective; the object moves after them, and “this also” replaces ‘the other’.
- *falsificato fia lo tuo parere* ‘falsified will be thy opinion’ → “thy thought will be refuted”: the subject moves to the front and the participle follows the auxiliary; “thought” renders ‘opinion’, and “refuted” renders ‘shown false’.

## Lines 85–117 — The Three Mirrors and the Heavens

> 85 S' egli è che questo raro non trapassi,  
> 86 esser conviene un termine, da onde  
> 87 lo suo contrario più passar non lassi;  

If it be that this rare matter goeth not throughout, needs must there be a limit, from which its contrary doth intercept its passing on;

- *questo raro* ‘this rare’ → “this rare matter”: a noun is supplied for the adjective used substantivally.
- *non trapassi* ‘pass not through’ → “goeth not throughout”: the negative follows the verb, and the Italian subjunctive becomes an English indicative.
- *esser conviene un termine* ‘be it is necessary a limit’ → “needs must there be a limit”: the impersonal construction becomes a modal one; the infinitive moves after the expression of necessity, and existential “there” is supplied.
- *più passar non lassi* ‘further to pass doth not let’ → “doth intercept its passing on”: a negative verb of permission becomes an affirmative verb of prevention; the infinitive becomes a gerund with a possessive, and ‘further’ is expressed by “on”.

> 88 ed indi l' altrui raggio si rifonde  
> 89 così, come color torna per vetro,  
> 90 lo qual di retro a sè piombo nasconde.  

and thence that other’s ray were so cast back, as colour doth return from glass which hideth lead behind it.

- *l' altrui raggio* ‘the ray of another’ → “that other’s ray”: the possessor is made demonstrative and placed before the noun.
- *si rifonde così* ‘is reflected thus’ → “were so cast back”: the present becomes a hypothetical “were”, and the adverb moves before the participle.
- *per vetro* ‘through glass’ → “from glass”: the preposition marks the point of return rather than the medium.
- *di retro a sè piombo nasconde* ‘behind itself lead hides’ → “hideth lead behind it”: the verb moves before its object, the phrase of place moves to the end, and the reflexive becomes a personal pronoun.

> 91 Or dirai tu ch' ei si dimostra tetro  
> 92 quivi lo raggio più che in altre parti,  
> 93 per esser lì rifratto più a retro.  

Now thou wilt urge that the ray here is darkened rather than in other parts, because here it is recast from further back.

- *dirai tu* ‘wilt say thou’ → “thou wilt urge”: the subject precedes the verb, and saying is made specifically the pressing of an objection.
- *ei si dimostra tetro quivi lo raggio* ‘it shows itself dark there, the ray’ → “the ray here is darkened”: the noun moves forward and replaces the anticipatory pronoun; an appearance of darkness becomes a passive verb, and ‘there’ becomes “here” before the verb.
- *più che* ‘more than’ → “rather than”: a comparison of degree is phrased as a contrast.
- *per esser lì rifratto* ‘for being there bent back’ → “because here it is recast”: the preposition and infinitive become a finite causal clause; ‘there’ becomes “here” and moves forward.
- *più a retro* ‘further back’ → “from further back”: “from” makes the point of return explicit.

> 94 Da questa instanzia può diliberarti  
> 95 esperienza, se giammai la provi,  
> 96 ch' esser suol fonte ai rivi di vostr' arti.  

From this plea experiment may disentangle thee, (if thou wilt make the proof) which ever is the spring of the rivers of your arts.

- *instanzia* ‘objection’ → “plea”: the technical term for an objection is rendered as a case urged.
- *può diliberarti esperienza* ‘can disentangle thee experiment’ → “experiment may disentangle thee”: the subject moves from after the infinitive to before the modal.
- *se giammai la provi* ‘if ever thou try it’ → “(if thou wilt make the proof)”: “wilt” is supplied, the verb becomes a verb–noun phrase, and the object pronoun referring to the experiment disappears.
- *esser suol* ‘is wont to be’ → “ever is”: customary action is expressed by an adverb rather than a governing verb and infinitive. “Ever” thus qualifies this clause rather than the preceding condition.
- *fonte ai rivi* ‘fount to the streams’ → “the spring of the rivers”: the article is supplied, ‘to’ becomes “of”, and the streams become rivers.

> 97 Tre specchi prenderai; e due rimovi  
> 98 da te d' un modo, e l' altro più rimosso  
> 99 tr' ambo li primi gli occhi tuoi ritrovi.  

Three mirrors thou shalt take, and set two equally remote from thee; and let the third further removed strike on thine eyes between the other two.

- *e due rimovi da te d' un modo* ‘and two remove from thee in one manner’ → “and set two equally remote from thee”: the object follows the verb; movement away becomes placement at a distance, and sameness of manner is specified as equality of distance.
- *e l' altro più rimosso tr' ambo li primi gli occhi tuoi ritrovi* ‘and let the other, more removed, between both the first ones find thine eyes’ → “and let the third further removed strike on thine eyes between the other two”: “let” renders the subjunctive; ‘the other’ is identified by ordinal position, ‘find’ becomes “strike on”, and the possessive precedes ‘eyes’. The phrase locating the mirror moves to the end, with the first mirrors now called “the other two”.

> 100 Rivolto ad essi fa che dopo il dosso  
> 101 ti stea un lume che i tre specchi accenda,  
> 102 e torni a te da tutti ripercosso.  

Turning to them, have a light set behind thy back, enkindling the three mirrors, and, back-smitten by them all, coming again to thee.

- *Rivolto ad essi* ‘turned toward them’ → “Turning to them”: a past participle denoting position becomes a present participle denoting the act.
- *fa che dopo il dosso ti stea un lume* ‘see that behind the back there stand for thee a light’ → “have a light set behind thy back”: the imperative and finite subjunctive become a causative with object and participle; the light moves forward, standing becomes being set, and the personal reference is expressed by “thy”.
- *che i tre specchi accenda* ‘which the three mirrors may enkindle’ → “enkindling the three mirrors”: the relative clause becomes a participial phrase with the object after the verb.
- *e torni a te da tutti ripercosso* ‘and may return to thee by all struck back’ → “and, back-smitten by them all, coming again to thee”: the finite verb becomes a participial expression; the phrase for the reflected light moves before it, with the participle before its agent and “them” supplied.

> 103 Benchè, nel quanto, tanto non si stenda  
> 104 la vista più lontana, lì vedrai  
> 105 come convien ch' egualmente risplenda.  

Whereas in size the more distant shew shall not have so great stretch, yet thou there shalt see it needs must shine as brightly as the others.

- *Benchè* ‘although’ → “Whereas”: concession is introduced as a contrast.
- *nel quanto* ‘in respect of quantity’ → “in size”: the substantival ‘how much’ is specified as extent.
- *tanto non si stenda la vista più lontana* ‘so far may not extend the appearance more distant’ → “the more distant shew shall not have so great stretch”: the subject moves before the verb and the adjective before the noun; the reflexive subjunctive becomes a future construction with “stretch” as a noun.
- *lì vedrai* ‘there thou shalt see’ → “yet thou there shalt see”: “yet” is supplied to resume the concession, and the adverb follows the subject.
- *come convien ch' egualmente risplenda* ‘how it is necessary that it shine equally’ → “it needs must shine as brightly as the others”: the introductory ‘how’ drops out, impersonal necessity and its subordinate subjunctive become a modal construction, and equality is expanded into an explicit comparison of brightness with the other images.

> 106 Or, come ai colpi delli caldi rai  
> 107 della neve riman nudo il suggetto  
> 108 e dal colore e dal freddo primai;  

Now,—as at the stroke of the warm rays the substrate of the snow is stripped both of the colour and the coldness which it had,—

- *ai colpi* ‘at the strokes’ → “at the stroke”: the plural becomes singular.
- *caldi rai* ‘hot rays’ → “warm rays”: the adjective is weaker in English.
- *della neve riman nudo il suggetto* ‘of the snow remains bare the substrate’ → “the substrate of the snow is stripped”: the subject and its dependent phrase are brought together before the verb, in reverse order; remaining bare becomes being stripped.
- *e dal colore e dal freddo primai* ‘both of the colour and of the cold that were there before’ → “both of the colour and the coldness which it had”: the preposition is not repeated, and the plural adjective of prior state becomes a relative clause of past possession.

> 109 così rimaso te nello intelletto  
> 110 voglio informar di luce sì vivace,  
> 111 che ti tremolerà nel suo aspetto.  

thee, so left stripped in thine intellect, would I inform with light so living, it shall tremble as thou lookest on it.

- *così rimaso te nello intelletto* ‘thus left thee in the intellect’ → “thee, so left stripped in thine intellect”: the object moves before the participial phrase, “stripped” is carried over from the comparison, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *voglio informar di luce sì vivace* ‘I wish to inform with light so vivid’ → “would I inform with light so living”: volition is expressed by inverted “would I”; “living” gives the adjective the force of life rather than brilliance.
- *che ti tremolerà nel suo aspetto* ‘that it will quiver to thee at the sight of it’ → “it shall tremble as thou lookest on it”: the result conjunction drops out; the person affected becomes the subject of looking, and the noun of sight becomes a temporal clause.

> 112 Dentro dal ciel della divina pace  
> 113 si gira un corpo, nella cui virtute  
> 114 l' esser di tutto suo contento giace.  

Within the heaven of the divine peace whirleth a body, in whose virtue lieth the being of all that it containeth.

- *si gira* ‘turns itself’ → “whirleth”: the reflexive becomes an intransitive verb of rotation.
- *l' esser di tutto suo contento giace* ‘the being of all its content lies’ → “lieth the being of all that it containeth”: the verb moves before the subject, and the possessive with a noun becomes a relative clause with an active verb.

> 115 Lo ciel seguente, ch' ha tante vedute,  
> 116 quell' esser parte per diverse essenze  
> 117 da lui distinte e da lui contenute;  

The heaven next following, which hath so many things to show, parteth this being amid diverse essences, which it distinguisheth and doth contain;

- *seguente* ‘following’ → “next following”: “next” makes immediate succession explicit.
- *tante vedute* ‘so many sights’ → “so many things to show”: the noun becomes a general noun with an infinitive, presenting the heaven as showing what is seen.
- *quell' esser parte* ‘that being it divides’ → “parteth this being”: the object moves after the verb and the demonstrative changes from ‘that’ to “this”.
- *per* ‘among’ → “amid”: the distributive preposition is rendered by one of position.
- *da lui distinte e da lui contenute* ‘by it distinguished and by it contained’ → “which it distinguisheth and doth contain”: the two passive participles become active verbs in a relative clause; the twice-stated agent becomes their single subject.

## Lines 118–148 — Why the Heavenly Lights Differ

> 118 gli altri giron per varie differenze  
> 119 le distinzion, che dentro da sè hanno,  
> 120 dispongono a lor fini e lor semenze.  

the other circling bodies by various differentiatings, dispose the distinct powers they have within themselves, unto their ends and to their fertilisings.

- *gli altri giron* ‘the other circles’ → “the other circling bodies”: the noun becomes a participle qualifying the supplied “bodies”.
- *per varie differenze* ‘by various differences’ → “by various differentiatings”: the noun denoting differences becomes a verbal noun denoting differentiation.
- *le distinzion* ‘the distinctions’ → “the distinct powers”: the noun becomes an adjective qualifying the supplied “powers”; the object is moved after the verb.
- *che dentro da sè hanno* ‘that within themselves they have’ → “they have within themselves”: the object relative is unexpressed, and the phrase of place follows the verb.
- *dispongono* ‘they dispose’ → “dispose”: the verb is brought forward from after the object and its relative clause.
- *e lor semenze* ‘and their seeds’ → “and to their fertilisings”: the preposition governing the first noun is repeated before the second, and the seeds are rendered in terms of a process.

> 121 Questi organi del mondo così vanno,  
> 122 come tu vedi omai, di grado in grado,  
> 123 chè di su prendono, e di sotto fanno.  

These organs of the universe go, as thou seest now, from grade to grade; for from above do they receive, and downward do they work.

- *del mondo* ‘of the world’ → “of the universe”: the broader English noun is chosen.
- *così vanno* ‘thus go’ → “go”: the anticipatory adverb is left out.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *di su prendono* ‘from above they take’ → “from above do they receive”: ‘take’ becomes “receive”, with auxiliary “do” before the subject.
- *di sotto fanno* ‘below they act’ → “downward do they work”: place becomes direction, and the verb is rendered by “work” with the same auxiliary inversion.

> 124 Riguarda bene omai sì com' io vado  
> 125 per questo loco al ver che tu disiri,  
> 126 sì che poi sappi sol tener lo guado.  

Now mark well how I thread this pass to the truth for which thou longest, that thou thereafter mayest know to keep the ford alone.

- *Riguarda bene omai* ‘look well now’ → “Now mark well”: the temporal adverb comes first, and looking is expressed as marking.
- *sì com' io vado per questo loco* ‘just how I go through this place’ → “how I thread this pass”: the reinforcing *sì* is left out; verb and preposition become a transitive verb, and the general ‘place’ becomes “pass”.
- *che tu disiri* ‘which thou desirest’ → “for which thou longest”: the transitive verb becomes a verb with a prepositional complement.
- *sì che poi sappi* ‘so that afterwards thou mayest know’ → “that thou thereafter mayest know”: ‘so’ is not separately expressed, the subject precedes the adverb, and “mayest” conveys the subjunctive.
- *sol tener lo guado* ‘alone to keep the ford’ → “to keep the ford alone”: “alone” is moved after the object.

> 127 Lo moto e la virtù dei santi giri,  
> 128 come dal fabbro l' arte del martello,  
> 129 dai beati motor convien che spiri;  

The movement and the virtue of the sacred wheelings, as the hammer’s art from the smith, must needs be an effluence from the blessed movers;

- *dei santi giri* ‘of the holy circles’ → “of the sacred wheelings”: the circles are rendered in terms of their turning.
- *dal fabbro l' arte del martello* ‘from the smith the art of the hammer’ → “the hammer’s art from the smith”: the source phrase comes last, and the possessor comes before “art”.
- *dai beati motor convien che spiri* ‘from the blessed movers it is necessary that it breathe forth’ → “must needs be an effluence from the blessed movers”: the impersonal necessity construction becomes a modal, the verb of breathing forth becomes a copula and noun, and the source phrase comes last.

> 130 e il ciel, cui tanti lumi fanno bello,  
> 131 dalla mente profonda che lui volve  
> 132 prende l' image, e fassene suggello.  

and the heaven which so many lights make beautiful, from the deep mind which rolleth it, taketh the image and thereof maketh the seal.

- *tanti lumi fanno bello* ‘so many lights make fair’ → “so many lights make beautiful”: “beautiful” is the choice for the predicative adjective.
- *che lui volve* ‘which it turns’ → “which rolleth it”: the object pronoun follows the verb, and turning is rendered as rolling.
- *prende l' image* ‘takes the image’ → “taketh the image”: the verb precedes its object as in Italian, but follows the relative clause without the Italian line break.
- *e fassene suggello* ‘and makes itself a seal of it’ → “and thereof maketh the seal”: “thereof” precedes the verb, the reflexive is not expressed, and the bare predicate noun takes the definite article.

> 133 E come l' alma dentro a vostra polve  
> 134 per differenti membra e conformate  
> 135 a diverse potenze si risolve;  

And as the soul within your dust, through members differing and conformed to divers powers, doth diffuse itself,

- *per differenti membra* ‘through differing members’ → “through members differing”: the modifier follows the noun.
- *si risolve* ‘resolves itself’ → “doth diffuse itself”: the verb is interpreted as one of spreading, with auxiliary “doth” and the reflexive after the verb.

> 136 così l' intelligenza sua bontate  
> 137 multiplicata per le stelle spiega,  
> 138 girando sè sopra sua unitate.  

so doth the Intelligence deploy its goodness, multiplied through the stars, revolving still on its own unity.

- *l' intelligenza sua bontate multiplicata per le stelle spiega* ‘the Intelligence its goodness multiplied through the stars unfolds’ → “doth the Intelligence deploy its goodness, multiplied through the stars”: the final verb becomes “deploy” before its object, with “doth” before the subject.
- *girando sè* ‘turning itself’ → “revolving still”: an intransitive verb replaces the reflexive construction, and “still” is supplied.
- *sopra sua unitate* ‘on its unity’ → “on its own unity”: “own” reinforces the possessive.

> 139 Virtù diversa fa diversa lega  
> 140 col prezioso corpo ch' ell' avviva,  
> 141 nel qual, sì come vita in voi, si lega.  

Diverse virtue maketh diverse alloy with the precious body which it quickeneth, wherein, as life in you, it is upbound.

- *Virtù diversa* ‘virtue diverse’ → “Diverse virtue”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *ch' ell' avviva* ‘which it enlivens’ → “which it quickeneth”: “quickeneth” is used in the sense of giving life.
- *nel qual* ‘in which’ → “wherein”: the preposition and relative pronoun become a relative adverb.
- *sì come vita in voi* ‘just as life in you’ → “as life in you”: the reinforcement of the comparison is not separately expressed.
- *si lega* ‘binds itself’ → “it is upbound”: the reflexive is rendered as a passive state.

> 142 Per la natura lieta onde deriva  
> 143 la virtù mista per lo corpo luce,  
> 144 come letizia per pupilla viva.  

By cause of the glad nature whence it floweth, the mingled virtue shineth through the body, as gladness doth through living pupil.

- *Per la natura lieta* ‘because of the nature glad’ → “By cause of the glad nature”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *onde deriva* ‘whence it derives’ → “whence it floweth”: derivation is rendered as flowing.
- *la virtù mista* ‘the virtue mingled’ → “the mingled virtue”: the participle precedes the noun.
- *per lo corpo luce* ‘through the body shines’ → “shineth through the body”: the verb precedes the prepositional phrase.
- *come letizia per pupilla viva* ‘as gladness through pupil living’ → “as gladness doth through living pupil”: “doth” stands for the verb left understood in Italian, and the adjective precedes the noun.

> 145 Da essa vien ciò che da luce a luce  
> 146 par differente, non da denso e raro:  

Thence cometh what seems different ’twixt light and light, and not from dense and rare;

- *Da essa* ‘from it’ → “Thence”: a source adverb replaces the preposition and pronoun.
- *ciò che* ‘that which’ → “what”: demonstrative and relative become a single fused relative.
- *da luce a luce par differente* ‘from light to light seems different’ → “seems different ’twixt light and light”: the verb and adjective come first, and a relation expressed by ‘from … to’ becomes one expressed by ‘’twixt … and’.
- *non da denso e raro* ‘not from dense and rare’ → “and not from dense and rare”: the coordinating conjunction is supplied.

> 147 essa è formal principio che produce,  
> 148 conforme a sua bontà, lo turbo e il chiaro.”  

this is the formal principle that produceth, conformably to its own excellence, the turbid and the clear.”

- *essa è formal principio* ‘it is formal principle’ → “this is the formal principle”: the pronoun becomes demonstrative, and the predicate noun takes a definite article.
- *conforme a sua bontà* ‘in conformity with its goodness’ → “conformably to its own excellence”: the expression of conformity is rendered adverbially, “own” reinforces the possessive, and “excellence” is chosen for ‘goodness’.

## Summary — Close in Image, Freer in Construction

Wicksteed often changes the order of a phrase while keeping its terms. In *quell’ esser parte*, the object precedes the verb; in “parteth this being” it follows, with “this” for ‘that’. Adjectives often move before their nouns, as in *la spera ottava*, “The eighth sphere”. Yet Wicksteed does not consistently prefer English prose order: “Three mirrors thou shalt take” keeps the object first, and “whirleth a body” keeps the subject after the verb. Two routine differences generally pass unmarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending identifies the subject, and leaves out articles before possessives, as in *li vostri liti*, “your own shores”.

Changes of construction often shift the point from which an action is expressed. The impersonal *vivesi qui*, ‘one lives here’, becomes “life is here sustained”; *da lui distinte e da lui contenute*, by contrast, passes from two passive participles to active verbs sharing a subject, “which it distinguisheth and doth contain”. A phrase can also become a clause: *retro ai sensi*, ‘behind the senses’, becomes “even when the senses give the lead”. Here “even” makes a contrast explicit, as “too” makes insufficiency explicit in “wings too short”. Elsewhere Wicksteed leaves a detail unexpressed: *mi disse* becomes “she said”, without the addressee, and *così vanno* becomes “go”, without ‘thus’.

The choice of words ranges from close correspondence to the replacement of one figure by another. For *legno*, ‘timber’, Wicksteed gives “keel”, still naming the vessel indirectly; for *alto sale*, ‘high salt’, he gives “the deep”. Yet *mio solco* remains “my furrow”, *l’ eterna margarita* remains “the eternal pearl”, and the repeated *diversa* in *Virtù diversa fa diversa lega* finds its counterpart in “Diverse virtue maketh diverse alloy”. Such repetitions and images often survive even where the grammar around them is recast.
