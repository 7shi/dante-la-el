# Paradiso 13

## Lines 1–12 — Imagine the Stars, the Wain, and the Horn

> 1 Imagini chi bene intender cupe  
> 2 quel ch' io or vidi (e ritenga l' image,  
> 3 mentre ch' io dico, come ferma rupe)  

Let him imagine, who would grasp rightly what I now beheld (and let him hold the image while I speak, like a firm rock),

- *Imagini chi bene intender cupe* ‘let whoever rightly to understand desires imagine’ → “Let him imagine, who would grasp rightly”: the subject of the initial subjunctive is anticipated by “him” and then expressed by the relative clause. The final verb of desire becomes “would” before “grasp”; the adverb moves from before the infinitive to after it.
- *quel ch’* ‘that which’ → “what”: the demonstrative and relative are combined.
- *ritenga* ‘let him retain’ → “let him hold”: the verb of retention is rendered by a simpler verb.
- *mentre ch’ io dico* ‘while I say’ → “while I speak”: the conjunction is expressed by one word, and the verb of saying is rendered intransitively.
- *come ferma rupe* ‘like firm rock’ → “like a firm rock”: an indefinite article is supplied.

> 4 quindici stelle che in diverse plage  
> 5 lo cielo avvivan di tanto sereno,  
> 6 che soperchia dell' aere ogni compage;  

fifteen of those stars that, in sundry regions, quicken the heaven with such brightness as to pierce all the knitted air,

- *quindici stelle* ‘fifteen stars’ → “fifteen of those stars”: the demonstrative and partitive construction are supplied.
- *lo cielo avvivan* ‘the heaven they quicken’ → “quicken the heaven”: the object moves after the verb.
- *di tanto sereno* ‘with so much clearness’ → “with such brightness”: a construction of quantity becomes one of degree, and the quality is expressed as brightness.
- *che soperchia* ‘that it surpasses’ → “as to pierce”: the finite result clause becomes an infinitive, and surpassing is interpreted as piercing through.
- *dell’ aere ogni compage* ‘of the air every close-knit structure’ → “all the knitted air”: the dependent noun becomes the head noun; the idea of structure is carried by the modifier “knitted”, and the distributive ‘every’ becomes “all” with a mass noun.

> 7 imagini quel Carro, a cui il seno  
> 8 basta del nostro cielo e notte e giorno,  
> 9 sì ch' al volger del temo non vien meno;  

let him imagine that wain for which the bosom of our heaven sufficeth night and day, so that it faileth not to the wain-pole’s sweep,

- *a cui* ‘to which’ → “for which”: the relation to the verb of sufficiency is expressed with “for”.
- *basta del nostro cielo* ‘suffices of our heaven’ → “of our heaven sufficeth”: the phrase qualifying ‘bosom’ moves before the verb, bringing it next to its noun.
- *e notte e giorno* ‘both night and day’ → “night and day”: the first member of the paired conjunction is dropped.
- *al volger del temo* ‘at the turning of the pole’ → “to the wain-pole’s sweep”: the phrase moves from before the verb to after it; “to” replaces ‘at’, the verbal noun becomes “sweep”, and the dependent noun becomes a preceding possessive, with “wain” supplied.
- *non vien meno* ‘does not come to less’ → “it faileth not”: the idiom is rendered by a single verb, with the negative following it.

> 10 imagini la bocca di quel corno,  
> 11 che si comincia in punta dello stelo  
> 12 a cui la prima rota va dintorno,  

let him imagine the mouth of that horn which starteth from the axle round which the primal circling goeth,

- *si comincia in punta dello stelo* ‘has its beginning at the tip of the shaft’ → “starteth from the axle”: the pronominal verb becomes a simple intransitive; location becomes a point of departure, the mention of the tip drops out, and the shaft is interpreted as an axle.
- *a cui … va dintorno* ‘to which … goes round’ → “round which” … “goeth”: the final adverb becomes a preposition before the relative pronoun, replacing the Italian preposition.
- *la prima rota* ‘the first wheel’ → “the primal circling”: the ordinal becomes “primal”, and the name of the revolving thing becomes a noun of motion.

## Lines 13–45 — The Double Dance and the Measure of Human Light

> 13 aver fatto di sè due segni in cielo,  

all to have made of themselves two signs in heaven,

- *aver fatto* ‘to have made’ → “all to have made”: “all” gathers up the subjects of the infinitive from the preceding lines.

> 14 qual fece la figliuola di Minoi  
> 15 allora che sentì di morte il gelo;  

such as Minos’ daughter made when she felt the chill of death;

- *qual fece la figliuola di Minoi* ‘such as made the daughter of Minos’ → “such as Minos’ daughter made”: the subject moves before the verb, and the phrase with *di* becomes a possessive before the noun.
- *allora che* ‘then when’ → “when”: the two words are reduced to one.
- *di morte il gelo* ‘of death the frost’ → “the chill of death”: the phrase of possession follows its noun, and the less intense “chill” renders *gelo*.

> 16 e l' un nell' altro aver li raggi suoi,  

and one to have its rays within the other,

- *l' un nell' altro aver li raggi suoi* ‘the one within the other to have the rays its own’ → “one to have its rays within the other”: the first article drops out, the possessive precedes its noun, and the phrase of place moves to the end.

> 17 ed ambedue girarsi per maniera,  
> 18 che l' uno andasse al prima e l' altro al poi:  

and both the two to turn them in such fashion that one should take the lead, and the other follow;

- *ambedue* ‘both’ → “both the two”: the number is made explicit a second time.
- *girarsi* ‘to turn themselves’ → “to turn them”: the simple object pronoun is used reflexively.
- *per maniera* ‘in a manner’ → “in such fashion”: “such” points ahead to the result clause.
- *l' uno andasse al prima e l' altro al poi* ‘the one should go to the before and the other to the after’ → “one should take the lead, and the other follow”: the first article drops out; the two adverbs used as nouns are recast as actions, with a separate verb for the second instead of an understood repetition of *andasse*.

> 19 ed avrà quasi l' ombra della vera  
> 20 costellazion e della doppia danza,  
> 21 che circulava il punto dov' io era;  

and he shall have as though the shade of the real constellation and the twofold dance which circled round the point whereat I was;

- *quasi l' ombra* ‘almost the shadow’ → “as though the shade”: the adverb of approximation becomes a phrase of comparison.
- *della vera costellazion e della doppia danza* ‘of the true constellation and of the double dance’ → “of the real constellation and the twofold dance”: “real” and “twofold” render the adjectives, and the first “of” serves for both nouns.
- *circulava il punto* ‘was circling the point’ → “circled round the point”: the imperfect is rendered by a simple past, and “round” makes the relation to the point explicit.
- *dov' * ‘where’ → “whereat”: the English relative includes a preposition of place.

> 22 poich' è tanto di là da nostra usanza,  
> 23 quanto di là dal mover della Chiana  
> 24 si move il ciel che tutti gli altri avanza.  

for it as far transcendeth our use as doth transcend the movement of Chiana the motion of that heaven which all the rest surpasseth.

- *è tanto di là da nostra usanza* ‘is so far beyond our custom’ → “it as far transcendeth our use”: being beyond becomes a transitive verb; “as far” sets up the comparison, and “use” has the sense of customary experience.
- *quanto di là dal mover della Chiana si move il ciel* ‘as far beyond the moving of the Chiana moves the heaven’ → “as doth transcend the movement of Chiana the motion of that heaven”: “transcend” repeats the verb of the first comparison; the Italian subject and verb become the noun phrase “the motion of that heaven”, with a demonstrative for the article. The article before the river name drops out.
- *tutti gli altri* ‘all the others’ → “all the rest”: a collective singular replaces the plural.

> 25 Lì si cantò non Bacco, non Peana,  

There did they sing, not Bacchus, and not Pæan,

- *si cantò* ‘there was singing’ → “did they sing”: the impersonal preterite becomes a personal plural, with inversion after “There” and the auxiliary “did”.
- *non Bacco, non Peana* ‘not Bacchus, not Pæan’ → “not Bacchus, and not Pæan”: the conjunction is supplied.

> 26 ma tre persone in divina natura,  
> 27 ed in una persona essa e l' umana.  

but three Persons in the divine nature, and it and the human nature in one Person.

- *in divina natura* ‘in divine nature’ → “in the divine nature”: the definite article is supplied.
- *in una persona essa e l' umana* ‘in one person it and the human’ → “it and the human nature in one Person”: the prepositional phrase moves to the end, and “nature”, understood after the Italian adjective, is repeated.

> 28 Compiè il cantare e il volger sua misura,  

The song and wheeling had fulfilled their measure,

- *Compiè il cantare e il volger sua misura* ‘completed the singing and the turning its measure’ → “The song and wheeling had fulfilled their measure”: the coordinated subject moves before the verb; the preterite becomes a past perfect. The nominal infinitives become “song” and “wheeling”, with only one article, and the singular possessive becomes plural to accord with the paired agents.

> 29 ed attesersi a noi quei santi lumi,  
> 30 felicitando sè di cura in cura.  

and to us turned their heed those sacred torches, rejoicing as they passed from charge to charge.

- *attesersi a noi* ‘addressed themselves to us’ → “to us turned their heed”: the prepositional phrase is put first, and an explicit object, “their heed”, conveys the force of the reflexive.
- *santi lumi* ‘holy lights’ → “sacred torches”: “sacred” renders the adjective, and “torches” makes the lights more concrete.
- *felicitando sè* ‘making themselves happy’ → “rejoicing”: an intransitive verb replaces the verb and its reflexive object.
- *di cura in cura* ‘from care to care’ → “as they passed from charge to charge”: “charge” takes *cura* in the sense of a duty, and a temporal clause supplies the act of passing.

> 31 Ruppe il silenzio nei concordi numi  
> 32 poscia la luce, in che mirabil vita  
> 33 del poverel di Dio narrata fumi,  

Then ’mid the harmonious divinities silence was broken by the light wherein the wondrous life of the poor man of God had been rehearsed to me,

- *Ruppe il silenzio … la luce* ‘broke the silence … the light’ → “silence was broken by the light”: the postponed Italian subject becomes the agent of a passive, and “silence”, without its article, becomes the subject.
- *nei concordi numi poscia* ‘among the concordant divinities then’ → “Then ’mid the harmonious divinities”: the adverb moves ahead of the phrase of place, both now precede “silence”, and “harmonious” renders *concordi*.
- *in che* ‘in which’ → “wherein”: preposition and relative are combined.
- *mirabil vita* ‘wondrous life’ → “the wondrous life”: the article is supplied.
- *poverel* ‘little poor man’ → “poor man”: the diminutive is not separately expressed.
- *narrata fumi* ‘recounted was to me’ → “had been rehearsed to me”: the passive preterite becomes a past perfect; the participle follows the auxiliaries, and the pronoun attached to *fu* becomes a separate prepositional phrase.

> 34 e disse: “Quando l' una paglia è trita,  
> 35 quando la sua semenza è già riposta,  

which said: “Since the one sheaf is thrashed, and its seed stored already,

- *e disse* ‘and said’ → “which said”: a relative clause replaces the coordinated verb.
- *Quando* ‘when’ → “Since”: the temporal relation is read causally.
- *paglia* ‘straw’ → “sheaf”: the material is rendered as a gathered unit.
- *quando* ‘when’ → “and”: coordination replaces the repeated subordinator.
- *è già riposta* ‘is already stored’ → “stored already”: the auxiliary is left understood, and the adverb follows the participle.

> 36 a batter l' altra dolce amor m' invita.  

to beat out the other sweet love inviteth me.

- *batter* ‘to beat’ → “to beat out”: the particle specifies the result of the beating.
- *m' invita* ‘me invites’ → “inviteth me”: the object pronoun follows the verb.

> 37 Tu credi che nel petto, onde la costa  
> 38 si trasse per formar la bella guancia,  
> 39 il cui palato a tutto il mondo costa,  

Thou holdest that into the breast wherefrom the rib was drawn to form the beauteous cheek for whose palate all the world doth pay,

- *credi* ‘believest’ → “holdest”: belief is phrased as holding a view.
- *nel petto* ‘in the breast’ → “into the breast”: the preposition marks the direction of infusion rather than location.
- *la costa si trasse* ‘the rib one drew’ → “the rib was drawn”: an English passive renders the construction with *si*.
- *il cui palato a tutto il mondo costa* ‘whose palate costs all the world’ → “for whose palate all the world doth pay”: the party bearing the cost becomes the subject of “doth pay”, and the Italian subject becomes a phrase with “for”.

> 40 ed in quel che, forato dalla lancia,  
> 41 e poscia e prima tanto satisfece,  
> 42 che d' ogni colpa vince la bilancia,  

and into that which, thrust by the lance, made satisfaction both for past and future, such as to turn the scale against all trespass,

- *in quel* ‘in that’ → “into that”: the directional preposition continues the construction of line 37.
- *forato* ‘pierced’ → “thrust”: the rendering names the stroke rather than the resulting perforation.
- *e poscia e prima* ‘both afterwards and before’ → “both for past and future”: the order is reversed, and adverbs of time become the objects of “for”, indicating what the satisfaction covers.
- *tanto satisfece, che* ‘so greatly satisfied that’ → “made satisfaction” … “such as to”: the verb becomes a verb and noun, and the degree-and-result construction becomes an infinitival phrase qualifying the satisfaction.
- *d' ogni colpa vince la bilancia* ‘of every fault overcomes the balance’ → “turn the scale against all trespass”: *vince* and its object become the idiom “turn the scale”; the fronted phrase with *di* comes last as an “against” phrase, with collective “all trespass” for distributive *ogni colpa*.

> 43 quantunque alla natura umana lece  
> 44 aver di lume, tutto fosse infuso  
> 45 da quel valor che l' uno e l' altro fece.  

such light as human nature may receive was all infused by that same Worth which made the one and the other.

- *quantunque alla natura umana lece aver di lume* ‘as much as to human nature it is permitted to have of light’ → “such light as human nature may receive”: “light” is brought forward into the correlative phrase; the dative becomes the subject of a personal modal construction, the adjective precedes its noun, and possession becomes reception.
- *tutto fosse infuso* ‘all were infused’ → “was all infused”: the imperfect subjunctive dependent on the verb of belief becomes an indicative passive, with “all” after the auxiliary.
- *quel valor* ‘that worth’ → “that same Worth”: “same” adds an explicit mark of identity.
- *l' uno e l' altro fece* ‘the one and the other made’ → “made the one and the other”: the verb moves before its objects.

## Lines 46–78 — The Light and Its Imperfect Imprint

> 46 E però ammiri ciò ch' io dissi suso,  
> 47 quando narrai che non ebbe secondo  
> 48 lo ben che nella quinta luce è chiuso.  

And so thou wonderest at what I said above, when I declared the good enclosed in the fifth light ne’er to have had a second.

- *ammiri* ‘thou wonderest at’: the Italian verb takes a direct object; “wonderest at” takes a preposition.
- *narrai che non ebbe secondo* ‘I related that it had no second’ → “I declared” … “ne’er to have had a second”: the finite clause becomes an infinitive after a verb of declaration; the simple past becomes a perfect infinitive, ‘not’ becomes ‘never’, and ‘second’ gains an article.
- *lo ben che nella quinta luce è chiuso* ‘the good that in the fifth light is enclosed’ → “the good enclosed in the fifth light”: the postponed subject comes before the infinitive; the relative clause is reduced to a participle, with the phrase of place after it.

> 49 Ora apri gli occhi a quel ch' io ti rispondo,  

Now ope thine eyes to what I answer thee,

- *gli occhi* ‘the eyes’ → “thine eyes”: the definite article becomes a possessive.

> 50 e vedrai il tuo credere e il mio dire  
> 51 nel vero farsi come centro in tondo.  

and thou shalt see what thou believest and what I say, strike on the truth as centre in the circle.

- *il tuo credere e il mio dire* ‘thy believing and my saying’ → “what thou believest and what I say”: the two substantivized infinitives become clauses denoting the things believed and said.
- *nel vero farsi* ‘in the true to become’ → “strike on the truth”: the substantivized adjective becomes a noun; the reflexive infinitive becomes a verb of impact, with the phrase it governs moved after it.
- *come centro in tondo* ‘as centre in circle’ → “as centre in the circle”: the second noun gains a definite article.

> 52 Ciò che non more e ciò che può morire  
> 53 non è se non splendor di quella idea  
> 54 che partorisce, amando, il nostro sire;  

That which dieth not, and that which can die, is nought save the reglow of that Idea which our Sire, in Loving, doth beget;

- *non è se non* ‘is not except’ → “is nought save”: a negative pronoun takes the place of verbal negation.
- *splendor* ‘radiance’ → “the reglow”: an article is added, and the noun chosen suggests a light shining back.
- *che partorisce, amando, il nostro sire* ‘which begets, loving, our sire’ → “which our Sire, in Loving, doth beget”: the subject moves before the verb, the gerund gains a preposition and comes before the verb as well, and the simple present is rendered with an auxiliary.

> 55 chè quella viva luce che sì mea  
> 56 dal suo lucente, che non si disuna  
> 57 da lui, nè dall' amor che a lor s' intrea,  

for that living Light which so outgoeth from its Source that it departeth not therefrom, nor from the Love that maketh three with them,

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the causal subordinator becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *mea* ‘flows’ → “outgoeth”: motion outward replaces the image of flowing.
- *dal suo lucente* ‘from its shining one’ → “from its Source”: the substantivized participle is rendered by a noun of origin.
- *non si disuna da lui* ‘does not disunite itself from him’ → “it departeth not therefrom”: an intransitive verb replaces the reflexive; the personal pronoun and preposition become an adverb referring back to “its Source”.
- *che a lor s' intrea* ‘that with them makes itself three’ → “that maketh three with them”: the coined reflexive verb is resolved into a verb and a numeral, without a reflexive pronoun; the phrase referring to the other two comes last.

> 58 per sua bontate il suo raggiare aduna,  
> 59 quasi specchiato, in nove sussistenze,  
> 60 eternalmente rimanendosi una.  

doth, of its goodness, focus its own raying, as though reflected, in nine existences, eternally abiding one.

- *per sua bontate* ‘through its goodness’ → “of its goodness”: the preposition changes; the phrase now intervenes between auxiliary and verb.
- *il suo raggiare aduna* ‘its radiating it gathers’ → “doth” … “focus its own raying”: the verb is rendered by an optical term with an auxiliary, its object comes after it, and “own” makes the possessive emphatic.
- *quasi specchiato* ‘as if mirrored’ → “as though reflected”: the English gives the effect rather than naming the mirror.
- *sussistenze* ‘subsistences’ → “existences”: a more general noun replaces the technical one.
- *rimanendosi* ‘remaining’ → “abiding”: a non-pronominal verb renders the Italian pronominal form.

> 61 Quindi discende all' ultime potenze  
> 62 giù d' atto in atto tanto divenendo,  
> 63 che più non fa che brevi contingenze;  

Thence it descendeth to the remotest potencies, down, from act to act, becoming such as maketh now mere brief contingencies;

- *ultime* ‘last’ → “remotest”: position in a sequence is expressed as distance.
- *tanto divenendo, che* ‘so far becoming, that’ → “becoming such as”: the expression of degree followed by a result clause becomes one of condition or character followed by a relative clause.
- *più non fa che* ‘no longer makes anything but’ → “maketh now mere”: the negative restriction becomes affirmative, with “now” on the verb and “mere” on its object.

> 64 e queste contingenze essere intendo  
> 65 le cose generate, che produce  
> 66 con seme e senza seme il ciel movendo.  

by which contingencies I understand the generated things which are produced from seed, or seedless, by the moving heaven.

- *e queste contingenze essere intendo* ‘and these contingencies to be I understand’ → “by which contingencies I understand”: a relative connection replaces the conjunction and demonstrative; the infinitive disappears, and the things meant become the direct object of “understand”.
- *le cose generate* ‘the things generated’ → “the generated things”: the participle moves before the noun.
- *che produce* ‘which [it] produces’ → “which are produced”: the relative object becomes the subject of a plural passive.
- *con seme e senza seme* ‘with seed and without seed’ → “from seed, or seedless”: accompaniment becomes origin, ‘and’ becomes “or”, and an adjective replaces the second prepositional phrase.
- *il ciel movendo* ‘heaven in moving’ → “by the moving heaven”: the Italian subject becomes the agent of the passive; the gerund describing its action becomes an attributive participle.

> 67 La cera di costoro, e chi la duce,  
> 68 non sta d' un modo, e però sotto il segno  
> 69 ideale poi più e men traluce:  

The wax of these, and that which mouldeth it, standeth not in one mode, and therefore, ’neath the ideal stamp, is more and less transparent;

- *chi la duce* ‘who leads it’ → “that which mouldeth it”: the personal relative becomes impersonal, the verb is adapted to the wax, and the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *d' un modo* ‘of one mode’ → “in one mode”: the preposition changes.
- *sotto il segno ideale* ‘under the stamp ideal’ → “’neath the ideal stamp”: the adjective moves before the noun; *segno*, literally ‘sign’ or ‘mark’, is taken in the sense of an impressed stamp.
- *poi più e men traluce* ‘then more and less shines through’ → “is more and less transparent”: ‘then’ is omitted; the action of shining through becomes a quality expressed by a copula and adjective.

> 70 ond' egli avvien ch' un medesimo legno,  
> 71 secondo specie, meglio e peggio frutta;  

whence cometh, that one same tree in kind better and worse doth fruit;

- *ond' egli avvien* ‘whence it happens’ → “whence cometh”: the impersonal pronoun is omitted, and happening is expressed as coming about.
- *legno* ‘wood’ → “tree”: the material stands in Italian for the living tree.
- *secondo specie* ‘according to species’ → “in kind”: a phrase of respect replaces one of classification.
- *frutta* ‘fruits’ → “doth fruit”: the simple present is rendered with an auxiliary.

> 72 e voi nascete con diverso ingegno.  

and ye are born with diverse genius.

- *nascete* ‘ye come to birth’ → “ye are born”: a simple intransitive verb becomes a passive expression.
- *ingegno* ‘natural endowment’ → “genius”: the English uses ‘genius’ in the sense of native capacity.

> 73 Se fosse a punto la cera dedutta,  

Were the wax exactly moulded,

- *Se fosse* ‘if … were’ → “Were”: inversion of verb and subject replaces the conditional conjunction.
- *dedutta* ‘brought [to the requisite state]’ → “moulded”: a verb specific to working wax replaces the more general participle.

> 74 e fosse il cielo in sua virtù suprema,  
> 75 la luce del suggel parrebbe tutta;  

and were the heaven in its supremest virtue, the light of the signet would be all apparent;

- *virtù suprema* ‘virtue supreme’ → “supremest virtue”: the adjective moves before the noun and takes an explicit superlative ending.
- *parrebbe tutta* ‘would appear entire’ → “would be all apparent”: the verb of appearing becomes a copula and adjective; ‘entire’, agreeing with ‘light’, is rendered by “all” before “apparent”.

> 76 ma la natura la dà sempre scema,  
> 77 similemente operando all' artista,  
> 78 ch' ha l' abito dell' arte e man che trema.  

but nature ever furnisheth it faulty, doing as doth the artist who hath the knack of the art and a trembling hand.

- *la natura* ‘the nature’ → “nature”: English drops the generic article.
- *la dà sempre scema* ‘it gives always deficient’ → “ever furnisheth it faulty”: the adverb moves before the verb and the object pronoun after it; giving becomes furnishing, and deficiency is expressed as faultiness.
- *similemente operando all' artista* ‘similarly working to the artist’ → “doing as doth the artist”: the gerund moves first, and the comparison becomes a clause with a supplied verb.
- *l' abito dell' arte* ‘the acquired habit of the art’ → “the knack of the art”: an established proficiency is expressed as a knack.
- *man che trema* ‘hand that trembles’ → “a trembling hand”: an article is supplied and the relative clause becomes a participle before the noun.

## Lines 79–111 — The Scope of Solomon’s Peerless Wisdom

> 79 Però se il caldo amor, la chiara vista  
> 80 della prima virtù dispone e segna,  
> 81 tutta la perfezion quivi s' acquista.  

Wherefore if the warm Love, if the clear Vision, of the primal Power dispose and stamp, entire perfection is acquired there.

- *Però* ‘but’ → “Wherefore”: the adversative is taken in an inferential sense.
- *la chiara vista* ‘the clear sight’ → “if the clear Vision”: the conditional conjunction is repeated before the second subject.
- *prima virtù* ‘first power’ → “primal Power”: the ordinal becomes an adjective of origin or primacy.
- *dispone e segna* ‘disposes and marks’ → “dispose and stamp”: the Italian verbs are singular; the English verbs agree with the two subjects together, and the second verb suggests making an impression.
- *tutta la perfezion* ‘all the perfection’ → “entire perfection”: adjective and article give way to a single adjective.
- *quivi s’ acquista* ‘there is acquired’ → “is acquired there”: the adverb moves after the verb, and the Italian passive with *si* becomes a passive with “is”.

> 82 Così fu fatta già la terra degna  
> 83 di tutta l' animal perfezione:  
> 84 così fu fatta la Vergine pregna.  

Thus was the clay made worthy once of the full animal perfection; and thus the Virgin was impregnated.

- *fu fatta già la terra degna* ‘was made once the earth worthy’ → “was the clay made worthy once”: “clay” is more specific than ‘earth’; the subject comes between auxiliary and participle, and the adverb moves after the adjective.
- *di tutta l’ animal perfezione* ‘of all the animal perfection’ → “of the full animal perfection”: an adjective following the article replaces the quantifier preceding it.
- *così fu fatta la Vergine pregna* ‘thus was made the Virgin pregnant’ → “and thus the Virgin was impregnated”: the conjunction is supplied, the subject precedes the auxiliary, and ‘made pregnant’ becomes one passive verb.

> 85 Sì ch' io commendo tua opinione:  
> 86 che l' umana natura mai non fue,  
> 87 nè fia, qual fu in quelle due persone.  

Wherefore I sanction thine opinion that human nature never was, nor shall be, such as in those two persons.

- *Sì ch’* ‘so that’ → “Wherefore”: a clause of consequence becomes an independent statement introduced by an inferential adverb.
- *commendo* ‘commend’ → “sanction”: praise is rendered as endorsement.
- *che l’ umana natura mai non fue* ‘that human nature never not was’ → “that human nature never was”: English expresses the negative once rather than twice and uses no article before ‘human nature’.
- *qual fu in quelle due persone* ‘as it was in those two persons’ → “such as in those two persons”: “such” makes the comparison explicit, but the repeated verb is omitted.

> 88 Or, s' io non procedessi avanti piùe,  
> 89 ‘Dunque, come costui fu senza pare?’  
> 90 comincerebber le parole tue.  

Now, should I proceed no further, ‘how then was he without a peer?’ were the beginning of thy words.

- *s’ io non procedessi avanti piùe* ‘if I did not proceed forward any further’ → “should I proceed no further”: inversion with “should” replaces the conjunction and imperfect subjunctive; “no further” absorbs the negation and both adverbs.
- *Dunque, come costui fu* ‘then, how this man was’ → “how then was he”: the connective follows the interrogative, the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun, and the verb precedes its subject.
- *comincerebber le parole tue* ‘would begin the words of thine’ → “were the beginning of thy words”: in Italian the words would begin with the quoted question; in English the question is the subject, ‘begin’ becomes a predicate noun, and the possessive precedes ‘words’.

> 91 Ma, perchè paia ben quel che non pare,  
> 92 pensa chi era, e la cagion che il mosse,  
> 93 quando fu detto: ‘Chiedi,’ a domandare.  

But, that what now appeareth not may be apparent, think who he was, and what the cause which moved him—when he was bidden: ‘Choose,’—to make demand.

- *perchè paia ben quel che non pare* ‘so that there may appear clearly that which does not appear’ → “that what now appeareth not may be apparent”: the subject clause moves before its verb; “be apparent” replaces the first ‘appear’, ‘clearly’ drops out, and “now” is supplied.
- *e la cagion* ‘and the cause’ → “and what the cause”: “what” makes this second object of thought parallel to the preceding indirect question.
- *quando fu detto* ‘when it was said’ → “when he was bidden”: the impersonal passive of saying becomes a personal passive of bidding.
- *Chiedi* ‘Ask’ → “Choose”: the imperative is rendered as choosing rather than asking.
- *a domandare* ‘to ask’ → “to make demand”: a verb becomes a verb and noun.

> 94 Non ho parlato sì che tu non posse  
> 95 ben veder ch' ei fu re, che chiese senno,  
> 96 acciocchè re sufficiente fosse;  

I have not spoken so but that thou mayst perceive he was a king, who chose such wit that as a king he might be adequate;

- *sì che tu non posse ben veder* ‘so that thou canst not well see’ → “so but that thou mayst perceive”: “but that” recasts the negative result clause in positive form; ‘well’ is not separately rendered, and ‘see’ becomes “perceive”.
- *ch’ ei fu re* ‘that he was king’ → “he was a king”: the complementizer is omitted and the predicate noun gains an article.
- *che chiese senno* ‘who asked for discernment’ → “who chose such wit”: asking becomes choosing, and “such” is supplied to anticipate the following clause.
- *acciocchè re sufficiente fosse* ‘in order that he might be a sufficient king’ → “that as a king he might be adequate”: the purpose clause is attached to “such wit”; ‘king’ becomes a phrase of capacity, and its adjective becomes the predicate.

> 97 non per saper lo numero in che enno  
> 98 li motor di quassù, o se *necesse*  
> 99 con contingente mai *necesse* fenno;  

not to know the number in which exist the mover spirits here above, nor if a necessary and a contingent premise can ever give a necessary conclusion;

- *in che enno* ‘in which are’ → “in which exist”: the verb of being is rendered explicitly as existential.
- *li motor di quassù* ‘the movers of up here’ → “the mover spirits here above”: ‘movers’ becomes a modifier of the supplied noun “spirits”, and the phrase of place loses its preposition.
- *o se* ‘or if’ → “nor if”: the negative governing the alternatives is repeated in the conjunction.
- *necesse con contingente mai necesse fenno* ‘a necessary with a contingent ever made a necessary’ → “a necessary and a contingent premise can ever give a necessary conclusion”: coordination replaces ‘with’; the understood logical nouns are supplied, the past ‘made’ becomes modal “can … give”, and the result moves after the verb.

> 100 non, *si est dare primum motum esse*,  
> 101 o se del mezzo cerchio far si puote  
> 102 triangol sì ch' un retto non avesse.  

nor whether we must grant a *primum motum*; nor whether in a semicircle can be constructed a triangle that shall have no right angle.

- *non* ‘not’ → “nor”: the repeated negative becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *si est dare primum motum esse* ‘whether it is possible to grant that a first motion exists’ → “whether we must grant a *primum motum*”: the impersonal Latin construction becomes personal, “must” expresses necessity, and the claim of existence is compressed into the object of “grant”.
- *o se* ‘or if’ → “nor whether”: the alternative is recast as a coordinated negative indirect question.
- *del mezzo cerchio* ‘of the half circle’ → “in a semicircle”: the relation is made explicitly spatial, the definite article becomes indefinite, and the two words become one.
- *far si puote triangol* ‘make one can a triangle’ → “can be constructed a triangle”: the modal precedes a passive infinitive, and ‘make’ becomes the geometrical “constructed”.
- *sì ch’ un retto non avesse* ‘such that it should not have a right one’ → “that shall have no right angle”: a relative clause replaces the construction with ‘such that’, “shall” replaces the imperfect subjunctive, and the negative moves onto the noun phrase, with “angle” made explicit.

> 103 Onde, se ciò ch' io dissi e questo note,  
> 104 regal prudenza è quel vedere impari,  
> 105 in che lo stral di mia intenzion percote.  

Wherefore, (if this and all that I have said thou note) that insight without peer whereon the arrow of my intention smiteth, is regal prudence.

- *se ciò ch’ io dissi e questo note* ‘if what I said and this thou note’ → “if this and all that I have said thou note”: the objects exchange places, “all” is supplied, and the simple past becomes a present perfect.
- *regal prudenza è quel vedere impari* ‘regal prudence is that unequalled seeing’ → “that insight without peer” … “is regal prudence”: the two sides of the equation exchange places, so that the relative clause now intervenes before the copula; the substantivized infinitive becomes an abstract noun, and the adjective becomes a phrase.
- *in che* ‘in which’ → “whereon”: the relative prepositional phrase becomes an adverb expressing impact on rather than direction into.

> 106 E se al *surse* drizzi gli occhi chiari,  
> 107 vedrai aver solamente rispetto  
> 108 ai regi, che son molti, e i buon son rari.  

And if to *rose* thou turn discerning eyes, thou shalt see that it hath respect only to kings, the which are many and the good ones few.

- *gli occhi chiari* ‘the eyes clear’ → “discerning eyes”: the article drops out, the adjective precedes the noun, and clarity is interpreted as discernment.
- *vedrai aver solamente rispetto ai regi* ‘thou shalt see [it] to have only regard to the kings’ → “thou shalt see that it hath respect only to kings”: the dependent infinitive becomes a finite clause, “only” moves next to the restricted phrase, and the generic plural loses its article.
- *che* ‘who’ → “the which”: a relative with an article replaces the simple relative pronoun.
- *e i buon son rari* ‘and the good are rare’ → “and the good ones few”: “ones” supplies a noun for the substantivized adjective, the second copula is omitted, and rarity is expressed as fewness.

> 109 Con questa distinzion prendi il mio detto,  
> 110 e così puote star con quel che credi  
> 111 del primo padre e del nostro diletto.  

Thus qualified do thou accept my saying; and so it may consist with what thou holdest of the first father and of our delight.

- *Con questa distinzion* ‘with this distinction’ → “Thus qualified”: a prepositional phrase becomes a participial qualification of the saying.
- *prendi* ‘take’ → “do thou accept”: ‘take’ becomes ‘accept’, with an emphatic imperative construction.
- *puote star con* ‘can stand with’ → “it may consist with”: the idiom of standing is replaced by one of consistency.
- *quel che credi* ‘that which thou believest’ → “what thou holdest”: a fused relative replaces demonstrative and relative, and believing is rendered as holding an opinion.

## Lines 112–142 — Haste in Judgment

> 112 E questo ti sia sempre piombo ai piedi,  
> 113 per farti mover lento, com' uom lasso,  
> 114 ed al sì ed al no, che tu non vedi:  

And let this ever be lead to thy feet, to make thee move slow, like a weary man; both to the yea and nay thou seest not;

- *questo ti sia sempre* ‘may this be ever to thee’ → “let this ever be”: “let” expresses the exhortative subjunctive; “ever” moves before “be”, and the dative is expressed by “thy” with “feet”.
- *com' uom lasso* ‘like man weary’ → “like a weary man”: the article is supplied and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *ed al sì ed al no* ‘both to the yes and to the no’ → “both to the yea and nay”: the second preposition and article are not repeated.
- *che tu non vedi* ‘which thou dost not see’ → “thou seest not”: the object relative is omitted and the negative follows the verb.

> 115 chè quegli è tra gli stolti bene abbasso,  
> 116 che senza distinzion afferma o nega,  
> 117 nell' un così come nell' altro passo;  

for he is right low down amongst the fools who maketh affirmation or negation without distinction between case and case;

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the reason is introduced by a coordinating conjunction.
- *quegli* ‘that man’ → “he”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *tra gli stolti bene abbasso* ‘among the fools right far down’ → “right low down amongst the fools”: the expression of degree and position comes first.
- *senza distinzion afferma o nega* ‘without distinction affirms or denies’ → “maketh affirmation or negation without distinction”: the two verbs become nouns governed by a single verb, and the qualifying phrase comes last.
- *nell' un così come nell' altro passo* ‘in the one case just as in the other’ → “between case and case”: parallel circumstances of affirming or denying become the two terms of a distinction.

> 118 perch' egl' incontra che più volte piega  
> 119 l' opinion corrente in falsa parte,  
> 120 e poi l' affetto lo intelletto lega.  

wherefore it chanceth many times swift-formed opinion leaneth the wrong way, and then conceit bindeth the intellect.

- *perch' egl' incontra che* ‘whence it happens that’ → “wherefore it chanceth”: the conjunction introducing what happens is omitted.
- *più volte* ‘several times’ → “many times”: the indefinite frequency is expressed with “many”.
- *piega l' opinion corrente* ‘bends the running opinion’ → “swift-formed opinion leaneth”: the subject moves before the intransitive verb and loses its article; the participle is taken to describe the speed with which the opinion is formed, and precedes its noun.
- *in falsa parte* ‘toward a false side’ → “the wrong way”: a directional noun phrase replaces the prepositional phrase.
- *l' affetto lo intelletto lega* ‘the affection the intellect binds’ → “conceit bindeth the intellect”: the first noun loses its article and is interpreted as attachment to one's own view; the object follows the verb.

> 121 Vie più che indarno da riva si parte,  
> 122 perchè non torna tal qual ei si move,  
> 123 chi pesca per lo vero e non ha l' arte:  

Far worse than vainly doth he leave the shore, since he returneth not as he puts forth, who fisheth for the truth and hath not the art;

- *Vie più che indarno* ‘far more than in vain’ → “Far worse than vainly”: the comparative of degree becomes explicitly adverse, and the adverbial phrase becomes a single adverb.
- *da riva si parte* ‘from shore departs’ → “doth he leave the shore”: the pronominal verb of departure becomes a transitive verb with an article before its object; “doth he” anticipates the subject given later by “who fisheth”.
- *non torna tal qual ei si move* ‘he returns not such as he sets out’ → “he returneth not as he puts forth”: the negative follows the verb, the word denoting the state in which he returns is not separately rendered, and setting out becomes “puts forth”.

> 124 e di ciò sono al mondo aperte prove  
> 125 Parmenide, Melisso, Brisso e molti  
> 126 i quali andavano, e non sapean dove.  

and of this to the world are open proofs, Parmenides, Melissus, Bryson, and the host who still were going, but they knew not whither.

- *sono al mondo aperte prove* ‘are to the world open proofs’ → “to the world are open proofs”: the prepositional phrase precedes the verb.
- *molti* ‘many’ → “the host”: a plural used substantivally becomes a definite singular collective.
- *andavano* ‘were going’ → “still were going”: “still” makes the continuance implicit in the imperfect explicit.
- *e non sapean dove* ‘and knew not where’ → “but they knew not whither”: the conjunction becomes adversative, the negative follows the verb, and “whither” makes the implied direction explicit.

> 127 Sì fe' Sabellio ed Arrio, e quegli stolti  
> 128 che furon come spade alle scritture  
> 129 in render torti li diritti volti.  

So did Sabellius and Arius, and those fools who were as swords unto the Scripture, in making the straight countenances crooked.

- *fe'* ‘did’ (singular) → “did”: the Italian verb is singular before the coordinated subjects; the English past tense does not mark number.
- *alle scritture* ‘to the scriptures’ → “unto the Scripture”: the plural becomes a collective singular.
- *in render torti li diritti volti* ‘in making crooked the straight countenances’ → “in making the straight countenances crooked”: the infinitive becomes a gerund, and the adjective of result follows the object.

> 130 Non sien le genti ancor troppo sicure  
> 131 a giudicar, sì come quei che stima  
> 132 le biade in campo pria che sien mature:  

Let not folk yet be too secure in judgment, as who should count the ears upon the field ere they be ripe;

- *Non sien le genti ancor* ‘be not the people yet’ → “Let not folk yet be”: “let” expresses the negative exhortation; the subject loses its article and, with “yet”, moves before “be”.
- *a giudicar* ‘in judging’ → “in judgment”: a noun replaces the infinitive.
- *sì come quei che stima* ‘just like him who estimates’ → “as who should count”: the demonstrative has no separate equivalent, the indicative becomes hypothetical, and estimating becomes counting.
- *le biade in campo* ‘the crops in field’ → “the ears upon the field”: the crop is represented by its ears; the preposition changes and the field takes an article.
- *pria che sien mature* ‘before they be ripe’ → “ere they be ripe”: the two-word conjunction becomes one word.

> 133 ch' io ho veduto tutto il verno prima  
> 134 il prun mostrarsi rigido e feroce,  
> 135 poscia portar la rosa in su la cima;  

for I have seen first all the winter through the thorn display itself hard and forbidding and then upon its summit bear the rose;

- *ch'* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *tutto il verno prima* ‘all the winter first’ → “first all the winter through”: “first” moves to the front, and “through” makes the duration explicit.
- *mostrarsi* ‘show itself’ → “display itself”: a different verb is used and the attached reflexive becomes a separate word.
- *rigido e feroce* ‘rigid and fierce’ → “hard and forbidding”: stiffness becomes hardness and fierceness is rendered as an impression made by the thorn.
- *poscia portar la rosa in su la cima* ‘afterward bear the rose up on the summit’ → “and then upon its summit bear the rose”: a conjunction is supplied, the phrase of place moves before the verb, and a possessive replaces the definite article.

> 136 e legno vidi già dritto e veloce  
> 137 correr lo mar per tutto suo cammino,  
> 138 perire al fine all' entrar della foce.  

and I have seen ere now a ship fare straight and swift over the sea through her entire course, and perish at the last, entering the harbour mouth.

- *legno vidi già* ‘a timber I saw once’ → “I have seen ere now a ship”: the name of the material is rendered by the vessel it denotes; the object follows the verb and adverb, and the simple past becomes a present perfect.
- *dritto e veloce correr lo mar* ‘straight and swift run the sea’ → “fare straight and swift over the sea”: the verb of motion precedes its modifiers, and the sea becomes the object of a preposition.
- *per tutto suo cammino* ‘through all its journey’ → “through her entire course”: the possessive is feminine in English and precedes the adjective of extent; the journey is called a “course”.
- *perire al fine* ‘perish at the end’ → “and perish at the last”: the conjunction is supplied and the time phrase is idiomatically recast.
- *all' entrar della foce* ‘at the entering of the mouth’ → “entering the harbour mouth”: the prepositional phrase with a nominal infinitive becomes a participial clause with a direct object; “harbour” specifies the kind of mouth.

> 139 Non creda donna Berta o ser Martino  
> 140 per vedere un furare, altro offerere,  
> 141 vedergli dentro al consiglio divino;  

Let not Dame Bertha or Squire Martin think, if they perceive one steal and one make offering, they therefore see them as in the divine counsel;

- *Non creda* ‘let not … believe’ → “Let not Dame Bertha or Squire Martin think”: “Let” expresses the subjunctive, with the subject before the English main verb; believing becomes thinking.
- *ser* ‘sir’ → “Squire”: a different English title is used.
- *per vedere* ‘from seeing’ → “if they perceive”: the phrase giving the ground of the belief becomes a finite condition.
- *altro offerere* ‘another offer’ → “and one make offering”: a conjunction is supplied, the contrastive pronoun becomes a repeated “one”, and the verb becomes a verb–noun phrase.
- *vedergli dentro al consiglio divino* ‘to see them within the divine counsel’ → “they therefore see them as in the divine counsel”: the dependent infinitive becomes a finite clause; “therefore” marks the inference, “as” is supplied, “in” replaces ‘within’, and the adjective precedes its noun.

> 142 chè quel può surgere, e quel può cadere.”  

for the one yet may rise and the other fall.”

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *quel può surgere* ‘that one may rise’ → “the one yet may rise”: the first demonstrative becomes the first member of an English contrast, and “yet” is supplied.
- *e quel può cadere* ‘and that one may fall’ → “and the other fall”: the repeated demonstrative becomes the contrasting “other”, and the second modal is understood rather than repeated.

## Summary — English Recasting and Italian Echoes

Wicksteed often moves a postponed subject before its verb or brings a modifier next to its noun: in “Minos’ daughter made”, the daughter precedes the verb, and *di Minoi* becomes a possessive before ‘daughter’. Yet he does not regularize every inversion. In “to beat out the other sweet love inviteth me” he keeps the infinitive ahead of the subject and verb, as in the Italian. He also preserves Dante’s repeated *imagini* with “let him imagine” and keeps the balance of *d’ atto in atto* in “from act to act”.

A change of construction often matters more than a change of order. The active *ruppe il silenzio*, ‘broke the silence’, becomes “silence was broken”, while the substantivized infinitives *il tuo credere e il mio dire* become two clauses, “what thou believest and what I say”. Word choice can sharpen an image or narrow its sense: *aduna*, ‘gathers’, becomes “focus” in the passage on light, and *lumi*, ‘lights’, becomes “torches”. Elsewhere Wicksteed stays close to the Italian image, retaining the wax, the signet, and the artist’s trembling hand.

Some additions spell out what the Italian leaves implicit: Wicksteed supplies “premise” and “conclusion” in the passage on logic and “angle” for *un retto*, ‘a right one’. Some details disappear: *in punta dello stelo*, ‘at the tip of the shaft’, becomes “from the axle”, and the repeated *può* at the close is represented by a single “may” serving both verbs. Two quieter adjustments recur throughout: English supplies subject pronouns where Italian verb endings suffice, as in *vedrai*, “thou shalt see”, and drops the Italian article before possessives, as in *il mio detto*, “my saying”.
