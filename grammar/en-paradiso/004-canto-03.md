# Paradiso 3

## Lines 1–24 — Faces Mistaken for Reflections

> 1 Quel sol, che pria d'amor mi scaldò il petto,  
> 2 di bella verità m' avea scoperto,  
> 3 provando e riprovando, il dolce aspetto;  

That sun which first warmed my bosom with love had thus unveiled for me, by proof and refutation, fair truth’s sweet aspect;

- *d'amor mi scaldò il petto* ‘with love warmed me the breast’ → “warmed my bosom with love”: the phrase naming what warmed him moves to the end; the indirect pronoun becomes a possessive, and ‘breast’ becomes “bosom”.
- *di bella verità … il dolce aspetto* ‘of fair truth … the sweet aspect’ → “fair truth’s sweet aspect”: the separated phrases are brought together at the end of the clause; the possessive replaces ‘of’ and does duty for the definite article before “sweet aspect”.
- *m' avea scoperto* ‘to me had uncovered’ → “had thus unveiled for me”: the indirect pronoun becomes a phrase following the verb, ‘uncovered’ becomes “unveiled”, and “thus” is supplied.
- *provando e riprovando* ‘proving and disproving’ → “by proof and refutation”: the two gerunds of means become nouns governed by an explicit “by”; the second is taken in the sense of refuting, not of proving again.

> 4 ed io, per confessar corretto e certo  
> 5 me stesso, tanto quanto si convenne,  
> 6 levai lo capo a proferer più erto.  

and I, to confess me corrected and assured, in measure as was meet, sloped up my head to speak.

- *per confessar corretto e certo me stesso* ‘to confess corrected and certain my own self’ → “to confess me corrected and assured”: the object moves before its predicates, the emphasis of ‘my own self’ is reduced to “me”, and the adjective ‘certain’ becomes the participial “assured”.
- *tanto quanto si convenne* ‘as much as was fitting’ → “in measure as was meet”: the matching words of degree give way to the noun “measure” followed by an *as*-clause.
- *levai lo capo a proferer più erto* ‘I raised the head to speak more erect’ → “sloped up my head to speak”: the definite article becomes a possessive; the final description of a more upright bearing is absorbed into the directional verb “sloped up”, with no explicit comparative.

> 7 Ma visione apparve, che ritenne  
> 8 a sè me tanto stretto per vedersi,  
> 9 che di mia confession non mi sovvenne.  

But there appeared to me a sight which so straitly held me to itself, to look upon it, that I bethought me not of my confession.

- *visione apparve* ‘a vision appeared’ → “there appeared to me a sight”: the subject follows the verb in a construction with anticipatory “there”; “to me” is supplied, and ‘vision’ becomes “sight”.
- *che ritenne a sè me tanto stretto* ‘which held to itself me so tight’ → “which so straitly held me to itself”: the description of how tightly the speaker was held becomes an adverb before the verb; the direct object now precedes the phrase of attachment.
- *per vedersi* ‘in order to be seen’ → “to look upon it”: the reflexive infinitive with passive sense becomes active, with the beholder understood as subject and the sight made its object.
- *di mia confession non mi sovvenne* ‘of my confession it did not occur to me’ → “I bethought me not of my confession”: the impersonal recollection becomes a personal reflexive verb, and the phrase naming what was forgotten moves to the end.

> 10 Quali per vetri trasparenti e tersi,  
> 11 o ver per acque nitide e tranquille,  
> 12 non sì profonde che i fondi sien persi,  

In such guise as, from glasses transparent and polished, or from waters clear and tranquil, not so deep that the bottom is darkened,

- *Quali* ‘such as’ → “In such guise as”: the plural adjective anticipating the returning images becomes a phrase of manner, with “guise” supplied.
- *per vetri* ‘through glasses’ → “from glasses”: the preposition marks the surface from which the images return rather than the medium through which they appear.
- *o ver per acque* ‘or else through waters’ → “or from waters”: the fuller alternative connective is reduced to “or”, and the preposition again becomes one of source.
- *che i fondi sien persi* ‘that the bottoms be lost’ → “that the bottom is darkened”: the plural becomes a generic singular, the subjunctive gives way to the indicative, and being lost to sight is expressed as darkening.

> 13 tornan dei nostri visi le postille  
> 14 debili sì che perla in bianca fronte  
> 15 non vien men tosto alle nostre pupille;  

come back the notes of our faces, so faint that a pearl on a white brow cometh not slowlier upon our pupils;

- *dei nostri visi le postille* ‘of our faces the notes’ → “the notes of our faces”: the dependent phrase follows its noun instead of preceding it.
- *debili sì* ‘faint so’ → “so faint”: the degree word precedes the adjective.
- *perla in bianca fronte* ‘pearl on white brow’ → “a pearl on a white brow”: English supplies indefinite articles to both singular nouns.
- *non vien men tosto* ‘comes no less soon’ → “cometh not slowlier”: the negative follows the verb, and the comparison with ‘soon’ becomes one with its opposite, ‘slowly’.
- *alle nostre pupille* ‘to our pupils’ → “upon our pupils”: “upon” replaces the preposition of approach.

> 16 tali vid' io più facce a parlar pronte,  
> 17 perch' io dentro all' error contrario corsi  
> 18 a quel ch' accese amor tra l' uomo e il fonte.  

so did I behold many a countenance, eager to speak; wherefore I fell into the counter error of that which kindled love between the man and fountain.

- *tali* ‘such’ → “so”: the plural adjective describing the faces becomes an adverb answering the comparison introduced in line 10.
- *vid' io* ‘saw I’ → “did I behold”: ‘see’ becomes “behold”, with an auxiliary carrying the past tense and permitting inversion.
- *più facce* ‘several faces’ → “many a countenance”: plurality is expressed by “many a” with a singular noun, and ‘face’ becomes “countenance”.
- *a parlar pronte* ‘to speak ready’ → “eager to speak”: the adjective moves before its complement, and readiness becomes eagerness.
- *dentro all' error contrario corsi* ‘into the error contrary I ran’ → “fell into the counter error”: “fell” replaces ‘ran’ and precedes its complement; the adjective also moves before its noun.
- *a quel* ‘to that’ → “of that”: the preposition linking the two errors changes with the rendering of ‘contrary’.
- *e il fonte* ‘and the fountain’ → “and fountain”: the second definite article is dropped.

> 19 Subito, sì com' io di lor m' accorsi,  
> 20 quelle stimando specchiati sembianti,  
> 21 per veder di cui fosser gli occhi torsi;  

No sooner was I aware of them, than, thinking them reflected images, I turned round my eyes to see of whom they were;

- *Subito, sì com' io di lor m' accorsi* ‘Immediately, as soon as I of them became aware’ → “No sooner was I aware of them, than”: the opening adverb and temporal clause are recast as a negative comparative with inversion and a balancing “than”; ‘became aware’ becomes “was I aware”, and its complement follows.
- *quelle stimando* ‘those deeming’ → “thinking them”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun after the participle, and ‘deeming’ becomes “thinking”.
- *specchiati sembianti* ‘mirrored appearances’ → “reflected images”: the specifically mirror-like description becomes “reflected”, and ‘appearances’ becomes “images”.
- *per veder di cui fosser* ‘to see of whom they might be’ → “to see of whom they were”: the purpose clause moves after the action it qualifies; the subjunctive of the indirect question is rendered by the ordinary English past.
- *gli occhi torsi* ‘the eyes I turned’ → “I turned round my eyes”: the object follows the verb, the article becomes possessive, and “round” makes the direction explicit.

> 22 e nulla vidi, e ritorsili avanti  
> 23 dritti nel lume della dolce guida,  
> 24 che sorridendo ardea negli occhi santi.  

and I saw naught, and turned them forward again straight on the light of my sweet guide, whose sacred eyes glowed as she smiled.

- *nulla vidi* ‘nothing I saw’ → “I saw naught”: the negative object follows the verb.
- *ritorsili avanti* ‘I turned-them-back forward’ → “turned them forward again”: the sense of the verbal prefix is carried by the separate “again”, placed after “forward”.
- *dritti nel lume* ‘directed straight into the light’ → “straight on the light”: the plural adjective agreeing with the eyes becomes a directional adverb, and ‘into’ becomes “on”.
- *che sorridendo ardea negli occhi santi* ‘who smiling burned in the holy eyes’ → “whose sacred eyes glowed as she smiled”: the guide, subject of ‘burned’, becomes the possessor in a relative clause whose subject is her eyes; “glowed” replaces ‘burned’, the epithet precedes its noun, and the gerund becomes a finite temporal clause at the end.

## Lines 25–57 — Beatrice’s Counsel and Piccarda’s Reply

> 25 “Non ti maravigliar perch' io sorrida,  
> 26 mi disse, appresso il tuo pueril coto,  

“Wonder not that I smile,” she said, “in presence of thy child-like thought,

- *Non ti maravigliar* ‘do not marvel’ → “Wonder not”: the English verb needs no counterpart to the reflexive *ti*, and the negative follows the verb.
- *perch' io sorrida* ‘because I smile’ → “that I smile”: the cause of wonder is expressed by a *that*-clause; the subjunctive has no distinct English rendering.
- *mi disse* ‘said to me’ → “she said”: the recipient is omitted.
- *appresso* ‘at’ or ‘in the face of’ → “in presence of”: a preposition is expanded into a phrase.

> 27 poi sopra il vero ancor lo piè non fida,  
> 28 ma ti rivolve, come suole, a vòto.  

since it trusts not its foot upon the truth, but turneth thee after its wont, to vacancy.

- *sopra il vero ancor lo piè non fida* ‘upon the true it does not yet trust the foot’ → “it trusts not its foot upon the truth”: the opening prepositional phrase moves after the verb and its object, and the substantivized adjective becomes a noun. The temporal qualification ‘yet’ is omitted, the object moves after the verb, and the article becomes a possessive.
- *ti rivolve* ‘thee it turns back’ → “turneth thee”: the pronoun follows the verb, and the backward or recurrent force of *ri-* is not separately expressed.
- *come suole* ‘as it is wont’ → “after its wont”: the finite clause becomes a prepositional phrase with a noun.
- *a vòto* ‘in vain’ or ‘toward emptiness’ → “to vacancy”: the translation takes the expression directionally rather than in its idiomatic sense of ‘to no purpose’.

> 29 Vere sustanzie son ciò che tu vedi,  
> 30 qui rilegate per manco di voto.  

True substances are they which thou beholdest, relegated here for failure of their vows.

- *ciò che tu vedi* ‘that which thou seest’ → “they which thou beholdest”: the singular demonstrative becomes a plural personal pronoun agreeing with “substances”; “beholdest” is a more marked verb than ‘seest’.
- *qui rilegate* ‘here relegated’ → “relegated here”: the adverb follows the participle.
- *per manco di voto* ‘for lack in respect of a vow’ → “for failure of their vows”: ‘lack’ is interpreted as “failure”; the singular becomes plural and a possessive is supplied.

> 31 Però parla con esse, ed odi, e credi;  

Wherefore speak with them, and listen and believe;

- Literal.

> 32 chè la verace luce che le appaga  
> 33 da sè non lascia lor torcer li piedi.”  

for the true light which satisfieth them, suffereth them not to turn their feet aside from it.”

- *che le appaga* ‘which them satisfies’ → “which satisfieth them”: the object follows the verb.
- *da sè* ‘from itself’ → “from it”: the reflexive becomes a personal pronoun, and the phrase moves to the end.
- *non lascia lor* ‘does not allow them’ → “suffereth them not”: ‘allow’ is rendered by “suffereth”, with the negative after verb and pronoun.
- *torcer li piedi* ‘to twist the feet’ → “to turn their feet aside”: the simple verb is rendered by verb and adverb, and the article becomes a possessive.

> 34 Ed io all' ombra, che parea più vaga  
> 35 di ragionar, drizza' mi, e cominciai,  
> 36 quasi com' uom cui troppa voglia smaga:  

And I to the shade who seemed most to long for converse turned me and began, as one whom too great longing doth confound:

- *parea più vaga di ragionar* ‘seemed more desirous of conversing’ → “seemed most to long for converse”: the comparative is taken as selecting one shade from the group and rendered as a superlative; the adjective becomes a verb of desire and the infinitive a noun.
- *quasi com' uom* ‘almost as a man’ → “as one”: the qualification ‘almost’ disappears, and the generic noun becomes a pronoun.
- *cui troppa voglia smaga* ‘whom excessive desire confounds’ → “whom too great longing doth confound”: the degree of desire is expressed by “too great”, and the simple verb is expanded with “doth”.

> 37 “O ben creato spirito, che ai rai  
> 38 di vita eterna la dolcezza senti,  
> 39 che non gustata non s' intende mai,  

“O well created spirit, who in the rays of eternal life dost feel the sweetness which, save tasted, may ne’er be understood;

- *ai rai di vita eterna* ‘at the rays of life eternal’ → “in the rays of eternal life”: the preposition changes and the adjective precedes its noun.
- *la dolcezza senti* ‘the sweetness thou feelest’ → “dost feel the sweetness”: the object moves after the verb, which is expanded with “dost”.
- *non gustata* ‘not tasted’ → “save tasted”: the negated participle becomes an exceptive condition.
- *non s' intende mai* ‘is never understood’ → “may ne’er be understood”: the passive *si* construction becomes an English passive with an added modal of possibility.

> 40 grazioso mi fia, se mi contenti  
> 41 del nome tuo e della vostra sorte.”  

it were acceptable to me, wouldst thou content me with thy name and with your lot.”

- *grazioso mi fia* ‘pleasing to me it will be’ → “it were acceptable to me”: the predicate adjective moves after the verb, the pronoun becomes a prepositional phrase, and the future becomes a hypothetical subjunctive.
- *se mi contenti* ‘if thou dost content me’ → “wouldst thou content me”: conditional inversion replaces *se*, the present becomes modal, and the object moves after the verb.
- *del nome tuo e della vostra sorte* ‘of the name of thine and of your lot’ → “with thy name and with your lot”: English ‘content’ takes “with” rather than ‘of’, and the first possessive moves before its noun. The change from singular to plural address is retained.

> 42 Ond' ella pronta e con occhi ridenti:  

Whereat she, eager and with smiling eyes:

- *pronta* ‘ready’ → “eager”: readiness is rendered as eagerness.
- *con occhi ridenti* ‘with eyes laughing’ → “with smiling eyes”: the participle precedes its noun, and ‘laughing’ becomes “smiling”.

> 43 “La nostra carità non serra porte  
> 44 a giusta voglia, se non come quella  
> 45 che vuol simile a sè tutta sua corte.  

“Our love doth no more bar the gate to a just wish, than doth that love which would have all its court like to itself.

- *carità* ‘charity’ → “love”: the broader English noun is chosen.
- *non serra porte* ‘does not shut gates’ → “doth no more bar the gate”: the simple negative becomes the first half of a comparison of degree; ‘shut’ becomes “bar”, and the bare plural becomes a definite singular.
- *a giusta voglia* ‘to just desire’ → “to a just wish”: the desire is expressed as a countable “wish”, with an indefinite article.
- *se non come quella* ‘except as that one’ → “than doth that love”: the exception becomes the second half of the comparison; the verb is represented by “doth”, and the noun understood after the demonstrative is supplied.
- *vuol simile a sè tutta sua corte* ‘wills like to itself all its court’ → “would have all its court like to itself”: the verb of will becomes “would have”, and the object precedes its predicate instead of following it.

> 46 Io fui nel mondo vergine sorella;  

In the world I was a virgin sister;

- *Io fui nel mondo* ‘I was in the world’ → “In the world I was”: the prepositional phrase moves to the front.
- *vergine sorella* ‘virgin sister’ → “a virgin sister”: English supplies an article for the predicate noun.

> 47 e se la mente tua ben si riguarda,  
> 48 non mi ti celerà l' esser più bella,  
> 49 ma riconoscerai ch' io son Piccarda,  

and if thy memory be rightly searched, my greater beauty will not hide me from thee, but thou wilt know me again for Piccarda,

- *la mente tua ben si riguarda* ‘thy mind looks well into itself’ → “thy memory be rightly searched”: the postposed possessive moves before the noun, ‘mind’ is narrowed to “memory”, and reflexive self-examination becomes a passive search; “be” marks the condition.
- *non mi ti celerà l' esser più bella* ‘will not hide me from thee the being more beautiful’ → “my greater beauty will not hide me from thee”: the subject moves from after the verb to before it. The nominalized infinitive and comparative adjective become a noun of quality with a comparative modifier, and a possessive replaces the article. The complements expressed by preverbal pronouns follow the verb.
- *riconoscerai ch' io son Piccarda* ‘thou wilt recognize that I am Piccarda’ → “thou wilt know me again for Piccarda”: “know me again” unpacks the verb of recognition, and the finite clause of identity becomes an object with a *for*-complement.

> 50 che, posta qui con questi altri beati,  
> 51 beata sono in la spera più tarda.  

who, placed here with these other blessed ones, am blessed in the sphere that moveth slowest.

- *questi altri beati* ‘these other blessed’ → “these other blessed ones”: “ones” gives the substantivized adjective an English noun substitute.
- *beata sono* ‘blessed I am’ → “am blessed”: the predicate follows the verb.
- *la spera più tarda* ‘the slowest sphere’ → “the sphere that moveth slowest”: the adjective becomes an adverb in a relative clause, making movement explicit.

> 52 Li nostri affetti, che solo infiammati  
> 53 son nel piacer dello Spirito Santo,  
> 54 letizian del su' ordine informati.  

Our affections, which are aflame only in the pleasure of the Holy Spirit, rejoice to be informed after his order.

- *solo infiammati son* ‘only inflamed are’ → “are aflame only”: the copula moves before its complement, the participle becomes “aflame”, and “only” is placed immediately before the prepositional phrase it limits.
- *Spirito Santo* ‘Spirit Holy’ → “Holy Spirit”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *letizian del su' ordine informati* ‘rejoice, by his order informed’ → “rejoice to be informed after his order”: the participial description becomes an infinitive complement of “rejoice”; the phrase preceding the participle moves after it, and “after” expresses conformity rather than agency.

> 55 E questa sorte, che par giù cotanto,  
> 56 però n' è data, perchè fur negletti  
> 57 li nostri voti, e vòti in alcun canto.”  

And this lot, which seemeth so far down, therefore is given us because our vows were slighted, and on some certain side were not filled in.”

- *giù cotanto* ‘down so much’ → “so far down”: degree is expressed as distance, before rather than after “down”.
- *n' è data* ‘to us is given’ → “is given us”: the recipient pronoun follows the passive verb.
- *fur negletti li nostri voti* ‘were neglected our vows’ → “our vows were slighted”: the subject moves before the passive verb, and ‘neglected’ becomes “slighted”.
- *vòti in alcun canto* ‘empty in some part’ → “on some certain side were not filled in”: the phrase of limitation moves to the front, ‘some’ is expanded to “some certain”, and the adjective becomes a negative passive with a repeated verb. The English does not reproduce the play of *voti* ‘vows’ against *vòti* ‘empty’.

## Lines 58–90 — His Will Is Our Peace

> 58 Ond' io a lei: “Nei mirabili aspetti  
> 59 vostri risplende non so che divino,  
> 60 che vi trasmuta dai primi concetti.  

Whereon I to her: “In your wondrous aspects a divine somewhat regloweth that doth transmute you from conceits of former times.

- *Nei mirabili aspetti vostri* ‘in the wondrous aspects of yours’ → “In your wondrous aspects”: the possessive moves from after the noun to before the adjective.
- *risplende non so che divino* ‘shines forth I know not what divine thing’ → “a divine somewhat regloweth”: the subject moves before the verb; the indefinite expression containing ‘I know not’ becomes the noun “somewhat”, with “divine” before it. Shining forth is expressed as glowing again or in return.
- *che vi trasmuta* ‘that you transmutes’ → “that doth transmute you”: the object follows the verb, and the simple present is expanded with “doth”.
- *dai primi concetti* ‘from the first conceptions’ → “from conceits of former times”: the definite article disappears, and the adjective qualifying the conceptions becomes a phrase dating them.

> 61 Però non fui a rimembrar festino;  

Wherefore I lagged in calling thee to mind;

- *non fui a rimembrar festino* ‘I was not, in remembering, quick’ → “I lagged in calling thee to mind”: a positive verb of delay replaces the negated verb and adjective; the infinitive becomes a gerund phrase with an explicit object.

> 62 ma or m' aiuta ciò che tu mi dici,  
> 63 sì che raffigurar m' è più latino.  

now what thou tellest me giveth such help that more articulately I retrace thee.

- *ma or* ‘but now’ → “now”: the adversative is omitted.
- *m' aiuta ciò che tu mi dici* ‘me helps that which thou to me sayest’ → “what thou tellest me giveth such help”: the subject clause moves before the verb; within it, the recipient follows “tellest”. The main verb becomes “giveth” with a noun, its personal object is left implicit, and “such” anticipates the result.
- *sì che raffigurar m' è più latino* ‘so that to recognize is easier for me’ → “that more articulately I retrace thee”: the result clause is introduced by “that” alone. The infinitive becomes a finite verb with an explicit object, the person to whom recognition is easier becomes its subject, and the comparative adjective of ease or clearness becomes an adverb of manner before the verb.

> 64 Ma dimmi: voi, che siete qui felici,  
> 65 desiderate voi più alto loco  
> 66 per più vedere, o per più farvi amici?”  

But tell me, ye whose blessedness is here, do ye desire a more lofty place, to see more, or to make yourselves more dear?”

- *voi, che siete qui felici* ‘you, who are here happy’ → “ye whose blessedness is here”: the adjective becomes a noun possessed by the addressees, and that noun becomes the subject of the relative clause.
- *desiderate voi* ‘desire ye’ → “do ye desire”: English uses an auxiliary for the question.
- *più alto loco* ‘higher place’ → “a more lofty place”: the indefinite article is supplied.
- *per più vedere* ‘in order more to see’ → “to see more”: the infinitive alone marks purpose, and the comparative follows the verb.
- *per più farvi amici* ‘in order more to make yourselves friends’ → “to make yourselves more dear”: the infinitive again marks purpose; the comparative moves after the reflexive, and the expression of friendship is interpreted as an adjective of dearness.

> 67 Con quelle altr' ombre pria sorrise un poco;  

With those other shades first she smiled a little,

- *quelle altr' ombre pria* ‘the other shades first’ → “those other shades first”: Italian locates *pria* before the verb, as does English, but the demonstrative qualifying the shades is more explicit in English than a definite article would be.

> 68 da indi mi rispose tanto lieta,  
> 69 ch' arder parea d' amor nel primo foco:  

then answered me so joyous that she seemed to burn in love’s first flame:

- *da indi* ‘from then’ → “then”: the two-word transition is reduced to an adverb.
- *mi rispose* ‘to me she answered’ → “answered me”: the pronoun follows the verb as its object.
- *arder parea* ‘to burn she seemed’ → “she seemed to burn”: the infinitive follows the finite verb.
- *d' amor nel primo foco* ‘of love in the first fire’ → “in love’s first flame”: the love of which she burns is construed as the possessor of the flame; “flame” particularizes ‘fire’.

> 70 “Frate, la nostra volontà quieta  
> 71 virtù di carità, che fa volerne  
> 72 sol quel ch' avemo, e d' altro non ci asseta.  

“Brother, the quality of love stilleth our will, and maketh us long only for what we have, and giveth us no other thirst.

- *la nostra volontà quieta virtù di carità* ‘our will quiets the power of charity’ → “the quality of love stilleth our will”: Italian orders the words object–verb–subject; English orders them subject–verb–object. The active power or virtue becomes a “quality”, and charity is rendered by the broader “love”.
- *che fa volerne sol quel ch' avemo* ‘which makes us will only that which we have’ → “and maketh us long only for what we have”: coordination replaces the relative clause; the personal pronoun attached to the infinitive moves before “long”, and willing something becomes longing for it.
- *e d' altro non ci asseta* ‘and for aught else does not make us thirst’ → “and giveth us no other thirst”: the causative verb becomes “giveth” with a noun; negation and ‘other’ now qualify the thirst rather than denying thirst for anything else.

> 73 Se disiassimo esser più superne,  
> 74 foran discordi gli nostri disiri  
> 75 dal voler di colui che qui ne cerne,  

Did we desire to be more aloft, our longings were discordant from his will who here assorteth us,

- *Se disiassimo* ‘if we desired’ → “Did we desire”: auxiliary inversion replaces the conditional conjunction and imperfect subjunctive.
- *esser più superne* ‘to be higher up’ → “to be more aloft”: the comparative retains a separate degree word, but the feminine plural adjective becomes an adverb of place.
- *foran discordi gli nostri disiri* ‘would be discordant our desires’ → “our longings were discordant”: the subject comes first, “longings” replaces ‘desires’, and the hypothetical “were” renders the conditional.
- *dal voler di colui* ‘from the will of him’ → “from his will”: the possessor becomes a prenominal possessive rather than the expressed antecedent of the following relative.
- *che qui ne cerne* ‘who here us distinguishes’ → “who here assorteth us”: distinguishing is interpreted as assigning to places, and the object follows the verb.

> 76 che vedrai non capere in questi giri,  
> 77 s' essere in caritate è qui *necesse*,  
> 78 e se la sua natura ben rimiri.  

and for that, thou wilt see, there is no room within these circles, if of necessity we have our being here in love, and if thou think again what is love’s nature.

- *che vedrai non capere in questi giri* ‘which thou wilt see not to fit in these circles’ → “and for that, thou wilt see, there is no room within these circles”: the connective relative becomes a conjunction and a backward-pointing demonstrative; the infinitive dependent on ‘see’ becomes an independent existential clause, with “thou wilt see” parenthetical.
- *s' essere in caritate è qui necesse* ‘if to be in charity is here necessary’ → “if of necessity we have our being here in love”: the infinitival subject becomes a personal finite clause, with ‘be’ expanded to “have our being”; the predicate of necessity becomes an opening adverbial, and “here” now accompanies the being in love.
- *e se la sua natura ben rimiri* ‘and if its nature thou well considerest’ → “and if thou think again what is love’s nature”: close consideration becomes thinking again; the object becomes an indirect question after the verb, and the possessive pronoun is resolved to “love’s”.

> 79 Anzi è formale ad esto beato *esse*  
> 80 tenersi dentro alla divina voglia,  
> 81 per ch' una fansi nostre voglie stesse.  

Nay, ’tis the essence of this blessed being to hold ourselves within the divine will, whereby our own wills are themselves made one.

- *è formale ad esto beato esse* ‘is essential to this blessed being’ → “’tis the essence of this blessed being”: an anticipatory subject is supplied for the following infinitive, and the adjective with its complement becomes a predicate noun with an ‘of’ phrase.
- *tenersi dentro alla divina voglia* ‘to keep oneself within the divine will’ → “to hold ourselves within the divine will”: the indefinite reflexive is made specifically first-person plural.
- *per ch' una fansi nostre voglie stesse* ‘whereby one become our wills themselves’ → “whereby our own wills are themselves made one”: the subject moves before the verb and the predicate “one” to the end. The reflexive verb of becoming is rendered as a passive, and the emphasis on the wills is expressed by both “own” and “themselves”.

> 82 Sì che, come noi sem di soglia in soglia  
> 83 per questo regno, a tutto il regno piace,  
> 84 come allo re ch' a suo voler ne invoglia;  

So that our being thus, from threshold unto threshold throughout the realm, is a joy to all the realm as to the king, who draweth our wills to what he willeth;

- *come noi sem* ‘as we are’ → “our being thus”: the finite clause of manner becomes a gerund phrase, the subject becomes possessive, and “thus” expresses the manner.
- *per questo regno* ‘through this realm’ → “throughout the realm”: “throughout” emphasizes extent, and the definite article replaces the demonstrative.
- *a tutto il regno piace* ‘to all the realm it pleases’ → “is a joy to all the realm”: the verb becomes a copula and predicate noun; the recipient follows the predicate.
- *ch' a suo voler ne invoglia* ‘who to his will inclines us’ → “who draweth our wills to what he willeth”: the personal object becomes “our wills”; the goal moves after the object, and the noun denoting the king’s will becomes a relative clause with a finite verb.

> 85 e la sua volontate è nostra pace:  

and his will is our peace;

- Literal.

> 86 ella è quel mare, al qual tutto si move  
> 87 ciò ch' ella crea e che natura face.”  

it is that sea to which all moves that it createth and that nature maketh.”

- *al qual tutto si move ciò* ‘toward which moves everything’ → “to which all moves”: the extended Italian subject, begun with *tutto* and completed after the verb by *ciò*, is gathered into “all” before the verb; the pronominal verb becomes the simple intransitive “moves”.

> 88 Chiaro mi fu allor com' ogni dove  
> 89 in cielo è Paradiso; e sì la grazia  
> 90 del sommo ben d'un modo non vi piove.  

Clear was it then to me how every where in heaven is Paradise, e’en though the grace of the chief Good doth not rain there after one only fashion.

- *Chiaro mi fu allor* ‘clear to me was then’ → “Clear was it then to me”: the impersonal construction gains an anticipatory “it”, and the experiencer moves after the verb and adverb.
- *e sì* ‘and yet’ → “e’en though”: the adversative coordination is read concessively and becomes subordination.
- *del sommo ben* ‘of the highest good’ → “of the chief Good”: an adjective of preeminence replaces the superlative of height.
- *d'un modo non vi piove* ‘in one manner does not there rain’ → “doth not rain there after one only fashion”: the manner phrase moves to the end and gains the explicit restriction “only”; the locative follows the verb, and English uses an auxiliary for the negation.

## Lines 91–123 — Piccarda’s Vow and Constance’s Veil

> 91 Ma sì com' egli avvien, se un cibo sazia,  
> 92 e d' un altro rimane ancor la gola,  
> 93 che quel si chiede, e di quel si ringrazia;  

But even as it chanceth, should one food sate us while for another the appetite remaineth, that returning thanks for that, we ask for this;

- *sì com’* ‘just as’ → “even as”: an English idiom of comparison replaces the Italian one.
- *se un cibo sazia* ‘if one food sates’ → “should one food sate us”: conditional inversion with “should” replaces the clause with ‘if’; an object is supplied for ‘sates’.
- *e d’ un altro rimane ancor la gola* ‘and for another remains still the throat’ → “while for another the appetite remaineth”: coordination becomes a clause of contrast; the bodily image becomes “the appetite”, which moves before the verb; *ancor* has no separate rendering.
- *che quel si chiede, e di quel si ringrazia* ‘that that is asked for, and for that thanks are given’ → “that returning thanks for that, we ask for this”: the two acts exchange places; the impersonal constructions become personal, with the thanking subordinated as a participle. The repeated demonstrative is differentiated as “that” for the food already had and “this” for the food requested.

> 94 così fec' io con atto e con parola,  
> 95 per apprender da lei qual fu la tela,  
> 96 onde non trasse insino a co' la spola.  

so with gesture and with word did I, to learn from her what was that web through which she had not drawn the shuttle to the end.

- *fec’ io con atto e con parola* ‘did I with act and with word’ → “with gesture and with word did I”: the phrases of manner move before the verb and subject; the outward act is specified as a gesture.
- *la tela* ‘the web’ → “that web”: a demonstrative replaces the article.
- *onde* ‘whereby’ → “through which”: the relative is resolved into an explicitly spatial phrase.
- *non trasse insino a co’ la spola* ‘she drew not as far as the end the shuttle’ → “she had not drawn the shuttle to the end”: the simple past becomes a past perfect; the shuttle comes before the phrase of extent, and ‘as far as’ is reduced to “to”.

> 97 “Perfetta vita ed alto merto inciela  
> 98 donna più su, mi disse, alla cui norma  
> 99 nel vostro mondo giù si veste e vela,  

“Perfected life and high desert enheaveneth a lady more aloft,” she said, “by whose rule down in your world there are who clothe and veil themselves,

- *Perfetta vita* ‘perfect life’ → “Perfected life”: the participial adjective suggests a perfection attained.
- *donna più su* ‘a lady higher up’ → “a lady more aloft”: the comparative of place is expressed with “more” and an adverb of elevation.
- *mi disse* ‘to me she said’ → “she said”: the indirect object is dropped.
- *nel vostro mondo giù* ‘in your world below’ → “down in your world”: the adverb of place moves to the front.
- *si veste e vela* ‘one clothes and veils oneself’ → “there are who clothe and veil themselves”: the generic singular becomes an existential construction with a plural relative clause; the reflexive follows both verbs.

> 100 perchè in fino al morir si vegghi e dorma  
> 101 con quello sposo ch' ogni voto accetta,  
> 102 che caritate a suo piacer conforma.  

that they, even till death, may wake and sleep with that Spouse who accepteth every vow that love hath made conform with his good pleasure.

- *perchè in fino al morir si vegghi e dorma* ‘in order that right up to dying one may wake and sleep’ → “that they, even till death, may wake and sleep”: the impersonal singular of the purpose clause becomes a plural referring back to those who take the veil; the substantivized infinitive becomes “death”.
- *ch’ ogni voto accetta* ‘who every vow accepts’ → “who accepteth every vow”: the object moves after the verb.
- *che caritate a suo piacer conforma* ‘that charity to his pleasure conforms’ → “that love hath made conform with his good pleasure”: “love” renders *caritate*; the present transitive verb becomes a causative phrase in the present perfect, followed rather than preceded by its complement. “Good” is supplied.

> 103 Dal mondo, per seguirla, giovinetta  
> 104 fuggi' mi, e nel suo abito mi chiusi,  
> 105 e promisi la via della sua setta.  

From the world, to follow her, I fled while yet a girl, and in her habit I enclosed myself, and promised the way of her company.

- *giovinetta fuggi’ mi* ‘a young girl, I fled away’ → “I fled while yet a girl”: the description moves after the verb and becomes a temporal phrase; “yet” conveys youth, and the pronominal form of ‘flee’ has no separate English particle.
- *nel suo abito mi chiusi* ‘in her habit myself I enclosed’ → “in her habit I enclosed myself”: the reflexive moves after the verb.
- *della sua setta* ‘of her order’ → “of her company”: a term for a body following a common rule is rendered by a more general collective noun.

> 106 Uomini poi, a mal più ch' al bene usi,  
> 107 fuor mi rapiron della dolce chiostra;  
> 108 e Dio si sa qual poi mia vita fusi.  

Thereafter men more used to ill than good tore me away from the sweet cloister; and God doth know what my life then became.

- *Uomini poi* ‘men then’ → “Thereafter men”: the temporal adverb moves before the subject.
- *a mal più ch’ al bene usi* ‘to ill more than to good accustomed’ → “more used to ill than good”: “more” modifies “used” directly, the adjective precedes its complements, and the second preposition and the article with ‘good’ are not repeated.
- *fuor mi rapiron* ‘out they snatched me’ → “tore me away”: the force of the verb is expressed by “tore”, the object follows it, and the initial directional adverb becomes the final “away”.
- *Dio si sa* ‘God himself knows’ → “God doth know”: emphatic “doth” takes the place of the pronominal emphasis.
- *qual poi mia vita fusi* ‘of what sort then my life was’ → “what my life then became”: “then” moves after the subject, and the past of ‘be’, with its pronominal particle, is interpreted as a change of state.

> 109 E quest' altro splendor, che ti si mostra  
> 110 dalla mia destra parte, e che s' accende  
> 111 di tutto il lume della spera nostra,  
> 112 ciò ch' io dico di me di sè intende.  

And this other splendour who revealeth herself to thee on my right side, and who kindleth herself with all the light of our sphere, doth understand of her that which I tell of me.

- *che ti si mostra* ‘who to thee shows herself’ → “who revealeth herself to thee”: “revealeth” replaces ‘shows’, and both pronouns follow the verb.
- *dalla mia destra parte* ‘at my right side’ → “on my right side”: the preposition of position changes.
- *che s’ accende* ‘who herself kindles’ → “who kindleth herself”: the reflexive moves after the verb.
- *ciò ch’ io dico di me di sè intende* ‘that which I say of myself of herself she understands’ → “doth understand of her that which I tell of me”: the main verb moves from last to first, followed by the phrase referring to the other splendour and then by the object clause. The reflexive reference is expressed by “of her”, and ‘say’ becomes “tell”.

> 113 Sorella fu, e così le fu tolta  
> 114 di capo l' ombra delle sacre bende.  

She was a sister, and from her head was taken in like manner the shadow of the sacred veil.

- *Sorella fu* ‘a sister she was’ → “She was a sister”: the predicate noun moves after the verb.
- *così le fu tolta di capo* ‘thus from her was taken off the head’ → “from her head was taken in like manner”: the dative marking the person affected becomes a possessive modifying “head”; the phrase about the head moves forward, and the adverb of manner becomes a phrase after the verb.
- *delle sacre bende* ‘of the sacred bands’ → “of the sacred veil”: the plural designation for the head covering becomes singular.

> 115 Ma poi che pur al mondo fu rivolta  
> 116 contra suo grado e contra buona usanza,  
> 117 non fu dal vel del cor giammai disciolta.  

Yet, turned back as she was into the world, against her pleasure and against good usage, from her heart’s veil never was she loosened.

- *Ma poi che pur al mondo fu rivolta* ‘but after she was nevertheless turned back to the world’ → “Yet, turned back as she was into the world”: the temporal clause becomes a concessive turn with “as”; “Yet” carries the contrast. The participle comes first, and the destination follows as “into the world”.
- *contra suo grado* ‘against her will’ → “against her pleasure”: inclination is expressed in terms of pleasure rather than will.
- *non fu dal vel del cor giammai disciolta* ‘she was not from the veil of the heart ever loosened’ → “from her heart’s veil never was she loosened”: the prepositional phrase moves to the front; the heart becomes explicitly hers, and its relation to the veil is expressed by a possessive. The separated negative and adverb are combined in “never”, with inversion following it.

> 118 Quest' è la luce della gran Costanza,  
> 119 che del secondo vento di Suave  
> 120 generò il terzo, e l' ultima possanza.”  

This is the light of the great Constance, who, from the second blast of Suabia, conceived the third and final might.”

- *del secondo vento di Suave* ‘of the second wind of Suabia’ → “from the second blast of Suabia”: “from” makes origin explicit, and “blast” is stronger than ‘wind’.
- *generò il terzo, e l’ ultima possanza* ‘bore the third, and the last power’ → “conceived the third and final might”: conception replaces bearing; the second article disappears, so that “third” and “final” jointly modify “might” instead of the latter phrase standing in apposition.

> 121 Così parlommi, e poi cominciò “*Ave,*  
> 122 *Maria,*” cantando; e cantando vanio  
> 123 come per acqua cupa cosa grave.  

Thus did she speak to me, and then began to sing *Ave Maria,* and vanished as she sang, like to a heavy thing through the deep water.

- *parlommi* ‘she spoke to me’ → “did she speak to me”: the simple past becomes an inverted construction with “did”.
- *cominciò “Ave, Maria,” cantando* ‘she began “Ave, Maria,” singing’ → “began to sing *Ave Maria,*”: the gerund following the sung words becomes an infinitive governed by “began” and preceding those words.
- *cantando vanio* ‘singing she vanished’ → “vanished as she sang”: the gerund becomes a finite temporal clause after the main verb.
- *come per acqua cupa cosa grave* ‘like, through deep water, a thing heavy’ → “like to a heavy thing through the deep water”: “like to” introduces the comparison; the thing compared comes before its path, both adjectives move before their nouns, and the water receives a definite article.

## Lines 124–130 — The Gaze Turns to Beatrice

> 124 La vista mia, che tanto la seguio  
> 125 quanto possibil fu, poi che la perse  
> 126 volsesi al segno di maggior disio,  

My sight, which followed her far as it might, when it had lost her turned to the target of a greater longing,

- *La vista mia* ‘the sight of mine’ → “My sight”: the possessive comes before the noun instead of after it.
- *tanto la seguio quanto possibil fu* ‘so far her it followed as was possible’ → “followed her far as it might”: the object pronoun follows the verb, and the expression of extent comes entirely after the object. The impersonal ‘was possible’ becomes a modal construction with sight as its subject.
- *poi che la perse* ‘after it lost her’ → “when it had lost her”: ‘after’ becomes “when”; the past perfect marks the loss as prior to the turn, and the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *volsesi* ‘turned itself’ → “turned”: English uses the verb intransitively, without the reflexive pronoun.
- *di maggior disio* ‘of greater longing’ → “of a greater longing”: the indefinite article is supplied.

> 127 ed a Beatrice tutta si converse;  

and bent itself all upon Beatrice;

- *a Beatrice tutta si converse* ‘to Beatrice all itself it turned’ → “bent itself all upon Beatrice”: the reflexive verb comes first, with its pronoun after it; “all” follows the pronoun instead of preceding it. ‘Turned to’ becomes “bent … upon” in the sense of directing the sight.

> 128 ma quella folgorò nello mio sguardo  
> 129 sì che da prima il viso non sofferse,  

but she so flashed upon my look, that at the first my sight endured it not;

- *quella* ‘that one’ → “she”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *folgorò nello mio sguardo sì* ‘flashed into my gaze so’ → “so flashed upon my look”: the degree adverb moves before the verb, and ‘into’ becomes “upon”.
- *da prima* ‘at first’ → “at the first”: the article is supplied.
- *non sofferse* ‘did not endure’ → “endured it not”: the negative follows the verb, and the object, implicit in Italian, is supplied.

> 130 e ciò mi fece a domandar più tardo.  

and this made me the slower with my questioning.

- *mi fece* ‘me made’ → “made me”: the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *a domandar più tardo* ‘to ask more slow’ → “the slower with my questioning”: the comparative comes before its complement and takes “the”; the infinitive becomes a verbal noun introduced by “with” and qualified by the supplied possessive “my”.

## Summary — Italian Order and English Idiom

Wicksteed often keeps Dante’s words but changes the order in which they reach the reader. The separated *di bella verità … il dolce aspetto* comes together as “fair truth’s sweet aspect”; *dei nostri visi le postille* becomes “the notes of our faces”, with the dependent phrase now following its noun. Objects likewise tend to follow their verbs, as in *nulla vidi*, “I saw naught”. Yet Wicksteed does not regularize every inversion: in “come back the notes of our faces” he keeps the verb before the subject, just as Dante does with *tornan … le postille*.

A change of construction can shift the emphasis even where the sense stays close. The paired actions of *provando e riprovando* become nouns of method in “by proof and refutation”. In *sorridendo ardea negli occhi santi*, it is the guide who burns; in “whose sacred eyes glowed as she smiled”, the eyes become the subject, and the gerund becomes a clause. Elsewhere the difference lies in a single choice of word: *carità* becomes the broader “love”, and *pronta*, ‘ready’, becomes “eager”. The play between *voti*, ‘vows’, and *vòti*, ‘empty’, is lost when the latter becomes “were not filled in”.

Some additions make explicit what the Italian leaves to be understood: *la spera più tarda*, ‘the slowest sphere’, becomes “the sphere that moveth slowest”. Other qualifications drop out, among them *ancor*, ‘yet’, in Beatrice’s remark about trusting one’s foot upon the truth. Two routine adjustments generally pass unmarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending identifies the subject, and omits the Italian article before a possessive, as in *Li nostri affetti*, “Our affections”. Such adjustments are compatible with considerable closeness: “his will is our peace” preserves the nouns, the possessives and the balance of *la sua volontate è nostra pace*.
