# Paradiso 26

## Lines 1–12 — Discourse in Place of Sight

> 1 Mentr' io dubbiava per lo viso spento,  

Whilst I was in suspense concerning my quenched sight,

- *dubbiava* ‘was in doubt’ → “was in suspense”: a verb of doubting becomes a phrase for a state of uncertainty.
- *per lo viso spento* ‘on account of the sight quenched’ → “concerning my quenched sight”: the cause of the uncertainty becomes its subject; the definite article becomes a possessive, and the participle moves before the noun.

> 2 della fulgida fiamma che lo spense  
> 3 uscì un spiro che mi fece attento,  

I was made heedful by a breath that issued from the glowing flame which quenched it,

- *della fulgida fiamma che lo spense* ‘from the shining flame which it quenched’ → “from the glowing flame which quenched it”: the phrase of origin moves from before the verb of issuing to after it; the adjective describes the flame as glowing rather than shining, and the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *uscì un spiro che mi fece attento* ‘there issued a breath that made me attentive’ → “I was made heedful by a breath that issued”: the main and relative clauses exchange functions. The breath becomes the agent of a passive main clause, with the speaker as subject; its issuing is expressed in the relative clause.

> 4 dicendo: “In tanto che tu ti risense  
> 5 della vista che hai in me consunta,  
> 6 ben è che ragionando la compense.  

saying: “Until thou hast again the sense of sight thou hast consumed on me, ’tis well thou compensate it by discourse.

- *In tanto che* ‘in the time that’ → “Until”: the English marks the end of the interval rather than its duration.
- *tu ti risense della vista* ‘thou recover thy sense of sight’ → “thou hast again the sense of sight”: the reflexive verb of recovery is recast as a verb of possession with an adverb; the faculty recovered is named as its direct object.
- *che hai in me consunta* ‘which thou hast on me consumed’ → “thou hast consumed on me”: the object relative is omitted, and the prepositional phrase moves after the participle.
- *ben è che* ‘well it is that’ → “’tis well”: the predicate follows the verb, and the conjunction introducing the subjunctive clause is omitted.
- *ragionando la compense* ‘by discoursing thou compensate it’ → “thou compensate it by discourse”: the object pronoun moves after the verb, and the preceding gerund becomes a following prepositional phrase with a noun.

> 7 Comincia dunque, e di' ove s' appunta  
> 8 l' anima tua, e fa ragion che sia  
> 9 la vista in te smarrita e non defunta;  

Begin then, and declare whereon thy mind is focussed; and assure thee that thy sight within thee is confounded, not destroyed;

- *di’* ‘say’ → “declare”: the English uses a more emphatic verb.
- *ove s’ appunta l’ anima tua* ‘where directs itself thy soul’ → “whereon thy mind is focussed”: the English makes the target explicit in “whereon”, renders the reflexive verb by a passive form, and moves the subject before it. Soul becomes mind, with the possessive before the noun.
- *fa ragion che sia* ‘reckon that there be’ → “assure thee that”: an injunction to consider something so becomes a reflexive injunction to be assured of it; the subjunctive copula is rendered later in the clause by the indicative “is”.
- *la vista in te* ‘the sight in thee’ → “thy sight within thee”: the article becomes a possessive, and “within” gives the preposition a more explicitly inward sense.
- *smarrita e non defunta* ‘lost and not dead’ → “confounded, not destroyed”: the participles of loss and death become terms of confusion and destruction, and the conjunction is omitted.

> 10 perchè la donna, che per questa dia  
> 11 region ti conduce, ha nello sguardo  
> 12 la virtù ch' ebbe la man d' Anania.”  

because the lady who through this divine region doth conduct thee hath in her look the power that was in Ananias’ hand.”

- *ti conduce* ‘thee conducts’ → “doth conduct thee”: the simple verb is expanded with an auxiliary, and the object pronoun moves after the main verb.
- *nello sguardo* ‘in the look’ → “in her look”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *ch’ ebbe la man d’ Anania* ‘that the hand of Ananias had’ → “that was in Ananias’ hand”: the Italian makes the hand the postposed subject of a verb of possession and the relative pronoun its object. The English makes the relative pronoun the subject and the hand the location of the power; the possessor’s name moves before the noun.

## Lines 13–45 — Why the Mind Loves the Highest Good

> 13 Io dissi: “Al suo piacere e tosto e tardo  
> 14 vegna rimedio agli occhi, che fur porte,  
> 15 quand' ella entrò col foco ond' io sempr' ardo.  

I said: “At her good pleasure, soon or late, let succour come to the eyes which were the gates when she did enter with the fire wherewith I ever burn.

- *Al suo piacere* ‘at her pleasure’ → “At her good pleasure”: “good” is supplied in the English idiom.
- *e tosto e tardo* ‘both soon and late’ → “soon or late”: the paired conjunctions become alternatives.
- *vegna rimedio* ‘may remedy come’ → “let succour come”: the subjunctive becomes a construction with “let”; “succour” renders ‘remedy’ as help.
- *che fur porte* ‘which were gates’ → “which were the gates”: the definite article is supplied.
- *ella entrò* ‘she entered’ → “she did enter”: the simple past is expanded with “did”.

> 16 Lo ben, che fa contenta questa corte,  
> 17 Alfa ed Omega è di quanta scrittura  
> 18 mi legge Amore, o lievemente o forte.”  

The good which satisfieth this court is Alpha and Omega of all the scripture which love readeth to me with light or heavy stress.”

- *fa contenta* ‘makes content’ → “satisfieth”: a verb and a predicative adjective become one verb.
- *Alfa ed Omega è* ‘Alpha and Omega is’ → “is Alpha and Omega”: the copula moves before its complement.
- *di quanta scrittura* ‘of as much writing as’ → “of all the scripture”: a construction expressing the full extent of the writing becomes one with “all” and a definite article.
- *mi legge Amore* ‘to me reads Love’ → “love readeth to me”: the subject moves before the verb and the indirect object after it.
- *o lievemente o forte* ‘either lightly or strongly’ → “with light or heavy stress”: the adverbs become adjectives modifying the supplied noun “stress”; the first marker of the alternative drops out, and ‘strongly’ is recast as the opposite of “light”.

> 19 Quella medesma voce, che paura  
> 20 tolta m' avea del subito abbarbaglio  
> 21 di ragionare ancor mi mise in cura;  

That same voice which had removed my terror at the sudden dazzlement, set my concern again upon discourse,

- *paura tolta m' avea* ‘fear taken from me had’ → “had removed my terror”: the auxiliary precedes the participle, ‘taken’ becomes “removed”, and the fear taken from the speaker becomes “my terror”, placed after the verb.
- *del subito abbarbaglio* ‘of the sudden dazzlement’ → “at the sudden dazzlement”: the preposition marks what prompted the terror.
- *di ragionare ancor mi mise in cura* ‘to discourse again put me in care’ → “set my concern again upon discourse”: instead of putting the speaker in a state of concern, the voice sets his concern upon something; the infinitive becomes a noun and moves to the end.

> 22 e disse: “Certo a più angusto vaglio  
> 23 ti conviene schiarar; dicer convienti  
> 24 che drizzò l' arco tuo a tal berzaglio.”  

and said: “Yea, through a closer sieve thou needs must strain; needs must thou tell me what it was that aimed thy bow at such a targe.”

- *Certo* ‘certainly’ → “Yea”: an adverb of assurance becomes an affirmative response.
- *a più angusto vaglio* ‘at a narrower sieve’ → “through a closer sieve”: the English supplies the article and makes the sieve something to pass through; “closer” describes its mesh.
- *ti conviene schiarar* ‘it behoves thee to clarify’ → “thou needs must strain”: the person under the obligation becomes the subject of a modal construction; “strain” takes its sense from the sieve rather than rendering ‘clarify’ directly.
- *dicer convienti* ‘to say behoves thee’ → “needs must thou tell me”: the impersonal obligation again becomes personal, the infinitive moves after it, and “tell” takes the supplied recipient “me”.
- *che drizzò* ‘what directed’ → “what it was that aimed”: the question is expanded with “it was that”, and “aimed” is more specific to the bow.
- *a tal berzaglio* ‘at such target’ → “at such a targe”: the article is supplied, and “targe” gives ‘target’ a more particular English form.

> 25 Ed io: “Per filosofici argomenti,  
> 26 e per autorità che quinci scende,  
> 27 cotale amor convien che in me s' imprenti;  

And I: “By philosophic arguments and by authority which down-cometh hence, such love must needs stamp itself on me;

- *per filosofici argomenti* ‘through philosophical arguments’ → “By philosophic arguments”: the preposition is rendered instrumentally.
- *per autorità* ‘through authority’ → “by authority”: the same instrumental rendering is repeated.
- *quinci scende* ‘from here descends’ → “down-cometh hence”: the indication of origin moves after the verb, and the downward motion is expressed in the compound.
- *cotale amor convien che in me s' imprenti* ‘such love, it is necessary that in me it imprint itself’ → “such love must needs stamp itself on me”: the impersonal verb and subjunctive clause become a modal predicate of “such love”; the reflexive follows “stamp”, and the impression is made “on” rather than ‘in’ the speaker.

> 28 chè il bene, in quanto ben, come s' intende,  
> 29 così accende amore, e tanto maggio,  
> 30 quanto più di bontate in sè comprende.  

for good, as good, so far as understood, kindleth love, and so much more by how much more of excellence it graspeth in itself.

- *il bene* ‘the good’ → “good”: English uses the abstract noun without the article.
- *come s' intende, così accende* ‘as it is understood, so it kindles’ → “so far as understood, kindleth”: the correlative becomes a qualification of degree, with a participle instead of the finite passive construction and no answering ‘so’ before “kindleth”.
- *e tanto maggio, quanto più* ‘and so much greater, the more’ → “and so much more by how much more”: the comparative of size becomes one of amount, and “by” makes the measure of the increase explicit.
- *di bontate in sè comprende* ‘of goodness in itself it contains’ → “of excellence it graspeth in itself”: “excellence” varies ‘goodness’, “graspeth” renders ‘contains’ in the sense of embracing, and the verb moves before the prepositional phrase.

> 31 Dunque all' essenza, ov' è tanto avvantaggio,  
> 32 che ciascun ben che fuor di lei si trova  
> 33 altro non è ch' un lume di suo raggio,  

Therefore to the Essence which hath such privilege that whatsoever good be found outside of it is nought else save a light of its own ray,

- *ov' è tanto avvantaggio* ‘where there is so great a pre-eminence’ → “which hath such privilege”: a locative relative with an existential verb becomes a relative of possession; the degree of pre-eminence is rendered as “such privilege”.
- *ciascun ben* ‘each good’ → “whatsoever good”: distributive ‘each’ becomes an unrestricted relative expression.
- *che fuor di lei si trova* ‘that outside of it is found’ → “be found outside of it”: the relative marker disappears after “whatsoever”, the reflexive construction becomes a subjunctive passive, and the phrase of place follows the verb.
- *altro non è ch'* ‘other it is not than’ → “is nought else save”: the negation is carried by “nought”, the verb moves first, and “save” introduces the exception.
- *di suo raggio* ‘of its ray’ → “of its own ray”: “own” adds emphasis.

> 34 più che in altra convien che si mova  
> 35 la mente, amando, di ciascun che cerne  
> 36 lo vero, in che si fonda questa prova.  

more than to any other must the mind needs move, in love, of whoso doth discern the truth whereon this proof is founded.

- *più che in altra* ‘more than in another’ → “more than to any other”: the preposition expresses direction, and “any” makes the comparison unrestricted.
- *convien che si mova la mente* ‘it is necessary that there move the mind’ → “must the mind needs move”: the impersonal necessity and its subjunctive clause become a modal construction; “the mind” comes before “move”, which needs no reflexive in English.
- *amando* ‘loving’ → “in love”: the gerund becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *di ciascun che cerne* ‘of each one who discerns’ → “of whoso doth discern”: the distributive antecedent and relative become the single general relative “whoso”, and the simple present is expanded with “doth”.
- *lo vero* ‘the true’ → “the truth”: a substantivized adjective becomes a noun.
- *in che si fonda questa prova* ‘in which is founded this proof’ → “whereon this proof is founded”: the foundation is conceived as something ‘on’ rather than ‘in’ which the proof rests; the subject precedes the English passive.

> 37 Tal vero allo intelletto mio sterne  
> 38 colui che mi dimostra il primo amore  
> 39 di tutte le sustanzie sempiterne.  

And this same truth is made level to my intellect by him who doth reveal to me the primal love of all the eternal beings.

- *Tal vero* ‘such truth’ → “And this same truth”: a conjunction is supplied, and ‘such’ becomes an explicit reference back to the same truth.
- *allo intelletto mio sterne colui* ‘to my intellect lays flat he’ → “is made level to my intellect by him”: the active verb becomes a passive with a predicative adjective; its subject becomes the agent after “by”, and the phrase naming the intellect follows the predicate.
- *mi dimostra* ‘to me shows’ → “doth reveal to me”: “reveal” is more specific than ‘show’, the simple verb is expanded with “doth”, and the indirect object follows it.
- *il primo amore* ‘the first love’ → “the primal love”: “primal” gives ‘first’ the sense of primacy.
- *di tutte le sustanzie sempiterne* ‘of all the substances everlasting’ → “of all the eternal beings”: “beings” replaces ‘substances’, and the adjective moves before the noun.

> 40 Sternel la voce del verace autore,  
> 41 che dice a Moisè, di sè parlando:  
> 42 *Io ti farò vedere ogni valore.*  

It is made level to me by the voice of that veracious author who saith to Moses, speaking of himself: *I will cause thee to see all worth.*

- *Sternel la voce* ‘lays it flat the voice’ → “It is made level to me by the voice”: the attached object becomes the subject of a passive, the voice becomes its agent, and “to me” is carried over from the preceding sentence.
- *del verace autore* ‘of the truthful author’ → “of that veracious author”: a demonstrative replaces the definite article.
- *di sè parlando* ‘of himself speaking’ → “speaking of himself”: the complement moves after the participle.
- *ti farò vedere* ‘thee I will make see’ → “I will cause thee to see”: the causative is expressed with “cause” and a *to*-infinitive, with the person caused to see placed between them.
- *ogni valore* ‘every worth’ → “all worth”: “all” treats worth as an undivided quantity rather than distributively.

> 43 Sternilmi tu ancora, cominciando  
> 44 l' alto preconio, che grida l' arcano  
> 45 di qui laggiù sopra ogni altro bando.”  

It is made level to me by thee also, where thou openest the lofty proclamation which doth herald upon earth the secrets of this place above all other declaration.”

- *Sternilmi tu ancora* ‘layest it flat for me thou also’ → “It is made level to me by thee also”: the attached direct object becomes the passive subject, the attached indirect object becomes a prepositional phrase, and the emphatic subject becomes the agent after “by”.
- *cominciando* ‘beginning’ → “where thou openest”: the gerund becomes a finite clause introduced by “where”; “openest” renders the act of beginning.
- *grida* ‘cries out’ → “doth herald”: the verb of crying aloud becomes one of proclamation, expanded with “doth”.
- *l' arcano di qui* ‘the secret of here’ → “the secrets of this place”: the singular becomes plural, and the adverb of place becomes a noun phrase.
- *laggiù* ‘down there’ → “upon earth”: the place indicated is named rather than pointed to, and the phrase moves before the object of “herald”.
- *sopra ogni altro bando* ‘above every other announcement’ → “above all other declaration”: distributive ‘every’ becomes “all”, with “declaration” treated collectively.

## Lines 46–78 — The Sources of Love and the Return of Sight

> 46 Ed io udi': “Per intelletto umano,  
> 47 e per autoritadi a lui concorde,  
> 48 de' tuoi amori a Dio guarda il soprano.  

And I heard: “As urged by human intellect and by authorities concordant with it, of thy loves keep for God the sovereign one.

- *Per intelletto umano* ‘by human intellect’ → “As urged by human intellect”: the translation supplies a participial construction, making intellect the source of an urging.
- *a lui concorde* ‘with it concordant’ → “concordant with it”: the adjective precedes its complement.
- *a Dio guarda il soprano* ‘toward God looks the highest’ → “keep for God the sovereign one”: the translation takes *guarda* as an imperative rather than an indicative, so that the highest love becomes the object of “keep” rather than the subject of ‘looks’. The prepositional phrase follows the verb, and “one” supplies a noun substitute for the substantivized adjective.

> 49 Ma di' ancor, se tu senti altre corde  
> 50 tirarti verso lui, sì che tu suone  
> 51 con quanti denti questo amor ti morde.”  

But tell me yet if thou feel other cords draw thee to-wards him, so that thou utter forth with how many teeth this love doth grip thee.”

- *di' ancor* ‘say yet’ → “tell me yet”: “tell” takes an expressed recipient, absent from the Italian.
- *tirarti* ‘to draw thee’ → “draw thee”: English uses the bare infinitive after the verb of perception.
- *tu suone* ‘thou sound’ → “thou utter forth”: the verb of sounding becomes a phrase of utterance.
- *ti morde* ‘thee bites’ → “doth grip thee”: gripping replaces biting, the object follows the verb, and the present is formed with an auxiliary.

> 52 Non fu latente la santa intenzione  
> 53 dell' aquila di Cristo, anzi m' accorsi  
> 54 dove volea menar mia professione.  

Not hidden was the sacred purpose of Christ’s eagle, but rather I perceived whither he willed to lead on my profession.

- *Non fu latente* ‘was not hidden’ → “Not hidden was”: the negated predicate adjective is put before the verb.
- *la santa intenzione* ‘the holy intention’ → “the sacred purpose”: both adjective and noun are rendered by near equivalents.
- *dell' aquila di Cristo* ‘of the eagle of Christ’ → “of Christ’s eagle”: the inner *di*-phrase becomes a possessive before the noun.
- *m' accorsi* ‘I became aware’ → “I perceived”: a reflexive verb of coming to awareness becomes a simple verb of perception.
- *menar* ‘to lead’ → “to lead on”: the particle adds a sense of onward direction.

> 55 Però ricominciai: “Tutti quei morsi,  
> 56 che posson far lo cor volger a Dio,  
> 57 alla mia caritate son concorsi;  

Wherefore I began again: “All those tooth-grips which have power to make the heart turn unto God co-work upon my love;

- *quei morsi* ‘those bites’ → “those tooth-grips”: a compound substitutes the teeth and their grip for the act of biting.
- *posson* ‘can’ → “have power to”: a modal verb is expanded into a phrase with a noun.
- *alla mia caritate son concorsi* ‘to my charity have concurred’ → “co-work upon my love”: the compound past becomes a simple present; the verb precedes its complement, and concurrence in forming charity becomes joint action “upon” love.

> 58 chè l' essere del mondo, e l' esser mio,  
> 59 la morte ch' ei sostenne perch' io viva,  
> 60 e quel che spera ogni fedel, com' io,  

for the being of the world and my own being, the death that he sustained that I might live, and that which each believer hopeth, as do I,

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *l' esser mio* ‘the being of mine’ → “my own being”: the postposed possessive is brought forward and reinforced by “own”.
- *perch' io viva* ‘in order that I live’ → “that I might live”: the present subjunctive of purpose is rendered with “might” after a past-tense verb.
- *quel che spera ogni fedel* ‘that which hopes every faithful one’ → “that which each believer hopeth”: the subject moves before the verb, distributive “each” renders *ogni*, and the substantivized adjective becomes the noun “believer”.
- *com' io* ‘as I’ → “as do I”: the auxiliary stands for the unexpressed repetition of the verb.

> 61 con la predetta conoscenza viva,  
> 62 tratto m' hanno del mar dell' amor torto,  
> 63 e del diritto m' han posto alla riva.  

together with the aforesaid living consciousness, have drawn me from the sea of the perverted and placed me on the shore of the right love.

- *con* ‘with’ → “together with”: the translation makes the additive force explicit.
- *la predetta conoscenza viva* ‘the aforesaid knowledge living’ → “the aforesaid living consciousness”: “consciousness” replaces ‘knowledge’, and the final adjective moves before the noun.
- *tratto m' hanno* ‘drawn me they have’ → “have drawn me”: the auxiliary precedes the participle and the object follows it.
- *del mar dell' amor torto* ‘from the sea of crooked love’ → “from the sea of the perverted”: “perverted” renders the figurative sense of *torto*, but *amor* is left unexpressed here.
- *e del diritto m' han posto alla riva* ‘and of the right [love] me have placed at the shore’ → “and placed me on the shore of the right love”: the repeated auxiliary is dropped, the object follows the participle, and the *del*-phrase follows “shore” instead of anticipating it. “Love”, explicit in the preceding Italian line, is supplied here instead; “on” replaces ‘at’.

> 64 Le fronde, onde s' infronda tutto l' orto  
> 65 dell' ortolano eterno, am'io cotanto,  
> 66 quanto da lui a lor di bene è porto.”  

The leaves wherewith all the garden of the eternal Gardener is leafed, I love in measure of the good that hath been proffered to them from him.”

- *s' infronda tutto l' orto* ‘clothes itself in leaves the whole garden’ → “all the garden of the eternal Gardener is leafed”: the subject, with its dependent phrase, comes before the verb; a reflexive verb becomes a passive.
- *dell' ortolano eterno* ‘of the Gardener eternal’ → “of the eternal Gardener”: the adjective comes before the noun.
- *am'io cotanto, quanto* ‘love I so much, as’ → “I love in measure of”: subject and verb change places, and a single prepositional phrase replaces the two correlative expressions of degree.
- *da lui a lor di bene è porto* ‘from him to them of good is offered’ → “the good that hath been proffered to them from him”: the partitive *di bene* becomes a definite noun phrase with a relative clause; the present passive becomes a perfect passive, and the two personal complements follow the verb in reverse order.

> 67 Sì com' io tacqui, un dolcissimo canto  
> 68 risonò per lo cielo, e la mia donna  
> 69 dicea con gli altri: “Santo, Santo, Santo!”  

Soon as I held my peace a sweetest song rang through the Heaven, and my Lady with the rest cried: “Holy, Holy, Holy!”

- *io tacqui* ‘I fell silent’ → “I held my peace”: a single verb becomes an idiom with a possessive and a noun.
- *un dolcissimo canto* ‘an exceedingly sweet song’ → “a sweetest song”: the Italian absolute superlative is reproduced with an English superlative in *-est* rather than an expression of degree.
- *risonò* ‘resounded’ → “rang”: the translation uses the simpler verb of sounding.
- *dicea con gli altri* ‘was saying with the others’ → “with the rest cried”: the accompanying phrase moves before the verb, “the rest” replaces ‘the others’, and the imperfect of saying becomes a simple past with the stronger sense of calling out.

> 70 E come a lume acuto si dissonna  
> 71 per lo spirto visivo che ricorre  
> 72 allo splendor che va di gonna in gonna,  

And as at a keen light one wakeneth from slumber by reason of the visual spirit which runneth to meet the glow that pierceth tunic after tunic,

- *a lume acuto* ‘at light sharp’ → “at a keen light”: an article is supplied and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *si dissonna* ‘one awakes’ → “one wakeneth from slumber”: the impersonal construction takes “one” as subject, and the state left behind, implicit in the verb, is named separately.
- *per lo spirto visivo* ‘through the spirit visual’ → “by reason of the visual spirit”: the preposition is expanded into an explicitly causal phrase and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *ricorre allo splendor* ‘runs back to the radiance’ → “runneth to meet the glow”: a phrase of meeting replaces the sense of return in the verb, and “glow” renders the noun of brightness.
- *va di gonna in gonna* ‘goes from tunic to tunic’ → “pierceth tunic after tunic”: passing from one to the next becomes piercing each in succession; the tunics become direct objects.

> 73 e lo svegliato ciò che vede abborre,  
> 74 sì nescia è la sua subita vigilia,  
> 75 fin che l' estimativa nol soccorre;  

and he thus awakened confoundeth what he seeth, so undiscerning is his sudden vigil until reflection cometh to its succour;

- *lo svegliato* ‘the awakened one’ → “he thus awakened”: the substantivized participle becomes a pronoun qualified by a participle, with “thus” supplied.
- *ciò che vede abborre* ‘what he sees he confuses’ → “confoundeth what he seeth”: the main verb moves before its object; “confoundeth” takes *abborre* in the sense of confused perception.
- *sì nescia è la sua subita vigilia* ‘so unknowing is his sudden wakefulness’ → “so undiscerning is his sudden vigil”: “undiscerning” specifies the kind of unknowing, and “vigil” renders the state of being awake.
- *fin che l' estimativa nol soccorre* ‘until the estimative faculty does not aid him’ → “until reflection cometh to its succour”: English omits the anticipatory negative after ‘until’; “reflection” replaces the name of a faculty, and the direct verb of aid becomes a phrase with a possessive referring to the vigil rather than to the awakened man.

> 76 così degli occhi miei ogni quisquilia  
> 77 fugò Beatrice col raggio de' suoi,  
> 78 che rifulgean da più di mille milia;  

so from mine eyes did Beatrice dissipate every scale with the ray of hers that might cast their glow more than a thousand miles;

- *ogni quisquilia fugò Beatrice* ‘every particle of refuse drove away Beatrice’ → “did Beatrice dissipate every scale”: the Italian order is object–verb–subject; English puts the subject between an auxiliary and the main verb and the object last. “Dissipate” replaces ‘drive away’, and “scale” gives the particle a specifically ocular sense.
- *che rifulgean da più di mille milia* ‘which shone from more than a thousand miles’ → “that might cast their glow more than a thousand miles”: the indicative imperfect becomes a modal expression of capacity; shining becomes casting a glow, and a distance reckoned with ‘from’ becomes the reach of that glow.

## Lines 79–111 — Dante Addresses Adam

> 79 onde, me' che dinanzi, vidi poi,  

whence better than before I saw thereafter,

- Literal.

> 80 e quasi stupefatto domandai  
> 81 d'un quarto lume, ch' io vidi con noi.  

and as one stupified, made question as to a fourth light which I perceived with us.

- *quasi stupefatto* ‘as if stupefied’ → “as one stupified”: “one” is supplied to carry the comparison.
- *domandai d'un quarto lume* ‘I asked about a fourth light’ → “made question as to a fourth light”: the verb of asking becomes a verb and noun, and the subject is carried over from the preceding clause.
- *vidi* ‘I saw’ → “I perceived”: the verb of sight is varied rather than repeated as in the Italian.

> 82 E la mia donna: “Dentro da que' rai  
> 83 vagheggia il suo fattor l' anima prima,  
> 84 che la prima virtù creasse mai.”  

And my Lady: “Within those rays holdeth amorous converse with its maker the first soul that the first Power e’er created.”

- *vagheggia il suo fattor* ‘gazes lovingly on its maker’ → “holdeth amorous converse with its maker”: a verb of loving contemplation with a direct object becomes a phrase suggesting mutual intercourse, with the maker introduced by “with”.
- *l' anima prima* ‘the soul first’ → “the first soul”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *che la prima virtù creasse mai* ‘that the first power ever created’ → “that the first Power e’er created”: the Italian subjunctive after the expression of precedence has no distinct equivalent in the English past tense, and the adverb moves before the verb.

> 85 Come la fronda, che flette la cima  
> 86 nel transito del vento, e poi si leva  
> 87 per la propria virtù che la sublima,  

As the spray which bendeth down its head as the wind passeth over, and doth then uplift itself by its own power which doth raise it up,

- *flette la cima* ‘bends the tip’ → “bendeth down its head”: the direction is explicit, the article becomes a possessive, and the tip of the spray is called its “head”.
- *nel transito del vento* ‘in the passage of the wind’ → “as the wind passeth over”: the prepositional phrase becomes a temporal clause, with the wind as subject; “over” gives the direction of passage.
- *poi si leva* ‘then raises itself’ → “doth then uplift itself”: an auxiliary is introduced before the adverb, and the reflexive follows the main verb.
- *la propria virtù* ‘the power of its own’ → “its own power”: the possessive expression is placed before the noun in place of the article.
- *la sublima* ‘lifts it high’ → “doth raise it up”: the single verb is expanded with an auxiliary and a particle, and the object follows “raise”.

> 88 fec' io in tanto in quanto ella diceva,  
> 89 stupendo; e poi mi rifece sicuro  
> 90 un disio di parlare, ond' io ardeva;  

did I, whilst she was speaking, all bemazed; and then was reassured by a desire to speak, wherewith I was a-burning;

- *in tanto in quanto ella diceva* ‘for so long as she spoke’ → “whilst she was speaking”: the paired expressions of duration become one conjunction, and the imperfect is rendered by the progressive.
- *stupendo* ‘marvelling’ → “all bemazed”: the active gerund becomes a description of a state, intensified by “all”.
- *mi rifece sicuro un disio di parlare* ‘made me secure again a desire to speak’ → “was reassured by a desire to speak”: what was the object is now the understood subject of a passive; the postposed Italian subject becomes the agent introduced by “by”.
- *ond' io ardeva* ‘with which I burned’ → “wherewith I was a-burning”: the imperfect becomes a progressive expression.

> 91 e cominciai: “O pomo, che maturo  
> 92 solo prodotto fosti, o padre antico,  
> 93 a cui ciascuna sposa è figlia e nuro;  

and I began: “O fruit, who wast alone produced mature, O ancient father who hast both daughter and daughter-in-law in every bride;

- *pomo* ‘apple’ → “fruit”: the English takes the noun in its broader sense.
- *che maturo solo prodotto fosti* ‘who, mature, alone produced wast’ → “who wast alone produced mature”: the passive auxiliary moves forward, and the adjective describing the fruit at its production moves to the end.
- *padre antico* ‘father ancient’ → “ancient father”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *a cui ciascuna sposa è figlia e nuro* ‘to whom every bride is daughter and daughter-in-law’ → “who hast both daughter and daughter-in-law in every bride”: the relative becomes the subject of a verb of possession; the Italian subject becomes a final phrase with “in”, and “both” is supplied.

> 94 devoto, quanto posso, a te supplico  
> 95 perchè mi parli: tu vedi mia voglia,  
> 96 e, per udirti tosto, non la dico.”  

devoutly as I may do I implore thee that thou speak to me; thou seest my will, and to hear thee the sooner I not utter it.”

- *devoto, quanto posso* ‘devout, as much as I can’ → “devoutly as I may”: an adjective describing the speaker becomes an adverb modifying his plea, and degree is expressed by the English comparison.
- *a te supplico* ‘to thee I make supplication’ → “do I implore thee”: the addressee becomes a direct object after the verb, and the auxiliary precedes the subject after the introductory adverbial phrase.
- *perchè mi parli* ‘that to me thou speak’ → “that thou speak to me”: the indirect object follows the verb.
- *voglia* ‘desire’ → “will”: the rendering emphasizes what is willed rather than the feeling of desire.
- *per udirti tosto* ‘in order to hear thee soon’ → “to hear thee the sooner”: the infinitive alone marks purpose, and the adverb becomes comparative.
- *non la dico* ‘I do not say it’ → “I not utter it”: “utter” replaces ‘say’, and the pronominal object moves after the verb.

> 97 Tal volta un animal coperto broglia  
> 98 sì che l' affetto convien che si paia  
> 99 per lo seguir che face a lui l' invoglia;  

Sometimes an animal swayeth beneath a covering so that its impulse must needs be apparent, since what envelopeth it followeth its movements;

- *un animal coperto broglia* ‘a covered animal stirs’ → “an animal swayeth beneath a covering”: the participle becomes a phrase of position after the verb, and the stirring is specified as swaying.
- *l' affetto convien che si paia* ‘the feeling, it is necessary that it show itself’ → “its impulse must needs be apparent”: the article becomes a possessive, the feeling is construed as an impulse, and the impersonal necessity governing a subjunctive becomes a modal expression with that impulse as subject.
- *per lo seguir che face a lui l' invoglia* ‘through the following that the wrapping does of it’ → “since what envelopeth it followeth its movements”: the causal preposition and nominal infinitive become a causal clause; the wrapping becomes a relative expression placed before the verb, and the thing followed is specified as the animal’s movements rather than the animal itself.

> 100 e similmente l' anima primaia  
> 101 mi facea trasparer per la coperta  
> 102 quant' ella a compiacermi venia gaia.  

and in like manner that first soul made appear through its covering with what elation it advanced to do me pleasure.

- *l' anima primaia* ‘the soul first’ → “that first soul”: the article becomes demonstrative and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *mi facea trasparer* ‘made shine through to me’ → “made appear”: the recipient is left unstated, and the optical verb is generalized to one of appearance.
- *per la coperta* ‘through the covering’ → “through its covering”: a possessive replaces the article.
- *quant' ella a compiacermi venia gaia* ‘how gladly it came to please me’ → “with what elation it advanced to do me pleasure”: the degree of the soul’s gladness becomes an opening phrase with an abstract noun; ‘came’ becomes “advanced”, and the purpose infinitive moves after the verb and expands into a phrase with “pleasure”.

> 103 Indi spirò: “Senz' essermi profferta  
> 104 da te, la voglia tua discerno meglio  
> 105 che tu qualunque cosa t' è più certa;  

And from it breathed: “Though not set forth to me by thee, I better do discern thy will than thou the thing which is most certain to thee,

- *Indi spirò* ‘thence it breathed’ → “And from it breathed”: “And” is supplied, and the adverb of source is expanded into a prepositional phrase.
- *Senz' essermi profferta da te* ‘without being put forth to me by thee’ → “Though not set forth to me by thee”: the preposition with a passive infinitive becomes a concessive phrase with a negated participle.
- *la voglia tua discerno meglio* ‘thy desire I discern better’ → “I better do discern thy will”: the object moves after the verb, the possessive before its noun, and the comparative before the supplied auxiliary; ‘desire’ becomes “will”.
- *qualunque cosa t' è più certa* ‘whatever thing is to thee most certain’ → “the thing which is most certain to thee”: the indefinite gives way to a definite noun with a relative clause, and the person to whom it is certain is placed last.

> 106 perch' io la veggio nel verace speglio  
> 107 che fa di sè pareglio all' altre cose,  
> 108 e nulla face lui di sè pareglio.  

because I see it in the veracious Mirror which doth make himself reflector of all other things, and nought doth make itself reflector unto him.

- *la veggio* ‘it I see’ → “I see it”: the object follows the verb.
- *fa di sè pareglio all' altre cose* ‘makes of itself a likeness to the other things’ → “doth make himself reflector of all other things”: the reflexive becomes a personal direct object, and ‘likeness to’ is interpreted in terms of reflection; “all” is supplied.
- *nulla face lui di sè pareglio* ‘nothing makes him a likeness of itself’ → “nought doth make itself reflector unto him”: on a direct-object reading of *lui*, the Mirror is what nothing can make a likeness of itself. The translation instead takes *lui* as ‘unto him’ and makes the reflexive the object, parallel to “make himself” in the preceding clause.

> 109 Tu vuoi saper quant' è che Dio mi pose  
> 110 nell' eccelso giardino, ove costei  
> 111 a così lunga scala ti dispose,  

Thou wouldst know how long the time since God placed me in the uplifted garden wherein she there prepared thee for so long a stair,

- *vuoi saper* ‘wish to know’ → “wouldst know”: the verb of wishing with an infinitive becomes a modal construction.
- *quant' è che* ‘how long it is since’ → “how long the time since”: “the time” makes the duration explicit, but there is no expressed verb corresponding to ‘is’.
- *mi pose* ‘me placed’ → “placed me”: the object follows the verb.
- *eccelso* ‘lofty’ → “uplifted”: a description of height becomes participial in form.
- *costei* ‘this woman’ → “she there”: the demonstrative pronoun becomes a personal pronoun with a separate pointing adverb.
- *a così lunga scala ti dispose* ‘for so long a stair thee prepared’ → “prepared thee for so long a stair”: the object pronoun follows the verb, and the phrase naming what the preparation was for moves to the end.

## Lines 112–142 — Adam on Exile, Time, and Speech

> 112 e quanto fu diletto agli occhi miei,  

and how long the delight endured unto my eyes,

- *quanto fu diletto* ‘how long it was a delight’ → “how long the delight endured”: the predicate noun becomes the subject, with a definite article, and the verb of duration takes the place of the copula.
- *agli occhi miei* ‘unto the eyes of mine’ → “unto my eyes”: the possessive comes before the noun rather than after it.

> 113 e la propria cagion del gran disdegno,  

and the true cause of the great indignation,

- *propria* ‘proper, actual’ → “true”: the adjective is rendered in the sense of the actual, as distinct from the supposed, cause.

> 114 e l' idioma ch' usai e ch' io fei.  

and the idiom which I used and which myself composed.

- *ch' io fei* ‘which I made’ → “which myself composed”: the repeated subject pronoun becomes emphatic, and the general verb of making becomes a verb of composition.

> 115 Or, figliuol mio, non il gustar del legno  
> 116 fu per sè la cagion di tanto esilio,  
> 117 ma solamente il trapassar del segno.  

Now know, my son, that not the tasting of the tree was in itself the cause of so great exile, but only the transgressing of the mark.

- *Or, figliuol mio* ‘Now, son of mine’ → “Now know, my son, that”: the possessive moves before the noun; an imperative and a conjunction are supplied, making the ensuing assertion its object.
- *gustar del legno* ‘tasting of the wood’ → “tasting of the tree”: the word for wood is rendered by the living tree it designates here.
- *per sè* ‘by itself’ → “in itself”: the preposition changes.
- *tanto esilio* ‘so much exile’ → “so great exile”: quantity is expressed as degree.
- *il trapassar del segno* ‘the going beyond the mark’ → “the transgressing of the mark”: the verb of passing beyond a limit becomes a verb of transgression, with the mark as its object.

> 118 Quindi, onde mosse tua donna Virgilio,  
> 119 quattromila trecento e due volumi  
> 120 di sol desiderai questo concilio;  

From that place whence thy Lady dispatched Virgil, four thousand three hundred and two revolutions of the sun went out my longing for this gathering;

- *Quindi* ‘thence’ → “From that place”: the adverb is expanded into a prepositional phrase with an explicit noun.
- *mosse tua donna Virgilio* ‘set in motion thy Lady Virgil’ → “thy Lady dispatched Virgil”: the subject moves before the verb, and causing someone to move is rendered as sending him.
- *volumi di sol* ‘turns of sun’ → “revolutions of the sun”: the noun is rendered in its sense of circular courses, and the definite article is supplied before the sun.
- *desiderai questo concilio* ‘I desired this assembly’ → “went out my longing for this gathering”: the finite verb becomes a possessive noun phrase, its direct object becomes the object of “for”, and “went out” is supplied as the verb of motion answering to “From that place”.

> 121 e vidi lui tornare a tutti i lumi  
> 122 della sua strada novecento trenta  
> 123 fiate, mentre ch' io in terra fu' mi.  

and I beheld him course through all the lights of his path nine hundred times and thirty whilst I abode on earth.

- *tornare a* ‘return to’ → “course through”: repeated arrival at the lights becomes motion through them.
- *strada* ‘road’ → “path”: a different word for the course is chosen.
- *novecento trenta fiate* ‘nine hundred thirty times’ → “nine hundred times and thirty”: the noun moves inside the numeral, and a conjunction is supplied.
- *mentre ch' io in terra fu' mi* ‘while I on earth was’ → “whilst I abode on earth”: the conjunction is rendered by one word, the verb of being by one of abiding, and the place phrase moves after it; the pronominal particle *mi* has no separate English word.

> 124 La lingua ch' io parlai fu tutta spenta  
> 125 innanzi assai ch' all' opra inconsumabile  
> 126 fosse la gente di Nembrot attenta;  

The tongue I spoke was all quenched long ere the work that ne’er might be completed was undertaken by the folk of Nimrod;

- *ch' io parlai* ‘which I spoke’ → “I spoke”: the object relative is unexpressed in English.
- *innanzi assai* ‘before by much’ → “long ere”: the measure of time comes before the conjunction.
- *all' opra inconsumabile fosse la gente di Nembrot attenta* ‘to the unfinishable work the people of Nimrod was intent’ → “the work that ne’er might be completed was undertaken by the folk of Nimrod”: the adjective becomes a modal relative clause. The work, rather than the people, becomes the subject; being intent on it is recast as undertaking it, in the passive, with the people as agent.

> 127 chè nullo effetto mai razionabile,  
> 128 per lo piacere uman, che rinnovella,  
> 129 seguendo il cielo, sempre fu durabile.  

for never yet did product of the reason maintain itself for ever, because of human preference which doth change in sequence with the heaven.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the explanatory clause is introduced by a coordinating conjunction.
- *nullo effetto mai razionabile* ‘no rational product ever’ → “never yet did product of the reason”: the negative passes from the noun to the fronted adverb, “yet” is supplied, and inversion brings in “did”; the adjective becomes a phrase naming the faculty that produces the effect.
- *per lo piacere uman* ‘because of the human pleasure’ → “because of human preference”: pleasure is construed as preference, the definite article disappears, and the causal phrase is deferred until after the main predicate.
- *che rinnovella* ‘which renews itself’ → “which doth change”: renewal is rendered more generally as change, with an auxiliary.
- *seguendo il cielo* ‘following the heaven’ → “in sequence with the heaven”: the gerund with a direct object becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *sempre fu durabile* ‘always was lasting’ → “maintain itself for ever”: copula and adjective become a reflexive verbal phrase dependent on the earlier auxiliary; the expression of duration follows it.

> 130 Opera naturale è ch' uom favella;  

That man should speak is nature’s doing;

- *Opera naturale* ‘a natural work’ → “nature’s doing”: the describing adjective becomes a possessive naming the doer, and the predicate moves from the beginning to the end.
- *ch' uom favella* ‘that man speaks’ → “That man should speak”: the subject clause moves to the beginning, and the simple indicative becomes a construction with “should”.

> 131 ma, così o così, natura lascia  
> 132 poi fare a voi secondo che v' abbella.  

but thus or thus nature permitteth to you as best seemeth you.

- *lascia poi fare a voi* ‘then leaves the doing to you’ → “permitteth to you”: permission is expressed by a single verb; neither the infinitive denoting the action nor the adverb marking the next step is separately rendered.
- *secondo che v' abbella* ‘according as it pleases you’ → “as best seemeth you”: pleasure becomes a judgment of what seems best, with a superlative not expressed in Italian.

> 133 Pria ch' io scendessi all' infernale ambascia,  
> 134 *J* s' appellava in terra il sommo bene,  
> 135 onde vien la letizia che mi fascia;  

Ere I descended to the infernal anguish, *J* was the name on earth of that supreme good whence cometh the gladness that doth swathe me;

- *J s' appellava in terra il sommo bene* ‘J was called on earth the highest good’ → “J was the name on earth of that supreme good”: in Italian the good is the subject and the initial letter its appellation. English makes the letter the subject, supplies the predicate noun “the name”, and attaches the good to it with “of”; the definite article becomes demonstrative.
- *che mi fascia* ‘that me swathes’ → “that doth swathe me”: an auxiliary is used and the object follows the verb.

> 136 *El* si chiamò da poi, e ciò conviene,  
> 137 chè l' uso de' mortali è come fronda  
> 138 in ramo, che sen va ed altra viene.  

*El* was he called thereafter; and this is fitting, for the use of mortals is as the leaf upon the branch which goeth and another followeth.

- *El si chiamò* ‘El it was called’ → “El was he called”: the pronominal construction becomes a passive with a personal pronoun for the good.
- *ciò conviene* ‘this befits’ → “this is fitting”: the finite verb becomes copula and adjective.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: coordination replaces subordination.
- *come fronda in ramo* ‘as leaf on branch’ → “as the leaf upon the branch”: both generic nouns acquire definite articles.
- *sen va* ‘goes away’ → “goeth”: departure is left to the verb alone.
- *altra viene* ‘another comes’ → “another followeth”: coming is expressed in terms of succession.

> 139 Nel monte, che si leva più dall' onda,  
> 140 fu' io, con vita pura, e disonesta,  
> 141 dalla prim' ora a quella che seconda,  
> 142 come il sol muta quadra, l' ora sesta.”  

On the mount which most doth rise from out the wave was I, with life pure and disgraced, from the first hour to that which followeth, when the sun changeth quadrant, next on the sixth hour.”

- *Nel monte* ‘in the mount’ → “On the mount”: English uses a preposition of surface.
- *si leva più* ‘raises itself most’ → “most doth rise”: the pronominal verb becomes an intransitive with an auxiliary, and the degree adverb moves before it.
- *dall' onda* ‘from the wave’ → “from out the wave”: “out” makes emergence explicit.
- *disonesta* ‘dishonourable’ → “disgraced”: a quality is rendered as a resulting condition.
- *seconda … l' ora sesta* ‘follows … the hour sixth’ → “followeth” … “next on the sixth hour”: “next on” makes immediate succession explicit, and the ordinal moves before the noun.
- *come il sol muta quadra* ‘as the sun changes quadrant’ → “when the sun changeth quadrant”: the conjunction is rendered in its temporal sense.

## Summary — Close Images, Freer Syntax

Wicksteed often keeps Dante’s images while changing the order in which their parts appear. In *e del diritto m’ han posto alla riva*, he keeps the shore but places “of the right love” after “the shore”, where the relation is easier to grasp in English. He does not always smooth out an inversion: in “Not hidden was the sacred purpose”, the subject still follows the verb. Two routine changes generally go unmarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending gives the person, and it drops the article before a possessive, as when *la mia donna* becomes “my Lady”.

Some changes of construction shift the emphasis. In *uscì un spiro che mi fece attento*, the breath’s issuing is the main action; Wicksteed begins instead with its effect, “I was made heedful”, and leaves the issuing to a relative clause. The impersonal *convien che* repeatedly gives way to a personal construction with “must needs”. A change can also preserve a pattern: Wicksteed turns the active forms of *sternere* into passives but repeats “is made level” as Dante repeats the verb.

Word choice ranges from close correspondence to interpretation. *Ragionando* becomes “by discourse”, preserving the sense while exchanging a gerund for a noun; *laggiù*, ‘down there’, becomes “upon earth”, naming what Dante only points to. In *o lievemente o forte*, the supplied noun “stress” gives the two adverbs something to qualify in English. Elsewhere a word drops out: *poi* has no separate equivalent in “permitteth to you”. Yet even where the grammar changes, the wording can remain strikingly close: “The leaves” and “is leafed” reproduce the kinship of *le fronde* and *s’ infronda*, keeping an echo that a more idiomatic paraphrase could easily lose.
