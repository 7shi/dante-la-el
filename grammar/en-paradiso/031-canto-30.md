# Paradiso 30

## Lines 1–15 — The Fading Triumph and the Turn to Beatrice

> 1 Forse sei mila miglia di lontano  
> 2 ci ferve l'ora sesta, e questo mondo  
> 3 china già l'ombra, quasi al letto piano,  

Perchance six thousand miles away from us blazeth the noon, and this world already slopeth its shadow as to a level couch,

- *sei mila miglia di lontano ci* ‘six thousand miles afar, to us’ → “six thousand miles away from us”: “away from us” makes *ci* the point from which the distance is reckoned, rather than leaving it as a pronoun before the verb.
- *ferve* ‘boils’ → “blazeth”: a verb of heat is rendered by one that also suggests light.
- *l'ora sesta* ‘the hour sixth’ → “the noon”: the numbered hour is replaced by its name.
- *china già l'ombra* ‘inclines already the shadow’ → “already slopeth its shadow”: the adverb moves before the verb, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *quasi al letto piano* ‘as if to the bed level’ → “as to a level couch”: the comparison is compressed to “as”; the bed becomes an indefinite “couch”, with the adjective before the noun.

> 4 quando il mezzo del cielo, a noi profondo,  
> 5 comincia a farsi tal, che alcuna stella  
> 6 perde il parere infino a questo fondo;  

when the midst of heaven deep above us, beginneth to grow such that here and there a star loseth power to shine down to this floor;

- *a noi profondo* ‘to us deep’ → “deep above us”: the adjective comes first, and “above” gives a spatial direction to what the Italian expresses as a relation to us.
- *a farsi tal* ‘to make itself such’ → “to grow such”: the reflexive construction becomes a verb of becoming.
- *alcuna stella* ‘some star’ → “here and there a star”: the indefinite adjective is expanded into a distributive phrase.
- *il parere* ‘the appearing’ → “power to shine”: the substantivized infinitive denotes being visible; the English supplies “power” and shifts from appearing to shining.
- *infino a questo fondo* ‘as far as this bottom’ → “down to this floor”: “down” makes the direction explicit, and “floor” gives a more concrete equivalent for the bottom.

> 7 e come vien la chiarissima ancella  
> 8 del sol più oltre, così il ciel si chiude  
> 9 di vista in vista infino alla più bella.  

and as the brightest handmaid of the Sun advanceth, so doth the heaven close up sight after sight even till the most fair.

- *vien … più oltre* ‘comes … farther on’ → “advanceth”: the verb and its adverbial phrase become one verb, placed after the whole subject.
- *la chiarissima ancella* ‘the very bright handmaid’ → “the brightest handmaid”: the intensive superlative is rendered as a relative superlative.
- *così il ciel si chiude* ‘so the heaven closes itself’ → “so doth the heaven close up”: the auxiliary moves before the subject; the reflexive gives way to “close up”, which takes the successive sights as its objects.
- *di vista in vista* ‘from sight to sight’ → “sight after sight”: a phrase marking passage from one to the next becomes a sequence of direct objects.
- *infino alla più bella* ‘as far as the fairest’ → “even till the most fair”: the limit is expressed by “even till”, with the superlative given in two words.

> 10 Non altrimenti il trionfo, che lude  
> 11 sempre dintorno al punto che mi vinse,  
> 12 parendo inchiuso da quel ch' egl' inchiude,  
> 13 a poco a poco al mio veder si estinse;  

Not otherwise the triumph which ever sporteth round the point which vanquished me, seeming embraced by that which it embraceth, little by little quenched itself from my sight;

- *lude sempre* ‘sports ever’ → “ever sporteth”: the adverb moves before the verb.
- *mi vinse* ‘me vanquished’ → “vanquished me”: the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *inchiuso da quel ch' egl' inchiude* ‘enclosed by that which it encloses’ → “embraced by that which it embraceth”: the English preserves the repetition but substitutes embracing for enclosing.
- *al mio veder si estinse* ‘to my seeing it went out’ → “quenched itself from my sight”: the pronominal verb of extinction becomes an active verb with a reflexive object; the phrase qualifying the extinction moves after the verb, with “from” instead of ‘to’ and the noun “sight” instead of the substantivized infinitive.

> 14 per che tornar con gli occhi a Beatrice  
> 15 nulla vedere ed amor mi costrinse.  

wherefore my seeing nought, and love, constrained me to turn with mine eyes to Beatrice.

- *per che* ‘for which reason’ → “wherefore”: the prepositional relative phrase becomes a single connective.
- *tornar con gli occhi a Beatrice* ‘to turn back with the eyes to Beatrice’ → “to turn with mine eyes to Beatrice”: the infinitive phrase moves from before the subject to after the governing verb; “turn” leaves the sense of return unexpressed, and “mine” replaces the definite article.
- *nulla vedere* ‘nothing to see’ → “my seeing nought”: the infinitive used as a subject becomes a gerund with an explicit possessive, and its object moves after it.
- *mi costrinse* ‘me constrained’ → “constrained me”: the object pronoun moves after the verb; the Italian verb is marked as singular despite its compound subject, a distinction the English past tense does not show.

## Lines 16–48 — Beauty Beyond Song and Entry into Pure Light

> 16 Se quanto infino a qui di lei si dice  
> 17 fosse conchiuso tutto in una loda,  
> 18 poca sarebbe a fornir questa vice.  

If that which up till here is said of her were all compressed into one act of praise ’twould be too slight to serve this present turn.

- *quanto* ‘all that’ → “that which”: the relative is expanded, without the sense of quantity in *quanto*.
- *di lei si dice* ‘of her one says’ → “is said of her”: the Italian *si* construction becomes an English passive, with “of her” after the verb.
- *fosse conchiuso tutto* ‘were comprised all together’ → “were all compressed”: “all” moves before the participle, and “compressed” stresses the gathering into little space.
- *in una loda* ‘in one praise’ → “into one act of praise”: “into” marks the result, and “act of” makes *loda* countable in English.
- *poca sarebbe* ‘little would it be’ → “’twould be too slight”: the predicate moves after the verb; “too” makes the insufficiency explicit.
- *a fornir questa vice* ‘to suffice for this occasion’ → “to serve this present turn”: “serve” takes “turn” as its object, and “present” amplifies “this”.

> 19 La bellezza ch' io vidi si trasmoda  
> 20 non pur di là da noi, ma certo io credo  
> 21 che solo il suo fattor tutta la goda.  

The beauty I beheld transcendeth measure, not only past our reach, but surely I believe that only he who made it enjoyeth it complete.

- *ch' io vidi* ‘that I saw’ → “I beheld”: the object relative is omitted, and “beheld” replaces ‘saw’.
- *si trasmoda* ‘goes beyond measure’ → “transcendeth measure”: the pronominal verb becomes a transitive verb with an explicit object.
- *di là da noi* ‘beyond us’ → “past our reach”: the persons become possessors of a supplied “reach”.
- *il suo fattor* ‘its maker’ → “he who made it”: the agent noun becomes a relative clause, its possessive becoming the object of “made”.
- *tutta la goda* ‘wholly it enjoys’ → “enjoyeth it complete”: the object follows the verb, and *tutta*, agreeing with the object, is placed last as “complete”. The subjunctive after *credo* becomes an indicative.

> 22 Da questo passo vinto mi concedo,  
> 23 più che giammai da punto di suo tema  
> 24 suprato fosse comico o tragedo.  

At this pass I yield me vanquished more than e’er yet was overborn by his theme’s thrust comic or tragic poet.

- *Da questo passo vinto mi concedo* ‘by this pass vanquished I grant myself’ → “At this pass I yield me vanquished”: “At” makes the pass the point of defeat rather than its agent; the participle moves after “I yield me”.
- *giammai* ‘ever’ → “e’er yet”: “yet” is added.
- *da punto di suo tema suprato fosse* ‘by a point of his theme overcome might be’ → “was overborn by his theme’s thrust”: the passive precedes its agent, and the subjunctive in the comparison becomes indicative. “thrust” takes *punto* in the sense of a stroke rather than a particular point or difficulty; the possessive replaces the *di* phrase.
- *comico o tragedo* ‘comic poet or tragedian’ → “comic or tragic poet”: two nouns for poets become adjectives sharing one noun.

> 25 Chè, come sole in viso che più trema,  
> 26 così lo rimembrar del dolce riso  
> 27 la mente mia di sè medesma scema.  

For as the Sun in sight that most trembleth, so the remembrance of the sweet smile sheareth my memory of its very self.

- *in viso* ‘upon the sight’ → “in sight”: the preposition expresses the sun’s presence in the sight rather than its action upon it.
- *lo rimembrar* ‘the remembering’ → “the remembrance”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun.
- *la mente mia di sè medesma scema* ‘my mind of its very self diminishes’ → “sheareth my memory of its very self”: the verb moves from the end to precede its object; “sheareth” gives the diminution the force of a cutting away. “memory” narrows ‘mind’, and the possessive precedes its noun.

> 28 Dal primo giorno ch' io vidi il suo viso  
> 29 in questa vita, infino a questa vista,  
> 30 non m' è il seguire al mio cantar preciso;  

From the first day when in this life I saw her face, until this sight, my song hath ne’er been cut off from the track;

- *ch' io vidi il suo viso in questa vita* ‘that I saw her face in this life’ → “when in this life I saw her face”: the temporal relative becomes “when”, and the phrase of time moves before the subject and verb.
- *non m' è il seguire al mio cantar preciso* ‘the following has not been cut off for me in my singing’ → “my song hath ne’er been cut off from the track”: “my song”, rather than *il seguire*, becomes the subject. The act of following is recast as “the track” from which the song might be cut off; the personal dative has no separate equivalent, and the negative becomes temporal “ne’er”.

> 31 ma or convien che mio seguir desista  
> 32 più retro a sua bellezza, poetando,  
> 33 come all'ultimo suo ciascuno artista.  

but now needs must my tracking cease from following her beauty further forth in poesy, as at his utmost reach must every artist.

- *convien che mio seguir desista* ‘it is necessary that my following cease’ → “needs must my tracking cease”: the impersonal verb and subjunctive clause become one modal construction; the substantivized infinitive becomes “tracking”.
- *più retro a sua bellezza* ‘further after her beauty’ → “from following her beauty further forth”: “from following” supplies an explicit verbal complement to “cease”; the expression of distance moves after “her beauty” and is expanded by “forth”.
- *poetando* ‘making poetry’ → “in poesy”: the gerund becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *all'ultimo suo ciascuno artista* ‘at his limit every artist’ → “at his utmost reach must every artist”: “reach” supplies a noun for the substantivized *ultimo*, the possessive moves forward, and “must” resumes the necessity left implicit in the Italian comparison.

> 34 Cotal, qual io la lascio a maggior bando  
> 35 che quel della mia tuba, che deduce  
> 36 l'ardua sua materia terminando,  

Such as I leave her for a mightier proclamation than of my trumpet, which draweth its arduous subject to a close,

- *Cotal, qual* ‘such, such as’ → “Such as”: the demonstrative and its answering relative are run together.
- *a maggior bando* ‘to a greater proclamation’ → “for a mightier proclamation”: the preposition marks what she is left for; “mightier” specifies the kind of greatness.
- *che quel della mia tuba* ‘than that of my trumpet’ → “than of my trumpet”: the demonstrative standing for the proclamation is omitted.
- *che deduce l'ardua sua materia terminando* ‘which leads its arduous matter along, ending’ → “which draweth its arduous subject to a close”: “subject” takes *materia* as subject matter; the possessive comes before the adjective. The action of the gerund becomes the goal of “draweth”, “to a close”.

> 37 con atto e voce d'espedito duce  
> 38 ricominciò: “Noi semo usciti fuore  
> 39 del maggior corpo al ciel, ch' è pura luce;  

with alert leader’s voice and gesture, did she again begin: “We have issued forth from the greatest body into the heaven which is pure light,

- *con atto e voce d'espedito duce* ‘with gesture and voice of a ready leader’ → “with alert leader’s voice and gesture”: the two nouns change places, the *di* phrase becomes a preceding possessive, and “alert” renders readiness as watchfulness.
- *ricominciò* ‘she began again’ → “did she again begin”: the prefix becomes a separate adverb, with the past tense carried by an inverted auxiliary.
- *semo usciti fuore* ‘are gone out’ → “have issued forth”: English uses ‘have’ rather than ‘be’ for the perfect, and “issued forth” for going out.
- *al ciel* ‘to the heaven’ → “into the heaven”: “into” specifies entry rather than simply direction.

> 40 luce intellettual piena d'amore,  
> 41 amor di vero ben pien di letizia,  
> 42 letizia che trascende ogni dolzore.  

light intellectual full-charged with love, love of true good full-charged with gladness, gladness which transcendeth every sweetness.

- *piena d'amore* ‘full of love’ → “full-charged with love”: “charged” is added, with a corresponding change of preposition.
- *pien di letizia* ‘full of gladness’ → “full-charged with gladness”: the same expansion and change of preposition recur.

> 43 Qui vederai l'una e l'altra milizia  
> 44 di Paradiso, e l'una in quegli aspetti  
> 45 che tu vedrai all'ultima giustizia.”  

Here shalt thou see the one and the other soldiery of Paradise, and the one in those aspects which thou shalt see at the last judgment.”

- *vederai* ‘thou wilt see’ → “shalt thou see”: the future is expressed by an auxiliary, inverted after the opening adverb.
- *l'una e l'altra milizia* ‘the one and the other host’ → “the one and the other soldiery”: “soldiery” denotes the body of soldiers rather than the organized host.
- *all'ultima giustizia* ‘at the last justice’ → “at the last judgment”: the act of judging replaces the abstract noun for justice.

> 46 Come subito lampo che discetti  
> 47 gli spiriti visivi, sì che priva  
> 48 dell'atto l'occhio di più forti obbietti;  

As a sudden flash of lightning which so shattereth the visual spirits as to rob the eye of power to realize e’en strongest objects;

- *subito lampo* ‘a sudden flash’ → “a sudden flash of lightning”: “of lightning” makes the kind of flash explicit.
- *discetti* ‘may scatter’ → “shattereth”: the verb of dispersal becomes one of breaking apart, and the subjunctive characterizing the imagined flash becomes indicative.
- *sì che priva* ‘so that it deprives’ → “so” … “as to rob”: “so” moves back before “shattereth”, and the finite result clause becomes an infinitive.
- *dell'atto l'occhio di più forti obbietti* ‘of its operation the eye in respect of stronger objects’ → “the eye of power to realize e’en strongest objects”: the eye moves immediately after “rob”; *atto* is recast as a power to perceive, with “realize” supplied to govern “objects”. The comparative becomes a superlative, and “e’en” adds emphasis.

## Lines 49–81 — Living Light and the River of Sparks

> 49 così mi circonfulse luce viva,  

so there shone around me a living light,

- *mi circonfulse* ‘me it encircled with light’ → “there shone around me”: the compound verb becomes a verb of shining with a preposition; the pronoun follows the preposition, and “there” introduces the delayed subject.
- *luce viva* ‘light living’ → “a living light”: the indefinite article is supplied and the adjective precedes the noun.

> 50 e lasciommi fasciato di tal velo  
> 51 del suo fulgor, che nulla m'appariva.  

leaving me swathed in such a web of its glow that naught appeared to me.

- *e lasciommi* ‘and it left me’ → “leaving me”: the coordinated past verb becomes a participle, without the conjunction.
- *di tal velo* ‘with such a veil’ → “in such a web”: the preposition changes, and the veil becomes a web.
- *del suo fulgor* ‘of its radiance’ → “of its glow”: “glow” is less emphatic than ‘radiance’.
- *m'appariva* ‘to me appeared’ → “appeared to me”: the indirect object follows the verb.

> 52 “Sempre l'amore, che quieta il cielo,  
> 53 accoglie in sè con sì fatta salute,  
> 54 per far disposto a sua fiamma il candelo.”  

“Ever doth the love which stilleth heaven, receive into itself with such like salutation, duly to fit the taper for its flame.”

- *Sempre l'amore … accoglie* ‘ever the love … receives’ → “Ever doth the love” … “receive”: an auxiliary, placed before the subject, carries the tense of the delayed verb.
- *il cielo* ‘the heaven’ → “heaven”: the definite article is dropped.
- *con sì fatta salute* ‘with so fashioned a greeting’ → “with such like salutation”: the participial description becomes an expression of likeness, without an indefinite article.
- *per far disposto a sua fiamma il candelo* ‘to make ready for its flame the taper’ → “duly to fit the taper for its flame”: the causative and adjective become one verb; “duly” is supplied, and the direct object moves before the phrase expressing what it is fitted for.

> 55 Non fur più tosto dentro a me venute  
> 56 queste parole brevi, ch' io compresi  
> 57 me sormontar di sopra a mia virtute;  

So soon as these brief words came into me I felt me to surmount my proper power;

- *Non fur più tosto dentro a me venute queste parole brevi, ch'* ‘no sooner had come within me these words brief than’ → “So soon as these brief words came into me”: the negative comparative and its following conjunction become a positive temporal clause; the pluperfect becomes a simple past, the subject moves before the verb, the adjective before its noun, and “into” expresses direction.
- *io compresi me sormontar* ‘I perceived myself to rise’ → “I felt me to surmount”: apprehension becomes feeling; the reflexive is conveyed by “me”, and the verb is made transitive.
- *di sopra a mia virtute* ‘above my capacity’ → “my proper power”: the prepositional phrase becomes the direct object, with the sense of ‘above’ carried by the preceding verb; “proper” makes possession emphatic.

> 58 e di novella vista mi raccesi,  
> 59 tale che nulla luce è tanto mera,  
> 60 che gli occhi miei non si fosser difesi.  

and kindled me with such new-given sight that there is no such brightness unalloyed that mine eyes might not hold their own with it.

- *di novella vista mi raccesi, tale* ‘with new sight I rekindled myself, such’ → “kindled me with such new-given sight”: the reflexive follows the verb; the sense of ‘again’ is lost, “given” is supplied, and the phrase describing the sight follows the verb, with its delayed qualifier brought before the noun.
- *nulla luce è tanto mera* ‘no light is so pure’ → “there is no such brightness unalloyed”: an existential construction replaces the subject and predicate; ‘light’ becomes “brightness”, ‘pure’ becomes “unalloyed”, and the degree word becomes “such” before the noun.
- *gli occhi miei* ‘the eyes mine’ → “mine eyes”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *non si fosser difesi* ‘would not have defended themselves’ → “might not hold their own with it”: a modal idiom replaces the reflexive pluperfect subjunctive, without its perfect tense; “with it” makes the opposing brightness explicit.

> 61 E vidi lume in forma di riviera  
> 62 fulvido di fulgore, intra due rive  
> 63 dipinte di mirabil primavera.  

And I saw a light, in river form, glow tawny betwixt banks painted with marvellous spring.

- *lume* ‘light’ → “a light”: the indefinite article is supplied.
- *in forma di riviera* ‘in form of a river’ → “in river form”: the dependent noun becomes an attributive noun before “form”.
- *fulvido di fulgore* ‘tawny of radiance’ → “glow tawny”: the noun of radiance becomes an infinitive dependent on the verb of seeing, and the colour adjective follows it.
- *intra due rive* ‘between two banks’ → “betwixt banks”: the numeral is omitted.

> 64 Di tal fiumana uscian faville vive,  
> 65 e d'ogni parte si mettean nei fiori,  
> 66 quasi rubin che oro circonscrive.  

From out this river issued living sparks, and dropped on every side into the blossoms, like rubies set in gold.

- *Di tal fiumana* ‘out of such a great stream’ → “From out this river”: “this” replaces ‘such’, and the simpler noun loses the suggestion of a great flow.
- *faville vive* ‘sparks living’ → “living sparks”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *d'ogni parte si mettean* ‘on every side they put themselves’ → “dropped on every side”: the reflexive becomes an intransitive verb specifying downward motion, and the adverbial phrase moves after it.
- *quasi* ‘as if’ → “like”: a preposition introduces the comparison directly.
- *rubin che oro circonscrive* ‘rubies that gold encircles’ → “rubies set in gold”: the active relative clause becomes a passive participial phrase; gold is no longer the grammatical agent, and encirclement becomes a setting.

> 67 Poi, come inebriate dagli odori,  
> 68 riprofondavan sè nel miro gurge,  
> 69 e, s'una entrava, un'altra n'uscia fuori.  

Then as inebriated with the odours they plunged themselves again into the marvellous swirl, and as one entered issued forth another.

- *dagli odori* ‘by the odours’ → “with the odours”: the preposition marking the cause changes.
- *riprofondavan sè* ‘they plunged again themselves’ → “they plunged themselves again”: the prefix becomes a separate adverb after the reflexive pronoun.
- *miro gurge* ‘wondrous eddy’ → “marvellous swirl”: the motion of the water is foregrounded in “swirl”.
- *s'una entrava* ‘if one entered’ → “as one entered”: the conditional is read temporally.
- *un'altra n'uscia fuori* ‘another from it issued out’ → “issued forth another”: the subject moves after the verb, and the pronoun denoting the source has no separate rendering.

> 70 “L'alto disio che mo t' infiamma ed urge  
> 71 d'aver notizia di ciò che tu vei,  
> 72 tanto mi piace più, quanto più turge.  

“The lofty wish that now doth burn and press thee to have more knowledge of the things thou seest, pleaseth me more the more it swelleth.

- *L'alto disio* ‘the high desire’ → “The lofty wish”: the adjective takes a figurative English equivalent and ‘desire’ becomes “wish”.
- *t' infiamma ed urge* ‘thee inflames and urges’ → “doth burn and press thee”: the shared object follows both verbs; an auxiliary is supplied, and the verbs of enkindling and urging become “burn” and “press”.
- *d'aver notizia* ‘to have knowledge’ → “to have more knowledge”: comparative degree is supplied.
- *di ciò che tu vei* ‘of that which thou seest’ → “of the things thou seest”: the singular demonstrative becomes a plural noun, and the object relative has no expressed pronoun.
- *tanto mi piace più, quanto più turge* ‘so much the more it pleases me, by how much the more it swells’ → “pleaseth me more the more it swelleth”: the correlated measures of degree are compressed into “more the more”, and the object pronoun follows the first verb.

> 73 Ma di quest' acqua convien che tu bei,  
> 74 prima che tanta sete in te si sazii.”  

But of this water needs thou first must drink, ere so great thirst in thee be slaked.”

- *convien che tu bei* ‘it is necessary that thou drink’ → “needs thou first must drink”: the impersonal verb and subjunctive clause become a personal modal construction, with necessity expressed by both “needs” and “must”; “first” anticipates the temporal clause.
- *tanta sete* ‘so great a thirst’ → “so great thirst”: English uses the noun without the indefinite article usual after ‘so great’.
- *si sazii* ‘should sate itself’ → “be slaked”: the pronominal subjunctive becomes a passive, with a verb specific to thirst.

> 75 Così mi disse il sol degli occhi miei;  

So spoke mine eyes’ sun unto me;

- *mi disse* ‘to me said’ → “spoke” … “unto me”: the verb of saying becomes one of speaking, and the indirect object is delayed until after the subject.
- *il sol degli occhi miei* ‘the sun of the eyes mine’ → “mine eyes’ sun”: the dependent phrase becomes a possessive before “sun”, displacing its article, and “mine” moves before “eyes”.

> 76 anco soggiunse: “Il fiume, e li topazii  
> 77 ch' entrano ed escono, e il rider dell' erbe  
> 78 son di lor vero ombriferi prefazii.  

then added: “The river and the topaz-gems that enter and go forth, and the smiling of the grasses are the shadowy prefaces of their reality.

- *anco* ‘also’ → “then”: addition becomes succession.
- *li topazii* ‘the topazes’ → “the topaz-gems”: the class noun is made explicit.
- *escono* ‘go out’ → “go forth”: outward movement is expressed as movement forth.
- *il rider* ‘the laughing’ → “the smiling”: the nominalized action is softened from laughter to a smile.
- *di lor vero ombriferi prefazii* ‘of their true being shadow-bearing prefaces’ → “the shadowy prefaces of their reality”: the dependent phrase moves after “prefaces”; the substantivized ‘true’ becomes “reality”, the compound adjective becomes “shadowy”, and the definite article is supplied.

> 79 Non che da sè sien queste cose acerbe:  

Not that such things are harsh as in themselves;

- *da sè sien queste cose acerbe* ‘of themselves be these things unripe’ → “such things are harsh as in themselves”: the subject moves before the verb and “such” replaces ‘these’; the subjunctive becomes indicative, the adjective is taken figuratively, and the initial qualification moves to the end with “as” supplied.

> 80 ma è difetto dalla parte tua,  
> 81 che non hai viste ancor tanto superbe.”  

but on thy side is the defect, in that thy sight not yet exalteth it so high.”

- *è difetto dalla parte tua* ‘there is a defect on the side thine’ → “on thy side is the defect”: the phrase of location moves first, with its possessive before the noun, and the defect is made definite.
- *che* ‘for’ → “in that”: the explanation is phrased as specifying what the defect consists in.
- *non hai viste ancor tanto superbe* ‘thou hast not yet powers of sight so lofty’ → “thy sight not yet exalteth it so high”: possession gives way to a clause with singular “sight” as subject; the negative and its time adverb come together, and the plural adjective of height becomes a verb of elevation with “it” and a phrase of degree.

## Lines 82–114 — The River Becomes a Circle of Light

> 82 Non è fantin che sì subito rua  
> 83 col volto verso il latte, se si svegli  
> 84 molto tardato dall' usanza sua,  

Never doth child so sudden rush with face turned to the milk, if he awake far later than his wont,

- *Non è fantin che sì subito rua* ‘there is no little child that rushes so suddenly’ → “Never doth child so sudden rush”: the negative statement of existence and its relative clause become a single clause beginning with a negative adverb; the adverb brings the auxiliary before the subject. The diminutive is lost, and “sudden” is used adverbially.
- *col volto verso il latte* ‘with the face toward the milk’ → “with face turned to the milk”: the article is dropped, and “turned” makes the direction of the face explicit.
- *molto tardato dall' usanza sua* ‘much delayed from his custom’ → “far later than his wont”: the past participle becomes a comparative adverb modifying “awake”; the preposition becomes the conjunction of comparison, and the possessive moves before the noun.

> 85 come fec' io, per far migliori spegli  
> 86 ancor degli occhi, chinandomi all' onda  
> 87 che si deriva, perchè vi s' immegli.  

as then did I, to make yet better mirrors of mine eyes, down bending to the wave which floweth that we may better us.

- *come fec' io* ‘as did I’ → “as then did I”: “then” is supplied.
- *per far migliori spegli ancor degli occhi* ‘to make better mirrors still of the eyes’ → “to make yet better mirrors of mine eyes”: “yet” moves before the comparative, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *chinandomi* ‘bending myself’ → “down bending”: the reflexive pronoun is not rendered separately; “down” expresses the direction of the movement.
- *si deriva* ‘is drawn off’ → “floweth”: the passive or middle construction becomes an intransitive verb, without the suggestion of a source from which the water is drawn.
- *perchè vi s' immegli* ‘so that one may better oneself therein’ → “that we may better us”: the indefinite person becomes first-person plural, with “us” used reflexively; the locative has no counterpart.

> 88 E sì come di lei bevve la gronda  
> 89 delle palpebre mie, così mi parve  
> 90 di sua lunghezza divenuta tonda.  

And no sooner drank of it mine eye-lids’ rim than into roundness seemed to change its length.

- *sì come … così* ‘just as … so’ → “no sooner” … “than”: the correlative construction is recast to express immediate succession.
- *di lei bevve* ‘of it drank’ → “drank of it”: the prepositional phrase follows the verb.
- *la gronda delle palpebre mie* ‘the eaves of my eyelids’ → “mine eye-lids’ rim”: “rim” replaces the architectural term, and the following possessive phrase becomes a preceding genitive.
- *mi parve di sua lunghezza divenuta tonda* ‘it seemed to me to have become round from its length’ → “into roundness seemed to change its length”: the perceiver is omitted; “its length” is made the subject of the change rather than its starting point. The resulting adjective becomes a noun after “into”, and a simple infinitive replaces the construction of completed change.

> 91 Poi, come gente stata sotto larve,  
> 92 che pare altro che prima, se si sveste  
> 93 la sembianza non sua in che disparve;  

Then—as folk under masks seem other than before, if they do off the semblance not their own wherein they hid them,—

- *gente stata sotto larve* ‘folk that have been under masks’ → “folk under masks”: the past participle, and with it the explicit reference to a prior state, is dropped.
- *che pare* ‘that seems’ → “seem”: the relative marker is dropped, making this the finite verb of the comparison; the singular verb agreeing with the Italian collective becomes plural.
- *se si sveste la sembianza non sua* ‘if it strips itself of the semblance not its own’ → “if they do off the semblance not their own”: a transitive expression for taking something off replaces the reflexive construction; the singular collective is again treated as plural, including in the possessive.
- *in che disparve* ‘in which it disappeared’ → “wherein they hid them”: the preposition and relative are combined; singular intransitive disappearance becomes plural reflexive concealment.

> 94 così mi si cambiaro in maggior feste  
> 95 li fiori e le faville, sì ch' io vidi  
> 96 ambo le corti del ciel manifeste.  

so changed before me into ampler joyance the flowers and the sparks, so that I saw both the two courts of heaven manifested.

- *mi si cambiaro* ‘were transformed for me’ → “changed before me”: “before me” makes the personal dative a phrase of perception, and the pronominal verb is rendered by an intransitive one.
- *in maggior feste* ‘into greater festivities’ → “into ampler joyance”: “ampler” expresses the comparative, and an abstract singular replaces the plural noun.
- *ambo le corti* ‘both the courts’ → “both the two courts”: “two” is separately expressed as well as “both”.
- *manifeste* ‘manifest’ → “manifested”: an adjective of state becomes a past participle suggesting disclosure.

> 97 O isplendor di Dio, per cu' io vidi  
> 98 l'alto trionfo del regno verace,  
> 99 dammi virtù a dir com' io lo vidi.  

O splendour of God whereby I saw the lofty triumph of the truthful realm, give me the power to tell how I beheld it.

- *per cu'* ‘through which’ → “whereby”: the preposition and relative become one relative adverb.
- *regno verace* ‘realm truthful’ → “truthful realm”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *dammi virtù* ‘give me power’ → “give me the power”: a definite article is supplied.
- *a dir* ‘to say’ → “to tell”: the verb of saying is rendered by one of recounting.
- *lo vidi* ‘it I saw’ → “I beheld it”: the object follows the verb, and “beheld” varies the verb instead of repeating “saw”.

> 100 Lume è lassù, che visibile face  
> 101 lo Creatore a quella creatura,  
> 102 che solo in lui vedere ha la sua pace;  

A light there is up yonder which maketh the Creator visible unto the creature, who only in beholding him hath its own peace;

- *Lume è lassù* ‘light is up there’ → “A light there is up yonder”: an indefinite article and existential “there” are supplied in addition to the expression of place.
- *che visibile face lo Creatore* ‘which visible makes the Creator’ → “which maketh the Creator visible”: the predicative adjective moves from before the verb to after its object.
- *a quella creatura* ‘to that creature’ → “unto the creature”: the demonstrative becomes a definite article.
- *solo in lui vedere* ‘only in him seeing’ → “only in beholding him”: the infinitive governed by a preposition becomes a gerund, its object moves after it, and “beholding” renders the verb of seeing.
- *sua pace* ‘its peace’ → “its own peace”: “own” reinforces the possessive.

> 103 e si distende in circular figura  
> 104 in tanto che la sua circonferenza  
> 105 sarebbe al sol troppo larga cintura.  

and it so far outstretcheth circle-wise that its circumference would be too loose a girdle for the sun.

- *si distende … in tanto che* ‘extends itself … to such a degree that’ → “so far outstretcheth” … “that”: the reflexive is rendered intransitively, and the expression of extent moves before the verb.
- *in circular figura* ‘in circular shape’ → “circle-wise”: an adverb replaces the prepositional phrase.
- *sarebbe al sol troppo larga cintura* ‘would be for the sun too wide a girdle’ → “would be too loose a girdle for the sun”: the phrase referring to the sun moves to the end, and the adjective of width is rendered in terms of fit.

> 106 Fassi di raggio tutta sua parvenza  
> 107 riflesso al sommo del Mobile primo,  
> 108 che prende quindi vivere e potenza.  

All its appearance is composed of rays reflected from the top of the First Moved, which draweth thence its life and potency.

- *Fassi di raggio tutta sua parvenza* ‘is made of a ray all its appearance’ → “All its appearance is composed of rays”: the postposed subject moves to the front, the pronominal construction becomes a passive of composition, and the singular becomes plural.
- *al sommo* ‘at the summit’ → “from the top”: the place of reflection is expressed as the point from which the rays come.
- *del Mobile primo* ‘of the first movable’ → “of the First Moved”: a term for that which moves or can be moved is rendered by a passive participle, and the ordinal precedes it.
- *prende quindi vivere e potenza* ‘takes thence life and power’ → “draweth thence its life and potency”: “draweth” replaces the verb of taking, the substantival infinitive becomes the noun “life”, and a possessive is supplied for the two nouns.

> 109 E come clivo in acqua di suo imo  
> 110 si specchia, quasi per vedersi adorno,  
> 111 quando è nell'erbe e nei fioretti opimo,  

And as a hill-side doth reflect itself in water at its foot, as if to look upon its own adornment when it is rich in grasses and in flowers,

- *clivo in acqua di suo imo si specchia* ‘a slope in water at its base mirrors itself’ → “a hill-side doth reflect itself in water at its foot”: the verb moves before the phrase locating the reflection; the image of mirroring is expressed by “reflect”, with an auxiliary, and the lowest part of the slope is called its “foot”.
- *quasi per vedersi adorno* ‘as if to see itself adorned’ → “as if to look upon its own adornment”: the reflexive object and its predicative adjective become a possessive phrase with an abstract noun.
- *quando è nell'erbe e nei fioretti opimo* ‘when it is in the grasses and in the little flowers rich’ → “when it is rich in grasses and in flowers”: the adjective moves before its complements, the definite articles disappear, and the diminutive of the second noun is lost.

> 112 sì soprastando al lume intorno intorno  
> 113 vidi specchiarsi in più di mille soglie,  
> 114 quanto di noi lassù fatto ha ritorno.  

so, mounting o’er the light, around, around, casting reflection in more than thousand ranks I saw all that of us hath won return up yonder.

- *soprastando al lume* ‘standing above the light’ → “mounting o’er the light”: a relation of position is expressed as one of ascent.
- *vidi specchiarsi* ‘I saw mirror itself’ → “casting reflection” … “I saw”: the reflexive infinitive dependent on the verb of seeing becomes a participial phrase with a noun object; it and the phrase locating the reflections now precede “I saw”.
- *in più di mille soglie* ‘on more than a thousand thresholds’ → “in more than thousand ranks”: “ranks” interprets the thresholds as rows, with a corresponding preposition; there is no indefinite article before “thousand”.
- *quanto di noi lassù fatto ha ritorno* ‘all that of us up there has made return’ → “all that of us hath won return up yonder”: “won” replaces the verb of making, the auxiliary precedes the participle, and the expression of place moves to the end.

## Lines 115–148 — The Eternal Rose and Henry’s Seat

> 115 E se l' infimo grado in sè raccoglie  
> 116 sì grande lume, quant' è la larghezza  
> 117 di questa rosa nell' estreme foglie?  

And if the lowest step gathereth so large a light within itself, what then the amplitude of the rose’s outmost petals?

- *in sè raccoglie sì grande lume* ‘within itself gathers so great a light’ → “gathereth so large a light within itself”: the phrase of place moves from before the verb to after its object; ‘great’ becomes “large”.
- *quant’ è la larghezza* ‘how great is the breadth’ → “what then the amplitude”: the question is recast without an expressed verb; “then” is supplied.
- *di questa rosa nell’ estreme foglie* ‘of this rose at its outermost leaves’ → “of the rose’s outmost petals”: the demonstrative becomes an article, ‘leaves’ becomes “petals”, and the breadth is made that of the petals rather than that of the rose measured at its outer leaves.

> 118 La vista mia nell'ampio e nell'altezza  
> 119 non si smarriva, ma tutto prendeva  
> 120 il quanto e il quale di quella allegrezza.  

My sight in the breadth and height lost itself not, but grasped the scope and nature of that joyance.

- *vista mia* ‘sight of mine’ → “My sight”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *nell’ampio e nell’altezza* ‘in the broad expanse and in the height’ → “in the breadth and height”: the substantivized adjective becomes an abstract noun parallel to “height”; the preposition and article are not repeated.
- *non si smarriva* ‘not itself did it lose’ → “lost itself not”: the reflexive and negative both move after the verb; the simple past renders the imperfect.
- *ma tutto prendeva il quanto e il quale* ‘but took in the whole quantity and quality’ → “but grasped the scope and nature”: ‘took in’ becomes “grasped”, with no separate equivalent for ‘whole’; the substantivized ‘how much’ and ‘of what kind’ become “scope and nature”, without a second article.

> 121 Presso e lontano lì nè pon nè leva,  
> 122 chè dove Dio senza mezzo governa,  
> 123 la legge natural nulla rileva.  

Near and far addeth not nor subtracteth there, for where God governeth without medium the law of nature hath no relevance.

- *lì nè pon nè leva* ‘there neither puts nor takes away’ → “addeth not nor subtracteth there”: the verbs become an arithmetical pair, the first negative follows its verb, and the adverb moves to the end.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *senza mezzo governa* ‘without intermediary governs’ → “governeth without medium”: the phrase moves after the verb.
- *la legge natural* ‘the law natural’ → “the law of nature”: the adjective becomes an *of*-phrase.
- *nulla rileva* ‘avails nothing’ → “hath no relevance”: the negative of degree becomes a determiner, and the verb’s sense is carried by a noun.

> 124 Nel giallo della rosa sempiterna,  
> 125 che si dilata, digrada e redole  
> 126 odor di lode al sol che sempre verna,  

Within the yellow of the eternal rose, which doth expand, rank upon rank, and reeketh perfume of praise unto the Sun that maketh spring for ever,

- *Nel* ‘in the’ → “Within the”: the preposition is made more explicit.
- *rosa sempiterna* ‘rose eternal’ → “eternal rose”: the adjective precedes its noun.
- *si dilata, digrada* ‘expands itself, descends by degrees’ → “doth expand, rank upon rank”: the reflexive becomes intransitive, and the second finite verb becomes an adverbial phrase, with no explicit downward motion.
- *redole odor* ‘breathes forth an odor’ → “reeketh perfume”: the verb of fragrance becomes “reeketh”, and the noun is made specifically fragrant.
- *sempre verna* ‘ever makes spring’ → “maketh spring for ever”: a single verb is expanded into verb and object, and the adverb becomes a phrase following them.

> 127 qual è colui che tace e dicer vuole,  
> 128 mi trasse Beatrice, e disse: “Mira  
> 129 quanto è il convento delle bianche stole!  

me—as who doth hold his peace yet fain would speak—Beatrice drew, and said: “Behold how great the white-robed concourse!

- *qual è colui che* ‘as is the one who’ → “as who”: the copula and the antecedent of the relative are suppressed.
- *tace e dicer vuole* ‘is silent and to speak wishes’ → “doth hold his peace yet fain would speak”: silence is expressed idiomatically, ‘and’ becomes adversative, and the wish is expressed before “speak”.
- *mi trasse Beatrice* ‘me drew Beatrice’ → “me” … “Beatrice drew”: the object is taken forward across the comparison, and the subject precedes the verb.
- *quanto è il convento delle bianche stole* ‘how great is the assembly of the white robes’ → “how great the white-robed concourse”: the copula drops out, and the robes become a compound adjective describing the assembled people.

> 130 Vedi nostra città quanto ella gira!  

See how large our city sweepeth!

- *nostra città quanto ella gira* ‘our city, how far it circles’ → “how large our city sweepeth”: the question of extent comes first, the resumptive pronoun drops out, and the circling is expressed as a sweep.

> 131 Vedi li nostri scanni sì ripieni,  
> 132 che poca gente omai ci si disira.  

See our thrones so filled that but few folk are now awaited there.

- *scanni* ‘seats’ → “thrones”: the noun is more specific.
- *poca gente omai ci si disira* ‘little folk now there is desired’ → “but few folk are now awaited there”: the singular collective takes plural agreement; “but” emphasizes the small number, desire becomes expectation, and the adverb of place moves to the end.

> 133 In quel gran seggio, a che tu gli occhi tieni  
> 134 per la corona che già v' è su posta,  
> 135 prima che tu a queste nozze ceni,  

On that great seat where thou dost fix thine eyes, for the crown’s sake already placed above it, ere at this wedding feast thyself do sup,

- *In quel gran seggio* ‘in that great seat’ → “On that great seat”: the preposition changes.
- *a che tu gli occhi tieni* ‘toward which thou thine eyes holdest’ → “where thou dost fix thine eyes”: a relative adverb replaces the directional phrase; “fix” replaces ‘hold’ and precedes its object.
- *per la corona* ‘because of the crown’ → “for the crown’s sake”: the causal preposition becomes a possessive construction.
- *che già v’ è su posta* ‘which already is set thereon’ → “already placed above it”: the finite relative clause becomes a participial modifier, and the place words become a prepositional phrase after the participle.
- *prima che tu a queste nozze ceni* ‘before thou at these nuptials sup’ → “ere at this wedding feast thyself do sup”: the plural becomes a singular feast; the subject moves after the phrase of place and is made emphatic.

> 136 sederà l'alma, che fia già agosta,  
> 137 dell'alto Enrico, ch' a drizzare Italia  
> 138 verrà in prima che ella sia disposta.  

shall sit the soul (on earth ’t will be imperial), of the lofty Henry who shall come to straighten Italy ere she be ready for it.

- *che fia già agosta* ‘which will by then be imperial’ → “on earth ’t will be imperial”: the relative clause becomes an independent parenthesis; “on earth” is supplied, while ‘by then’ has no direct equivalent.
- *alto* ‘high’ → “lofty”: the adjective is rendered by a more marked term.
- *a drizzare Italia verrà* ‘to straighten Italy will come’ → “shall come to straighten Italy”: the verb of motion precedes the infinitive of purpose.
- *in prima che ella sia disposta* ‘before she be disposed’ → “ere she be ready for it”: disposition is expressed as readiness, with an explicit complement.

> 139 La cieca cupidigia, che vi ammalia,  
> 140 simili fatti v' ha al fantolino,  
> 141 che muor di fame e caccia via la balia;  

The blind greed which bewitcheth you hath made you like the little child who dieth of hunger and chaseth off his nurse;

- *cupidigia* ‘cupidity’ → “greed”: a plainer noun is used.
- *vi ammalia* ‘you bewitches’ → “bewitcheth you”: the object follows the verb.
- *simili fatti v’ ha al fantolino* ‘like made you has to the little child’ → “hath made you like the little child”: the auxiliary precedes the participle, the object follows it, and “like” stands next to its complement without a preposition.
- *la balia* ‘the nurse’ → “his nurse”: a possessive replaces the article.

> 142 e fia prefetto nel foro divino  
> 143 allora tal, che palese e coperto  
> 144 non anderà con lui per un cammino.  

and he who then presideth in the court of things divine shall be such an one as, openly and covertly, shall not tread the same path with him.

- *fia prefetto nel foro divino allora tal, che* ‘will be prefect in the divine court then such a one that’ → “he who then presideth in the court of things divine shall be such an one as”: the office becomes a verb in a present-tense relative clause, with “then” moved before it; the adjective modifying the court becomes a phrase specifying what it deals with. The future copula now links the expanded subject to “such an one”.
- *palese* ‘open’ → “openly”: the adjective used adverbially becomes an adverb.
- *coperto* ‘covered’ → “covertly”: the participle used adverbially likewise becomes an adverb.
- *non anderà con lui per un cammino* ‘will not go with him along one path’ → “shall not tread the same path with him”: going along a path becomes treading it, ‘one’ is interpreted as “the same”, and the phrase of accompaniment moves to the end.

> 145 Ma poco poi sarà da Dio sofferto  
> 146 nel santo offizio; ch' ei sarà detruso  

But short space thereafter shall he be endured of God in the sacred office;

- *poco poi* ‘a little afterward’ → “short space thereafter”: “space” makes the interval explicit and allows it to be read as the duration of the enduring.
- *sarà da Dio sofferto* ‘will be by God endured’ → “shall he be endured of God”: the agent follows the participle rather than intervening between auxiliary and participle.
- *santo offizio* ‘holy office’ → “sacred office”: a different adjective is used.

> 147 là dove Simon mago è per suo merto,  
> 148 e farà quel d'Anagna esser più giuso.”  

for he shall be thrust down where Simon Magus is for his desert, and lower down shall force him of Anagna.”

- *ch’* ‘because’ → “for”: the causal link is made coordinating.
- *là dove* ‘there where’ → “where”: the separate demonstrative of place is omitted.
- *è per suo merto* ‘is by his merit’ → “is for his desert”: the preposition expresses the grounds of the placement, and “desert” conveys what is deserved without implying praise.
- *farà quel d’Anagna esser più giuso* ‘will make him of Anagna be farther down’ → “lower down shall force him of Anagna”: the causative with an infinitive becomes the single verb “force”, and the resulting position moves from the end to the front.

## Summary — Recasting the Phrase, Preserving the Pattern

Wicksteed often changes the order of words without changing the order of ideas. Object pronouns follow their verbs rather than preceding them, as in *mi vinse*, “vanquished me”; adjectives that follow their nouns move before them, as in *faville vive*, “living sparks”. He also supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending identifies the subject, and drops the article before a possessive, as when *li nostri scanni* becomes “our thrones”. Yet he does not consistently prefer ordinary English order: “light intellectual” keeps the adjective after the noun, and “issued living sparks” keeps the subject after the verb.

Changes of construction often matter more than shifts of position. The reflexive *a farsi tal* becomes “to grow such”, while the impersonal *convien che mio seguir desista* becomes the personal modal construction “needs must my tracking cease”. A phrase may also change its grammatical shape without losing its image: *rubin che oro circonscrive*, ‘rubies that gold encircles’, becomes “rubies set in gold”. The active relative clause gives way to a passive participle, but the contrast of the two materials remains.

Wicksteed sometimes brings out what the Italian leaves implicit and sometimes leaves a detail unexpressed. In “power to shine”, *il parere*, ‘being visible’, becomes a capacity attributed to the star; “past our reach” supplies a measure of capacity for *di là da noi*, ‘beyond us’. Conversely, “betwixt banks” leaves out *due*, and “flowers” loses the diminutive of *fioretti*. Where the Italian relies on repetition, however, the prose often stays close: “around, around” answers *intorno intorno*, and the passage from *luce* to *amore* to *letizia* retains its linked phrasing, each successive term taken up again even as *piena* and *pien* are expanded to “full-charged”.
