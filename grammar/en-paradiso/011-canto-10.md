# Paradiso 10

## Lines 1–12 — Divine Order and the Reader’s Gaze

> 1 Guardando nel suo figlio con l' amore  
> 2 che l' uno e l' altro eternalmente spira,  

Gazing upon his Son with the Love which the one and the other eternally breathes forth,

- *Guardando nel suo figlio* ‘gazing into his Son’ → “Gazing upon his Son”: the preposition changes from ‘into’ to “upon”.
- *spira* ‘breathes’ → “breathes forth”: “forth” makes the outward direction explicit.

> 3 lo primo ed ineffabile valore  
> 4 quanto per mente o per loco si gira  
> 5 con tanto ordine fe', ch' esser non puote  
> 6 senza gustar di lui chi ciò rimira.  

the primal and ineffable Worth, made whatsoever circleth through mind or space with so great order that whoso looketh on it may not be without some taste of him.

- *quanto per mente o per loco si gira con tanto ordine fe'* ‘whatever through mind or through place turns, with so much order made’ → “made whatsoever circleth through mind or space with so great order”: the main verb moves from after its object and the phrase of manner to immediately after its subject. Within the object, the pronominal verb becomes the simple intransitive “circleth” and moves before the prepositional phrase; ‘place’ becomes “space”, and the second preposition is not repeated. In the phrase of manner, quantity is expressed as greatness of degree.
- *esser non puote* ‘be cannot’ → “may not be”: the modal precedes the infinitive, with “not” between them.
- *senza gustar di lui* ‘without tasting of him’ → “without some taste of him”: the infinitive becomes a noun qualified by the added “some”.
- *chi ciò rimira* ‘whoever contemplates this’ → “whoso looketh on it”: the subject moves from the end of the clause to before its verb; contemplation is rendered by “looketh on”, and the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun.

> 7 Leva dunque, lettor, all' alte rote  
> 8 meco la vista dritto a quella parte  
> 9 dove l' un moto e l' altro si percote;  

Then, reader, raise with me thy sight to the exalted wheels, directed to that part where the one movement smiteth on the other;

- *Leva dunque, lettor* ‘raise then, reader’ → “Then, reader, raise”: the connective and the vocative both move before the imperative.
- *all' alte rote* ‘to the high wheels’ → “to the exalted wheels”: ‘high’ becomes “exalted”, and the destination moves to after “with me thy sight”.
- *la vista* ‘the sight’ → “thy sight”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *dritto* ‘straight’ → “directed”: an adverb of direction becomes a participle qualifying “sight”.
- *l' un moto e l' altro si percote* ‘the one movement and the other strike against each other’ → “the one movement smiteth on the other”: the coordinated subject and reciprocal construction give way to a single subject, with the other movement made the object of “on”.

> 10 e lì comincia a vagheggiar nell' arte  
> 11 di quel maestro, che dentro a sè l' ama  
> 12 tanto che mai da lei l' occhio non parte.  

and amorously there begin to gaze upon that Master’s art, who within himself so loveth it, that never doth he part his eye from it.

- *vagheggiar* ‘gaze lovingly’ → “amorously” … “gaze”: the affectionate sense of the verb is carried by a separate adverb, moved forward to precede “there begin”.
- *nell' arte di quel maestro* ‘in the art of that master’ → “upon that Master’s art”: “upon” replaces ‘in’ after the verb of gazing; the following *di* phrase becomes a possessive before “art”, displacing “art” from immediately before the phrase naming the master to immediately before “who”.
- *l' ama tanto* ‘it he loves so much’ → “so loveth it”: the degree expression moves before the verb and the object pronoun after it.
- *da lei l' occhio non parte* ‘from it the eye he does not part’ → “doth he part his eye from it”: after “never”, English uses an inverted auxiliary rather than a second negative; the verb precedes its object, the article becomes a possessive, and the phrase of separation comes last.

## Lines 13–45 — The Inclined Heavens and the Ascent into the Sun

> 13 Vedi come da indi si dirama  
> 14 l' obbliquo cerchio che i pianeti porta,  
> 15 per satisfare al mondo che li chiama;  

See how thence offbrancheth the oblique circle that beareth the planets, to satisfy the world that calleth on them;

- *si dirama* ‘branches off’ → “offbrancheth”: the sense of the Italian pronominal verb is carried by an English compound, with ‘off’ first.
- *che i pianeti porta* ‘that the planets bears’ → “that beareth the planets”: the object moves after the verb.
- *che li chiama* ‘that them calls’ → “that calleth on them”: the object moves after the verb, and “on” gives the call the sense of an appeal.

> 16 e se la strada lor non fosse torta,  
> 17 molta virtù nel ciel sarebbe in vano,  
> 18 e quasi ogni potenza quaggiù morta:  

and were their pathway not inclined, much virtue in the heaven were in vain, and dead were almost every potency on earth;

- *e se la strada lor non fosse torta* ‘and if the road of theirs were not bent’ → “and were their pathway not inclined”: inversion replaces *se*; the possessive comes before the noun, and “inclined” specifies the sense of ‘bent’.
- *sarebbe in vano* ‘would be in vain’ → “were in vain”: an English subjunctive replaces the Italian conditional.
- *e quasi ogni potenza quaggiù morta* ‘and almost every potency down here dead’ → “and dead were almost every potency on earth”: Wicksteed supplies the verb understood from the preceding clause, fronts the predicate adjective, and names the place instead of pointing to it.

> 19 e se da dritto più o men lontano  
> 20 fosse il partire, assai sarebbe manco  
> 21 e giù e su dell' ordine mondano.  

and if, from the straight course, or more or less remote were the departure, much were lacking to the cosmic order below and eke above.

- *da dritto* ‘from the straight’ → “from the straight course”: “course” supplies a noun for the substantivized adjective.
- *più o men lontano* ‘more or less distant’ → “or more or less remote”: the first “or” is supplied, giving the alternatives the form ‘either … or’.
- *assai sarebbe manco* ‘much would be lacking’ → “much were lacking”: the subjunctive again replaces the conditional.
- *e giù e su* ‘both below and above’ → “below and eke above”: the paired conjunctions become a single “and” reinforced by “eke”; the phrase moves to the end.
- *dell' ordine mondano* ‘of the order of the world’ → “to the cosmic order”: the Italian says how much *of* the order would be missing; English says what would be lacking *to* it. The adjective precedes the noun in English.

> 22 Or ti riman, lettor, sopra il tuo banco,  
> 23 retro pensando a ciò che si preliba,  
> 24 s' esser vuoi lieto assai prima che stanco.  

Now stay thee, reader, on thy bench, back thinking on this foretaste, wouldst thou have good joyance ere that thou be weary.

- *ti riman* ‘remain’ → “stay thee”: Wicksteed retains the Italian pronominal turn with an English imperative and a following pronoun.
- *a ciò che si preliba* ‘on that which is tasted beforehand’ → “on this foretaste”: a demonstrative and a noun replace the antecedent and its relative clause.
- *s' esser vuoi lieto assai* ‘if to be thou wishest glad exceedingly’ → “wouldst thou have good joyance”: inversion replaces ‘if’; being glad becomes having joy, and the degree of gladness is expressed by “good” before the noun.
- *prima che stanco* ‘before [being] weary’ → “ere that thou be weary”: the elliptical comparison is expanded into a finite clause.

> 25 Messo t' ho innanzi: omai per te ti ciba;  

I have set before thee; now feed thou thyself,

- *Messo t' ho innanzi* ‘set before thee have I’ → “I have set before thee”: the auxiliary precedes the participle, and the pronoun follows “before”.
- *omai* ‘from now on’ → “now”: the onward force of the adverb is not separately expressed.
- *per te ti ciba* ‘by thyself feed thyself’ → “feed thou thyself”: the reflexive follows the imperative; the emphatic “thou” helps convey the force of *per te*, which has no separate prepositional equivalent.

> 26 chè a sè torce tutta la mia cura  
> 27 quella materia ond' io son fatto scriba.  

for that matter whereof I have made me scribe, now wresteth to itself my total care.

- *a sè torce tutta la mia cura quella materia ond' io son fatto scriba* ‘to itself turns all my care that matter whereof I am become scribe’ → “that matter whereof I have made me scribe, now wresteth to itself my total care”: the subject, together with its relative clause, moves from after the verb and object to the head of the clause. Wicksteed gives the resultative construction an active reflexive sense, making the speaker the agent of his becoming a scribe. “now” is supplied, “wresteth” is stronger than ‘turns’, and “to itself” follows the verb. “total” makes the quantifier an adjective following the possessive.

> 28 Lo ministro maggior della natura,  
> 29 che del valor del cielo il mondo imprenta  
> 30 e col suo lume il tempo ne misura,  

The greatest minister of Nature, who with the worth of heaven stampeth the world, and with his light measureth the time for us,

- *Lo ministro maggior* ‘the minister greatest’ → “The greatest minister”: the adjective, superlative in sense here, precedes the noun.
- *il mondo imprenta* ‘the world stamps’ → “stampeth the world”: the object moves after the verb.
- *il tempo ne misura* ‘the time for us measures’ → “measureth the time for us”: the verb moves before its object, and the dative pronoun becomes a final prepositional phrase.

> 31 con quella parte che su si rammenta  
> 32 congiunto, si girava per le spire  
> 33 in che più tosto ognora s' appresenta.  

united with that part now called to mind, was circling on the spirals whereon he doth present him ever earlier.

- *con quella parte che su si rammenta congiunto* ‘with that part which above is mentioned united’ → “united with that part now called to mind”: the participle moves to the front; the finite relative becomes a participial phrase, and “now called to mind” substitutes a present act of recollection for a reference to what is mentioned above.
- *si girava per le spire* ‘turned through the spirals’ → “was circling on the spirals”: a progressive renders the imperfect; the English verb needs no reflexive, and “on” replaces ‘through’.
- *in che* ‘in which’ → “whereon”: the relative takes up the English construction with “on”.
- *più tosto ognora s' appresenta* ‘earlier ever presents himself’ → “he doth present him ever earlier”: the adverbs move after the verb and into English order; “doth” carries the inflection, and the reflexive is rendered by postverbal “him”.

> 34 Ed io era con lui; ma del salire  
> 35 non m' accors' io, se non com' uom s' accorge,  
> 36 anzi il primo pensier, del suo venire.  

And I was with him; but of my ascent I was no more aware than is a man, ere his first thought, aware that it is coming.

- *del salire* ‘of the ascending’ → “of my ascent”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun with an explicit possessive.
- *non m' accors' io, se non com' uom s' accorge* ‘I did not perceive it, except as a man perceives’ → “I was no more aware than is a man, ere his first thought, aware”: the exception becomes a comparison of degree, the verbs of perception become forms of ‘be aware’, and the intervening time phrase now falls between “is” and the repeated “aware”.
- *anzi il primo pensier* ‘before the first thought’ → “ere his first thought”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *del suo venire* ‘of its coming’ → “that it is coming”: a finite clause replaces the preposition and substantivized infinitive.

> 37 È Beatrice quella che sì scorge  
> 38 di bene in meglio, sì subitamente  
> 39 che l' atto suo per tempo non si sporge.  

’Tis Beatrice who leadeth thus from good to better, so instantly that her act doth not expatiate through time.

- *sì scorge* ‘thus guides’ → “leadeth thus”: the adverb moves after the verb.
- *l' atto suo* ‘the act of hers’ → “her act”: the possessive precedes the noun.
- *per tempo non si sporge* ‘through time does not extend itself’ → “doth not expatiate through time”: an intransitive verb replaces the pronominal one, and the time phrase moves to the end.

> 40 Quant' esser convenia da sè lucente  
> 41 quel ch' era dentro al sol dov' io entra'mi,  
> 42 non per color, ma per lume parvente!  

How shining in itself must that needs be which in the sun, whereinto I had entered, itself revealeth not by hue, but light!

- *Quant' esser convenia da sè lucente* ‘how much it was needful to be of itself shining’ → “How shining in itself must that needs be”: “How” directly modifies “shining”; ‘of itself’ becomes “in itself”, and the impersonal imperfect becomes the personal construction “must … needs be”, without an explicit past tense.
- *quel ch' era dentro al sol … parvente* ‘that which was within the sun … apparent’ → “that” … “which in the sun” … “itself revealeth”: the demonstrative moves inside the English modal construction; ‘was apparent’ becomes a present-tense reflexive verb, and ‘within’ becomes “in”.
- *dov' io entra'mi* ‘where I entered’ → “whereinto I had entered”: the relative makes direction explicit, the pluperfect replaces the simple past, and the Italian pronominal form has no English reflexive.
- *non per color, ma per lume* ‘not by color, but by light’ → “not by hue, but light”: “hue” renders *color*, and “by” is not repeated.

> 43 Perch' io lo ingegno, l' arte e l' uso chiami,  
> 44 sì nol direi che mai s' imaginasse,  
> 45 ma creder puossi, e di veder si brami.  

Though I should summon genius, art, tradition, ne’er could I so express it as to make it imaged; but it may be believed—and let men long to see it.

- *Perch' io … chiami* ‘although I call upon’ → “Though I should summon”: the subjunctive is rendered with “should”, and the verb moves before its objects.
- *lo ingegno, l' arte e l' uso* ‘the intellect, the art and the practice’ → “genius, art, tradition”: the articles and conjunction disappear; “genius” stresses native capacity, and “tradition” interprets *uso* as established practice rather than the exercise of skill.
- *sì nol direi che mai s' imaginasse* ‘I would not so tell it that it might ever be imagined’ → “ne’er could I so express it as to make it imaged”: the negative and ‘ever’ are brought together at the front; the conditional becomes one of ability, and a causative infinitive replaces the finite clause of result.
- *creder puossi* ‘believed it can be’ → “it may be believed”: the infinitive, placed first in Italian, follows the modal in the English passive.
- *e di veder si brami* ‘and let it be longed to see’ → “and let men long to see it”: the impersonal subjunctive becomes an exhortation with “men” as its subject; the infinitive follows “long” and receives an explicit object.

## Lines 46–78 — Beatrice’s Smile and the Singing Crown

> 46 E se le fantasie nostre son basse  
> 47 a tanta altezza, non è maraviglia,  
> 48 chè sopra il sol non fu occhio ch' andasse.  

And if our fantasies are low for such an exaltation, it is no marvel, for never was there eye that could transcend the sun.

- *le fantasie nostre* ‘the fantasies of ours’ → “our fantasies”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *a tanta altezza* ‘at so great a height’ → “for such an exaltation”: the height against which the fantasies are measured becomes an exaltation for which they are low.
- *non è maraviglia* ‘is not a marvel’ → “it is no marvel”: English uses an impersonal subject and puts the negative on the noun instead of the verb.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *sopra il sol non fu occhio ch' andasse* ‘above the sun there was no eye that went’ → “never was there eye that could transcend the sun”: the negative becomes temporal and moves to the front; the phrase of direction, separated in Italian from the subjunctive verb of motion, becomes the verb “transcend” with a direct object. “could” makes the potential sense of the subjunctive explicit.

> 49 Tal era quivi la quarta famiglia  
> 50 dell' alto padre che sempre la sazia,  
> 51 mostrando come spira e come figlia.  

Such, there, was the fourth household of the exalted Father who ever satisfieth it, shewing how he doth breathe, and how beget.

- *era quivi* ‘was there’ → “there, was”: the adverb moves before the verb.
- *famiglia* ‘family’ → “household”: the collective noun is rendered in its broader domestic sense.
- *alto* ‘high’ → “exalted”: the adjective of height becomes a participial adjective of rank.
- *la sazia* ‘it he sates’ → “satisfieth it”: the verb of filling to satiety is less specific in English, and the object follows it.
- *spira e come figlia* ‘breathes and how he begets’ → “he doth breathe, and how beget”: the two finite verbs become bare verb forms governed by a single auxiliary.

> 52 E Beatrice cominciò: “Ringrazia,  
> 53 ringrazia il sol degli angeli, ch' a questo  
> 54 sensibil t' ha levato per sua grazia.”  

And Beatrice began: “Give thanks, give thanks to the sun of the Angels, who of his grace hath to this sun of sense exalted thee.”

- *Ringrazia, ringrazia il sol* ‘thank, thank the sun’ → “Give thanks, give thanks to the sun”: the repeated transitive verb becomes a verb and noun, with the recipient introduced by a preposition.
- *a questo sensibil* ‘to this perceptible one’ → “to this sun of sense”: the understood noun is supplied, and the adjective becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *t' ha levato* ‘thee hath raised’ → “hath” … “exalted thee”: the object moves after the participle, which is separated from its auxiliary by the phrase of destination; “exalted” gives ‘raised’ a more elevated diction.
- *per sua grazia* ‘by his grace’ → “of his grace”: the phrase moves from the end of the clause to before the auxiliary, with a change of preposition.

> 55 Cor di mortal non fu mai sì digesto  
> 56 a divozione ed a rendersi a Dio  
> 57 con tutto il suo gradir cotanto presto,  
> 58 com' a quelle parole mi fec' io;  

Never was heart of mortal so disposed unto devotion, and so keen to give itself to God with all its will, as at those words was I;

- *Cor di mortal non fu mai* ‘heart of mortal was not ever’ → “Never was heart of mortal”: the negative and the adverb combine in an initial “Never”, with verb and subject inverted.
- *digesto* ‘digested’ → “disposed”: English gives the figurative sense of the participle rather than its physical image.
- *ed a rendersi a Dio … cotanto presto* ‘and to yield oneself to God … so very ready’ → “and so keen to give itself to God”: the adjective and its degree word move before the infinitive; readiness becomes keenness, and reflexive yielding becomes giving itself.
- *con tutto il suo gradir* ‘with all its taking pleasure’ → “with all its will”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun of volition.
- *com' a quelle parole mi fec' io* ‘as at those words I made myself’ → “as at those words was I”: a reflexive construction expressing a change of disposition becomes a statement of state; the emphatic subject still comes last.

> 59 e sì tutto il mio amore in lui si mise,  
> 60 che Beatrice eclissò nell' obblio.  

and so wholly was my love committed unto him, it eclipsed Beatrice in oblivion.

- *e sì tutto il mio amore in lui si mise* ‘and so all my love placed itself in him’ → “and so wholly was my love committed unto him”: the adjective quantifying the love becomes an adverb qualifying the verb; reflexive self-placement becomes passive commitment, with the prepositional phrase moved to the end.
- *che Beatrice eclissò* ‘that Beatrice was eclipsed’ → “it eclipsed Beatrice”: the result conjunction is left unexpressed, and the Italian can be read intransitively, with Beatrice as subject. Wicksteed reads the verb transitively, with the love as subject and Beatrice as object.

> 61 Non le dispiacque; ma sì se ne rise,  
> 62 che lo splendor degli occhi suoi ridenti  
> 63 mia mente unita in più cose divise.  

Her it displeased not; but she so smiled thereat, the splendour of her laughing eyes parted my erst united mind amongst things multiform.

- *Non le dispiacque* ‘it did not displease her’ → “Her it displeased not”: the indirect object of the Italian verb becomes the fronted direct object of the English verb, with the negative last.
- *se ne rise* ‘laughed at it’ → “smiled thereat”: laughter is rendered as smiling, with no separate reflexive element, and the pronominal adverb becomes a following English adverb.
- *che* ‘that’ → “the splendour”: the result clause begins directly with its subject, without an equivalent for the conjunction.
- *degli occhi suoi ridenti* ‘of the eyes of hers laughing’ → “of her laughing eyes”: both the possessive and the participial adjective move before the noun.
- *mia mente unita* ‘my mind united’ → “my erst united mind”: the participle precedes the noun, and “erst” makes its former state explicit.
- *in più cose divise* ‘among several things divided’ → “parted” … “amongst things multiform”: the verb moves before its object and its prepositional complement; a word of number becomes a postposed adjective of variety.

> 64 Io vidi più fulgor vivi e vincenti  
> 65 far di noi centro e di sè far corona,  
> 66 più dolci in voce che in vista lucenti.  

Then saw I many a glow, living and conquering, make of us a centre, and of themselves a crown; sweeter in voice than shining in appearance.

- *Io vidi* ‘I saw’ → “Then saw I”: a temporal link is supplied, and the verb precedes its subject.
- *più fulgor* ‘many radiances’ → “many a glow”: a singular distributive construction replaces the plural.
- *far di noi centro e di sè far corona* ‘make of us centre and of themselves make crown’ → “make of us a centre, and of themselves a crown”: articles are supplied for the predicate nouns, and one verb does duty for both occurrences.
- *in vista lucenti* ‘in appearance shining’ → “shining in appearance”: the participle precedes its qualifying phrase.

> 67 Così cinger la figlia di Latona  
> 68 vedem talvolta, quando l' aere è pregno  
> 69 sì che ritenga il fil che fa la zona.  

Thus girt we sometimes see Latona’s daughter, when the air is so impregnated as to retain the thread that makes her zone.

- *cinger la figlia di Latona vedem talvolta* ‘encircle the daughter of Latona we see sometimes’ → “girt we sometimes see Latona’s daughter”: the active infinitive becomes a passive participle describing the daughter; the object moves after the verb of seeing, the frequency adverb before it, and the possessive before the noun.
- *è pregno sì* ‘is pregnant so’ → “is so impregnated”: a participle replaces the adjective, with the degree adverb before it.
- *che ritenga* ‘that it retain’ → “as to retain”: the finite subjunctive result clause becomes an infinitive construction.
- *la zona* ‘the zone’ → “her zone”: a possessive replaces the definite article.

> 70 Nella corte del ciel, ond' io rivegno,  
> 71 si trovan molte gioie care e belle  
> 72 tanto che non si posson trar del regno,  

In the court of heaven, whence I have returned, are many gems so dear and beauteous that from that realm they may not be withdrawn,

- *rivegno* ‘I come back’ → “I have returned”: present becomes present perfect.
- *si trovan* ‘are found’ → “are”: the passive sense of the pronominal verb becomes a statement of existence.
- *gioie* ‘jewels’ → “gems”: the English chooses the more specific noun.
- *care e belle tanto* ‘dear and beautiful so much’ → “so dear and beauteous”: the degree word moves before the adjectives.
- *non si posson trar del regno* ‘they cannot be drawn out of the realm’ → “from that realm they may not be withdrawn”: the phrase of origin is fronted, the article becomes demonstrative, and the modal and verb of removal are recast.

> 73 e il canto di quei lumi era di quelle;  
> 74 chi non s' impenna sì che lassù voli,  
> 75 dal muto aspetti quindi le novelle.  

and the song of these lights was of such; he who doth not so wing himself that he may fly up there, must look for news thence from the dumb.

- *quei lumi* ‘those lights’ → “these lights”: the demonstrative changes from distal to proximal.
- *di quelle* ‘of those’ → “of such”: reference to members of a previously named group becomes reference to their kind.
- *chi* ‘whoever’ → “he who”: the single relative is expanded into antecedent and relative.
- *non s' impenna sì* ‘does not feather himself so’ → “doth not so wing himself”: feathering becomes winging; the degree adverb moves before the main verb and the reflexive after it.
- *lassù voli* ‘up there he fly’ → “he may fly up there”: a modal expresses the subjunctive, and the adverb of place follows the verb.
- *dal muto aspetti quindi le novelle* ‘from the mute man let him expect thence the news’ → “must look for news thence from the dumb”: the exhortative subjunctive becomes a modal of necessity, and expecting becomes looking for. The source moves to the end, the object comes before “thence” and loses its article, and “the dumb” does not mark the Italian singular.

> 76 Poi, sì cantando, quegli ardenti soli  
> 77 si fur girati intorno a noi tre volte,  
> 78 come stelle vicine ai fermi poli,  

When, so singing, those burning suns had circled round us thrice, like stars neighbouring the fixed poles,

- *Poi … si fur girati* ‘then … they had turned themselves’ → “When” … “had circled”: the temporal adverb becomes a subordinating conjunction, making the whole passage a temporal clause; the reflexive perfect becomes an active intransitive perfect.
- *ardenti soli* ‘ardent suns’ → “burning suns”: the participial sense of the adjective is brought out.
- *tre volte* ‘three times’ → “thrice”: the numeral and noun become one adverb.
- *vicine ai fermi poli* ‘near to the fixed poles’ → “neighbouring the fixed poles”: an adjective with a prepositional complement becomes a participle with a direct object.

## Lines 79–111 — Thomas Names the Lights of the Garland

> 79 donne mi parver, non da ballo sciolte,  
> 80 ma che s' arrestin tacite ascoltando  
> 81 fin che le nuove note hanno ricolte.  

they seemed as ladies, not from the dance released, but pausing, silent, listening till they catch the notes renewed.

- *donne mi parver* ‘ladies they seemed to me’ → “they seemed as ladies”: the verb comes first, the comparison is made explicit by “as”, and ‘to me’ is omitted.
- *da ballo* ‘from dance’ → “from the dance”: the definite article is supplied.
- *ma che s' arrestin* ‘but who should come to a stop’ → “but pausing”: the relative clause with a reflexive verb in the subjunctive becomes a participle.
- *fin che le nuove note hanno ricolte* ‘until the new notes they have gathered’ → “till they catch the notes renewed”: the object follows the verb, the perfect becomes a simple present, and ‘new’ becomes a participle following the noun.

> 82 E dentro all' un senti' cominciar: “Quando  
> 83 lo raggio della grazia, onde s' accende  
> 84 verace amore, e che poi cresce amando  

And within one I heard begin: “Since the ray of grace,—whereat true love is kindled, and then doth grow, by loving,

- *all' un* ‘the one’ → “one”: the article is dropped, leaving the light indefinite.
- *Quando* ‘when’ → “Since”: the conjunction is taken causally rather than temporally.
- *della grazia* ‘of the grace’ → “of grace”: the article is omitted.
- *onde s' accende verace amore* ‘by which is kindled true love’ → “whereat true love is kindled”: the relative expresses occasion rather than means, and the subject precedes the passive verb.
- *e che poi cresce* ‘and which then grows’ → “and then doth grow”: the relative pronoun is omitted; the verb is coordinated directly with the preceding verb.
- *amando* ‘loving’ → “by loving”: the preposition makes the instrumental force of the gerund explicit.

> 85 multiplicato, in te tanto risplende,  
> 86 che ti conduce su per quella scala,  
> 87 u' senza risalir nessun discende,  

multifold—doth so glow in thee as to conduct thee up upon that stairway, which, save to reascend, no one descendeth,

- *multiplicato* ‘multiplied’ → “multifold”: the participle becomes an expression of degree.
- *in te tanto risplende* ‘in thee so greatly shines’ → “doth so glow in thee”: the phrase of place moves after the verb, and shining is rendered as glowing.
- *che ti conduce* ‘that it conducts thee’ → “as to conduct thee”: the finite result clause becomes an infinitive, with the object after the verb.
- *su per* ‘up along’ → “up upon”: the second preposition marks the surface rather than the course.
- *u'* ‘where’ → “which”: the relative adverb becomes the object of “descendeth”.
- *senza risalir* ‘without ascending again’ → “save to reascend”: the negative prepositional phrase becomes an exception expressed by an infinitive.

> 88 qual ti negasse il vin della sua fiala  
> 89 per la tua sete, in libertà non fora,  
> 90 se non com' acqua ch' al mar non si cala.  

whoso refused his vial’s wine to quench thy thirst, were no more free than water that should flow not to the sea.

- *qual ti negasse* ‘whoever should deny thee’ → “whoso refused”: the imperfect subjunctive becomes an English preterite with hypothetical force; the indirect object is omitted.
- *il vin della sua fiala* ‘the wine of his vial’ → “his vial’s wine”: the possessive relation is reversed in order.
- *per la tua sete* ‘for thy thirst’ → “to quench thy thirst”: a purpose infinitive supplies the action left implicit in the preposition.
- *in libertà non fora, se non com'* ‘in freedom he would not be, except as’ → “were no more free than”: the noun becomes an adjective, and the negative with an exception becomes a comparison of degree.
- *ch' al mar non si cala* ‘that to the sea does not go down’ → “that should flow not to the sea”: descent is rendered as flow, the indicative becomes hypothetical, and the destination moves to the end.

> 91 Tu vuoi saper di quai piante s' infiora  
> 92 questa ghirlanda, che intorno vagheggia  
> 93 la bella donna ch' al ciel t' avvalora.  

Thou wouldst know with what plants this garland is enflowered, which amorously doth circle round the beauteous lady who strengtheneth thee for heaven.

- *vuoi saper* ‘wish to know’ → “wouldst know”: a modal construction replaces the verb of wishing with its infinitive.
- *di quai piante* ‘of which plants’ → “with what plants”: the preposition marks what adorns the garland rather than what its flowers consist of.
- *s' infiora questa ghirlanda* ‘enflowers itself this garland’ → “this garland is enflowered”: the subject moves before the verb, and the reflexive construction becomes passive.
- *intorno vagheggia* ‘round about gazes lovingly on’ → “amorously doth circle round”: motion becomes the verb, while the loving regard is reduced to an adverb.
- *ch' al ciel t' avvalora* ‘who for heaven gives thee strength’ → “who strengtheneth thee for heaven”: the object follows the verb, and the phrase of purpose comes last.

> 94 Io fui degli agni della santa greggia,  
> 95 che Domenico mena per cammino,  
> 96 u' ben s' impingua, se non si vaneggia.  

I was of the lambs of the sacred flock that Dominic leadeth upon the way where is good fattening if there be no straying.

- *per cammino* ‘along a path’ → “upon the way”: “upon” replaces the preposition of passage, and the way is made definite.
- *u' ben s' impingua* ‘where one fattens well’ → “where is good fattening”: the impersonal verb becomes a noun in an existential construction, and the adverb becomes an adjective.
- *se non si vaneggia* ‘if one does not wander’ → “if there be no straying”: a second impersonal verb becomes a noun, with the negative attached to the noun rather than to the verb.

> 97 Questi, che m' è a destra più vicino,  
> 98 frate e maestro fummi, ed esso Alberto  
> 99 fu di Colonia, ed io Thomas d'Aquino.  

This, who most neighboureth me upon the right, brother and master was to me, and he was Albert of Cologne, I Thomas of Aquino.

- *m' è a destra più vicino* ‘is to me on the right more near’ → “most neighboureth me upon the right”: ‘is near’ becomes a transitive verb, the comparative is taken as a relative superlative, and the phrase of position comes last.
- *ed esso Alberto fu di Colonia* ‘and he Albert was of Cologne’ → “and he was Albert of Cologne”: the copula moves before the name, bringing the name and the place together.

> 100 Se sì di tutti gli altri esser vuoi certo,  
> 101 di retro al mio parlar ten vien col viso  
> 102 girando su per lo beato serto.  

If in like manner thou wouldst be assured of all the rest, take way with thy sight after my words, circling above along the blessed wreath.

- *di tutti gli altri esser vuoi certo* ‘of all the others to be thou wishest certain’ → “thou wouldst be assured of all the rest”: the modal precedes the infinitive, ‘certain’ becomes ‘assured’, and the collective “the rest” follows it in place of the fronted plural.
- *di retro al mio parlar* ‘behind my speaking’ → “after my words”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a plural noun, and the phrase moves after the direction to follow.
- *ten vien col viso* ‘come on with the sight’ → “take way with thy sight”: the pronominal verb of motion becomes a verb and noun, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *su per* ‘up along’ → “above along”: upward direction becomes position above.

> 103 Quell' altro fiammeggiare esce del riso  
> 104 di Grazian, che l' uno e l' altro foro  
> 105 aiutò sì che piace in Paradiso.  

This next flaming issueth from the smile of Gratian, who gave such aid to the one and the other forum, as is acceptable in Paradise.

- *Quell' altro* ‘that other’ → “This next”: the demonstrative changes, and distinction becomes succession.
- *esce del riso* ‘comes out of the laughter’ → “issueth from the smile”: the motion is expressed by a single verb with “from”, and the milder sense of the noun is chosen.
- *l' uno e l' altro foro aiutò sì* ‘the one and the other forum he helped so’ → “gave such aid to the one and the other forum”: the object moves after the verb and becomes a recipient introduced by “to”; ‘helped so’ becomes a verb with a qualified noun.
- *che piace in Paradiso* ‘that it pleases in Paradise’ → “as is acceptable in Paradise”: the result clause becomes a relative clause answering to “such”, and the active verb becomes a copula and adjective.

> 106 L' altro, ch' appresso adorna il nostro coro,  
> 107 quel Pietro fu, che con la poverella  
> 108 offerse a santa Chiesa suo tesoro.  

The other who doth next adorn our choir, was that Peter who, with the poor widow, offered his treasure unto Holy Church.

- *quel Pietro fu* ‘that Peter was’ → “was that Peter”: the copula precedes its complement.
- *la poverella* ‘the poor little woman’ → “the poor widow”: widowhood is supplied, and the diminutive is not separately rendered.
- *offerse a santa Chiesa suo tesoro* ‘offered to Holy Church his treasure’ → “offered his treasure unto Holy Church”: the direct object precedes the recipient.

> 109 La quinta luce, ch' è tra noi più bella,  
> 110 spira di tale amor, che tutto il mondo  
> 111 laggiù ne gola di saper novella:  

The fifth light, which amongst us is most fair, doth breathe from such a love that all the world down there thirsteth to know the news of it;

- *ch' è tra noi più bella* ‘which is among us more fair’ → “which amongst us is most fair”: the phrase of comparison moves before the copula, and the comparative is given its superlative force in context.
- *di tale amor* ‘of such love’ → “from such a love”: the preposition is read as indicating source, and the indefinite article is supplied.
- *ne gola di saper novella* ‘of it hungers to know tidings’ → “thirsteth to know the news of it”: an image of thirst replaces one of appetite, the article is supplied before “news”, and the pronominal reference moves to the end.

## Lines 112–144 — The Circle of Lights and the Chiming Clock

> 112 entro v' è l' alta mente u' sì profondo  
> 113 saper fu messo, che, se il vero è vero,  
> 114 a veder tanto non surse il secondo.  

within there is the lofty mind, to which a wisdom so profound was granted, that, if the truth be true, no second ever rose to such full vision.

- *u’* ‘wherein’ → “to which”: the mind becomes the recipient rather than the place in which wisdom was put.
- *sì profondo saper* ‘so profound a knowledge’ → “a wisdom so profound”: the infinitive used as a noun becomes “wisdom”; the adjective and its modifier follow the noun.
- *fu messo* ‘was put’ → “was granted”: bestowal replaces placement.
- *se il vero è vero* ‘if the true is true’ → “if the truth be true”: the substantivized adjective becomes an abstract noun, and the indicative becomes an English subjunctive.
- *a veder tanto non surse il secondo* ‘to see so much there arose not a second’ → “no second ever rose to such full vision”: the negative attaches to the subject, which moves before the verb; “ever” is supplied. The opening infinitive becomes a final noun phrase, with the extent of the seeing expressed as fullness of vision.

> 115 Appresso vedi il lume di quel cero  
> 116 che, giuso in carne, più addentro vide  
> 117 l' angelica natura e il ministero.  

Next look upon that taper’s light, which, in the flesh below, saw deepest into the angelic nature and its ministry.

- *vedi* ‘see’ → “look upon”: a verb of directed attention replaces the simple verb of sight.
- *il lume di quel cero* ‘the light of that taper’ → “that taper’s light”: the possessor comes first, in the possessive case.
- *giuso in carne* ‘below in flesh’ → “in the flesh below”: the adverb moves to the end, and English supplies the definite article.
- *più addentro vide* ‘further inward saw’ → “saw deepest into”: the verb comes first, the comparative is interpreted as a superlative, and “into” links the seeing to its object.
- *il ministero* ‘the ministry’ → “its ministry”: a possessive replaces the article.

> 118 Nell' altra piccioletta luce ride  
> 119 quell' avvocato dei tempi cristiani,  
> 120 del cui latino Augustin si provvide.  

In the next little light laugheth that pleader for the Christian times, with whose discourse Augustine fortified him.

- *altra* ‘other’ → “next”: difference is rendered as succession.
- *avvocato dei tempi cristiani* ‘advocate of the times Christian’ → “pleader for the Christian times”: the relation is made explicitly one of pleading on behalf of something, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *del cui latino* ‘of whose Latin’ → “with whose discourse”: the preposition becomes instrumental, and the name of the language is rendered in the sense of speech or writing.
- *si provvide* ‘provided himself’ → “fortified him”: providing oneself with something becomes strengthening oneself by it; the reflexive precedes the Italian verb but follows the English verb in the older form “him”.

> 121 Or, se tu l' occhio della mente trani  
> 122 di luce in luce, retro alle mie lode,  
> 123 già dell' ottava con sete rimani.  

Now if thou drawest thy mind’s eye from light to light, following my praises, already for the eighth thou art athirst.

- *l’occhio della mente trani* ‘the eye of the mind drawest’ → “drawest thy mind’s eye”: the verb moves before its object; the mind is made explicitly the addressee’s, and its relation to the eye is expressed by a preceding possessive.
- *retro alle mie lode* ‘behind my praises’ → “following my praises”: a phrase of position becomes a participial phrase of movement.
- *dell’ottava con sete rimani* ‘for the eighth with thirst thou remainest’ → “for the eighth thou art athirst”: remaining is reduced to being, and the phrase with a noun becomes a predicate adjective following the verb.

> 124 Per vedere ogni ben dentro vi gode  
> 125 l' anima santa, che il mondo fallace  
> 126 fa manifesto a chi di lei ben ode.  

In seeing every good therein rejoiceth the sainted soul, which unmasketh the deceitful world to whoso giveth it good hearing.

- *Per vedere* ‘through seeing’ → “In seeing”: the infinitive after a preposition becomes an English gerund, and the preposition presents the seeing as that in which the joy consists.
- *dentro vi* ‘inside, there’ → “therein”: the two indications of place are gathered into one adverb.
- *l’anima santa* ‘the soul holy’ → “the sainted soul”: the adjective moves before the noun and takes participial form.
- *il mondo fallace fa manifesto* ‘the world deceitful makes manifest’ → “unmasketh the deceitful world”: verb and complement become one figurative verb, placed before its object; the adjective precedes the noun.
- *a chi di lei ben ode* ‘to whoever of her hears well’ → “to whoso giveth it good hearing”: hearing becomes something given to the soul; the verb becomes a noun, the adverb an adjective, and the feminine pronoun a neuter object pronoun.

> 127 Lo corpo ond' ella fu cacciata giace  
> 128 giuso in Cieldauro, ed essa da martiro  
> 129 e da esilio venne a questa pace.  

The body whence it was chased forth, lieth down below in Cieldauro and itself from martyrdom and exile came unto this peace.

- *fu cacciata* ‘was driven out’ → “was chased forth”: expulsion is expressed by a verb of pursuit with a directional adverb.
- *giuso* ‘below’ → “down below”: the single adverb is expanded.
- *essa* ‘she’ → “itself”: the explicit feminine subject becomes an emphatic neuter pronoun.
- *da martiro e da esilio* ‘from martyrdom and from exile’ → “from martyrdom and exile”: one preposition serves both nouns.

> 130 Vedi oltre fiammeggiar l' ardente spiro  
> 131 d' Isidoro, di Beda e di Riccardo  
> 132 che a considerar fu più che viro.  

See flaming next the glowing breath of Isidore, of Bede, and of Richard, who, in contemplating, was more than man.

- *oltre fiammeggiar* ‘further on to flame’ → “flaming next”: the spatial adverb is rendered in terms of succession and moved after the verb; the infinitive dependent on the verb of seeing becomes a participle.
- *ardente* ‘burning’ → “glowing”: the image shifts from burning to radiance.
- *viro* ‘a man’ → “man”: English uses the bare noun in a generic sense.

> 133 Questi, onde a me ritorna il tuo riguardo,  
> 134 è il lume d' uno spirto, che in pensieri  
> 135 gravi a morir gli parve venir tardo:  

The one from which thy glance returneth unto me, is the light of a spirit who, in weighty thoughts, him seemed went all too slowly to his death;

- *Questi* ‘this one’ → “The one”: the demonstrative becomes a definite article with a pronoun.
- *onde a me ritorna il tuo riguardo* ‘whence to me returns thy glance’ → “from which thy glance returneth unto me”: the relative adverb is unpacked into preposition and pronoun; the subject moves before the verb, the destination after it.
- *pensieri gravi* ‘thoughts weighty’ → “weighty thoughts”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *a morir gli parve venir tardo* ‘to die it seemed to him he came late’ → “him seemed went all too slowly to his death”: the impersonal expression of seeming comes first; the dependent infinitive becomes the finite “went”, reversing the direction of ‘come’. Lateness becomes excessive slowness, intensified by “all too”, and the opening infinitive becomes a final noun phrase with an explicit possessive.

> 136 essa è la luce eterna di Sigieri,  
> 137 che, leggendo nel vico degli strami,  
> 138 sillogizzò invidiosi veri.”  

it is the light eternal of Sigier who, lecturing in the *Vicus Straminis*, syllogized truths that brought him into hate.”

- *leggendo* ‘reading’ → “lecturing”: the participle is given its academic sense.
- *nel vico degli strami* ‘in the street of the straw’ → “in the *Vicus Straminis*”: the Italian street name is given in Latin rather than translated into English.
- *invidiosi veri* ‘odium-provoking truths’ → “truths that brought him into hate”: the adjective before the noun is expanded into a relative clause after it, making explicit who incurred the hostility.

> 139 Indi come orologio, che ne chiami  
> 140 nell' ora che la sposa di Dio surge  
> 141 a mattinar lo sposo perchè l' ami,  

Then as the horologue, that calleth us, what hour the spouse of God riseth to sing her matins to her spouse that he may love her,

- *come orologio* ‘as a clock’ → “as the horologue”: the generic noun, bare in Italian, receives a definite article.
- *ne chiami* ‘us may call’ → “calleth us”: the pronoun follows the verb, and the Italian subjunctive becomes an English indicative.
- *nell’ora che* ‘in the hour when’ → “what hour”: the preposition and relative construction give way to an idiomatic expression of time.
- *a mattinar lo sposo* ‘to give the bridegroom a morning song’ → “to sing her matins to her spouse”: the one verb is expanded into singing a named morning office; its direct object becomes the recipient after “to”, and the two possessives are supplied.
- *perchè l’ami* ‘in order that her he love’ → “that he may love her”: the purpose subjunctive is expressed with “may”, and the object pronoun moves after the verb.

> 142 che l' una parte l' altra tira ed urge,  
> 143 tin tin sonando con sì dolce nota,  
> 144 che il ben disposto spirto d' amor turge;  

wherein one part drawing and thrusting other, giveth a chiming sound of so sweet note, that the well-ordered spirit with love swelleth;

- *che* ‘which’ → “wherein”: the relative is interpreted as locative, referring to what happens within the clock.
- *l’una parte l’altra tira ed urge* ‘the one part the other pulls and pushes’ → “one part drawing and thrusting other”: the articles disappear, the shared object moves after both verbs, and the finite verbs become participles.
- *tin tin sonando* ‘tin tin sounding’ → “giveth a chiming sound”: the imitative syllables are rendered by a description of the sound, and the participle becomes a finite verb with a noun object.
- *con sì dolce nota* ‘with so sweet a note’ → “of so sweet note”: instead of a phrase of accompaniment modifying the sounding, English has a phrase describing the sound itself; the noun is used without an article.
- *ben disposto* ‘well disposed’ → “well-ordered”: disposition is expressed as order.
- *d’amor* ‘of love’ → “with love”: English changes the preposition to suit the verb of swelling.

## Lines 145–148 — The Glorious Wheel in Harmony

> 145 così vid' io la gloriosa rota  
> 146 moversi, e render voce a voce in tempra  
> 147 ed in dolcezza ch' esser non può nota,  

so did I see the glorious wheel revolve and render voice to voice in harmony and sweetness that may not be known

- *vid' io* ‘saw I’ → “did I see”: the simple past becomes a construction with “did”; the subject follows the auxiliary rather than the main verb.
- *moversi* ‘to move itself’ → “revolve”: the intransitive English verb drops the reflexive form and makes the circular motion explicit.
- *in tempra* ‘in tune’ → “in harmony”: the noun for tuning or accord is rendered by “harmony”.
- *ed in dolcezza* ‘and in sweetness’ → “and sweetness”: the second preposition is omitted; the two nouns share the first “in”.
- *esser non può nota* ‘be cannot known’ → “may not be known”: the modal and its negation come before the infinitive rather than after it, and “may” expresses possibility.

> 148 se non colà dove gioir s' insempra.  

except where joy maketh itself eternal.

- *se non colà dove* ‘if not there where’ → “except where”: the negative condition is rendered by a word of exception, and the separate indication of place, ‘there’, is omitted.
- *gioir* ‘rejoicing’ → “joy”: the Italian infinitive used as a noun becomes a noun for the state rather than the activity.
- *s' insempra* ‘itself eternizes’ → “maketh itself eternal”: the single verb formed from *sempre* ‘always’ is unfolded into a causative verb and an adjective, with the reflexive pronoun following the verb.

## Summary — Changed Constructions, Preserved Echoes

Wicksteed often moves the verb before its object: *il mondo imprenta* ‘the world stamps’ becomes “stampeth the world”. He likewise brings modifiers forward or turns a following *di* phrase into a preceding possessive, as in *il lume di quel cero* ‘the light of that taper’, rendered “that taper’s light”. Yet he does not regularize every inversion: in “with love swelleth” the verb still follows its complement, as it does in *d’ amor turge*.

A change of construction often carries a change of emphasis. In *senza gustar di lui* ‘without tasting of him’, an act becomes something possessed in a measure: “without some taste of him”. Conversely, *il mondo fallace fa manifesto* ‘makes the deceitful world manifest’ becomes “unmasketh the deceitful world”, concentrating verb and complement in a single, more figurative verb. Wicksteed sometimes divides the force of one word between two: in *vagheggiar*, the loving quality of the gaze passes into “amorously”, set apart from “gaze”. Elsewhere he makes a choice more specific, rendering *torta* ‘bent’ as “inclined” and *la poverella* ‘the poor little woman’ as “the poor widow”.

Some additions and omissions are routine: English supplies subject pronouns where Italian gives the person by the verb ending, and omits the article before a possessive, as in *il mio amore*, “my love”. Others affect the phrasing: “erst” makes explicit the former unity of *mia mente unita*, while the second *in* of *in tempra ed in dolcezza* disappears in “in harmony and sweetness”. Where repetition matters, however, Wicksteed can keep it even while changing the grammar: *Ringrazia, ringrazia* becomes “Give thanks, give thanks”. He also keeps *voce a voce* almost word for word in “voice to voice”; and although *s’ insempra* takes three words in “maketh itself eternal”, the English preserves both the reflexive turn and the making of eternity into an action.
