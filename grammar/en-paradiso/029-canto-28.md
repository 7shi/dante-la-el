# Paradiso 28

## Lines 1–12 — A Flame Reflected in a Mirror

> 1 Poscia che contro alla vita presente  
> 2 dei miseri mortali aperse il vero  
> 3 quella che imparadisa la mia mente;  

When, counter to the present life of wretched mortals, the truth had been revealed by her who doth emparadise my mind;

- *Poscia che* ‘after’ → “When”: the conjunction marks time without itself saying ‘after’.
- *vita presente* ‘life present’ → “present life”: the adjective comes before the noun.
- *dei miseri mortali* ‘of the wretched mortals’ → “of wretched mortals”: the definite article is dropped in the general expression.
- *aperse il vero quella* ‘opened the truth she’ → “the truth had been revealed by her”: the postposed subject becomes the agent of a passive, and the object becomes its subject. ‘Opened’ becomes “revealed”, and the simple past becomes a past perfect, marking the priority expressed in Italian by the opening conjunction.
- *imparadisa* ‘emparadises’ → “doth emparadise”: the single present-tense verb is rendered with an auxiliary.

> 4 come in lo specchio fiamma di doppiero  
> 5 vede colui che se n'alluma retro,  
> 6 prima che l'abbia in vista o in pensiero,  

as in the mirror a taper’s flame, kindled behind a man, is seen of him or ere itself be in his sight or thought,

- *fiamma di doppiero* ‘flame of a double taper’ → “a taper’s flame”: the genitive precedes the noun, and “taper” does not specify a double taper.
- *vede colui che se n'alluma retro* ‘sees he who is lit by it from behind’ → “kindled behind a man, is seen of him”: the active verb with its postposed subject becomes a passive with the man as agent. The relative clause describing the man as lit by the flame becomes a participial phrase describing the flame as kindled behind the man.
- *prima che l'abbia in vista o in pensiero* ‘before he have it in sight or in thought’ → “or ere itself be in his sight or thought”: ‘before’ becomes the archaic conjunction “or ere”. The object of ‘have’ becomes the emphatic subject “itself” of “be”; the man is represented by a possessive, and the preposition is not repeated.

> 7 e sè rivolge, per veder se il vetro  
> 8 gli dice il vero, e vede ch' ei s' accorda  
> 9 con esso, come nota con suo metro;  

and he turneth back to see whether the glass speak truth to him, and seeth it accordant with it as song-words to their measure;

- *sè rivolge* ‘turns himself round’ → “he turneth back”: the reflexive construction becomes an intransitive verb with an adverb of direction.
- *gli dice il vero* ‘tells him the truth’ → “speak truth to him”: ‘tells’ becomes “speak”, in the subjunctive rather than the indicative; the article is dropped, and the indirect object comes last in a prepositional phrase.
- *vede ch' ei s' accorda* ‘sees that it agrees’ → “seeth it accordant”: the subject of the finite clause becomes the object of “seeth”, and the pronominal verb becomes a predicative adjective.
- *nota con suo metro* ‘a note with its measure’ → “song-words to their measure”: the singular *nota* is interpreted as the words sung rather than the musical note. The possessive accordingly has a plural antecedent, and ‘with’ becomes “to”.

> 10 così la mia memoria si ricorda  
> 11 ch' io feci, riguardando nei begli occhi,  
> 12 onde a pigliarmi fece Amor la corda:  

so doth my memory recall it chanced to me, gazing upon the beauteous eyes whence love had made the noose to capture me;

- *così la mia memoria si ricorda* ‘so my memory remembers’ → “so doth my memory recall”: the pronominal verb becomes a transitive verb, with an auxiliary placed before the subject.
- *ch' io feci* ‘that I did’ → “it chanced to me”: the conjunction is dropped, and a personal verb of doing becomes an impersonal expression of happening, with the speaker as its indirect object.
- *riguardando nei begli occhi* ‘gazing into the beautiful eyes’ → “gazing upon the beauteous eyes”: ‘into’ becomes “upon”, and the adjective takes the poetic form “beauteous”.
- *a pigliarmi fece Amor la corda* ‘to catch me made love the cord’ → “love had made the noose to capture me”: the subject moves before the verb, the simple past becomes a past perfect, and the purpose infinitive moves from the beginning to the end. ‘Cord’ is rendered more specifically as “noose”, and ‘catch’ as “capture”.

## Lines 13–45 — The Point of Light and the Nine Circles

> 13 e com' io mi rivolsi, e furon tocchi  
> 14 li miei da ciò che pare in quel volume,  
> 15 quandunque nel suo giro ben s' adocchi,  

and when I turned, and mine own were smitten by what appeareth in that volume whene’er upon its circling the eye is rightly fixed,

- *com’ io mi rivolsi* ‘as I turned myself’ → “when I turned”: the conjunction is expressly temporal, and the verb becomes intransitive rather than reflexive.
- *furon tocchi li miei* ‘were touched mine’ → “mine own were smitten”: the possessive subject comes before the verb, “own” gives it emphasis, and “smitten” is stronger than ‘touched’.
- *da ciò che* ‘by that which’ → “by what”: antecedent and relative are combined.
- *nel suo giro* ‘in its turning’ → “upon its circling”: the preposition changes to suit “fixed”.
- *ben s’ adocchi* ‘one looks well’ → “the eye is rightly fixed”: the impersonal act of looking becomes a passive construction with “the eye” as subject; the adverb follows the subject and qualifies “fixed”.

> 16 un punto vidi che raggiava lume  
> 17 acuto sì che il viso, ch' egli affoca,  
> 18 chiuder conviensi, per lo forte acume;  

a point I saw which rayed forth light so keen, needs must the vision that it flameth on be closed because of its strong poignancy;

- *raggiava* ‘radiated’ → “rayed forth”: the direction of emission is made explicit.
- *acuto sì* ‘keen so’ → “so keen”: the degree adverb precedes the adjective.
- *che il viso, ch’ egli affoca, chiuder conviensi* ‘that the sight, which it sets aflame, it is necessary to close’ → “needs must the vision that it flameth on be closed”: the result conjunction is left unexpressed; impersonal necessity becomes a modal construction, with the sight now the subject of a passive infinitive. The relative clause makes the vision something flamed upon rather than something set aflame.
- *per lo forte acume* ‘because of the strong sharpness’ → “because of its strong poignancy”: the article becomes a possessive, and ‘sharpness’ is rendered by a noun suggesting a piercing effect.

> 19 e quale stella par quinci più poca,  
> 20 parrebbe luna locata con esso,  
> 21 come stella con stella si colloca.  

and whatever star from here appeareth smallest, were seen a moon neighboured with it, as star with star is neighboured.

- *quale stella* ‘whichever star’ → “whatever star”: the indefinite relative is rendered with “whatever”.
- *par quinci più poca* ‘appears from here smaller’ → “from here appeareth smallest”: the place adverb moves before the verb, and the comparative becomes a superlative.
- *parrebbe luna* ‘would appear a moon’ → “were seen a moon”: the conditional of seeming becomes a hypothetical passive of seeing.
- *locata con esso* ‘placed with it’ → “neighboured with it”: placement is expressed as proximity.
- *si colloca* ‘is placed’ → “is neighboured”: the Italian passive construction is retained in sense, but the verb again expresses proximity.

> 22 Forse cotanto, quanto pare appresso  
> 23 alo cinger la luce che il dipigne,  
> 24 quando il vapor, che il porta, più è spesso,  

Perhaps as close as the halo seemeth to gird the luminary that doth paint it, whenso the vapour which supporteth it is thickest,

- *cotanto, quanto* ‘so much, as’ → “as close as”: the degree of distance is expressed as closeness, anticipating the distance construction in the next line.
- *pare appresso alo cinger* ‘seems close a halo to gird’ → “the halo seemeth to gird”: the subject moves before the finite verb and takes a definite article; the sense of *appresso* has been carried forward into “as close as”.
- *la luce* ‘the light’ → “the luminary”: the source of light is named rather than the light.
- *il dipigne* ‘it paints’ → “doth paint it”: the object pronoun moves after the verb, and an auxiliary is added.
- *quando* ‘when’ → “whenso”: the conjunction is given the force of ‘whenever’.
- *il porta* ‘it carries’ → “supporteth it”: the verb expresses support rather than carriage, and the object follows it.
- *più è spesso* ‘more is thick’ → “is thickest”: the degree expression follows the verb as a single superlative adjective.

> 25 distante intorno al punto un cerchio d' igne  
> 26 si girava sì ratto, ch' avria vinto  
> 27 quel moto che più tosto il mondo cigne;  

at such interval around the point there wheeled a circle of fire so rapidly it had surpassed the motion which doth swiftest gird the universe;

- *distante* ‘distant’ → “at such interval”: an adjective becomes a prepositional phrase, with “such” resuming the comparison.
- *un cerchio d’ igne si girava* ‘a circle of fire was turning’ → “there wheeled a circle of fire”: the subject is postponed in a presentational construction with “there”; the pronominal imperfect becomes a simple intransitive past.
- *sì ratto, ch’* ‘so swiftly that’ → “so rapidly”: the result conjunction is unexpressed.
- *avria vinto* ‘would have outstripped’ → “it had surpassed”: a pluperfect form with hypothetical force stands for the Italian past conditional.
- *quel moto* ‘that motion’ → “the motion”: the demonstrative becomes a definite article.
- *più tosto il mondo cigne* ‘more swiftly the world girds’ → “doth swiftest gird the universe”: the degree adverb becomes superlative, an auxiliary is added, and the object follows the verb; ‘world’ is given its wider sense of “universe”.

> 28 e questo era d' un altro circuncinto,  
> 29 e quel dal terzo, e il terzo poi dal quarto,  
> 30 dal quinto il quarto, e poi dal sesto il quinto.  

and this was by a second girt around, that by a third, and the third by a fourth, by a fifth the fourth, then by a sixth the fifth.

- *d’ un altro* ‘by another’ → “by a second”: the next circle is identified by its number.
- *e quel dal terzo* ‘and that by the third’ → “that by a third”: the conjunction is dropped and the ordinal takes an indefinite article.
- *poi dal quarto* ‘then by the fourth’ → “by a fourth”: the temporal adverb is dropped and the article changes.
- *dal quinto* ‘by the fifth’ → “by a fifth”: the article becomes indefinite.
- *e poi dal sesto* ‘and then by the sixth’ → “then by a sixth”: the conjunction is dropped and the article becomes indefinite.

> 31 Sopra seguiva il settimo sì sparto  
> 32 già di larghezza, che il messo di Juno  
> 33 intero a contenerlo sarebbe arto.  

Thereafter followed the seventh, already in its stretch so far outspread that were the messenger of Juno made complete, it were too strait to hold it.

- *Sopra* ‘above’ → “Thereafter”: spatial order becomes order of succession.
- *sì sparto già di larghezza* ‘so spread already in breadth’ → “already in its stretch so far outspread”: the time adverb and the phrase of extent move forward; “its” and “far” are supplied, and breadth is phrased as stretch.
- *il messo di Juno intero* ‘the messenger of Juno entire’ → “were the messenger of Juno made complete”: the adjective becomes the predicate of a hypothetical clause, with completion expressed as something done to the messenger.
- *a contenerlo sarebbe arto* ‘to contain it would be narrow’ → “it were too strait to hold it”: the infinitive moves to the end; the conditional becomes subjunctive “were”, and “too” makes the insufficiency explicit.

> 34 Così l' ottavo e il nono; e ciascheduno  
> 35 più tardo si movea, secondo ch' era  
> 36 in numero distante più dall' uno;  

And so the eighth and ninth; and each one moved slower according as in number it was more remote from unity;

- *Così* ‘so’ → “And so”: an opening conjunction is supplied.
- *e il nono* ‘and the ninth’ → “and ninth”: the ordinal shares the preceding article.
- *più tardo si movea* ‘more slowly moved itself’ → “moved slower”: the pronominal imperfect becomes a simple intransitive past, with the comparative after it.
- *era in numero* ‘it was in number’ → “in number it was”: the phrase of respect moves before the verb.
- *distante più dall’ uno* ‘distant more from the one’ → “more remote from unity”: the degree adverb precedes the adjective, and the substantivized numeral becomes an abstract noun.

> 37 e quello avea la fiamma più sincera,  
> 38 cui men distava la favilla pura;  
> 39 credo, però che più di lei s' invera.  

and that one had the clearest flame, from which the pure spark was least distant; because, I take it, it sinketh deepest into the truth thereof.

- *la fiamma più sincera* ‘the flame most pure’ → “the clearest flame”: purity is rendered as clarity, and the superlative precedes the noun.
- *cui men distava la favilla pura* ‘from which less distant was the spark pure’ → “from which the pure spark was least distant”: the subject moves before the predicate and its adjective before the noun; the comparative becomes a superlative, and the verb of distance becomes a copula with an adjective.
- *credo, però che* ‘I believe, because’ → “because, I take it”: the qualification of belief follows the conjunction and becomes parenthetical and idiomatic.
- *più di lei s’ invera* ‘more in it makes itself true’ → “it sinketh deepest into the truth thereof”: the pronominal verb is expanded into a verb of motion and a noun of truth; degree becomes superlative depth, and the relation to the spark is expressed by the final “thereof”.

> 40 La donna mia, che mi vedeva in cura  
> 41 forte sospeso, disse: “Da quel punto  
> 42 depende il cielo, e tutta la natura.  

My Lady, who beheld me in toil of deep suspense, said: “From that point doth hang heaven and all nature.

- *La donna mia* ‘the lady my’ → “My Lady”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *mi vedeva* ‘me saw’ → “beheld me”: the object follows the verb, and the imperfect is rendered by a simple past.
- *in cura forte sospeso* ‘in concern greatly suspended’ → “in toil of deep suspense”: concern becomes “toil”; the participle describing the speaker becomes a noun dependent on “toil”, and its intensifier becomes the adjective “deep”.
- *depende* ‘hangs’ → “doth hang”: an auxiliary is added.
- *il cielo, e tutta la natura* ‘the heaven, and all the nature’ → “heaven and all nature”: both definite articles disappear.

> 43 Mira quel cerchio che più gli è congiunto,  
> 44 e sappi che il suo movere è sì tosto  
> 45 per l' affocato amore ond' egli è punto.”  

Look on that circle which is most conjoint thereto, and know its movement is so swift by reason of the enkindled love whereby ’tis pierced.”

- *Mira* ‘behold’ → “Look on”: a transitive imperative becomes a verb with a preposition.
- *più gli è congiunto* ‘more to it is joined’ → “is most conjoint thereto”: the comparative becomes a superlative, the copula comes first, and the pronominal complement moves to the end as “thereto”.
- *sappi che* ‘know that’ → “know”: the complement conjunction is unexpressed.
- *il suo movere* ‘its moving’ → “its movement”: the substantivized infinitive becomes an ordinary noun.
- *per* ‘for’ → “by reason of”: the causal preposition is expanded into a phrase.
- *ond’ egli è punto* ‘by which it is pricked’ → “whereby ’tis pierced”: the relative phrase becomes a single adverb, the subject and copula are contracted, and ‘pricked’ becomes the stronger “pierced”.

## Lines 46–78 — Measuring the Circles by Their Virtue

> 46 Ed io a lei: “Se il mondo fosse posto  
> 47 con l'ordine, ch' io veggio in quelle rote,  
> 48 sazio m'avrebbe ciò che m' è proposto.  

And I to her: “Were the universe disposed in the order I behold in these wheelings, then were I satisfied with what is set before me.

- *Se il mondo fosse posto* ‘if the world were placed’ → “Were the universe disposed”: inversion replaces the conditional conjunction; ‘world’ becomes “universe” and ‘placed’ becomes “disposed”.
- *con l'ordine, ch' io veggio* ‘with the order that I see’ → “in the order I behold”: the preposition changes, the object relative is left unexpressed, and ‘see’ becomes “behold”.
- *quelle rote* ‘those wheels’ → “these wheelings”: the demonstrative changes from distant to near, and a word for the motion replaces the name of the things moving.
- *sazio m'avrebbe ciò che m' è proposto* ‘sated me would have that which is proposed to me’ → “then were I satisfied with what is set before me”: “then” is supplied. The Italian subject follows the verb (‘what is proposed to me would have sated me’); in English the person satisfied becomes the subject, the perfect conditional gives way to “were” with a participle, and “with” introduces what satisfies him. ‘Proposed to me’ becomes “set before me”.

> 49 Ma nel mondo sensibile si puote  
> 50 veder le volte tanto più divine,  
> 51 quant' elle son dal centro più remote.  

But in the universe of sense we may see the circlings more divine as from the centre they are more removed.

- *mondo sensibile* ‘sensible world’ → “universe of sense”: ‘world’ again becomes “universe”; the adjective becomes a phrase with “of”.
- *si puote veder* ‘one can see’ → “we may see”: the impersonal construction becomes first-person plural.
- *le volte* ‘the revolutions’ → “the circlings”: the translator chooses a verbal noun suggesting continuous motion.
- *tanto più divine, quant' elle son dal centro più remote* ‘so much more divine, by how much they are from the centre more distant’ → “more divine as from the centre they are more removed”: the paired expressions of degree are reduced to a comparative with “as”; ‘from the centre’ moves before the subject, and ‘distant’ becomes “removed”.

> 52 Onde, se il mio disio dee aver fine  
> 53 in questo miro ed angelico templo,  
> 54 che solo amore e luce ha per confine,  

Wherefore, if it behoveth my desire to find its goal in this wondrous and angelic temple which hath only love and light for boundary,

- *il mio disio dee aver fine* ‘my desire must have an end’ → “it behoveth my desire to find its goal”: the subject of a personal modal verb becomes the object of an impersonal verb; ‘have an end’ becomes “find its goal”, with a possessive supplied.
- *che solo amore e luce ha per confine* ‘which only love and light has for boundary’ → “which hath only love and light for boundary”: the verb moves before its object.

> 55 udir conviemmi ancor perchè l'esemplo  
> 56 e l'esemplare non vanno d' un modo;  
> 57 chè io per me indarno ciò contemplo.”  

needs must I further hear wherefore the copy and the pattern go not in one fashion; for, for myself, I gaze on it in vain.”

- *udir conviemmi ancor* ‘to hear is needful for me still’ → “needs must I further hear”: the initial infinitive and impersonal verb with an attached pronoun become a personal modal construction; ‘still’ becomes “further” and precedes “hear”.
- *l'esemplo e l'esemplare* ‘the example and the exemplar’ → “the copy and the pattern”: the related Italian nouns are differentiated by choosing the sense ‘copy’ for the first and ‘pattern’ for the second.
- *non vanno* ‘do not go’ → “go not”: the negative follows the verb.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a coordinating conjunction replaces the subordinating one.
- *io per me indarno ciò contemplo* ‘I for myself in vain this contemplate’ → “for myself, I gaze on it in vain”: ‘for myself’ precedes the subject, ‘in vain’ moves to the end, and ‘this’ becomes “it”; a verb with a direct object becomes “gaze on” with its object following.

> 58 “Se li tuoi diti non sono a tal nodo  
> 59 sufficienti, non è maraviglia,  
> 60 tanto, per non tentare, è fatto sodo.”  

“And if for such a knot thy fingers are not able, no marvel is it; so hard hath it become by never being tried.”

- *Se li tuoi diti non sono a tal nodo sufficienti* ‘if thy fingers are not for such a knot sufficient’ → “And if for such a knot thy fingers are not able”: “And” is supplied, ‘for such a knot’ moves before the subject, and ‘sufficient’ becomes “able”.
- *non è maraviglia* ‘it is not a marvel’ → “no marvel is it”: the negation is attached to the noun, and the predicate is placed first.
- *tanto, per non tentare, è fatto sodo* ‘so much, through not attempting, has it grown hard’ → “so hard hath it become by never being tried”: “so” and “hard” are brought together at the front, and the intervening causal phrase moves to the end. Its active infinitive with no specified agent becomes passive, making the knot the thing tried; ‘not’ is strengthened to “never”.

> 61 Così la donna mia; poi disse: “Piglia  
> 62 quel ch' io ti dicerò, se vuoi saziarti,  
> 63 ed intorno da esso t' assottiglia.  

So my Lady; and then said: “Take that which I shall tell thee, wouldst thou be satisfied, and ply thy wit around it.

- *poi disse* ‘then said’ → “and then said”: a conjunction is supplied.
- *ti dicerò* ‘to thee I shall tell’ → “I shall tell thee”: the indirect object follows the verb.
- *se vuoi saziarti* ‘if thou wishest to sate thyself’ → “wouldst thou be satisfied”: inversion replaces ‘if’, and the reflexive infinitive becomes passive.
- *intorno da esso t' assottiglia* ‘about it sharpen thyself’ → “ply thy wit around it”: the reflexive verb is interpreted as an injunction to exercise the understanding, with “thy wit” as an explicit object; the phrase meaning ‘about it’ moves to the end.

> 64 Li cerchi corporai sono ampi ed arti,  
> 65 secondo il più e il men della virtute,  
> 66 che si distende per tutte lor parti.  

The corporeal circles are ample or strait according to the more or less of the virtue which spreadeth over all their parts.

- *Li cerchi corporai* ‘the circles corporeal’ → “The corporeal circles”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *ampi ed arti* ‘ample and strait’ → “ample or strait”: the conjunction makes the two sizes alternatives.
- *il più e il men* ‘the more and the less’ → “the more or less”: “or” replaces ‘and’, and the second article is dropped.
- *si distende per tutte lor parti* ‘extends itself throughout all their parts’ → “spreadeth over all their parts”: an intransitive verb replaces the reflexive, and ‘throughout’ becomes “over”.

> 67 Maggior bontà vuol far maggior salute;  
> 68 maggior salute maggior corpo cape,  
> 69 s' egli ha le parti egualmente compiute.  

Greater excellence hath purpose to work greater weal; and greater weal is comprehended in the greater body if that the parts be equally consummate.

- *bontà* ‘goodness’ → “excellence”: the translator chooses a term for degree of perfection.
- *vuol far maggior salute* ‘wills to make greater good’ → “hath purpose to work greater weal”: willing becomes having a purpose, with ‘make’ rendered as “work” and ‘good’ as “weal”.
- *maggior salute maggior corpo cape* ‘greater good a greater body holds’ → “and greater weal is comprehended in the greater body”: “and” is supplied. The initial Italian object becomes the English passive subject, and the Italian subject becomes a phrase with “in”; the body also receives a definite article.
- *s' egli ha le parti egualmente compiute* ‘if it has the parts equally perfected’ → “if that the parts be equally consummate”: “that” expands the conjunction; the body is no longer the subject of ‘has’, and its parts instead become the subject of subjunctive “be”. ‘Perfected’ is rendered as “consummate”.

> 70 Dunque costui, che tutto quanto rape  
> 71 l'altro universo seco, corrisponde  
> 72 al cerchio che più ama e che più sape.  

Therefore the one which sweepeth with it all the rest of the universe, correspondeth to the circle that most loveth and most knoweth.

- *costui* ‘this one’ → “the one”: the demonstrative force is lost.
- *tutto quanto rape l'altro universo seco* ‘the whole of the other universe it carries off with itself’ → “sweepeth with it all the rest of the universe”: the verb, which in Italian interrupts the object phrase, comes first and becomes “sweepeth”; ‘with itself’ follows it directly as “with it”. The whole of ‘the other universe’ is expressed as “all the rest of the universe”.
- *più ama e che più sape* ‘loves more and that knows more’ → “most loveth and most knoweth”: the comparatives have superlative force in context; the second relative is not repeated.

> 73 Per che, se tu alla virtù circonde  
> 74 la tua misura, non alla parvenza  
> 75 delle sustanzie che t'appaion tonde,  

Wherefore, if thou draw thy measure round the virtue, not the semblance of the substances which appear to thee in circles,

- *Per che* ‘for which reason’ → “Wherefore”: the relative phrase becomes a connective adverb.
- *alla virtù circonde la tua misura* ‘about the virtue thou settest thy measure’ → “draw thy measure round the virtue”: English distributes the sense of encircling between “draw” and “round”, puts the measure immediately after the verb, and moves the virtue to the end.
- *non alla parvenza* ‘not about the appearance’ → “not the semblance”: “round” is understood rather than repeated, and ‘appearance’ becomes “semblance”.
- *t'appaion tonde* ‘to thee appear round’ → “appear to thee in circles”: the pronoun follows the verb, and a prepositional phrase replaces the predicate adjective.

> 76 tu vederai mirabil conseguenza,  
> 77 di maggio a più e di minore a meno,  
> 78 in ciascun cielo, a sua intelligenza.”  

thou wilt see a wondrous congruence of greater unto more and smaller unto less in every heaven to its intelligence.”

- *conseguenza* ‘consequence’ → “congruence”: the noun is taken in the sense of correspondence rather than of something following.
- *di minore a meno* ‘of smaller to less’ → “smaller unto less”: the “of” introducing the first pair is not repeated.
- *in ciascun cielo, a sua intelligenza* ‘in each heaven, to its intelligence’ → “in every heaven to its intelligence”: distributive ‘each’ becomes “every”.

## Lines 79–111 — The Angelic Choirs and the Vision of Truth

> 79 Come rimane splendido e sereno  
> 80 l' emisperio dell'aer, quando soffia  
> 81 Borea da quella guancia ond' è più leno,  

As the hemisphere of air becometh shining and serene when Boreas bloweth from his gentler cheek,

- *rimane splendido e sereno l' emisperio dell'aer* ‘remains bright and serene the hemisphere of the air’ → “the hemisphere of air becometh shining and serene”: the subject moves before the verb, the article before ‘air’ drops out, and remaining bright becomes a change of state.
- *soffia Borea* ‘blows Boreas’ → “Boreas bloweth”: subject and verb exchange places.
- *da quella guancia ond' è più leno* ‘from that cheek whence he is gentler’ → “from his gentler cheek”: the possessive replaces the demonstrative, and the comparison passes from a relative clause about Boreas to an adjective modifying the cheek.

> 82 per che si purga e risolve la roffia  
> 83 che pria turbava, sì che il ciel ne ride  
> 84 con le bellezze d' ogni sua paroffia;  

whereby is purged and is resolved the film which erst obscured it, so that the heaven laugheth with the beauties of its every district;

- *si purga e risolve* ‘clears and dissolves’ → “is purged and is resolved”: the construction with *si* becomes two explicit passives.
- *la roffia* ‘the murk’ → “the film”: a word for obscuring matter is rendered as a thin covering.
- *che pria turbava* ‘which formerly troubled’ → “which erst obscured it”: the verb is made specifically visual, and its understood object is expressed.
- *il ciel ne ride* ‘the heaven laughs for it’ → “the heaven laugheth”: the pronoun marking the cause of the laughter is left out.
- *d' ogni sua paroffia* ‘of every district of its own’ → “of its every district”: the possessive moves before the distributive adjective.

> 85 così fec' io, poi che mi provvide  
> 86 la donna mia del suo risponder chiaro,  
> 87 e, come stella in cielo, il ver si vide.  

so did I, when my Lady had made provision to me of her clear-shining answer; and like a star in heaven the truth was seen.

- *poi che* ‘after’ → “when”: the conjunction marks time without itself specifying that the answer came first.
- *mi provvide la donna mia del suo risponder chiaro* ‘provided me my lady with her clear answering’ → “my Lady had made provision to me of her clear-shining answer”: the subject moves before the verb and its possessive before the noun; the simple past becomes a pluperfect phrase with a noun, and the recipient follows it in a prepositional phrase. The substantivized infinitive becomes ‘answer’, with the expanded adjective placed before it.
- *il ver si vide* ‘one saw the true’ → “the truth was seen”: the substantivized adjective becomes an abstract noun, and the construction with *si* is rendered by an explicit passive.

> 88 E poi che le parole sue restaro,  
> 89 non altrimenti ferro disfavilla  
> 90 che bolle, come i cerchi sfavillaro.  

And when her words stayed, no otherwise doth iron shoot forth sparkles, when it boileth, than did the circles sparkle.

- *poi che* ‘after’ → “when”: explicit succession becomes a temporal connection.
- *le parole sue* ‘the words of hers’ → “her words”: the postposed possessive moves before the noun.
- *non altrimenti ferro disfavilla* ‘not otherwise does iron sparkle’ → “no otherwise doth iron shoot forth sparkles”: the negative modifies the adverb directly, and the single verb becomes a verb of emission with an object.
- *che bolle* ‘that boils’ → “when it boileth”: the relative clause modifying ‘iron’ becomes a temporal clause.
- *come i cerchi sfavillaro* ‘as the circles sparkled’ → “than did the circles sparkle”: English completes the comparison with “than” and divides the past verb between an auxiliary before the subject and an infinitive after it.

> 91 Lo incendio lor seguiva ogni scintilla;  

And every spark followed their blaze;

- *Lo incendio lor seguiva ogni scintilla* ‘the blaze of theirs followed every spark’ (object–verb–subject) → “And every spark followed their blaze”: Wicksteed puts the subject first and the object last, moves the possessive before its noun, and supplies the opening conjunction.

> 92 ed eran tante, che il numero loro  
> 93 più che il doppiar degli scacchi s' immilla.  

and their numbers were such as ran to thousands beyond the duplication of the chessboard.

- *ed eran tante, che il numero loro* ‘and they were so many that their number’ → “and their numbers were such as”: the sparks cease to be the grammatical subject; their singular ‘number’ becomes the plural subject of the main clause, and the result clause becomes a comparison with “such as”.
- *più che il doppiar degli scacchi s' immilla* ‘more than the doubling of the chess squares thousandfolds itself’ → “ran to thousands beyond the duplication of the chessboard”: the coined reflexive verb becomes a phrase of numerical extent, moves ahead of the comparison, and changes from present to past. The board as a whole replaces its plural squares.

> 94 Io sentiva osannar di coro in coro  
> 95 al punto fisso che li tiene all'*ubi*,  
> 96 e terrà sempre, nel qual sempre foro;  

From choir to choir I heard Hosanna sung to that fixed point which holdeth and shall ever hold them to the *where*, in which they have been ever;

- *Io sentiva osannar di coro in coro* ‘I was hearing hosanna-ing from choir to choir’ → “From choir to choir I heard Hosanna sung”: the phrase of progression moves to the front, the imperfect becomes a simple past, and the active infinitive becomes the name of what is sung followed by a passive participle.
- *al punto fisso* ‘to the point fixed’ → “to that fixed point”: a demonstrative replaces the article, and the adjective moves before the noun.
- *che li tiene all'ubi, e terrà sempre* ‘which them holds at the where, and will hold always’ → “which holdeth and shall ever hold them to the *where*”: the verbs are brought together before their shared complements, the object pronoun follows them, ‘at’ becomes “to”, and the adverb moves inside the future verb phrase.
- *nel qual sempre foro* ‘in which they always were’ → “in which they have been ever”: the simple past becomes a present perfect, and the adverb moves after the verb.

> 97 e quella, che vedeva i pensier dubi  
> 98 nella mia mente, disse: “I cerchi primi  
> 99 t' hanno mostrati i Serafi e i Cherubi.  

and she who saw the questioning thoughts within my mind, said: “The first circles have revealed to thee the Seraphs and the Cherubs.

- *vedeva* ‘was seeing’ → “saw”: a simple past renders the imperfect.
- *i pensier dubi* ‘the thoughts doubtful’ → “the questioning thoughts”: the adjective moves before the noun, and uncertainty is rendered as questioning.
- *nella mia mente* ‘in my mind’ → “within my mind”: the preposition is made more emphatically locative.
- *I cerchi primi* ‘the circles first’ → “The first circles”: the ordinal moves before the noun.
- *t' hanno mostrati* ‘to thee have shown’ → “have revealed to thee”: the recipient moves after the verb, ‘shown’ becomes “revealed”, and the participle no longer marks agreement with the plural objects that follow.

> 100 Così veloci seguono i suoi vimi,  
> 101 per simigliarsi al punto quanto ponno,  
> 102 e posson quanto a veder son sublimi.  

So swift they follow their withies that they may liken them unto the point as most they may; and they may in measure as they are sublime in vision.

- *per simigliarsi al punto* ‘in order to make themselves like the point’ → “that they may liken them unto the point”: the infinitive of purpose becomes a finite clause with a modal, and “them” does duty for the reflexive.
- *quanto ponno* ‘as much as they can’ → “as most they may”: the expression of degree takes “most”, and ability is expressed by “may”.
- *e posson quanto a veder son sublimi* ‘and they can according as in seeing they are sublime’ → “and they may in measure as they are sublime in vision”: the proportional force of *quanto* is expanded, ability is again expressed by “may”, and the infinitive specifying the respect in which they are sublime becomes a noun phrase placed last.

> 103 Quegli altri amor, che intorno a lor vonno,  
> 104 si chiaman Troni del divin aspetto,  
> 105 perchè il primo ternaro terminonno.  

Those other loves which course around them are named Thrones of the divine aspect, because they brought to its completion the first ternary.

- *che intorno a lor vonno* ‘which around them go’ → “which course around them”: the verb moves before the phrase of place, and ‘go’ becomes the more specific “course”.
- *si chiaman* ‘are called’ → “are named”: the naming construction with *si* becomes an English passive with a different verb.
- *perchè il primo ternaro terminonno* ‘because the first ternary they completed’ → “because they brought to its completion the first ternary”: the object moves after the verb, and the simple verb becomes a phrase with a noun and possessive.

> 106 E dei saper che tutti hanno diletto,  
> 107 quanto la sua veduta si profonda  
> 108 nel vero, in che si queta ogn' intelletto.  

And thou shouldst know that all have their delight in measure as their sight sinketh more deep into the truth wherein every intellect is stilled.

- *dei saper* ‘thou must know’ → “thou shouldst know”: obligation is rendered less categorically.
- *hanno diletto* ‘have delight’ → “have their delight”: a possessive is supplied.
- *quanto la sua veduta si profonda* ‘according as their sight deepens’ → “in measure as their sight sinketh more deep”: the proportional conjunction is expanded, and the pronominal verb becomes a verb of motion with an explicit comparative.
- *nel vero* ‘into the true’ → “into the truth”: an abstract noun replaces the substantivized adjective.
- *si queta ogn' intelletto* ‘comes to rest every intellect’ → “every intellect is stilled”: the subject moves before the verb, and coming to rest is rendered by a passive.

> 109 Quinci si può veder come si fonda  
> 110 l' esser beato nell' atto che vede,  
> 111 non in quel ch' ama, che poscia seconda;  

Hence may be seen how the being blessed is founded on the act that seeth, not that which loveth, which after followeth;

- *si può veder* ‘one can see’ → “may be seen”: the impersonal construction becomes a modal passive.
- *come si fonda l' esser beato* ‘how is founded the being blessed’ → “how the being blessed is founded”: the infinitival subject moves before the verb, and the construction with *si* becomes an explicit passive.
- *nell' atto* ‘in the act’ → “on the act”: the preposition changes to suit the English construction with “founded”.
- *non in quel ch' ama* ‘not in that which loves’ → “not that which loveth”: the second preposition is left understood.
- *che poscia seconda* ‘which afterward comes second’ → “which after followeth”: the verb of taking second place is rendered by the more general verb of following.

## Lines 112–139 — The Ranks of the Angelic Orders

> 112 e del vedere è misura mercede,  
> 113 che grazia partorisce e buona voglia;  

and the measure of sight is the merit which grace begetteth and the righteous will;

- *del vedere è misura mercede* ‘of seeing is measure merit’ → “the measure of sight is the merit”: the substantivized infinitive becomes the noun “sight”; its phrase follows “measure” instead of coming first. The English supplies definite articles and puts “merit” after the verb rather than setting ‘measure’ and ‘merit’ together after it.
- *buona voglia* ‘good will’ → “the righteous will”: the English supplies an article and makes ‘good’ more specifically “righteous”.

> 114 così di grado in grado si procede.  

and thus from rank to rank the progress goeth.

- *così* ‘thus’ → “and thus”: the conjunction is supplied.
- *di grado in grado* ‘from rank into rank’ → “from rank to rank”: “to” marks the succeeding rank.
- *si procede* ‘one proceeds’ → “the progress goeth”: the impersonal verb becomes a noun with a verb of motion.

> 115 L' altro ternaro, che così germoglia  
> 116 in questa primavera sempiterna,  
> 117 che notturno Ariete non dispoglia,  

The second ternary which thus flowereth in this eternal spring which nightly Aries doth not despoil,

- *L' altro ternaro* ‘the other ternary’ → “The second ternary”: the English gives the group's position rather than calling it ‘the other’.
- *germoglia* ‘buds’ → “flowereth”: flowering replaces putting forth shoots.
- *primavera sempiterna* ‘spring everlasting’ → “eternal spring”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *non dispoglia* ‘strips not’ → “doth not despoil”: the English forms the negative with an auxiliary.

> 118 perpetualmente Osanna sverna  
> 119 con tre melode, che suonano in tree  
> 120 ordini di letizia, onde s' interna.  

unceasingly unwintereth Hosanna with three melodies which sound in the three orders of gladness, whereof it is three-plied.

- *perpetualmente* ‘perpetually’ → “unceasingly”: the English expresses duration as absence of interruption.
- *Osanna sverna* ‘Hosanna it sings’ → “unwintereth Hosanna”: the verb comes before its object. Where the Italian verb can signify singing as birds do in spring, the English coinage renders its suggestion of winter's passing rather than saying ‘sings’.
- *in tree* ‘in three’ → “in the three”: the definite article is supplied.
- *onde s' interna* ‘whereby it makes itself threefold’ → “whereof it is three-plied”: on the reading of the verb as formed from ‘three’, the English substitutes a state of composition for the reflexive action, and “whereof” presents the orders as constituents.

> 121 In essa gerarchia son le tre dee:  

In that hierarchy are the three divinities,

- *essa gerarchia* ‘that same hierarchy’ → “that hierarchy”: the demonstrative does not separately express the emphasis on identity.
- *dee* ‘goddesses’ → “divinities”: the English noun is not specifically feminine.

> 122 prima Dominazioni, e poi Virtudi;  
> 123 l'ordine terzo di Podestadi ee.  

first Dominations, and then Virtues; the third order is of Powers.

- *l'ordine terzo di Podestadi ee* ‘the order third of Powers is’ → “the third order is of Powers”: the ordinal precedes its noun, and the verb precedes the phrase naming the order.

> 124 Poscia nei due penultimi tripudi  
> 125 Principati ed Arcangeli si girano;  

Then in the two last-save-one upleapings, Principalities and Archangels whirl;

- *penultimi* ‘penultimate’ → “last-save-one”: a compound phrase replaces the single adjective.
- *tripudi* ‘joyous dances’ → “upleapings”: the English singles out the leaping movement.
- *si girano* ‘turn themselves’ → “whirl”: the reflexive becomes an intransitive verb suggesting rapid rotation.

> 126 l'ultimo è tutto d' Angelici ludi.  

the last consisteth all of Angelic sports.

- *è tutto d'* ‘is wholly of’ → “consisteth all of”: a verb expressly denoting composition replaces ‘is’.

> 127 Questi ordini di su tutti rimirano,  

These orders all gaze upward,

- *di su tutti rimirano* ‘above all gaze’ → “all gaze upward”: the direction is put after the verb, leaving “all” next to the subject.

> 128 e di giù vincon sì che verso Dio  
> 129 tutti tirati sono e tutti tirano.  

and downward have such conquering might that toward God all are drawn and all draw.

- *vincon sì* ‘prevail so’ → “have such conquering might”: the act of prevailing becomes possession of a power; the adverb of degree becomes a determiner qualifying “conquering might”.
- *tirati sono* ‘drawn are’ → “are drawn”: the auxiliary precedes the participle.

> 130 E Dionisio con tanto disio  
> 131 a contemplar questi ordini si mise,  
> 132 che li nomò e distinse com' io.  

And Dionysius with such yearning set himself to contemplate these orders that he named them and distinguished them as I.

- *a contemplar questi ordini si mise* ‘to contemplate these orders set himself’ → “set himself to contemplate these orders”: the governing verb and its reflexive pronoun precede the infinitive phrase, and the pronoun follows the verb.
- *li nomò e distinse* ‘them he named and distinguished’ → “he named them and distinguished them”: the single object pronoun before the first verb becomes a pronoun after each verb.

> 133 Ma Gregorio da lui poi si divise;  

But Gregory afterward departed from him,

- *da lui poi si divise* ‘from him afterward separated himself’ → “afterward departed from him”: the reflexive verb becomes intransitive, and the phrase denoting separation follows it.

> 134 onde, sì tosto come l'occhio aperse  
> 135 in questo ciel, di sè medesmo rise.  

wherefore so soon as he opened his eye in this heaven he smiled at his own self.

- *l'occhio aperse* ‘the eye he opened’ → “he opened his eye”: the object follows the verb, and a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *di sè medesmo rise* ‘at his very self he laughed’ → “he smiled at his own self”: the English chooses the milder sense of the verb and puts it before the phrase denoting its object.

> 136 E se tanto segreto ver proferse  
> 137 mortale in terra, non voglio ch' ammiri;  

And if so hidden truth was uttered forth by mortal upon earth, I would not have thee marvel;

- *tanto segreto ver proferse mortale* ‘so hidden a truth uttered a mortal’ → “so hidden truth was uttered forth by mortal”: the fronted object becomes the subject of a passive verb, and the Italian subject becomes the agent introduced by “by”. “Forth” gives the force of the Italian verb in a separate word.
- *in terra* ‘on earth’ → “upon earth”: “upon” renders the locative preposition.
- *non voglio ch' ammiri* ‘I do not wish that thou shouldst marvel’ → “I would not have thee marvel”: a present verb of wishing with a subjunctive clause becomes “would not have” with an object pronoun and bare infinitive.

> 138 chè chi il vide quassù gliel discoperse  
> 139 con altro assai del ver di questi giri.”  

for he who saw it here above revealed it to him, with much beside of truth about these circles.”

- *chi* ‘whoever’ → “he who”: the single relative with its antecedent understood becomes a pronoun followed by a relative.
- *gliel discoperse* ‘to him it uncovered’ → “revealed it to him”: the two pronouns attached to one another before the verb are separated and placed after it, direct object first; figurative uncovering becomes explicit revelation.
- *con altro assai* ‘with other things in abundance’ → “with much beside”: the expression of quantity comes first, and “beside” conveys the sense of something additional.
- *del ver di questi giri* ‘of the truth of these circles’ → “of truth about these circles”: the article is omitted, and the second ‘of’ is interpreted as marking a topic.

## Summary — Changes of Order, Construction, and Diction

Wicksteed often brings a postposed Italian subject before its verb, puts an object pronoun after the verb, or moves an adjective before its noun: *vita presente* becomes “present life”. Two routine changes generally pass unremarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where Italian gives the person in the verb ending, and drops the article before a possessive, as in *la mia memoria*, “my memory”. Elsewhere Wicksteed supplies a connective such as “and” or “then”, or leaves a conjunction understood: *sappi che* ‘know that’ becomes “know”.

Changes of order sometimes go with a recasting of the clause. With *aperse il vero* ‘opened the truth’, the Italian object becomes the subject of “the truth had been revealed by her”: the passive accommodates the delayed mention of the agent, and the past perfect conveys the succession less explicitly marked by “When” than by *Poscia che*. An impersonal construction can become personal, as when *si puote veder* ‘one can see’ becomes “we may see”. Wicksteed also expands compact verbs: *s’ invera* finds expression in “sinketh deepest into the truth thereof”, while *s’ immilla* becomes “ran to thousands”.

The choice of words can narrow a meaning or bring out a contrast. *Corda* ‘cord’ becomes “noose”, specifying its use; *l’esemplo e l’esemplare* becomes “the copy and the pattern”, distinguishing the two terms rather than matching their likeness in sound. Yet Wicksteed does not regularize every turn of phrase. He keeps the fronted object of *un punto vidi* in “a point I saw”, follows the shifting order of the numbered circles in “by a fifth the fourth, then by a sixth the fifth”, and preserves both the repetition and the change of voice in *tutti tirati sono e tutti tirano*, “all are drawn and all draw”.
