# Paradiso 14

## Lines 1–12 — Ripples and the Need for Another Truth

> 1 Dal centro al cerchio, e sì dal cerchio al centro,  
> 2 movesi l' acqua in un ritondo vaso,  
> 3 secondo ch' è percossa fuori o dentro.  

From centre to circumference and again from circumference to centre vibrates the water in a rounded vessel according as ’tis smitten from without or from within.

- *Dal centro al cerchio* ‘from the centre to the circle’ → “From centre to circumference”: the articles drop out, and “circumference” specifies the boundary of the circle.
- *e sì* ‘and so’ → “and again”: the adverb marks the return direction rather than the likeness of the two movements.
- *dal cerchio al centro* ‘from the circle to the centre’ → “from circumference to centre”: as in the outward movement, the articles drop out and “circumference” replaces ‘circle’.
- *movesi* ‘moves itself’ → “vibrates”: a more specific verb of motion replaces the pronominal verb.
- *ritondo* ‘round’ → “rounded”: a participial adjective replaces the simple adjective.
- *fuori o dentro* ‘outside or inside’ → “from without or from within”: the repeated “from” makes the two adverbs indicate the source of the blow.

> 4 Nella mia mente fe' subito caso  
> 5 questo ch' io dico, sì come si tacque  
> 6 la gloriosa vita di Tommaso,  

Into my mind this thought dropped sudden, just as the glorious life of Thomas held its peace,

- *Nella mia mente* ‘in my mind’ → “Into my mind”: place becomes direction.
- *fe' subito caso* ‘made a sudden fall’ → “dropped sudden”: the verb and its object become a single verb of falling, with “sudden” used adverbially after it.
- *questo ch' io dico* ‘this that I say’ → “this thought”: the relative clause gives way to a noun identifying ‘this’, and the subject moves before the verb of falling.
- *si tacque la gloriosa vita di Tommaso* ‘fell silent the glorious life of Thomas’ → “the glorious life of Thomas held its peace”: the subject moves before the verb, and the pronominal verb of becoming silent gives way to a possessive idiom.

> 7 per la similitudine che nacque  
> 8 del suo parlare e di quel di Beatrice,  

because of the resemblance that sprang from his discourse, and then from Beatrice’s,

- *del suo parlare* ‘from his speaking’ → “from his discourse”: a noun replaces the substantivized infinitive.
- *e di quel di Beatrice* ‘and from that of Beatrice’ → “and then from Beatrice’s”: “then” is supplied, and the independent possessive replaces the demonstrative followed by an *of*-phrase.

> 9 a cui sì cominciar dopo lui piacque:  

whom to begin thus after him it pleased:

- *a cui* ‘to whom’ → “whom”: English makes the person pleased a direct object rather than an indirect one.
- *sì cominciar* ‘thus to begin’ → “to begin thus”: the adverb follows the infinitive.
- *piacque* ‘was pleasing’ → “it pleased”: English supplies a formal “it” for the construction with the infinitive.

> 10 “A costui fa mestieri, e nol vi dice  
> 11 nè con la voce nè pensando ancora,  
> 12 d' un altro vero andare alla radice.  

“This man hath need, and telleth it you not, neither with voice, nor as yet with his thought, to track another truth unto its root.

- *A costui fa mestieri* ‘to this one it is needful’ → “This man hath need”: the masculine demonstrative is expanded with “man”, and the person to whom something is needful becomes the subject of a personal verb of possession.
- *e nol vi dice* ‘and not it to you he tells’ → “and telleth it you not”: the object pronouns move after the verb, in the order ‘it, you’, and the negative comes last.
- *nè con la voce* ‘neither with the voice’ → “neither with voice”: the definite article drops out.
- *nè pensando ancora* ‘nor by thinking yet’ → “nor as yet with his thought”: the gerund becomes a prepositional phrase parallel to “with voice”, a possessive is supplied, and the temporal adverb is expanded and moved forward.
- *d' un altro vero andare alla radice* ‘of another truth to go to the root’ → “to track another truth unto its root”: the substantivized adjective becomes the noun “truth”; the phrase modifying ‘root’ becomes the direct object of “track”, a transitive verb in place of ‘go’. The supplied possessive “its” restores the link between truth and root.

## Lines 13–45 — The Enduring Radiance of the Blessed

> 13 Ditegli se la luce, onde s' infiora  
> 14 vostra sustanzia, rimarrà con voi  
> 15 eternamente sì com' ella è ora;  

Tell if the light wherewith your being blossometh, eternally will cleave to you as now,

- *Ditegli* ‘tell him’ → “Tell”: the indirect object is omitted.
- *s' infiora vostra sustanzia* ‘flowers itself your substance’ → “your being blossometh”: the subject moves before the verb; ‘substance’ becomes “being,” and a simple intransitive verb replaces the reflexive.
- *rimarrà con voi eternamente* ‘will remain with you eternally’ → “eternally will cleave to you”: the adverb moves forward, and remaining with someone is expressed as cleaving to them.
- *sì com' ella è ora* ‘just as it is now’ → “as now”: the emphatic ‘just’ and the subject and verb of the comparison are omitted.

> 16 e, se rimane, dite come, poi  
> 17 che sarete visibili rifatti,  
> 18 esser potrà ch' al veder non vi noi.”  

and if it doth remain, tell how, when ye grow visible again, it may not grieve your vision.”

- *se rimane* ‘if it remains’ → “if it doth remain”: the simple present is expanded with the auxiliary.
- *poi che sarete visibili rifatti* ‘after you will have been made visible again’ → “when ye grow visible again”: ‘after’ becomes “when”; English uses a present in the temporal clause, and the passive construction becomes the intransitive “grow.”
- *esser potrà ch' al veder non vi noi* ‘will it be possible that in seeing it not trouble you’ → “it may not grieve your vision”: the future of possibility and the dependent subjunctive are compressed into one modal construction. Seeing becomes the object of “grieve,” and the person affected becomes its possessor.

> 19 Come da più letizia pinti e tratti  
> 20 alla fiata quei che vanno a rota  
> 21 levan la voce e rallegrano gli atti,  

As by access of gladness thrust and drawn, at once all they who circle in the dance uplift their voice and gladden their gestures,

- *da più letizia* ‘by greater gladness’ → “by access of gladness”: the comparative becomes a noun denoting an increase.
- *alla fiata* ‘at times’ → “at once”: the translation takes the phrase as marking simultaneous rather than occasional action.
- *quei che vanno a rota* ‘those who go in a ring’ → “all they who circle in the dance”: “all” is supplied, circular movement becomes the verb, and the dance is made explicit.
- *levan la voce* ‘raise the voice’ → “uplift their voice”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *rallegrano gli atti* ‘gladden the movements’ → “gladden their gestures”: a possessive again replaces the article, and the movements are specified as gestures.

> 22 così all' orazion pronta e devota  
> 23 li santi cerchi mostrar nuova gioia  
> 24 nel tornear e nella mira nota.  

so at the eager and devoted prayer the sacred circles showed new joy in their revolving and their wondrous note.

- *all' orazion pronta e devota* ‘at the prayer ready and devout’ → “at the eager and devoted prayer”: both adjectives precede the noun; readiness becomes eagerness, and ‘devout’ becomes “devoted.”
- *li santi cerchi* ‘the holy circles’ → “the sacred circles”: “sacred” replaces ‘holy.’
- *mostrar* ‘show’ → “showed”: the shortened preterite plural is expressed by the English past tense, not by the infinitive that the shortened form resembles.
- *nel tornear* ‘in the turning’ → “in their revolving”: a possessive replaces the article of the substantivized infinitive.
- *e nella mira nota* ‘and in the wondrous note’ → “and their wondrous note”: English lets the preceding preposition govern both nouns rather than repeating it, and substitutes a possessive for the article.

> 25 Qual si lamenta perchè qui si moia,  
> 26 per viver colassù, non vide quive  
> 27 lo refrigerio dell' eterna ploia.  

Whoso lamenteth that we here must die to live up yonder seeth not here the refreshment of the eternal shower.

- *si lamenta* ‘laments himself’ → “lamenteth”: the Italian pronominal verb needs no reflexive in English.
- *perchè qui si moia* ‘because here one dies’ → “that we here must die”: a clause of complaint replaces one of cause; the impersonal subjunctive becomes first-person plural, and “must” makes necessity explicit.
- *non vide quive* ‘did not see there’ → “seeth not here”: the past becomes present and the adverb of place changes from ‘there’ to “here.”

> 28 Quell' uno e due e tre che sempre vive,  
> 29 e regna sempre in tre e due e uno,  
> 30 non circonscritto, e tutto circonscrive,  

That One and Two and Three who ever liveth and reigneth ever in Three and Two and One, not circumscribed, but all circumscribing,

- *e tutto circonscrive* ‘and circumscribes all’ → “but all circumscribing”: the conjunction becomes adversative, the finite verb becomes a participle, and its object moves before it.

> 31 tre volte era cantato da ciascuno  
> 32 di quegli spirti con tal melodia,  
> 33 ch' ad ogni merto saria giusto muno.  

three times was hymned by each one of those spirits with such melody as were a fit reward to any merit.

- *era cantato* ‘was sung’ → “was hymned”: the English verb specifies the kind of singing.
- *ch' ad ogni merto saria giusto muno* ‘that to every merit it would be a just reward’ → “as were a fit reward to any merit”: “as” completes the English comparison after “such”; the conditional becomes subjunctive “were,” the reward becomes “fit” rather than ‘just,’ and the phrase specifying the merit moves to the end, with “any” for ‘every.’

> 34 Ed io udi' nella luce più dia  
> 35 del minor cerchio una voce modesta,  
> 36 forse qual fu dall' angelo a Maria,  

And I heard in the divinest light of the smaller circle an unassuming voice, perchance such as the Angel’s unto Mary,

- *nella luce più dia* ‘in the light most divine’ → “in the divinest light”: the postposed adjective and its separate degree word become an inflected adjective before the noun.
- *del minor cerchio* ‘of the lesser circle’ → “of the smaller circle”: “smaller” specifies size.
- *una voce modesta* ‘a voice modest’ → “an unassuming voice”: the adjective moves before the noun, and “unassuming” renders modesty of manner.
- *qual fu dall' angelo a Maria* ‘such as was from the angel to Mary’ → “such as the Angel’s unto Mary”: the verb and the preposition of source give way to a possessive standing for the angel’s voice.

> 37 risponder: “Quanto fia lunga la festa  
> 38 di Paradiso, tanto il nostro amore  
> 39 si raggerà dintorno cotal vesta.  

answering: “As long as the festival of Paradise shall be, so long our love shall cast round us the rays of such a garment.

- *risponder* ‘answer’ → “answering”: the infinitive dependent on the verb of hearing becomes a present participle.
- *Quanto fia lunga la festa di Paradiso* ‘however long shall be the festival of Paradise’ → “As long as the festival of Paradise shall be”: the adjective is drawn into the opening comparison and the subject precedes the verb.
- *si raggerà dintorno cotal vesta* ‘will ray about itself such a garment’ → “shall cast round us the rays of such a garment”: the idea of radiating passes from the verb into the noun “rays,” with “cast” as the verb; the reflexive reference becomes “us,” and the garment, rather than being the direct object, qualifies the rays.

> 40 La sua chiarezza seguirà l' ardore,  
> 41 l' ardor la visione, e quella è tanta,  
> 42 quanta ha di grazia sopra il suo valore.  

Its brightness shall keep pace with our ardour, our ardour with our vision, and that shall be as great as it hath grace beyond its proper worth.

- *seguirà l' ardore* ‘will follow the ardour’ → “shall keep pace with our ardour”: following becomes keeping pace with, and a possessive replaces the article.
- *l' ardor la visione* ‘the ardour the vision’ → “our ardour with our vision”: possessives replace the articles; “with” carries forward the construction of the preceding English verb phrase.
- *e quella è tanta* ‘and that is so great’ → “and that shall be as great”: the present becomes future, and “as great” opens the English comparison.
- *quanta ha di grazia* ‘as much as it has of grace’ → “as it hath grace”: the second expression of quantity and its partitive construction are absorbed into the comparison.
- *sopra il suo valore* ‘above its worth’ → “beyond its proper worth”: “beyond” renders excess over a measure, and “proper” reinforces the possessive.

> 43 Come la carne gloriosa e santa  
> 44 fia rivestita, la nostra persona  
> 45 più grata fia per esser tutta quanta.  

Whenas the garment of the glorified and sainted flesh shall be resumed, our person shall be more acceptable by being all complete.

- *la carne gloriosa e santa fia rivestita* ‘the flesh glorious and holy shall be put on again’ → “the garment of the glorified and sainted flesh shall be resumed”: the image in the Italian verb is made explicit by supplying “the garment” as subject, with the flesh dependent on it. The adjectives move before “flesh” and take participial form.
- *più grata fia* ‘more pleasing shall be’ → “shall be more acceptable”: the verb precedes its complement, and “acceptable” renders ‘pleasing.’
- *per esser tutta quanta* ‘for being whole in its entirety’ → “by being all complete”: the ground is expressed with “by” and a gerund rather than a preposition and infinitive; “all” intensifies “complete.”

## Lines 46–78 — The Risen Flesh and the New Circle

> 46 Per che s' accrescerà ciò che ne dona  
> 47 di gratuito lume il sommo bene;  
> 48 lume ch' a lui veder ne condiziona:  

Whereby shall grow that which the highest Good giveth to us of unearned light, light which enableth us him to see;

- *s' accrescerà* ‘will be increased’ → “shall grow”: the pronominal verb becomes an intransitive verb.
- *ne dona di gratuito lume il sommo bene* ‘to us gives of freely given light the highest Good’ → “the highest Good giveth to us of unearned light”: the subject moves from the end to before the verb, and the indirect object moves after it; the adjective describes the light as unearned rather than as freely bestowed.
- *ch' a lui veder ne condiziona* ‘which for seeing him fits us’ → “which enableth us him to see”: the governing verb and its object move before the infinitive phrase; the construction with *a* becomes an infinitive dependent on “enableth”.

> 49 onde la vision crescer conviene,  
> 50 crescer l' ardor che di quella s' accende,  
> 51 crescer lo raggio che da esso viene.  

wherefore the vision must needs wax, and wax the ardour which is kindled by it, and wax the ray which goeth forth from it.

- *crescer conviene* ‘to increase is necessary’ → “must needs wax”: the impersonal expression of necessity becomes a modal governing the verb of increase.
- *crescer l' ardor* ‘increase the ardour’ → “and wax the ardour”: a conjunction is supplied before the repeated infinitive.
- *di quella s' accende* ‘from that catches fire’ → “is kindled by it”: the pronominal verb becomes passive; the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun, and its phrase follows the verb.
- *crescer lo raggio* ‘increase the ray’ → “and wax the ray”: again a conjunction is supplied.
- *da esso viene* ‘from it comes’ → “goeth forth from it”: coming becomes going forth, and the phrase of origin moves after the verb.

> 52 Ma sì come carbon che fiamma rende,  
> 53 e per vivo candor quella soperchia  
> 54 sì che la sua parvenza si difende,  

But like the coal which giveth forth the flame, and by its living glow o’ercometh it, so that its own appearance is maintained,

- *sì come carbon* ‘just as coal’ → “like the coal”: the comparative becomes a preposition, and the noun takes an article.
- *fiamma rende* ‘flame gives out’ → “giveth forth the flame”: the object follows the verb and takes an article.
- *per vivo candor* ‘by living brightness’ → “by its living glow”: the possessor is made explicit, and brightness is rendered as glow.
- *quella soperchia* ‘that it surpasses’ → “o’ercometh it”: the demonstrative object becomes a personal pronoun after the verb; surpassing becomes overcoming.
- *la sua parvenza si difende* ‘its appearance defends itself’ → “its own appearance is maintained”: the possessive is reinforced, and the reflexive image gives way to a passive of preservation.

> 55 così questo fulgor, che già ne cerchia,  
> 56 fia vinto in apparenza dalla carne  
> 57 che tutto dì la terra ricoperchia;  

so shall this glow which doth already swathe us, be conquered in appearance by the flesh which yet and yet the earth o’ercovereth;

- *questo fulgor … fia vinto* ‘this radiance … will be overcome’ → “shall this glow” … “be conquered”: the finite auxiliary moves before the subject, leaving the rest of the passive after the relative clause; radiance becomes glow.
- *già ne cerchia* ‘already encircles us’ → “doth already swathe us”: an auxiliary is supplied, the object follows the verb, and encircling becomes wrapping.
- *tutto dì* ‘all the time’ → “yet and yet”: repetition conveys continuance in place of the Italian expression of duration.

> 58 nè potrà tanta luce affaticarne,  
> 59 chè gli organi del corpo saran forti  
> 60 a tutto ciò che potrà dilettarne.”  

nor shall such light have power to baffle us, for the organs of the body shall be strong to all that may delight us.”

- *potrà tanta luce affaticarne* ‘will be able, so much light, to weary us’ → “shall such light have power to baffle us”: the finite verb of ability is distributed between an auxiliary before the subject and a phrase after it; quantity becomes quality, and wearying becomes baffling.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: coordination replaces subordination.
- *potrà dilettarne* ‘will be able to delight us’ → “may delight us”: the future expression of capacity becomes a modal of possibility without an explicit future.

> 61 Tanto mi parver subiti ed accorti  
> 62 e l' uno e l' altro coro a dicer: *Amme*,  
> 63 che ben mostrar disio dei corpi morti;  

So swift and eager to cry *Amen*, meseemed, was the one and the other chorus, that verily they showed desire for their dead bodies;

- *mi parver* ‘to me they seemed’ → “meseemed, was”: the plural verb of seeming becomes an impersonal parenthesis, with a separate singular linking verb for the two choruses.
- *subiti ed accorti* ‘prompt and alert’ → “swift and eager”: the adjectives are brought forward before the expression of seeming; alertness is interpreted as eagerness.
- *a dicer* ‘to say’ → “to cry”: the infinitive phrase moves before the subject and gives the utterance greater force.
- *ben mostrar* ‘well they showed’ → “verily they showed”: the adverb becomes an affirmation.
- *disio dei corpi morti* ‘desire of the bodies dead’ → “desire for their dead bodies”: the object of desire is introduced by “for”, possession replaces the definite article, and the adjective precedes the noun.

> 64 forse non pur per lor, ma per le mamme,  
> 65 per li padri, e per gli altri che fur cari,  
> 66 anzi che fosser sempiterne fiamme.  

not only, as I take it, for themselves, but for their mothers and their fathers and the others who were dear, ere they became eternal flames.

- *forse non pur* ‘perhaps not only’ → “not only, as I take it”: the adverb of uncertainty becomes a first-person parenthesis and moves after the restrictive phrase.
- *per lor* ‘for them’ → “for themselves”: the reference back to the subject is made explicitly reflexive.
- *per le mamme* ‘for the mothers’ → “for their mothers”: a possessive replaces the article, and the English noun is less familiar in register.
- *per li padri* ‘for the fathers’ → “and their fathers”: a conjunction replaces the repeated preposition, and a possessive replaces the article.
- *per gli altri* ‘for the others’ → “the others”: the preposition is not repeated.
- *che fosser sempiterne fiamme* ‘that they were everlasting flames’ → “they became eternal flames”: after “ere” no separate conjunction is needed; the subjunctive of being is interpreted as a change of state and rendered by a simple past.

> 67 Ed ecco intorno, di chiarezza pari,  
> 68 nascere un lustro sopra quel che v' era,  
> 69 a guisa d' orizzonte che rischiari.  

And lo! around, of lustre equable, upspring a shining beyond what was there, in fashion of a brightening horizon.

- *di chiarezza pari* ‘of equal brightness’ → “of lustre equable”: brightness becomes lustre, and equability takes the place of equality.
- *nascere un lustro* ‘to be born a radiance’ → “upspring a shining”: the infinitive presented by *ecco* becomes a bare verb of upward movement; the noun is rendered by a verbal noun.
- *sopra quel che v' era* ‘above that which was there’ → “beyond what was there”: the spatial relation is rendered as extension beyond rather than position above, and the antecedent and relative are combined in “what”.
- *d' orizzonte che rischiari* ‘of a horizon that grows bright’ → “of a brightening horizon”: the relative clause with a subjunctive becomes a participle before the noun.

> 70 E sì come al salir di prima sera  
> 71 comincian per lo ciel nuove parvenze,  
> 72 sì che la vista pare e non par vera;  

And as, at the first rise of evening, new things-to-see begin to show in heaven, so that the sight doth, yet doth not, seem real;

- *sì come* ‘just as’ → “as”: the reinforcing element is not separately rendered.
- *al salir di prima sera* ‘at the rising of early evening’ → “at the first rise of evening”: the nominal infinitive becomes a noun, and “first” qualifies the rise rather than the evening.
- *comincian per lo ciel nuove parvenze* ‘begin throughout the sky new appearances’ → “new things-to-see begin to show in heaven”: the subject moves before the verb and is recast as things available to sight; “to show” is supplied, and the spatial phrase moves to the end, with “in” for ‘throughout’.
- *pare e non par vera* ‘seems and does not seem real’ → “doth, yet doth not, seem real”: the repeated verb is expressed once, after two auxiliaries, and the conjunction is made adversative.

> 73 parvemi lì novelle sussistenze  
> 74 cominciar a vedere, e fare un giro  
> 75 di fuor dall' altre due circonferenze.  

I there began perceive new-come existences making a circle out beyond the other two circumferences.

- *parvemi … cominciar a vedere* ‘it seemed to me … to begin to see’ → “I there began perceive”: the qualification of seeming drops out; the perceiver becomes the subject of a finite verb of beginning, followed by a bare verb of perception.
- *novelle sussistenze* ‘new subsistences’ → “new-come existences”: the object moves after the verb of perception; newness is interpreted as recent arrival, and the technical noun becomes the broader “existences”.
- *e fare un giro* ‘and to make a circle’ → “making a circle”: the conjunction disappears, and the infinitive becomes a participle modifying the beings perceived.
- *di fuor dall' altre due circonferenze* ‘outside the other two circumferences’ → “out beyond the other two circumferences”: exterior position is expressed by a doubled spatial phrase.

> 76 O vero isfavillar del santo spiro,  
> 77 come si fece subito e candente  
> 78 agli occhi miei che vinti non soffriro!  

Oh very sparkling of the Holy Breath! how sudden and how glowing it became before my eyes, which, vanquished, might not bear it!

- *isfavillar* ‘act of flashing forth’ → “sparkling”: the nominal infinitive becomes an English verbal noun, without a separate expression of outward movement.
- *come si fece subito e candente* ‘how it made itself sudden and incandescent’ → “how sudden and how glowing it became”: the pronominal verb becomes a verb of change of state after the adjectives; the exclamatory adverb is repeated, and incandescence becomes glowing.
- *agli occhi miei* ‘to the eyes of mine’ → “before my eyes”: the preposition changes, and the possessive precedes the noun.
- *non soffriro* ‘did not endure’ → “might not bear it”: the simple past becomes an expression of inability, and an object is supplied.

## Lines 79–111 — The Luminous Cross in Mars

> 79 Ma Beatrice sì bella e ridente  
> 80 mi si mostrò, che tra quelle vedute  
> 81 si vuol lasciar che non seguir la mente.  

But Beatrice showed herself to me so beauteous and smiling, it must be left amongst those sights that followed not my memory.

- *sì bella e ridente mi si mostrò* ‘so beauteous and smiling to me herself showed’ → “showed herself to me so beauteous and smiling”: the verb comes first, the two pronouns follow it in reverse order, and the description comes last.
- *che tra quelle vedute si vuol lasciar* ‘that amongst those sights one must leave it’ → “it must be left amongst those sights”: the result conjunction is left implicit, the impersonal construction becomes a passive with an explicit subject, and the phrase giving the place follows the verb.
- *che non seguir la mente* ‘which the memory did not follow’ → “that followed not my memory”: the sights become the subject of “followed” and memory its object, rather than the reverse; the possessive replaces the article.

> 82 Quindi ripreser gli occhi miei virtute  
> 83 a rilevarsi, e vidimi translato  
> 84 sol con mia donna in più alta salute.  

Therefrom my eyes regained their power to uplift them, and I saw me transported, only with my Lady, to more exalted weal.

- *ripreser gli occhi miei virtute* ‘regained the eyes mine power’ → “my eyes regained their power”: the subject precedes the verb, its possessive precedes the noun, and a second possessive is supplied before “power”.
- *a rilevarsi* ‘to raise themselves again’ → “to uplift them”: the sense of renewed movement is not separately expressed, and the object is a personal rather than an explicitly reflexive pronoun.
- *vidimi* ‘saw myself’ → “I saw me”: “me” does duty for the reflexive.
- *in più alta salute* ‘into higher blessedness’ → “to more exalted weal”: the preposition marks the destination, and height and blessedness are expressed as exaltation and weal.

> 85 Ben m' accors' io ch' io era più levato,  
> 86 per l' affocato riso della stella,  
> 87 che mi parea più roggio che l' usato.  

Surely did I perceive that I was more uplifted by the enkindled smile of the star which seemed to me more ruddy than his wont.

- *Ben m' accors' io* ‘well did I become aware’ → “Surely did I perceive”: the adverb expresses assurance, and a simple verb of perception replaces the pronominal verb of becoming aware.
- *più levato* ‘higher up’ → “more uplifted”: the comparative of position is expressed in terms of being raised.
- *per* ‘from’ → “by”: the smile is the sign from which the speaker perceives the change; the English preposition can also attach to “uplifted”.
- *mi parea* ‘to me seemed’ → “seemed to me”: the pronoun follows the verb.
- *che l' usato* ‘than the customary’ → “than his wont”: a possessive referring to the star replaces the article.

> 88 Con tutto il core, e con quella favella  
> 89 ch' è una in tutti, a Dio feci olocausto,  
> 90 qual conveniasi alla grazia novella;  

With all the heart, and in that tongue which is one unto all, to God I made burnt sacrifice such as befitted this new-given grace;

- *con quella favella* ‘with that tongue’ → “in that tongue”: the preposition changes from instrument to medium.
- *in tutti* ‘in all’ → “unto all”: what is one in all is phrased as one for all.
- *olocausto* ‘a burnt offering’ → “burnt sacrifice”: the single noun becomes a descriptive phrase without an article.
- *qual conveniasi alla grazia novella* ‘such as was fitting to the grace new’ → “such as befitted this new-given grace”: a transitive verb replaces the pronominal verb with its preposition; the article becomes demonstrative, and the adjective moves before the noun and gains the notion of giving.

> 91 e non er' anco del mio petto esausto  
> 92 l' ardor del sacrificio, ch' io conobbi  
> 93 esso litare stato accetto e fausto;  

and not yet from my bosom was drawn out the ardour of the sacrifice before I knew the prayer had been accepted and propitious;

- *non er' anco del mio petto esausto* ‘was not yet from my breast exhausted’ → “not yet from my bosom was drawn out”: the negative and the phrase of origin precede the auxiliary, and exhaustion is expressed as a drawing out.
- *ch' io conobbi* ‘when I recognized’ → “before I knew”: the temporal relation is recast with “before” after “not yet”, and recognition is rendered as knowledge.
- *esso litare stato accetto e fausto* ‘that very act of sacrificing to have been acceptable and auspicious’ → “the prayer had been accepted and propitious”: “the prayer” replaces the emphatically specified act of sacrifice; the past infinitive becomes a finite past perfect, and the adjective of acceptability becomes a participle of acceptance.

> 94 chè con tanto lucore e tanto robbi  
> 95 m' apparvero splendor dentro a due raggi  
> 96 ch' io dissi: “O Elios che sì gli addobbi!”  

for with such shining, and so ruddy, within two rays, splendours appeared to me, that I exclaimed: “O God! who thus dost glorify them!”

- *con tanto lucore* ‘with so much radiance’ → “with such shining”: degree is expressed by “such”, and a verbal noun takes the place of the noun for radiance.
- *tanto robbi* ‘so red’ → “so ruddy”: a less direct colour term is used.
- *m' apparvero splendor dentro a due raggi* ‘to me appeared splendours within two rays’ → “within two rays, splendours appeared to me”: the place comes first, the subject precedes the verb, and the pronoun follows it.
- *dissi* ‘said’ → “exclaimed”: the manner of utterance is made explicit.
- *Elios* ‘Elios’ → “God”: the translation gives the understood addressee rather than the name used in the invocation.
- *gli addobbi* ‘them dost adorn’ → “dost glorify them”: glorification replaces adornment, and the object follows the verb.

> 97 Come, distinta da minori e maggi  
> 98 lumi, biancheggia tra i poli del mondo  
> 99 Galassia sì che fa dubbiar ben saggi,  

As, pricked out with less and greater lights, between the poles of the universe the Milky Way so gleameth white as to set very sages questioning,

- *distinta da minori e maggi lumi* ‘marked by smaller and greater lights’ → “pricked out with less and greater lights”: the participle becomes a more pictorial phrase, “with” replaces the preposition of agency, and smaller size is expressed by “less”.
- *biancheggia tra i poli del mondo Galassia* ‘gleams white between the poles of the world the Galaxy’ → “between the poles of the universe the Milky Way so gleameth white”: place and subject are brought before the verb; “universe” renders the cosmic sense of the word for world, and the familiar English name replaces ‘Galaxy’.
- *sì che fa dubbiar ben saggi* ‘so that it makes even sages doubt’ → “so” … “as to set very sages questioning”: the degree word is moved back before the verb of gleaming, the finite result clause becomes an infinitive, and a causative with an infinitive of doubting becomes “set” with a participle of questioning.

> 100 sì costellati facean nel profondo  
> 101 Marte quei rai il venerabil segno,  
> 102 che fan giunture di quadranti in tondo.  

so did those rays, star-decked, make in the depth of Mars the venerable sign which crossing quadrant lines make in a circle.

- *sì costellati facean nel profondo Marte quei rai* ‘so, bestarred, made in deep Mars those rays’ → “so did those rays, star-decked, make in the depth of Mars”: an auxiliary precedes the subject and the main verb follows it; the description of the rays moves after them, and the adjective of depth becomes a noun governing “of Mars”.
- *il venerabil segno* ‘the sign worthy of veneration’ → “the venerable sign”: the adjective is rendered by its shorter English cognate.
- *che fan giunture di quadranti in tondo* ‘which make the junctions of quadrants in a round’ → “which crossing quadrant lines make in a circle”: the subject moves before the verb; junctions are expressed as lines that cross, and the substantivized ‘round’ becomes the noun “circle”.

> 103 Qui vince la memoria mia lo ingegno:  

Here my memory doth outrun my wit,

- *vince la memoria mia lo ingegno* ‘surpasses the memory mine the intellect’ → “my memory doth outrun my wit”: the subject precedes the verb and its possessive precedes the noun; surpassing becomes outrunning, and the second faculty too receives a possessive.

> 104 chè quella croce lampeggiava Cristo,  
> 105 sì ch' io non so trovare esempio degno.  

for that cross so flashed forth Christ I may not find example worthy.

- *lampeggiava* ‘was flashing’ → “flashed forth”: the simple past does not mark the ongoing aspect of the imperfect, and “forth” gives the flash a direction.
- *sì ch' io non so trovare* ‘so that I know not how to find’ → “so” … “I may not find”: the degree word moves before the verb of flashing, the result conjunction is left implicit, and a modal verb replaces knowing how.

> 106 Ma chi prende sua croce e segue Cristo,  
> 107 ancor mi scuserà di quel ch' io lasso,  
> 108 vedendo in quell' albor balenar Cristo.  

But whoso taketh his cross and followeth Christ shall yet forgive me what I leave unsaid, when he shall see Christ lighten in that glow.

- *ancor mi scuserà* ‘yet me will excuse’ → “shall yet forgive me”: the adverb follows the auxiliary, the pronoun follows the main verb, and excusing is rendered as forgiving.
- *di quel ch' io lasso* ‘for that which I leave’ → “what I leave unsaid”: the preposition disappears in the construction with “forgive”, the relative contracts to “what”, and “unsaid” specifies the omission.
- *vedendo in quell' albor balenar Cristo* ‘seeing in that radiance Christ flash’ → “when he shall see Christ lighten in that glow”: the gerund becomes a finite temporal clause in the future; the place moves to the end, and Christ precedes rather than follows the infinitive of flashing.

> 109 Di corno in corno, e tra la cima e il basso,  
> 110 si movean lumi, scintillando forte  
> 111 nel congiungersi insieme e nel trapasso.  

From horn to horn, from summit unto base, were moving lights that sparkled mightily in meeting one another and in passing.

- *e tra la cima e il basso* ‘and between the summit and the bottom’ → “from summit unto base”: the conjunction and articles disappear, and a span between two points becomes a movement from one to the other.
- *si movean lumi* ‘moved themselves lights’ → “were moving lights”: the pronominal imperfect becomes an English progressive without a reflexive.
- *scintillando forte* ‘sparkling strongly’ → “that sparkled mightily”: the gerund becomes a finite relative clause, and the intensifier is rendered by “mightily”.
- *nel congiungersi insieme* ‘in joining themselves together’ → “in meeting one another”: meeting replaces joining, and reflexive plus adverb becomes an explicit reciprocal.
- *nel trapasso* ‘in the passage across’ → “in passing”: an article and noun give way to a gerund, without a separate expression for movement across.

## Lines 112–139 — The Cross’s Melody and the Delight of Beatrice’s Eyes

> 112 Così si veggion qui diritte e torte,  
> 113 veloci e tarde, rinnovando vista,  
> 114 le minuzie dei corpi, lunghe e corte,  

So we see here, straight, twisted, swift, or slow, changing appearance, long or short, the motes of bodies

- *si veggion* ‘are seen’ → “we see”: the passive construction becomes active, with the observers as subject.
- *diritte e torte, veloci e tarde* ‘straight and crooked, swift and slow’ → “straight, twisted, swift, or slow”: “twisted” renders *torte*; the first conjunction drops out and the second becomes disjunctive.
- *rinnovando vista* ‘renewing appearance’ → “changing appearance”: change rather than renewal is expressed.
- *le minuzie dei corpi, lunghe e corte* ‘the minute particles of the bodies, long and short’ → “long or short, the motes of bodies”: the last pair of adjectives moves before the noun phrase, “or” replaces ‘and’, “motes” gives a specific sense to ‘minute particles’, and the article belonging to ‘bodies’ drops out.

> 115 moversi per lo raggio, onde si lista  
> 116 talvolta l' ombra, che per sua difesa  
> 117 la gente con ingegno ed arte acquista.  

moving through the ray which doth sometimes streak the shade, which folk with skill and art contrive for their defence.

- *moversi* ‘to move’ → “moving”: the infinitive dependent on the verb of seeing becomes a participle.
- *onde si lista talvolta l' ombra* ‘with which is sometimes striped the shade’ → “which doth sometimes streak the shade”: the instrumental relative becomes the subject of an active verb, the shade becomes its object, and the adverb stands between the auxiliary and the main verb.
- *per sua difesa la gente con ingegno ed arte acquista* ‘for its defence the people with ingenuity and art obtains’ → “folk with skill and art contrive for their defence”: the purpose phrase moves to the end; the singular collective with its article becomes “folk” with plural agreement and “their”; “skill” renders ‘ingenuity’, and “contrive” puts the emphasis on making rather than obtaining.

> 118 E come giga ed arpa, in tempra tesa  
> 119 di molte corde, fa dolce tintinno  
> 120 a tal da cui la nota non è intesa,  

And as viol and harp tuned in harmony of many cords, make sweet chiming to one by whom the notes are not apprehended

- *in tempra tesa di molte corde* ‘in a tuning stretched taut of many strings’ → “tuned in harmony of many cords”: “tuned” qualifies the instruments directly; “harmony” expresses the accord of the tuning, without separately expressing the tension.
- *fa dolce tintinno* ‘makes sweet tinkling’ → “make sweet chiming”: the singular verb becomes plural with the two instruments as subject; “chiming” renders the sound noun.
- *a tal* ‘to such a one’ → “to one”: the demonstrative force of *tal* drops out.
- *la nota non è intesa* ‘the note is not understood’ → “the notes are not apprehended”: the singular becomes plural, with corresponding change of agreement, and “apprehended” renders ‘understood’.

> 121 così dai lumi che lì m' apparinno  
> 122 s' accogliea per la croce una melode,  
> 123 che mi rapiva senza intender l' inno.  

so from the lights that there appeared to me was gathered on the cross a strain that rapt me albeit I followed not the hymn.

- *lì m' apparinno* ‘there to me appeared’ → “there appeared to me”: the indirect object follows the verb.
- *s' accogliea* ‘was gathering together’ → “was gathered”: the imperfect middle construction is rendered as a past passive, without marking the ongoing action.
- *per la croce* ‘along the cross’ → “on the cross”: a preposition of position replaces one suggesting distribution.
- *una melode* ‘a melody’ → “a strain”: a different musical noun is used.
- *mi rapiva* ‘me was carrying away’ → “rapt me”: the object follows the verb, and a simple past renders the imperfect.
- *senza intender l' inno* ‘without understanding the hymn’ → “albeit I followed not the hymn”: the preposition and infinitive become a concessive finite clause; “followed not” expresses the lack of understanding.

> 124 Ben m' accors' io ch' ell' era d' alte lode,  
> 125 però che a me venìa: “Risurgi e vinci,”  
> 126 com' a colui che non intende ed ode.  

Well I discerned it was of lofty praise, for there came to me “Rise thou up and conquer,” as to who understandeth not, but heareth.

- *m' accors' io ch' ell' era* ‘became aware I that it was’ → “I discerned it was”: the emphatically postposed subject moves before the verb, the pronominal verb becomes transitive, and the conjunction ‘that’ drops out.
- *d' alte lode* ‘of high praises’ → “of lofty praise”: “lofty” renders ‘high’, and an English mass noun replaces the plural.
- *però che* ‘because’ → “for”: a coordinating explanation replaces the causal subordinate clause.
- *a me venìa* ‘to me was coming’ → “there came to me”: presentational “there” is supplied, the recipient follows the verb, and the simple past renders the imperfect.
- *Risurgi* ‘rise again’ → “Rise thou up”: “up” replaces the sense of ‘again’, and the imperative has an explicit “thou”.
- *a colui che* ‘to him who’ → “to who”: the demonstrative antecedent is not separately rendered.
- *e* ‘and’ → “but”: coordination becomes explicitly adversative.

> 127 Io m' innamorava tanto quinci,  
> 128 che infino a lì non fu alcuna cosa  
> 129 che mi legasse con sì dolci vinci.  

And so was I enamoured there, that up till then there had been naught that me had bound with so sweet chains.

- *Io m' innamorava tanto quinci* ‘I was falling in love so much from this’ → “And so was I enamoured there”: “And” is supplied; the degree word moves to the front, with inversion of verb and subject. The process expressed by the pronominal imperfect becomes a state, and “there” gives *quinci* a local rather than a source sense.
- *infino a lì* ‘up to there’ → “up till then”: the limit is expressed in time rather than place.
- *non fu alcuna cosa* ‘there was not anything’ → “there had been naught”: the simple past becomes pluperfect, and the negation and ‘any thing’ are combined in “naught”.
- *mi legasse* ‘might bind me’ → “me had bound”: the imperfect subjunctive after the negative antecedent becomes a pluperfect indicative.
- *vinci* ‘bonds’ → “chains”: the more general noun is made specific.

> 130 Forse la mia parola par tropp' osa,  
> 131 posponendo il piacer degli occhi belli,  
> 132 nei quai mirando mio disio ha posa.  

Perchance my saying may appear too bold, as slighting the delight of those fair eyes, gazing in which my longing hath repose.

- *la mia parola* ‘my word’ → “my saying”: the noun is rendered as the act or product of saying.
- *par tropp' osa* ‘appears too daring’ → “may appear too bold”: the modal “may” adds a second expression of possibility alongside “Perchance”; “bold” renders ‘daring’.
- *posponendo* ‘putting second’ → “as slighting”: relative ranking becomes depreciation, and “as” is supplied to mark the apparent ground of the charge.
- *degli occhi belli* ‘of the eyes fair’ → “of those fair eyes”: the article becomes demonstrative, and the adjective precedes its noun.
- *nei quai mirando* ‘in which gazing’ → “gazing in which”: the participle moves before the relative phrase.
- *mio disio ha posa* ‘my desire has rest’ → “my longing hath repose”: “longing” and “repose” render ‘desire’ and ‘rest’.

> 133 Ma chi s' avvede che i vivi suggelli  
> 134 d' ogni bellezza più fanno più suso,  
> 135 e ch' io non m' era lì rivolto a quelli,  

But he who doth advise him how the living signets of all beauty have ever more effect in higher region, and that I there had not yet turned to them,

- *chi s' avvede che* ‘whoever perceives that’ → “he who doth advise him how”: the indefinite relative gains an explicit antecedent, perception is expressed by the reflexive idiom “advise him”, and declarative ‘that’ becomes “how”.
- *più fanno più suso* ‘do more higher up’ → “have ever more effect in higher region”: ‘do more’ is expanded into an expression of effect, “ever” marks progression, and a noun of place is supplied for the comparative adverb.
- *non m' era lì rivolto a quelli* ‘had not there turned myself toward those’ → “there had not yet turned to them”: the place adverb moves before the auxiliary, “yet” is supplied, the pronominal verb becomes intransitive, and the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun.

> 136 escusar puommi di quel ch' io m' accuso  
> 137 per escusarmi, e vedermi dir vero:  

may find excuse from my own accusation, brought that I may excuse it; and may see that I speak truth;

- *escusar puommi di quel ch' io m' accuso* ‘can excuse me for that of which I accuse myself’ → “may find excuse from my own accusation”: the modal moves first; ‘excuse me’ becomes a verb and noun without the personal object, and the relative clause with its reflexive verb becomes a possessive noun phrase.
- *per escusarmi* ‘in order to excuse myself’ → “brought that I may excuse it”: “brought” is supplied to qualify “accusation”; the purpose infinitive becomes a finite clause, and “it” replaces the reflexive object.
- *e vedermi dir vero* ‘and see me speak true’ → “and may see that I speak truth”: the governing modal is repeated, the object and infinitive become a finite content clause, and adverbial ‘true’ becomes the noun “truth”.

> 138 chè il piacer santo non è qui dischiuso,  
> 139 perchè si fa, montando, più sincero.  

for the sacred joy is not excluded here, which as it mounteth groweth more unalloyed.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the causal conjunction is rendered by a coordinating one.
- *il piacer santo* ‘the delight holy’ → “the sacred joy”: both noun and adjective are recast, and the adjective moves before the noun.
- *non è qui dischiuso* ‘is not here shut out’ → “is not excluded here”: “excluded” renders the privative sense of *dischiuso*, and the place adverb follows the participle.
- *perchè si fa, montando, più sincero* ‘because it becomes, ascending, purer’ → “which as it mounteth groweth more unalloyed”: the translation takes the final clause as relative to the joy rather than as a causal explanation. The gerund becomes a finite clause introduced by “as” and placed before the main verb; “groweth” renders becoming, and “more unalloyed” expresses increasing purity.

## Summary — Recasting the Syntax, Keeping the Repetitions

Wicksteed often brings a subject forward or moves a pronoun after its verb: in *m’ apparvero splendor*, ‘to me appeared splendours’, both changes produce “splendours appeared to me”. The rearrangements are not uniformly toward ordinary English order, however. In *ch’ a lui veder ne condiziona*, the governing verb moves forward, but the object of the infinitive still precedes it: “which enableth us him to see”. Two routine differences generally pass unmarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending identifies the subject, and omits the article before a possessive, as in *il nostro amore*, “our love”.

Changes of construction often carry a shift of emphasis. The impersonal *A costui fa mestieri*, ‘to this one it is needful’, becomes the personal “This man hath need”; *senza intender l’ inno*, ‘without understanding the hymn’, becomes a finite clause that makes the contrast explicit: “albeit I followed not the hymn”. Wicksteed can compress as well as expand: *sì com’ ella è ora*, ‘just as it is now’, contracts to “as now”, while *Ditegli*, ‘tell him’, loses its recipient in “Tell”. Elsewhere a supplied word narrows the sense: *quel ch’ io lasso*, ‘what I leave’, becomes “what I leave unsaid”.

The choice of a verb can sharpen an image: *movesi*, ‘moves’, becomes “vibrates”, and *ne cerchia*, ‘encircles us’, becomes “swathe us”, picking up the earlier image of a garment. Yet Wicksteed stays close to the Italian where an effect depends on the order or recurrence of words. He keeps the reversal from “One and Two and Three” to “Three and Two and One”, answers each occurrence of *crescer* with “wax”, and preserves the paradox of *pare e non par vera* in “doth, yet doth not, seem real”. In the last example it is the auxiliary rather than the main verb that repeats: the grammar changes, but the hesitation remains audible.
