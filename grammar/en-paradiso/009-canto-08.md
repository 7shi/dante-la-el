# Paradiso 8

## Lines 1–15 — The Ascent to Venus

> 1 Solea creder lo mondo in suo periclo  
> 2 che la bella Ciprigna il folle amore  
> 3 raggiasse, volta nel terzo epiciclo:  

The world was wont to think in its peril that the fair Cyprian rayed down mad love, rolled in the third epicycle;

- *Solea creder lo mondo* ‘was wont to believe the world’ → “The world was wont to think”: the subject moves from after the infinitive to before the finite verb; ‘believe’ becomes “think”.
- *il folle amore raggiasse* ‘the mad love she might ray forth’ → “rayed down mad love”: the verb moves before its object, the article is dropped, and “down” makes the direction explicit. The Italian has an imperfect subjunctive after the verb of belief; the English has a simple past.

> 4 per che non pure a lei facean onore  
> 5 di sacrificio e di votivo grido  
> 6 le genti antiche nell' antico errore,  

wherefore not only to her did they do honour of sacrifice and votive cry, those ancient folk in the ancient error,

- *facean onore* ‘did honour’ → “did they do honour”: after the fronted “not only to her”, English uses an inverted auxiliary with “they” anticipating the later noun subject.
- *le genti antiche* ‘the peoples ancient’ → “those ancient folk”: the article becomes a demonstrative, the adjective moves before the noun, and the plural noun becomes the collective “folk”, in apposition to “they”.

> 7 ma Dione onoravano e Cupido,  
> 8 quella per madre sua, questo per figlio,  

but Dione did they honour, and Cupid, the one as her mother, the other as her son,

- *Dione onoravano e Cupido* ‘Dione they honoured and Cupid’ → “Dione did they honour, and Cupid”: the objects still stand on either side of the verb, but English uses an inverted auxiliary and an infinitive in place of the single finite verb.
- *quella per madre sua, questo per figlio* ‘that one as mother of hers, this one as son’ → “the one as her mother, the other as her son”: the contrasting demonstratives become a ‘one … other’ pair; the possessive moves before the first noun and is supplied with the second.

> 9 e dicean ch' ei sedette in grembo a Dido;  

and told how he had sat in Dido’s lap;

- *dicean ch'* ‘they said that’ → “told how”: ‘said’ becomes “told”, and the declarative conjunction becomes “how”.
- *sedette* ‘sat’ → “had sat”: the simple past becomes a past perfect.
- *in grembo a Dido* ‘in the lap of Dido’ → “in Dido’s lap”: the Italian marks the possessor with a phrase after the noun; English puts the possessive before it.

> 10 e da costei, ond' io principio piglio,  
> 11 pigliavan il vocabol della stella  

and from her from whom I take my start, they took the name of the star

- *costei* ‘this woman’ → “her”: the personal pronoun loses the demonstrative force.
- *principio piglio* ‘a beginning I take’ → “I take my start”: the object follows the verb, ‘beginning’ becomes “start”, and a possessive is supplied.
- *pigliavan* ‘they used to take’ → “they took”: the simple past does not expressly mark the habitual force of the imperfect.
- *il vocabol* ‘the word’ → “the name”: English specifies the word’s function as a designation.

> 12 che il sol vagheggia or da coppa, or da ciglio.  

which courts the sun, now from the nape, now from the brow.

- *il sol vagheggia* ‘the sun it gazes on amorously’ → “courts the sun”: the object moves after the verb, and the amorous regard is rendered as courtship.
- *da coppa … da ciglio* ‘from nape … from brow’ → “from the nape” … “from the brow”: English supplies a definite article with each noun.

> 13 Io non m' accorsi del salire in ella;  

I had no sense of rising into her,

- *non m' accorsi* ‘I did not notice’ → “I had no sense”: the negative verb of perception, pronominal in Italian, becomes a verb of possession with a negated noun.
- *del salire in ella* ‘of the ascent into her’ → “of rising into her”: the Italian infinitive used as a noun with the article becomes an English gerund without an article.

> 14 ma d' esservi entro mi fece assai fede  
> 15 la donna mia, ch' io vidi far più bella.  

but my lady gave me full faith that I was there, because I saw her grow more beautiful.

- *d' esservi entro* ‘of being there within’ → “that I was there”: the infinitive becomes a finite clause placed after “faith”; “there” renders the locative pronoun, without a separate word for ‘within’.
- *mi fece assai fede la donna mia* ‘made me ample assurance the lady of mine’ → “my lady gave me full faith”: the subject moves from last to first, with its possessive before the noun; the idiom for giving sufficient assurance becomes “gave me full faith”.
- *ch' io vidi far più bella* ‘whom I saw become more beautiful’ → “because I saw her grow more beautiful”: the relative clause modifying ‘my lady’ is rendered causally, with “her” as the object of “saw”; “grow” expresses the change of state.

## Lines 16–48 — The Circling Lights Draw Near

> 16 E come in fiamma favilla si vede,  
> 17 e come in voce voce si discerne,  
> 18 quando una è ferma e l' altra va e riede;  

And as we see a spark within a flame, and as a voice within a voice may be distinguished, if one stayeth firm, and the other cometh and goeth;

- *in fiamma favilla si vede* ‘in a flame a spark is seen’ → “we see a spark within a flame”: the impersonal observer becomes an explicit “we”; the verb comes first, the spark becomes its object, and the phrase locating it moves to the end.
- *in voce voce si discerne* ‘within a voice a voice is distinguished’ → “a voice within a voice may be distinguished”: the voice distinguished comes before the voice containing it, and “may” makes the possibility of distinguishing it explicit.
- *quando* ‘when’ → “if”: a temporal conjunction becomes conditional.
- *è ferma* ‘is steady’ → “stayeth firm”: the verb of being becomes one of remaining.
- *va e riede* ‘goes and returns’ → “cometh and goeth”: the English idiom reverses the directions and does not specify a return.

> 19 vid' io in essa luce altre lucerne  
> 20 moversi in giro più e men correnti,  
> 21 al modo, credo, di lor viste eterne.  

so in that light itself I perceived other torches moving in a circle more and less swift, after the measure, I suppose, of their eternal vision.

- *vid' io in essa luce* ‘saw I in that very light’ → “so in that light itself I perceived”: “so” completes the preceding comparisons explicitly; the phrase of place comes first, the intensive follows “light,” and the subject precedes the verb. ‘Saw’ becomes “perceived.”
- *altre lucerne* ‘other lamps’ → “other torches”: the kind of light named changes.
- *moversi* ‘move themselves’ → “moving”: the reflexive infinitive after a verb of perception becomes an intransitive participle.
- *correnti* ‘running’ → “swift”: a participle of motion becomes an adjective of speed.
- *al modo* ‘according to the manner’ → “after the measure”: manner is interpreted as degree.
- *credo* ‘I believe’ → “I suppose”: the verb of belief becomes one of conjecture.
- *di lor viste eterne* ‘of their visions eternal’ → “of their eternal vision”: the plural becomes a collective singular, and the adjective precedes the noun.

> 22 Di fredda nube non disceser venti,  
> 23 o visibili o no, tanto festini,  
> 24 che non paressero impediti e lenti  

From a chill cloud there ne’er descended blasts, or visible or no, so rapidly as not to seem hindered and lagging

- *Di fredda nube non disceser venti* ‘from a cold cloud did not descend winds’ → “From a chill cloud there ne’er descended blasts”: “there” introduces the postponed subject, simple negation becomes ‘never,’ and “blasts” is more specific than ‘winds.’
- *tanto festini* ‘so swift’ → “so rapidly”: an adjective agreeing with the winds becomes an adverb modifying their descent.
- *che non paressero* ‘that they would not seem’ → “as not to seem”: the finite subjunctive result clause becomes an infinitive construction.
- *lenti* ‘slow’ → “lagging”: an adjective becomes a participial expression.

> 25 a chi avesse quei lumi divini  
> 26 veduti a noi venir, lasciando il giro  
> 27 pria cominciato in gli alti Serafini.  

to whoso should have seen those lights divine advance towards us, quitting the circling that hath its first beginning in the exalted Seraphim.

- *quei lumi divini veduti* ‘those lights divine seen’ → “seen those lights divine”: the participle moves from after its object to before it.
- *a noi venir* ‘towards us come’ → “advance towards us”: ‘come’ becomes “advance,” and the phrase of direction follows the verb.
- *lasciando il giro* ‘leaving the circuit’ → “quitting the circling”: the noun is rendered as an activity rather than a circuit.
- *pria cominciato* ‘first begun’ → “that hath its first beginning”: the past participle becomes a finite relative clause, with ‘begun’ recast as a possessed noun and ‘first’ modifying that noun.
- *alti* ‘high’ → “exalted”: height is expressed as elevation in rank.

> 28 E dentro a quei che più innanzi appariro,  
> 29 sonava *Osanna* sì che unque poi  
> 30 di riudir non fui senza disiro.  

And within those who most in front appeared, *Hosannah* sounded in such wise that never since have I been free from longing to re-hear it.

- *sonava Osanna* ‘sounded Hosannah’ → “Hosannah sounded”: the subject precedes the verb.
- *unque poi di riudir non fui senza disiro* ‘ever afterward of hearing again I was not without desire’ → “never since have I been free from longing to re-hear it”: the negation moves into “never since,” and the simple past becomes the present perfect. ‘Without desire’ becomes “free from longing”; the complement of ‘desire’ follows it as an infinitive, with “it” supplied as object.

> 31 Indi si fece l' un più presso a noi,  
> 32 e solo incominciò: “Tutti sem presti  
> 33 al tuo piacer, perchè di noi ti gioi.  

Then one drew himself more nigh to us, and alone began: “All we are ready at thy will, that thou mayst have thy joy of us.

- *si fece l' un più presso* ‘made himself the one nearer’ → “one drew himself more nigh”: the subject loses the definite article and moves before the verb; ‘made himself’ becomes “drew himself.”
- *Tutti sem presti* ‘all are-we ready’ → “All we are ready”: “we” separates the quantifier from the verb, rather than leaving ‘all’ to stand as the subject expression.
- *al tuo piacer* ‘at thy pleasure’ → “at thy will”: pleasure is taken in the sense of what the addressee wills.
- *perchè di noi ti gioi* ‘so that in us thou mayst rejoice’ → “that thou mayst have thy joy of us”: the reflexive verb of rejoicing becomes “have” with a possessive noun, and its complement moves to the end.

> 34 Noi ci volgiam coi Principi celesti  
> 35 d' un giro, e d' un girare, e d' una sete,  
> 36 ai quali tu del mondo già dicesti:  

We roll with those celestial Princes in one circle and in one circling and in one thirst, to whom thou from the world didst sometime say:

- *ci volgiam* ‘turn ourselves’ → “roll”: the reflexive construction becomes an intransitive verb.
- *coi Principi celesti* ‘with the Princes celestial’ → “with those celestial Princes”: a demonstrative replaces the definite article, and the adjective moves before the noun.
- *d' un giro, e d' un girare, e d' una sete* ‘of one circle and of one turning and of one thirst’ → “in one circle and in one circling and in one thirst”: the repeated preposition becomes “in”; “circle” and “circling” reproduce the noun–infinitive distinction with a noun and a verbal noun.
- *già dicesti* ‘once saidst’ → “didst sometime say”: the simple past is expanded with “didst,” and the temporal adverb falls between auxiliary and verb.

> 37 *Voi che intendendo il terzo ciel movete;*  

*Ye who by understanding give the third heaven motion,*

- *il terzo ciel movete* ‘the third heaven ye move’ → “give the third heaven motion”: the single transitive verb becomes “give” with the noun “motion”; the heaven, formerly the object of ‘move,’ follows “give” as recipient of the motion.

> 38 e sem sì pien d' amor che per piacerti  
> 39 non fia men dolce un poco di quiete.”  

and so full of love are we that, to pleasure thee, a space of quiet shall be no less sweet to us.”

- *sem sì pien d' amor* ‘we are so full of love’ → “so full of love are we”: the predicate is placed before both verb and subject.
- *non fia men dolce un poco di quiete* ‘will not be less sweet a little quiet’ → “a space of quiet shall be no less sweet to us”: the subject moves before the verb; ‘a little’ is expressed as a duration, the negation attaches to “less,” and “to us” makes the experiencers explicit.

> 40 Poscia che gli occhi miei si furo offerti  
> 41 alla mia donna riverenti, ed essa  
> 42 fatti gli avea di sè contenti e certi,  

When mine eyes had been raised in reverence to my Lady, and she had satisfied them with herself and given them assurance,

- *Poscia che* ‘after’ → “When”: the conjunction no longer expressly marks the action as prior.
- *gli occhi miei si furo offerti* ‘my eyes had offered themselves’ → “mine eyes had been raised”: the postposed possessive moves before the noun, and the reflexive act of offering becomes a passive act of raising.
- *alla mia donna riverenti* ‘to my Lady reverent’ → “in reverence to my Lady”: the adjective agreeing with the eyes becomes a phrase of manner and moves before the phrase of direction.
- *fatti gli avea di sè contenti e certi* ‘made them she had with herself content and sure’ → “had satisfied them with herself and given them assurance”: the auxiliary precedes the participles; one causative verb governing two adjectives becomes two coordinated verbs, the second with a noun in place of the adjective.

> 43 rivolsersi alla luce, che promessa  
> 44 tanto s' avea, e: “Di' chi siete?” fue  
> 45 la voce mia di grande affetto impressa.  

they turned them back to the light which so largely had made proffer of itself, and, “Say who ye be,” was my word, with great affection stamped.

- *rivolsersi* ‘they turned themselves back’ → “they turned them back”: the reflexive is expressed by the unmarked object form “them.”
- *che promessa tanto s' avea* ‘which had promised itself so much’ → “which so largely had made proffer of itself”: promising becomes making a proffer, with the reflexive in an “of” phrase; the expression of degree precedes the auxiliary instead of falling between participle and auxiliary.
- *Di' chi siete* ‘say who you are’ → “Say who ye be”: the singular imperative is not marked for number in English, though “ye” retains the plural form of address in the question.
- *la voce mia* ‘my voice’ → “my word”: the utterance replaces the voice, and the possessive precedes the noun.
- *di grande affetto* ‘of great affection’ → “with great affection”: the preposition changes to suit “stamped.”

> 46 E quanta e quale vid' io lei far piùe  
> 47 per allegrezza nuova che s' accrebbe,  
> 48 quand' io parlai, all' allegrezze sue!  

Ah! how I saw it wax in quantity and kind at the new joy which, when I spoke, was added to its joys!

- *E quanta e quale vid' io lei far piùe* ‘and how much greater and of what quality I saw it become’ → “Ah! how I saw it wax in quantity and kind”: an interjection replaces the opening conjunction. The exclamatory expressions of magnitude and quality are recast as “how” and a phrase following “wax”; the subject precedes the verb of seeing, and the increase is expressed by “wax” rather than by ‘become’ with ‘more.’
- *per allegrezza nuova* ‘through joy new’ → “at the new joy”: the preposition of cause changes, the definite article is supplied, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *che s' accrebbe, quand' io parlai, all' allegrezze sue* ‘which added itself, when I spoke, to its joys’ → “which, when I spoke, was added to its joys”: the reflexive becomes passive, the temporal clause moves before the verb of addition, and the possessive precedes its noun.

## Lines 49–81 — Charles Martel’s Crowns and His Warning to His Brother

> 49 Così fatta mi disse: “Il mondo m' ebbe  
> 50 giù poco tempo; e, se più fosse stato,  
> 51 molto sarà di mal, che non sarebbe.  

Thus changed, it said to me: “The world held me below but little space; had it been more much ill shall be that had not been.

- *Così fatta* ‘thus made’ → “Thus changed”: the participle is rendered in terms of a change rather than its result.
- *mi disse* ‘to me said’ → “it said to me”: the indirect object follows the verb.
- *m' ebbe* ‘me had’ → “held me”: the verb is more specific, and the object follows it.
- *poco tempo* ‘little time’ → “but little space”: “but” is supplied to emphasize the shortness, and duration is expressed as a “space”.
- *e, se più fosse stato* ‘and, if it had been more’ → “had it been more”: the conjunction is dropped, and inversion takes the place of the conditional *se*.
- *molto sarà di mal* ‘much will be of evil’ → “much ill shall be”: the partitive construction disappears, bringing “much ill” together before the verb.
- *che non sarebbe* ‘that would not be’ → “that had not been”: the simple conditional becomes a counterfactual perfect.

> 52 La mia letizia mi ti tien celato,  
> 53 che mi raggia dintorno, e mi nasconde  
> 54 quasi animal di sua seta fasciato.  

My joy holdeth me concealed from thee, raying around me, and hideth me like to a creature swathed in its own silk.

- *mi ti tien celato* ‘me from thee keeps concealed’ → “holdeth me concealed from thee”: the two preverbal pronouns are separated; “me” follows the verb and “from thee” follows the participle.
- *che mi raggia dintorno* ‘which about me rays’ → “raying around me”: the relative clause becomes a participial phrase, and the pronoun follows the preposition.
- *mi nasconde* ‘me hides’ → “hideth me”: the object follows the verb.
- *quasi animal* ‘as an animal’ → “like to a creature”: the comparison takes a prepositional form, with the broader “creature” for *animal*.
- *di sua seta fasciato* ‘in its silk swathed’ → “swathed in its own silk”: the participle comes first, and “own” makes the possessive emphatic.

> 55 Assai m' amasti, ed avesti bene onde;  
> 56 chè, s' io fossi giù stato, io ti mostrava  
> 57 di mio amor più oltre che le fronde.  

Much didst thou love me, and thou hadst good cause; for had I stayed below I had shown thee a further growth of love than the mere leaves.

- *Assai m' amasti* ‘much me thou lovedst’ → “Much didst thou love me”: the fronted adverb is retained, but the past tense is expressed with an inverted auxiliary, and the object follows “love”.
- *avesti bene onde* ‘thou hadst well wherefore’ → “thou hadst good cause”: the adverb becomes an adjective, and the ground for loving, expressed by *onde*, becomes a noun.
- *s' io fossi giù stato* ‘if I had below remained’ → “had I stayed below”: inversion replaces the conditional conjunction, and “below” follows the participle.
- *io ti mostrava* ‘I was showing thee’ → “I had shown thee”: the Italian imperfect, used as the consequence of an unfulfilled condition, becomes a counterfactual perfect; the object follows the verb.
- *di mio amor più oltre che le fronde* ‘of my love farther than the leaves’ → “a further growth of love than the mere leaves”: “further” modifies the supplied noun “growth”, “of love” follows it, the possessive drops out, and “mere” is supplied.

> 58 Quella sinistra riva che si lava  
> 59 di Rodano, poi ch' è misto con Sorga,  
> 60 per suo signore a tempo m' aspettava;  

That left bank which is bathed by Rhone after it hath mingled with Sorgue, me for its timely lord awaited;

- *si lava di Rodano* ‘washes itself in the Rhône’ → “is bathed by Rhone”: the reflexive becomes a passive, the river is construed as the agent, and its article drops out.
- *è misto con Sorga* ‘is mixed with Sorgue’ → “hath mingled with Sorgue”: a resultant state becomes a completed action, with an intransitive verb.
- *per suo signore a tempo m' aspettava* ‘for its lord in due time me awaited’ → “me for its timely lord awaited”: the object moves ahead of the phrase describing it, and the adverbial *a tempo* becomes an adjective modifying “lord”.

> 61 e quel corno d' Ausonia, che s' imborga  
> 62 di Bari, di Gaeta e di Catona,  
> 63 da ove Tronto e Verde in mare sgorga.  

so did that corner of Ausonia, down from where Tronto and Verde discharge into the sea, citied by Bari, Gaeta and Catona.

- *e quel corno d' Ausonia* ‘and that horn of Ausonia’ → “so did that corner of Ausonia”: “so did” resumes the preceding verb instead of simply coordinating its subjects; the geographical ‘horn’ becomes a “corner”.
- *che s' imborga di Bari, di Gaeta e di Catona* ‘which towns itself with Bari, with Gaeta and with Catona’ → “citied by Bari, Gaeta and Catona”: the reflexive relative clause becomes a passive participial phrase placed after the geographical limit, and one “by” serves all three names.
- *da ove* ‘from where’ → “down from where”: a direction is supplied.
- *Tronto e Verde in mare sgorga* ‘Tronto and Verde into sea gushes’ → “Tronto and Verde discharge into the sea”: the singular Italian verb becomes plural, “discharge” replaces the image of gushing, and the destination follows the verb with an article.

> 64 Fulgeami già in fronte la corona  
> 65 di quella terra che il Danubio riga  
> 66 poi che le ripe tedesche abbandona;  

Upon my brow already glowed the crown of the land the Danube watereth after it hath left its German banks;

- *Fulgeami già in fronte* ‘shone for me already on the brow’ → “Upon my brow already glowed”: the dative becomes a possessive, the phrase for the place comes first, and “glowed” follows the adverb.
- *quella terra che il Danubio riga* ‘that land which the Danube waters’ → “the land the Danube watereth”: a definite article replaces the demonstrative, and the object relative is unexpressed.
- *le ripe tedesche abbandona* ‘the banks German it leaves’ → “it hath left its German banks”: the present becomes a perfect, the object follows the verb, the adjective precedes the noun, and a possessive replaces the article.

> 67 e la bella Trinacria, che caliga  
> 68 tra Pachino e Peloro, sopra il golfo  
> 69 che riceve da Euro maggior briga,  

and fair Trinacria which darkeneth between Pachynus and Pelorus, o’er the gulf tormented most by Eurus,

- *la bella Trinacria* ‘the fair Trinacria’ → “fair Trinacria”: the article drops out before the qualified name.
- *che riceve da Euro maggior briga* ‘which receives from Eurus greater trouble’ → “tormented most by Eurus”: the finite relative clause becomes a passive participial phrase; the noun for trouble becomes a verb of suffering, and the comparative adjective becomes a superlative adverb before the agent.

> 70 non per Tifeo, ma per nascente solfo,  

(not for Typheus, but for sulphur that ariseth there)

- *nascente solfo* ‘rising sulphur’ → “sulphur that ariseth there”: the prefixed participle becomes a following relative clause, and “there” is supplied.

> 71 attesi avrebbe li suoi regi ancora,  
> 72 nati per me di Carlo e di Ridolfo,  

would yet have looked to have its kings, sprung through me from Charles and Rudolf,

- *attesi avrebbe li suoi regi ancora* ‘awaited would have its kings still’ → “would yet have looked to have its kings”: “yet” moves into the verb phrase, the auxiliary precedes the participle, and direct expectation becomes looking to have something.
- *nati* ‘born’ → “sprung”: descent is expressed by a different participle.

> 73 se mala signoria, che sempre accora  
> 74 li popoli suggetti, non avesse  
> 75 mosso Palermo a gridar: *Mora, mora.*  

had not ill lordship, which doth ever cut the heart of subject peoples, moved Palermo to shriek out: *Die! die!*

- *se mala signoria … non avesse mosso* ‘if ill lordship … had not moved’ → “had not ill lordship, which doth ever cut the heart of subject peoples, moved”: inversion replaces *se*, moving the auxiliary and negative before the subject.
- *sempre accora li popoli suggetti* ‘always grieves the peoples subject’ → “doth ever cut the heart of subject peoples”: a simple verb becomes an auxiliary and a phrase making the heart explicit; the peoples become the complement of “of”, the adjective precedes the noun, and the article drops out.
- *gridar* ‘cry’ → “shriek out”: the verb is intensified and a particle added.
- *Mora, mora* ‘let him die, let him die’ → “Die! die!”: the third-person subjunctives of exhortation become direct imperatives.

> 76 E se mio frate questo antivedesse,  
> 77 l' avara povertà di Catalogna  
> 78 già fuggiria, perchè non gli offendesse;  

And had my brother seen it in good time, he would already flee the greedy poverty of Catalonia, lest it should work him ill;

- *se mio frate questo antivedesse* ‘if my brother foresaw this’ → “had my brother seen it in good time”: inversion replaces *se*, the imperfect subjunctive becomes a past perfect, and foresight becomes seeing in time; “it” replaces ‘this’ and follows the participle.
- *l' avara povertà di Catalogna già fuggiria* ‘the greedy poverty of Catalonia already he would flee’ → “he would already flee the greedy poverty of Catalonia”: the object moves after the verb, and the adverb stands between auxiliary and infinitive.
- *perchè non gli offendesse* ‘so that it might not harm him’ → “lest it should work him ill”: “lest” absorbs the negative purpose, and the simple verb of harming becomes a phrase with “ill” as its object.

> 79 chè veramente provveder bisogna  
> 80 per lui, o per altrui, sì ch' a sua barca  
> 81 carcata più di carco non si pogna.  

and of a truth provision needs be made by him or by another, lest on his barque already laden heavier load be laid.

- *chè veramente* ‘for truly’ → “and of a truth”: coordination replaces the explanatory conjunction, and a prepositional phrase replaces the adverb.
- *provveder bisogna per lui, o per altrui* ‘to provide is necessary on his part or on another’s’ → “provision needs be made by him or by another”: the infinitive becomes a noun with a passive verb, the impersonal necessity is recast around that noun, and the alternatives are expressed as agents.
- *sì ch' … non* ‘so that … not’ → “lest”: one conjunction expresses both purpose and negation.
- *a sua barca carcata* ‘onto his barque laden’ → “on his barque already laden”: “on” expresses the place of loading, and “already” makes the prior burden explicit.
- *più di carco … si pogna* ‘more of a load … be put’ → “heavier load be laid”: the partitive comparison of quantity becomes an adjective of weight, and the passive *si* construction becomes an English passive with “laid”.

## Lines 82–114 — Bitter Fruit From Sweet Seed

> 82 La sua natura, che di larga parca  
> 83 discese, avria mestier di tal milizia  
> 84 che non curasse di mettere in arca.”  

His nature,—mean descendant from a generous forebear,—were in need of soldiery who should not give their care to storing in the chest.”

- *che di larga parca discese* ‘which from a generous [nature] descended a stingy one’ → “mean descendant from a generous forebear”: the relative clause becomes an apposition; the verb of descent becomes a noun, “mean” moves before it, and “forebear” supplies a personal noun for the understood ‘nature’.
- *avria mestier di* ‘would have need of’ → “were in need of”: the conditional of ‘have’ becomes the irrealis “were” with a prepositional phrase.
- *tal milizia* ‘such soldiery’ → “soldiery”: the qualification ‘such’, pointing forward to the relative clause, is omitted.
- *che non curasse di mettere in arca* ‘that cared not to put into a chest’ → “who should not give their care to storing in the chest”: the singular subjunctive describing the kind of soldiery wanted becomes “should” with a verb phrase treating the soldiery as persons in the plural; the infinitive of ‘put’ becomes the gerund “storing”, and the chest receives a definite article.

> 85 “Però ch' io credo che l' alta letizia  
> 86 che il tuo parlar m' infonde, signor mio,  
> 87 là 've ogni ben si termina e s' inizia,  

“Sire, in that I believe the lofty joy which thy discourse poureth into me, there where every good hath end and hath beginning

- *Però ch'* ‘because’ → “in that”: the causal conjunction becomes a prepositional turn.
- *credo che* ‘believe that’ → “believe”: the conjunction introducing what is believed is left implicit.
- *che il tuo parlar m' infonde* ‘which thy speaking into me pours’ → “which thy discourse poureth into me”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun for the discourse itself, and the destination follows the verb.
- *signor mio* ‘my lord’ → “Sire”: the possessive form of address becomes a single title and moves to the beginning of the sentence.
- *si termina e s' inizia* ‘ends and begins’ → “hath end and hath beginning”: the two pronominal verbs become forms of ‘have’ with nouns.

> 88 per te si veggia, come la vegg' io,  
> 89 grata m' è più, e anco questo ho caro,  
> 90 perchè il discerni rimirando in Dio.  

is seen by thee even as I see it, ’t is more grateful to me; and this too I hold dear, that thou discernest it looking on God.

- *per te si veggia* ‘by thee be seen’ → “is seen by thee”: the agent follows the passive verb, and the subjunctive dependent on ‘believe’ becomes an indicative.
- *come la vegg' io* ‘as it see I’ → “even as I see it”: “even” strengthens the comparison, the emphatically postposed subject moves before the verb, and the object follows it.
- *grata m' è più* ‘pleasing to me is more’ → “’t is more grateful to me”: the understood subject is resumed by a pronoun, the comparative marker comes before the adjective, and the recipient follows it.
- *e anco questo ho caro* ‘and also this I have dear’ → “and this too I hold dear”: “too” follows the demonstrative, and ‘have dear’ becomes “hold dear”.
- *perchè il discerni* ‘because it thou discernest’ → “that thou discernest it”: the clause states what is held dear rather than giving a reason; the object follows the verb.
- *rimirando in Dio* ‘gazing into God’ → “looking on God”: the English uses a less intensive verb and changes the preposition.

> 91 Fatto m' hai lieto, e così mi fa chiaro,  
> 92 poichè, parlando, a dubitar m' hai mosso,  
> 93 come uscir può di dolce seme amaro.”  

Thou hast rejoiced me, now enlighten me; for in speaking thou hast moved me to question how from sweet seed may come forth bitter.”

- *Fatto m' hai lieto* ‘made me hast thou glad’ → “Thou hast rejoiced me”: a transitive verb replaces ‘make’ with a predicative adjective; the auxiliary precedes the participle, and the object follows it.
- *e così mi fa chiaro* ‘and so make me clear’ → “now enlighten me”: the conjunction and adverb give way to a temporal transition; a single imperative replaces ‘make’ with its adjective, and the object follows.
- *poichè* ‘since’ → “for”: the subordinate reason becomes a coordinate clause.
- *parlando* ‘speaking’ → “in speaking”: a preposition is supplied.
- *a dubitar m' hai mosso* ‘to doubt me hast thou moved’ → “thou hast moved me to question”: the object and infinitive follow the finite verb phrase, and ‘doubt’ is rendered as inquiry.
- *uscir può di dolce seme amaro* ‘come out can from sweet seed bitter’ → “from sweet seed may come forth bitter”: the phrase of origin moves ahead of both verbs, and the modal precedes the infinitive.

> 94 Questo io a lui; ed egli a me: “S' io posso  

Thus I to him; and he to me: “If I can

- *Questo* ‘this’ → “Thus”: the demonstrative standing for what was said becomes an adverb.

> 95 mostrarti un vero, a quel che tu domandi  
> 96 terrai il viso come tieni il dosso.  

show a certain truth to thee, thou wilt get before thine eyes the thing thou askest just as thou hast it now behind thy back.

- *mostrarti un vero* ‘show thee a truth’ → “show a certain truth to thee”: the indirect object becomes a prepositional phrase after the direct object, and “certain” is supplied.
- *a quel che tu domandi terrai il viso* ‘toward that which thou askest thou wilt hold thy face’ → “thou wilt get before thine eyes the thing thou askest”: instead of turning one's face toward the object, one gets the object before one's eyes; the verb moves to the front, and ‘that which’ becomes a noun with an unexpressed relative.
- *come tieni il dosso* ‘as thou holdest thy back’ → “just as thou hast it now behind thy back”: “just” and “now” are supplied; instead of holding one's back toward the thing, one has it behind one's back.

> 97 Lo ben che tutto il regno che tu scandi  
> 98 volge e contenta, fa esser virtute  
> 99 sua provvidenza in questi corpi grandi;  

The Good which doth revolve and satisfy the whole realm thou art climbing, maketh its providence become a virtuous power in these great bodies;

- *tutto il regno che tu scandi volge e contenta* ‘the whole realm which thou climbest turns and contents’ → “doth revolve and satisfy the whole realm thou art climbing”: the two verbs move before their shared object and take the auxiliary “doth”; the relative pronoun after ‘realm’ is implicit, and the simple present of ‘climb’ becomes progressive.
- *fa esser virtute sua provvidenza* ‘makes be a power its providence’ → “maketh its providence become a virtuous power”: “its providence” moves before the infinitive, ‘be’ becomes “become”, and the single noun for operative virtue or power is expanded into adjective and noun.

> 100 e non pur le nature provvedute  
> 101 son nella mente ch' è da sè perfetta,  
> 102 ma esse insieme con la lor salute.  

and not only is provision made for the diverse-natured creatures, by the mind that is perfection in itself, but for their weal too, co-related with them.

- *non pur le nature provvedute son* ‘not only the natures provided for are’ → “not only is provision made for the diverse-natured creatures”: the plural subject of the passive becomes the complement of “for” in a singular construction with “provision”; ‘natures’ is interpreted as creatures of differing natures.
- *nella mente* ‘in the mind’ → “by the mind”: the mind becomes the agent rather than the seat of provision.
- *ch' è da sè perfetta* ‘that is of itself perfect’ → “that is perfection in itself”: the adjective becomes an abstract noun, and the qualification follows it.
- *ma esse insieme con la lor salute* ‘but they together with their welfare’ → “but for their weal too, co-related with them”: welfare becomes the principal complement of the understood provision; the creatures, emphatically resumed by ‘they’ in Italian, become “them”, and being taken ‘together with’ is expressed as a relation.

> 103 Per che quantunque questo arco saetta  
> 104 disposto cade a provveduto fine,  
> 105 sì come cosa in suo segno diretta.  

Wherefore whate’er this bow dischargeth doth alight disposed to a provided end, even as a thing directed to its mark.

- *quantunque questo arco saetta* ‘whatever this bow shoots’ → “whate’er this bow dischargeth”: “dischargeth” replaces the verb specifically suggestive of shooting arrows.
- *disposto cade* ‘disposed falls’ → “doth alight disposed”: the participle follows the finite verb, and “alight” describes the landing rather than the fall.
- *sì come* ‘just as’ → “even as”: “even” gives the force of the comparison.
- *in suo segno diretta* ‘at its mark directed’ → “directed to its mark”: the participle precedes the phrase of aim, and the preposition expresses direction.

> 106 Se ciò non fosse, il ciel che tu cammine  
> 107 producerebbe sì li suoi effetti,  
> 108 che non sarebbero arti, ma ruine;  

Were this not so, the heaven thou art traversing would so bring its effects to being, that they would be not works of art, but ruins;

- *Se ciò non fosse* ‘if this were not’ → “Were this not so”: inversion replaces the conditional conjunction, and “so” is supplied.
- *che tu cammine* ‘which thou treadest’ → “thou art traversing”: the relative pronoun is implicit, and a progressive verb of passage replaces the simple verb of walking.
- *producerebbe sì li suoi effetti* ‘would produce thus its effects’ → “would so bring its effects to being”: the adverb moves before the main verb, and ‘produce’ becomes a phrase enclosing the object.
- *non sarebbero arti* ‘would not be arts’ → “they would be not works of art”: the negative moves to the contrasted complement, and ‘arts’ is interpreted as their products.

> 109 e ciò esser non può, se gl' intelletti  
> 110 che movon queste stelle non son manchi,  
> 111 e manco il primo che non gli ha perfetti.  

and this may not be, if the intellects which move these stars be not defective, and defective, too, that primal one which failed to perfect them.

- *ciò esser non può* ‘this be cannot’ → “this may not be”: the modal precedes the infinitive.
- *non son manchi* ‘are not deficient’ → “be not defective”: the Italian indicative becomes a subjunctive in the conditional clause.
- *e manco il primo* ‘and deficient the first’ → “and defective, too, that primal one”: “too” makes the parallel explicit, and the substantivized ordinal becomes a demonstrative phrase with “one”.
- *che non gli ha perfetti* ‘which has not perfected them’ → “which failed to perfect them”: the negative perfect becomes an affirmative verb of failure with an infinitive, and the object follows the infinitive.

> 112 Vuoi tu che questo ver più ti s' imbianchi?”  

Wouldst thou that this truth be more illuminated?”

- *Vuoi* ‘dost thou wish’ → “Wouldst thou”: the present verb of wishing becomes a modal form of invitation.
- *ti s' imbianchi* ‘grow white for thee’ → “be more illuminated”: the pronominal verb of whitening becomes a passive of illumination, and the personal complement has no separate rendering.

> 113 Ed io: “Non già, perchè impossibil veggio  
> 114 che la natura, in quel ch' è uopo, stanchi.”  

And I: “Not so, for I see ’tis impossible that nature, in the needful, should fall short.”

- *Non già* ‘certainly not’ → “Not so”: “so” refers back to the proposal in place of the Italian emphatic particle.
- *impossibil veggio* ‘impossible I see’ → “I see ’tis impossible”: the verb comes first, and the predicative adjective becomes a finite clause.
- *in quel ch' è uopo* ‘in that which is needful’ → “in the needful”: a substantivized adjective replaces the demonstrative and relative clause.
- *stanchi* ‘fail’ → “should fall short”: the subjunctive after the expression of impossibility is marked by “should”, and a verbal phrase replaces the single verb.

## Lines 115–148 — Natural Aptitudes and Human Offices

> 115 Ond' egli ancora: “Or di', sarebbe il peggio  
> 116 per l' uomo in terra se non fosse cive?”  

Whence he again: “Now, say, would it be worse for man on earth were he no citizen?”

- *sarebbe il peggio* ‘would it be the worse’ → “would it be worse”: the article is dropped.
- *se non fosse cive* ‘if he were not a citizen’ → “were he no citizen”: inversion replaces the conditional conjunction, and the negative attaches to the noun rather than to the verb.

> 117 “Sì, rispos' io, e qui ragion non cheggio.”  

“Yea,” I replied, “and here I ask no reason.”

- *rispos' io* ‘replied I’ → “I replied”: the subject precedes the verb.
- *ragion non cheggio* ‘reason I do not ask’ → “I ask no reason”: the object moves after the verb, and the negative becomes a determiner of “reason”.

> 118 “E può egli esser, se giù non si vive  
> 119 diversamente per diversi offici?  

“And may that be, except men live below diversely and with diverse offices?

- *può egli esser* ‘can it be’ → “may that be”: “may” expresses possibility, and “that” points back to what has just been proposed.
- *se giù non si vive* ‘if down here one does not live’ → “except men live below”: “except” takes up both the condition and its negative; the impersonal singular becomes a personal plural, and the adverb moves after the verb.
- *per diversi offici* ‘through diverse offices’ → “and with diverse offices”: “and” is supplied to coordinate the phrase with “diversely”, and “with” replaces the preposition of means.

> 120 No, se il maestro vostro ben vi scrive.”  

No, if your master write the truth for you.”

- *il maestro vostro* ‘the master of yours’ → “your master”: the postposed possessive moves before the noun.
- *ben vi scrive* ‘rightly writes to you’ → “write the truth for you”: the adverb becomes a direct object, the dative becomes a following phrase with “for”, and the Italian indicative is rendered by an English subjunctive.

> 121 Sì venne deducendo insino a quici;  

Up to this point he came deduction-wise;

- *Sì venne deducendo* ‘thus he came deducing’ → “he came deduction-wise”: the opening adverb and the gerund are represented together by an expression of manner.
- *insino a quici* ‘as far as here’ → “Up to this point”: the limit of the deduction is put first and expressed by a noun phrase.

> 122 poscia conchiuse: “Dunque esser diverse  
> 123 convien dei vostri effetti le radici:  

then the conclusion: “Therefore must needs the roots of your effects be diverse;

- *poscia conchiuse* ‘then he concluded’ → “then the conclusion”: a noun with an article replaces the finite verb.
- *esser diverse convien dei vostri effetti le radici* ‘to be diverse it is necessary for the roots of your effects’ → “must needs the roots of your effects be diverse”: the impersonal verb becomes a modal expression; the infinitive and adjective move after their subject, and the roots are named before, rather than after, their dependent possessive phrase.

> 124 per che un nasce Solone, ed altro Xerse,  
> 125 altro Melchisedech, ed altro quello  
> 126 che volando per l' aere il figlio perse.  

wherefore is one born Solon and one Xerxes, one Melchizedek, and one the man who, soaring through the welkin, lost his son.

- *un nasce Solone* ‘one is born Solon’ → “is one born Solon”: English uses a passive form for the Italian verb of birth and inverts auxiliary and subject.
- *ed altro Xerse, altro Melchisedech, ed altro quello* ‘and another Xerxes, another Melchizedek, and another that one’ → “and one Xerxes, one Melchizedek, and one the man”: repeated “one” replaces ‘another’, and the demonstrative becomes a definite noun phrase.
- *volando per l' aere* ‘flying through the air’ → “soaring through the welkin”: both the verb and the noun receive more elevated English equivalents.
- *il figlio perse* ‘the son he lost’ → “lost his son”: the object follows the verb, and a possessive replaces the article.

> 127 La circular natura, ch' è suggello  
> 128 alla cera mortal, fa ben sua arte,  
> 129 ma non distingue l' un dall' altro ostello.  

That which in circling hath its nature, and is the seal upon the mortal wax, plieth aright its art, but maketh not distinction between one or other tenement.

- *La circular natura* ‘the circular nature’ → “That which in circling hath its nature”: the adjective becomes a phrase describing an activity, and the noun becomes the object of “hath” in a relative construction.
- *ch' è suggello alla cera mortal* ‘which is a seal to the wax mortal’ → “and is the seal upon the mortal wax”: coordination replaces a separate relative clause; the predicate noun takes a definite article, “upon” replaces the dative relation, and the adjective precedes “wax”.
- *fa ben sua arte* ‘does its art well’ → “plieth aright its art”: “plieth” specializes the general verb, and “aright” gives ‘well’ the sense of ‘correctly’.
- *non distingue l' un dall' altro ostello* ‘does not distinguish the one dwelling from the other’ → “maketh not distinction between one or other tenement”: the verb becomes a verb–noun expression; the opposition of one dwelling to the other becomes a phrase with “between” and “or”, and “tenement” renders ‘dwelling’.

> 130 Quinci addivien ch' Esaù si diparte  
> 131 per seme da Iacob, e vien Quirino  
> 132 da sì vil padre che si rende a Marte.  

Wherefore it cometh that Esau severeth himself in seed from Jacob, and Quirinus cometh of so base father that he is assigned to Mars.

- *Quinci addivien* ‘from this it comes about’ → “Wherefore it cometh”: an expression of consequence replaces the adverb of origin, and “cometh” alone renders ‘comes about’.
- *si diparte per seme da Iacob* ‘differs by seed from Jacob’ → “severeth himself in seed from Jacob”: the pronominal verb of divergence is made explicitly reflexive, and “in” replaces ‘by’.
- *e vien Quirino* ‘and comes Quirinus’ → “and Quirinus cometh”: the subject moves before the verb.
- *si rende a Marte* ‘one attributes him to Mars’ → “he is assigned to Mars”: the construction with *si* becomes an explicit personal passive.

> 133 Natura generata il suo cammino  
> 134 simil farebbe sempre ai generanti,  
> 135 se non vincesse il provveder divino.  

The begotten nature would ever take a course like its begetters, did not divine provision overrule.

- *Natura generata* ‘nature begotten’ → “The begotten nature”: an article is supplied, and the participle precedes the noun.
- *il suo cammino simil farebbe sempre ai generanti* ‘its course it would always make like the begetters’ → “would ever take a course like its begetters”: the verb precedes its object and the comparison follows it; ‘make’ becomes “take”, the adverb moves before “take”, and the possessive shifts from the course to the begetters.
- *se non vincesse il provveder divino* ‘if divine providing did not prevail’ → “did not divine provision overrule”: inversion replaces ‘if’; the substantivized infinitive becomes “provision”, its adjective moves before it, and “overrule” replaces ‘prevail’.

> 136 Or quel che t' era retro t' è davanti;  

Now that which was behind thee is before;

- *quel che t' era retro* ‘that which to thee was behind’ → “that which was behind thee”: the dative pronoun becomes the complement of “behind”.
- *t' è davanti* ‘is before thee’ → “is before”: the second reference to ‘thee’ is omitted.

> 137 ma perchè sappi che di te mi giova,  
> 138 un corollario voglio che t' ammanti.  

but that thou mayst know that I delight in thee, I will have a corollary wrap thee round.

- *perchè sappi* ‘in order that thou know’ → “that thou mayst know”: the purpose clause uses a modal auxiliary for the Italian subjunctive.
- *di te mi giova* ‘in thee it gives me pleasure’ → “I delight in thee”: the person experiencing pleasure becomes the subject, and the phrase referring to ‘thee’ moves to the end.
- *un corollario voglio che t' ammanti* ‘a corollary I wish that it may cloak thee’ → “I will have a corollary wrap thee round”: the fronted ‘corollary’ follows “will have”; a bare infinitive replaces the subjunctive clause, its object follows it, and the image of clothing becomes one of wrapping.

> 139 Sempre natura, se fortuna trova  
> 140 discorde a sè, come ogni altra semente

- *fa mala prova* ‘makes a poor showing’ → “doth nature, if she find fortune unharmonious with herself, like any other seed out of its proper region, make an ill essay”: the opening “Ever” occasions inversion with “doth”, separating the auxiliary from “make”; ‘poor showing’ is expressed as “an ill essay”.
- *se fortuna trova* ‘if fortune she finds’ → “if she find fortune”: the object moves after the verb, and an English subjunctive replaces the indicative.
- *discorde a sè* ‘at variance with herself’ → “unharmonious with herself”: an adjective of disharmony renders ‘at variance’, with “with” for the Italian preposition.
- *sua region* ‘its region’ → “its proper region”: “proper” is supplied to emphasize that the region is the seed’s own.

> 142 E se il mondo laggiù ponesse mente  
> 143 al fondamento che natura pone,  
> 144 seguendo lui, avria buona la gente.  

And if the world down there took heed to the foundation nature layeth, and followed it, it would have satisfaction in its folk.

- *ponesse mente al fondamento* ‘should set its mind on the foundation’ → “took heed to the foundation”: the idiom of directing the mind becomes one of heeding; an English conditional past renders the imperfect subjunctive.
- *che natura pone* ‘that nature lays’ → “nature layeth”: the object relative pronoun is left unexpressed.
- *seguendo lui* ‘following it’ → “and followed it”: a coordinated finite verb within the condition replaces the gerund.
- *avria buona la gente* ‘it would have its people good’ → “it would have satisfaction in its folk”: instead of retaining ‘good’ as a predicate of the people, the translation makes “satisfaction” the object of “have” and puts the folk in a prepositional phrase.

> 145 Ma voi torcete alla religione  
> 146 tal che fia nato a cingersi la spada,  

But ye wrench to a religious order him born to gird the sword,

- *torcete* ‘twist’ → “wrench”: the English verb emphasizes forcible redirection.
- *alla religione* ‘to religion’ → “to a religious order”: the institutional sense is made explicit, with an indefinite article.
- *tal che fia nato* ‘one who will be born’ → “him born”: the indefinite antecedent becomes a pronoun, and the relative clause is reduced to a participle without the future auxiliary.
- *a cingersi la spada* ‘to gird the sword on himself’ → “to gird the sword”: the reflexive indicating the wearer is omitted.

> 147 e fate re di tal ch' è da sermone;  

and make a king of him who should be for discourse;

- *fate re di tal* ‘make king of one’ → “make a king of him”: an indefinite article is supplied before the title, and “him” replaces the indefinite antecedent.
- *è da sermone* ‘is suited to discourse’ → “should be for discourse”: the suitability expressed by the Italian prepositional construction is conveyed by a modal verb and “for”.

> 148 onde la traccia vostra è fuor di strada.”  

wherefore your track runneth abroad the road.”

- *la traccia vostra* ‘the track of yours’ → “your track”: the postposed possessive moves before the noun.
- *è fuor di strada* ‘is off the road’ → “runneth abroad the road”: a verb of motion replaces the copula, and “abroad” renders the sense of deviation.

## Summary — Reordering the Sentence, Keeping the Image

Wicksteed often changes the order of words without changing the emphasis of the phrase. In *Assai m’ amasti*, for instance, he keeps the initial word of degree but moves the object after the verb and uses an inverted auxiliary: “Much didst thou love me”. Elsewhere he moves a delayed subject to the front, as when *la donna mia* becomes “my lady”. Two routine differences generally go unremarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending identifies the subject, and omits the Italian definite article before a possessive, as in *La mia letizia*, “My joy”.

Changes of construction often let Wicksteed redistribute the work of a single verb. The two pronominal verbs in *si termina e s’ inizia* become “hath end and hath beginning”; conversely, the participle and adjective in *Fatto m’ hai lieto* are gathered into one verb in “Thou hast rejoiced me”. An Italian participle can expand into a relative clause, as *nascente solfo* becomes “sulphur that ariseth there”; a clause can also contract into a participial phrase, as *che mi raggia dintorno* becomes “raying around me”.

In his choice of words, Wicksteed sometimes specifies what the Italian leaves implicit: *raggiasse* becomes “rayed down”, with the direction supplied, and *carcata* becomes “already laden”, with the timing explicit. At other points he leaves a detail unexpressed: “that I was there” renders *d’ esservi entro* without a separate word for *entro*, ‘within’. Yet he stays close where repetition or an image gives a passage its shape. “In one circle and in one circling and in one thirst” preserves both the threefold pattern and the distinction between *giro* and *girare*; “the seal upon the mortal wax” retains the concrete terms *suggello* and *cera* even as their grammatical setting changes.
