# Paradiso 11

## Lines 1–24 — Earthly Cares and the Eternal Light

> 1 O insensata cura dei mortali,  
> 2 quanto son difettivi sillogismi  
> 3 quei che ti fanno in basso batter l' ali!  

Insensate care of mortals! Oh how false the arguments which make thee downward beat thy wings!

- *O* ‘O’ → “Oh”: the exclamation moves from the address to the care of mortals to the beginning of the next clause.
- *quanto son difettivi sillogismi quei che* ‘how defective syllogisms are those which’ → “how false the arguments which”: the verb ‘are’ drops out, ‘defective’ becomes “false”, and the more general “arguments” replaces ‘syllogisms’. Instead of saying what ‘those which’ are, English makes “the arguments” itself the antecedent of “which”.
- *ti fanno* ‘thee make’ → “make thee”: the object follows the verb.
- *l' ali* ‘the wings’ → “thy wings”: a possessive replaces the definite article.

> 4 Chi retro a iura, e chi ad aforismi  
> 5 sen giva, e chi seguendo sacerdozio,  
> 6 e chi regnar per forza o per sofismi,  

One was following after law, and one aphorisms, one was pursuing priesthood, and one dominion by violence or by quibbles,

- *Chi retro a iura, e chi ad aforismi sen giva* ‘one after laws, and one after aphorisms, went his way’ → “One was following after law, and one aphorisms”: the shared verb moves up to the first subject; going one’s way after something becomes the progressive “was following after”. The Latin plural ‘laws’ becomes the collective singular “law”, and the preposition is not repeated before “aphorisms”.
- *e chi seguendo sacerdozio* ‘and one following priesthood’ → “one was pursuing priesthood”: the conjunction drops out, and the participial phrase becomes an independent progressive clause, with “pursuing” for ‘following’.
- *regnar per forza o per sofismi* ‘to rule by force or by sophisms’ → “dominion by violence or by quibbles”: the infinitive becomes a noun understood as the object of the preceding verb; ‘force’ becomes “violence”, and ‘sophisms’ becomes “quibbles”.

> 7 e chi rubare, e chi civil negozio,  
> 8 chi nel diletto della carne involto  
> 9 s' affaticava, e chi si dava all' ozio;  

and another plunder, and another civil business, and one, tangled in the pleasures of the flesh, was moiling, and one abandoned him to ease;

- *chi rubare* ‘one to steal’ → “another plunder”: “another” varies the repeated ‘one’, and the infinitive becomes a noun parallel to the preceding noun.
- *chi civil negozio* ‘one civil business’ → “another civil business”: the rendering again varies ‘one’ to “another”.
- *chi nel diletto della carne involto* ‘one in the delight of the flesh entangled’ → “and one, tangled in the pleasures of the flesh”: a conjunction is supplied, the participle moves before its complement, and singular ‘delight’ becomes plural “pleasures”.
- *s' affaticava* ‘wearied himself’ → “was moiling”: a reflexive imperfect becomes an intransitive progressive.
- *si dava all' ozio* ‘gave himself to idleness’ → “abandoned him to ease”: ‘gave’ is strengthened to “abandoned”, “him” is used reflexively, and ‘idleness’ becomes “ease”.

> 10 quando, da tutte queste cose sciolto,  
> 11 con Beatrice m' era suso in cielo  
> 12 cotanto gloriosamente accolto.  

the whilst, from all these things released, with Beatrice up in heaven thus gloriously was I received.

- *quando* ‘when’ → “the whilst”: the translation uses an expression of simultaneity rather than ‘when’.
- *m' era suso in cielo … accolto* ‘was up in heaven … received’ → “up in heaven” … “was I received”: the auxiliary moves down to join the participle, with the subject inverted between them; the Italian pronominal *m'* has no separate equivalent.
- *cotanto* ‘so greatly’ → “thus”: an expression of degree becomes one of manner.

> 13 Poi che ciascuno fu tornato ne lo  
> 14 punto del cerchio, in che avanti s' era,  
> 15 fermossi come a candelier candelo.  

When each had come again to that point of the circle whereat he was before, he stayed him, as the taper in its stand.

- *Poi che* ‘after that’ → “When”: the conjunction of explicit succession becomes a more general temporal conjunction.
- *fu tornato* ‘was returned’ → “had come again”: English uses a perfect with ‘have’ rather than ‘be’ and distributes the sense of ‘return’ between “come” and “again”.
- *ne lo punto del cerchio* ‘in the point of the circle’ → “to that point of the circle”: the preposition marks the destination, and a demonstrative replaces the definite article.
- *in che avanti s' era* ‘in which before he was’ → “whereat he was before”: a relative adverb replaces the prepositional relative, ‘before’ moves after the verb, and the pronominal *s'* has no separate equivalent.
- *fermossi* ‘stopped himself’ → “he stayed him”: the reflexive verb is rendered by ‘stay’ with “him” used reflexively.
- *a candelier candelo* ‘in candlestick candle’ → “the taper in its stand”: the candle comes before its holder; “the” and “its” are supplied, with “taper” for ‘candle’ and the less specific “stand” for ‘candlestick’.

> 16 Ed io senti' dentro a quella lumiera,  
> 17 che pria m' avea parlato, sorridendo  
> 18 incominciar, facendosi più mera:  

And within that light which first had spoken to me I heard smiling begin, as it grew brighter:

- *io senti' dentro a quella lumiera, che pria m' avea parlato* ‘I heard within that light which first to me had spoken’ → “within that light which first had spoken to me I heard”: the subject and verb move after the phrase locating the sound and the relative clause qualifying the light; within the relative clause, the indirect object moves after the verb.
- *facendosi più mera* ‘making itself purer’ → “as it grew brighter”: the reflexive gerund becomes a finite temporal clause; becoming purer or clearer is rendered as increasing in brightness.

> 19 “Così com' io del suo raggio risplendo,  
> 20 sì, riguardando nella luce eterna,  
> 21 li tuoi pensieri, onde cagioni, apprendo.  

“Even as I glow with its ray, so, gazing into the Eternal Light, I apprehend whence thou dost take occasion for thy thoughts.

- *Così com'* ‘just as’ → “Even as”: “Even” gives the emphasis of the opening comparison.
- *del suo raggio risplendo* ‘with its ray I shine’ → “I glow with its ray”: the verb moves before its complement, and ‘shine’ becomes “glow”.
- *nella luce eterna* ‘in the light eternal’ → “into the Eternal Light”: the preposition gives the gaze a direction, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *li tuoi pensieri, onde cagioni, apprendo* ‘thy thoughts, whence thou derivest them, I apprehend’ → “I apprehend whence thou dost take occasion for thy thoughts”: the main verb comes first and takes the whole ‘whence’ clause as its object. The thoughts are no longer named first as the object of apprehension; they become the complement of “for” in the expanded expression “take occasion for”.

> 22 Tu dubbi, ed hai voler che si discerna  
> 23 in sì aperta e in sì distesa lingua  
> 24 lo dicer mio, ch' al tuo sentir si sterna,  

Thou questionest and wouldst fain discern, in such open and dispread discourse as may be level to thine understanding, my utterance

- *Tu dubbi* ‘thou doubtest’ → “Thou questionest”: doubt is expressed as questioning.
- *hai voler che si discerna* ‘hast a wish that there be discerned’ → “wouldst fain discern”: possession of a wish becomes a modal expression of desire; the passive subjunctive becomes an active infinitive whose understood subject is the person addressed.
- *in sì aperta e in sì distesa lingua* ‘in so open and in so extended a tongue’ → “in such open and dispread discourse”: one “in such” serves both adjectives; ‘extended’ becomes “dispread”, and ‘tongue’ becomes “discourse”.
- *lo dicer mio* ‘the saying of mine’ → “my utterance”: the substantival infinitive becomes a noun with a preceding possessive. Its phrase moves to the end and serves as the object of the active “discern”.
- *ch' al tuo sentir si sterna* ‘that it may spread itself out to thy understanding’ → “as may be level to thine understanding”: “as” answers the earlier “such”; the reflexive image of something spreading itself out becomes a state of being “level”, and the complement moves after the predicate.

## Lines 25–57 — Two Guides and a Rising Sun

> 25 ove dinanzi dissi: *u' ben s' impingua*,  

wherein I said but now: *Where is good fattening*,

- *ove* ‘where’ → “wherein”: the English makes the relation to the earlier wording explicit.
- *dinanzi dissi* ‘before I said’ → “I said but now”: the time expression follows the verb and specifies the recent past.
- *u' ben s' impingua* ‘where one fattens well’ → “Where is good fattening”: the impersonal verb becomes a noun with “is”, and the adverb becomes an adjective.

> 26 e là u' dissi: *non surse il secondo*;  

and wherein I said: *No second ever rose*;

- *là u'* ‘there where’ → “wherein”: the two indications of place are compressed into one relative adverb.
- *non surse il secondo* ‘there did not arise the second’ → “No second ever rose”: the negative is transferred from the verb to the subject, which now precedes the verb; “ever” is supplied.

> 27 e qui è uopo che ben si distingua.  

and here we need to make precise distinction.

- *è uopo che ben si distingua* ‘it is needful that one distinguish well’ → “we need to make precise distinction”: impersonal necessity with a subjunctive clause becomes a personal verb with an infinitive; the act of distinguishing is expressed by a noun with “make”, and the adverb by the adjective “precise”.

> 28 La provvidenza, che governa il mondo  
> 29 con quel consiglio nel quale ogni aspetto  
> 30 creato è vinto pria che vada al fondo,  

The providence which governeth the world,—with counsel wherein every creature’s gaze must stay, defeated, e’er it reach the bottom,—

- *quel consiglio* ‘that counsel’ → “counsel”: the demonstrative is omitted.
- *nel quale* ‘in which’ → “wherein”: the relative and its preposition become one adverb.
- *ogni aspetto creato* ‘every created gaze’ → “every creature’s gaze”: the participle qualifying the gaze becomes a possessive naming the one who gazes.
- *è vinto* ‘is defeated’ → “must stay, defeated”: the simple passive becomes a necessity to halt, with the participle describing the resulting state.
- *che vada al fondo* ‘it go to the bottom’ → “it reach the bottom”: the conjunction introducing the Italian subjunctive has no separate equivalent after “e’er”; motion to a point is expressed by a transitive verb of attainment.

> 31 però che andasse ver lo suo diletto  
> 32 la sposa di colui, ch' ad alte grida  
> 33 disposò lei col sangue benedetto,  
> 34 in sè sicura ed anco a lui più fida,  

in order that the spouse of him, who with loud cries espoused her with the blessed blood, might go toward her delight, secure within herself and faithfuller to him,

- *andasse ver lo suo diletto la sposa di colui, ch' ad alte grida disposò lei col sangue benedetto* ‘might go toward her delight the spouse of him, who with loud cries espoused her with the blood blessed’ → “the spouse of him, who with loud cries espoused her with the blessed blood, might go toward her delight”: the predicate is postponed until after its subject and the relative clause attached to that subject; “blessed” precedes the noun.
- *in sè sicura* ‘in herself secure’ → “secure within herself”: the adjective comes first, and “within” gives the preposition a more explicitly inward sense.
- *anco a lui più fida* ‘also to him more faithful’ → “faithfuller to him”: ‘also’ is omitted, the comparative is expressed by an ending, and its complement follows it.

> 35 due principi ordinò in suo favore,  
> 36 che quinci e quindi le fosser per guida.  

two Princes did ordain on her behalf, who on this side and that should be for guides.

- *ordinò* ‘ordained’ → “did ordain”: the simple past is expressed with an auxiliary.
- *in suo favore* ‘in her favour’ → “on her behalf”: the prepositional idiom is changed.
- *quinci e quindi* ‘from here and from there’ → “on this side and that”: an opposition of positions replaces the paired adverbs of direction.
- *le fosser per guida* ‘might be to her for a guide’ → “should be for guides”: the subjunctive is rendered with “should”, the dative meaning ‘to her’ is omitted, and the singular complement becomes plural.

> 37 L' un fu tutto serafico in ardore,  

The one was all seraphic in his ardour,

- *in ardore* ‘in ardour’ → “in his ardour”: the possessive is supplied.

> 38 l' altro per sapienza in terra fue  
> 39 di cherubica luce uno splendore.  

the other by his wisdom was on earth a splendour of cherubic light.

- *per sapienza* ‘by wisdom’ → “by his wisdom”: the possessive is supplied.
- *in terra fue* ‘on earth was’ → “was on earth”: the verb precedes the place expression.
- *di cherubica luce uno splendore* ‘of cherubic light a splendour’ → “a splendour of cherubic light”: the noun precedes its complement.

> 40 Dell' un dirò, però che d' ambedue  
> 41 si dice l' un pregiando, qual ch' uom prende,  
> 42 perchè ad un fine fur l' opere sue.  

Of one will I discourse, because of both the two he speaketh who doth either praise, which so he will; for to one end their works.

- *Dell' un* ‘of the one’ → “Of one”: the definite article is omitted.
- *dirò* ‘I shall speak’ → “will I discourse”: the future is expressed by an auxiliary, placed before the subject after the fronted complement; “discourse” is more expansive than ‘speak’.
- *d' ambedue* ‘of both’ → “of both the two”: the English spells out the number already contained in ‘both’.
- *si dice l' un pregiando* ‘one speaks in praising the one’ → “he speaketh who doth either praise”: the impersonal construction becomes personal, the gerund becomes a finite relative clause with “doth”, and ‘the one’ becomes “either”.
- *qual ch' uom prende* ‘whichever one takes’ → “which so he will”: an expression of choice by taking becomes one of willingness, and the indefinite subject is assimilated to the preceding “he”.
- *fur l' opere sue* ‘were their works’ → “their works”: the verb is left understood, and the possessive precedes the noun.

> 43 Intra Tupino e l' acqua che discende  
> 44 del colle eletto del beato Ubaldo,  
> 45 fertile costa d' alto monte pende,  

Between Tupino and the stream that drops from the hill chosen by the blessed Ubaldo, a fertile slope hangs from a lofty mount,

- *l' acqua* ‘the water’ → “the stream”: the body of running water is named rather than the water itself.
- *discende* ‘descends’ → “drops”: the verb of descent is rendered more sharply.
- *del beato Ubaldo* ‘of the blessed Ubaldo’ → “by the blessed Ubaldo”: the phrase is construed as the agent of the preceding participle.
- *fertile costa* ‘fertile slope’ → “a fertile slope”: the indefinite article is supplied.
- *d' alto monte pende* ‘from lofty mount hangs’ → “hangs from a lofty mount”: the verb precedes its complement, and an indefinite article is supplied.

> 46 onde Perugia sente freddo e caldo  
> 47 da porta Sole, e di retro le piange  
> 48 per grave giogo Nocera con Gualdo.  

wherefrom Perugia feeleth cold and heat through Porta Sole, and behind it waileth Nocera, for the heavy yoke, and Gualdo.

- *da porta Sole* ‘from Porta Sole’ → “through Porta Sole”: the gate is expressed as a passage rather than a point of origin.
- *di retro le piange* ‘behind, wails to it’ → “behind it waileth”: the dative pronoun is construed with the indication of place and becomes the object of “behind”.
- *per grave giogo Nocera* ‘for heavy yoke Nocera’ → “Nocera, for the heavy yoke”: the subject is moved before the causal phrase, and the definite article is supplied.
- *con Gualdo* ‘with Gualdo’ → “and Gualdo”: accompaniment becomes coordination.

> 49 Di questa costa, là dov' ella frange  
> 50 più sua rattezza, nacque al mondo un sole,  
> 51 come fa questo talvolta di Gange.  

From this slope, where most it breaks the steepness of decline, was born into the world a sun, even as is this some whiles from Ganges.

- *là dov'* ‘there where’ → “where”: the demonstrative adverb is omitted.
- *ella frange più sua rattezza* ‘it breaks most its steepness’ → “most it breaks the steepness of decline”: the adverb is fronted; the possessive gives way to a definite article, and the added complement specifies the downward inclination.
- *al mondo* ‘to the world’ → “into the world”: the English idiom expresses entry.
- *come fa questo* ‘as does this one’ → “even as is this”: “even” reinforces the comparison, and the substitute verb is made passive to echo “was born”.

> 52 Però chi d' esso loco fa parole  
> 53 non dica *Ascesi*, che direbbe corto,  
> 54 ma *Oriente*, se proprio dir vuole.  

Wherefore who speaketh of that place, let him not say *Assisi*, ’twere to speak short, but *Orient*, would he name it right.

- *d' esso loco fa parole* ‘of that place makes words’ → “speaketh of that place”: the verb and noun become a single verb, followed by its complement.
- *non dica* ‘may he not say’ → “let him not say”: the independent subjunctive of injunction is rendered with “let”.
- *che direbbe corto* ‘for he would speak short’ → “’twere to speak short”: the causal conjunction is left implicit, and the personal conditional becomes an impersonal construction with an infinitive.
- *se proprio dir vuole* ‘if rightly to speak he wishes’ → “would he name it right”: conditional inversion replaces ‘if’; the verb of wishing comes first, ‘speak’ is specialized to naming with an explicit object, and the adverb comes last.

> 55 Non era ancor molto lontan dall' orto,  
> 56 ch' ei cominciò a far sentir la terra  
> 57 della sua gran virtute alcun conforto;  

Not yet was he far distant from his rising when he began to make the earth to feel from his great power a certain strengthening;

- *Non era ancor* ‘he was not yet’ → “Not yet was he”: the negative and adverb are brought together at the front, producing inversion.
- *molto lontan* ‘very far’ → “far distant”: the degree and distance are expressed by an adverb modifying “distant”.
- *dall' orto* ‘from the rising’ → “from his rising”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *ch'* ‘that’ → “when”: the connective is given an explicitly temporal sense.
- *a far sentir la terra* ‘to make feel the earth’ → “to make the earth to feel”: the one made to feel is placed between the two verbs, and the dependent infinitive is marked with “to”.
- *della sua gran virtute* ‘of his great virtue’ → “from his great power”: the preposition marks the source, and the noun is taken in the sense of efficacy.
- *alcun conforto* ‘some comfort’ → “a certain strengthening”: the indefinite adjective is expanded, and the noun is taken in the active sense of imparting strength.

## Lines 58–90 — Francis Weds Poverty and Gains Followers

> 58 chè per tal donna giovinetto in guerra  
> 59 del padre corse, a cui, com' alla morte,  
> 60 la porta del piacer nessun disserra;  

for in his youth for such a lady did he rush into war against his father, to whom, as unto death, not one unbars the gate of his good pleasure;

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *per tal donna giovinetto* ‘for such a lady, a youth’ → “in his youth for such a lady”: the description of the subject becomes a phrase of time and moves before the phrase beginning with “for”.
- *in guerra del padre corse* ‘into war with the father he ran’ → “did he rush into war against his father”: the verb moves forward, with auxiliary “did” and inversion; “rush” gives the force of running, “against” makes the opposition explicit, and “his” identifies the father.
- *la porta del piacer nessun disserra* ‘the door of pleasure no one unbars’ → “not one unbars the gate of his good pleasure”: subject and verb move before the object; “not one” renders the negative pronoun, “gate” replaces ‘door’, and “his good pleasure” supplies a possessor and expands ‘pleasure’ into an expression for willingness.

> 61 ed innanzi alla sua spirital corte,  
> 62 *et coram patre* le si fece unito;  

and in the spiritual court that had rule over him, and in his father’s presence he was united to her,

- *innanzi alla sua spirital corte* ‘before his spiritual court’ → “in the spiritual court that had rule over him”: “in” replaces ‘before’, and the possessive becomes a relative clause specifying jurisdiction.
- *et coram patre* ‘and before the father’ → “and in his father’s presence”: the Latin preposition becomes a phrase with “presence”, and “his” specifies the relationship.
- *le si fece unito* ‘to her he made himself united’ → “he was united to her”: the reflexive construction becomes passive, and the complement follows the participle.

> 63 poscia di dì in dì l' amò più forte.  

and then from day to day loved her more strongly.

- *poscia* ‘afterwards’ → “and then”: the time adverb is rendered with an added conjunction.
- *l' amò più forte* ‘her he loved more strongly’ → “loved her more strongly”: the object pronoun moves after the verb.

> 64 Questa, privata del primo marito,  
> 65 mille e cent' anni e più dispetta e scura  
> 66 fino a costui si stette senza invito.  

She, reft of her first husband, a thousand and a hundred years and more, despised, obscure, even till him stood without invitation.

- *Questa* ‘this one’ → “She”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *privata del primo marito* ‘deprived of the first husband’ → “reft of her first husband”: “reft” renders the participle, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *dispetta e scura* ‘despised and obscure’ → “despised, obscure”: the conjunction is dropped.
- *fino a costui* ‘until this man’ → “even till him”: “even” adds emphasis, and a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *si stette* ‘remained’ → “stood”: a simple verb of position renders the pronominal verb of continuance.

> 67 Nè valse udir che la trovò sicura  
> 68 con Amiclate, al suon della sua voce,  
> 69 colui ch' a tutto il mondo fe' paura;  

And nought availed her the report that she was found unterrified together with Amyclas, when sounded that man’s voice, who struck all the world with terror;

- *Nè valse udir* ‘nor did it avail to hear’ → “And nought availed her the report”: the negative conjunction is recast as “And nought”, an indirect object is supplied, and the act of hearing becomes the thing heard.
- *che la trovò sicura* ‘that he found her secure’ → “that she was found unterrified”: active becomes passive, the object becomes the subject, and a positive adjective becomes a negative one specifying absence of fear.
- *con Amiclate* ‘with Amyclas’ → “together with Amyclas”: “together” amplifies the preposition.
- *al suon della sua voce* ‘at the sound of his voice’ → “when sounded that man’s voice”: the prepositional phrase becomes a time clause, the noun ‘sound’ becomes its verb, and the possessive identifies the speaker more explicitly.
- *colui ch' a tutto il mondo fe' paura* ‘he who to all the world gave fear’ → “who struck all the world with terror”: the postponed subject of ‘found’ no longer stands separately; the relative attaches to the man already mentioned. The recipient becomes the direct object of “struck”, and ‘fear’ becomes “with terror”.

> 70 nè valse esser costante nè feroce,  
> 71 sì che, dove Maria rimase giuso,  
> 72 ella con Cristo salse in sulla croce.  

and nought availed her to have been so constant and undaunted, that she, when Mary stayed below, mounted the cross with Christ.

- *nè valse esser* ‘nor did it avail to be’ → “and nought availed her to have been”: the negation is recast, “her” is supplied, and the simple infinitive becomes perfect.
- *costante nè feroce, sì che* ‘constant nor fierce, so that’ → “so constant and undaunted, that”: the repeated negative connective becomes “and”, ‘fierce’ is interpreted as ‘undaunted’, and “so” moves before the adjectives.
- *dove Maria rimase giuso* ‘where Mary remained below’ → “when Mary stayed below”: the relation of place or contrast is rendered as one of time.
- *ella con Cristo salse in sulla croce* ‘she with Christ went up onto the cross’ → “she,” … “mounted the cross with Christ”: the subject moves before the intervening clause, the phrase of accompaniment moves to the end, and the cross becomes the direct object of “mounted”.

> 73 Ma perch' io non proceda troppo chiuso,  
> 74 Francesco e Povertà per questi amanti  
> 75 prendi oramai nel mio parlar diffuso.  

But, lest I should proceed too covertly, Francis and Poverty as these two lovers now accept in speech outspread.

- *perch' io non proceda* ‘in order that I may not proceed’ → “lest I should proceed”: the negative purpose is expressed by “lest” with “should”, without a separate negative.
- *troppo chiuso* ‘too closed’ → “too covertly”: an adjective describing the speaker becomes an adverb of manner.
- *per questi amanti* ‘for these lovers’ → “as these two lovers”: “as” renders the predicative sense of the preposition, and “two” is supplied.
- *prendi oramai* ‘take henceforth’ → “now accept”: “accept” renders ‘take’, and the shortened time expression precedes the imperative.
- *nel mio parlar diffuso* ‘in my unfolded speech’ → “in speech outspread”: the possessive is dropped, and “outspread” retains the image of unfolding.

> 76 La lor concordia e i lor lieti sembianti  
> 77 amore e maraviglia e dolce sguardo  
> 78 faceano esser cagion di pensier santi;  

Their harmony and joyous semblance, made love and wonder and tender looks the cause of sacred thoughts;

- *La lor concordia* ‘their concord’ → “Their harmony”: “harmony” renders ‘concord’.
- *i lor lieti sembianti* ‘their joyous looks’ → “joyous semblance”: the possessive is not repeated, and the plural becomes a collective singular.
- *dolce sguardo* ‘sweet look’ → “tender looks”: the quality is rephrased and the singular becomes plural.
- *faceano esser cagion* ‘were making [them] be cause’ → “made” … “the cause”: the causative verb moves before its objects, the imperfect is rendered by a simple past, the infinitive disappears, and “the” is supplied.
- *pensier santi* ‘thoughts holy’ → “sacred thoughts”: “sacred” renders ‘holy’ and precedes the noun.

> 79 tanto che il venerabile Bernardo  
> 80 si scalzò prima, e retro a tanta pace  
> 81 corse, e correndo gli parv' esser tardo.  

so that the venerable Bernard first cast off his sandals and ran to follow so great peace, and as he ran him thought him all too slow.

- *si scalzò prima* ‘unshod himself first’ → “first cast off his sandals”: the adverb moves forward, and the reflexive verb becomes a phrase naming what he took off.
- *retro a tanta pace corse* ‘after so great a peace he ran’ → “ran to follow so great peace”: the verb moves forward, and the phrase of direction becomes an infinitive of purpose.
- *correndo* ‘running’ → “as he ran”: a gerund becomes a finite time clause.
- *gli parv' esser tardo* ‘to him he seemed to be slow’ → “him thought him all too slow”: the archaic impersonal “him thought” renders seeming to him; the second “him” expresses the person judged slow, the infinitive is dropped, and “all too” adds degree.

> 82 O ignota ricchezza, o ben ferace!  

Oh wealth unrecognised, oh fertile good!

- *ignota ricchezza* ‘unknown wealth’ → “wealth unrecognised”: “unrecognised” interprets ‘unknown’ and follows the noun.
- *ben ferace* ‘good fertile’ → “fertile good”: the adjective precedes the noun.

> 83 Scalzasi Egidio, scalzasi Silvestro,  
> 84 retro allo sposo, sì la sposa piace.  

Unsandals him Egidius, unsandals him Sylvester, following the spouse, so doth the bride delight.

- *Scalzasi Egidio, scalzasi Silvestro* ‘unshoes himself Egidius, unshoes himself Sylvester’ → “Unsandals him Egidius, unsandals him Sylvester”: the verbs specify sandals, and “him” is used reflexively in each phrase.
- *retro allo sposo* ‘after the bridegroom’ → “following the spouse”: a participle replaces the preposition, and “spouse” is less specific than ‘bridegroom’.
- *sì la sposa piace* ‘so greatly the bride pleases’ → “so doth the bride delight”: the emphatic adverb is followed by auxiliary inversion, and “delight” renders ‘please’.

> 85 Indi sen va quel padre e quel maestro  
> 86 con la sua donna, e con quella famiglia  
> 87 che già legava l' umile capestro;  

Thence took his way, this father and this master, together with his lady, and with the household already binding on the humble cord;

- *sen va* ‘he goes away’ → “took his way”: the pronominal expression becomes a phrase with a possessive object, and the narrative present becomes past.
- *quel padre e quel maestro* ‘that father and that master’ → “this father and this master”: both demonstratives change from distal to proximal.
- *con la sua donna* ‘with his lady’ → “together with his lady”: “together” amplifies the preposition.
- *con quella famiglia* ‘with that household’ → “with the household”: the demonstrative becomes a definite article.
- *che già legava l' umile capestro* ‘which the humble halter already bound’ → “already binding on the humble cord”: the translation takes the relative as subject rather than object, so that the household ties on the cord rather than the cord binding the household. A participial phrase replaces the finite relative clause, “on” specifies the action, and “cord” renders ‘halter’.

> 88 nè gli gravò viltà di cor le ciglia,  
> 89 per esser fi' di Pietro Bernardone,  
> 90 nè per parer dispetto a maraviglia.  

nor abjectness of heart weighed down his brow, that he was Pietro Bernadone’s son, nor that he seemed so marvellous despised.

- *nè gli gravò viltà di cor le ciglia* ‘nor did baseness of heart weigh down the brows for him’ → “nor abjectness of heart weighed down his brow”: the nominal subject moves before the verb, the dative becomes a possessive, and the plural becomes singular.
- *per esser fi' di Pietro Bernardone* ‘for being son of Pietro Bernardone’ → “that he was Pietro Bernadone’s son”: a causal preposition with an infinitive becomes a finite clause, and the father’s name becomes a preceding possessive.
- *nè per parer* ‘nor for seeming’ → “nor that he seemed”: the parallel preposition and infinitive likewise become a finite clause.
- *dispetto a maraviglia* ‘despised to a wonder’ → “so marvellous despised”: the phrase of degree becomes an intensifier before the participle.

## Lines 91–123 — Francis’s Seals and His Worthy Colleague

> 91 Ma regalmente sua dura intenzione  
> 92 ad Innocenzio aperse, e da lui ebbe  
> 93 primo sigillo a sua religione.  

But royally his stern intent to Innocent revealed he, and from him had the first imprint upon his Order.

- *dura intenzione* ‘hard intention’ → “stern intent”: the adjective is rendered in its sense of unyielding determination.
- *aperse* ‘he opened’ → “revealed he”: opening an intention is rendered as revealing it; the expressed subject follows the verb.
- *primo sigillo* ‘first seal’ → “the first imprint”: the article is supplied, and the seal is rendered as the mark it makes.
- *a sua religione* ‘to his religious order’ → “upon his Order”: the order is made the surface receiving the imprint rather than the recipient of a seal.

> 94 Poi che la gente poverella crebbe  
> 95 retro a costui, la cui mirabil vita  
> 96 meglio in gloria del ciel si canterebbe,  

When the poor folk increased, after his track whose marvellous life were better sung in heaven’s glory,

- *Poi che* ‘after’ → “When”: succession is expressed by a more general conjunction of time.
- *la gente poverella* ‘the folk poor and lowly’ → “the poor folk”: the adjective comes before the noun, and its diminutive force has no separate expression.
- *retro a costui* ‘behind this man’ → “after his track”: the demonstrative becomes a possessive, and the track is supplied.
- *meglio in gloria del ciel si canterebbe* ‘better in the glory of heaven would be sung’ → “were better sung in heaven’s glory”: the conditional construction with *si* becomes a subjunctive passive; the adverb stands within the verb phrase, and the phrase of place follows it, with the possessor before the noun.

> 97 di seconda corona redimita  
> 98 fu per Onorio dall' eterno spiro  
> 99 la santa voglia d' esto archimandrita.  

then was the holy will of this chief shepherd circled with a second crown by Honorius at the eternal inspiration.

- *di seconda corona redimita fu* ‘with second crown encircled was’ → “then was” … “circled with a second crown”: the resumptive “then” and the article are supplied; the auxiliary moves forward, and the crown phrase follows the participle.
- *per Onorio* ‘through Honorius’ → “by Honorius”: the intermediary is expressed as the agent of the passive.
- *dall' eterno spiro* ‘by the eternal breath’ → “at the eternal inspiration”: the breath becomes an inspiration, and agency becomes prompting.
- *la santa voglia d' esto archimandrita* ‘the holy desire of this archimandrite’ → “the holy will of this chief shepherd”: the subject moves from the end to a place between auxiliary and participle; desire is rendered as will, and the title by a descriptive phrase.

> 100 E poi che, per la sete del martiro,  
> 101 nella presenza del Soldan superba  
> 102 predicò Cristo e gli altri che il seguiro,  

And when, in thirst of martyrdom, in the proud presence of the Soldan, he preached Christ and his followers;

- *poi che* ‘after’ → “when”: the explicit relation of succession is loosened.
- *per la sete del martiro* ‘because of the thirst for martyrdom’ → “in thirst of martyrdom”: the motive becomes a state, the article disappears, and “of” replaces the preposition of the object desired.
- *nella presenza del Soldan superba* ‘in the presence of the Soldan proud’ → “in the proud presence of the Soldan”: the adjective moves from after the intervening genitive to immediately before the noun it qualifies.
- *gli altri che il seguiro* ‘the others who followed him’ → “his followers”: the relative clause becomes a noun with a possessive, and ‘others’ is not separately rendered.

> 103 e per trovare a conversione acerba  
> 104 troppo la gente, per non stare indarno,  
> 105 reddissi al frutto dell' italica erba;  

and because he found the folk too crude against conversion,—not to stay in vain,—returned to gather fruit from the Italian herbage;

- *per trovare a conversione acerba troppo la gente* ‘for finding, as regards conversion, unripe too much the folk’ → “because he found the folk too crude against conversion”: the causal infinitive becomes a finite clause; its object comes directly after the verb, the degree word precedes the adjective, and unripeness in respect of conversion becomes crudeness “against conversion”.
- *per non stare indarno* ‘in order not to stay in vain’ → “not to stay in vain”: the purpose is conveyed by the bare construction with “to”, without a separate equivalent for *per*.
- *reddissi al frutto dell' italica erba* ‘he returned to the fruit of the Italian grass’ → “returned to gather fruit from the Italian herbage”: the pronominal verb becomes a simple verb; the destination becomes a purpose with “gather” supplied, the fruit loses its article, and the genitive becomes a phrase of source.

> 106 nel crudo sasso, intra Tevero ed Arno,  
> 107 da Cristo prese l' ultimo sigillo,  
> 108 che le sue membra due anni portarno.  

then on the harsh rock between Tiber and Arno, from Christ did he receive that final imprint which his limbs two years carried.

- *nel crudo sasso* ‘in the harsh rock’ → “then on the harsh rock”: “then” is supplied to resume the preceding clauses, and the locative preposition becomes “on”.
- *prese* ‘he took’ → “did he receive”: taking is rendered as receiving, with an auxiliary and inversion.
- *l' ultimo sigillo* ‘the last seal’ → “that final imprint”: the article becomes demonstrative, and the seal is again rendered as its impression.

> 109 Quando a colui ch' a tanto ben sortillo  
> 110 piacque di trarlo suso alla mercede,  
> 111 ch' ei meritò nel suo farsi pusillo,  

When it pleased him who for such good ordained him, to draw him up to his reward which he had earned in making himself lowly,

- *a colui ch' a tanto ben sortillo piacque* ‘to him who to so great a good destined him it pleased’ → “it pleased him who for such good ordained him”: the impersonal verb comes first with an anticipatory “it”; its dative becomes a direct object, ‘so great a good’ becomes “such good”, and destination is expressed as ordination for a purpose.
- *di trarlo suso* ‘of drawing him up’ → “to draw him up”: the Italian infinitive introduced by *di* is rendered by an English to-infinitive.
- *alla mercede* ‘to the reward’ → “to his reward”: a possessive replaces the article.
- *meritò* ‘merited’ → “had earned”: the simple past becomes a past perfect, and merit is expressed as earning.
- *nel suo farsi pusillo* ‘in his making himself little’ → “in making himself lowly”: the possessive marking the subject of the substantival infinitive drops out, and littleness is rendered as lowliness.

> 112 ai frati suoi, sì com' a giuste rede,  
> 113 raccomandò la sua donna più cara,  
> 114 e comandò che l' amassero a fede;  

to his brethren, as to his right heirs, his dearest lady he commended, and bade that they should love her faithfully;

- *sì com' a giuste rede* ‘just as to rightful heirs’ → “as to his right heirs”: the comparison is not separately reinforced, and a possessive is supplied.
- *raccomandò la sua donna più cara* ‘he commended his lady most dear’ → “his dearest lady he commended”: the object moves before the verb, and the superlative becomes a single word before the noun.
- *comandò che l' amassero* ‘commanded that her they love’ → “bade that they should love her”: the dependent imperfect subjunctive is expressed with “should”, and the object follows the verb.
- *a fede* ‘with faith’ → “faithfully”: a prepositional phrase becomes an adverb.

> 115 e del suo grembo l' anima preclara  
> 116 mover si volle, tornando al suo regno,  

and from her bosom the illustrious soul willed to depart, turning to its own realm,

- *l' anima preclara* ‘the soul illustrious’ → “the illustrious soul”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *mover si volle* ‘to move itself it willed’ → “willed to depart”: the governing verb comes first, and pronominal movement is rendered as departure.
- *tornando al suo regno* ‘returning to its realm’ → “turning to its own realm”: “turning” does not explicitly express return, and “own” reinforces the possessive.

> 117 ed al suo corpo non volle altra bara.  

and for its body would no other bier.

- *al suo corpo* ‘to its body’ → “for its body”: the dative is expressed as a phrase of intended use.
- *non volle altra bara* ‘it did not will another bier’ → “would no other bier”: the verb of volition becomes “would”, and the negative moves from the verb to the noun phrase.

> 118 Pensa oramai qual fu colui, che degno  
> 119 collega fu a mantener la barca  
> 120 di Pietro in alto mar per dritto segno!  

Think now what he was, who was a worthy colleague to maintain the barque of Peter in deep sea towards the right sign!

- *oramai* ‘by now’ → “now”: the sense of a point by this time reached is left implicit.
- *qual fu colui* ‘of what sort was that man’ → “what he was”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun, and the subject precedes the verb.
- *degno collega fu* ‘worthy colleague was’ → “was a worthy colleague”: the verb precedes its complement, and an article is supplied.
- *in alto mar* ‘on the high sea’ → “in deep sea”: the rendering takes *alto* in its sense of depth.
- *per dritto segno* ‘by a straight mark’ → “towards the right sign”: the mark becomes a goal of movement, straightness is rendered as rightness, and a definite article is supplied.

> 121 E questi fu il nostro patriarca;  

And such was our patriarch;

- *questi* ‘this man’ → “such”: a demonstrative identifying the person becomes a word characterizing him.

> 122 per che qual segue lui, com' ei comanda,  
> 123 discerner puoi che buone merce carca.  

wherefore who followeth him as he commandeth, thou must perceive, loadeth him with good wares.

- *per che* ‘for which reason’ → “wherefore”: the preposition and relative are condensed into a connective adverb.
- *discerner puoi che* ‘discern thou canst that’ → “thou must perceive”: ability becomes necessity, the modal precedes the infinitive, and the clause becomes parenthetical rather than introducing its complement with ‘that’.
- *buone merce carca* ‘good wares loads’ → “loadeth him with good wares”: the verb comes first, “him” is supplied as its object, and the wares become the complement of “with” rather than the direct object.

## Lines 124–139 — The Straying Flock and the Rebuke

> 124 Ma il suo peculio di nuova vivanda  
> 125 è fatto ghiotto sì ch' esser non puote  
> 126 che per diversi salti non si spanda;  

But his flock hath grown so greedy for new viands, it may not be but that through divers glades it strayeth;

- *di nuova vivanda* ‘of new food’ → “for new viands”: the complement is moved from before the verb to after “greedy”; the preposition changes and the singular becomes plural.
- *è fatto ghiotto sì* ‘is become greedy so’ → “hath grown so greedy”: becoming is expressed by the English perfect of ‘grow’, and the degree adverb comes before the adjective.
- *ch' esser non puote* ‘that be it cannot’ → “it may not be”: the first result conjunction is left unexpressed, and the infinitive follows the modal.
- *che … non si spanda* ‘that … it should not spread itself out’ → “but that … it strayeth”: “but that” carries the force of the second negative after “it may not be”; the reflexive subjunctive becomes a nonreflexive indicative, and dispersal is rendered as straying.
- *per diversi salti* ‘through various pastures’ → “through divers glades”: “glades” gives a more specifically woodland sense to the grazing grounds.

> 127 e quanto le sue pecore remote  
> 128 e vagabonde più da esso vanno,  
> 129 più tornano all' ovil di latte vote.  

and the more his sheep distant and wandering depart from him, the emptier of milk they return foldwards.

- *quanto … più* ‘by how much … more’ → “the more”: the two separated elements of the first comparative are expressed together at the head of the clause.
- *da esso vanno* ‘from him they go’ → “depart from him”: “depart” makes the motion away explicit, and the prepositional phrase follows the verb.
- *più tornano … di latte vote* ‘more they return … of milk empty’ → “the emptier of milk they return”: the degree adverb is absorbed into the inflected comparative with “the”; the adjective precedes its complement, and both precede the verb.
- *all' ovil* ‘to the fold’ → “foldwards”: the preposition, article, and noun become a single directional adverb at the end of the clause.

> 130 Ben son di quelle che temono il danno,  
> 131 e stringonsi al pastor; ma son sì poche,  

There are of them, indeed, who fear the loss and cleave close to the shepherd, but they are so few

- *Ben son di quelle* ‘indeed there are of those’ → “There are of them, indeed”: the emphatic adverb moves to the end of the phrase, and the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun.
- *stringonsi* ‘press themselves close’ → “cleave close”: an intransitive verb with an adverb replaces the reflexive verb.

> 132 che le cappe fornisce poco panno.  

that little cloth doth furnish forth their cowls.

- *le cappe fornisce poco panno* ‘the cowls furnishes little cloth’ → “little cloth doth furnish forth their cowls”: the subject moves from last to first and the object from first to last; the definite article becomes possessive, and the simple verb is expanded with auxiliary “doth” and particle “forth”.

> 133 Or, se le mie parole non son fioche,  
> 134 se la tua audienza è stata attenta,  
> 135 se ciò ch' ho detto alla mente rivoche,  

Now if my words have not been faint, if thy listening hath been attent, if thou call back to mind what I have said,

- *non son fioche* ‘are not faint’ → “have not been faint”: the present becomes a present perfect.
- *la tua audienza … attenta* ‘thy hearing … attentive’ → “thy listening … attent”: the noun is rendered by a verbal noun emphasizing the act of listening; “attent” is the older English form of the adjective.
- *ciò ch' ho detto alla mente rivoche* ‘that which I have said to the mind thou recall’ → “thou call back to mind what I have said”: the final verb becomes “call back” and moves to the front; the phrase of destination follows it without an article, and the opening object clause comes last, with “what” for ‘that which’.

> 136 in parte fia la tua voglia contenta,  

in part thy will must now be satisfied,

- *fia la tua voglia contenta* ‘will be thy desire satisfied’ → “thy will must now be satisfied”: the subject precedes the verb, “will” renders ‘desire’, and the future is recast as a modal inference with “now” supplied.

> 137 perchè vedrai la pianta onde si scheggia,  

for thou shalt see the plant from which they whittle,

- *perchè* ‘because’ → “for”: a coordinating explanation replaces the subordinating conjunction.
- *onde si scheggia* ‘from which one cuts a chip’ → “from which they whittle”: the impersonal construction becomes a generic plural, and the cutting of a chip is expressed by the single verb “whittle”.

> 138 e vedrai il coregger che argomenta,  
> 139 *U' ben s' impingua, se non si vaneggia.*”  

and thou shalt see the rebuke that is intended in: *Where is good fattening if there be no straying.*”

- *il coregger* ‘the correcting’ → “the rebuke”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun naming the correction as an utterance.
- *che argomenta* ‘which it argues’ → “that is intended in”: the active construction in which the following saying conveys the correction becomes a passive one, with the saying introduced by “in”.
- *ben s' impingua* ‘one fattens well’ → “is good fattening”: the impersonal verb becomes a noun in an existential construction, and the adverb becomes its adjective.
- *se non si vaneggia* ‘if one does not wander’ → “if there be no straying”: the negated impersonal verb becomes a negative noun phrase in an existential subjunctive; “straying” takes the wandering in the pastoral sense.

## Summary — English Order and Italian Echoes

Wicksteed often brings forward a verb that Dante holds back until after its object or complement: *li tuoi pensieri … apprendo* begins in English with “I apprehend”, and *di cherubica luce uno splendore* becomes “a splendour of cherubic light”. Yet he does not consistently smooth out the Italian order. In “Of one will I discourse” he keeps the opening emphasis on *Dell' un*, and in “which his limbs two years carried” he leaves the verb to the end, as Dante does.

Changes of wording sometimes broaden the sense, as when *sillogismi* becomes “arguments”; elsewhere they make an action more specific, as when *si scalzò*, ‘unshod himself’, becomes “cast off his sandals”. Wicksteed also shifts meaning from one part of speech to another. In *ben s' impingua*, ‘one fattens well’, the verb and adverb become a noun and adjective in “is good fattening”. A gerund can become a finite clause, as *correndo* becomes “as he ran”; conversely, *gli altri che il seguiro*, ‘the others who followed him’, contracts to “his followers”.

Small omissions and additions often go together. *All' ovil*, ‘to the fold’, loses its preposition and article as separate words, but “foldwards” preserves the direction. In *non surse il secondo* the negative passes from verb to subject, while “ever” reinforces it in “No second ever rose”. Two routine adjustments generally go unmarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending identifies the subject, and drops the article that Italian uses before a possessive, as in *le mie parole*, “my words”.

For all these shifts, Wicksteed stays close to patterns of repetition and phrasing. He answers the doubled *scalzasi* with the same “unsandals him” construction twice, and keeps each of the three successive *se* clauses near the close as a separate condition beginning with “if”. Even where the grammar changes, the recurring phrase is still audible: the rendering of *u' ben s' impingua* as “Where is good fattening” is the same at its first appearance and its last.
