# Paradiso 29

## Lines 1–12 — Beatrice’s Pause and Reply

> 1 Quando ambo e due i figli di Latona,  
> 2 coperti del Montone e della Libra,  
> 3 fanno dell'orizzonte insieme zona,  

When both the two children of Latona, covered by the Ram and by the Scales, make the horizon their girdle at one same moment,

- *ambo e due i figli* ‘both and two the children’ → “both the two children”: the conjunction drops out, and the article moves before the numeral.
- *del Montone e della Libra* ‘with the Ram and with the Balance’ → “by the Ram and by the Scales”: the coverings are introduced by “by,” and the singular *Libra* is rendered by a plural noun.
- *fanno dell'orizzonte … zona* ‘make of the horizon a girdle’ → “make the horizon their girdle”: the horizon becomes the direct object of “make,” and the possessive “their” is supplied.
- *insieme* ‘together’ → “at one same moment”: the adverb is expanded into a phrase specifying simultaneity and moved to the end of the clause.

> 4 quant' è dal punto che il zenit inlibra,  
> 5 infin che l'uno e l'altro da quel cinto,  
> 6 cambiando l'emisperio, si dilibra,  

as long as from the point when the zenith balanceth the scale, till one and the other from that belt unbalanceth itself, changing its hemisphere,

- *quant' è* ‘as much as it is’ → “as long as”: the measure is expressed as duration, without a finite verb.
- *che* ‘that’ → “when”: the link to ‘the point’ is made explicitly temporal.
- *inlibra* ‘holds in balance’ → “balanceth the scale”: the image contained in the verb is expressed by a verb with the added object “the scale.”
- *cambiando l'emisperio* ‘changing the hemisphere’ → “changing its hemisphere”: the article becomes a possessive, and the participial phrase moves after the verb it qualifies.
- *si dilibra* ‘itself unbalances’ → “unbalanceth itself”: the reflexive pronoun follows the verb.

> 7 tanto, col volto di riso dipinto,  
> 8 si tacque Beatrice, riguardando  
> 9 fisso nel punto che m'aveva vinto;  

so long, with a smile traced on her countenance, did Beatrice hold her peace, gazing fixedly on the point which had o’ermastered me;

- *tanto* ‘so much’ → “so long”: as in the corresponding phrase in line 4, English makes the measure one of time.
- *col volto di riso dipinto* ‘with the face painted with a smile’ → “with a smile traced on her countenance”: the smile rather than the face becomes the object of “with” and the thing described as “traced”; the face becomes the surface, and the article becomes a possessive.
- *si tacque Beatrice* ‘kept silent Beatrice’ → “did Beatrice hold her peace”: the pronominal verb becomes an idiom with a possessive object; the past tense is carried by “did,” with the subject between the auxiliary and the main verb.
- *fisso* ‘fixed’ → “fixedly”: the adjective used adverbially becomes an adverb in form as well.
- *nel punto* ‘in the point’ → “on the point”: the preposition changes with the English construction for directing one’s gaze.
- *m'aveva vinto* ‘me had conquered’ → “had o’ermastered me”: the object pronoun moves after the verb, and conquest is expressed as mastery.

> 10 poi cominciò: “Io dico, non domando  
> 11 quel che tu vuoli udir, perch' io l' ho visto  
> 12 dove s'appunta ogni *ubi* ed ogni *quando.*  

then she began: “I tell, not ask, that which thou fain wouldst hear; for I have seen it where every *where* and every *when* is focussed.

- *vuoli udir* ‘wish to hear’ → “fain wouldst hear”: the finite verb of desire and its infinitive give way to an adverb of willingness with a modal and a bare infinitive.
- *perch'* ‘because’ → “for”: a coordinating conjunction replaces the subordinating one.
- *l' ho visto* ‘it have seen’ → “have seen it”: the object pronoun moves after the participle.
- *s'appunta ogni ubi ed ogni quando* ‘comes to a point every where and every when’ → “every *where* and every *when* is focussed”: the subject moves before the verb, and the pronominal verb of convergence becomes a passive expression of focus.

## Lines 13–45 — The Simultaneous Creation of All Things

> 13 Non per aver a sè di bene acquisto,  
> 14 ch' esser non può, ma perchè suo splendore  
> 15 potesse, risplendendo, dir: *Subsisto.*  

Not to have gain of any good unto himself, which may not be, but that his splendour might, as it glowed, declare, *I am.*

- *Non per aver a sè di bene acquisto* ‘not in order to have unto himself of good a gain’ → “Not to have gain of any good unto himself”: the infinitive alone expresses purpose; the object follows “have”, ahead of both its modifier and the phrase naming the recipient. “Any” is supplied under the negation.
- *ch' esser non può* ‘which be cannot’ → “which may not be”: the infinitive follows the modal, and “may not” expresses impossibility.
- *ma perchè … potesse* ‘but in order that … might be able’ → “but that his splendour might”: “that” introduces the purpose clause, and the modal alone renders the imperfect subjunctive.
- *risplendendo* ‘shining forth’ → “as it glowed”: the gerund becomes a finite clause of time; “glowed” does not repeat the stem of the noun for splendour.
- *dir: Subsisto* ‘say: I subsist’ → “declare, *I am.*”: the verb of speaking is strengthened, while the Latin verb is rendered by the simpler verb of being.

> 16 In sua eternità di tempo fuore,  
> 17 fuor d'ogni altro comprender, come i piacque,  
> 18 s'aperse in nuovi amor l' eterno amore.  

In his eternity beyond time, beyond all other comprehension, as was his pleasure, the eternal love revealed him in new loves.

- *di tempo fuore* ‘of time outside’ → “beyond time”: the postposed adverb and its complement become a preposition followed by its object.
- *fuor d'ogni altro comprender* ‘outside of every other comprehending’ → “beyond all other comprehension”: “beyond” again serves as a preposition; the substantivized infinitive becomes an abstract noun, with “all” for the distributive ‘every’.
- *come i piacque* ‘as it pleased him’ → “as was his pleasure”: the impersonal past verb becomes a copula with a noun, and the indirect object becomes a possessive.
- *s'aperse in nuovi amor l' eterno amore* ‘opened itself in new loves the eternal love’ → “the eternal love revealed him in new loves”: the subject moves from last to first; opening becomes revealing, and the reflexive is rendered by a personal object pronoun.

> 19 Nè prima quasi torpente si giacque;  

Nor did he lie, as slumbering, before;

- *Nè prima quasi torpente si giacque* ‘nor before as if torpid did he lie’ → “Nor did he lie, as slumbering, before”: the verb moves forward, with auxiliary inversion after the negative; its pronominal form has no separate English reflexive. The time adverb moves to the end, and the qualified state of torpor is rendered by a comparison to slumbering.

> 20 chè nè prima nè poscia procedette  
> 21 lo discorrer di Dio sopra quest'acque.  

for nor before nor after was the process of God’s outflowing over these waters.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a coordinating conjunction replaces the subordinating one.
- *procedette lo discorrer di Dio* ‘proceeded the coursing of God’ → “was the process of God’s outflowing”: the finite verb of proceeding gives way to “was” and the noun “process”; the substantivized infinitive becomes “outflowing”, preceded by a possessive rather than followed by an *of*-phrase.

> 22 Forma e materia congiunte e purette  
> 23 usciro ad esser che non avea fallo,  
> 24 come d'arco tricorde tre saette;  

Form and matter, united and in purity, issued into being which had no flaw, as from a three-stringed bow three arrows;

- *congiunte e purette* ‘conjoined and quite pure’ → “united and in purity”: the second adjective becomes a prepositional phrase, without a separate equivalent for the force of the suffix.
- *usciro ad esser* ‘came forth to being’ → “issued into being”: the verb of coming out becomes “issued”, and the preposition marks entry.
- *d'arco tricorde* ‘from bow three-stringed’ → “from a three-stringed bow”: the article is supplied and the adjective precedes the noun.

> 25 e come in vetro, in ambra od in cristallo  
> 26 raggio risplende sì che dal venire  
> 27 all'esser tutto non è intervallo;  

and as in glass, in amber, or in crystal, a ray so gloweth that from its coming to its pervading all, there is no interval;

- *raggio risplende sì* ‘ray shines forth so’ → “a ray so gloweth”: an article is supplied, “gloweth” renders the verb of shining, and the degree adverb precedes the verb.
- *dal venire all'esser tutto* ‘from the coming to the being all’ → “from its coming to its pervading all”: possessives identify the ray as the subject of both verbal nouns; being wholly present is interpreted as pervading the whole medium.
- *non è intervallo* ‘is no interval’ → “there is no interval”: the existential construction takes introductory “there”.

> 28 così il triforme effetto del suo Sire  
> 29 nell'esser suo raggiò insieme tutto,  
> 30 senza distinzion nell' esordire.  

so the threefold effect of its Lord rayed out all at once into its being, without distinction of beginning.

- *il triforme effetto* ‘the three-formed effect’ → “the threefold effect”: the adjective expresses threefoldness without naming form.
- *nell'esser suo raggiò insieme tutto* ‘into the being its it rayed together, all of it’ → “rayed out all at once into its being”: the destination follows the verb, with the possessive before the noun; “out” gives the verb direction, and completeness and simultaneity are gathered into one adverbial phrase.
- *nell' esordire* ‘in the beginning’ → “of beginning”: the substantivized infinitive denoting the act of beginning becomes the complement of “distinction”, with “of” rather than ‘in’.

> 31 Concreato fu ordine e costrutto  
> 32 alle sustanzie: e quelle furon cima  
> 33 nel mondo, in che puro atto fu produtto.  

Co-created was order and co-woven with the substances; and those were the summit in the universe wherein pure act was produced.

- *e costrutto alle sustanzie* ‘and constructed for the substances’ → “and co-woven with the substances”: construction becomes weaving; the prefix of the earlier participle is carried over, and the dative becomes a phrase of accompaniment.
- *furon cima nel mondo* ‘were summit in the world’ → “were the summit in the universe”: the predicate noun takes a definite article, and “universe” renders ‘world’.
- *fu produtto* ‘was brought forth’ → “was produced”: the passive is retained with a single lexical verb for bringing forth.

> 34 Pura potenza tenne la parte ima;  

Pure potentiality held the lowest place;

- *Pura potenza* ‘pure potency’ → “Pure potentiality”: the technical noun is rendered by its more explicitly modal equivalent.
- *la parte ima* ‘the part lowest’ → “the lowest place”: “place” renders ‘part’, and the superlative precedes the noun.

> 35 nel mezzo strinse potenza con atto  
> 36 tal vime, che giammai non si divima.  

in the midst power twisted such a withy with act as shall ne’er be unwithied.

- *strinse potenza con atto tal vime* ‘bound potency with act such a withe’ → “power twisted such a withy with act”: the postverbal subject moves before the verb and is now called “power”; the bond is twisted rather than drawn tight, and the direct object precedes the phrase with “with”.
- *che giammai non si divima* ‘that it never unbinds itself’ → “as shall ne’er be unwithied”: “such” is answered by “as”; the negative words have a single English equivalent, and the pronominal present becomes a future passive. The coined verb preserves the verbal echo of the noun for the bond.

> 37 Jeronimo vi scrisse lungo tratto  
> 38 di secoli degli Angeli creati  
> 39 anzi che l'altro mondo fosse fatto;  

Jerome wrote to you of a long stretch of ages wherein the Angels were created ere aught else of the universe was made;

- *vi scrisse lungo tratto di secoli* ‘to you wrote a long stretch of centuries’ → “wrote to you of a long stretch of ages”: the recipient follows the verb; “of” introduces the matter written about, an article is supplied, and “ages” replaces the more determinate ‘centuries’.
- *degli Angeli creati* ‘of the Angels created’ → “wherein the Angels were created”: the phrase with a participle is expanded into a finite passive clause, linked to the ages by a relative adverb of time.
- *anzi che l'altro mondo fosse fatto* ‘before the other world should be made’ → “ere aught else of the universe was made”: the rest of the world is expressed by an indefinite pronoun with a partitive complement, and the imperfect subjunctive becomes an English indicative passive.

> 40 ma questo vero è scritto in molti lati  
> 41 dagli scrittor dello Spirito Santo;  
> 42 e tu te n' avvedrai, se bene agguati:  

but the truth I tell is writ on many a page of the writers of the Holy Spirit, and thou shalt be aware of it if well thou look;

- *questo vero* ‘this truth’ → “the truth I tell”: the demonstrative becomes a definite article plus a relative clause identifying the speaker’s assertion.
- *in molti lati* ‘in many places’ → “on many a page”: the places are specified as pages, with a corresponding change of preposition; the plural becomes distributive singular.
- *dagli scrittor* ‘by the writers’ → “of the writers”: the agents of the passive become possessors of the pages.
- *te n' avvedrai* ‘of it thou wilt become aware’ → “thou shalt be aware of it”: the pronominal verb of coming to notice becomes a phrase denoting awareness, with its complement after it.

> 43 ed anche la ragione il vede alquanto,  
> 44 che non concederebbe che i motori  
> 45 senza sua perfezion fosser cotanto.  

and also reason seeth it some little, which would not grant that the movers should so long abide without their perfecting.

- *il vede alquanto* ‘it sees somewhat’ → “seeth it some little”: the object pronoun follows the verb, and the adverb of degree becomes a two-word expression.
- *senza sua perfezion fosser cotanto* ‘without their perfection should be so long’ → “should so long abide without their perfecting”: the phrase of privation moves to the end; the singular possessive referring back to the movers becomes plural in English, and the abstract noun becomes a verbal noun. Duration, implicit in the use of ‘so much’, is made explicit in “so long”; the imperfect subjunctive of being becomes “should” with a verb of continuance.

## Lines 46–78 — The Angels’ Fall, Merit, and Vision

> 46 Or sai tu dove e quando questi amori  
> 47 furon eletti, e come; sì che spenti  
> 48 nel tuo disio già sono tre ardori.  

Now dost thou know where and when these Loves were chosen and how, so that three flames are quenched already in thy longing.

- *sai tu* ‘knowest thou’ → “dost thou know”: the inflected verb is rendered with an auxiliary before the subject and the main verb after it.
- *spenti nel tuo disio già sono tre ardori* ‘quenched in thy desire already are three ardours’ → “three flames are quenched already in thy longing”: the subject comes first, the auxiliary precedes the participle, and the phrase expressing where the quenching takes place comes last. ‘Ardours’ becomes the more concrete “flames”, and ‘desire’ becomes “longing”.

> 49 Nè giugneriesi numerando al venti  
> 50 sì tosto, come degli Angeli parte  
> 51 turbò il suggetto dei vostri elementi.  

Nor should one, counting, come so soon to twenty as did a part of the Angels disturb the substrate of your elements.

- *giugneriesi numerando al venti sì tosto* ‘would one arrive by counting at twenty so soon’ → “should one, counting, come so soon to twenty”: the impersonal conditional is rendered with “should one” and an infinitive; the gerund of means is set between subject and infinitive, and “so soon” precedes the number.
- *degli Angeli parte turbò* ‘of the Angels a part disturbed’ → “did a part of the Angels disturb”: the partitive phrase follows “a part”, and the simple past is divided into an auxiliary before the subject and an infinitive after it.

> 52 L'altra rimase, e cominciò quest'arte,  
> 53 che tu discerni, con tanto diletto,  
> 54 che mai da circuir non si diparte.  

The rest abode and began this art which thou perceivest, with so great delight that from circling round they ne’er depart.

- *L'altra* ‘the other [part]’ → “The rest”: the adjective with an understood noun becomes a collective expression for the remainder.
- *discerni* ‘discernest’ → “perceivest”: the verb of distinguishing is rendered by a more general verb of perception.
- *tanto diletto* ‘so much delight’ → “so great delight”: degree is expressed in terms of greatness rather than amount.
- *mai da circuir non si diparte* ‘never from circling does it depart’ → “from circling round they ne’er depart”: the negative is placed after the phrase it preceded, and the Italian negative concord is rendered by a single negative. The sense of the infinitive is expanded into “circling round”, and the singular referring to the other part becomes plural “they”.

> 55 Principio del cader fu il maledetto  
> 56 superbir di colui, che tu vedesti  
> 57 da tutti i pesi del mondo costretto.  

The beginning of the fall was the accursed pride of him whom thou didst see constrained by all the weights of the universe.

- *Principio del cader* ‘beginning of the falling’ → “The beginning of the fall”: an article is supplied before ‘beginning’, and the substantivized infinitive is rendered by the noun “fall”.
- *il maledetto superbir* ‘the accursed being proud’ → “the accursed pride”: a substantivized infinitive becomes an abstract noun.
- *vedesti* ‘sawest’ → “didst see”: an auxiliary and infinitive replace the simple past.
- *da tutti i pesi del mondo costretto* ‘by all the weights of the world constrained’ → “constrained by all the weights of the universe”: the participle precedes its complement, and “universe” gives ‘world’ its wider sense.

> 58 Quelli, che vedi qui, furon modesti  
> 59 a riconoscer sè dalla bontate,  
> 60 che gli avea fatti a tanto intender presti;  

Those whom thou seest here were modest to acknowledge themselves derived from that same Excellence which made them swift to so great understanding;

- *a riconoscer sè dalla bontate* ‘to acknowledge themselves from the goodness’ → “to acknowledge themselves derived from that same Excellence”: “derived” makes the relation of origin explicit; “that same” replaces the definite article, and “Excellence” interprets ‘goodness’.
- *che gli avea fatti* ‘which them had made’ → “which made them”: the pluperfect becomes a simple past, and the object follows the verb.
- *a tanto intender presti* ‘for so much understanding ready’ → “swift to so great understanding”: the adjective is placed before its complement and rendered as ‘swift’ rather than ‘ready’; the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun qualified by “so great”.

> 61 per che le viste lor furo esaltate  
> 62 con grazia illuminante e con lor merto,  
> 63 sì ch' hanno piena e ferma volontate.  

wherefore their vision was exalted with grace illuminating and with their merit, so that they have their will full and established.

- *le viste lor furo esaltate* ‘the sights of theirs were exalted’ → “their vision was exalted”: the possessive precedes the noun, and the plural noun and verb become singular.
- *hanno piena e ferma volontate* ‘they have full and firm will’ → “they have their will full and established”: the possessive is supplied, the two qualifications follow the noun as complements, and ‘firm’ becomes the participial “established”.

> 64 E non voglio che dubbi, ma sie certo  
> 65 che ricever la grazia è meritorio,  
> 66 secondo che l'affetto l' è aperto.  

And I would not have thee doubt, but be assured that ’tis a merit to receive the grace by laying the affection open to it.

- *non voglio che dubbi* ‘I do not wish that thou doubt’ → “I would not have thee doubt”: the present verb of wishing and its subjunctive clause become a modal construction with an object and bare infinitive.
- *ma sie certo* ‘but be certain’ → “but be assured”: certainty is expressed by a participle rather than by ‘certain’.
- *ricever la grazia è meritorio* ‘to receive the grace is meritorious’ → “’tis a merit to receive the grace”: an anticipatory subject moves the infinitive to the end, and a noun with an article replaces the predicate adjective.
- *secondo che l'affetto l' è aperto* ‘according as the affection is open to it’ → “by laying the affection open to it”: a clause expressing proportion becomes a phrase of means; the state of being open becomes an act of laying open, with ‘the affection’ now its object.

> 67 Omai d' intorno a questo consistorio  
> 68 puoi contemplare assai, se le parole  
> 69 mie son ricolte, senz' altro aiutorio.  

Now, as concerns this consistory much mayst thou contemplate (if my words have been upgathered) with no other aid.

- *Omai* ‘by now’ → “Now”: the temporal adverb is simplified.
- *d' intorno a questo consistorio* ‘concerning this consistory’ → “as concerns this consistory”: a prepositional expression becomes a verbal one.
- *puoi contemplare assai* ‘thou canst contemplate much’ → “much mayst thou contemplate”: “much” is brought to the front, the modal precedes the subject, and ability is expressed by “mayst”.
- *le parole mie son ricolte* ‘the words of mine are gathered’ → “my words have been upgathered”: the possessive moves before the noun, and a present expression of the resulting state becomes a perfect passive.
- *senz' altro aiutorio* ‘without other aid’ → “with no other aid”: the negation moves from the preposition to the determiner.

> 70 Ma perchè in terra per le vostre scuole  
> 71 si legge che l'angelica natura  
> 72 è tal che intende e si ricorda e vuole,  

But since on earth in your schools ’tis said in lectures that the angelic nature is such as understandeth and remembereth and willeth,

- *si legge* ‘one reads’ → “’tis said in lectures”: the impersonal verb becomes a passive of saying, with the academic sense of ‘reading’ made explicit by “in lectures”.
- *è tal che* ‘is such that’ → “is such as”: the following verbs are introduced by relative “as” rather than by ‘that’.

> 73 ancor dirò, perchè tu veggi pura  
> 74 la verità che laggiù si confonde,  
> 75 equivocando in sì fatta lettura.  

I will speak on, that thou mayst see in purity the truth that down there is confounded by the equivocations of such like discourse.

- *ancor dirò* ‘further I shall speak’ → “I will speak on”: continuation is expressed by a particle after the verb rather than an adverb before it.
- *perchè tu veggi pura la verità* ‘in order that thou see the truth pure’ → “that thou mayst see in purity the truth”: purpose and the subjunctive are rendered by “that” with “mayst”; the adjective qualifying ‘truth’ becomes an adverbial phrase.
- *si confonde* ‘gets confused’ → “is confounded”: the pronominal construction becomes an English passive.
- *equivocando in sì fatta lettura* ‘by equivocating in such a lecture’ → “by the equivocations of such like discourse”: the gerund becomes a plural noun, and the lecture in which the equivocation occurs becomes the discourse to which the equivocations belong.

> 76 Queste sustanzie, poichè fur gioconde  
> 77 della faccia di Dio, non volser viso  
> 78 da essa, da cui nulla si nasconde:  

These substances, since first they gathered joy from the face of God, have never turned their vision from it wherefrom nought is concealed;

- *poichè fur gioconde della faccia di Dio* ‘since they were joyful in the face of God’ → “since first they gathered joy from the face of God”: “first” makes the starting point explicit; a copula and adjective become a verb and object, with the face expressed as the source of the joy.
- *non volser viso da essa* ‘did not turn their face from it’ → “have never turned their vision from it”: the simple past becomes a present perfect, ‘not’ becomes “never”, and ‘face’ is interpreted as “vision”.
- *da cui nulla si nasconde* ‘from which nothing hides itself’ → “wherefrom nought is concealed”: a preposition and relative pronoun become a relative adverb, and the pronominal verb is rendered as a passive.

## Lines 79–111 — The Love of Show and the Neglect of Scripture

> 79 però non hanno vedere interciso  
> 80 da nuovo obbietto, e però non bisogna  
> 81 rimemorar per concetto diviso.  

wherefore their sight is never intercepted by a fresh object, and so behoveth not to call aught back to memory because thought hath been cleft.

- *non hanno vedere interciso* ‘they have not their seeing cut off’ → “their sight is never intercepted”: what they have becomes the subject of a passive verb; the simple negative becomes one of time.
- *da nuovo obbietto* ‘by fresh object’ → “by a fresh object”: the indefinite article is supplied.
- *non bisogna rimemorar* ‘there is no need to recollect’ → “behoveth not to call aught back to memory”: the impersonal expression of necessity becomes a single verb; the Italian infinitive is expanded into a phrase with an explicit object, “aught”.
- *per concetto diviso* ‘on account of divided thought’ → “because thought hath been cleft”: the prepositional phrase becomes a causal clause, with a perfect passive verb in place of the participial modifier.

> 82 Sì che laggiù non dormendo si sogna,  
> 83 credendo e non credendo dicer vero;  
> 84 ma nell'uno è più colpa e più vergogna.  

Wherefore they dream, down there, though sleeping not; thinking or thinking not they speak the truth; but more in one than other is the fault and shame.

- *laggiù non dormendo si sogna* ‘down there, not sleeping, one dreams’ → “they dream, down there, though sleeping not”: the impersonal construction becomes a personal plural; the main verb moves ahead of both its modifiers, the negative follows the participle, and “though” makes the concession explicit.
- *credendo e non credendo* ‘believing and not believing’ → “thinking or thinking not”: thinking replaces believing, the conjunction becomes disjunctive, and the second negative follows its participle.
- *dicer vero* ‘to speak true’ → “they speak the truth”: the infinitive becomes a finite clause, and the bare *vero* becomes a noun with an article.
- *nell'uno è più colpa e più vergogna* ‘in the one is more fault and more shame’ → “more in one than other is the fault and shame”: “more” is brought forward and used only once; the article on ‘one’ disappears, the other term of the comparison is supplied, and an article introduces the paired nouns.

> 85 Voi non andate giù per un sentiero  
> 86 filosofando; tanto vi trasporta  
> 87 l'amor dell'apparenza e il suo pensiero.  

Ye below tread not on one path when ye philosophise, so far doth love of show, and the thought it begets transport you.

- *Voi non andate giù per un sentiero filosofando* ‘you do not go down along one path philosophising’ → “Ye below tread not on one path when ye philosophise”: “below” is attached to the subject; going along becomes treading on, with the negative after the verb, and the gerund becomes a temporal clause.
- *tanto vi trasporta l'amor dell'apparenza e il suo pensiero* ‘so much does the love of appearance carry you away, and the thought of it’ → “so far doth love of show, and the thought it begets transport you”: degree is expressed as distance; the compound subject comes before the main verb and the object after it. The articles on love and appearance drop out, appearance becomes “show”, and the possessive is interpreted as a relation of origin.

> 88 Ed ancor questo quassù si comporta  
> 89 con men disdegno, che quando è posposta  
> 90 la divina scrittura, o quando è torta.  

Yet even this with lesser indignation is endured here above than when divine Scripture is thrust behind or wrenched aside.

- *Ed ancor* ‘and also’ → “Yet even”: the connection becomes adversative and the addition emphatic.
- *quassù si comporta con men disdegno* ‘here above is borne with less disdain’ → “with lesser indignation is endured here above”: the phrase of manner moves before the verb and the place adverb after it; disdain becomes indignation.
- *che quando è posposta la divina scrittura* ‘than when is put after the divine Scripture’ → “than when divine Scripture is thrust behind”: the subject moves before the passive verb and loses its article; “thrust” gives the setting behind a more forceful expression.
- *o quando è torta* ‘or when it is twisted’ → “or wrenched aside”: the second temporal conjunction and finite verb are not repeated, and “aside” gives the wrenching a direction.

> 91 Non vi si pensa quanto sangue costa  
> 92 seminarla nel mondo, e quanto piace  
> 93 chi umilmente con essa s' accosta.  

They think not how great the cost of blood to sow it in the world, and how he pleaseth who humbly keepeth by its side.

- *Non vi si pensa* ‘one does not think on it’ → “They think not”: the impersonal construction becomes a personal plural, the anticipatory ‘on it’ drops out, and the negative follows the verb.
- *quanto sangue costa* ‘how much blood it costs’ → “how great the cost of blood”: the question of quantity becomes one of magnitude; the verb becomes a noun, with blood in a phrase dependent on it.
- *quanto piace chi* ‘how much pleases whoever’ → “how he pleaseth who”: degree is expressed by “how” alone, and the single relative subject is unfolded into an antecedent before the verb and a relative after it.
- *con essa s' accosta* ‘draws close to it’ → “keepeth by its side”: reflexive movement towards something becomes remaining beside it, with “side” supplied.

> 94 Per apparer ciascun s' ingegna, e face  
> 95 sue invenzioni, e quelle son trascorse  
> 96 dai predicanti, e il vangelio si tace.  

Each one straineth his wit to make a show and plieth his inventions; and these are handled by the preachers, and the Gospel left in silence.

- *Per apparer ciascun s' ingegna* ‘to appear each one exerts himself’ → “Each one straineth his wit to make a show”: the purpose phrase moves to the end and becomes an idiom; reflexive exertion becomes straining an explicitly named faculty.
- *face sue invenzioni* ‘makes his inventions’ → “plieth his inventions”: the verb describes putting the inventions to use rather than making them.
- *quelle son trascorse* ‘those are run through’ → “these are handled”: the demonstrative changes from distant to near, and running through the inventions becomes handling them.
- *il vangelio si tace* ‘the Gospel is passed over in silence’ → “the Gospel left in silence”: the Italian verb of silence becomes a participle with a prepositional phrase; the auxiliary is understood from the preceding passive.

> 97 Un dice che la luna si ritorse  
> 98 nella passion di Cristo e s' interpose,  
> 99 per che il lume del sol giù non si porse;  

One saith the moon drew herself back when Christ suffered, and interposed herself that the sun’s light spread not itself below;

- *che* ‘that’ → “One saith the moon”: the conjunction introducing what is said is omitted.
- *si ritorse* ‘turned herself back’ → “drew herself back”: drawing back replaces turning back.
- *nella passion di Cristo* ‘in the passion of Christ’ → “when Christ suffered”: the temporal prepositional phrase becomes a clause, with Christ as subject and the noun of suffering recast as a verb.
- *per che* ‘on account of which’ → “that”: the causal relative phrase becomes a conjunction introducing the result.
- *il lume del sol giù non si porse* ‘the light of the sun did not extend itself down’ → “the sun’s light spread not itself below”: the dependent noun becomes a preceding possessive, extending becomes spreading, the negative follows the verb, and the directional adverb moves to the end as an adverb of place.

> 100 ed altri che la luce si nascose  
> 101 da sè; però agl' Ispani ed agl' Indi,  
> 102 com' a' Giudei, tale eclissi rispose.  

and others, that the light concealed itself of its own self; wherefore that same eclipse responded to the Spaniards and the Indians as to the Jews.

- *da sè* ‘by itself’ → “of its own self”: the preposition changes and “own” reinforces the reflexive.
- *agl' Ispani ed agl' Indi, com' a' Giudei* ‘to the Spaniards and to the Indians, as to the Jews’ → “to the Spaniards and the Indians as to the Jews”: the recipients move after the verb and the second ‘to’ is not repeated.
- *tale eclissi rispose* ‘such an eclipse responded’ → “that same eclipse responded”: the rendering identifies the eclipse as the same one rather than describing it as such.

> 103 Non ha Fiorenza tanti Lapi e Bindi,  
> 104 quante sì fatte favole per anno  
> 105 in pergamo si gridan quinci e quindi;  

Florence hath not so many Lapos and Bindos as the fables of such fashion that yearly are proclaimed from the pulpit on this side and on that;

- *Non ha Fiorenza* ‘has not Florence’ → “Florence hath not”: the subject moves before the verb and the negative after it.
- *quante sì fatte favole* ‘as many such-fashioned fables as’ → “as the fables of such fashion that”: the comparative quantity is expressed without a second “many”; the noun takes an article and a following phrase in place of its preceding modifier, and “that” introduces the relative clause.
- *per anno* ‘per year’ → “yearly”: the prepositional phrase becomes an adverb.
- *in pergamo si gridan* ‘in the pulpit are cried out’ → “are proclaimed from the pulpit”: the place phrase moves after the verb and marks the source rather than the location; crying out becomes proclaiming.
- *quinci e quindi* ‘here and there’ → “on this side and on that”: the two adverbs are expanded into phrases of place.

> 106 sì che le pecorelle, che non sanno,  
> 107 tornan dal pasco pasciute di vento,  
> 108 e non le scusa non veder lo danno.  

so that the sheep, who know not aught, return from their pasture fed with wind, and not to see their loss doth not excuse them.

- *le pecorelle* ‘the little sheep’ → “the sheep”: the diminutive is unmarked.
- *che non sanno* ‘who do not know’ → “who know not aught”: the negative follows the verb and an indefinite object is supplied.
- *dal pasco* ‘from the pasture’ → “from their pasture”: a possessive replaces the article.
- *non le scusa non veder lo danno* ‘does not excuse them not to see the harm’ → “not to see their loss doth not excuse them”: the infinitive subject moves before the finite verb and its object follows it; harm is rendered as loss, with a possessive in place of the article.

> 109 Non disse Cristo al suo primo convento:  
> 110 *Andate e predicate al mondo ciance,*  
> 111 ma diede lor verace fondamento;  

Christ said not to his first assembly: *Go and preach trifles to the world;*—but gave to them the true foundation;

- *Non disse Cristo* ‘said not Christ’ → “Christ said not”: the subject precedes the verb and the negative follows it.
- *predicate al mondo ciance* ‘preach to the world idle talk’ → “preach trifles to the world”: the direct object moves before the recipient, and the word for idle utterances is rendered by a term for things of little consequence.
- *verace fondamento* ‘true foundation’ → “the true foundation”: the definite article is supplied.

## Lines 112–145 — Preaching and the Mirrors of Eternal Light

> 112 e quel tanto sonò nelle sue guance,  

that, and that only, sounded on their lips;

- *e quel tanto* ‘and that alone’ → “that, and that only”: the demonstrative is stated twice, with the conjunction between the two occurrences.
- *nelle sue guance* ‘in their cheeks’ → “on their lips”: both the preposition and the part of the mouth named are changed.

> 113 sì ch' a pugnar, per accender la fede,  
> 114 dell' evangelio fero scudo e lance.  

wherefore for their battle to kindle faith they made both shield and lance out of the Gospel.

- *sì ch’* ‘so that’ → “wherefore”: a clause of result becomes a statement of consequence.
- *a pugnar* ‘to fight’ → “for their battle”: the infinitive becomes a noun with a possessive.
- *per accender la fede* ‘in order to kindle the faith’ → “to kindle faith”: purpose is expressed by the infinitive alone, and the article is dropped.
- *dell’ evangelio* ‘of the Gospel’ → “out of the Gospel”: the phrase is moved from before the verb to the end and made more explicitly a phrase of material.
- *scudo e lance* ‘shield and lances’ → “both shield and lance”: “both” is supplied, and the second noun becomes singular to match the first.

> 115 Ora si va con motti e con iscede  
> 116 a predicare, e pur che ben si rida,  
> 117 gonfia il cappuccio, e più non si richiede.  

Now they go forth with jests and with grimaces to preach, and if loud laughter rise, the hood inflates and no more is required.

- *si va* ‘one goes’ → “they go forth”: the impersonal singular becomes a personal plural, and “forth” is supplied.
- *iscede* ‘buffooneries’ → “grimaces”: the more general term for antics is given a specifically facial sense.
- *pur che ben si rida* ‘provided only that one laughs heartily’ → “if loud laughter rise”: the proviso becomes a simple condition; impersonal laughing becomes the noun “laughter” with a new verb, and the adverb becomes an adjective of volume.
- *gonfia il cappuccio* ‘swells the hood’ → “the hood inflates”: the postposed subject is moved before the verb.
- *più non si richiede* ‘more is not required’ → “no more is required”: the negative is attached to “more” rather than to the verb.

> 118 Ma tale uccel nel becchetto s' annida,  
> 119 che, se il vulgo il vedesse, vederebbe  
> 120 la perdonanza di che si confida;  

But such a bird is nestling in the hood-tail that if the crowd should see it, they would see what pardon they are trusting in;

- *nel becchetto s’ annida* ‘in the hood-tail nests’ → “is nestling in the hood-tail”: the place phrase is moved after the verb, and the pronominal simple present becomes an English progressive.
- *il vulgo il vedesse* ‘the common people it saw’ → “the crowd should see it”: the collective is rendered by “the crowd”, the object follows the verb, and “should see” expresses the imperfect subjunctive in the supposition.
- *vederebbe* ‘would see’ → “they would see”: the singular verb agreeing with the collective gives way to plural reference to its members.
- *la perdonanza di che si confida* ‘the pardon in which one trusts’ → “what pardon they are trusting in”: a definite noun with a relative clause becomes an indirect question; the impersonal present becomes a personal plural progressive, with the preposition at the end.

> 121 per cui tanta stoltizia in terra crebbe,  
> 122 che, senza prova d'alcun testimonio,  
> 123 ad ogni promission si converrebbe.  

wherefore such folly hath increased on earth that without proof of any testimony the folk would jump with any promise.

- *per cui* ‘through which’ → “wherefore”: a relative phrase is rendered by a connective adverb.
- *tanta stoltizia* ‘so much folly’ → “such folly”: quantity is expressed instead by “such” in the construction of result.
- *in terra crebbe* ‘on earth grew’ → “hath increased on earth”: the place phrase follows the verb, and the simple past becomes a present perfect.
- *alcun testimonio* ‘any witness’ → “any testimony”: the witness is rendered by the evidence given.
- *ad ogni promission si converrebbe* ‘to every promise one would assent’ → “the folk would jump with any promise”: the impersonal construction acquires an explicit collective subject; assent is expressed by the idiom “jump with”, and the phrase governed by the verb moves after it, with “any” for ‘every’.

> 124 Di questo ingrassa il porco sant' Antonio,  
> 125 ed altri ancor che son assai più porci,  
> 126 pagando di moneta senza conio.  

Whereby Antonio fatteneth his swine, and others too, more swinish far than they, paying with money that hath no imprint.

- *Di questo* ‘on this’ → “Whereby”: the demonstrative phrase becomes a relative adverb of means.
- *ingrassa il porco sant’ Antonio* ‘fattens the pig Saint Antonio’ → “Antonio fatteneth his swine”: the saint’s name, set last in Italian, is taken as the subject and put first; the title is omitted, the definite article becomes a possessive, and the singular pig becomes the collective “swine”.
- *che son assai più porci* ‘who are much more pigs’ → “more swinish far than they”: the relative and copula drop out, the noun becomes an adjective, the intensifier follows the adjective, and the second term of comparison is made explicit.
- *di moneta senza conio* ‘in coin without a stamp’ → “with money that hath no imprint”: the preposition of payment changes, “money” replaces ‘coin’, and a prepositional phrase becomes a relative clause with a negative determiner.

> 127 Ma perchè siam digressi assai, ritorci  
> 128 gli occhi oramai verso la dritta strada,  
> 129 sì che la via col tempo si raccorci.  

But since we have digressed enough, turn back thine eyes now to the true path, so that our journey may contract with our time.

- *assai* ‘much’ → “enough”: extent is rendered as sufficiency.
- *gli occhi* ‘the eyes’ → “thine eyes”: English specifies the possessor where Italian uses the definite article.
- *verso la dritta strada* ‘toward the straight road’ → “to the true path”: direction becomes destination, and ‘straight’ is rendered in the sense of ‘right’ or ‘true’.
- *la via col tempo si raccorci* ‘the way with the time may shorten’ → “our journey may contract with our time”: the way becomes the journey, both definite articles become possessives, and the time phrase moves after the verb.

> 130 Questa natura sì oltre s' ingrada  
> 131 in numero, che mai non fu loquela,  
> 132 nè concetto mortal che tanto vada:  

This nature ranketh so wide in number that ne’er was speech nor thought of mortal that advanced so far:

- *sì oltre s’ ingrada in numero* ‘so far ascends in degree in number’ → “ranketh so wide in number”: the pronominal verb of gradation becomes “ranketh”, placed before the expression of extent, and distance becomes width.
- *mai non fu* ‘never was there’ → “ne’er was”: the Italian negative pair is expressed by a single negative adverb.
- *concetto mortal* ‘mortal conception’ → “thought of mortal”: the adjective qualifying the conception becomes a phrase naming the thinker.
- *tanto vada* ‘so far may go’ → “advanced so far”: the present subjunctive of the relative clause becomes a simple past, the verb is more directional, and the expression of extent follows it.

> 133 e se tu guardi quel che si rivela  
> 134 per Daniel, vedrai che in sue migliaia  
> 135 determinato numero si cela.  

and if thou look at that which is revealed by Daniel, thou shalt see that in his thousands determinate number is lost to sight.

- *guardi quel* ‘look upon that’ → “look at that”: the Italian direct object is linked to the English verb by “at”.
- *si rivela per Daniel* ‘is revealed through Daniel’ → “is revealed by Daniel”: the passive use of the pronominal verb becomes an English passive, and the intermediary is expressed as its agent.
- *si cela* ‘hides itself’ → “is lost to sight”: the pronominal verb becomes a phrase describing the result for the observer.

> 136 La prima luce, che tutta la raia,  
> 137 per tanti modi in essa si recepe,  
> 138 quanti son gli splendori a che s'appaia.  

The primal light which doth o’erray it all, is received by it in so many ways as are the splendours wherewithal it paireth.

- *La prima luce* ‘the first light’ → “The primal light”: the ordinal is rendered by an adjective suggesting primacy.
- *tutta la raia* ‘all of it irradiates’ → “doth o’erray it all”: the verb precedes its object, the quantifier follows the pronoun, and the auxiliary “doth” is supplied.
- *per tanti modi in essa si recepe* ‘in so many ways in it is received’ → “is received by it in so many ways”: both prepositional phrases move after the verb, in reverse order; the pronominal passive becomes an English passive, and the phrase naming that in which the light is received names the receiver instead.
- *quanti son gli splendori* ‘as many as are the splendours’ → “as are the splendours”: the second, explicitly numerical member of the Italian correlation is reduced to “as”.
- *a che s’appaia* ‘to which it pairs itself’ → “wherewithal it paireth”: the relation is expressed as pairing *with* rather than pairing *to*, and the English verb is intransitive rather than pronominal.

> 139 Onde, però che all'atto che concepe  
> 140 segue l'affetto, d'amor la dolcezza  
> 141 diversamente in essa ferve e tepe.  

Wherefore, since affection followeth on the act that doth conceive, the sweetness of love in diverse fashion boileth or is warm in them.

- *all’atto che concepe segue l’affetto* ‘upon the act that conceives follows affection’ → “affection followeth on the act that doth conceive”: the postposed subject comes first, the complement moves after the verb, and “doth” is supplied in the relative clause.
- *d’amor la dolcezza* ‘of love the sweetness’ → “the sweetness of love”: the dependent phrase follows its noun.
- *diversamente* ‘diversely’ → “in diverse fashion”: an adverb becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *in essa* ‘in it’ → “in them”: the reference shifts from the singular nature to its plural members, and the phrase moves to the end.
- *ferve e tepe* ‘boils and is warm’ → “boileth or is warm”: the conjunction of addition becomes one of alternative.

> 142 Vedi l'eccelso omai, e la larghezza  
> 143 dell'eterno valor, poscia che tanti  
> 144 speculi fatti s' ha, in che si spezza,  
> 145 uno manendo in sè, come davanti.”  

See now the height and breadth of the eternal worth, since it hath made itself so many mirrors wherein it breaketh, remaining in itself one as before.”

- *l’eccelso omai, e la larghezza* ‘the lofty now, and the breadth’ → “now the height and breadth”: the substantivized adjective becomes an abstract noun parallel to “breadth”, the adverb moves before both nouns, and they share one article.
- *tanti speculi fatti s’ ha* ‘so many mirrors made for itself it has’ → “it hath made itself so many mirrors”: the auxiliary precedes the participle, and the reflexive beneficiary comes before the direct object rather than after the participle.
- *in che si spezza* ‘in which it breaks itself’ → “wherein it breaketh”: the preposition and relative become one relative adverb, and the English verb is intransitive.
- *uno manendo in sè* ‘one remaining in itself’ → “remaining in itself one”: the predicative “one”, placed first in Italian, is moved after the participle and its prepositional phrase.

## Summary — Italian Order and English Idiom

Wicksteed often changes the order of words while keeping their grammatical relations intact. A subject placed last in Italian may come first in English, as when *l’eterno amore* moves ahead of its verb as “the eternal love”. Object pronouns generally move the other way: *l’ho visto* becomes “I have seen it”. Two routine adjustments are mostly left unmarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where Italian gives the subject by the verb ending alone, and drops the article before a possessive, as in *le vostre scuole*, “your schools”.

Changes of construction sometimes alter the emphasis. The gerund *filosofando* becomes the clause “when ye philosophise”, making the relation one of time; the impersonal *si va* becomes the personal plural “they go forth”. An addition can make an image explicit: *inlibra*, ‘holds in balance’, becomes “balanceth the scale”. Elsewhere a shade of meaning drops out, as when *pecorelle*, ‘little sheep’, becomes simply “sheep”. A change of vocabulary can also shift the image without expanding it: *nelle sue guance*, ‘in their cheeks’, becomes “on their lips”.

Yet Wicksteed keeps close to the Italian where a balance of phrases or an echo between words carries the expression. He retains the repetition of *in vetro, in ambra od in cristallo* in “in glass, in amber, or in crystal”. Even where the syntax changes, the verbal pattern can survive: the pronominal present *non si divima* becomes a future passive, but “withy” and “unwithied” reproduce the distinctive echo of *vime* and *divima*.
