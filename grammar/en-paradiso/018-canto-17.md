# Paradiso 17

## Lines 1–18 — The Desire to Know What Is to Come

> 1 Qual venne a Climenè, per accertarsi  
> 2 di ciò ch' avea incontro a sè udito,  
> 3 quei ch' ancor fa li padri ai figli scarsi;  

As came to Clymene, to have assurance as to that which he had heard uttered against himself, he who still maketh fathers grudging to their sons;

- *Qual* ‘such as’ → “As”: the opening word of the comparison is rendered as a conjunction.
- *per accertarsi* ‘to assure himself’ → “to have assurance”: the reflexive verb of the purpose clause becomes a verb with a noun as its object.
- *di ciò* ‘of that’ → “as to that”: the complement of the reflexive verb becomes a phrase specifying what the assurance concerns.
- *ch' avea incontro a sè udito* ‘which he had against himself heard’ → “which he had heard uttered against himself”: the participle moves next to its auxiliary; “uttered” is supplied, giving “against himself” a verb of saying to qualify.
- *quei* ‘that one’ → “he”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *li padri ai figli scarsi* ‘the fathers to the sons sparing’ → “fathers grudging to their sons”: the generic article before ‘fathers’ drops out, the article before ‘sons’ becomes a possessive, and the predicate adjective, rendered “grudging”, comes before its complement.

> 4 tale era io, e tale era sentito  
> 5 e da Beatrice e dalla santa lampa,  
> 6 che pria per me avea mutato sito.  

such was I; and such was I felt both by Beatrice and by the sacred lamp which had already, for my sake, changed its position.

- *e da Beatrice e dalla* ‘and by Beatrice and by the’ → “both by Beatrice and by the”: the first of the paired conjunctions becomes “both”.
- *santa* ‘holy’ → “sacred”: a different adjective is used.
- *pria per me avea mutato sito* ‘previously for me had changed place’ → “had already, for my sake, changed its position”: the auxiliary moves ahead of the modifiers; ‘previously’ becomes “already”, ‘for me’ is expanded to “for my sake”, and the bare noun ‘place’ becomes “its position”.

> 7 Per che mia donna: “Manda fuor la vampa  
> 8 del tuo disio, mi disse, sì ch' ell' esca  
> 9 segnata bene della interna stampa;  

Wherefore my Lady: “Let forth the heat of thy desire,” she said, “that it may issue, struck aright with the internal stamp;

- *Manda fuor* ‘send forth’ → “Let forth”: the imperative of sending is rendered by one of letting out.
- *la vampa* ‘the flame’ → “the heat”: the effect of a flame replaces the flame itself.
- *mi disse* ‘to me she said’ → “she said”: the indirect object is omitted.
- *sì ch' ell' esca* ‘so that it may come out’ → “that it may issue”: ‘so’ has no separate equivalent, and “issue” renders the outward motion in a single verb.
- *segnata bene* ‘marked well’ → “struck aright”: “struck” makes the act of stamping explicit, and “aright” takes ‘well’ in the sense of ‘correctly’.

> 10 non perchè nostra conoscenza cresca  
> 11 per tuo parlare, ma perchè t' ausi  
> 12 a dir la sete, sì che l' uom ti mesca.”  

not that our knowledge may increase by thy discourse, but that thou mayst learn to tell thy thirst, that men may mingle for thee.”

- *t' ausi* ‘thou mayst accustom thyself’ → “thou mayst learn”: learning replaces becoming accustomed, and the reflexive construction disappears.
- *la sete* ‘the thirst’ → “thy thirst”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *sì che l' uom ti mesca* ‘so that one may pour for thee’ → “that men may mingle for thee”: ‘so’ has no separate equivalent; the generic singular becomes a plural, the indirect object moves after the verb, and “mingle” takes the verb in the sense of mixing a drink rather than pouring it.

> 13 “O cara piota mia, che sì t' insusi  
> 14 che, come veggion le terrene menti  
> 15 non capere in triangolo due ottusi,  

“Dear turf, wherein I root me, who art so high uplifted that even as earthly minds perceive that two obtuse angles may not find room in one triangle,

- *O cara piota mia* ‘O dear turf of mine’ → “Dear turf, wherein I root me”: the vocative particle drops out, and the possessive becomes a relative clause expressing the speaker’s relation to the turf.
- *che sì t' insusi* ‘who so raisest thyself on high’ → “who art so high uplifted”: the reflexive verb becomes a participial construction with “art”.
- *come veggion le terrene menti* ‘as see the earthly minds’ → “even as earthly minds perceive”: “even” is supplied, the generic article drops out, the subject precedes the verb, and seeing is rendered as perceiving.
- *non capere in triangolo due ottusi* ‘not to fit in a triangle two obtuse [angles]’ → “that two obtuse angles may not find room in one triangle”: the infinitive after the verb of perception becomes a finite clause with “that” and “may”; its subject moves to the front, “angles” supplies the noun implicit in the adjective, “find room” renders ‘fit’, and “one” makes the singular explicit.

> 16 così vedi le cose contingenti  
> 17 anzi che sieno in sè, mirando il punto  
> 18 a cui tutti li tempi son presenti;  

so thou dost see contingent things, or ere themselves exist, gazing upon the point whereto all times are present;

- *così vedi* ‘so thou seest’ → “so thou dost see”: the simple present is expanded with the auxiliary “dost”.
- *le cose contingenti* ‘the things contingent’ → “contingent things”: the generic article drops out and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *anzi che sieno in sè* ‘before they be in themselves’ → “or ere themselves exist”: the temporal conjunction becomes the archaic “or ere”; the subjunctive of ‘be’ becomes the unmarked present “exist”, and the prepositional phrase becomes the emphatic subject “themselves”.
- *mirando il punto* ‘beholding the point’ → “gazing upon the point”: the directly transitive verb becomes one taking a preposition.
- *a cui* ‘to which’ → “whereto”: the preposition and relative pronoun are combined in a relative adverb.
- *tutti li tempi* ‘all the times’ → “all times”: the definite article is omitted.

## Lines 19–51 — Foreknowledge and the Prophecy of Exile

> 19 mentre ch' io era a Virgilio congiunto  
> 20 su per lo monte che l' anime cura,  
> 21 e discendendo nel mondo defunto,  

whilst I was companioned by Virgil along the mount which cureth souls, and down-going through the world defunct,

- *a Virgilio congiunto* ‘to Virgil joined’ → “companioned by Virgil”: companionship replaces junction, the participle precedes the name, and ‘to’ becomes “by”.
- *su per lo monte* ‘up along the mount’ → “along the mount”: the upward direction is left unexpressed.
- *che l' anime cura* ‘which the souls cureth’ → “which cureth souls”: the object follows the verb and loses its article.
- *discendendo nel* ‘descending in the’ → “down-going through the”: the simple verb becomes a compound, and the preposition marks passage through rather than location in.

> 22 dette mi fur di mia vita futura  
> 23 parole gravi; avvenga ch' io mi senta  
> 24 ben tetragono ai colpi di ventura:  

heavy words were said to me anent my future life; albeit I feel me squarely set against the blows of fortune;

- *dette mi fur di mia vita futura parole gravi* ‘said to me were of my life future words heavy’ → “heavy words were said to me anent my future life”: the delayed subject comes first, both adjectives precede their nouns, and the auxiliary precedes the participle. The recipient follows the verb, and the phrase giving the subject matter comes last, with ‘of’ rendered as “anent”.
- *avvenga ch' io mi senta* ‘although I myself may feel’ → “albeit I feel me”: a single conjunction replaces the Italian conjunctive phrase, the subjunctive is not distinguished in English, and the reflexive follows the verb.
- *ben tetragono ai colpi* ‘well foursquare to the blows’ → “squarely set against the blows”: the adjective becomes an adverb qualifying the supplied “set”; the intensifier is not separately rendered, and ‘to’ becomes “against”.

> 25 per che la voglia mia saria contenta  
> 26 d' intender qual fortuna mi s' appressa;  
> 27 chè saetta previsa vien più lenta.”  

wherefore my will were well content to hear what the disaster drawing nigh to me; for the arrow seen before cometh less rudely.”

- *la voglia mia saria contenta* ‘the will of mine would be content’ → “my will were well content”: the possessive comes before the noun, the conditional becomes hypothetical “were”, and “well” is supplied.
- *d' intender* ‘to understand’ → “to hear”: the verb is taken in the sense of hearing rather than understanding.
- *qual fortuna mi s' appressa* ‘what fortune to me draws near’ → “what the disaster drawing nigh to me”: “disaster” gives ‘fortune’ an expressly adverse sense; the article is supplied, the finite reflexive verb becomes a participle, and “to me” follows it.
- *saetta previsa* ‘an arrow foreseen’ → “the arrow seen before”: the generic noun takes the definite article, and the idea of foresight is expressed by an adverb after the participle.
- *vien più lenta* ‘comes slower’ → “cometh less rudely”: an adjective agreeing with the arrow becomes an adverb of manner, and a greater degree of slowness becomes a lesser degree of harshness.

> 28 Così diss' io a quella luce stessa,  
> 29 che pria m' avea parlato, e come volle  
> 30 Beatrice, fu la mia voglia confessa.  

So spake I unto that same light which had before addressed me, and, as Beatrice willed, was my wish confessed.

- *quella luce stessa* ‘that light itself’ → “that same light”: the emphatic adjective follows the noun in Italian but precedes it in English.
- *che pria m' avea parlato* ‘which before to me had spoken’ → “which had before addressed me”: “before” moves inside the verb phrase, and speaking to someone becomes addressing a direct object, placed after the verb.
- *come volle Beatrice* ‘as willed Beatrice’ → “as Beatrice willed”: the subject precedes the verb.

> 31 Nè per ambage, in che la gente folle  
> 32 già s' inviscava pria che fosse anciso  
> 33 l' agnel di Dio che le peccata tolle,  

In no dark sayings, such as limed the foolish folk of old, before the Lamb of God who taketh sins away, was slain,

- *Nè per ambage* ‘nor by circumlocutions’ → “In no dark sayings”: the initial negative connective becomes a negative determiner, ‘by’ becomes “In”, and indirectness is expressed as obscurity.
- *in che la gente folle già s' inviscava* ‘in which the folk foolish formerly got themselves limed’ → “such as limed the foolish folk of old”: the sayings become the subject of an active verb rather than the medium in which the folk became entangled. The folk become its object, their adjective precedes the noun, and the time expression moves to the end.
- *pria che fosse anciso l' agnel di Dio* ‘before was slain the Lamb of God’ → “before the Lamb of God” … “was slain”: the subject and its following relative clause precede the passive verb in English; the Italian subjunctive is not marked.
- *che le peccata tolle* ‘who the sins takes away’ → “who taketh sins away”: the object loses its article and comes between the verb and its particle.

> 34 ma per chiare parole, e con preciso  
> 35 latin, rispose quell' amor paterno,  
> 36 chiuso e parvente del suo proprio riso:  

but in clear words, and with precise discourse, answered that love paternal, hidden and revealed by his own smile:

- *per chiare parole* ‘by clear words’ → “in clear words”: the preposition of means becomes one of expression.
- *con preciso latin* ‘with precise Latin’ → “with precise discourse”: the language name is taken in its extended sense of speech.
- *chiuso e parvente del suo proprio riso* ‘enclosed and apparent by its own smile’ → “hidden and revealed by his own smile”: enclosure becomes concealment, and an adjective of visibility becomes a passive participle parallel to “hidden”. The possessive refers personally to the speaker.

> 37 “La contingenza, che fuor del quaderno  
> 38 della vostra materia non si stende,  
> 39 tutta è dipinta nel cospetto eterno;  

“Contingency, which beyond the sheet of your material stretcheth not, is all limned in the eternal aspect;

- *La contingenza* ‘the contingency’ → “Contingency”: English drops the article before the abstract noun.
- *fuor del quaderno* ‘outside the quire’ → “beyond the sheet”: “beyond” expresses the limit, and a single sheet stands for the gathering of sheets.
- *della vostra materia* ‘of your matter’ → “of your material”: “material” is used as the noun in place of ‘matter’.
- *non si stende* ‘not itself stretches’ → “stretcheth not”: the verb is intransitive rather than reflexive, and the negative follows it.
- *tutta è dipinta* ‘all is painted’ → “is all limned”: “all” follows the auxiliary, and painting is rendered as limning.
- *nel cospetto eterno* ‘in the sight eternal’ → “in the eternal aspect”: “aspect” stands for ‘sight’, and the adjective precedes the noun.

> 40 necessità però quindi non prende,  
> 41 se non come dal viso, in che si specchia,  
> 42 nave che per corrente giù discende.  

albeit it deriveth not necessity from this, no more than doth the ship that droppeth down the stream from the sight wherein she doth reflect herself.

- *necessità però quindi non prende* ‘necessity, however, thence it does not take’ → “albeit it deriveth not necessity from this”: the adversative becomes a subordinating conjunction; taking becomes deriving, with the negative after the verb, the object after the negative, and ‘thence’ expanded into a final prepositional phrase.
- *se non come* ‘except as’ → “no more than doth”: an exceptive comparison becomes a negative comparison of degree, with an auxiliary standing for the preceding verb.
- *dal viso, in che si specchia* ‘from the sight in which it mirrors itself’ → “from the sight wherein she doth reflect herself”: the whole phrase moves after the ship and its verb; “wherein” combines preposition and relative, “doth” is supplied, and the ship takes the personal “she” and “herself”.
- *nave che per corrente giù discende* ‘a ship that by the current down descends’ → “the ship that droppeth down the stream”: the generic ship takes the definite article, descent becomes dropping, and the current is expressed as the stream down which the ship moves rather than the means of its descent.

> 43 Da indi, sì come viene ad orecchia  
> 44 dolce armonia da organo, mi viene  
> 45 a vista il tempo che ti s' apparecchia.  

Thence, as cometh to the ear sweet harmony from an organ, cometh to my sight the time that is in store for thee.

- *sì come* ‘just as’ → “as”: the reinforcing adverb is left unexpressed.
- *ad orecchia* ‘to ear’ → “to the ear”: English supplies the definite article.
- *da organo* ‘from organ’ → “from an organ”: English supplies the indefinite article.
- *mi viene a vista* ‘to me comes to sight’ → “cometh to my sight”: the personal pronoun becomes a possessive modifying “sight”.
- *che ti s' apparecchia* ‘that for thee prepares itself’ → “that is in store for thee”: the reflexive verb of preparation becomes an expression of state, and the beneficiary follows it.

> 46 Qual si partì Ippolito d' Atene  
> 47 per la spietata e perfida noverca,  
> 48 tal di Fiorenza partir ti conviene.  

As Hippolytus was severed from Athens by machination of his cruel and perfidious stepmother, so must thou needs sever thee from Florence.

- *Qual si partì Ippolito d' Atene* ‘as departed Hippolytus from Athens’ → “As Hippolytus was severed from Athens”: the subject precedes the verb, and a reflexive verb of departure becomes a passive verb of separation.
- *per la spietata e perfida noverca* ‘because of the pitiless and perfidious stepmother’ → “by machination of his cruel and perfidious stepmother”: “machination” supplies a specific means, the article becomes a possessive, and ‘pitiless’ becomes “cruel”.
- *tal di Fiorenza partir ti conviene* ‘so from Florence to depart behoves thee’ → “so must thou needs sever thee from Florence”: the impersonal construction becomes a personal modal with emphatic “needs”; departure becomes reflexive severance, and the place phrase moves to the end.

> 49 Questo si vuole, questo già si cerca,  
> 50 e tosto verrà fatto a chi ciò pensa  
> 51 là dove Cristo tutto dì si merca.  

So it is willed, so already plotted, and so shall be accomplished soon, by him who pondereth upon it in the place where Christ, day in day out, is put to sale.

- *Questo si vuole* ‘this is willed’ → “So it is willed”: the demonstrative subject becomes an adverb with a separate pronoun as subject.
- *questo già si cerca* ‘this already is sought’ → “so already plotted”: the repeated demonstrative again becomes an adverb, the subject and auxiliary are understood, and seeking becomes plotting.
- *e tosto verrà fatto* ‘and soon will be done’ → “and so shall be accomplished soon”: “so” is supplied to continue the parallel, doing becomes accomplishment, and the time adverb comes last.
- *a chi ciò pensa* ‘for whoever thinks this’ → “by him who pondereth upon it”: the dative is interpreted as the agent of the accomplishment; the indefinite relative gains an explicit antecedent, and the demonstrative object becomes a pronoun governed by “upon”.
- *là dove* ‘there where’ → “in the place where”: the adverb becomes a prepositional phrase with an explicit noun.
- *tutto dì* ‘all day’ → “day in day out”: duration is expressed as continual repetition.
- *si merca* ‘is traded’ → “is put to sale”: the passive sense is retained but trading becomes the more specific act of offering for sale.

## Lines 52–84 — Exile and the First Refuge

> 52 La colpa seguirà la parte offensa  
> 53 in grido, come suol; ma la vendetta  
> 54 fia testimonio al ver che la dispensa.  

The blame shall cleave unto the injured side in fame, as is the wont; but vengeance shall bear witness to the truth which doth dispense it.

- *seguirà la parte offensa* ‘shall follow the side injured’ → “shall cleave unto the injured side”: following becomes attachment, with a preposition before the object; the adjective precedes the noun.
- *in grido* ‘in outcry’ → “in fame”: the rendering takes the cry to mean what is publicly said.
- *come suol* ‘as it is wont’ → “as is the wont”: the verb of custom becomes a noun with an article.
- *la vendetta* ‘the vengeance’ → “vengeance”: the article is dropped.
- *fia testimonio al ver* ‘shall be witness to the true’ → “shall bear witness to the truth”: bearing witness replaces being a witness; the substantivized adjective becomes an abstract noun.
- *la dispensa* ‘it dispenses’ → “doth dispense it”: the object pronoun moves after the verb, and the auxiliary is supplied.

> 55 Tu lascerai ogni cosa diletta  
> 56 più caramente, e questo è quello strale  
> 57 che l' arco dello esilio pria saetta.  

Thou shalt abandon everything beloved most dearly; this is the arrow which the bow of exile shall first shoot.

- *lascerai* ‘shalt leave’ → “shalt abandon”: the verb is stronger.
- *ogni cosa* ‘every thing’ → “everything”: adjective and noun become one pronoun.
- *più caramente* ‘more dearly’ → “most dearly”: the comparative is taken in a superlative sense.
- *e questo è quello strale* ‘and this is that arrow’ → “this is the arrow”: the connective is dropped and the demonstrative becomes an article.
- *dello esilio* ‘of the exile’ → “of exile”: the article is dropped.
- *pria saetta* ‘first shoots’ → “shall first shoot”: the present becomes future.

> 58 Tu proverai sì come sa di sale  
> 59 lo pane altrui, e com' è duro calle  
> 60 lo scendere e il salir per l' altrui scale.  

Thou shalt make trial of how salt doth taste another’s bread, and how hard the path to descend and mount upon another’s stair.

- *proverai* ‘shalt try’ → “shalt make trial of”: a transitive verb becomes a verb and noun with a preposition.
- *sì come sa di sale* ‘just how it tastes of salt’ → “how salt doth taste”: the reinforcement of *come* is dropped; the noun governed by *di* becomes a predicative adjective placed before the verb, with “doth” supplied.
- *lo pane altrui* ‘the bread of another’ → “another’s bread”: the possessor comes first and replaces the article.
- *com' è duro calle* ‘how hard a path is’ → “how hard the path”: the copula disappears and the path is made definite.
- *lo scendere e il salir* ‘the descending and the ascending’ → “to descend and mount”: the two substantivized infinitives with articles become infinitives sharing one “to”.
- *per l' altrui scale* ‘by another’s stairs’ → “upon another’s stair”: the preposition changes and the plural becomes a collective singular.

> 61 E quel che più ti graverà le spalle  
> 62 sarà la compagnia malvagia e scempia,  
> 63 con la qual tu cadrai in questa valle,  

And that which most shall weigh thy shoulders down, shall be the vicious and ill company with which thou shalt fall down into this vale,

- *ti graverà le spalle* ‘shall burden the shoulders for thee’ → “shall weigh thy shoulders down”: the affected person becomes the possessor of the shoulders; a verb with a particle replaces the single verb.
- *la compagnia malvagia e scempia* ‘the company wicked and foolish’ → “the vicious and ill company”: the adjectives come before the noun; “ill” gives a general adverse judgment rather than specifically denoting folly.
- *cadrai in questa valle* ‘thou shalt fall into this vale’ → “thou shalt fall down into this vale”: the direction implicit in falling is made explicit.

> 64 che tutta ingrata, tutta matta ed empia  
> 65 si farà contro a te; ma poco appresso  
> 66 ella, non tu, n' avrà rossa la tempia.  

for all ungrateful, all mad and impious shall they become against thee; but, soon after, their temples and not thine shall redden for it.

- *che* ‘which’ → “for”: a relative clause describing the company becomes an explanatory independent clause.
- *tutta ingrata, tutta matta ed empia si farà* ‘all ungrateful, all mad and impious will make itself’ → “all ungrateful, all mad and impious shall they become”: the reflexive takes the inchoative sense ‘become’; the singular collective is construed as plural and its members become the explicit, inverted subject.
- *ella, non tu, n' avrà rossa la tempia* ‘it, not thou, will have the temple red from it’ → “their temples and not thine shall redden for it”: the contrasted subjects become possessives joined by “and”; the singular body part becomes a plural subject, having it red becomes its reddening, and the preverbal *ne* becomes a final prepositional phrase.

> 67 Di sua bestialitate il suo processo  
> 68 farà la prova, sì che a te fia bello  
> 69 l' averti fatta parte per te stesso.  

Of their brutishness their progress shall make proof, so that it shall be for thy fair fame to have made a party for thyself.

- *Di sua bestialitate il suo processo* ‘of its brutishness its course’ → “Of their brutishness their progress”: both possessors are construed as plural; “progress” renders the course things take.
- *farà la prova* ‘shall make the proof’ → “shall make proof”: the article is dropped.
- *a te fia bello* ‘to thee shall be fair’ → “it shall be for thy fair fame”: anticipatory “it” is supplied; fairness to the addressee becomes a benefit to his reputation, with “fame” supplied.
- *l' averti fatta parte per te stesso* ‘the having made thyself a party by thyself’ → “to have made a party for thyself”: the articulated perfect infinitive becomes a to-infinitive; “a” is supplied, and the reflexive and the emphatic phrase of separateness are represented by a single “for thyself”.

> 70 Lo primo tuo rifugio e il primo ostello  
> 71 sarà la cortesia del gran Lombardo,  
> 72 che in su la scala porta il santo uccello,  

Thy first refuge and first hostelry shall be the courtesy of the great Lombard, who on the ladder beareth the sacred bird,

- *Lo primo tuo rifugio* ‘the first thy refuge’ → “Thy first refuge”: the possessive moves before the ordinal.
- *il primo ostello* ‘the first hostelry’ → “first hostelry”: the second article is dropped, letting “Thy” govern both nouns.
- *in su la scala* ‘up on the ladder’ → “on the ladder”: the compound preposition becomes a simple one.

> 73 chè in te avrà sì benigno riguardo  
> 74 che del fare e del chieder, tra voi due,  
> 75 fia primo quel che tra gli altri è più tardo.  

for he shall cast so benign regard on thee that of doing and demanding, that shall be first betwixt you two, which betwixt others most doth lag.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *in te avrà sì benigno riguardo* ‘toward thee he shall have so benign regard’ → “he shall cast so benign regard on thee”: having regard becomes casting it, and its direction is expressed after the object.
- *del fare e del chieder* ‘of the doing and of the asking’ → “of doing and demanding”: the articles and the repeated preposition disappear; “demanding” renders asking.
- *tra voi due, fia primo quel* ‘between you two, shall be first that’ → “that shall be first betwixt you two”: the demonstrative subject moves ahead of the verb and the prepositional phrase moves to the end.
- *tra gli altri è più tardo* ‘between the others is later’ → “betwixt others most doth lag”: the article is dropped; a copula and comparative adjective become a verb of lagging with a superlative adverb.

> 76 Con lui vedrai colui che impresso fue  
> 77 nascendo sì da questa stella forte,  
> 78 che notabili fien l' opere sue.  

With him shalt thou see the one who so at his birth was stamped by this strong star, that notable shall be his deeds.

- *impresso fue nascendo sì* ‘stamped was, in being born, so’ → “so at his birth was stamped”: the gerund becomes a prepositional phrase with a possessive noun; the degree adverb is brought forward and the auxiliary put before the participle.
- *stella forte* ‘star strong’ → “strong star”: the adjective precedes the noun.

> 79 Non se ne son le genti ancora accorte,  
> 80 per la novella età; chè pur nove anni  
> 81 son queste rote intorno di lui torte.  

Not yet have folk taken due note of him, because of his young age, for only nine years have these wheels rolled round him.

- *Non se ne son le genti ancora accorte* ‘the people have not yet become aware of it’ → “Not yet have folk taken due note of him”: the negative and temporal adverb are brought together at the front, with inversion; the article is dropped, reflexive awareness becomes taking note, “due” is supplied, and *ne* is referred to the person.
- *per la novella età* ‘because of the young age’ → “because of his young age”: a possessive replaces the article.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: subordination becomes coordination.
- *son queste rote intorno di lui torte* ‘are these wheels around him turned’ → “have these wheels rolled round him”: the perfect takes ‘have’ rather than ‘be’ as auxiliary; “rolled” renders turning and precedes the phrase of motion.

> 82 Ma pria che il Guasco l' alto Enrico inganni,  
> 83 parran faville della sua virtute  
> 84 in non curar d' argento, nè d' affanni.  

But ere the Gascon have deceived the lofty Henry, sparkles of his virtue shall appear in carelessness of silver and of toils.

- *pria che … inganni* ‘before … deceive’ → “ere” … “have deceived”: the present subjunctive becomes a perfect subjunctive, marking the deception as accomplished at the prospective limit.
- *parran faville della sua virtute* ‘shall appear sparks of his virtue’ → “sparkles of his virtue shall appear”: the verb moves after its subject; “sparkles” renders the sparks as flashes.
- *in non curar* ‘in not caring’ → “in carelessness”: an explicitly negated infinitive becomes an abstract noun.
- *nè d' affanni* ‘nor of troubles’ → “and of toils”: the negative connective becomes additive under “carelessness”; “toils” renders the troubles as hardships.

## Lines 85–117 — A Benefactor Foretold and Counsel Sought

> 85 Le sue magnificenze conosciute  
> 86 saranno ancora, sì che i suoi nimici  
> 87 non ne potran tener le lingue mute.  

His deeds munificent shall yet be known so that concerning them his very foes shall not be able to keep silent tongues.

- *Le sue magnificenze* ‘his munificences’ → “His deeds munificent”: the plural noun is rendered by a noun for the acts and an adjective describing them.
- *conosciute saranno ancora* ‘known shall be yet’ → “shall yet be known”: the participle moves after the auxiliary, with the adverb between “shall” and “be”.
- *i suoi nimici* ‘his foes’ → “his very foes”: “very” supplies emphasis.
- *non ne potran tener le lingue mute* ‘shall not about them be able to keep the tongues mute’ → “concerning them” … “shall not be able to keep silent tongues”: the pronominal *ne* becomes a phrase placed before the subject; the article disappears, and the predicate adjective becomes an adjective before “tongues”.

> 88 A lui t' aspetta ed ai suoi benefici;  

Look to him and to his benefits;

- *A lui t' aspetta ed ai suoi benefici* ‘on him wait, and on his benefits’ → “Look to him and to his benefits”: the pronominal verb of expectation becomes “Look to”, without a reflexive pronoun, and the imperative precedes both its complements.

> 89 per lui fia trasmutata molta gente,  
> 90 cambiando condizion, ricchi e mendici;  

by him shall many folk be changed, altering state, the wealthy and the beggars;

- *fia trasmutata molta gente* ‘shall be transformed much folk’ → “shall many folk be changed”: the singular collective is construed as a countable plural with “many”; the subject moves between the auxiliaries rather than following the participle.
- *ricchi e mendici* ‘rich and beggars’ → “the wealthy and the beggars”: the substantival adjective and the noun are each given a definite article.

> 91 e porteraine scritto nella mente  
> 92 di lui, ma nol dirai”: e disse cose  
> 93 incredibili a quei che fien presente.  

and thou shalt bear it written in thy mind of him, but shalt not tell it”;—and he told me things past the belief even of who shall see them.

- *porteraine scritto nella mente* ‘thou shalt carry thereof written in the mind’ → “thou shalt bear it written in thy mind”: the enclitic *ne* is represented by the direct object “it”, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *nol dirai* ‘not it shalt thou tell’ → “shalt not tell it”: the negative follows the auxiliary and the object follows the verb instead of preceding it.
- *disse cose* ‘he said things’ → “he told me things”: the verb of saying becomes one of telling, with the recipient made explicit.
- *incredibili a quei che fien presente* ‘incredible to those who shall be present’ → “past the belief even of who shall see them”: the adjective becomes a phrase with the noun “belief”; “even” adds emphasis, and being present is interpreted as seeing the things, with “them” supplied.

> 94 Poi giunse: “Figlio, queste son le chiose  
> 95 di quel che ti fu detto; ecco le insidie  

Then he added: “Son, these are the notes on what hath been said to thee;

- *le chiose di quel che ti fu detto* ‘the glosses of that which to thee was said’ → “the notes on what hath been said to thee”: “notes on” replaces the noun with its *di* complement, demonstrative and relative are fused in “what”, and the simple past passive becomes a present perfect passive with the recipient after it.

> 96 che retro a pochi giri son nascose.  

behold the snares that behind but few circlings are hidden.

- *retro a pochi giri* ‘behind a few turnings’ → “behind but few circlings”: “behind” takes its complement directly, and “but” makes the smallness of the number emphatic.

> 97 Non vo' però ch' a' tuoi vicini invidie,  

Yet would I not have thee envious of thy neighbours,

- *Non vo' però ch' a' tuoi vicini invidie* ‘I do not, however, wish that thou envy thy neighbours’ → “Yet would I not have thee envious of thy neighbours”: the adversative moves to the front; the present verb of wishing and its subjunctive clause become “would … have” with an object and a predicate adjective. The neighbours, placed before the Italian verb with *a*, follow the English adjective with “of”.

> 98 poscia che s' infutura la tua vita  
> 99 vie più là che il punir di lor perfidie.”  

since thy life shall be prolonged far beyond falling of the penalty upon their perfidies.”

- *s' infutura la tua vita* ‘extends itself into the future thy life’ → “thy life shall be prolonged”: the subject moves before the verb, and a pronominal present becomes a future passive; futurity is conveyed by the auxiliary rather than by the stem of the verb.
- *vie più là che* ‘much farther than’ → “far beyond”: the comparison with *che* becomes a preposition of extent intensified by “far”.
- *il punir di lor perfidie* ‘the punishing of their perfidies’ → “falling of the penalty upon their perfidies”: the substantival infinitive becomes the falling of a penalty, and what is punished becomes the object of “upon”.

> 100 Poi che tacendo si mostrò spedita  
> 101 l' anima santa di metter la trama  
> 102 in quella tela ch' io le porsi ordita,  

When by his silence the sacred soul showed he had finished setting of the woof across the warp I had held out in readiness to him,

- *Poi che tacendo* ‘after, by being silent’ → “When by his silence”: the temporal conjunction is rendered by “When”, and the gerund becomes a prepositional phrase with a possessive noun.
- *si mostrò spedita l' anima santa di metter* ‘showed itself quit, the soul holy, of setting’ → “the sacred soul showed he had finished setting”: the postponed subject moves before the verb and its adjective before the noun; the reflexive and predicate adjective become a clause with a personal subject and a past perfect verb of completion.
- *la trama in quella tela* ‘the weft in that web’ → “of the woof across the warp”: “of” marks the object of the English verbal noun; “across” specifies direction rather than position, and the demonstrative and the more general *tela* become “the warp”.
- *ch' io le porsi ordita* ‘which I held out to it warped’ → “I had held out in readiness to him”: the object relative is unexpressed, the simple past becomes past perfect, and the participle describing the web as already warped becomes a phrase of readiness. The indirect object moves to the end and refers to the speaker as “him” rather than agreeing with the feminine *anima*.

> 103 io cominciai, come colui che brama,  
> 104 dubitando, consiglio da persona  
> 105 che vede e vuol dirittamente, ed ama:  

I began, as he who longeth in doubt for counsel from one who seeth and willeth straight, and loveth:

- *dubitando* ‘doubting’ → “in doubt”: the gerund becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *consiglio da persona* ‘counsel from a person’ → “for counsel from one”: “longeth” requires “for” where the Italian takes a direct object, and the noun becomes the pronoun “one”.

> 106 “Ben veggio, padre mio, sì come sprona  
> 107 lo tempo verso me, per colpo darmi  
> 108 tal ch' è più grave a chi più s' abbandona;  

“Well do I see, my father, how time cometh spurring toward me to give me such a buffet as is heaviest to whoso most abandoneth himself;

- *Ben veggio* ‘well I see’ → “Well do I see”: the fronted adverb is retained with an auxiliary and inversion.
- *padre mio* ‘father mine’ → “my father”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *sì come sprona lo tempo verso me* ‘just how spurs the time toward me’ → “how time cometh spurring toward me”: *sì* has no separate equivalent, the subject loses its article and precedes the verb, and the movement implicit in *sprona* is expressed by “cometh” with a participle.
- *per colpo darmi tal* ‘in order a blow to give me such’ → “to give me such a buffet”: the purpose infinitive is expressed by “to”; its object follows the verb and recipient, with “such” moved before the noun and an indefinite article inserted.
- *ch' è più grave a chi più s' abbandona* ‘that is heavier to whoever more abandons himself’ → “as is heaviest to whoso most abandoneth himself”: “as” answers the preceding “such”, both comparatives become superlatives, and the reflexive follows its verb.

> 109 per che di provedenza è buon ch' io m' armi  
> 110 sì che, se loco m' è tolto più caro,  
> 111 io non perdessi gli altri per miei carmi.  

wherefore with foresight it were well to arm me, that if the dearest place be reft from me, I lose not all the rest by reason of my songs.

- *è buon ch' io m' armi* ‘it is good that I arm myself’ → “it were well to arm me”: the indicative becomes hypothetical “were”, and the finite subjunctive clause becomes an infinitive with “me” in place of the reflexive.
- *sì che* ‘so that’ → “that”: the purpose clause has no separate ‘so’.
- *se loco m' è tolto più caro* ‘if the place to me is taken away most dear’ → “if the dearest place be reft from me”: the postposed *più caro* becomes a prenominal superlative with an article; the indicative passive becomes subjunctive, and the dative moves after it as “from me”.
- *io non perdessi gli altri* ‘I might not lose the others’ → “I lose not all the rest”: the imperfect subjunctive becomes an uninflected present form with the negative after it; the other places are collectively “the rest”, with “all” supplied.
- *per miei carmi* ‘through my poems’ → “by reason of my songs”: the preposition is expanded into an explicitly causal phrase, and *carmi* is rendered as “songs”.

> 112 Giù per lo mondo senza fine amaro,  
> 113 e per lo monte del cui bel cacume  
> 114 gli occhi della mia donna mi levaro,  

Down in the world endlessly bitter, and along the mount from whose fair summit my Lady’s eyes uplifted me,

- *Giù per lo mondo senza fine amaro* ‘down through the world without end bitter’ → “Down in the world endlessly bitter”: a preposition of passage becomes one of location, and the phrase modifying the adjective becomes an adverb.
- *del cui bel cacume* ‘from the fair summit of which’ → “from whose fair summit”: the possessive relative incorporates the relation expressed by ‘of which’ and serves as the determiner of “summit”.
- *gli occhi della mia donna mi levaro* ‘the eyes of my lady me lifted’ → “my Lady’s eyes uplifted me”: the possessive phrase precedes “eyes” in place of an *of* complement, and the object pronoun follows the verb.

> 115 e poscia per lo ciel di lume in lume  
> 116 ho io appreso quel che, s' io il ridico,  
> 117 a molti fia sapor di forte agrume;  

and after, through the heaven from light to light, I have learnt that which if I tell again, will have strong-bitter flavour unto many;

- *di lume in lume* ‘from light into light’ → “from light to light”: the repeated-noun construction uses “to” rather than a preposition of entry.
- *ho io appreso* ‘have I learnt’ → “I have learnt”: the subject precedes the auxiliary instead of following it.
- *s' io il ridico* ‘if I it retell’ → “if I tell again”: the prefix becomes the separate adverb “again”, and the object pronoun is not separately rendered.
- *a molti fia sapor di forte agrume* ‘to many it will be a taste of sharp sourness’ → “will have strong-bitter flavour unto many”: the dative moves from first to last, being a taste becomes having a flavour, and the *di* phrase naming the pungent taste becomes a compound adjective before the noun.

## Lines 118–142 — The Charge to Speak the Truth

> 118 e s' io al vero son timido amico,  
> 119 temo di perder vita tra coloro  
> 120 che questo tempo chiameranno antico.”  

and if to truth I am a shrinking friend, I fear to lose life amongst those who shall call this time ancient.”

- *io al vero son timido amico* ‘I to the true am a timid friend’ → “to truth I am a shrinking friend”: the subject moves next to its verb; the substantivized adjective becomes an abstract noun, “a” is supplied before the predicate noun, and the participle “shrinking” renders the adjective.
- *che questo tempo chiameranno antico* ‘who this time will call ancient’ → “who shall call this time ancient”: the object moves after the verb.

> 121 La luce in che rideva il mio tesoro,  
> 122 ch' io trovai lì, si fe' prima corrusca,  
> 123 quale a raggio di sole specchio d' oro;  

The light wherein was smiling my treasure which I there had found, first coruscated as at the sun’s rays doth a golden mirror;

- *rideva* ‘smiled’ → “was smiling”: the progressive brings out the duration of the imperfect.
- *ch' io trovai lì* ‘which I found there’ → “which I there had found”: the simple past becomes a pluperfect, and the adverb moves before the verb.
- *si fe' prima corrusca* ‘made itself first flashing’ → “first coruscated”: a single intransitive verb replaces the reflexive verb and predicate adjective; the adverb now precedes the verb.
- *quale a raggio di sole* ‘such as at a ray of sun’ → “as at the sun’s rays doth”: the comparison is introduced by “as”; the singular ray becomes plural, the sun becomes a preceding possessive with a definite article, and “doth” supplies a verb understood in Italian.
- *specchio d' oro* ‘a mirror of gold’ → “a golden mirror”: the phrase of material becomes an adjective before the noun, and the indefinite article is supplied.

> 124 indi rispose: “Coscienza fusca  
> 125 o della propria o dell' altrui vergogna  
> 126 pur sentirà la tua parola brusca.  

then answered: “Conscience darkened, or by its own or by another’s shame, will in truth feel thy utterance grating.

- *fusca* ‘dark’ → “darkened”: a participle replaces the adjective.
- *o della propria o dell' altrui vergogna* ‘either by its own or by another’s shame’ → “or by its own or by another’s shame”: the first member of the alternative is introduced by “or” rather than ‘either’.
- *pur sentirà* ‘will indeed feel’ → “will in truth feel”: a prepositional phrase renders the emphatic particle and stands between the auxiliary and the main verb.
- *la tua parola brusca* ‘thy harsh word’ → “thy utterance grating”: “utterance” renders the singular ‘word’, and “grating” stands as a complement after the object of “feel” rather than as an attributive adjective.

> 127 Ma nondimen, rimossa ogni menzogna,  
> 128 tutta tua vision fa manifesta,  
> 129 e lascia pur grattar, dov' è la rogna;  

But none the less, every lie set aside, make thy entire vision manifest, and let them scratch wherever is the scab;

- *rimossa ogni menzogna* ‘removed every lie’ → “every lie set aside”: the absolute construction is retained, but its participle follows the noun and is rendered by a phrasal verb.
- *tutta tua vision fa manifesta* ‘all thy vision make manifest’ → “make thy entire vision manifest”: the imperative moves before its object, and ‘all’ becomes the adjective “entire” after the possessive.
- *lascia pur grattar* ‘just let scratch’ → “let them scratch”: “them” supplies an agent for the infinitive; the permissive emphasis of *pur* has no separate rendering.
- *dov' è la rogna* ‘where is the mange’ → “wherever is the scab”: “wherever” generalizes the relative adverb, and the name of the skin complaint becomes “the scab”.

> 130 chè, se la voce tua sarà molesta  
> 131 nel primo gusto, vital nutrimento  
> 132 lascerà poi quando sarà digesta.  

for if thy voice be grievous at first taste, yet vital nutriment shall it leave thereafter when digested.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a coordinating conjunction replaces the subordinating one.
- *sarà molesta* ‘will be troublesome’ → “be grievous”: the future indicative becomes a present subjunctive after “if”; “grievous” renders the adjective.
- *nel primo gusto* ‘in the first taste’ → “at first taste”: the preposition changes and the article disappears.
- *vital nutrimento lascerà poi* ‘vital nutriment it will leave afterwards’ → “yet vital nutriment shall it leave thereafter”: “yet” makes the contrast explicit; the object remains first, but the auxiliary precedes the expressed subject.
- *quando sarà digesta* ‘when it will be digested’ → “when digested”: the future passive is reduced to its participle.

> 133 Questo tuo grido farà come il vento,  
> 134 che le più alte cime più percote;  
> 135 e ciò non fia d' onor poco argomento.  

This cry of thine shall do as doth the wind, which smiteth most upon the loftiest summits; and this shall be no little argument of honour.

- *Questo tuo grido* ‘this thy cry’ → “This cry of thine”: the possessive moves into an “of” phrase after the noun.
- *come il vento* ‘like the wind’ → “as doth the wind”: the comparison becomes a clause with a supplied verb.
- *le più alte cime più percote* ‘the highest summits it strikes most’ → “smiteth most upon the loftiest summits”: the verb moves before the summits, now introduced by “upon” rather than taken as a direct object; the adverb follows the verb, and “loftiest” renders the superlative.
- *non fia d' onor poco argomento* ‘will not be of honour a small argument’ → “shall be no little argument of honour”: the negation moves from the verb to the noun phrase, and “of honour” follows the noun it qualifies.

> 136 Però ti son mostrate in queste rote,  
> 137 nel monte e nella valle dolorosa  
> 138 pur l' anime che son di fama note;  

Therefore have been displayed to thee, in these wheels, upon the mount, and in the dolorous vale, only souls known to fame;

- *ti son mostrate* ‘to thee are shown’ → “have been displayed to thee”: the present passive becomes a perfect passive, “displayed” renders the participle, and the recipient moves after the verb.
- *nel monte* ‘in the mount’ → “upon the mount”: the preposition changes.
- *nella valle dolorosa* ‘in the vale dolorous’ → “in the dolorous vale”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *pur l' anime* ‘only the souls’ → “only souls”: the definite article is omitted.
- *che son di fama note* ‘that are by fame known’ → “known to fame”: the relative clause is reduced to a participial phrase; “to fame” follows the participle in place of the preceding phrase with *di*.

> 139 chè l' animo di quel ch' ode non posa,  
> 140 nè ferma fede per esempio ch' haia  
> 141 la sua radice incognita e nascosa,  
> 142 nè per altro argomento che non paia.”  

for the soul of him who heareth resteth not nor fixeth faith by an example which hath its root unknown and hidden, nor other unconspicuous argument.”

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *l' animo* ‘the mind’ → “the soul”: “soul” renders the word for the hearer’s inward or mental faculty.
- *non posa* ‘not rests’ → “resteth not”: the negative follows the verb.
- *per esempio* ‘by example’ → “by an example”: an indefinite article is supplied.
- *ch' haia* ‘that may have’ → “which hath”: the relative clause takes the indicative in English rather than the Italian subjunctive.
- *nè per altro argomento che non paia* ‘nor by another argument that does not appear’ → “nor other unconspicuous argument”: “by” is understood from the preceding phrase rather than repeated, “other” stands without an article, and the negative relative clause with a subjunctive verb becomes a single adjective before the noun.

## Summary — Italian Sequence and English Idiom

Wicksteed often changes the order of words without changing their relationships. The delayed subject *parole gravi* becomes the opening “heavy words”: the subject moves ahead of the verb and the adjective ahead of the noun. Object pronouns likewise tend to follow the English verb rather than precede it. Two routine adjustments generally go unremarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where Italian gives the subject by the verb ending alone, and drops the article before a possessive, as when *la voce tua* becomes “thy voice”.

A change of construction can shift the emphasis. In *a dir la sete*, the article gives way to a possessive in “to tell thy thirst”; in *t’ausi*, ‘accustom thyself’ becomes “thou mayst learn”, losing the reflexive form as well as the suggestion of habit. Elsewhere a grammatical change leaves the sense close: *dubitando*, ‘doubting’, becomes “in doubt”. Wicksteed sometimes leaves a reinforcement such as *sì* without a separate equivalent, but also supplies what the Italian leaves implicit: “angles” gives *due ottusi* its understood noun. Other additions are more interpretive. “By machination” would specify more than a simple ‘because of’; Wicksteed’s phrase “by machination of his cruel and perfidious stepmother” does just that. Similarly, “disaster” makes *fortuna* expressly adverse, and “the heat” gives the effect of *la vampa* rather than the flame itself.

Yet Wicksteed does not consistently smooth Dante’s phrasing into ordinary English order. In “answered that love paternal” he keeps both the subject after the verb and the adjective after its noun, just as in *rispose quell’ amor paterno*. He also preserves the repeated movement of *di lume in lume* in “from light to light” and the pairing of *vital nutrimento* in “vital nutriment”. Such closeness is often local: a phrase can retain Dante’s order, repetition, or choice of image even where the sentence around it has been recast.
