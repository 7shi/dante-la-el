# Paradiso 18

## Lines 1–18 — Love in Beatrice’s Eyes

> 1 Già si godeva solo del suo verbo  
> 2 quello specchio beato, ed io gustava  
> 3 lo mio, temprando col dolce l' acerbo;  

Already was that blessed mirror rejoicing only in his own discourse, and I was tasting mine, tempering with the sweet the bitter;

- *Già si godeva solo del suo verbo quello specchio beato* ‘already rejoiced only in his word that mirror blessed’ → “Already was that blessed mirror rejoicing only in his own discourse”: the pronominal imperfect becomes an English progressive, with the postponed subject placed between the auxiliary and the participle. The adjective precedes the noun; ‘word’ becomes “discourse,” and “own” makes the possessive emphatic.
- *gustava* ‘tasted’ → “was tasting”: the progressive brings out the continuing action of the imperfect.

> 4 e quella donna, ch' a Dio mi menava,  
> 5 disse: “Muta pensier, pensa ch' io sono  
> 6 presso a colui ch' ogni torto disgrava.”  

and that Lady, who was leading me to God, said: “Change thy thought; think that I am nigh to him who every wrong unloadeth.”

- *ch' a Dio mi menava* ‘who to God me led’ → “who was leading me to God”: the imperfect becomes a progressive; the object pronoun follows the verb, and the phrase of direction comes last.
- *Muta pensier* ‘change thought’ → “Change thy thought”: the possessive is supplied.

> 7 Io mi rivolsi all' amoroso suono  
> 8 del mio conforto, e quale io allor vidi  
> 9 negli occhi santi amor, qui l' abbandono;  

I turned me to the lovesome sound of my comfort, and what love I then beheld within the sacred eyes, I here attempt not;

- *Io mi rivolsi* ‘I myself turned’ → “I turned me”: the reflexive pronoun follows the verb.
- *quale io allor vidi negli occhi santi amor* ‘what manner of love I then saw in the eyes holy’ → “what love I then beheld within the sacred eyes”: “what love” brings together the initial qualifier and the delayed noun. ‘Saw’ becomes “beheld”, ‘in’ becomes “within”, and the adjective precedes ‘eyes.’
- *qui l' abbandono* ‘here I leave it’ → “I here attempt not”: a negated verb of attempting replaces the affirmative verb of leaving; the preceding object is not taken up again by a pronoun.

> 10 non perch' io pur del mio parlar diffidi,  
> 11 ma per la mente che non può reddire  
> 12 sopra sè tanto, s' altri non la guidi.  

not because merely I distrust my speech, but for my memory which may not re-ascend so far above itself unless another guide it.

- *io pur del mio parlar diffidi* ‘I merely of my speaking am distrustful’ → “merely I distrust my speech”: “merely” precedes the subject; the verb governing a prepositional complement becomes a transitive verb with its object following. The substantivized infinitive becomes “speech”, and the Italian subjunctive has no distinct English marking here.
- *la mente* ‘the mind’ → “my memory”: the article becomes a possessive, and the broader noun is rendered as the particular faculty of recollection.
- *non può reddire* ‘cannot return’ → “may not re-ascend”: inability is expressed by “may not”, and the verb of return is given an explicitly upward direction.
- *sopra sè tanto* ‘above itself so far’ → “so far above itself”: the expression of degree comes first.
- *s' altri non la guidi* ‘if another does not guide it’ → “unless another guide it”: “unless” absorbs the negation, and the object pronoun follows the verb.

> 13 Tanto poss' io di quel punto ridire  
> 14 che, rimirando lei, lo mio affetto  
> 15 libero fu da ogni altro disire,  

So much anent this point may I retell, that as I gazed upon her my affection was freed from every other longing

- *Tanto poss' io di quel punto ridire* ‘so much can I of that moment tell again’ → “So much anent this point may I retell”: the prepositional phrase moves before the modal and subject. The modal “may” renders ability, “anent” renders ‘of’ in the sense ‘concerning,’ and “this point” changes the demonstrative and leaves the temporal sense of ‘moment’ less explicit.
- *rimirando lei* ‘gazing upon her’ → “as I gazed upon her”: the gerund becomes a finite temporal clause.
- *libero fu* ‘free was’ → “was freed”: the preceding adjective of state becomes a following passive participle.

> 16 fin che il piacere eterno, che diretto  
> 17 raggiava in Beatrice, dal bel viso  
> 18 mi contentava col secondo aspetto.  

whilst the eternal joy which rayed direct on Beatrice was satisfying me with its derived aspect from the fair face.

- *fin che* ‘as long as’ → “whilst”: the rendering expresses simultaneity rather than explicitly marking duration.
- *il piacere eterno* ‘the pleasure eternal’ → “the eternal joy”: “joy” replaces ‘pleasure,’ and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *diretto raggiava in Beatrice* ‘direct shone in Beatrice’ → “rayed direct on Beatrice”: “rayed” makes the image of rays explicit; “direct” follows the verb as an adverb rather than preceding it as an adjective agreeing with the subject. The preposition “on” replaces ‘in.’
- *dal bel viso mi contentava col secondo aspetto* ‘from the fair face me satisfied with the second aspect’ → “was satisfying me with its derived aspect from the fair face”: “from the fair face” moves from before the main verb to the end of the clause. The imperfect becomes a progressive, and the object pronoun follows the verb. The article becomes a possessive, and “derived” interprets ‘second’ as indicating a relation of dependence rather than simply giving an ordinal.

## Lines 19–51 — The Spirits Named Upon the Cross

> 19 Vincendo me col lume d' un sorriso,  
> 20 ella mi disse: “Volgiti ed ascolta,  
> 21 chè non pur nei miei occhi è Paradiso.”  

O’ercoming me with the light of a smile, she said to me: “Turn thee, and hearken, for not only in my eyes is Paradise.”

- *ella mi disse* ‘she to me said’ → “she said to me”: the indirect object follows the verb.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.

> 22 Come si vede qui alcuna volta  
> 23 l' affetto nella vista, s' ello è tanto  
> 24 che da lui sia tutta l' anima tolta,  

As here sometimes we read the affection in the countenance, if it be so great that all the mind is taken up by it,

- *si vede qui alcuna volta* ‘one sees here sometimes’ → “here sometimes we read”: the impersonal construction becomes a first-person plural; the adverbials move before the subject, and seeing becomes reading.
- *nella vista* ‘in the outward appearance’ → “in the countenance”: the translation specifies the face.
- *da lui sia tutta l’ anima tolta* ‘by it be all the soul taken’ → “all the mind is taken up by it”: the subject precedes the verb, the agent comes last, and the subjunctive gives way to the indicative. The soul becomes the mind, and the added adverb makes the sense one of absorption.

> 25 così nel fiammeggiar del fulgor santo,  
> 26 a ch' io mi volsi, conobbi la voglia  
> 27 in lui di ragionarmi ancora alquanto.  

so in the flaming of the sacred glow to which I turned me, I recognised the will in him yet further somewhat to discourse with me.

- *fulgor santo* ‘radiance holy’ → “sacred glow”: the adjective precedes the noun, and both words are rendered by near equivalents.
- *io mi volsi* ‘I myself turned’ → “I turned me”: the reflexive pronoun follows the verb.
- *di ragionarmi* ‘of discoursing to me’ → “to discourse with me”: the complement of the noun becomes an English infinitive, and the attached dative becomes a phrase with “with”.
- *ancora alquanto* ‘further a little’ → “yet further somewhat”: the adverbials move from after the infinitive to before it, and the first is expanded to two words.

> 28 Ei cominciò: “In questa quinta soglia  
> 29 dell' arbore, che vive della cima  
> 30 e frutta sempre e mai non perde foglia,  

He began: “In this fifth range of the tree which liveth from the summit, and ever beareth fruit, and never sheddeth leaf,

- *soglia* ‘threshold’ → “range”: the translation uses a word for a level rather than a threshold.
- *della cima* ‘from the top’ → “from the summit”: a more elevated noun is chosen.
- *frutta sempre* ‘fruits always’ → “ever beareth fruit”: a single verb becomes verb and object, and the adverb goes before them.
- *mai non perde foglia* ‘never loses leaf’ → “never sheddeth leaf”: English has one negative for the Italian negative pair, and shedding is more specific than losing.

> 31 spiriti son beati, che giù, prima  
> 32 che venissero al ciel, fur di gran voce,  
> 33 sì ch' ogni Musa ne sarebbe opima.  

are spirits blessed, who below, ere they came unto heaven, were of a great name, so that every Muse would be enriched by them.

- *spiriti son beati* ‘spirits are blessed’ → “are spirits blessed”: the verb moves before the noun, completing the locative construction begun in line 28.
- *prima che venissero* ‘before they should come’ → “ere they came”: the conjunction is compressed into one word, and the imperfect subjunctive becomes a simple past.
- *di gran voce* ‘of great voice’ → “of a great name”: the translation takes the phrase in the sense of renown, substitutes name for voice, and supplies an article.
- *ne sarebbe opima* ‘from them would be rich’ → “would be enriched by them”: the adjective becomes a passive participle, and the source of the riches is expressed after it as the agent.

> 34 Però mira nei corni della croce:  
> 35 quello ch'io nomerò, lì farà l' atto  
> 36 che fa in nube il suo foco veloce.”  

Wherefore gaze upon the horns of the cross; he whom I shall name shall there do the act which in a cloud its swift flame doth.”

- *mira nei corni* ‘look into the horns’ → “gaze upon the horns”: a more sustained verb of looking is used, with a different preposition.
- *quello* ‘that one’ → “he”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *lì farà* ‘there will do’ → “shall there do”: the adverb moves between the auxiliary and the main verb.
- *fa in nube il suo foco veloce* ‘does in cloud its fire swift’ → “in a cloud its swift flame doth”: the verb moves to the end, an indefinite article is supplied, and the adjective precedes the noun; fire becomes flame.

> 37 Io vidi per la croce un lume tratto  
> 38 dal nomar Josuè, com' ei si feo,  
> 39 nè mi fu noto il dir prima che il fatto.  

I saw a light drawn along the cross at the naming of Joshua, as it was done; nor was the word known to me ere the fact.

- *per la croce un lume tratto* ‘along the cross a light drawn’ → “a light drawn along the cross”: the phrase of motion follows the object and participle.
- *dal nomar Josuè* ‘by the naming of Joshua’ → “at the naming of Joshua”: the translation marks the occasion rather than explicitly making the naming the cause.
- *com’ ei si feo* ‘as it came to pass’ → “as it was done”: the pronominal form is rendered as a passive.
- *mi fu noto il dir* ‘to me was known the saying’ → “was the word known to me”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun; the subject moves between the copula and its complement, and the dative goes last.
- *prima che il fatto* ‘before the deed’ → “ere the fact”: the translation uses a noun for what occurred rather than for the doing of it.

> 40 Ed al nome dell' alto Maccabeo  
> 41 vidi moversi un altro roteando,  
> 42 e letizia era ferza del paleo.  

And at the name of the lofty Maccabee I saw another move, wheeling, and gladness was the lash unto the top.

- *moversi un altro* ‘move itself another’ → “another move”: the subject of the infinitive precedes it, and the English verb is intransitive rather than reflexive.
- *ferza del paleo* ‘lash of the top’ → “the lash unto the top”: the translation supplies an article before the predicate noun and changes the relation from ‘of’ to one of application.

> 43 Così per Carlo Magno e per Orlando  
> 44 due ne seguì lo mio attento sguardo,  
> 45 com' occhio segue suo falcon volando.  

Thus for Charlemagne and for Orlando two more were followed by my keen regard, as the eye followeth its falcon flying.

- *due ne seguì lo mio attento sguardo* ‘two of them followed my attentive gaze’ → “two more were followed by my keen regard”: in the Italian, the two are the object and the gaze is the subject. The passive keeps the two first but makes them the subject; the partitive becomes additive, and attentive looking becomes keen regard.
- *occhio segue suo falcon volando* ‘eye follows its falcon flying’ → “the eye followeth its falcon flying”: English supplies a definite article for the generic singular.

> 46 Poscia trasse Guglielmo, e Rinoardo,  
> 47 e il duca Gottifredi la mia vista  
> 48 per quella croce, e Roberto Guiscardo.  

Then drew my sight along that cross William and Rinoardo and the duke Godfrey, and Robert Guiscard.

- *trasse Guglielmo, e Rinoardo, e il duca Gottifredi la mia vista per quella croce, e Roberto Guiscardo* ‘drew William, and Rinoardo, and the duke Godfrey my sight along that cross, and Robert Guiscard’ → “drew my sight along that cross William and Rinoardo and the duke Godfrey, and Robert Guiscard”: the object and the phrase of motion move directly after the verb, leaving the names together as its delayed subject. The Italian verb is singular before the enumeration; the English past form does not mark number.

> 49 Indi, tra l' altre luci mota e mista,  
> 50 mostrommi l' alma che m' avea parlato,  
> 51 qual era tra i cantor del cielo artista.  

Thereon amongst the other lights, moving and mingling, the soul which had discoursed to me showed me his artist quality among heaven’s singers.

- *Indi* ‘then’ → “Thereon”: the simple adverb of succession becomes a connective referring back to the preceding action.
- *mota e mista* ‘moved and mixed’ → “moving and mingling”: two past participles agreeing with the soul become active present participles.
- *mostrommi l’ alma che m’ avea parlato* ‘showed me the soul that to me had spoken’ → “the soul which had discoursed to me showed me”: the subject and its relative clause move before the main verb. Within the relative clause, speaking becomes discoursing, and the indirect object follows the verb.
- *qual era tra i cantor del cielo artista* ‘what an artist he was among the singers of heaven’ → “his artist quality among heaven’s singers”: the embedded clause becomes a noun phrase with a supplied possessive; a noun for quality is introduced, with artist as its modifier. The phrase identifying the company comes last, and the Italian ‘of’ construction becomes an English possessive.

## Lines 52–84 — Beatrice’s Brightening and the Letters of Light

> 52 Io mi rivolsi dal mio destro lato  
> 53 per vedere in Beatrice il mio dovere,  
> 54 o per parole o per atto segnato;  

I turned to my right side to see in Beatrice my duty, whether by speech or gesture indicated,

- *Io mi rivolsi* ‘I turned myself’ → “I turned”: the English verb is intransitive rather than reflexive.
- *dal mio destro lato* ‘on my right side’ → “to my right side”: the English marks the direction of the turn.
- *o per parole o per atto segnato* ‘either by words or by an act marked’ → “whether by speech or gesture indicated”: the alternatives are introduced by “whether”; plural ‘words’ becomes the uncountable “speech”; the second ‘by’ is not repeated; ‘act’ is narrowed to “gesture”, and ‘marked’ becomes “indicated”.

> 55 e vidi le sue luci tanto mere,  
> 56 tanto gioconde, che la sua sembianza  
> 57 vinceva gli altri e l' ultimo solere.  

and I saw her eyes so clear, so joyous, that her semblance surpassed all former usage and the last.

- *le sue luci* ‘her lights’ → “her eyes”: the translation names what the metaphor denotes.
- *mere* ‘pure’ → “clear”: the adjective is rendered in terms of clarity.
- *vinceva* ‘was vanquishing’ → “surpassed”: the comparison is expressed without the figure of conquest, and the imperfect is rendered by a simple past.
- *gli altri e l' ultimo solere* ‘the other and the last wont’ → “all former usage and the last”: “all former” interprets the plural ‘the others’; the shared noun ‘wont’, an infinitive used as a noun, becomes “usage” and is placed with the first rather than the last term.

> 58 E come, per sentir più dilettanza  
> 59 bene operando, l' uom di giorno in giorno  
> 60 s' accorge che la sua virtute avanza;  

And as by feeling more delight in doing well, man from day to day perceiveth that his virtue gaineth ground;

- *per sentir* ‘through feeling’ → “by feeling”: the Italian preposition with an infinitive becomes “by” with a gerund.
- *bene operando* ‘well doing’ → “in doing well”: “in” is supplied to connect the activity with the delight, and the adverb follows the verb.
- *l' uom* ‘the man’ → “man”: the generic definite article disappears.
- *s' accorge* ‘becomes aware’ → “perceiveth”: the pronominal verb of awareness becomes a simple verb of perception.
- *avanza* ‘advances’ → “gaineth ground”: a verb becomes a verb-and-noun idiom.

> 61 sì m' accors' io che il mio girare intorno  
> 62 col cielo insieme avea cresciuto l' arco,  
> 63 veggendo quel miracol più adorno.  

so did I perceive that my circling round together with the heaven had increased its arc, seeing this miracle yet more adorned.

- *m' accors' io* ‘became aware I’ → “did I perceive”: the pronominal verb again becomes a verb of perception; the subject now stands between the added past auxiliary and the main verb.
- *col cielo insieme* ‘with the heaven together’ → “together with the heaven”: the adverb moves before the prepositional phrase.
- *l' arco* ‘the arc’ → “its arc”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *quel miracol più adorno* ‘that miracle more adorned’ → “this miracle yet more adorned”: the demonstrative changes from distal to proximal, and “yet” reinforces the comparison.

> 64 E quale è il trasmutare in picciol varco  
> 65 di tempo in bianca donna, quando il volto  
> 66 suo si discarca di vergogna il carco;  

And such change as cometh in short passage of time over a fair dame, when her countenance unburdeneth shame’s burden,

- *E quale è il trasmutare* ‘and of what kind is the changing’ → “And such change as cometh”: the substantivized infinitive becomes the noun “change”; the qualitative comparison is recast with “such … as”, and the copula becomes a verb of occurrence.
- *in picciol varco di tempo* ‘in small passage of time’ → “in short passage of time”: ‘small’ is rendered by the specifically temporal “short”.
- *in bianca donna* ‘in a white woman’ → “over a fair dame”: “over” goes with “cometh”; ‘white’ becomes “fair”, and ‘woman’ is rendered by “dame”.
- *il volto suo* ‘the countenance of hers’ → “her countenance”: the postposed possessive moves before the noun.
- *si discarca di vergogna il carco* ‘unloads from itself of shame the load’ → “unburdeneth shame’s burden”: the reflexive element disappears; the echoed verb and noun are rendered with ‘burden’ rather than ‘load’, and the prepositional genitive becomes a possessive.

> 67 tal fu negli occhi miei, quando fui volto,  
> 68 per lo candor della temprata stella  
> 69 sesta, che dentro a sè m' avea ricolto.  

was presented to my eyes, when I turned me, because of the white glow of the temperate sixth star which had received me into it.

- *tal fu negli occhi miei* ‘such was it in the eyes of mine’ → “was presented to my eyes”: the answering ‘such’ has been absorbed into the opening of the comparison; the copula becomes a passive verb of presentation, ‘in’ becomes “to”, and the possessive moves before the noun.
- *quando fui volto* ‘when I was turned’ → “when I turned me”: a passive form becomes an active reflexive.
- *per lo candor* ‘through the whiteness’ → “because of the white glow”: the preposition is made explicitly causal, and the single noun of brightness becomes an adjective and noun.
- *temprata stella sesta* ‘temperate star sixth’ → “temperate sixth star”: the ordinal moves before the noun.
- *che dentro a sè m' avea ricolto* ‘which within itself me had received’ → “which had received me into it”: the object pronoun and the phrase of place move after the verb; “into” marks direction, and the reflexive becomes “it”.

> 70 Io vidi in quella giovial facella  
> 71 lo sfavillar dell' amor che lì era,  
> 72 segnare agli occhi miei nostra favella.  

I saw in that torch of Jove the sparkling of the love which was therein signalling to my eyes our speech.

- *giovial facella* ‘Jovian little torch’ → “torch of Jove”: the adjective becomes a following prepositional phrase, and the diminutive is not expressed.
- *che lì era* ‘which there was’ → “which was therein”: the adverb follows the verb, and “therein” makes inclusion explicit.
- *segnare* ‘to mark out’ → “signalling”: the infinitive dependent on the verb of seeing becomes a present participle, and the English verb emphasizes communication by signs.

> 73 E come augelli surti di riviera,  
> 74 quasi congratulando a lor pasture,  
> 75 fanno di sè or tonda or lunga schiera,  

And as birds, risen from the bank, as though rejoicing together o’er their pasture, make themselves now a round, now a long, flock,

- *quasi congratulando a lor pasture* ‘as if rejoicing together at their pastures’ → “as though rejoicing together o’er their pasture”: ‘at’ becomes “o’er”, and plural ‘pastures’ becomes collective singular “pasture”.
- *fanno di sè* ‘make of themselves’ → “make themselves”: the prepositional phrase becomes a direct reflexive object.
- *or tonda or lunga schiera* ‘now round now long array’ → “now a round, now a long, flock”: an article is supplied with each adjective, and the general term for a formation becomes the bird-specific “flock”.

> 76 sì dentro ai lumi sante creature  
> 77 volitando cantavano, e faciensi  
> 78 or *di*, or *i*, or *elle* in sue figure.  

so within the lights the sacred creatures flying sang, and in their shapings made themselves now D, now I, now L.

- *sante creature* ‘sacred creatures’ → “the sacred creatures”: the definite article is supplied.
- *volitando* ‘flitting about’ → “flying”: the suggestion of repeated movement in different directions is lost.
- *faciensi … in sue figure* ‘made themselves … in their figures’ → “in their shapings made themselves”: the final prepositional phrase moves before the verb, and ‘figures’ becomes “shapings”.
- *or di, or i, or elle* ‘now dee, now i, now ell’ → “now D, now I, now L”: the written-out letter names are represented by the letters themselves.

> 79 Prima cantando a sua nota moviensi;  

First singing to their note they moved,

- *a sua nota* ‘to its own note’ → “to their note”: the possessive is construed with the plural creatures as a group.
- *moviensi* ‘moved themselves’ → “they moved”: the pronominal imperfect becomes an intransitive simple past.

> 80 poi diventando l' un di questi segni,  
> 81 un poco s' arrestavano e taciensi.  

then as they made themselves one of these signs, a little space would stay and hold their peace.

- *diventando* ‘becoming’ → “as they made themselves”: the gerund becomes a finite temporal clause, and becoming is recast as an action upon themselves.
- *un poco* ‘a little’ → “a little space”: a noun of duration is supplied.
- *s' arrestavano e taciensi* ‘stopped themselves and kept silent’ → “would stay and hold their peace”: “would” brings out the repeated action of the imperfects; the first reflexive becomes intransitive “stay”, and the second verb becomes an idiom with a possessive object.

> 82 O diva Pegasea, che gl' ingegni  
> 83 fai gloriosi, e rendili longevi,  
> 84 ed essi teco le cittadi e i regni,  

O goddess Pegasæan, who givest glory unto genius, and renderest it long life, as with thy aid doth it to cities and to realms,

- *che gl' ingegni fai gloriosi* ‘who the talents makest glorious’ → “who givest glory unto genius”: the verb and predicative adjective become a verb with the noun “glory”; the plural direct object becomes the collective singular “genius” and follows “unto”.
- *e rendili longevi* ‘and renderest them long-lived’ → “and renderest it long life”: the pronoun becomes singular to agree with “genius”, and a predicative adjective becomes the noun phrase for what is bestowed.
- *ed essi teco le cittadi e i regni* ‘and they with thee the cities and the realms’ → “as with thy aid doth it to cities and to realms”: coordination becomes comparison; ‘with thee’ is interpreted as assistance and placed before the singular subject. “doth” stands for the action left implicit in Italian; the two direct objects become phrases with “to”, without the definite articles.

## Lines 85–117 — The Letters Become an Eagle

> 85 illustrami di te, sì ch' io rilevi  
> 86 le lor figure com' io l' ho concette:  

make me bright with thyself, that I may throw into relief their figures as I have them in conception;

- *illustrami* ‘illumine me’ → “make me bright”: the single verb becomes a verb with a predicate adjective.
- *di te* ‘of thee’ → “with thyself”: the preposition changes, and the pronoun takes the emphatic form.
- *sì ch’ io rilevi* ‘so that I bring out’ → “that I may throw into relief”: “that” alone introduces the purpose, “may” expresses the subjunctive, and a phrase replaces the single verb.
- *com’ io l’ ho concette* ‘as I have conceived them’ → “as I have them in conception”: the perfect becomes a construction with “have” as the main verb, the pronoun after it, and a noun in place of the participle.

> 87 paia tua possa in questi versi brevi.  

let thy might show in these brief verses.

- *paia tua possa* ‘may thy might appear’ → “let thy might show”: “let” with an infinitive replaces the subjunctive of wish; the subject of the Italian verb precedes its English equivalent, “show”.
- *versi brevi* ‘verses brief’ → “brief verses”: the adjective precedes the noun.

> 88 Mostrarsi dunque in cinque volte sette  
> 89 vocali e consonanti; ed io notai  
> 90 le parti sì come mi parver dette.  

They displayed them then in five times seven vowels and consonants, and I took note of the members, even as they appeared in utterance to me.

- *Mostrarsi dunque* ‘they showed themselves, then’ → “They displayed them then”: “displayed” renders the verb, and the archaic “them” does the work of the reflexive.
- *notai le parti* ‘noted the parts’ → “took note of the members”: the transitive verb becomes a phrase governing “of”, and “members” renders ‘parts’.
- *sì come mi parver dette* ‘even as to me they seemed spoken’ → “even as they appeared in utterance to me”: a prepositional phrase replaces the participle, and the experiencer moves to the end.

> 91 *Diligite justitiam*, primai  
> 92 fur verbo e nome di tutto il dipinto;  
> 93 *qui judicatis terram*, fur sezzai.  

*Diligite justitiam*, were the first verb and substantive of all the picturing; *qui judicatis terram* were the last.

- *primai fur verbo e nome* ‘first were verb and noun’ → “were the first verb and substantive”: “first” moves after the verb into a noun phrase with a supplied article; the grammatical term “substantive” renders ‘noun’.
- *di tutto il dipinto* ‘of the whole picture’ → “of all the picturing”: “all” precedes the article, and a verbal noun replaces the word for what is pictured.
- *fur sezzai* ‘were last’ → “were the last”: the article is supplied.

> 94 Poscia nell' *emme* del vocabol quinto  
> 95 rimasero ordinate, sì che Giove  
> 96 pareva argento lì d' oro distinto.  

Then ordered in the M of the fifth word they stayed, so that Jove seemed silver in that place, pricked out with gold;

- *nell’ emme del vocabol quinto rimasero ordinate* ‘in the M of the word fifth they remained ordered’ → “ordered in the M of the fifth word they stayed”: the participle moves from the end to the front, separating it from the finite verb, and the ordinal precedes “word”.
- *lì* ‘there’ → “in that place”: an adverb becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *d’ oro distinto* ‘with gold marked’ → “pricked out with gold”: “pricked out” gives the marking a more specific sense, and its complement follows it.

> 97 E vidi scendere altre luci dove  
> 98 era il colmo dell' *emme*, e lì quetarsi,  
> 99 cantando, credo, il ben ch' a sè le move.  

and I saw descending other lights where was the M’s peak, and there still them; singing, I take it, the good that moveth them unto himself.

- *scendere* ‘descend’ → “descending”: the infinitive after the verb of seeing becomes a participle.
- *il colmo dell’ emme* ‘the peak of the M’ → “the M’s peak”: a possessive before the noun replaces the following prepositional phrase.
- *quetarsi* ‘grow quiet’ → “still them”: the reflexive verb of becoming becomes a transitive verb with “them” used reflexively.
- *credo* ‘I believe’ → “I take it”: an idiom with an expressed object replaces the single verb.
- *ch’ a sè le move* ‘that unto itself them moves’ → “that moveth them unto himself”: both complements follow the verb, their order is reversed, and the reflexive referring to the good is rendered personally.

> 100 Poi, come nel percoter dei ciocchi arsi  
> 101 surgono innumerabili faville,  
> 102 onde gli stolti sogliono augurarsi,  

Then, as at the smiting of burnt brands there rise innumerable sparks, wherefrom the foolish ones use to draw augury,

- *ciocchi arsi* ‘brands burnt’ → “burnt brands”: the participle precedes the noun.
- *surgono* ‘rise’ → “there rise”: introductory “there” is supplied before the verb and its following subject.
- *gli stolti* ‘the foolish’ → “the foolish ones”: “ones” supplies a nominal head for the adjective.
- *augurarsi* ‘take omens for themselves’ → “draw augury”: the verb becomes a verb and noun, without an expressed reflexive.

> 103 risurger parve quindi più di mille  
> 104 luci, e salir quali assai e quai poco,  
> 105 sì come il sol, che l' accende, sortille;  

meseemed there rose thence more than thousand lights, and mounted some much, some little, even as the sun which kindleth them, ordained them;

- *risurger parve quindi* ‘to rise again it seemed thence’ → “meseemed there rose thence”: the verb of seeming comes first and gains an explicit experiencer; the infinitive becomes a finite verb with introductory “there”, and the sense ‘again’ is not expressed.
- *e salir quali assai e quai poco* ‘and to mount, some much and some little’ → “and mounted some much, some little”: the infinitive becomes a finite verb, and the conjunction between the two groups is omitted.
- *che l’ accende* ‘which them kindles’ → “which kindleth them”: the object follows the verb.
- *sortille* ‘allotted them’ → “ordained them”: allotting is rendered as ordaining.

> 106 e quietata ciascuna in suo loco,  
> 107 la testa e il collo d' un' aquila vidi  
> 108 rappresentare a quel distinto foco.  

and when each one had stilled it in its place, an eagle’s head and neck I saw presented by that pricked-out fire.

- *quietata ciascuna in suo loco* ‘each one having come to rest in its place’ → “when each one had stilled it in its place”: the absolute participial construction becomes a temporal clause in the past perfect, with the subject first and “it” used reflexively.
- *la testa e il collo d’ un’ aquila* ‘the head and the neck of an eagle’ → “an eagle’s head and neck”: the possessive moves before both nouns, replacing the separate articles.
- *rappresentare a quel distinto foco* ‘represented by that marked fire’ → “presented by that pricked-out fire”: the Italian infinitive after the verb of seeing is construed passively and rendered by a participle; “pricked-out” specifies the manner of marking and precedes the noun.

> 109 Quei che dipinge lì non ha chi il guidi,  
> 110 ma esso guida, e da lui si rammenta  
> 111 quella virtù ch' è forma per li nidi;  

He who there painteth hath not one to guide him, but he himself doth guide, and from him cometh to the mind that power which is form unto the nests;

- *lì non ha chi il guidi* ‘there has not anyone who him may guide’ → “there painteth hath not one to guide him”: “there” moves before the verb of painting; the indefinite relative with a subjunctive becomes “one” with an infinitive, and the object follows “guide”.
- *esso guida* ‘he himself guides’ → “he himself doth guide”: the simple present is expanded with “doth”.
- *si rammenta* ‘is recalled’ → “cometh to the mind”: the pronominal verb is interpreted as a coming to mind rather than rendered as a passive.
- *per li nidi* ‘for the nests’ → “unto the nests”: the preposition of relation changes.

> 112 l' altra beatitudo, che contenta  
> 113 pareva in prima d' ingigliarsi all' *emme*,  
> 114 con poco moto seguitò la imprenta.  

the other blessedness, which at first seemed content to twine the M with lilies, by a slight motion followed the imprint.

- *contenta pareva in prima* ‘content seemed at first’ → “at first seemed content”: the adverbial moves before the verb and the predicate adjective after it.
- *d’ ingigliarsi all’ emme* ‘to fashion itself into lilies at the M’ → “to twine the M with lilies”: the reflexive infinitive becomes a transitive one with the M as its object; the lilies implicit in the Italian verb become a separate noun.
- *con poco moto* ‘with little motion’ → “by a slight motion”: an instrumental “by” replaces ‘with’, and a quantity of motion becomes a single qualified motion.

> 115 O dolce stella, quali e quante gemme  
> 116 mi dimostraro che nostra giustizia  
> 117 effetto sia del ciel che tu ingemme!  

O sweet star, what quality and magnitude of gems made plain to me that our justice is the effect of the heaven thou dost engem!

- *quali e quante gemme* ‘what gems and how many’ → “what quality and magnitude of gems”: the paired adjectives become abstract nouns governing “of gems”; number is rendered as magnitude.
- *mi dimostraro* ‘to me demonstrated’ → “made plain to me”: a verb and adjective replace the single verb, and the indirect object follows them.
- *effetto sia* ‘an effect be’ → “is the effect”: the verb precedes its predicate, the subjunctive becomes indicative, and the translation supplies a definite article for the bare Italian noun.
- *che tu ingemme* ‘that thou engemmest’ → “thou dost engem”: the object relative is left unexpressed, and the simple verb is expanded with “dost”.

## Lines 118–136 — A Prayer Against Buying and Selling in the Temple

> 118 Per ch' io prego la mente, in che s' inizia  
> 119 tuo moto e tua virtute, che rimiri  
> 120 ond' esce il fummo che il tuo raggio vizia;  

Wherefore I pray the mind wherein thy motion and thy power hath beginning, to look upon the place whence issueth the smoke that vitiates thy ray;

- *s' inizia tuo moto e tua virtute* ‘has its beginning thy motion and thy power’ → “thy motion and thy power hath beginning”: the pronominal verb becomes a verb and noun, and the compound subject is moved before the verb; the verb remains singular.
- *che rimiri* ‘that it look upon’ → “to look upon”: the request expressed by a finite subjunctive clause becomes an infinitive dependent on “pray”.
- *ond' esce* ‘whence comes out’ → “the place whence issueth”: an explicit antecedent is supplied for the relative adverb, and the motion out is expressed by “issueth”.
- *il tuo raggio vizia* ‘thy ray it vitiates’ → “vitiates thy ray”: the object is moved after the verb.

> 121 sì ch' un' altra fiata omai s' adiri  
> 122 del comperare e vender dentro al templo,  
> 123 che si murò di segni e di martiri.  

so that once more the wrath be kindled against the buying and the selling in the temple which made its walls of miracles and martyrdoms.

- *un' altra fiata omai* ‘another time now’ → “once more”: the phrase for repetition is recast, and the separate time adverb is left out.
- *s' adiri del* ‘it be angered at the’ → “the wrath be kindled against the”: the mind is no longer the subject; the anger implicit in the pronominal verb becomes the subject of a passive metaphor, with “against” for the object of anger.
- *e vender* ‘and selling’ → “and the selling”: the article attached only to the first infinitive in Italian is repeated for the second in English.
- *dentro al templo* ‘within the temple’ → “in the temple”: the more explicit expression of interior position is reduced to “in”.
- *che si murò* ‘which was walled’ → “which made its walls”: the construction with *si*, naturally read as passive, is taken actively, with the temple as agent; the walls implicit in the verb become an explicit possessed object.
- *di segni e di martiri* ‘of signs and of martyrdoms’ → “of miracles and martyrdoms”: “miracles” gives the particular sense of ‘signs’; the preposition is not repeated.

> 124 O milizia del ciel, cu' io contemplo,  
> 125 adora per color che sono in terra  
> 126 tutti sviati retro al malo esempio.  

O soldiery of heaven, whom I look upon, pray for them who have all gone astray on earth, following the ill example.

- *contemplo* ‘contemplate’ → “look upon”: a phrasal verb of seeing replaces the single verb.
- *color che sono in terra tutti sviati* ‘those who are on earth all astray’ → “them who have all gone astray on earth”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun; the state expressed by ‘are … astray’ becomes a present perfect of going astray, with the place phrase moved to the end.
- *retro al* ‘after the’ → “following the”: a participle makes explicit the action implicit in the preposition.

> 127 Già si solea con le spade far guerra;  

Erst ’twas the wont to make war with swords;

- *Già si solea* ‘formerly one used to’ → “Erst ’twas the wont”: the impersonal imperfect of customary action becomes an impersonal clause with a predicate noun.
- *con le spade far guerra* ‘with the swords to make war’ → “to make war with swords”: the instrumental phrase follows the infinitive rather than preceding it, and the generic article is dropped.

> 128 ma or si fa togliendo or qui or quivi  
> 129 lo pan che il pio padre a nessun serra:  

now it is made by withholding, now here, now there, the bread the tender father bars from none;

- *ma or* ‘but now’ → “now”: the adversative conjunction is omitted.
- *togliendo* ‘taking away’ → “by withholding”: English marks the gerund explicitly as expressing means, and substitutes keeping something back for taking it away.
- *che il pio padre a nessun serra* ‘which the compassionate father to no one bars’ → “the tender father bars from none”: the object relative is unexpressed, the adjective is rendered by “tender”, and the phrase for the person denied the bread follows the verb with “from” rather than ‘to’.

> 130 ma tu, che sol per cancellare scrivi,  
> 131 pensa che Pietro e Paolo, che moriro  
> 132 per la vigna che guasti, ancor son vivi.  

but thou, who but to cancel, dost record, reflect that Peter and Paul who died for the vineyard thou layest waste, are living yet.

- *sol per cancellare scrivi* ‘only in order to cancel writest’ → “but to cancel, dost record”: restrictive ‘only’ becomes “but”, purpose is expressed by the bare phrase “to cancel”, and the simple verb of writing becomes an auxiliary with a verb of recording.
- *pensa* ‘think’ → “reflect”: the imperative is rendered by a verb of deliberate consideration.
- *che guasti* ‘which thou spoilest’ → “thou layest waste”: the object relative is omitted, and a verbal phrase replaces the single verb.
- *ancor son vivi* ‘still are alive’ → “are living yet”: the adjective of state becomes participial in form, and the adverb moves from first to last.

> 133 Ben puoi tu dire: “I' ho fermo il disiro  
> 134 sì a colui che volle viver solo  
> 135 e che per salti fu tratto al martiro,  
> 136 ch' io non conosco il Pescator nè Polo.”  

Though thou indeed mayst urge: “I have so fixed my longing on him who lived a solitary, and by tripping steps was drawn to martyrdom, that I know not the fisherman nor Paul.”

- *Ben puoi tu dire* ‘well mayst thou say’ → “Though thou indeed mayst urge”: the concessive force is made explicit by a conjunction; the subject precedes the modal, “indeed” renders the affirmation, and ‘say’ becomes the more argumentative “urge”.
- *I' ho fermo il disiro sì a colui* ‘I have fixed the desire so upon him’ → “I have so fixed my longing on him”: the degree adverb moves forward from after the object to before the participle, and the definite noun becomes a possessed “my longing”.
- *che volle viver solo* ‘who willed to live alone’ → “who lived a solitary”: the verb of willing disappears, its infinitive takes over the past tense, and ‘alone’ becomes a noun phrase describing the subject.
- *e che per salti* ‘and who through leaps’ → “and by tripping steps”: the relative is not repeated, and the movements are rendered as steps qualified by a participle.
- *al martiro* ‘to the martyrdom’ → “to martyrdom”: the definite article is dropped.
- *io non conosco* ‘I not know’ → “I know not”: the negative moves from before the verb to after it.

## Summary — Word Order, Idiom, and Emphasis

Wicksteed often rearranges a phrase without changing its emphasis. In *quale io allor vidi negli occhi santi amor*, he brings the delayed noun forward to form “what love”, keeping the object at the head of the clause. Adjectives commonly move before their nouns, as in *versi brevi*, “brief verses”, and object pronouns move after the verb. Yet Wicksteed does not consistently prefer ordinary English order: “where was the M’s peak” keeps the subject after the verb, as in *era il colmo dell’ emme*.

Changes of construction often bring out a distinction implicit in the Italian. An imperfect becomes a progressive in *gustava*, “was tasting”, while “would stay and hold their peace” gives *s’ arrestavano e taciensi* the force of repeated action. The gerund *rimirando lei* becomes the finite clause “as I gazed upon her”. Elsewhere a change of voice preserves the order of attention: “two more were followed by my keen regard” keeps *due* first by making it the subject of a passive rather than the object of *seguì*.

The choice of words can narrow a meaning or interpret an image. *La mente* becomes “my memory” rather than ‘the mind’, and *secondo* becomes “derived” rather than ‘second’. Some details disappear: “torch of Jove” does not mark the diminutive in *giovial facella*, and “once more” conveys *un’ altra fiata* without separately rendering *omai*. Conversely, “the place whence issueth” supplies an antecedent for *ond’ esce*. Two quieter adjustments recur throughout: English supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending identifies the subject, and omits the article that Italian uses before a possessive, as in *lo mio affetto*, “my affection”.

For all these shifts, Wicksteed keeps close to the Italian where its phrasing carries readily into English. “Five times seven” preserves the form of *cinque volte sette* rather than giving the total, and “now here, now there” keeps both the order and the repetition of *or qui or quivi*.
