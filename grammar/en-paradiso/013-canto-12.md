# Paradiso 12

## Lines 1–24 — The Two Circling Garlands

> 1 Sì tosto come l' ultima parola  
> 2 la benedetta fiamma per dir tolse,  

Soon as the blessed flame had taken up the final word to speak,

- *Sì tosto come* ‘as soon as’ → “Soon as”: the first member of the comparison is left unexpressed.
- *l' ultima parola la benedetta fiamma per dir tolse* ‘the final word the blessed flame to speak took’ → “the blessed flame had taken up the final word to speak”: the subject comes first, the object follows the finite verb, and the infinitive comes last. The simple past becomes a past perfect, and ‘took’ is expanded to “had taken up”.

> 3 a rotar cominciò la santa mola;  

began the sacred millstone to revolve,

- *a rotar cominciò la santa mola* ‘to revolve began the sacred millstone’ → “began the sacred millstone to revolve”: the infinitive moves from the beginning to the end, while the subject still follows the finite verb.

> 4 e nel suo giro tutta non si volse  
> 5 prima ch' un' altra di cerchio la chiuse,  

and in its rolling had not turned full round ere a second, circling, embraced it

- *nel suo giro* ‘in its revolution’ → “in its rolling”: a verbal noun suggests the ongoing movement rather than the circuit.
- *tutta non si volse* ‘it did not turn itself all the way’ → “had not turned full round”: the simple past becomes a past perfect, the reflexive verb becomes intransitive, and the expression of completeness moves after the verb.
- *un' altra* ‘another’ → “a second”: the indefinite is made explicitly ordinal.
- *di cerchio* ‘with a circle’ → “circling”: a prepositional phrase becomes a participle.
- *la chiuse* ‘it enclosed’ → “embraced it”: the object pronoun moves after the verb, and enclosure is expressed as an embrace.

> 6 e moto a moto, e canto a canto colse:  

and struck motion to its motion and song to its song;

- *e moto a moto, e canto a canto colse* ‘and motion to motion, and song to song matched’ → “and struck motion to its motion and song to its song”: the final verb moves before both pairs and is rendered by “struck”; each second noun gains a possessive.

> 7 canto che tanto vince nostre muse,  
> 8 nostre sirene, in quelle dolci tube,  
> 9 quanto primo splendor quel ch' ei refuse.  

song which so far surpasseth our Muses, our Sirens, in those sweet tubes, as the first splendour that which it back throweth.

- *tanto vince* ‘so much surpasses’ → “so far surpasseth”: degree is expressed in terms of distance.
- *primo splendor* ‘first splendour’ → “the first splendour”: the definite article is supplied.
- *refuse* ‘threw back’ → “back throweth”: the Italian preterite becomes a present, and the sense of return is expressed by a separate adverb before the verb.

> 10 Come si volgon per tenera nube  
> 11 due archi paralleli e concolori,  

As sweep o’er the thin mist two bows, parallel and like in colour,

- *si volgon* ‘turn themselves’ → “sweep”: the reflexive construction becomes an intransitive verb of movement.
- *per tenera nube* ‘through soft cloud’ → “o’er the thin mist”: the preposition changes, a definite article is supplied, and softness of cloud becomes thinness of mist.
- *concolori* ‘of one colour’ → “like in colour”: a single adjective denoting sameness is expanded into a phrase denoting likeness.

> 12 quando Giunone a sua ancella iube,  

when Juno maketh behest to her handmaiden,

- *a sua ancella iube* ‘to her handmaiden commands’ → “maketh behest to her handmaiden”: the final verb becomes a verb-and-noun phrase placed before the addressee.

> 13 nascendo di quel d' entro quel di fuori,  

the one without born from the one within—

- *nascendo di quel d' entro quel di fuori* ‘being born from the one within the one without’ → “the one without born from the one within”: the subject moves from last to first, and the Italian gerund becomes a past participle.

> 14 a guisa del parlar di quella vaga,  
> 15 ch' amor consunse come sol vapori;  

in fashion of the speech of that wandering nymph whom love consumed as the sun doth the vapours,—

- *del parlar* ‘of the speaking’ → “of the speech”: a substantivized infinitive becomes a noun.
- *di quella vaga* ‘of that wandering one’ → “of that wandering nymph”: a noun is supplied for the substantivized feminine adjective.
- *come sol vapori* ‘as sun vapours’ → “as the sun doth the vapours”: both nouns gain articles, and “doth” stands in for the verb left understood in Italian.

> 16 e fanno qui la gente esser presaga,  
> 17 per lo patto che Dio con Noè pose,  
> 18 del mondo che giammai più non si allaga:  

making folk here on earth foreknow, in virtue of the compact that God made with Noah, that the world never shall be drowned again;

- *e fanno* ‘and they make’ → “making”: a coordinated finite verb becomes a participle, without the conjunction.
- *qui la gente* ‘here the people’ → “folk here on earth”: the collective noun loses its article, and the adverb moves after it and is expanded to specify the place.
- *esser presaga* ‘to be foreknowing’ → “foreknow”: the infinitive and predicate adjective become a single bare infinitive after the causative.
- *per lo patto* ‘by the compact’ → “in virtue of the compact”: the simple preposition is expanded into a phrase.
- *con Noè pose* ‘with Noah set’ → “made with Noah”: the verb precedes the prepositional phrase, and English uses the idiom of making a compact.
- *del mondo che giammai più non si allaga* ‘of the world, that never again is flooded’ → “that the world never shall be drowned again”: what Italian introduces as the object of foreknowledge with ‘of’ becomes the subject inside a single content clause. The pronominal present becomes a future passive, flooding is rendered as drowning, and the negative expression is divided between “never” before the verb and “again” after it.

> 19 così di quelle sempiterne rose  
> 20 volgeansi circa noi le due ghirlande,  

so of those sempiternal roses revolved around us the two garlands,

- *volgeansi* ‘turned themselves’ → “revolved”: an intransitive verb replaces the reflexive form.

> 21 e sì l' estrema all' ultima rispose.  

and so the outmost answered to the other.

- *all' ultima rispose* ‘to the last answered’ → “answered to the other”: the verb moves before its complement, and the garland designated by its position in a sequence is identified simply as the other of the pair.

> 22 Poichè il tripudio e l' alta festa grande,  
> 23 sì del cantare e sì del fiammeggiarsi  
> 24 luce con luce gaudiose e blande,  

Soon as the dance and high great festival,—alike of song and flashing light with light, gladsome and benign,—

- *Poichè* ‘when’ → “Soon as”: the temporal conjunction is rendered by a phrase emphasizing immediacy.
- *l' alta festa grande* ‘the high festival great’ → “high great festival”: the second adjective moves before the noun, and the article is not repeated after “the dance”.
- *sì del cantare e sì del fiammeggiarsi* ‘both of the singing and of the flashing upon one another’ → “alike of song and flashing”: one introductory “alike” replaces the repeated markers of coordination; the preposition is not repeated, and the substantivized infinitives become an ordinary noun and a verbal noun, without articles or an explicit reciprocal pronoun.
- *blande* ‘gentle’ → “benign”: the adjective is rendered in terms of kindliness rather than gentleness.

## Lines 25–57 — The Two Champions and Dominic’s Birth

> 25 insieme a punto ed a voler quetarsi,  

accordant at a point of time and act of will had stilled them,

- *insieme a punto ed a voler* ‘together in point of time and in willing’ → “accordant at a point of time and act of will”: the adverb becomes an adjective; “of time” makes the sense of *punto* explicit, and the infinitive used as a noun becomes “act of will”.
- *quetarsi* ‘to come to rest’ → “had stilled them”: the reflexive infinitive, dependent on a verb in the preceding line, becomes a finite transitive verb in the past perfect, with the things brought to rest as its object.

> 26 pur come gli occhi ch' al piacer che i move  
> 27 conviene insieme chiudere e levarsi,  

like to the eyes which at the pleasure that moveth them must needs be closed and lifted in accord,

- *pur come* ‘just as’ → “like to”: a phrase of likeness replaces the conjunction, without a separate word for the intensifier.
- *conviene insieme chiudere e levarsi* ‘it is necessary together to close and raise themselves’ → “must needs be closed and lifted in accord”: the impersonal construction becomes a modal one with the eyes as subject; the actions are put in the passive, and *insieme* is moved after both verbs and rendered as agreement rather than simply simultaneity.

> 28 del cor dell' una delle luci nuove  
> 29 si mosse voce, che l' ago alla stella  
> 30 parer mi fece in volgermi al suo dove;  

from out the heart of one of the new lights there moved a voice which made me seem the needle to the star in turning me to where it was;

- *del cor* ‘from the heart’ → “from out the heart”: “out” makes the motion from within explicit.
- *delle luci nuove* ‘of the lights new’ → “of the new lights”: the adjective precedes its noun.
- *si mosse voce* ‘moved a voice’ → “there moved a voice”: English inserts introductory “there” and an indefinite article; the pronominal verb is rendered by an intransitive one.
- *che l' ago alla stella parer mi fece* ‘which the needle to the star to seem me made’ → “which made me seem the needle to the star”: the causative verb comes first, followed by its object, the bare infinitive, and the comparison.
- *in volgermi al suo dove* ‘in turning myself to its where’ → “in turning me to where it was”: the reflexive takes the form “me”, and the possessive with a substantivized adverb becomes a clause of place.

> 31 e cominciò: “L' amor che mi fa bella  
> 32 mi tragge a ragionar dell' altro duca,  
> 33 per cui del mio sì ben ci si favella.  

and it began: “The love which maketh me beautiful draweth me to discourse of the other chief, on whose account such fair utterance is made to us concerning mine.

- *mi fa bella* ‘me makes beautiful’ → “maketh me beautiful”: the object follows the verb.
- *mi tragge* ‘me draws’ → “draweth me”: the object follows the verb.
- *duca* ‘leader’ → “chief”: a term for the person in command replaces the term for one who leads.
- *del mio sì ben ci si favella* ‘of mine so well to us one speaks’ → “such fair utterance is made to us concerning mine”: the impersonal verb becomes a passive phrase with a noun as subject; the adverb of manner becomes an adjective qualified by “such”, and the topic of the speaking moves to the end.

> 34 Degno è che dov' è l' un l' altro s' induca,  
> 35 sì che com' elli ad una militaro,  
> 36 così la gloria loro insieme luca.  

Meet is it that wherever is the one the other be led in, that, as they warred together, so may their glory shine in union.

- *dov' è l' un* ‘where is the one’ → “wherever is the one”: “wherever” gives the relative a more expressly general force.
- *l' altro s' induca* ‘the other be introduced’ → “the other be led in”: the Italian *si* construction is rendered by an English passive, with the sense of the verb divided between “led” and “in”.
- *sì che* ‘so that’ → “that”: the conjunction of purpose has no separate equivalent for *sì*.
- *ad una militaro* ‘as one served as soldiers’ → “warred together”: the phrase of unity follows the verb; “warred” stresses fighting rather than service.
- *così la gloria loro insieme luca* ‘so the glory of them together may shine’ → “so may their glory shine in union”: the modal expressing the subjunctive precedes the subject, the possessive precedes its noun, and the adverb becomes a phrase following the verb.

> 37 L' esercito di Cristo, che sì caro  
> 38 costò a riarmar, retro all' insegna  
> 39 si movea tardo, suspiccioso e raro,  

Christ’s army, which it cost so dear to re-equip, was following the standard, laggard, fearsome and thin-ranked;

- *L' esercito di Cristo* ‘the army of Christ’ → “Christ’s army”: the following *di* phrase becomes a preceding possessive.
- *che sì caro costò a riarmar* ‘which so dear it cost to rearm’ → “which it cost so dear to re-equip”: the phrase of cost follows the verb, and “re-equip” is broader than ‘rearm’.
- *retro all' insegna si movea* ‘behind the standard was moving’ → “was following the standard”: verb and preposition give way to a transitive verb with the standard as its object; the progressive renders the imperfect.
- *tardo, suspiccioso e raro* ‘slow, mistrustful and sparse’ → “laggard, fearsome and thin-ranked”: “laggard” suggests falling behind; “fearsome” renders apprehension rather than suspicion, and “thin-ranked” specifies in what respect the army was sparse.

> 40 quando lo imperador, che sempre regna,  
> 41 provvide alla milizia ch' era in forse,  
> 42 per sola grazia, non per esser degna;  

when the Emperor who ever reigneth took counsel for his soldiery that was in peril, of his grace only, not that it was worthy;

- *sempre regna* ‘always reigns’ → “ever reigneth”: “ever” expresses continuance.
- *provvide alla milizia* ‘made provision for the soldiery’ → “took counsel for his soldiery”: the verbal phrase stresses deliberation; “his” replaces the definite article.
- *ch' era in forse* ‘that was in doubt’ → “that was in peril”: uncertainty of outcome is interpreted as danger.
- *per sola grazia* ‘by grace alone’ → “of his grace only”: the preposition changes, a possessive is added, and the restrictive modifier follows the noun.
- *non per esser degna* ‘not for being worthy’ → “not that it was worthy”: the preposition and infinitive become a finite clause with an expressed subject and past tense.

> 43 e, com' è detto, a sua sposa soccorse  
> 44 con due campioni, al cui fare, al cui dire  
> 45 lo popol disviato si raccorse.  

and, as hath been said, came to the succour of his spouse with two champions, at whose doing, at whose saying, the straggling squadron gathered itself again.

- *com' è detto* ‘as is said’ → “as hath been said”: the present passive becomes a perfect passive.
- *a sua sposa soccorse* ‘to his spouse he brought aid’ → “came to the succour of his spouse”: the simple verb becomes a phrase with “succour” as a noun, and the beneficiary follows it.
- *lo popol disviato* ‘the people gone astray’ → “the straggling squadron”: the noun acquires a military sense, and a preceding present participle replaces a following past participle.
- *si raccorse* ‘gathered itself back together’ → “gathered itself again”: “again” gives separate expression to the sense of return.

> 46 In quella parte, ove surge ad aprire  
> 47 Zefiro dolce le novelle fronde,  
> 48 di che si vede Europa rivestire,  

To-wards that part where sweet Zephyr riseth to open the new leaves, wherewith Europe seeth herself reclad,

- *In quella parte* ‘in that part’ → “To-wards that part”: direction replaces position.
- *surge ad aprire Zefiro dolce* ‘rises to open Zephyr sweet’ → “sweet Zephyr riseth to open”: the delayed subject precedes the verb, and its adjective precedes the name.
- *fronde* ‘leafy branches’ → “leaves”: the rendering singles out the foliage.
- *di che si vede Europa rivestire* ‘with which sees herself Europe being reclothed’ → “wherewith Europe seeth herself reclad”: the preposition and relative become one word, the subject precedes the finite verb, and a past participle replaces the infinitive, presenting the result of the reclothing.

> 49 non molto lungi al percoter dell' onde,  
> 50 retro alle quali, per la lunga foga,  
> 51 lo sol talvolta ad ogni uom si nasconde,  

not far off from the smiting of the waves, behind the which, because of their long stretch, the sun sometimes hideth himself from all,

- *non molto lungi* ‘not very far’ → “not far off”: the degree word is omitted and “off” is added.
- *per la lunga foga* ‘because of the long rush’ → “because of their long stretch”: the definite article becomes a possessive, and “stretch” interprets the phrase in terms of extent rather than impetus.
- *ad ogni uom si nasconde* ‘from every man hides himself’ → “hideth himself from all”: the verb precedes the prepositional phrase, and a collective pronoun replaces the distributive singular.

> 52 siede la fortunata Calaroga,  
> 53 sotto la protezion del grande scudo,  
> 54 in che soggiace il leone e soggioga.  

the fortune-favoured Calahorra sitteth under protection of the mighty shield, whereon submits the lion, and subdueth.

- *siede la fortunata Calaroga* ‘sits the fortunate Calaroga’ → “the fortune-favoured Calahorra sitteth”: the subject comes first, and the simple adjective becomes a compound suggesting favour bestowed.
- *sotto la protezion* ‘under the protection’ → “under protection”: the definite article is omitted.
- *grande* ‘great’ → “mighty”: the adjective is interpreted in terms of power.
- *in che* ‘in which’ → “whereon”: a relative adverb replaces the preposition and pronoun, with the relation construed as ‘on’ rather than ‘in’.
- *soggiace* ‘lies beneath’ → “submits”: the verb of position is given a figurative sense.

> 55 Dentro vi nacque l' amoroso drudo  
> 56 della fede cristiana, il santo atleta,  
> 57 benigno ai suoi, ed ai nemici crudo;  

Therewithin was born the amorous frere of the Christian faith, the sacred athlete, benignant to his own and cruel to his foes;

- *Dentro vi nacque* ‘within there was born’ → “Therewithin was born”: the two indications of place are combined in one adverb.
- *drudo* ‘lover’ → “frere”: the noun denotes a brother or friar instead of a lover, leaving the sense of love to the adjective.
- *della fede cristiana* ‘of the faith Christian’ → “of the Christian faith”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *santo* ‘holy’ → “sacred”: a different adjective of consecration is chosen.
- *ed ai nemici crudo* ‘and to the enemies harsh’ → “and cruel to his foes”: the adjective precedes its complement, “cruel” strengthens ‘harsh’, and a possessive replaces the definite article.

## Lines 58–90 — Dominic’s Early Signs and Vocation

> 58 e come fu creata, fu repleta  
> 59 sì la sua mente di viva virtute,  
> 60 che nella madre lei fece profeta.  

and, so soon as created, his mind was so replete with living virtue, that in his mother’s womb he made her prophetess.

- *come fu creata* ‘as soon as it was created’ → “so soon as created”: the temporal clause is reduced to a participial phrase.
- *fu repleta sì la sua mente* ‘was replete so his mind’ → “his mind was so replete”: the subject moves before the verb and the degree adverb before the adjective.
- *di viva virtute* ‘of living virtue’ → “with living virtue”: the preposition changes.
- *nella madre* ‘in the mother’ → “in his mother’s womb”: the relationship is made possessive and the place more specific.
- *lei fece profeta* ‘her he made prophet’ → “he made her prophetess”: the object follows the verb, and the English noun marks the feminine explicitly.

> 61 Poichè le sponsalizie fur compiute  
> 62 al sacro fonte intra lui e la fede,  
> 63 u' si dotar di mutua salute;  

When the espousals were complete at the sacred font, betwixt him and the faith, where they gave dower of mutual salvation,

- *fur compiute* ‘were completed’ → “were complete”: an adjective of state replaces the passive participle.
- *si dotar di mutua salute* ‘they endowed each other with mutual salvation’ → “they gave dower of mutual salvation”: the act of endowing becomes a verb and noun; the reciprocal pronoun has no separate rendering.

> 64 la donna, che per lui l' assenso diede,  
> 65 vide nel sonno il mirabile frutto  
> 66 ch' uscir dovea di lui e delle rede;  

the lady who for him gave the assent saw in her sleep the marvellous fruit destined to issue from him and from his heirs;

- *l’ assenso diede* ‘the assent gave’ → “gave the assent”: the object follows the verb.
- *nel sonno* ‘in the sleep’ → “in her sleep”: a possessive replaces the article.
- *che uscir dovea* ‘which to issue was to’ → “destined to issue”: a participial phrase replaces the relative clause, and the infinitive follows the expression of futurity.
- *delle rede* ‘from the heirs’ → “from his heirs”: a possessive replaces the article.

> 67 e perchè fosse, quale era, in costrutto,  
> 68 quinci si mosse spirito a nomarlo  
> 69 del possessivo di cui era tutto.  

and that he might in very construing be what he was, a spirit from up here moved them to call him by the possessive adjective of him whose he all was.

- *perchè fosse, quale era, in costrutto* ‘in order that he might be, such as he was, in construction’ → “that he might in very construing be what he was”: the phrase about construction moves inside the verb phrase; “very” is added, and ‘such as’ becomes “what”.
- *quinci si mosse spirito a nomarlo* ‘from here a spirit stirred to name him’ → “a spirit from up here moved them to call him”: the subject moves to the front; “up” makes the direction explicit. The reflexive verb becomes transitive, with “them” supplied as the people moved to act.
- *del possessivo* ‘with the possessive’ → “by the possessive adjective”: the preposition changes and the grammatical noun understood after ‘possessive’ is supplied.
- *di cui era tutto* ‘of whom he was wholly’ → “of him whose he all was”: the antecedent is made explicit, the relative becomes possessive, and “all” moves before the verb.

> 70 Dominico fu detto; ed io ne parlo  
> 71 sì come dell' agricola, che Cristo  
> 72 elesse all' orto suo per aiutarlo.  

Dominic was he named; and I speak of him as of the husbandman whom Christ chose for his orchard, to bring aid to it.

- *fu detto* ‘was called’ → “was he named”: the verb of calling is rendered by one of naming.
- *io ne parlo* ‘I of him speak’ → “I speak of him”: the pronominal complement follows the verb as a prepositional phrase.
- *sì come* ‘just as’ → “as”: the reinforcement of the comparison is not separately rendered.
- *all’ orto suo* ‘for the garden his’ → “for his orchard”: the possessive moves before the noun, and ‘garden’ is rendered more specifically.
- *per aiutarlo* ‘to help it’ → “to bring aid to it”: the simple transitive verb becomes a verb and noun with a prepositional complement.

> 73 Ben parve messo e famigliar di Cristo;  
> 74 chè il primo amor che in lui fu manifesto  
> 75 fu al primo consiglio che diè Cristo.  

Well did he show himself a messenger and a familiar of Christ, for the first love made manifest in him was to the first counsel that Christ gave.

- *Ben parve* ‘well he appeared’ → “Well did he show himself”: the verb of appearing becomes a reflexive expression, with an emphatic auxiliary after the fronted adverb.
- *messo e famigliar* ‘messenger and familiar’ → “a messenger and a familiar”: articles are supplied for the two predicate nouns.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a coordinating explanation replaces the subordinate causal clause.
- *che in lui fu manifesto* ‘which in him was manifest’ → “made manifest in him”: the finite relative becomes a participial phrase; “made” replaces the copula, and the phrase of place comes last.
- *diè Cristo* ‘gave Christ’ → “Christ gave”: the subject precedes the verb.

> 76 Spesse fiate fu tacito e desto  
> 77 trovato in terra dalla sua nutrice  
> 78 come dicesse: *Io son venuto a questo.*  

Many a time, silent and awake, was he found on the floor, by her who nursed him, as who should say, *It was for this I came.*

- *Spesse fiate* ‘frequent times’ → “Many a time”: an idiomatic singular replaces the plural.
- *fu tacito e desto trovato* ‘was silent and awake found’ → “silent and awake, was he found”: the two adjectives move before the auxiliary.
- *in terra* ‘on the ground’ → “on the floor”: the place is rendered more specifically.
- *dalla sua nutrice* ‘by his nurse’ → “by her who nursed him”: the agent noun becomes a pronoun and relative clause; the possessive relationship is expressed by the object of the verb.
- *come dicesse* ‘as if he were saying’ → “as who should say”: an indefinite relative and modal auxiliary render the hypothetical comparison.
- *Io son venuto a questo* ‘I have come to this’ → “It was for this I came”: a cleft construction brings the purpose phrase to the front, ‘to’ becomes “for”, and the Italian perfect becomes a simple past.

> 79 O padre suo veramente Felice!  

Oh father his, Felice in good sooth!

- *veramente Felice* ‘truly Felice’ → “Felice in good sooth”: an adverbial phrase replaces the adverb and follows the name.

> 80 o madre sua veramente Giovanna,  
> 81 se interpretata val come si dice!  

Oh mother his, Giovanna in good sooth, if the word means, translated, what they say!

- *veramente Giovanna* ‘truly Giovanna’ → “Giovanna in good sooth”: the adverb becomes a phrase following the name.
- *se interpretata val* ‘if, interpreted, it signifies’ → “if the word means, translated”: the referent of the feminine participle is supplied as “the word”, the participle is rendered as translation rather than interpretation, and it follows the verb.
- *come si dice* ‘as is said’ → “what they say”: the comparison becomes the object of “means”, and impersonal *si* becomes indefinite “they”.

> 82 Non per lo mondo, per cui mo s' affanna  
> 83 di retro ad Ostiense ed a Taddeo,  
> 84 ma per amor della verace manna,  

Not for the world for whose sake now men toil after him of Ostia and Thaddeus, but for love of the true manna,

- *per cui* ‘for which’ → “for whose sake”: the preposition is expanded into a phrase of purpose.
- *s’ affanna* ‘one toils’ → “men toil”: the generic singular construction becomes an explicit plural.
- *di retro ad Ostiense* ‘after the Ostian’ → “after him of Ostia”: the title becomes a pronoun with a phrase of place.
- *della verace manna* ‘of the veracious manna’ → “of the true manna”: the adjective is rendered by the simpler “true”.

> 85 in picciol tempo gran dottor si feo,  
> 86 tal che si mise a circuir la vigna,  
> 87 che tosto imbianca, se il vignaio è reo;  

in short season he became a mighty teacher, such that he set him to go round the vineyard, which soon turneth gray if the vine-dresser be to blame;

- *in picciol tempo* ‘in little time’ → “in short season”: both the measure and the noun of duration are recast.
- *gran dottor si feo* ‘a great doctor he made himself’ → “he became a mighty teacher”: the reflexive becomes a verb of becoming, the predicate follows it, and the title is rendered in its teaching sense.
- *si mise a circuir* ‘he set himself to go around’ → “he set him to go round”: the reflexive is expressed by the older use of the object pronoun “him”.
- *imbianca* ‘whitens’ → “turneth gray”: a verb of change with a predicate adjective replaces the single verb, and the colour changes.
- *è reo* ‘is culpable’ → “be to blame”: the adjective becomes an infinitival idiom, and the indicative gives way to the English conditional subjunctive.

> 88 ed alla sedia, che fu già benigna  
> 89 più ai poveri giusti, non per lei,  
> 90 ma per colui che siede, che traligna,  

and from the seat which erst was more benign to the just poor—not in itself, but in him who sitteth on it, and degenerateth—

- *alla sedia* ‘to the seat’ → “from the seat”: English presents the seat as the source rather than the addressee.
- *fu già benigna più* ‘was formerly benign more’ → “erst was more benign”: the time adverb moves before the verb and the comparative adverb before the adjective.
- *ai poveri giusti* ‘to the poor just’ → “to the just poor”: the qualifying adjective precedes the substantivized ‘poor’.
- *non per lei, ma per colui* ‘not on account of it, but on account of him’ → “not in itself, but in him”: the causal prepositions become “in”, and the pronoun referring to the seat becomes intensive.
- *che siede* ‘who sits’ → “who sitteth on it”: the complement identifying the seat is supplied.
- *che traligna* ‘who degenerates’ → “and degenerateth”: the second relative clause becomes a coordinated predicate.

## Lines 91–123 — Dominic’s Mission and the Abandoned Track

> 91 non dispensare o due o tre per sei,  
> 92 non la fortuna di prima vacante,  
> 93 non *decimas quae sunt pauperum Dei*  

not to dispense or two or three for six, not for the fortune of the next vacancy, not for the tithes belonging to God’s poor,

- *non la fortuna di prima vacante* ‘not the fortune of the first vacancy’ → “not for the fortune of the next vacancy”: English supplies “for” in anticipation of the later verb of asking; ‘first’ is reckoned forward as “next”, and the definite article is supplied before it.
- *non decimas* ‘not tithes’ → “not for the tithes”: the Latin accusative gives the object directly; English supplies “for” and the definite article.
- *quae sunt pauperum Dei* ‘which are of the poor of God’ → “belonging to God’s poor”: the relative clause with a possessive genitive becomes a participial phrase, and the second genitive is moved before “poor”.

> 94 addomandò; ma contro al mondo errante  
> 95 licenza di combatter per lo seme,  
> 96 del qual ti fascian ventiquattro piante.  

he made demand; but for leave against the erring world to fight for that seed wherefrom these four and twenty plants ensheaf thee.

- *addomandò* ‘he asked’ → “he made demand”: a single verb becomes a verb and noun.
- *contro al mondo errante* ‘against the world erring’ → “against the erring world”: the adjective moves before its noun, and the phrase follows rather than precedes “leave”.
- *licenza di combatter* ‘leave to fight’ → “for leave” … “to fight”: English supplies “for” after “made demand” and separates the infinitive from “leave”.
- *lo seme* ‘the seed’ → “that seed”: the definite article becomes demonstrative.
- *del qual* ‘of which’ → “wherefrom”: the relative phrase becomes an adverb explicitly indicating origin.
- *ti fascian ventiquattro piante* ‘thee encircle twenty-four plants’ → “these four and twenty plants ensheaf thee”: the subject moves before the verb and the object after it; “these” is supplied, the units precede the tens, and “ensheaf” figures the encircling as a binding into a sheaf.

> 97 Poi con dottrina e con volere insieme  
> 98 con l' uficio apostolico si mosse,  
> 99 quasi torrente ch' alta vena preme;  

Then with teaching and with will together, with the apostolic office he moved forth, like a torrent that a deep vein out-presseth,

- *l’ uficio apostolico* ‘the office apostolic’ → “the apostolic office”: the adjective moves before the noun.
- *si mosse* ‘set himself in motion’ → “he moved forth”: an intransitive verb with a directional adverb replaces the reflexive construction.
- *quasi torrente* ‘as if a torrent’ → “like a torrent”: the comparison is made with a preposition, and English supplies the article.
- *alta vena preme* ‘a deep vein presses’ → “a deep vein out-presseth”: English supplies an article and adds the sense of outward pressure to the verb.

> 100 e negli sterpi eretici percosse  
> 101 l' impeto suo, più vivamente quivi  
> 102 dove le resistenze eran più grosse.  

and his rush smote amongst the stumps of heresy most livingly where the resistances were grossest.

- *negli sterpi eretici* ‘in the heretical scrub’ → “amongst the stumps of heresy”: “amongst” distributes the action among individual “stumps”; the adjective becomes an “of” phrase, and the whole phrase moves after the verb.
- *percosse l’ impeto suo* ‘smote the impetus of him’ → “his rush smote”: the subject moves before the verb, its possessive before the noun, and the impetus is expressed as a “rush”.
- *più vivamente quivi* ‘more vividly there’ → “most livingly”: the comparative becomes a superlative, the adverb is rendered by “livingly”, and the separate ‘there’ before ‘where’ drops out.
- *più grosse* ‘thicker’ → “grossest”: the comparative becomes a superlative.

> 103 Di lui si fecer poi diversi rivi,  
> 104 onde l' orto cattolico si riga,  
> 105 sì che i suoi arbuscelli stan più vivi.  

From him then diverse streamlets sprung, whereby the Catholic orchard is so watered that its shrubs have the fuller life.

- *si fecer poi diversi rivi* ‘there formed themselves then diverse streams’ → “then diverse streamlets sprung”: “then” and the subject move before the verb; an intransitive verb of origin replaces the reflexive, and “streamlets” gives the streams a diminutive sense.
- *l’ orto cattolico* ‘the garden catholic’ → “the Catholic orchard”: the adjective moves before the noun, and “orchard” is more specific than ‘garden’.
- *si riga, sì che* ‘is watered, so that’ → “is so watered that”: “so” moves into the preceding clause to qualify “watered”.
- *i suoi arbuscelli stan più vivi* ‘its little trees stand more alive’ → “its shrubs have the fuller life”: “shrubs” replaces the diminutive; a verb of possession and a qualified noun replace the verb of state and comparative adjective.

> 106 Se tal fu l' una rota della biga,  
> 107 in che la santa Chiesa si difese,  
> 108 e vinse in campo la sua civil briga,  

If such was the one wheel of the chariot wherein Holy Church defended her, and won in open field her civil strife,

- *l’ una rota* ‘the one of the wheels’ → “the one wheel”: the Italian singles out one of a pair; English places “one” directly before “wheel”.
- *biga* ‘two-horse chariot’ → “chariot”: the English noun does not specify the two-horse team.
- *in che* ‘in which’ → “wherein”: a relative adverb replaces the preposition and pronoun.
- *la santa Chiesa si difese* ‘the holy Church herself defended’ → “Holy Church defended her”: the article drops out, and the reflexive clitic becomes a personal pronoun after the verb, used reflexively.
- *in campo* ‘in the field’ → “in open field”: “open” is supplied.

> 109 ben ti dovrebbe assai esser palese  
> 110 l' eccellenza dell' altra, di cui Tomma  
> 111 dinanzi al mio venir fu sì cortese.  

clear enough should be to thee the excellence of that other, concerning whom, ere my coming, Thomas was so courteous.

- *ben ti dovrebbe assai esser palese* ‘well to thee should sufficiently be plain’ → “clear enough should be to thee”: the predicate adjective and its degree word move to the front, “to thee” moves after “be”, and the initial intensifier has no separate equivalent.
- *dell’ altra* ‘of the other’ → “of that other”: the article becomes demonstrative.
- *di cui* ‘of whom’ → “concerning whom”: the preposition explicitly marks the person as the subject of the courtesy.
- *Tomma dinanzi al mio venir* ‘Thomas before my coming’ → “ere my coming, Thomas”: the time phrase moves before the subject, and a verbal noun renders the substantivized infinitive.

> 112 Ma l' orbita, che fe' la parte somma  
> 113 di sua circonferenza, è derelitta,  
> 114 sì ch'è la muffa dov'era la gromma.  

But the track which the highest part of its circumference took hath been so abandoned, that there now is mold where once was crust.

- *che fe’ la parte somma di sua circonferenza* ‘which made the part highest of its circumference’ → “which the highest part of its circumference took”: the subject moves before the verb and the adjective before its noun; the track is said to be taken rather than made.
- *è derelitta, sì ch’* ‘is abandoned, so that’ → “hath been so abandoned, that”: the present state becomes a perfect passive, and “so” moves back to qualify “abandoned”.
- *è la muffa dov’era la gromma* ‘is the mold where was the crust’ → “there now is mold where once was crust”: existential “there” is supplied, the articles drop out, and “now” and “once” make explicit the contrast conveyed by the tenses.

> 115 La sua famiglia, che si mosse dritta  
> 116 coi piedi alle sue orme, è tanto volta,  
> 117 che quel dinanzi a quel di retro gitta;  

His household, who marched straight with feet in his footprints, hath turned so round, that the toe striketh on the heel’s imprint;

- *si mosse dritta* ‘moved straight’ → “marched straight”: a more specific verb of motion replaces the pronominal verb.
- *coi piedi alle sue orme* ‘with the feet at his tracks’ → “with feet in his footprints”: the article before “feet” drops out, and the relation to the tracks is expressed as being “in” footprints.
- *è tanto volta* ‘is so turned’ → “hath turned so round”: a state is rendered as a completed movement, with “round” supplying its direction.
- *quel dinanzi a quel di retro gitta* ‘the part in front onto the part behind it casts’ → “the toe striketh on the heel’s imprint”: the translation identifies the two parts as toe and heel, supplies “imprint”, and replaces the construction with ‘casts’ by “striketh” followed by the phrase naming the place struck.

> 118 e tosto si vedrà della ricolta  
> 119 della mala coltura, quando il loglio  
> 120 si lagnerà che l'arca gli sia tolta.  

and soon shall sight be had of the harvest of the ill-culture, when the tare shall wail that the chest is reft from it.

- *si vedrà* ‘one will see’ → “shall sight be had”: the impersonal verb of seeing becomes a passive phrase with “sight” as its subject.
- *mala coltura* ‘bad cultivation’ → “ill-culture”: adjective and noun become a compound.
- *il loglio si lagnerà* ‘the darnel will complain’ → “the tare shall wail”: the plant name is rendered by “tare”, and an intransitive verb expressing a cry replaces the pronominal verb of complaint.
- *l’arca gli sia tolta* ‘the chest from it be taken away’ → “the chest is reft from it”: the subjunctive becomes indicative, “reft” renders the taking away, and the dative pronoun becomes a prepositional phrase after the participle.

> 121 Ben dico, chi cercasse a foglio a foglio  
> 122 nostro volume, ancor troveria carta  
> 123 u' leggerebbe: *Io mi son quel ch' io soglio;*  

I well allow that whoso should search leaf after leaf through our volume, might yet find a page where he might read: *I am as I was wont;*

- *Ben dico* ‘well I say’ → “I well allow that”: saying becomes conceding, and “that” is supplied to introduce what is conceded.
- *a foglio a foglio nostro volume* ‘leaf by leaf our volume’ → “leaf after leaf through our volume”: the repeated preposition becomes a phrase of succession, and “through” is supplied before what in Italian is the direct object of the search.
- *ancor troveria carta* ‘still would find a page’ → “might yet find a page”: the conditional result becomes a possibility, and “yet” follows the modal rather than preceding it.
- *u’ leggerebbe* ‘where he would read’ → “where he might read”: the conditional again becomes a modal of possibility.
- *Io mi son quel ch’ io soglio* ‘I am what I am wont to be’ → “I am as I was wont”: the reinforcing pronominal element has no separate equivalent, identity with ‘what’ becomes comparison with “as”, and the present verb of custom becomes past.

## Lines 124–145 — Bonaventura Names His Companions and Credits Thomas

> 124 ma non fia da Casal, nè d' Acquasparta,  
> 125 là onde vegnon tali alla scrittura,  
> 126 che l' un la fugge e l' altro la coarta.  

but not from Casale, nor from Acquasparta shall he be, whence come such to our Scripture that the one shirketh, the other draweth it yet tighter.

- *non fia da Casal, nè d' Acquasparta* ‘he shall not be from Casale, nor from Acquasparta’ → “not from Casale, nor from Acquasparta shall he be”: the two phrases of origin come before the verb, and the negative goes with the first phrase rather than with the verb.
- *là onde* ‘there whence’ → “whence”: the separate word for ‘there’ drops out.
- *alla scrittura* ‘to the writing’ → “to our Scripture”: the definite article gives way to a possessive.
- *la fugge* ‘it flees’ → “shirketh”: the verb of flight becomes one of avoidance, and its object is left unexpressed.
- *e l' altro la coarta* ‘and the other constrains it’ → “the other draweth it yet tighter”: the conjunction drops out, the object follows the verb, and a verb of constraint is expanded into a phrase with an intensified comparative.

> 127 Io son la vita di Bonaventura  
> 128 da Bagnoregio, che nei grandi offici  
> 129 sempre posposi la sinistra cura.  

I am the life of Bonaventura of Bagnoregio, who in the great offices did ever place behind the lefthand care.

- *da Bagnoregio* ‘from Bagnoregio’ → “of Bagnoregio”: origin is expressed with “of”.
- *sempre posposi* ‘always I placed behind’ → “did ever place behind”: the simple past is rendered with an auxiliary, with the adverb between auxiliary and verb.
- *la sinistra cura* ‘the left care’ → “the lefthand care”: the adjective is made more explicit with the compound “lefthand”.

> 130 Illuminato ed Augustin son quici,  
> 131 che fur dei primi scalzi poverelli,  
> 132 che nel capestro a Dio si fero amici.  

Illuminato and Augustine are here, who were of the first unshod poor brethren, that with the cord made themselves friends to God.

- *poverelli* ‘poor little ones’ → “poor brethren”: the diminutive substantive becomes an adjective with a supplied noun; the diminutive itself is not rendered.
- *nel capestro* ‘in the halter’ → “with the cord”: the preposition is taken instrumentally, and the more general “cord” replaces ‘halter’.
- *a Dio si fero amici* ‘to God themselves they made friends’ → “made themselves friends to God”: the reflexive follows the verb, and the phrase naming God moves to the end.

> 133 Ugo da San Vittore è qui con elli,  
> 134 e Pietro Mangiadore, e Pietro Ispano  
> 135 lo qual giù luce in dodici libelli;  

Hugh of St Victor is here with them, and Pietro Mangiadore, and Pietro Ispano, who giveth light below in twelve booklets;

- *da San Vittore* ‘from St Victor’ → “of St Victor”: the name is linked by “of” rather than ‘from’.
- *giù luce* ‘below shines’ → “giveth light below”: the verb becomes a verb and noun, and the adverb follows them.

> 136 Natan profeta, e il metropolitano  
> 137 Crisostomo, ed Anselmo, e quel Donato  
> 138 ch' alla prim' arte degnò por la mano;  

Nathan the prophet, the metropolitan Chrysostom, and Anselm, and that Donatus who deigned to set his hand to the first art;

- *Natan profeta* ‘Nathan prophet’ → “Nathan the prophet”: the appositive takes an article in English.
- *e il metropolitano* ‘and the metropolitan’ → “the metropolitan”: the conjunction is omitted.
- *alla prim' arte degnò por la mano* ‘to the first art deigned to set the hand’ → “deigned to set his hand to the first art”: the phrase naming the art moves to the end, and the definite article before the body part becomes a possessive.

> 139 Rabano è qui, e lucemi da lato  
> 140 il Calabrese abate Gioacchino,  
> 141 di spirito profetico dotato.  

Rabanus is here, and there shineth at my side the Calabrian abbot Joachim, dowed with prophetic spirit.

- *lucemi da lato* ‘shines to me at the side’ → “there shineth at my side”: “there” introduces the postponed subject, and the pronoun attached to the verb becomes a possessive within the phrase of place.
- *di spirito profetico dotato* ‘with spirit prophetic endowed’ → “dowed with prophetic spirit”: the participle precedes its complement, and the adjective precedes its noun.

> 142 Ad inveggiar cotanto paladino  
> 143 mi mosse la infiammata cortesia  
> 144 di fra Tommaso, e il discreto latino;  

To emulous speech of so great paladin moved me the enkindled courtesy of brother Thomas and his well-judged discourse,

- *Ad inveggiar* ‘to emulate’ → “To emulous speech”: the infinitive becomes a noun phrase; emulation is expressed by an adjective, and “speech” specifies the form it takes.
- *cotanto paladino* ‘so great a paladin’ → “of so great paladin”: the object of the infinitive becomes an “of” phrase dependent on “speech”.
- *mi mosse* ‘me moved’ → “moved me”: the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *il discreto latino* ‘the discerning language’ → “his well-judged discourse”: a possessive replaces the article, the adjective describes the result of judgment, and ‘language’ is taken in the sense of ‘discourse’.

> 145 e mosse meco questa compagnia.”  

and moved this company with me.”

- *meco questa compagnia* ‘with me this company’ → “this company with me”: the direct object comes before the phrase of accompaniment.

## Summary — Italian Order in English Prose

Wicksteed often brings a delayed subject forward, moves an object pronoun after its verb, or places an adjective before its noun. Thus *Zefiro dolce* precedes the verb as “sweet Zephyr”, and *mi tragge* becomes “draweth me”. Yet he does not uniformly smooth away Dante’s inversions. The subject still follows “began” in “began the sacred millstone to revolve”, and “revolved around us the two garlands” keeps both the verb and the phrase of place before the subject. He also stays close to the repeated pairings of *moto a moto* and *canto a canto*, though he brings their common verb forward and supplies “its” in each pair.

Changes of construction often alter the emphasis without greatly changing the sense. The coordinated verb *e fanno* becomes the participle “making”; conversely, *al suo dove*, literally ‘to its where’, opens out into the clause “to where it was”. An impersonal verb of seeing, *si vedrà*, becomes “shall sight be had”, while the pronominal *volgeansi* needs only the intransitive “revolved”. Tense is flexible too: *refuse*, ‘threw back’, becomes the present “back throweth”.

Some choices make an image more specific: *orto*, ‘garden’, becomes “orchard”, and *lo popol disviato*, ‘the people gone astray’, becomes “the straggling squadron”, carrying the military vocabulary into the noun itself. Elsewhere the wording remains strikingly close, as in “sempiternal roses” and “the sacred athlete”. Additions and omissions need not be large to affect the phrasing: *qui* expands to “here on earth”, while the repeated markers in *sì del cantare e sì del fiammeggiarsi* are gathered into a single “alike”. Two routine differences generally pass unremarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where Italian gives the subject through the verb ending, and drops the Italian definite article before a possessive, as when *la sua mente* becomes “his mind”.
