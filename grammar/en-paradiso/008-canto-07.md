# Paradiso 7

## Lines 1–12 — The Hymn, the Dance, and Dante’s Hesitation

> 1 “*Osanna sanctus Deus Sabaoth,*  
> 2 *superillustrans claritate tua*  
> 3 *felices ignes horum malachoth!*”  

“Hosannah! Holy God of Sabaoth! making lustrous by thy brightness from above the blessed fires of these kingdoms!”

- *Sabaoth* ‘of hosts’ → “of Sabaoth”: the Hebrew word is retained rather than translated, and its relation to “God” is expressed by “of”.
- *superillustrans* ‘illuminating from above’ → “making lustrous” … “from above”: the verb becomes a phrase with a participle and an adjective; the sense of the prefix is given separately, after “by thy brightness”.
- *claritate tua* ‘by brightness thine’ → “by thy brightness”: the possessive precedes the noun, and “by” expresses the Latin ablative.
- *felices ignes* ‘blessed fires’ → “the blessed fires”: the definite article is supplied.

> 4 Così, volgendosi alla nota sua,  
> 5 fu viso a me cantare essa sustanza,  
> 6 sopra la qual doppio lume s' addua:  

So, revolving to its own note, I saw that being sing, on whom the twin lights double one another:

- *volgendosi* ‘turning itself’ → “revolving”: an intransitive participle replaces the reflexive.
- *alla nota sua* ‘to the note of its own’ → “to its own note”: the possessive follows the noun in Italian and precedes it in English.
- *fu viso a me cantare essa sustanza* ‘was seen by me to sing that substance’ → “I saw that being sing”: the passive becomes active, the observer becomes the subject, and the subject of the Italian passive becomes the object of “saw”, placed before the infinitive. “Being” renders the more abstract *sustanza*.
- *sopra la qual* ‘upon which’ → “on whom”: the relative is personal in English.
- *doppio lume s' addua* ‘a double light doubles itself’ → “the twin lights double one another”: the single light described as double becomes a definite plural pair, and the reflexive is interpreted reciprocally.

> 7 ed essa e l' altre mossero a sua danza,  
> 8 e, quasi velocissime faville,  
> 9 mi si velar di subita distanza.  

and it and the others entered on their dance, and like most rapid sparks, veiled them from me by sudden distance.

- *mossero a sua danza* ‘moved to its dance’ → “entered on their dance”: motion towards or to the measure of the dance is rendered as beginning it; the singular possessor gives way to the dancers collectively.
- *quasi* ‘as if’ → “like”: a preposition makes the comparison directly.
- *mi si velar* ‘from me themselves they veiled’ → “veiled them from me”: the verb comes first, the reflexive is expressed by “them” referring back to the dancers, and the dative *mi* becomes a phrase with “from”.

> 10 Io dubitava, e dicea: “Dille, dille,”  
> 11 fra me, “dille,” diceva, “alla mia donna  
> 12 che mi disseta con le dolci stille;”  

I, hesitating, said, “speak to her, speak to her,” within myself, “speak to her,” I said, “to my lady who slaketh my thirst with the sweet drops;”

- *Io dubitava, e dicea* ‘I was in doubt, and was saying’ → “I, hesitating, said”: the first imperfect becomes a participle expressing hesitation, the conjunction drops out, and the second imperfect becomes a simple past.
- *Dille, dille* ‘tell her, tell her’ → “speak to her, speak to her”: the verb of saying with its attached indirect object becomes an intransitive verb with a prepositional phrase.
- *fra me* ‘among myself’ → “within myself”: the idiom is rendered by a preposition of inwardness.
- *dille* ‘tell her’ → “speak to her”: the same change of verb and construction is repeated.
- *diceva* ‘I was saying’ → “I said”: the imperfect again becomes a simple past.
- *mi disseta* ‘quenches me’ → “slaketh my thirst”: what is quenched is named explicitly, and the Italian object pronoun becomes the possessive modifying “thirst”.

## Lines 13–45 — Just Vengeance and the Cross

> 13 ma quella riverenza che s' indonna  
> 14 di tutto me, pur per BE e per ICE,  
> 15 mi richinava come l'uom ch' assonna.  

but that reverence which all o’ermastereth me, though but by Be or Ice, again down-bowed me, as a man who slumbers.

- *che s' indonna di tutto me* ‘which makes itself mistress of all of me’ → “which all o’ermastereth me”: the reflexive verb and its prepositional complement become a transitive verb with a direct object; “all” is moved before the verb.
- *pur per BE e per ICE* ‘even for BE and for ICE’ → “though but by Be or Ice”: the limiting force of *pur* is expressed by “though but”; the repeated preposition is given once, and ‘and’ becomes “or”.
- *mi richinava* ‘me it bent down again’ → “again down-bowed me”: the force of the prefix is expressed separately by “again”, the object follows the verb, and the imperfect becomes a simple past.
- *come l'uom ch' assonna* ‘like the man who grows drowsy’ → “as a man who slumbers”: the generic definite article becomes indefinite, and the onset of sleep is rendered as a state.

> 16 Poco sofferse me cotal Beatrice,  
> 17 e cominciò, raggiandomi d' un riso  
> 18 tal, che nel foco faria l'uom felice:  

Short time Beatrice left me thus; and began, casting the ray upon me of a smile such as would make one blessed though in the flame:

- *Poco sofferse me cotal Beatrice* ‘little did Beatrice suffer me in such a state’ → “Short time Beatrice left me thus”: *poco* is expanded into a phrase of duration, ‘suffer’ in the sense of ‘allow to remain’ becomes “left”, and *cotal*, qualifying *me*, becomes the adverb “thus”. The subject moves from the end to before the verb.
- *raggiandomi d' un riso* ‘irradiating me with a smile’ → “casting the ray upon me of a smile”: the ray implicit in the verb becomes its object, *mi* becomes a prepositional phrase, and the smile is made the source of the ray rather than the means of irradiation.
- *tal, che nel foco faria l'uom felice* ‘such that in the fire it would make the man happy’ → “such as would make one blessed though in the flame”: the result clause becomes a relative construction with “as”; the generic ‘the man’ becomes “one”, and ‘happy’ becomes “blessed”. The phrase of place moves to the end, with “flame” for ‘fire’ and an explicit concessive “though”.

> 19 “Secondo mio infallibile avviso,  
> 20 come giusta vendetta giustamente  
> 21 vengiata fosse, t' ha in pensier miso;  

“According to my thought that cannot err, how just vengeance justly was avenged, hath set thee pondering;

- *infallibile avviso* ‘infallible judgment’ → “thought that cannot err”: the noun becomes “thought”, and its preceding adjective becomes a following relative clause.
- *vengiata fosse* ‘avenged should have been’ → “was avenged”: auxiliary and participle change order, and the Italian imperfect subjunctive passive in the indirect question becomes a simple past indicative passive.
- *t' ha in pensier miso* ‘thee it has in thought put’ → “hath set thee pondering”: the object moves after the verbal group, and the prepositional phrase between auxiliary and participle becomes a following participle denoting an activity.

> 22 ma io ti solverò tosto la mente:  
> 23 e tu ascolta, chè le mie parole  
> 24 di gran sentenza ti faran presente.  

but I will speedily release thy mind; and do thou hearken, for my words shall make thee gift of an august pronouncement.

- *ti solverò tosto la mente* ‘for thee I shall release straightway the mind’ → “I will speedily release thy mind”: the personal dative becomes a possessive modifying “mind”, and the adverb moves between auxiliary and verb.
- *e tu ascolta* ‘and thou listen’ → “and do thou hearken”: the imperative gains the emphatic auxiliary “do”.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *di gran sentenza ti faran presente* ‘of a weighty utterance to thee they will make a gift’ → “shall make thee gift of an august pronouncement”: the gift’s content moves from the beginning to the end, the recipient follows the verb, and *gran* is rendered as “august”.

> 25 Per non soffrire alla virtù che vuole  
> 26 freno a suo prode, quell' uom che non nacque,  
> 27 dannando sè, dannò tutta sua prole;  

Because he not endured for his own good a rein upon the power that wills, that man who ne’er was born, as he condemned himself, condemned his total offspring;

- *Per non soffrire* ‘for not enduring’ → “Because he not endured”: the causal infinitive becomes a finite past clause.
- *alla virtù che vuole freno a suo prode* ‘upon the faculty that wills a rein for his benefit’ → “for his own good a rein upon the power that wills”: the phrase of benefit moves to the front and gains the emphatic “own”; the phrase naming what is restrained now follows “rein”.
- *non nacque* ‘was not born’ → “ne’er was born”: simple negation is made explicitly temporal.
- *dannando sè* ‘condemning himself’ → “as he condemned himself”: the gerund becomes a finite clause introduced by “as”.
- *tutta sua prole* ‘all his offspring’ → “his total offspring”: the quantifier before the possessive becomes an adjective after it.

> 28 onde l' umana specie inferma giacque  
> 29 giù per secoli molti in grande errore,  
> 30 fin ch' al Verbo di Dio di scender piacque,  

wherefore the human race lay sick down there for many an age, in great error, till it pleased the Word of God to descend

- *l' umana specie* ‘the human species’ → “the human race”: “race” replaces ‘species’.
- *inferma giacque* ‘sick it lay’ → “lay sick”: the predicative adjective moves after the verb.
- *giù* ‘down below’ → “down there”: the direction is expressed with an explicit place adverb.
- *per secoli molti* ‘for ages many’ → “for many an age”: the following plural modifier becomes a preceding quantifier with a singular noun.
- *al Verbo di Dio di scender piacque* ‘to the Word of God to descend was pleasing’ → “it pleased the Word of God to descend”: English uses anticipatory “it”, makes the Italian dative the object of “pleased”, and moves the finite verb before both that object and the infinitive.

> 31 u' la natura, che dal suo Fattore  
> 32 s' era allungata, unio a sè in persona  
> 33 con l' atto sol del suo eterno amore.  

where he joined that nature which had gone astray from its Creator to himself, in person, by sole act of his eternal Love.

- *la natura* ‘the nature’ → “that nature”: the definite article becomes a demonstrative.
- *dal suo Fattore s' era allungata* ‘from its Maker had distanced itself’ → “had gone astray from its Creator”: the reflexive expression of distance becomes an intransitive expression of deviation, ‘Maker’ becomes “Creator”, and the prepositional phrase moves after the verb.
- *unio a sè* ‘he united to himself’ → “he joined” … “to himself”: the verb moves ahead of its long direct object while its prepositional complement remains after the object.
- *con l' atto sol* ‘with the act alone’ → “by sole act”: the means is marked by “by”, the article disappears, and the modifier precedes the noun.

> 34 Or drizza il viso a quel ch' or si ragiona:  

Now turn thy sight to what I now discourse:

- *drizza il viso* ‘direct the face’ → “turn thy sight”: “turn” replaces ‘direct’, the face is rendered as the faculty of sight, and a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *a quel ch' or si ragiona* ‘to that which is now being discussed’ → “to what I now discourse”: antecedent and relative are fused in “what”, and the impersonal construction becomes first-person active.

> 35 Questa natura al suo Fattore unita,  
> 36 qual fu creata, fu sincera e buona;  

This nature, so united to its Maker, as it was when created was unalloyed and good;

- *al suo Fattore unita* ‘to its Maker united’ → “so united to its Maker”: the participle precedes its complement, and “so” is supplied.
- *qual fu creata* ‘such as it was created’ → “as it was when created”: “when” makes creation a temporal qualification of “was”.
- *sincera* ‘pure’ → “unalloyed”: purity is expressed in terms of absence of admixture.

> 37 ma per sè stessa fu ella sbandita  
> 38 di Paradiso, però che si torse  
> 39 da via di verità e da sua vita.  

but by its own self had it been exiled from Paradise, because it swerved from the way of truth, and from its proper life.

- *per sè stessa* ‘through itself’ → “by its own self”: the emphatic reflexive is expanded into a possessive phrase.
- *fu ella sbandita* ‘was it banished’ → “had it been exiled”: the simple past passive becomes a past perfect passive.
- *si torse* ‘twisted itself away’ → “it swerved”: the reflexive becomes an intransitive verb of deviation.
- *da via di verità* ‘from a way of truth’ → “from the way of truth”: English supplies a definite article where the Italian has no article.
- *da sua vita* ‘from its life’ → “from its proper life”: “proper” is supplied to qualify the life in question.

> 40 La pena dunque che la croce porse,  
> 41 s' alla natura assunta si misura,  
> 42 nulla giammai sì giustamente morse;  

As for the penalty, then, inflicted by the cross,—if it be measured by the Nature taken on, never did any other bite as justly;

- *La pena dunque* ‘the penalty, then’ → “As for the penalty, then”: “As for” explicitly marks the noun phrase as the topic.
- *che la croce porse* ‘which the cross offered’ → “inflicted by the cross”: the active relative clause becomes a passive participial phrase, with “inflicted” for ‘offered’.
- *s' alla natura assunta si misura* ‘if against the assumed nature it is measured’ → “if it be measured by the Nature taken on”: the Italian *si* construction becomes an explicit passive, with subjunctive “be” for the Italian indicative; the standard of comparison follows the verb, and the participle *assunta* becomes the phrasal “taken on”.
- *nulla giammai sì giustamente morse* ‘none ever so justly bit’ → “never did any other bite as justly”: the negative force passes from the subject to the fronted adverb, requiring auxiliary “did”; “other” makes the comparison explicit, and the phrase of degree moves after the verb.

> 43 e così nulla fu di tanta ingiura,  
> 44 guardando alla persona che sofferse,  
> 45 in che era contratta tal natura.  

and, in like manner, ne’er was any so outrageous if we look to the Person who endured it, in whom this nature was contracted.

- *così* ‘likewise’ → “in like manner”: the adverb is expanded into a prepositional phrase.
- *nulla fu* ‘none was’ → “ne’er was any”: negation passes from the pronoun to a temporal adverb, and the subject follows the verb.
- *di tanta ingiura* ‘of such great wrong’ → “so outrageous”: a prepositional phrase of degree becomes a modified adjective.
- *guardando alla persona* ‘looking to the person’ → “if we look to the Person”: the gerund becomes a finite conditional clause with an explicit first-person plural subject.
- *che sofferse* ‘who suffered’ → “who endured it”: “endured” takes an explicit object absent from the Italian.
- *era contratta tal natura* ‘was contracted such a nature’ → “this nature was contracted”: the subject precedes the verb, and *tal* is taken as pointing back to the nature just mentioned rather than describing its kind.

## Lines 46–78 — Just Vengeance and the Mode of Redemption

> 46 Però d' un atto uscir cose diverse;  

So from one act issued effects apart;

- *Però* ‘therefore’ → “So”: the inference is expressed by a shorter connective.
- *cose diverse* ‘things different’ → “effects apart”: the general noun becomes one denoting results, and the adjective of difference becomes an expression of separateness.

> 47 chè a Dio ed ai Giudei piacque una morte:  

God and the Jews rejoiced in one same death;

- *chè a Dio ed ai Giudei piacque una morte* ‘for to God and to the Jews was pleasing one death’ → “God and the Jews rejoiced in one same death”: the causal connective is omitted; the two to whom the death was pleasing become the subjects of a verb of rejoicing, and the death, formerly the subject, follows “in”. “same” makes explicit the identity implied by ‘one’.

> 48 per lei tremò la terra e il ciel s' aperse.  

thereat shuddered the earth and heaven opened.

- *per lei* ‘on account of it’ → “thereat”: the preposition and pronoun referring back to the death become one adverb.
- *tremò* ‘trembled’ → “shuddered”: a stronger verb is used.
- *il ciel s' aperse* ‘the heaven opened itself’ → “heaven opened”: the article is omitted, and an intransitive verb does the work of the pronominal form.

> 49 Non ti dee oramai parer più forte,  
> 50 quando si dice che giusta vendetta  
> 51 poscia vengiata fu da giusta corte.  

No more, now, should it seem hard saying to thee that just vengeance was afterward avenged by a just court.

- *Non ti dee oramai parer più forte, quando si dice* ‘it ought not to you by now to seem any longer hard, when it is said’ → “No more, now, should it seem hard saying to thee”: the negative and ‘any longer’ are gathered at the front, before “now”; the modal precedes its subject, and the person to whom it seems hard comes later. The impersonal clause introduced by ‘when’ is reduced to “saying”.
- *poscia vengiata fu* ‘afterward avenged was’ → “was afterward avenged”: the auxiliary moves before both adverb and participle.
- *da giusta corte* ‘by just court’ → “by a just court”: the indefinite article is supplied.

> 52 Ma io veggi' or la tua mente ristretta  
> 53 di pensier in pensier dentro ad un nodo,  
> 54 del qual con gran disio solver s' aspetta.  

But now I see thy mind from thought to thought entangled in a knot, from which, with great desire, it release awaiteth.

- *io veggi' or* ‘I see now’ → “now I see”: the adverb moves before the subject.
- *ristretta di pensier in pensier dentro ad un nodo* ‘confined from thought to thought within a knot’ → “from thought to thought entangled in a knot”: the phrase of progression moves before the participle; confinement becomes entanglement, and ‘within’ is shortened to “in”.
- *solver s' aspetta* ‘to loose itself it awaits’ → “it release awaiteth”: the reflexive infinitive becomes a noun, the object of “awaiteth”.

> 55 Tu dici: ‘Ben discerno ciò ch' i' odo;  
> 56 ma, perchè Dio volesse, m' è occulto,  
> 57 a nostra redenzion pur questo modo.’  

Thou sayest, *Yea, what I hear I understand; but why God willed for our redemption this only mode, is hidden from me.*

- *Ben discerno ciò ch' i' odo* ‘well I discern that which I hear’ → “Yea, what I hear I understand”: the emphatic adverb becomes a separate affirmation; the object clause comes first, ‘that which’ contracts to “what”, and discerning becomes understanding.
- *perchè Dio volesse* ‘why God should will’ → “why God willed”: the imperfect subjunctive of the indirect question becomes a simple past.
- *m' è occulto* ‘to me is hidden’ → “is hidden from me”: the predicate is deferred until the question is complete, and the Italian dative becomes a phrase with “from” after the participle.
- *a nostra redenzion* ‘to our redemption’ → “for our redemption”: the preposition explicitly marks purpose.
- *pur questo modo* ‘just this mode’ → “this only mode”: the restricting word is placed between demonstrative and noun.

> 58 Questo decreto, frate, sta sepulto  
> 59 agli occhi di ciascuno, il cui ingegno  
> 60 nella fiamma d' amor non è adulto.  

This decree, my brother, is buried from the eyes of everyone whose wit is not matured within love’s flame.

- *frate* ‘brother’ → “my brother”: a possessive is supplied in the address.
- *sta sepulto agli occhi* ‘lies buried to the eyes’ → “is buried from the eyes”: the verb of remaining in a state becomes a copula, and “from” expresses concealment where Italian specifies those to whose sight the decree is hidden.
- *il cui ingegno* ‘the wit of whom’ → “whose wit”: the possessive relative does not take a separate article in English.
- *nella fiamma d' amor non è adulto* ‘in the flame of love is not full-grown’ → “is not matured within love’s flame”: the predicate comes first, with a participial expression for the adjective; ‘in’ becomes “within”, and the possessor precedes the noun.

> 61 Veramente, però ch' a questo segno  
> 62 molto si mira, e poco si discerne,  
> 63 dirò perchè tal modo fu più degno.  

But since this target much is aimed at, and discerned but little, I will declare why such mode was more worthy.

- *Veramente* ‘truly’ → “But”: the adverb is taken in its adversative rather than its affirming sense.
- *a questo segno molto si mira, e poco si discerne* ‘at this mark one aims much, and little one discerns’ → “this target much is aimed at, and discerned but little”: “target” specifies the kind of mark; the impersonal constructions become coordinated passives with a common subject, leaving “at” after the first participle. “little” moves after the second participle and gains the limiter “but”.
- *dirò* ‘I shall say’ → “I will declare”: a more formal verb of statement is used.

> 64 La divina bontà, che da sè sperne  
> 65 ogni livore, ardendo in sè scintilla  
> 66 sì, che dispiega le bellezze eterne.  

The divine excellence, which spurns all envy from it, burning within itself shooteth such sparkles out as to display the eternal beauties.

- *bontà* ‘goodness’ → “excellence”: the noun is rendered in the broader sense of worth.
- *da sè sperne ogni livore* ‘from itself spurns every rancour’ → “spurns all envy from it”: the phrase of exclusion moves to the end and loses its reflexive form; ‘every’ becomes the mass quantifier “all”, and the feeling is specified as envy.
- *ardendo in sè* ‘burning in itself’ → “burning within itself”: the preposition gives greater emphasis to interiority.
- *scintilla sì, che dispiega* ‘sparkles so that it unfolds’ → “shooteth such sparkles out as to display”: the verb of sparkling becomes a verb of emission with a plural noun as object; the degree expressed by ‘so’ is attached to that noun as “such”. The finite result clause becomes an infinitive, with displaying in place of unfolding.

> 67 Ciò che da lei senza mezzo distilla  
> 68 non ha poi fine, perchè non si move  
> 69 la sua imprenta, quand' ella sigilla.  

That which distilleth from it without mean, thereafter hath no end; because its imprint may not be removed when it hath stamped the seal.

- *da lei senza mezzo distilla* ‘from it without intermediary distils’ → “distilleth from it without mean”: the verb moves before both prepositional phrases; “mean” is used in the sense of an intermediary.
- *non ha poi fine* ‘has not thereafter an end’ → “thereafter hath no end”: the adverb comes first, and negation attaches to the noun instead of the verb.
- *non si move la sua imprenta* ‘does not move its imprint’ → “its imprint may not be removed”: the postposed subject comes first; the pronominal verb of movement becomes a passive of removal with an added modal.
- *quand' ella sigilla* ‘when it seals’ → “when it hath stamped the seal”: the simple present becomes a completed action, and the single verb is expanded into a verb and an explicit object.

> 70 Ciò che da essa senza mezzo piove  
> 71 libero è tutto, perchè non soggiace  
> 72 alla virtute delle cose nuove.  

That which down raineth from it without mean, is all free, because not subject to the power of changing things.

- *da essa senza mezzo piove* ‘from it without intermediary rains’ → “down raineth from it without mean”: the verb moves before both prepositional phrases; “down” makes the direction explicit.
- *libero è tutto* ‘free is wholly’ → “is all free”: the copula comes first and the degree word precedes the adjective.
- *non soggiace* ‘it is not subject’ → “not subject”: the finite verb of subjection becomes an adjective without an expressed copula.
- *delle cose nuove* ‘of the new things’ → “of changing things”: the article is omitted, and ‘new’ is interpreted as denoting things subject to change.

> 73 Più l' è conforme, e però più le piace;  
> 74 chè l' ardor santo, ch' ogni cosa raggia,  
> 75 nella più simigliante è più vivace.  

It is more close conformed to it, therefore more pleasing to it; for the sacred glow that rayeth over everything, in that most like itself is the most living.

- *Più l' è conforme* ‘more to it is conformable’ → “It is more close conformed to it”: the degree expression follows the copula and gains “close”; the adjective becomes a participle, with its complement placed last.
- *e però più le piace* ‘and therefore more it pleases it’ → “therefore more pleasing to it”: the conjunction disappears, and the finite verb becomes a predicate sharing the earlier copula, with the recipient expressed by “to”.
- *l' ardor santo* ‘the ardour holy’ → “the sacred glow”: the adjective moves before the noun, and the words for holiness and heat become words for sacredness and radiance.
- *ch' ogni cosa raggia* ‘that irradiates each thing’ → “that rayeth over everything”: instead of preceding the verb as its direct object, the things irradiated follow “over” as a single collective pronoun.
- *nella più simigliante* ‘in the most similar one’ → “in that most like itself”: “that” stands for the understood noun, and “itself” supplies the term of comparison.
- *è più vivace* ‘is more alive’ → “is the most living”: the comparative is rendered as a superlative.

> 76 Di tutte queste cose s' avvantaggia  
> 77 l' umana creatura, e, s' una manca,  
> 78 di sua nobilità convien che caggia.  

All these points of vantage hath the human creature, and should one fail, needs must it fall from its nobility.

- *Di tutte queste cose s' avvantaggia l' umana creatura* ‘of all these things takes advantage the human creature’ → “All these points of vantage hath the human creature”: the prepositional complement becomes a direct object; the sense of advantage passes from the pronominal verb into “points of vantage”, leaving possession as the verb.
- *s' una manca* ‘if one is lacking’ → “should one fail”: modal inversion replaces the conditional conjunction and indicative; being lacking becomes failing.
- *di sua nobilità convien che caggia* ‘from its nobility it is necessary that it fall’ → “needs must it fall from its nobility”: the impersonal expression with a subjunctive clause becomes an emphatic modal construction; the phrase naming what is lost moves to the end.

## Lines 79–111 — Sin and the Restoration of Human Dignity

> 79 Solo il peccato è quel che la disfranca,  
> 80 e falla dissimile al sommo bene,  
> 81 per che del lume suo poco s' imbianca;  

Sin only is the thing that doth disfranchise it, and maketh it unlike to the highest good, so that its light the less doth brighten it;

- *Solo il peccato* ‘only sin’ → “Sin only”: the limiting word moves after the noun, and the generic article drops out.
- *quel che* ‘that which’ → “the thing that”: the demonstrative is expanded into a noun phrase.
- *la disfranca* ‘it disfranchises’ → “doth disfranchise it”: the object follows the verb, and the simple present is rendered with an auxiliary.
- *al sommo bene* ‘to the supreme good’ → “to the highest good”: the adjective is rendered by an English superlative of degree.
- *per che* ‘wherefore’ → “so that”: the relative expression becomes a conjunction of result.
- *del lume suo poco s' imbianca* ‘with its light it is little whitened’ → “its light the less doth brighten it”: the light becomes the subject and what receives it the object; whitening becomes brightening, and the absolute *poco* becomes comparative.

> 82 ed in sua dignità mai non riviene,  
> 83 se non riempie dove colpa vota,  
> 84 contra mal dilettar, con giuste pene.  

and to its dignity it ne’er may come again, except it fill again where fault hath made a void, against the ill delight setting just penalty.

- *in sua dignità mai non riviene* ‘into its dignity it never returns’ → “to its dignity it ne’er may come again”: the verb of return is unpacked, the indicative acquires a modal, and the two Italian marks of negation yield one English negative.
- *se non riempie* ‘if it does not refill’ → “except it fill again”: the negative condition becomes an exception with a subjunctive, and the prefix of the verb is rendered separately.
- *dove colpa vota* ‘where fault empties’ → “where fault hath made a void”: the present becomes a perfect, and the result of the emptying is expressed by a noun.
- *contra mal dilettar* ‘against evil delighting’ → “against the ill delight”: the substantival infinitive becomes a noun with a definite article.
- *con giuste pene* ‘with just penalties’ → “setting just penalty”: the prepositional phrase becomes a participial one, and the plural becomes singular.

> 85 Vostra natura, quando peccò tota  
> 86 nel seme suo, da queste dignitadi,  
> 87 come da Paradiso fu remota;  

Your nature, when it sinned in its totality in its first seed, from these dignities, even as from Paradise, was parted;

- *tota* ‘whole’ → “in its totality”: the adjective agreeing with *natura* becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *nel seme suo* ‘in its seed’ → “in its first seed”: the possessive precedes the noun, and “first” is supplied to specify which seed is meant.
- *come* ‘as’ → “even as”: the comparison is reinforced.
- *fu remota* ‘was removed’ → “was parted”: removal is rendered as separation.

> 88 nè ricovrar poteansi, se tu badi  
> 89 ben sottilmente, per alcuna via,  
> 90 senza passar per l' un di questi guadi:  

nor might they be recovered, if thou look right keenly, by any way save passing one or the other of these fords:

- *ricovrar poteansi* ‘recovered could they be’ → “might they be recovered”: the modal precedes the infinitive, and the postposed passive *si* construction becomes an English personal passive.
- *se tu badi ben sottilmente* ‘if thou heed very subtly’ → “if thou look right keenly”: attention is expressed as looking, the intensifier is rendered by “right”, and subtle discrimination as keenness.
- *per alcuna via* ‘through any way’ → “by any way”: the preposition expresses means rather than passage.
- *senza passar per* ‘without passing through’ → “save passing”: the negative qualification becomes an exception; “passing” takes a direct object.
- *l' un di questi guadi* ‘one of these fords’ → “one or the other of these fords”: the alternative implicit in the singular choice is made explicit.

> 91 o che Dio, solo per sua cortesia,  
> 92 dimesso avesse, o che l' uom per sè isso  
> 93 avesse satisfatto a sua follia.  

either that God, of his sole courtesy, should have remitted; or that man should of himself have given satisfaction for his folly.

- *solo per sua cortesia* ‘solely through his courtesy’ → “of his sole courtesy”: the adverb becomes an adjective modifying the noun, and the preposition changes.
- *dimesso avesse* ‘remitted had’ → “should have remitted”: the auxiliary precedes the participle, and the pluperfect subjunctive is rendered with a modal.
- *l' uom per sè isso avesse satisfatto* ‘man by his very self had made satisfaction’ → “man should of himself have given satisfaction”: the generic article drops out, the emphatic pronoun is absorbed into “of himself”, and the modal rendering of the subjunctive is split around that phrase. The verb becomes a verb and noun.
- *a sua follia* ‘to his folly’ → “for his folly”: English marks what satisfaction is given for rather than construing it with ‘to’.

> 94 Ficca mo l' occhio per entro l' abisso  
> 95 dell'eterno consiglio, quanto puoi  
> 96 al mio parlar distrettamente fisso.  

Fix now thine eye within the abyss of the eternal counsel, as close attached as e’er thou mayest to my discourse.

- *Ficca* ‘plant’ → “Fix”: the forceful verb is rendered by the usual English verb for directing the eye.
- *l' occhio* ‘the eye’ → “thine eye”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *per entro* ‘right into’ → “within”: direction into the interior becomes position within it.
- *quanto puoi al mio parlar distrettamente fisso* ‘as much as thou canst, on my speaking closely fixed’ → “as close attached as e’er thou mayest to my discourse”: the comparison is built around the degree of attachment, and “e’er” is supplied. The phrase naming the object of attention moves to the end; the substantival infinitive becomes a noun.

> 97 Non potea l' uomo nei termini suoi  
> 98 mai satisfar, per non poter ir giuso  
> 99 con umiltate, ubbidiendo poi,  
> 100 quanto disubbidiendo intese ir suso;  

Man had not power, within his own boundaries, ever to render satisfaction; since he might not go in humbleness by after-obedience so deep down as in disobedience he had framed to exalt himself on high;

- *Non potea l' uomo* ‘could not man’ → “Man had not power”: the generic noun loses its article and precedes the predicate; the modal becomes a verb with a noun object.
- *nei termini suoi* ‘within his limits’ → “within his own boundaries”: the possessive moves before the noun and is strengthened by “own”; limits are expressed as boundaries.
- *mai satisfar* ‘ever to satisfy’ → “ever to render satisfaction”: the infinitive becomes a verb with a noun object.
- *per non poter ir giuso* ‘for not being able to go down’ → “since he might not go” … “so deep down”: the causal infinitive becomes a finite clause; the direction of movement is postponed and expanded into an expression of depth anticipating the comparison.
- *con umiltate* ‘with humility’ → “in humbleness”: an accompanying quality is expressed with “in”.
- *ubbidiendo poi* ‘obeying afterwards’ → “by after-obedience”: the gerund becomes a noun governed by a preposition of means, and the adverb becomes the first element of a compound.
- *quanto disubbidiendo* ‘as much as by disobeying’ → “as in disobedience”: the degree of the comparison is expressed earlier, and the gerund becomes a prepositional phrase with a noun.
- *intese ir suso* ‘he intended to go up’ → “he had framed to exalt himself on high”: the simple past becomes a pluperfect; going up becomes a reflexive act of exaltation, with the upward direction also expressed separately.

> 101 e questa è la cagion perchè l' uom fue  
> 102 da poter satisfar per sè dischiuso.  

and this the cause why from the power to render satisfaction by himself man was shut off.

- *è* ‘is’ → “this the cause”: the copula between demonstrative and noun is unexpressed.
- *l' uom fue … dischiuso* ‘man was … excluded’ → “man was shut off”: the generic article drops out, the separated parts of the passive are brought together, and the subject and passive follow the phrase specifying what man was excluded from.
- *da poter satisfar per sè* ‘from being able to satisfy by himself’ → “from the power to render satisfaction by himself”: the modal infinitive becomes a noun, and the infinitive dependent on it becomes a verb with a noun object.

> 103 Dunque a Dio convenia con le vie sue  
> 104 riparar l' uomo a sua intera vita,  
> 105 dico con l' una, o ver con ambo e due.  

Wherefore needs must God with his own ways reinstate man in his unmaimed life, I mean with one way or with both the two.

- *a Dio convenia* ‘it behoved God’ → “needs must God”: the impersonal imperfect with a dative becomes a personal construction with a modal of necessity.
- *con le vie sue* ‘with his ways’ → “with his own ways”: the possessive moves before the noun and gains emphasis.
- *riparar l' uomo a sua intera vita* ‘to restore man to his whole life’ → “reinstate man in his unmaimed life”: the modal takes a bare infinitive, the generic article drops out, and reinstatement is construed with “in” rather than ‘to’; wholeness is expressed as absence of injury.
- *dico* ‘I say’ → “I mean”: the verb of saying becomes one of clarification.
- *con l' una* ‘with the one’ → “with one way”: the noun understood in Italian is supplied instead of the article.
- *o ver* ‘or indeed’ → “or”: the reinforced alternative is expressed by the conjunction alone.
- *con ambo e due* ‘with both and two’ → “with both the two”: the conjunction of the emphatic Italian expression gives way to an article.

> 106 Ma perchè l' opra è tanto più gradita  
> 107 dell' operante, quanto più appresenta  
> 108 della bontà del core ond' è uscita;  

But because the doer’s deed is the more gracious the more it doth present us of the heart’s goodness whence it issued,

- *l' opra … dell' operante* ‘the work … of the one working’ → “the doer’s deed”: the delayed genitive is placed before its noun; the substantival participle becomes an agent noun, and the related English nouns preserve the Italian verbal echo.
- *è tanto più gradita … quanto più* ‘is so much more pleasing … by how much more’ → “is the more gracious the more”: the paired measures of degree become paired English comparatives; being pleasing or welcome becomes being gracious.
- *appresenta* ‘it presents’ → “it doth present us”: the simple present is rendered with an auxiliary, and a recipient is supplied.
- *della bontà del core* ‘of the goodness of the heart’ → “of the heart’s goodness”: the dependent genitive becomes a prenominal possessive.
- *ond' è uscita* ‘from which it has come out’ → “whence it issued”: the compound past becomes a simple past, and coming out is expressed by a single verb.

> 109 la divina bontà, che il mondo imprenta,  
> 110 di proceder per tutte le sue vie  
> 111 a rilevarvi suso fu contenta;  

the divine Goodness which doth stamp the world, deigned to proceed on all his ways to lift you up again;

- *il mondo imprenta* ‘the world it stamps’ → “doth stamp the world”: the object moves after the verb, and the simple present is rendered with an auxiliary.
- *di proceder per tutte le sue vie* ‘to proceed by all its ways’ → “to proceed on all his ways”: the phrase follows rather than precedes the governing predicate; the ways are construed with “on”, and the possessive refers personally to God in the masculine.
- *a rilevarvi suso* ‘to raise you again upwards’ → “to lift you up again”: the attached object becomes a separate pronoun, and the sense of the prefix is expressed after the directional adverb.
- *fu contenta* ‘was pleased’ → “deigned”: the copula and predicative adjective become a single verb implying gracious willingness, moved before the infinitives.

## Lines 112–144 — Redemption and the Origins of Life

> 112 nè tra l' ultima notte e il primo die  
> 113 sì alto e sì magnifico processo,  
> 114 o per l' una o per l' altra fu o fie:  

nor between the last night and the first day was, nor shall be, so lofty and august a progress made on one or on the other;

- *sì alto e sì magnifico processo* ‘so lofty and so magnificent a proceeding’ → “so lofty and august a progress”: the noun phrase follows rather than precedes the verbs; the second *sì* is not repeated, *magnifico* becomes “august”, and *processo* is rendered as “progress”.
- *o per l' una o per l' altra* ‘either by the one or by the other’ → “on one or on the other”: the initial alternative marker and the first article disappear; “on” replaces the preposition of means or route.
- *fu o fie* ‘was or shall be’ → “was, nor shall be,” and “made”: the verbs move forward, the second alternative takes up the opening negative, and the supplied participle gives both verbs a passive construction.

> 115 chè più largo fu Dio a dar sè stesso,  
> 116 a far l' uom sufficiente a rilevarsi,  
> 117 che s'egli avesse sol da sè dimesso;  

for more generous was God in giving of himself to make man able to uplift himself again, than had he only of himself granted remission;

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a coordinating explanation replaces the subordinating conjunction.
- *più largo* ‘more liberal’ → “more generous”: the adjective of open-handedness is rendered by a synonym.
- *a dar sè stesso* ‘in giving himself’ → “in giving of himself”: the gerund follows “in”, and the direct object becomes an *of*-phrase.
- *a far l' uom sufficiente* ‘to make man sufficient’ → “to make man able”: an adjective of capacity replaces one of sufficiency.
- *a rilevarsi* ‘to raise himself again’ → “to uplift himself again”: the verb becomes “uplift”, with the sense of its prefix expressed separately by “again”.
- *se egli avesse sol da sè dimesso* ‘if he had only of himself remitted’ → “had he only of himself granted remission”: conditional inversion replaces *se*; the act expressed by the participle is recast as a verb with a noun object.

> 118 e tutti gli altri modi erano scarsi  
> 119 alla giustizia, se il Figliuol di Dio  
> 120 non fosse umiliato ad incarnarsi.  

and all other modes fell short of justice, except the Son of God had humbled him to become flesh.

- *tutti gli altri modi* ‘all the other modes’ → “all other modes”: the definite article is dropped.
- *erano scarsi alla giustizia* ‘were insufficient for justice’ → “fell short of justice”: a verbal idiom replaces the copula and adjective, changing the preposition with them.
- *se il Figliuol di Dio non fosse umiliato* ‘if the Son of God had not been humbled’ → “except the Son of God had humbled him”: the negative condition becomes an exception; the humbled state is expressed by an active verb with “him” in the sense of ‘himself’.
- *ad incarnarsi* ‘to incarnate himself’ → “to become flesh”: the reflexive verb is unpacked as a change of state.

> 121 Or, per empierti bene ogni disio,  
> 122 ritorno a dichiarare in alcun loco,  
> 123 perchè tu veggi lì così com' io.  

Now, to fill full for thee every desire, I go back to explain a certain passage, that thou may’st there discern e’en as do I.

- *per empierti bene ogni disio* ‘in order to fill for thee well every desire’ → “to fill full for thee every desire”: the infinitive alone marks purpose, the attached dative becomes “for thee”, and the adverb becomes the resultative “full”.
- *in alcun loco* ‘in a certain place’ → “a certain passage”: the place is understood as a passage of discourse and becomes the direct object of “explain”.
- *perchè tu veggi lì* ‘in order that thou see there’ → “that thou may’st there discern”: the purpose subjunctive is expressed with a modal; “discern” gives the intellectual sense of seeing, and the adverb precedes it.
- *così com' io* ‘just as I’ → “e’en as do I”: “e’en” supplies the emphasis of the comparison, and the elliptical second verb is represented by “do” before the pronoun.

> 124 Tu dici: ‘Io veggio l' acqua, io veggio il foco,  
> 125 l' aer e la terra, e tutte lor misture  
> 126 venire a corruzione, e durar poco’;  

Thou sayest: *I see the water, I see the fire, the air, the earth, and all their combinations meet their dissolution and endure but little*;

- *l' aer e la terra* ‘the air and the earth’ → “the air, the earth”: the conjunction between the two nouns is omitted.
- *tutte lor misture* ‘all their mixtures’ → “all their combinations”: the noun for things mixed is rendered by the more general “combinations”.
- *venire a corruzione* ‘come to corruption’ → “meet their dissolution”: “meet” takes a direct object in place of the prepositional complement, “dissolution” replaces ‘corruption’, and a possessive is supplied.
- *durar poco* ‘to last little’ → “endure but little”: “endure” replaces ‘last’, and “but” makes the limitation explicit.

> 127 e queste cose pur fur creature;  
> 128 per che, se ciò ch' ho detto è stato vero,  
> 129 esser dovrien da corruzion sicure.  

and yet these things were creatures, so that if that which I have said to thee be true, they ought to be secure against corruption.

- *queste cose pur* ‘these things nevertheless’ → “yet these things”: the adversative moves before the subject.
- *per che* ‘on account of which’ → “so that”: the relative phrase becomes a conjunction of consequence.
- *ciò ch' ho detto* ‘that which I have said’ → “that which I have said to thee”: the listener is made explicit.
- *è stato vero* ‘has been true’ → “be true”: a present subjunctive replaces the compound past indicative.
- *esser dovrien da corruzion sicure* ‘to be they would have to from corruption secure’ → “they ought to be secure against corruption”: the modal precedes its infinitive, the adjective precedes its complement, and “against” replaces ‘from’.

> 130 Gli angeli, frate, e il paese sincero  
> 131 nel qual tu sei, dir si posson creati,  
> 132 sì come sono, in loro essere intero;  

The Angels, brother, and the unsullied country in which thou art, may be declared to be created, even as they are, in their entire being;

- *il paese sincero* ‘the country pure’ → “the unsullied country”: the adjective moves before the noun and expresses purity as absence of a stain.
- *dir si posson creati* ‘can be said created’ → “may be declared to be created”: the infinitive no longer precedes the modal, passive *si* is rendered by an English passive, and “declared” takes an explicit infinitival complement.
- *in loro essere intero* ‘in their being entire’ → “in their entire being”: the adjective precedes its noun.

> 133 ma gli elementi che tu hai nomati  
> 134 e quelle cose che di lor si fanno,  
> 135 da creata virtù sono informati.  

but the elements which thou hast named and all the things compounded of them, have by created virtue been informed.

- *quelle cose che di lor si fanno* ‘those things which of them are made’ → “all the things compounded of them”: “all the” replaces the demonstrative; the relative clause with passive *si* becomes a participial phrase, and “compounded” specifies the manner of making.
- *da creata virtù sono informati* ‘by created virtue are informed’ → “have by created virtue been informed”: the present passive becomes a perfect passive, with the agent phrase placed inside the verb group.

> 136 Creata fu la materia ch' egli hanno,  
> 137 creata fu la virtù informante  
> 138 in queste stelle, che intorno a lor vanno.  

Created was the matter which they hold, created was the informing virtue in these stars which sweep around them.

- *ch' egli hanno* ‘which they have’ → “which they hold”: “hold” replaces the ordinary verb of possession.
- *la virtù informante* ‘the virtue informing’ → “the informing virtue”: the participial adjective precedes its noun.
- *che intorno a lor vanno* ‘which around them go’ → “which sweep around them”: the verb moves before its complement, and “sweep” gives a more specific motion than ‘go’.

> 139 L' anima d' ogni bruto e delle piante  
> 140 di complession potenziata tira  
> 141 lo raggio e il moto delle luci sante.  

The life of every brute and of the plants is drawn from compounds having potency, by the ray and movement of the sacred lights.

- *L' anima* ‘the soul’ → “The life”: the noun is rendered in the sense of an animating life rather than as ‘soul’.
- *di complession potenziata* ‘from a composition endowed with potency’ → “from compounds having potency”: the generic singular becomes plural, the participial modifier becomes a phrase with “having”, and the source follows the verb.
- *tira* ‘draws’ → “is drawn”: the active verb becomes passive, making the initial object the English subject.
- *lo raggio e il moto* ‘the ray and the movement’ → “by the ray and movement”: the postposed subject becomes a *by*-phrase, with one article shared by both nouns.
- *delle luci sante* ‘of the lights holy’ → “of the sacred lights”: “sacred” replaces ‘holy’ and precedes the noun.

> 142 Ma vostra vita senza mezzo spira  
> 143 la somma beninanza, e la innamora  
> 144 di sè, sì che poi sempre la disira.  

But your life is breathed without mean by the supreme beneficence who maketh it enamoured of itself, so that thereafter it doth ever long for it.

- *senza mezzo spira* ‘without intermediary breathes’ → “is breathed without mean”: the active verb becomes passive, with “your life” as subject rather than object; the phrase denoting absence of mediation follows the verb.
- *la somma beninanza* ‘the supreme goodness’ → “by the supreme beneficence”: the Italian subject becomes the agent of the passive, and “beneficence” renders goodness as the disposition to confer good.
- *e la innamora di sè* ‘and makes it fall in love with itself’ → “who maketh it enamoured of itself”: a relative clause replaces coordination, and the single causative verb becomes “maketh” with an object and a participial complement.
- *sì che poi sempre la disira* ‘so that afterwards it always desires it’ → “so that thereafter it doth ever long for it”: “thereafter” and “ever” render the two time adverbs; the supplied auxiliary precedes “ever”, and the direct-object verb becomes “long for”, with its object following rather than preceding it.

## Lines 145–148 — An Inference of Resurrection

> 145 E quinci puoi argomentare ancora  
> 146 vostra resurrezion, se tu ripensi  
> 147 come l'umana carne fessi allora  
> 148 che li primi parenti intrambo fensi.”  

And hence thou further may’st infer your resurrection, if thou think again how was the making of the human flesh then when the first parents both of them were formed.”

- *puoi argomentare ancora* ‘thou canst infer further’ → “thou further may’st infer”: “may’st” expresses possibility, and “further” moves from after the infinitive to before the modal verb.
- *ripensi* ‘thou reconsiderest’ → “thou think again”: the sense of the prefix is expressed by a separate adverb, and the Italian present indicative becomes an uninflected English subjunctive after “if”.
- *come l'umana carne fessi* ‘how the human flesh was made’ → “how was the making of the human flesh”: the passive sense of the Italian verb is recast with the noun “making”; the flesh, originally the subject, becomes the complement of “of”, and “was” comes before the new subject.
- *intrambo* ‘both’ → “both of them”: the pronoun phrase makes the reference back to “the first parents” explicit.
- *fensi* ‘were made’ → “were formed”: the Italian repeats the verb used of the flesh, now in the plural; the English varies the verb.

## Summary — Close Echoes and Changes of Construction

Wicksteed often changes the order of a phrase without changing its terms: *alla nota sua* becomes “to its own note”, and *inferma giacque* becomes “lay sick”. He does not invariably smooth away an inversion, however: “Created was the matter” keeps the participle, verb, and subject in the order of *Creata fu la materia*, and “created was” repeats the opening just as the Italian does. The close verbal echo of *giusta vendetta giustamente vengiata* likewise survives in “just vengeance justly was avenged”.

Changes of construction can shift the emphasis more than changes of order. In *fu viso a me cantare essa sustanza*, the singer is the subject of a passive; in “I saw that being sing”, the observer becomes the subject. Conversely, the active *tira* becomes “is drawn”, with the source of the action named in a phrase beginning with “by”. A gerund may become a finite clause, as *dannando sè* becomes “as he condemned himself”; a verb may also yield a noun, as *ubbidiendo poi* (‘obeying afterwards’) becomes “by after-obedience”.

The choice of a word sometimes makes a relation more explicit: *cose diverse* (‘different things’) becomes “effects apart”, identifying the things as results. Elsewhere the English unpacks what the Italian leaves within a verb: *mi disseta* becomes “slaketh my thirst”. Wicksteed supplies “first” in “its first seed” for *nel seme suo*, but leaves the explanatory *chè* before *a Dio ed ai Giudei* untranslated. Two quieter adjustments recur throughout: English supplies subject pronouns where Italian identifies the subject by the verb ending alone, as in *dirò*, “I will declare”, and omits the Italian article before a possessive, as in *le mie parole*, “my words”.
