# Paradiso 20

## Lines 1–15 — The Eagle Falls Silent and the Lights Sing

> 1 Quando colui che tutto il mondo alluma  
> 2 dell' emisperio nostro sì discende,  
> 3 che il giorno d' ogni parte si consuma,  

When he who doth illumine all the world descendeth so from our hemisphere that day on every side is done away,

- *tutto il mondo alluma* ‘all the world illumines’ → “doth illumine all the world”: the simple present is expressed with an auxiliary, and the verb precedes its object.
- *dell' emisperio nostro sì discende* ‘from the hemisphere of ours so descends’ → “descendeth so from our hemisphere”: the verb moves ahead of both the adverb and the phrase of departure; the possessive moves ahead of the noun.
- *il giorno* ‘the day’ → “day”: the article is dropped.
- *d' ogni parte* ‘from every part’ → “on every side”: a phrase with ‘from’ becomes one of position, and ‘part’ becomes “side”.
- *si consuma* ‘spends itself’ → “is done away”: the Italian middle construction becomes an English passive idiom.

> 4 lo ciel, che sol di lui prima s' accende,  
> 5 subitamente si rifà parvente  
> 6 per molte luci, in che una risplende.  

the heaven which before is kindled by him only, now straightway maketh itself reappear by many lights wherein the one regloweth.

- *sol di lui prima s' accende* ‘only by him before takes fire’ → “before is kindled by him only”: the adverb of time moves to the front, the middle verb becomes passive, and the phrase naming the agent moves after the verb, with “only” last.
- *subitamente* ‘straightway’ → “now straightway”: “now” is supplied.
- *si rifà parvente* ‘itself makes again apparent’ → “maketh itself reappear”: the reflexive follows the verb, and the predicative adjective becomes an infinitive bearing the prefix of repetition.
- *in che* ‘in which’ → “wherein”: preposition and relative are combined in one word.
- *una risplende* ‘one shines’ → “the one regloweth”: the definite article is supplied, and the English brings out a sense of renewed or reflected glowing rather than simply shining.

> 7 E quest' atto del ciel mi venne a mente,  
> 8 come il segno del mondo e dei suoi duci  
> 9 nel benedetto rostro fu tacente;  

And this act of heaven came to my mind when the ensign of the world and of its leaders within its blessed beak was silent;

- *del ciel* ‘of the heaven’ → “of heaven”: the article is dropped.
- *mi venne a mente* ‘to me came to mind’ → “came to my mind”: the dative pronoun before the verb becomes a possessive modifying “mind”.
- *come* ‘as’ → “when”: the conjunction is rendered in its temporal sense.
- *il segno* ‘the sign’ → “the ensign”: the more specific term is chosen.
- *nel benedetto rostro* ‘in the blessed beak’ → “within its blessed beak”: ‘in’ is expanded to “within”, and possession is made explicit in place of the article.
- *fu tacente* ‘was keeping silence’ → “was silent”: the present participle is rendered by an adjective.

> 10 però che tutte quelle vive luci,  
> 11 vie più lucenti, cominciaron canti  
> 12 da mia memoria labili e caduci.  

because all those living lights, far brightlier shining, began songs which from my memory must slip and fall.

- *però che* ‘for the reason that’ → “because”: the causal locution becomes one conjunction.
- *vie più lucenti* ‘far more shining’ → “far brightlier shining”: the comparison is carried by an adverb modifying “shining” rather than by the degree of the adjective itself.
- *da mia memoria labili e caduci* ‘from my memory apt to slip and apt to fall’ → “which from my memory must slip and fall”: the two adjectives qualifying the songs become verbs in a supplied relative clause; “must” presents the liability they express as a necessity.

> 13 O dolce amor, che di riso t' ammanti,  
> 14 quanto parevi ardente in quei flailli  
> 15 ch' avieno spirto sol di pensier santi!  

O sweet love, smile-bemantled, how glowing didst thou seem in those flute holes breathed on only by sacred ponderings!

- *che di riso t' ammanti* ‘who with a smile dost mantle thyself’ → “smile-bemantled”: the relative clause with its active reflexive verb becomes a compound participial adjective; the noun for what serves as the mantle is incorporated into the compound.
- *quanto parevi ardente* ‘how much thou seemedst ardent’ → “how glowing didst thou seem”: “glowing” replaces ‘ardent’ and moves next to the exclamative “how”; the imperfect is expressed by an English past with an auxiliary before the subject.
- *flailli* ‘flutes’ → “flute holes”: the translation specifies the openings rather than naming the instruments as wholes.
- *ch' avieno spirto* ‘that had breath’ → “breathed on”: the relative clause of possession becomes a passive participial phrase, with what had breath represented as receiving it.
- *di pensier santi* ‘of thoughts holy’ → “by sacred ponderings”: the phrase qualifying the breath becomes the agent of the passive; “ponderings” gives ‘thoughts’ a more active sense, and “sacred” precedes its noun.

## Lines 16–48 — The Eagle’s Voice and Eye

> 16 Poscia che i cari e lucidi lapilli,  
> 17 ond' io vidi ingemmato il sesto lume,  
> 18 poser silenzio agli angelici squilli,  

When the dear and shining stones, whereby I saw the sixth heaven gemmed, had imposed silence on the angelic chimes,

- *Poscia che* ‘after’ → “When”: the conjunction marks time without itself specifying ‘after’.
- *lapilli* ‘little stones’ → “stones”: the diminutive is not expressed.
- *ingemmato il sesto lume* ‘gemmed the sixth light’ → “the sixth heaven gemmed”: the object comes before its participle; ‘heaven’ names what the ‘light’ designates.
- *poser silenzio* ‘set silence’ → “had imposed silence”: ‘imposed’ gives the English turn of phrase, and the simple past becomes a pluperfect.
- *agli angelici squilli* ‘to the angelic peals’ → “on the angelic chimes”: the preposition changes with the verb, and ‘peals’ becomes ‘chimes’.

> 19 udir mi parve un mormorar di fiume,  
> 20 che scende chiaro giù di pietra in pietra,  
> 21 mostrando l' ubertà del suo cacume.  

meseemed to hear the murmuring of a river which droppeth clear from rock to rock and showeth the abundance of its source.

- *udir mi parve* ‘to hear it seemed to me’ → “meseemed to hear”: the impersonal verb and its pronoun are fused and precede the infinitive.
- *un mormorar di fiume* ‘a murmuring of river’ → “the murmuring of a river”: the indefinite article becomes definite before ‘murmuring’, and an indefinite article is supplied before ‘river’.
- *scende chiaro giù* ‘descends clear down’ → “droppeth clear”: the verb changes, and the downward direction is not expressed by a separate adverb.
- *di pietra in pietra* ‘from stone to stone’ → “from rock to rock”: ‘rock’ replaces ‘stone’ in both places.
- *mostrando* ‘showing’ → “and showeth”: the gerund becomes a second finite verb joined by a conjunction.
- *cacume* ‘summit’ → “source”: the translation gives the river’s point of origin rather than the height from which it comes.

> 22 E come suono al collo della cetra  
> 23 prende sua forma, e sì come al pertugio  
> 24 della sampogna vento che penetra,  

And as the sound taketh its form in the lute-neck, or at the opening of the pipes the wind that entereth,

- *suono* ‘sound’ → “the sound”: the definite article is supplied.
- *al collo della cetra* ‘at the neck of the cithara’ → “in the lute-neck”: ‘at’ becomes ‘in’, the instrument is called a ‘lute’, and its name is compounded with ‘neck’.
- *prende sua forma* ‘takes its form’ → “taketh its form”: the verb and object are brought forward from after the phrase of place.
- *e sì come* ‘and just as’ → “or”: a disjunction replaces the conjunction and repeated mark of comparison.
- *della sampogna* ‘of the bagpipe’ → “of the pipes”: a singular instrument name is rendered by a plural.
- *vento* ‘wind’ → “the wind”: the definite article is supplied.
- *penetra* ‘penetrates’ → “entereth”: a verb of entry replaces one of penetration.

> 25 così, rimosso d' aspettare indugio,  
> 26 quel mormorar dell' aquila salissi  
> 27 su per lo collo, come fosse bugio.  

so, delay of expectation done away, that murmuring of the eagle rose up through its neck as it were hollow;

- *rimosso d' aspettare indugio* ‘removed, of waiting, delay’ → “delay of expectation done away”: the absolute construction is retained, but the noun comes first and the participle last; the infinitive ‘to wait’ is rendered by the noun ‘expectation’.
- *salissi su per lo collo* ‘ascended up through the neck’ → “rose up through its neck”: ‘rose’ renders the pronominal verb without a pronoun, and the definite article becomes a possessive.

> 28 Fecesi voce quivi, e quindi uscissi  
> 29 per lo suo becco in forma di parole,  
> 30 quali aspettava il core, ov' io le scrissi.  

there it became a voice and issued thence, out from its beak, in form of words, such as the heart awaited, whereon I wrote them.

- *Fecesi voce quivi* ‘made itself voice there’ → “there it became a voice”: the adverb comes first, the reflexive verb becomes a verb of becoming, and an indefinite article is supplied.
- *e quindi uscissi* ‘and thence went out’ → “and issued thence, out”: ‘issued’ renders the pronominal verb without a pronoun; the adverb of origin follows the verb, with ‘out’ carried into the next phrase.
- *per lo suo becco* ‘through its beak’ → “from its beak”: the beak is treated as the point of emergence rather than the passage.
- *quali aspettava il core* ‘such as awaited the heart’ → “such as the heart awaited”: the heart, the postposed subject in Italian, comes before the verb in English.
- *ov' io le scrissi* ‘where I them wrote’ → “whereon I wrote them”: ‘where’ becomes ‘whereon’, and the object pronoun follows the verb.

> 31 “La parte in me che vede, e pate il sole  
> 32 nell' aquile mortali, incominciommi,  
> 33 or fisamente riguardar si vuole,  

“That part in me which seeth and which doth endure the sun in mortal eagles,” it began to me, “must now fixedly be gazed upon,

- *La parte* ‘the part’ → “That part”: a demonstrative replaces the definite article.
- *e pate* ‘and endures’ → “and which doth endure”: the relative pronoun is repeated, and the simple verb is rendered with an auxiliary.
- *nell' aquile mortali* ‘in the eagles mortal’ → “in mortal eagles”: the generic plural loses its article, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *incominciommi* ‘began-to-me’ → “it began to me”: the enclitic indirect object is rendered by a separate prepositional phrase.
- *or fisamente riguardar si vuole* ‘now fixedly to gaze upon is required’ → “must now fixedly be gazed upon”: the construction with *si vuole* and an active infinitive becomes a modal with a passive infinitive; the modal precedes the adverbs.

> 34 perchè dei fochi, ond' io figura fommi,  
> 35 quelli, onde l' occhio in testa mi scintilla,  
> 36 e' di tutti i lor gradi son li sommi.  

for of the fires wherefromout I make my figure, those with which the eye sparkleth in my head, of all their ranks are chief.

- *perchè* ‘because’ → “for”: the causal subordinate clause becomes a coordinate explanation.
- *ond' io figura fommi* ‘whereof I a figure make for myself’ → “wherefromout I make my figure”: the relative adverb is expanded to express the material out of which the figure is made; the object follows the verb, and a possessive takes the place of the reflexive dative.
- *in testa mi scintilla* ‘in the head sparkles for me’ → “sparkleth in my head”: the verb precedes the phrase of place, and the Italian dative is expressed as a possessive with ‘head’.
- *e' di tutti i lor gradi son li sommi* ‘they, of all their ranks, are the highest’ → “of all their ranks are chief”: the resumptive subject pronoun is dropped, and the articulated superlative becomes an unarticled predicate adjective.

> 37 Colui che luce in mezzo per pupilla,  
> 38 fu il cantor dello Spirito Santo,  
> 39 che l' arca traslatò di villa in villa:  

He who shineth midmost, as the pupil, was the singer of the Holy Spirit who bore the ark from city unto city;

- *in mezzo* ‘in the middle’ → “midmost”: a prepositional phrase becomes a single adverb.
- *per pupilla* ‘as pupil’ → “as the pupil”: the definite article is supplied.
- *l' arca traslatò* ‘the ark transferred’ → “bore the ark”: the object follows the verb, and ‘bore’ expresses the carrying rather than the transfer.
- *di villa in villa* ‘from town to town’ → “from city unto city”: ‘city’ is chosen for the older sense of *villa* as a settlement.

> 40 ora conosce il merto del suo canto,  
> 41 in quanto effetto fu del suo consiglio,  
> 42 per lo remunerar ch' è altrettanto.  

now knoweth he the merit of his song, in so far as ’twas the effect of his own counsel, by the remuneration like unto it.

- *in quanto effetto fu del suo consiglio* ‘insofar as effect it was of his counsel’ → “in so far as ’twas the effect of his own counsel”: the copula precedes its predicate, ‘effect’ receives an article, and ‘own’ adds emphasis to the possessive.
- *ch' è altrettanto* ‘which is as much’ → “like unto it”: the finite relative clause becomes an adjectival phrase, and equality of measure is expressed as likeness with an explicit object of comparison.

> 43 Dei cinque, che mi fan cerchio per ciglio,  
> 44 colui, che più al becco mi s' accosta,  
> 45 la vedovella consolò del figlio:  

Of the five who make the eyebrow’s arch, he who doth neighbour closest on the beak consoled the widow for her son;

- *mi fan cerchio per ciglio* ‘make me a circle for an eyebrow’ → “make the eyebrow’s arch”: the dative referring to the speaker disappears, ‘circle’ becomes ‘arch’, and the eyebrow, instead of being what the arrangement serves as, is made the possessor of the arch.
- *più al becco mi s' accosta* ‘draws himself nearer to the beak of mine’ → “doth neighbour closest on the beak”: the pronominal verb of approach becomes a verb of position with an auxiliary; the comparative is made superlative, the phrase naming the beak comes last, and the speaker’s possessive dative is not expressed.
- *la vedovella consolò* ‘the little widow consoled’ → “consoled the widow”: the object follows the verb, and the diminutive is lost.
- *del figlio* ‘for the son’ → “for her son”: the article is rendered by a possessive.

> 46 ora conosce quanto caro costa  
> 47 non seguir Cristo, per l' esperienza  
> 48 di questa dolce vita e dell' opposta.  

now knoweth he how dear it costs Christ not to follow, by his experience of this sweet life and of the opposite.

- *non seguir Cristo* ‘not to follow Christ’ → “Christ not to follow”: the object is brought before the negated infinitive.
- *per l' esperienza* ‘by the experience’ → “by his experience”: a possessive replaces the definite article.

## Lines 49–81 — What the Holy Lights Now Know

> 49 E quel che segue in la circonferenza,  
> 50 di che ragiono, per l' arco superno,  
> 51 morte indugiò per vera penitenza:  

And he who followeth on the circumference whereof I tell, upon the upper arch, death did delay by his true penitence;

- *quel che* ‘that one who’ → “he who”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *in la circonferenza* ‘in the circumference’ → “on the circumference”: the English preposition treats the circumference as a line on which the figure stands.
- *di che ragiono* ‘of which I discourse’ → “whereof I tell”: the relative adverb takes the place of preposition and pronoun, and “tell” replaces ‘discourse’.
- *per l' arco superno* ‘along the arch upper’ → “upon the upper arch”: position replaces direction along the curve, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *indugiò* ‘delayed’ → “did delay”: the simple past is rendered with an auxiliary.
- *per vera penitenza* ‘by true penitence’ → “by his true penitence”: the possessive is supplied.

> 52 ora conosce che il giudizio eterno  
> 53 non si trasmuta, perchè degno preco  
> 54 fa crastino laggiù dell' odierno.  

now knoweth he that the eternal judgment is not transmuted when a worthy prayer giveth unto to-morrow upon earth what was to-day’s.

- *ora conosce* ‘now knows’ → “now knoweth he”: the verb precedes its expressed subject in English.
- *il giudizio eterno* ‘the judgment eternal’ → “the eternal judgment”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *non si trasmuta* ‘does not change’ → “is not transmuted”: the Italian pronominal verb becomes an English passive.
- *perchè* ‘because’ → “when”: the prayer is presented as the occasion rather than the cause.
- *degno preco* ‘worthy prayer’ → “a worthy prayer”: the indefinite article is supplied.
- *fa crastino laggiù dell' odierno* ‘makes a tomorrow down there out of the today’ → “giveth unto to-morrow upon earth what was to-day’s”: making one day out of another becomes a transfer to one day of what belonged to the other; “upon earth” specifies the place indicated by ‘down there’.

> 55 L' altro che segue, con le leggi e meco,  
> 56 sotto buona intenzion che fe' mal frutto,  
> 57 per cedere al pastor si fece Greco:  

The next who followeth, with the laws and me, with good intention that bore evil fruit, to give place to the pastor, made himself a Greek;

- *L' altro* ‘the other’ → “The next”: the distinction between figures is expressed as succession.
- *meco* ‘with me’ → “me”: the English pronoun shares the preposition already used for “the laws”.
- *sotto buona intenzion* ‘under good intention’ → “with good intention”: accompaniment replaces ‘under’.
- *fe' mal frutto* ‘made bad fruit’ → “bore evil fruit”: English uses the idiom of bearing fruit rather than making it.
- *per cedere* ‘in order to yield’ → “to give place”: the bare infinitive expresses purpose, and yielding is rendered by a verb and noun.
- *Greco* ‘Greek’ → “a Greek”: the predicate is made explicitly nominal with an indefinite article.

> 58 ora conosce come il mal, dedutto  
> 59 dal suo bene operar, non gli è nocivo,  
> 60 avvegna che sia il mondo indi distrutto.  

now knoweth he that the ill deduced from his good deed hurteth not him though the world be destroyed thereby.

- *ora conosce* ‘now knows’ → “now knoweth he”: the verb precedes its expressed subject.
- *dal suo bene operar* ‘from his acting well’ → “from his good deed”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun for the deed, and its adverb becomes an adjective.
- *non gli è nocivo* ‘is not harmful to him’ → “hurteth not him”: the copula and adjective become a transitive verb; the indirect object becomes a direct object following the negation.
- *sia il mondo indi distrutto* ‘be the world thereby destroyed’ → “the world be destroyed thereby”: the subject moves before the subjunctive verb, and the adverb moves after the participle.

> 61 E quel che vedi nell' arco declivo  
> 62 Guglielmo fu, cui quella terra plora  
> 63 che piange Carlo e Federico vivo:  

And him thou seest on the down-sloping arch was William, whom that land deploreth which weepeth for that Charles and Frederick live;

- *quel che vedi* ‘that one whom thou seest’ → “him thou seest”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun in the objective case, and the object relative is unexpressed.
- *nell' arco declivo* ‘in the arch sloping down’ → “on the down-sloping arch”: the preposition changes, and the modifier precedes the noun.
- *Guglielmo fu* ‘William was’ → “was William”: the copula precedes the name.
- *che piange Carlo e Federico vivo* ‘which bewails Charles and Frederick alive’ → “which weepeth for that Charles and Frederick live”: the Italian names are objects of lament, with a singular predicate adjective; English makes them the joint subject of a finite plural verb in a clause giving the reason for weeping.

> 64 ora conosce come s' innamora  
> 65 lo ciel del giusto rege, ed al sembiante  
> 66 del suo fulgore il fa vedere ancora.  

now knoweth he how heaven is enamoured of the righteous king, and by the semblance of his glow he maketh it yet seen.

- *ora conosce* ‘now knows’ → “now knoweth he”: the verb precedes its expressed subject.
- *s' innamora lo ciel* ‘falls in love the heaven’ → “heaven is enamoured”: the postposed subject comes first without an article, and a state replaces the pronominal verb of becoming.
- *del giusto rege* ‘of the just king’ → “of the righteous king”: “righteous” gives the adjective a specifically moral force.
- *al sembiante* ‘at the appearance’ → “by the semblance”: the appearance is construed as the means of making something visible.
- *del suo fulgore* ‘of his radiance’ → “of his glow”: “glow” renders the word for brilliance.
- *il fa vedere ancora* ‘makes it be seen still’ → “he maketh it yet seen”: the object pronoun moves after the causative verb, a passive participle replaces the active infinitive with passive sense, and the final adverb moves before the participle.

> 67 Chi crederebbe giù nel mondo errante,  
> 68 che Rifeo Troiano in questo tondo  
> 69 fosse la quinta delle luci sante?  

Who would believe, down in the erring world, the Trojan Ripheus in this circle to be the fifth of the holy lights?

- *nel mondo errante* ‘in the world erring’ → “in the erring world”: the participial adjective precedes the noun.
- *che Rifeo Troiano* ‘that Ripheus the Trojan’ → “the Trojan Ripheus”: the finite clause introduced by ‘that’ gives way to an accusative-and-infinitive construction, and the designation precedes the name.
- *in questo tondo* ‘in this round’ → “in this circle”: the substantivized adjective is rendered by a noun of shape.
- *fosse* ‘were’ → “to be”: the imperfect subjunctive becomes an infinitive after “believe”.
- *delle luci sante* ‘of the lights holy’ → “of the holy lights”: the adjective precedes the noun.

> 70 ora conosce assai di quel che il mondo  
> 71 veder non può della divina grazia,  
> 72 benchè sua vista non discerna il fondo.”  

now knoweth he right much of the divine grace that the world hath no power to see, albeit his sight discerneth not the bottom.”

- *ora conosce* ‘now knows’ → “now knoweth he”: the verb precedes its expressed subject.
- *assai* ‘much’ → “right much”: an intensifier is supplied.
- *di quel che il mondo veder non può della divina grazia* ‘of that which the world see cannot of divine grace’ → “of the divine grace that the world hath no power to see”: English moves grace forward and makes it the antecedent of the relative, dispensing with the separate ‘of that’; the adjective precedes its noun, and inability is expressed by a phrase about power followed by an infinitive.
- *non discerna il fondo* ‘may not discern the bottom’ → “discerneth not the bottom”: English uses the indicative rather than the subjunctive governed by the concessive conjunction, and places the negation after the verb.

> 73 Quale allodetta che in aere si spazia  
> 74 prima cantando, e poi tace contenta  
> 75 dell' ultima dolcezza che la sazia,  

Like to the lark who soareth in the air, first singing and then silent, content with the last sweetness that doth sate her,

- *Quale allodetta* ‘like a little lark’ → “Like to the lark”: English uses the definite article and does not reproduce the diminutive.
- *in aere si spazia* ‘in air ranges freely’ → “soareth in the air”: the pronominal verb of free movement becomes a verb of upward flight; the place follows the verb and gains an article.
- *tace* ‘falls silent’ → “silent”: an adjective replaces the finite verb.
- *dell' ultima dolcezza* ‘of the last sweetness’ → “with the last sweetness”: English uses “with” after “content”.
- *la sazia* ‘her sates’ → “doth sate her”: the auxiliary is supplied and the object pronoun follows the verb.

> 76 tal mi sembiò l' imago della imprenta  
> 77 dell' eterno piacere, al cui disio  
> 78 ciascuna cosa, quale ell' è, diventa.  

so seemed to me the image of the imprint of the eternal pleasure, by longing for whom each thing becometh what it is.

- *tal mi sembiò* ‘such to me seemed’ → “so seemed to me”: the correlative adjective becomes an adverb, and the pronoun becomes a prepositional phrase following the verb.
- *al cui disio* ‘at whose desire’ → “by longing for whom”: English takes the relative as the object of the longing rather than its possessor, and expresses the relation as one of means.
- *quale ell' è, diventa* ‘such as it is, becomes’ → “becometh what it is”: the verb moves before its complement, and a ‘what’ clause replaces the expression of quality.

> 79 Ed avvegna ch' io fossi al dubbiar mio  
> 80 lì quasi vetro allo color che il veste,  
> 81 tempo aspettar tacendo non patio;  

And albeit there I was to my questioning like glass unto the colour which it clothes, yet would it not endure to bide its time in silence;

- *ch' io fossi al dubbiar mio lì* ‘that I were to the doubting of mine there’ → “there I was to my questioning”: “albeit” needs no following ‘that’, and the Italian concessive subjunctive becomes an indicative; the possessive moves before the verbal noun, and doubting is rendered as questioning; the adverb moves forward from after the phrase about doubting to before “I was”.
- *quasi vetro allo color che il veste* ‘like glass to the colour that clothes it’ → “like glass unto the colour which it clothes”: English takes the glass as the subject of clothing and the colour as its object, reversing the relation suggested by the Italian object pronoun.
- *tempo aspettar tacendo non patio* ‘to await a time, keeping silent, it did not endure’ → “yet would it not endure to bide its time in silence”: “yet” marks the return from the concession; the governing verb moves forward and gains the modal “would”; waiting for a time becomes the possessive idiom “bide its time”, and the gerund becomes a prepositional phrase.

## Lines 82–114 — Love and Hope Conquer the Divine Will

> 82 ma della bocca: “Che cose son queste?”  
> 83 mi pinse con la forza del suo peso;  
> 84 per ch' io di corruscar vidi gran feste.  

but from my mouth: “What things are these?” it thrust by force of its own weight, whereat I saw great glee of coruscation.

- *della bocca* ‘from the mouth’ → “from my mouth”: the possessive replaces the definite article.
- *mi pinse* ‘it thrust for me’ → “it thrust”: the verb is retained, but the personal pronoun has no separate rendering; its relation to the speaker is expressed instead by “my” before “mouth”.
- *con la forza* ‘with the force’ → “by force”: the preposition changes and the article drops out.
- *del suo peso* ‘of its weight’ → “of its own weight”: “own” is supplied for emphasis.
- *per ch' io* ‘on account of which I’ → “whereat I”: a relative adverb replaces the phrase of consequence.
- *di corruscar vidi gran feste* ‘of flashing I saw great rejoicings’ → “saw great glee of coruscation”: the verb comes before its object, the plural festivities become a singular feeling, and the infinitive used as a noun becomes an abstract noun placed last.

> 85 Poi appresso con l' occhio più acceso  
> 86 lo benedetto segno mi rispose,  
> 87 per non tenermi in ammirar sospeso:  

Then straightway, with its eye more kindled, the blessed ensign answered me, that it might not hold me in suspense of wonder:

- *Poi appresso* ‘then after that’ → “Then straightway”: succession becomes immediate succession.
- *con l' occhio* ‘with the eye’ → “with its eye”: a possessive replaces the article.
- *mi rispose* ‘to me answered’ → “answered me”: the pronoun follows the verb and is its direct object in English.
- *per non tenermi* ‘in order not to hold me’ → “that it might not hold me”: the infinitive of purpose becomes a finite clause with a modal verb.
- *in ammirar sospeso* ‘in wondering suspended’ → “in suspense of wonder”: the participle becomes the governing noun, and the infinitive becomes a noun dependent on it.

> 88 “Io veggio che tu credi queste cose,  
> 89 perch' io le dico, ma non vedi come;  
> 90 sì che, se son credute, sono ascose.  

“I see that thou believest these things because I tell them thee, but the how thou seest not; so that, although believed, yet are they hidden.

- *io le dico* ‘I them tell’ → “I tell them thee”: the object follows the verb, and the recipient is supplied.
- *ma non vedi come* ‘but thou seest not how’ → “but the how thou seest not”: the interrogative adverb is treated as a noun, given an article, and placed before the verb.
- *se son credute* ‘if they are believed’ → “although believed”: the condition is read as a concession, and the finite passive is reduced to a participle.
- *sono ascose* ‘they are hidden’ → “yet are they hidden”: “yet” makes the contrast explicit, and the verb precedes the pronoun.

> 91 Fai come quei, che la cosa per nome  
> 92 apprende ben; ma la sua quiditate  
> 93 veder non può, se altri non la prome.  

Thou art as he who doth apprehend the thing by name, but may not see its quidity unless another bring it forth to light.

- *Fai come quei* ‘thou doest as he’ → “Thou art as he”: a comparison of action becomes one expressed with the verb of being.
- *che la cosa per nome apprende ben* ‘who the thing by name apprehends well’ → “who doth apprehend the thing by name”: the verb moves before its object and takes the auxiliary “doth”; the adverb meaning ‘well’ is omitted.
- *ma la sua quiditate veder non può* ‘but its quidity see he cannot’ → “but may not see its quidity”: the modal comes first, followed by the infinitive and its object.
- *se altri non la prome* ‘if another does not bring it forth’ → “unless another bring it forth to light”: the negative condition becomes an affirmative clause after “unless”, with a subjunctive verb; the object moves after the verb, and “to light” expands the image of bringing forth.

> 94 *Regnum cœlorum* violenza pate  
> 95 da caldo amore e da viva speranza,  
> 96 che vince la divina volontate;  

The kingdom of heaven suffereth violence from warm love and living hope which conquereth the divine will;

- *Regnum cœlorum* ‘kingdom of the heavens’ → “The kingdom of heaven”: the Latin phrase is translated with an initial article and a singular “heaven”.
- *violenza pate* ‘violence suffers’ → “suffereth violence”: the object moves after the verb.
- *da caldo amore e da viva speranza* ‘from warm love and from living hope’ → “from warm love and living hope”: the preposition is not repeated.

> 97 non a guisa che l' uomo all' uom sopranza,  
> 98 ma vince lei, perchè vuole esser vinta,  
> 99 e vinta vince con sua beninanza.  

not in fashion wherein man subdueth man, but conquereth it because it willeth to be conquered, and, conquered, with its own benignity doth conquer.

- *a guisa che* ‘in the manner that’ → “in fashion wherein”: the noun has no article in this turn of phrase, and a relative adverb replaces the conjunction.
- *l' uomo all' uom sopranza* ‘man over man prevails’ → “man subdueth man”: both generic articles disappear, and the verb of prevailing over someone becomes a transitive verb followed by its object.
- *con sua beninanza* ‘with its benignity’ → “with its own benignity”: “own” is added, and the phrase moves before the verb.
- *vince* ‘conquers’ → “doth conquer”: in the last occurrence the simple present is expanded with the auxiliary “doth”.

> 100 La prima vita del ciglio e la quinta  
> 101 ti fa maravigliar, perchè ne vedi  
> 102 la region degli angeli dipinta.  

The first life of the eyebrow and the fifth set thee a-marvelling, because thou seest the region of the angels painted with them.

- *ti fa maravigliar* ‘thee makes marvel’ → “set thee a-marvelling”: the object follows the verb; singular *fa* becomes a plural verb agreeing with the two lives, and the infinitive becomes an *-ing* form.
- *ne vedi la region degli angeli dipinta* ‘with them thou seest the region of the angels painted’ → “thou seest the region of the angels painted with them”: the preverbal pronoun becomes a prepositional phrase after the participle.

> 103 Dei corpi suoi non uscir, come credi,  
> 104 Gentili, ma Cristiani, in ferma fede,  
> 105 quel dei passuri, e quel dei passi piedi.  

From their bodies they issued not, as thou supposest, Gentiles, but Christians in established faith, in the feet that—to the one—should suffer, and—to the other—had already suffered.

- *non uscir* ‘did not issue’ → “they issued not”: the negative follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *come credi* ‘as thou believest’ → “as thou supposest”: belief is rendered as supposition.
- *in ferma fede* ‘in firm faith’ → “in established faith”: the adjective of firmness becomes a participial adjective.
- *quel dei passuri, e quel dei passi piedi* ‘the one of the to-suffer, and the other of the having-suffered feet’ → “in the feet that—to the one—should suffer, and—to the other—had already suffered”: the shared noun comes first, and the two participial modifiers become relative predicates, one prospective and one past perfect. The two instances of *quel* become phrases of standpoint; “in” expresses the object of faith, and “already” makes the temporal contrast explicit.

> 106 Chè l' una dello inferno, u' non si riede  
> 107 giammai a buon voler, tornò all' ossa,  
> 108 e ciò di viva speme fu mercede;  

For the one from hell,—where none returneth ever to right will,—came back unto its bones, and this was the reward of living hope;

- *dello inferno* ‘from the hell’ → “from hell”: English uses the bare noun.
- *u' non si riede giammai* ‘where one does not return ever’ → “where none returneth ever”: the impersonal construction with a negated verb becomes a personal verb with a negative subject.
- *a buon voler* ‘to good willing’ → “to right will”: the nominal infinitive becomes the noun “will”, qualified in terms of rightness rather than goodness.
- *tornò all' ossa* ‘returned to the bones’ → “came back unto its bones”: the simple verb becomes a verb and adverb, and a possessive replaces the article.
- *e ciò di viva speme fu mercede* ‘and this of living hope was reward’ → “and this was the reward of living hope”: the phrase dependent on ‘reward’ moves after it, and “reward” takes a definite article.

> 109 di viva speme, che mise la possa  
> 110 nei preghi fatti a Dio per suscitarla,  
> 111 sì che potesse sua voglia esser mossa.  

the living hope which put might into the prayers made unto God to raise him up, that his will might have power to be moved.

- *di viva speme* ‘of living hope’ → “the living hope”: instead of repeating the preposition, English repeats the noun phrase in apposition, with a definite article.
- *mise la possa nei preghi* ‘put the might in the prayers’ → “put might into the prayers”: the abstract noun loses its article, and “into” gives the preposition a directional force.
- *per suscitarla* ‘in order to raise her’ → “to raise him up”: the purpose is expressed by the infinitive alone; the feminine pronoun referring to the soul becomes masculine in reference to the person, and “up” expands the verb.
- *sì che potesse sua voglia esser mossa* ‘so that might its will be moved’ → “that his will might have power to be moved”: “that” alone introduces the clause, the subject precedes the modal, the possessive refers to the person as masculine, and the force of *potesse* is drawn out as “might have power”.

> 112 L' anima gloriosa, onde si parla,  
> 113 tornata nella carne, in che fu poco,  
> 114 credette in lui che poteva aiutarla:  

The glorious soul, whereof is the discourse, returning to the flesh where it abode short space, believed in him who had the power to aid it;

- *L' anima gloriosa* ‘the soul glorious’ → “The glorious soul”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *onde si parla* ‘of which one speaks’ → “whereof is the discourse”: the impersonal verb of speaking becomes a noun with the verb of being.
- *tornata nella carne* ‘having returned into the flesh’ → “returning to the flesh”: the completed return is rendered by a present participle, and “to” replaces ‘into’.
- *in che fu poco* ‘in which it was a little’ → “where it abode short space”: a relative adverb replaces the prepositional relative, and the verb of being with an adverb of duration becomes a verb of staying with an explicit expression of time.
- *poteva aiutarla* ‘could aid it’ → “had the power to aid it”: the modal force is expressed by a verb and noun followed by an infinitive.

## Lines 115–148 — Predestination and the Limits of Judgment

> 115 e credendo s' accese in tanto foco  
> 116 di vero amor, ch' alla morte seconda  
> 117 fu degna di venire a questo gioco.  

and believing kindled into so great flame of very love, that at the second death it was worthy to come unto this mirth.

- *s' accese* ‘set itself alight’ → “kindled”: the reflexive becomes an intransitive verb.
- *in tanto foco* ‘in so great a fire’ → “into so great flame”: the preposition marks entry into a state, and ‘fire’ becomes “flame,” without an article.
- *di vero amor* ‘of true love’ → “of very love”: “very” is used adjectivally in the sense of ‘true.’
- *alla morte seconda* ‘at the death second’ → “at the second death”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *a questo gioco* ‘to this play’ → “unto this mirth”: the word for play is rendered by one for delight.

> 118 L' altra, per grazia, che da sì profonda  
> 119 fontana stilla che mai creatura  
> 120 non pinse l' occhio infino alla prim' onda,  
> 121 tutto suo amor laggiù pose a drittura;  

The other, by that grace which welleth from so deep a fountain that never creature thrust eye down to its first wave, set all his love below on righteousness,

- *per grazia* ‘by grace’ → “by that grace”: the demonstrative is supplied in anticipation of the relative clause.
- *che da sì profonda fontana stilla* ‘which from so deep a fountain trickles’ → “which welleth from so deep a fountain”: the verb moves before the phrase of source; dripping or trickling becomes welling.
- *che mai creatura non pinse l' occhio* ‘that no creature ever thrust the eye’ → “that never creature thrust eye”: “never” alone carries the Italian negative, and the article before ‘eye’ disappears.
- *infino alla prim' onda* ‘as far as the first wave’ → “down to its first wave”: “down” makes the direction explicit, and a possessive replaces the article.
- *tutto suo amor laggiù pose* ‘all his love down there he placed’ → “set all his love below”: the verb moves before its object, and “below” does not separately express ‘there.’
- *a drittura* ‘to righteousness’ → “on righteousness”: the preposition changes to suit “set.”

> 122 per che, di grazia in grazia, Dio gli aperse  
> 123 l' occhio alla nostra redenzion futura:  

wherefore from grace to grace God opened his eye to our redemption yet to come;

- *gli aperse l' occhio* ‘to him opened the eye’ → “opened his eye”: the personal indirect object becomes a possessive.
- *alla nostra redenzion futura* ‘to our redemption future’ → “to our redemption yet to come”: an infinitive phrase with “yet” replaces the adjective.

> 124 onde credette in quella, e non sofferse  
> 125 da indi il puzzo più del paganesmo,  
> 126 e riprendiene le genti perverse.  

whereat he believed therein, and thenceforth endured not the mire of paganism, and reproved the folk perverse concerning it.

- *onde* ‘whence’ → “whereat”: the connection is expressed as a response rather than as a consequence proceeding from what precedes.
- *in quella* ‘in that’ → “therein”: a pronominal adverb replaces the preposition and demonstrative.
- *non sofferse da indi … più* ‘did not suffer from then on … any longer’ → “thenceforth endured not”: the time phrase moves before the verb, and ‘any longer’ is not separately rendered.
- *il puzzo* ‘the stench’ → “the mire”: an image of filth replaces one of smell.
- *riprendiene le genti perverse* ‘was rebuking for it the perverse peoples’ → “reproved the folk perverse concerning it”: the imperfect becomes a simple past, the plural noun becomes collective, and the sense of the clitic is expressed by a phrase after the object.

> 127 Quelle tre donne gli fur per battesmo,  
> 128 che tu vedesti dalla destra rota,  
> 129 dinanzi al battezzar più d' un millesmo.  

Those three dames stood as baptism for him, whom thou didst see at the right wheel, more than a thousand years before baptising.

- *gli fur per battesmo* ‘to him were for baptism’ → “stood as baptism for him”: ‘were for’ becomes “stood as,” and the indirect object moves to the end.
- *vedesti* ‘sawest’ → “didst see”: the simple past is expressed with an auxiliary.
- *dalla destra rota* ‘by the right wheel’ → “at the right wheel”: the preposition is rendered as one of location.
- *dinanzi al battezzar più d' un millesmo* ‘before the baptising by more than a millennium’ → “more than a thousand years before baptising”: the interval moves first and is counted in years; the article of the substantivized infinitive disappears before the English gerund.

> 130 O predestinazion, quanto remota  
> 131 è la radice tua da quegli aspetti  
> 132 che la prima cagion non veggion *tota*!  

O predestination, how far withdrawn is thy root from such vision as sees not the first cause entire!

- *quanto remota* ‘how remote’ → “how far withdrawn”: an adverb and participle replace the adjective.
- *la radice tua* ‘the root of thine’ → “thy root”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *da quegli aspetti che … non veggion* ‘from those eyes that … do not see’ → “from such vision as sees not”: plural beholders are rendered by a singular faculty, and the demonstrative and relative become the correlative “such … as”; the verb moves before its object.

> 133 E voi, mortali, tenetevi stretti  
> 134 a giudicar, chè noi, che Dio vedemo,  
> 135 non conosciamo ancor tutti gli eletti;  

And ye mortals, hold yourselves straitly back from judging; for we who see God, know not as yet all the elect;

- *tenetevi stretti a giudicar* ‘hold yourselves tight in judging’ → “hold yourselves straitly back from judging”: the predicative adjective becomes an adverb, “back” makes the restraint explicit, and the infinitive becomes a gerund after “from.”
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a coordinating conjunction replaces the subordinating one.
- *Dio vedemo* ‘God we see’ → “see God”: the object moves after the verb.
- *ancor* ‘yet’ → “as yet”: the adverb is expanded to a phrase.

> 136 ed enne dolce così fatto scemo,  
> 137 perchè il ben nostro in questo ben s' affina,  
> 138 che quel che vuole Iddio e noi volemo.”  

and sweet to us is such defect because our good in this good is refined, that what God willeth we too will.”

- *ed enne dolce* ‘and is to us sweet’ → “and sweet to us is”: the adjective comes first and the copula last; the attached pronoun is rendered by a prepositional phrase.
- *così fatto scemo* ‘a lack of this kind’ → “such defect”: the descriptive phrase is compressed into a determiner.
- *s' affina* ‘refines itself’ → “is refined”: the reflexive construction becomes passive.
- *quel che vuole Iddio* ‘that which wills God’ → “what God willeth”: a single relative replaces demonstrative and relative, and the subject precedes the verb.
- *e noi volemo* ‘also we will’ → “we too will”: the additive moves after the emphatic pronoun.

> 139 Così da quella imagine divina,  
> 140 per farmi chiara la mia corta vista,  
> 141 data mi fu soave medicina.  

So by this divine image to clear my curtailed vision was given me sweet medicine.

- *da quella imagine divina* ‘by that image divine’ → “by this divine image”: the demonstrative changes from distal to proximal, and the adjective moves before the noun.
- *per farmi chiara la mia corta vista* ‘to make clear for me my short sight’ → “to clear my curtailed vision”: a single verb replaces ‘make clear,’ the personal dative has no separate rendering, and ‘short’ becomes “curtailed.”
- *data mi fu* ‘given me was’ → “was given me”: the auxiliary moves before the participle.

> 142 E come a buon cantor buon citarista  
> 143 fa seguitar lo guizzo della corda,  
> 144 in che più di piacer lo canto acquista;  

And as on a good singer a good harpist maketh the quivering of the chord attend, wherein the song gaineth more pleasantness,

- *a buon cantor buon citarista* ‘to good singer good cithara-player’ → “on a good singer a good harpist”: “on” goes with the later “attend,” the two indefinite articles are supplied, and the instrumentalist is called a harpist.
- *fa seguitar lo guizzo della corda* ‘makes follow the flick of the string’ → “maketh the quivering of the chord attend”: the thing made to follow moves before the infinitive; ‘follow’ becomes “attend,” and the string's quick movement is rendered as “quivering.”
- *più di piacer lo canto acquista* ‘more of pleasure the song gains’ → “the song gaineth more pleasantness”: the object moves after subject and verb, the partitive preposition disappears, and “pleasantness” names a quality of the song.

> 145 sì, mentre che parlò, sì mi ricorda  
> 146 ch' io vidi le due luci benedette,  
> 147 pur come batter d' occhi si concorda,  
> 148 con le parole mover le fiammette.  

so whilst he spake I mind me that I saw the two blessed lights, just as the beating of the eyes concordeth, making their flames to quiver to the words.

- *sì mi ricorda* ‘thus it comes back to me’ → “I mind me”: the second *sì* has no separate rendering, and an impersonal construction becomes a personal reflexive one.
- *le due luci benedette* ‘the two lights blessed’ → “the two blessed lights”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *batter d' occhi si concorda* ‘beating of eyes accords’ → “the beating of the eyes concordeth”: articles are supplied for both nouns in English, and an intransitive verb renders the Italian pronominal form.
- *con le parole mover le fiammette* ‘with the words move the little flames’ → “making their flames to quiver to the words”: the phrase of accompaniment moves to the end and takes “to”; the infinitive after the verb of seeing becomes a participial causative with “quiver.” A possessive replaces the article, and the diminutive is not expressed.

## Summary — Close Echoes and Shifts of Construction

Wicksteed often keeps Dante’s repetitions even when he rearranges the words within a phrase. The recurring *ora conosce* is consistently “now knoweth he”, and *di grazia in grazia* is matched almost word for word by “from grace to grace”. Elsewhere, objects move after their verbs, as in *l' arca traslatò*, “bore the ark”, and adjectives before their nouns, as in *le due luci benedette*, “the two blessed lights”. Two routine changes generally pass unmarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where Italian gives the subject through the verb ending, and drops the article before a possessive, as in *il suo canto*, “his song”.

A change of construction can preserve an image while altering how it is expressed. The reflexive clause *che di riso t' ammanti* contracts to “smile-bemantled”; in the other direction, the adjectives *labili e caduci* expand into the verbal phrase “must slip and fall”, with “must” turning a liability into a necessity. Possession, too, can shift its grammatical place: in *gli aperse l' occhio*, the person is the indirect object of the verb; in “opened his eye”, the possessive modifies the noun.

Some departures sharpen a reference; others change the image or leave a shade of meaning unexpressed. For *cacume*, ‘summit’, Wicksteed gives “source”, naming the river’s point of origin rather than its height; for *puzzo*, ‘stench’, he gives “mire”, replacing smell with an image of filth. The diminutive of *vedovella* disappears in “widow”, while *subitamente* gains a separate time marker in “now straightway”. Yet where the force lies in a verbal echo, Wicksteed can follow closely: *vinta vince* remains audible in “conquered” and “doth conquer”, even though he moves the phrase about benignity between them.
