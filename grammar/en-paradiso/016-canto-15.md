# Paradiso 15

## Lines 1–18 — The Silent Lyre and the Sudden Flame

> 1 Benigna volontade, in cui si liqua  
> 2 sempre l' amor che drittamente spira,  
> 3 come cupidità fa nell' iniqua,  

The benign will—wherein distilleth ever the love that hath the right perfume, as doth, in the grudging will, cupidity—

- *Benigna volontade* ‘benign will’ → “The benign will”: the definite article is supplied.
- *si liqua* ‘melts’ → “distilleth”: the English substitutes distilling for melting; the reflexive form is rendered by an intransitive verb.
- *che drittamente spira* ‘that breathes rightly’ → “that hath the right perfume”: Wicksteed takes the breathing as the giving forth of a scent; the verb becomes a phrase with “hath”, and the adverb becomes an adjective qualifying “perfume”.
- *come cupidità fa nell' iniqua* ‘as cupidity does in the unjust one’ → “as doth, in the grudging will, cupidity”: the subject moves from before the verb to after the prepositional phrase; the understood “will” is made explicit, and ‘unjust’ is interpreted more narrowly as “grudging”.

> 4 silenzio pose a quella dolce lira,  
> 5 e fece quietar le sante corde,  
> 6 che la destra del cielo allenta e tira.  

imposed silence on that sweet lyre and stilled the sacred strings, which the right hand of heaven looseneth and stretcheth.

- *silenzio pose a* ‘silence placed upon’ → “imposed silence on”: the verb precedes its object, and ‘place’ becomes “imposed”.
- *fece quietar* ‘made fall quiet’ → “stilled”: the causative verb and infinitive become one transitive verb.
- *le sante corde* ‘the holy strings’ → “the sacred strings”: ‘holy’ is rendered “sacred”.
- *tira* ‘draws’ → “stretcheth”: the English expresses the effect of the pull on the strings.

> 7 Come saranno ai giusti preghi sorde  
> 8 quelle sustanzie, che, per darmi voglia  
> 9 ch' io le pregassi, a tacer fur concorde?  

How shall those beings unto righteous prayers be deaf, who, to excite in me the will to make my prayer to them, agreed in silence?

- *Come saranno ai giusti preghi sorde quelle sustanzie* ‘how will be to just prayers deaf those substances’ → “How shall those beings unto righteous prayers be deaf”: the future is split into auxiliary and infinitive, with the subject moved forward between them; ‘just’ becomes “righteous”, and “beings” renders the more technical ‘substances’.
- *per darmi voglia* ‘to give me desire’ → “to excite in me the will”: giving becomes arousing, the recipient becomes a phrase with “in”, and the abstract noun acquires an article.
- *ch' io le pregassi* ‘that I should pray to them’ → “to make my prayer to them”: the finite subjunctive clause with an expressed subject becomes an infinitive; the verb of praying becomes a verb with a possessive noun phrase, and its pronominal object follows as “to them”.
- *a tacer fur concorde* ‘to keep silent were of one accord’ → “agreed in silence”: the copula and adjective become a verb, and the preceding infinitive specifying what they agreed to do becomes a following prepositional phrase.

> 10 Ben è che senza termine si doglia  
> 11 chi, per amor di cosa che non duri,  
> 12 eternalmente quell' amor si spoglia.  

Right is it he should grieve without a limit, who, for the love of what endureth not, eternally doth strip him of this love.

- *Ben è che* ‘well it is that’ → “Right is it”: “Right” expresses fitness rather than simply ‘well’; the anticipatory “it” is supplied and the conjunction is omitted.
- *senza termine si doglia chi* ‘without end should grieve whoever’ → “he should grieve without a limit, who”: the phrase of extent follows the verb and gains an article; the reflexive subjunctive becomes “should grieve”, and its postponed subject is distributed between the anticipatory “he” and the relative “who”.
- *per amor di cosa* ‘for love of a thing’ → “for the love of what”: English supplies the article before “love” and substitutes the fused relative “what” for the noun and its following relative link.
- *che non duri* ‘that may not last’ → “endureth not”: the relative link is absorbed into the preceding “what”; the subjunctive becomes an indicative, with the negative after the verb.
- *quell' amor si spoglia* ‘of that love strips himself’ → “doth strip him of this love”: the English places the love after the verb, introduces it with “of”, and changes the demonstrative from ‘that’ to “this”; it uses auxiliary “doth” and the personal pronoun “him” reflexively.

> 13 Quale per li seren tranquilli e puri  
> 14 discorre ad ora ad or subito foco,  
> 15 movendo gli occhi che stavan sicuri,  

As through the tranquil and pure skies darteth, from time to time, a sudden flame setting a-moving eyes that erst were steady,

- *Quale* ‘such as’ → “As”: the comparative adjective becomes a conjunction.
- *per li seren tranquilli e puri* ‘through the clear skies tranquil and pure’ → “through the tranquil and pure skies”: the substantivized *seren* is rendered by the noun “skies” without a separate word for their clearness; both adjectives move before the noun.
- *discorre* ‘runs across’ → “darteth”: the English specifies a swift, sudden motion.
- *subito foco* ‘sudden fire’ → “a sudden flame”: the indefinite article is supplied, and “flame” replaces the broader ‘fire’.
- *movendo gli occhi* ‘moving the eyes’ → “setting a-moving eyes”: the simple participle is expanded into a causative phrase, and the definite article is omitted.
- *che stavan sicuri* ‘that stood untroubled’ → “that erst were steady”: “were steady” describes a state rather than using the verb ‘stood’; “erst” makes the contrast with the previous state explicit.

> 16 e pare stella che tramuti loco,  

seeming a star that changeth place,

- *e pare* ‘and it seems’ → “seeming”: a coordinated finite verb becomes a participle.

> 17 se non che dalla parte ond' ei s' accende  
> 18 nulla sen perde, ed esso dura poco;  

save that from where it kindleth no star is lost, and that itself endureth but a little;

- *dalla parte ond' ei s' accende* ‘from the part where it takes fire’ → “from where it kindleth”: “where” serves without an expressed antecedent, and the reflexive verb becomes an intransitive one.
- *nulla sen perde* ‘none of them is lost’ → “no star is lost”: the partitive pronoun is replaced by the explicit noun “star”.
- *ed esso* ‘and it’ → “and that itself”: “that” is supplied to mark the second clause as dependent on “save”; “itself” gives emphasis to the expressed pronoun.
- *dura poco* ‘lasts little’ → “endureth but a little”: the bare adverb of duration becomes “a little”, reinforced by the restrictive “but”.

## Lines 19–51 — A Star Descends and Speaks

> 19 tale, dal corno che in destro si stende,  
> 20 al piè di quella croce corse un astro  
> 21 della costellazion che lì risplende:  

such from the horn that stretcheth to the right unto that cross’s foot, darted a star of the constellation that is there a-glow;

- *in destro si stende* ‘to the right extends itself’ → “stretcheth to the right”: the reflexive becomes an intransitive verb, and the direction follows it.
- *al piè di quella croce* ‘to the foot of that cross’ → “unto that cross’s foot”: the possessive comes before the noun in place of the following prepositional phrase.
- *corse* ‘ran’ → “darted”: a more particular verb of swift movement.
- *lì risplende* ‘there shines’ → “is there a-glow”: the single verb becomes a copula and a predicate, with the adverb between them.

> 22 nè si partì la gemma dal suo nastro,  
> 23 ma per la lista radial trascorse,  
> 24 che parve foco retro ad alabastro.  

nor did the gem depart from off its riband, but coursed along the radial line, like fire burning behind alabaster.

- *nè si partì la gemma* ‘nor departed the gem’ → “nor did the gem depart”: the English negative inversion puts an auxiliary before the subject and the main verb after it; the pronominal Italian verb becomes a simple intransitive.
- *dal suo nastro* ‘from its riband’ → “from off its riband”: “off” is supplied.
- *per la lista radial trascorse* ‘through the strip radial it coursed’ → “coursed along the radial line”: the path follows the verb, the adjective precedes the noun, and the strip becomes a line traced “along” rather than passed through.
- *che parve foco retro ad alabastro* ‘so that it seemed fire behind alabaster’ → “like fire burning behind alabaster”: a finite clause of apparent likeness becomes a phrase of comparison; “burning” is supplied.

> 25 Sì pia l' ombra d' Anchise si porse,  
> 26 se fede merta nostra maggior musa,  
> 27 quando in Elisio del figlio s' accorse.  

With suchlike tenderness Anchises’ shade proffered itself, if our greatest Muse deserveth credit, when in Elysium he perceived his son.

- *Sì pia* ‘so tender’ → “With suchlike tenderness”: the adjective describing the shade becomes a noun in a phrase of manner.
- *l' ombra d' Anchise* ‘the shade of Anchises’ → “Anchises’ shade”: the following complement becomes a preceding possessive.
- *si porse* ‘itself proffered’ → “proffered itself”: the reflexive follows the verb.
- *fede merta nostra maggior musa* ‘credence merits our greatest Muse’ → “our greatest Muse deserveth credit”: object–verb–subject becomes subject–verb–object.
- *del figlio s' accorse* ‘of the son he became aware’ → “he perceived his son”: the pronominal verb with a preceding prepositional complement becomes a transitive verb with a following object; the article becomes possessive.

> 28 “*O sanguis meus, o superinfusa*  
> 29 *gratia Dei, sicut tibi, cui*  
> 30 *bis unquam coeli ianua reclusa?*”  

“Oh blood of mine! oh grace of God poured o’er thee! to whom, was ever twice, as unto thee, heaven’s gate thrown open?”

- *sanguis meus* ‘my blood’ → “blood of mine”: English uses a possessive phrase after the noun.
- *superinfusa gratia Dei* ‘poured-over grace of God’ → “grace of God poured o’er thee”: the participle follows the noun phrase, and its recipient is made explicit.
- *sicut tibi, cui bis unquam* ‘as to thee, to whom twice ever’ → “to whom, was ever twice, as unto thee”: the question comes before the comparison, the auxiliary intervenes, and the two adverbs exchange places.
- *coeli ianua reclusa* ‘heaven’s gate opened’ → “heaven’s gate thrown open”: English supplies the finite passive with the earlier “was” and expands the participle to “thrown open”.

> 31 Così quel lume; ond' io m' attesi a lui,  

So spake that light; wherefore I gave my heed to him.

- *Così quel lume* ‘thus that light’ → “So spake that light”: the verb of speaking is supplied.
- *io m' attesi a lui* ‘I bent myself to him’ → “I gave my heed to him”: the pronominal verb of attention becomes a verb with a noun object.

> 32 poscia rivolsi alla mia donna il viso,  
> 33 e quinci e quindi stupefatto fui:  

Then I turned back my sight unto my Lady, and on this side and that I was bemazed;

- *rivolsi alla mia donna il viso* ‘I turned back to my Lady my face’ → “I turned back my sight unto my Lady”: the object precedes the directional phrase, and *viso* is taken in the sense of sight rather than face.
- *quinci e quindi* ‘from here and from there’ → “on this side and that”: the two adverbs become a phrase of position.
- *stupefatto fui* ‘amazed I was’ → “I was bemazed”: the predicate follows the copula.

> 34 chè dentro agli occhi suoi ardeva un riso  
> 35 tal ch' io pensai co' miei toccar lo fondo  
> 36 della mia grazia e del mio Paradiso.  

for in her eyes was blazing such a smile, I thought with mine I had touched the bottom both of my grace and of my Paradise.

- *dentro agli occhi suoi* ‘within the eyes of hers’ → “in her eyes”: the expression of interior position is shortened and the possessive precedes the noun.
- *ardeva* ‘blazed’ → “was blazing”: the imperfect is rendered by a past progressive.
- *un riso tal ch' io pensai* ‘a smile such that I thought’ → “such a smile, I thought”: “such” precedes the article and the result connective is left implicit.
- *co' miei toccar* ‘with mine to touch’ → “with mine I had touched”: the present infinitive dependent on the verb of thinking becomes a finite past-perfect clause.
- *della mia grazia e del mio Paradiso* ‘of my grace and of my Paradise’ → “both of my grace and of my Paradise”: “both” is supplied.

> 37 Indi, a udire ed a veder giocondo,  
> 38 giunse lo spirto al suo principio cose  
> 39 ch' io non intesi, sì parlò profondo:  

Then—joyous both to hearing and to sight—the spirit added things to his beginning I understood not, so profound his speech;

- *a udire ed a veder giocondo* ‘to hear and to see joyous’ → “joyous both to hearing and to sight”: the adjective comes first, the infinitives become names of faculties, and “both” is supplied.
- *giunse lo spirto al suo principio cose* ‘added the spirit to his beginning things’ → “the spirit added things to his beginning”: the subject precedes the verb, and the direct object precedes the prepositional complement.
- *ch' io non intesi* ‘which I did not understand’ → “I understood not”: the object relative is omitted and the negative follows the verb.
- *sì parlò profondo* ‘so profoundly he spoke’ → “so profound his speech”: the verb becomes a possessed noun, and its modifier a predicate adjective with no expressed copula.

> 40 nè per elezion mi si nascose,  
> 41 ma per necessità, chè il suo concetto  
> 42 al segno dei mortal si soprappose.  

neither of choice hid he himself from me, but of necessity, for above the target of mortals his thought took its place.

- *per elezion* ‘by choice’ → “of choice”: the preposition changes.
- *mi si nascose* ‘from me himself he hid’ → “hid he himself from me”: the two preceding pronouns become complements after the inverted verb and subject.
- *per necessità* ‘by necessity’ → “of necessity”: the preposition changes as in the preceding phrase.
- *il suo concetto al segno dei mortal si soprappose* ‘his conception placed itself above the mark of mortals’ → “above the target of mortals his thought took its place”: the subject follows the phrase of position; “above” carries the sense of the verb prefix, and the reflexive becomes “took its place”.

> 43 E quando l' arco dell' ardente affetto  
> 44 fu sì sfocato, che il parlar discese  
> 45 in ver lo segno del nostro intelletto;  

And when the bow of ardent love was so tempered that his discourse descended towards the target of our intellect;

- *dell' ardente affetto* ‘of the ardent affection’ → “of ardent love”: the article disappears and *affetto* is rendered as “love”.
- *fu sì sfocato* ‘was so cooled’ → “was so tempered”: the abatement of heat is expressed as a moderation of intensity.
- *il parlar* ‘the speaking’ → “his discourse”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun with a possessive in place of the article.
- *in ver* ‘in the direction of’ → “towards”: a single preposition renders the phrase.

> 46 la prima cosa che per me s' intese,  
> 47 “Benedetto sie tu, fu, trino ed uno,  
> 48 che nel mio seme sei tanto cortese.”  

the first I understood was, “Blessed be thou, thou Three and One, who art so greatly courteous in my seed.”

- *la prima cosa che per me s' intese* ‘the first thing that by me was understood’ → “the first I understood”: the noun and the relative are unexpressed, and the passive construction becomes active.
- *Benedetto sie tu, fu* ‘Blessed be thou, was’ → “was, “Blessed be thou”: the reporting verb is moved ahead of the quotation instead of interrupting it.
- *trino ed uno* ‘threefold and one’ → “thou Three and One”: the pronoun is repeated and the adjective of threefoldness becomes a numeral.
- *nel mio seme sei tanto cortese* ‘in my seed art so courteous’ → “art so greatly courteous in my seed”: the prepositional phrase moves to the end, and the single degree word is expanded to “so greatly”.

> 49 E seguitò: “Grato e lontan digiuno,  
> 50 tratto leggendo nel magno volume  
> 51 u' non si muta mai bianco nè bruno,  

And followed on: “A dear long-cherished hunger, drawn from the reading of the mighty volume wherein not changeth ever white nor black,

- *seguitò* ‘followed’ → “followed on”: the particle makes the sense of continuation explicit.
- *Grato e lontan digiuno* ‘dear and long-standing fast’ → “A dear long-cherished hunger”: an article is supplied and the conjunction omitted; duration becomes long cherishing, and the fast is rendered by the hunger it entails.
- *tratto leggendo nel magno volume* ‘drawn by reading in the great volume’ → “drawn from the reading of the mighty volume”: the gerund becomes a noun phrase introduced by “from”, and the volume becomes the object of the reading rather than where one reads.
- *non si muta mai* ‘never changes’ → “not changeth ever”: the negative is distributed on either side of the verb, and the Italian pronominal form becomes a simple intransitive.

## Lines 52–84 — The Invitation to Speak and the Heart’s Thanks

> 52 soluto hai, figlio, dentro a questo lume  
> 53 in ch' io ti parlo, mercè di colei  
> 54 ch' all' alto volo ti vestì le piume.  

thou hast assuaged, my son, within this light, wherein I speak to thee; thanks unto her who for the lofty flight clad thee with wings.

- *soluto hai* ‘assuaged hast’ → “thou hast assuaged”: the auxiliary comes before the participle.
- *figlio* ‘son’ → “my son”: the possessive is supplied in the address.
- *in ch' io ti parlo* ‘in which I to thee speak’ → “wherein I speak to thee”: the preposition and relative become one word, and the indirect object follows the verb.
- *ti vestì le piume* ‘on thee put the feathers’ → “clad thee with wings”: the person becomes the direct object of “clad,” the clothing is introduced by “with,” and the feathers are rendered as wings.

> 55 Tu credi che a me tuo pensier mei  
> 56 da quel ch' è primo, così come raia  
> 57 dall' un, se si conosce, il cinque e il sei;  

Thou deemest that to me thy thought hath way e’en from the primal Thought, as ray forth from the monad, rightly known, the pentad and the hexad;

- *mei* ‘may pass’ → “hath way”: the subjunctive of a verb of motion becomes an indicative verb with a noun.
- *da quel ch' è primo* ‘from that which is first’ → “e’en from the primal Thought”: “e’en” is supplied, the relative clause becomes an adjective, and “Thought” makes the referent explicit.
- *così come raia* ‘just as rays’ → “as ray forth”: the correlative is reduced to “as”; “forth” is added, and the verb is plural in agreement with the two following subjects rather than singular as in Italian.
- *dall' un* ‘from the one’ → “from the monad”: the numeral becomes a technical noun.
- *se si conosce* ‘if one knows it’ → “rightly known”: the conditional clause becomes a passive participial phrase, and “rightly” is supplied.
- *il cinque e il sei* ‘the five and the six’ → “the pentad and the hexad”: the numerals become technical nouns.

> 58 e però chi io mi sia, e perch' io paia  
> 59 più gaudioso a te, non mi domandi,  
> 60 che alcun altro in questa turba gaia.  

and therefore, who I be, or why I seem to thee more gladsome than another in this festive throng thou makest not demand.

- *chi io mi sia* ‘who I myself may be’ → “who I be”: the pronominal *mi* has no separate rendering.
- *e perch' io paia / più gaudioso a te* ‘and why I may seem more joyous to thee’ → “or why I seem to thee more gladsome”: the conjunction becomes disjunctive, the subjunctive is rendered by the ordinary English present, and the person to whom it seems is placed before the comparative.
- *non mi domandi* ‘thou dost not ask me’ → “thou makest not demand”: the verb becomes a verb and noun, the indirect object is omitted, and the asking is placed after the completed comparison.
- *che alcun altro* ‘than any other’ → “than another”: the two indefinites are condensed into one.
- *in questa turba gaia* ‘in this throng gay’ → “in this festive throng”: the adjective is rendered by “festive” and placed before the noun.

> 61 Tu credi il vero; chè minori e grandi  
> 62 di questa vita miran nello speglio,  
> 63 in che, prima che pensi, il pensier pandi.  

Rightly thou deemest; for less and great in this life gaze on the mirror whereon, or ere thou thinkest, thou dost outspread thy thought.

- *Tu credi il vero* ‘thou believest the truth’ → “Rightly thou deemest”: the object becomes an adverb placed first.
- *minori e grandi / di questa vita* ‘lesser ones and great ones of this life’ → “less and great in this life”: the substantival adjectives have no plural marking in English, and membership expressed by ‘of’ becomes location.
- *nello speglio, / in che* ‘in the mirror, in which’ → “on the mirror whereon”: both prepositions change from ‘in’ to “on,” and the second is combined with the relative.
- *prima che pensi* ‘before thou think’ → “or ere thou thinkest”: the conjunction is rendered by the fuller temporal idiom “or ere,” and the subjunctive by an indicative.
- *il pensier pandi* ‘the thought thou spreadest out’ → “thou dost outspread thy thought”: the object follows the verb, the possessive replaces the definite article, and the simple verb is rendered with auxiliary “dost.”

> 64 Ma perchè il sacro amore, in che io veglio  
> 65 con perpetua vista e che m' asseta  
> 66 di dolce disiar, s' adempia meglio,  

But that the sacred love, wherein I watch with sight unintermitted, and which setteth me athirst with a sweet longing, may be fulfilled the better,

- *perchè* ‘in order that’ → “that”: the purpose conjunction is rendered by “that” alone.
- *in che* ‘in which’ → “wherein”: preposition and relative become one word.
- *con perpetua vista* ‘with perpetual sight’ → “with sight unintermitted”: the adjective becomes a negative participial form and follows the noun.
- *m' asseta* ‘me it makes thirsty’ → “setteth me athirst”: a single causative verb is expanded, with the object after “setteth.”
- *di dolce disiar* ‘of sweet desiring’ → “with a sweet longing”: the preposition changes, and the substantival infinitive becomes a noun with an indefinite article.
- *s' adempia meglio* ‘may fulfil itself better’ → “may be fulfilled the better”: the pronominal construction becomes passive, and “the” is supplied before the comparative.

> 67 la voce tua sicura, balda e lieta  
> 68 suoni la volontà, suoni il disio,  
> 69 a che la mia risposta è già decreta.”  

secure and bold and joyous let thy voice sound forth the will, sound forth the longing, whereto my answer already is decreed.”

- *la voce tua sicura, balda e lieta* ‘the voice of thine secure, bold and joyous’ → “secure and bold and joyous let thy voice”: the adjectives are moved before the subject, an additional “and” links the first two, and the possessive precedes the noun.
- *suoni la volontà, suoni il disio* ‘may it sound the will, may it sound the desire’ → “sound forth the will, sound forth the longing”: the exhortative subjunctives depend in English on the preceding “let”; “forth” is supplied both times, and ‘desire’ is rendered by “longing.”
- *a che* ‘to which’ → “whereto”: preposition and relative become one word.
- *è già decreta* ‘is already decreed’ → “already is decreed”: the adverb precedes the auxiliary.

> 70 Io mi volsi a Beatrice, e quella udio  
> 71 pria ch' io parlassi, ed arrosemi un cenno  
> 72 che fece crescer l' ali al voler mio.  

I turned to Beatrice, and she heard ere that I spoke, and granted me a signal that made the wings of my desire increase.

- *Io mi volsi* ‘I turned myself’ → “I turned”: English uses the intransitive verb without a reflexive object.
- *quella* ‘that one’ → “she”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *pria ch' io parlassi* ‘before I should speak’ → “ere that I spoke”: the Italian imperfect subjunctive becomes an English simple past.
- *arrosemi un cenno* ‘added to me a sign’ → “granted me a signal”: adding becomes granting, and the attached pronoun becomes a separate indirect object.
- *fece crescer l' ali al voler mio* ‘made grow the wings to my willing’ → “made the wings of my desire increase”: the noun phrase comes between the causative verb and its infinitive; ‘to’ becomes “of,” and the substantival infinitive with following possessive becomes “my desire.”

> 73 Poi cominciai così: “L' affetto e il senno,  
> 74 come la prima equalità v' apparse,  
> 75 d' un peso per ciascun di voi si fenno;  

Then I thus began: “Love and intelligence, soon as the prime equality appeared to you, became of equal poise to each of you,

- *cominciai così* ‘I began thus’ → “I thus began”: the adverb precedes the verb.
- *L' affetto e il senno* ‘the affection and the understanding’ → “Love and intelligence”: the articles are dropped, and both abstract nouns receive different English equivalents.
- *come* ‘as’ → “soon as”: the temporal sense is made explicit by “soon.”
- *la prima equalità* ‘the first equality’ → “the prime equality”: the ordinal is rendered by “prime.”
- *v' apparse* ‘to you appeared’ → “appeared to you”: the indirect object follows the verb.
- *d' un peso per ciascun di voi si fenno* ‘of one weight for each of you they made themselves’ → “became of equal poise to each of you”: the pronominal verb becomes “became” and comes first; identity of weight becomes equality of poise, and ‘for’ becomes “to.”

> 76 però che il sol, che v' allumò ed arse  
> 77 col caldo e con la luce, è sì iguali,  
> 78 che tutte simiglianze sono scarse.  

because the sun which lightened you and warmed with heat and brightness hath such equality that illustrations all fall short of it.

- *v' allumò ed arse* ‘you it illumined and burned’ → “lightened you and warmed”: the object comes after the first verb, and burning is softened to warming.
- *col caldo e con la luce* ‘with the heat and with the light’ → “with heat and brightness”: both articles and the repeated preposition are omitted, and ‘light’ becomes “brightness.”
- *è sì iguali* ‘is so equal’ → “hath such equality”: a predicative adjective becomes the object of “hath,” with “such” in place of the adverb of degree.
- *tutte simiglianze sono scarse* ‘all likenesses are inadequate’ → “illustrations all fall short of it”: the likenesses become “illustrations,” “all” follows the noun, and a predicate adjective becomes a verbal idiom with its standard of comparison expressed.

> 79 Ma voglia ed argomento nei mortali,  
> 80 per la cagion ch' a voi è manifesta,  
> 81 diversamente son pennuti in ali.  

But unto mortals, will and instrument, for reason manifest to you, unequally are feathered in their wings.

- *voglia ed argomento nei mortali* ‘will and means in mortals’ → “unto mortals, will and instrument”: the prepositional phrase is moved before the subject and recast with “unto”; the means is rendered as an “instrument.”
- *per la cagion ch' a voi è manifesta* ‘for the reason that to you is manifest’ → “for reason manifest to you”: the article is omitted, the relative clause is reduced to an adjective phrase, and its complement follows the adjective.
- *diversamente* ‘differently’ → “unequally”: difference is specified as inequality.
- *in ali* ‘in wings’ → “in their wings”: the possessive is supplied.

> 82 Ond' io che son mortal, mi sento in questa  
> 83 disagguaglianza, e però non ringrazio  
> 84 se non col core alla paterna festa.  

Wherefore I, a mortal, feel the stress of this unequalness, and therefore only with my heart give thanks for the paternal greeting.

- *che son mortal* ‘who am mortal’ → “a mortal”: the relative clause becomes an appositive noun phrase.
- *mi sento in questa / disagguaglianza* ‘I feel myself in this inequality’ → “feel the stress of this unequalness”: the reflexive and the construction with ‘in’ give way to a direct object, “the stress,” not separately expressed in Italian.
- *non ringrazio / se non col core* ‘I do not give thanks except with the heart’ → “only with my heart give thanks”: the negative and exception become a positive restriction placed before the verb, and a possessive replaces the article.
- *alla paterna festa* ‘at the paternal welcome’ → “for the paternal greeting”: “for” introduces what is being acknowledged, and the welcome is rendered as a greeting.

## Lines 85–117 — Family Roots and Frugal Florence

> 85 Ben supplico io a te, vivo topazio,  
> 86 che questa gioia preziosa ingemmi,  
> 87 perchè mi facci del tuo nome sazio.”  

But I may and do entreat thee, living topaz, who dost be-gem this precious jewel, that thou assuage me with thy name.”

- *Ben supplico io a te* ‘indeed I entreat unto thee’ → “But I may and do entreat thee”: the opening affirmation becomes a contrast and an assertion of what the speaker may do and actually does; the subject moves before the verb, and the addressee becomes its direct object.
- *che questa gioia preziosa ingemmi* ‘who this jewel precious dost be-gem’ → “who dost be-gem this precious jewel”: the verb moves before its object, and the adjective before its noun.
- *perchè mi facci del tuo nome sazio* ‘that thou make me with thy name sated’ → “that thou assuage me with thy name”: a single verb replaces the causative verb and predicate adjective; the object pronoun follows the verb instead of preceding it.

> 88 “O fronda mia, in cu' io compiacemmi  
> 89 pure aspettando, io fui la tua radice”;  

“Oh leaf of mine, in whom I took delight, only expecting thee, I was thy taproot,”

- *fronda mia* ‘my leafy branch’ → “leaf of mine”: a single leaf stands for the leafy growth, and the possessive becomes an *of*-phrase.
- *in cu' io compiacemmi* ‘in whom I pleased myself’ → “in whom I took delight”: a verb and noun replace the reflexive verb.
- *pure aspettando* ‘only expecting’ → “only expecting thee”: the object of the participle is made explicit.
- *radice* ‘root’ → “taproot”: the translation specifies a particular kind of root.

> 90 cotal principio, rispondendo, femmi.  

such opening in his answer made he me.

- *rispondendo* ‘answering’ → “in his answer”: the participle becomes a prepositional phrase with a possessive.

> 91 Poscia mi disse: “Quel da cui si dice  
> 92 tua cognazion, e che cent' anni e piùe  
> 93 girato ha il monte in la prima cornice,  

Then said: “He from whom thy kindred hath its name, and who a hundred years and more hath circled round the Mount on the first terrace,

- *mi disse* ‘said to me’ → “said”: the indirect object is omitted.
- *da cui si dice tua cognazion* ‘from whom is named thy kindred’ → “from whom thy kindred hath its name”: the passive construction becomes an active one, with the subject before the verb and the name expressed as its object.
- *girato ha il monte* ‘circled hath the Mount’ → “hath circled round the Mount”: the auxiliary moves before the participle, and “round” makes the direction explicit.

> 94 mio figlio fu, e tuo bisavo fue:  

was son to me, and thy grandfather’s father;

- *mio figlio fu* ‘my son was’ → “was son to me”: the verb moves before its complement, and the possessive becomes “to me”.
- *e tuo bisavo fue* ‘and thy great-grandfather was’ → “and thy grandfather’s father”: the relationship is expressed in two steps, and the repeated verb is omitted.

> 95 ben si convien che la lunga fatica  
> 96 tu gli raccorci con l' opere tue.  

meet it is, that with thy works thou shouldst abate his long-stretched toil for him.

- *ben si convien* ‘well does it befit’ → “meet it is”: the impersonal verb becomes a predicate adjective with a copula; the intensifier has no separate equivalent.
- *che la lunga fatica tu gli raccorci con l' opere tue* ‘that the long toil thou for him shorten with the works of thine’ → “that with thy works thou shouldst abate his long-stretched toil for him”: the phrase of means comes first rather than last, with the possessive before the noun. The subjunctive becomes a modal construction; shortening becomes abating, the direct object moves after the verb, “his” replaces the definite article, and “long-stretched” expands the adjective. The indirect object moves to the end.

> 97 Fiorenza, dentro dalla cerchia antica,  
> 98 ond' ella toglie ancora e terza e nona,  
> 99 si stava in pace, sobria e pudica.  

Florence, within the ancient circling wherefrom she still receiveth tierce and nones, abode in peace, sober and chaste.

- *dentro dalla cerchia antica* ‘inside the circuit ancient’ → “within the ancient circling”: “within” does the work of the compound expression, the enclosing circuit becomes a “circling”, and the adjective moves before the noun.
- *toglie ancora e terza e nona* ‘takes still both tierce and nones’ → “still receiveth tierce and nones”: the adverb moves before the verb, taking becomes receiving, and the first of the paired conjunctions is omitted.
- *si stava* ‘stayed’ → “abode”: the English verb conveys the continuing state without a reflexive.

> 100 Non avea catenella, non corona,  
> 101 non donne contigiate, non cintura  
> 102 che fosse a veder più che la persona.  

There was no chain or coronet, nor dames decked out, nor girdle that should set folk more a-gaze than she who wore it.

- *Non avea catenella, non corona* ‘she had no little chain, no crown’ → “There was no chain or coronet”: possession becomes existence; the diminutive of the first noun disappears, the second noun is narrowed to a small crown, and “or” replaces the second negative.
- *non donne contigiate, non cintura* ‘no women ornamented, no girdle’ → “nor dames decked out, nor girdle”: the negatives become coordinating conjunctions, “dames” is more specific than ‘women’, and ornamentation is expressed by a phrasal verb.
- *che fosse a veder più che la persona* ‘that were more to look at than the person’ → “that should set folk more a-gaze than she who wore it”: the subjunctive becomes a modal construction; being looked at becomes causing people to gaze, with the viewers supplied. The person is identified explicitly as the wearer.

> 103 Non faceva, nascendo, ancor paura  
> 104 la figlia al padre, chè il tempo e la dote  
> 105 non fuggian quinci e quindi la misura.  

As yet the daughter’s birth struck not the father with dismay; for wedding day and dowry evaded not the measure on this side and on that.

- *Non faceva, nascendo, ancor paura la figlia al padre* ‘the daughter did not yet, in being born, cause fear to the father’ → “As yet the daughter’s birth struck not the father with dismay”: the adverb moves to the front; the act of being born becomes the subject, with the Italian subject now a possessive. Causing fear becomes striking with dismay, making the father a direct rather than an indirect object.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a coordinating conjunction replaces a subordinating one.
- *il tempo e la dote* ‘the time and the dowry’ → “wedding day and dowry”: the occasion meant by ‘the time’ is specified, and both articles are omitted.
- *non fuggian quinci e quindi la misura* ‘did not flee on this side and on that the measure’ → “evaded not the measure on this side and on that”: flight becomes evasion; the negative follows the verb, and the expressions of direction move after its object.

> 106 Non avea case di famiglia vote;  

There were no mansions empty of the household;

- *Non avea case di famiglia vote* ‘she had no houses of household empty’ → “There were no mansions empty of the household”: possession becomes existence; houses become mansions, the adjective moves before its complement, and the definite article is supplied before “household”.

> 107 non v' era giunto ancor Sardanapalo  
> 108 a mostrar ciò che in camera si puote.  

Sardanapalus had not yet arrived to show what may be done within the chamber.

- *non v' era giunto ancor Sardanapalo* ‘had not arrived there yet Sardanapalus’ → “Sardanapalus had not yet arrived”: the subject moves to the front, the negative and temporal adverb stand between auxiliary and participle, and the indication of place is omitted.
- *ciò che in camera si puote* ‘that which in chamber one can [do]’ → “what may be done within the chamber”: “what” combines antecedent and relative; the impersonal construction becomes passive, with the implied action expressed by “be done”. The place phrase moves to the end and acquires an article.

> 109 Non era vinto ancora Montemalo  
> 110 dal vostro Uccellatoio, che, com' è vinto  
> 111 nel montar su, così sarà nel calo.  

Not yet was Montemalo overpassed by your Uccellatoio, which, as it hath been passed in the uprising, shall be in the fall.

- *Non era vinto ancora Montemalo* ‘not was surpassed yet Montemalo’ → “Not yet was Montemalo overpassed”: the temporal adverb joins the negative at the front, and the subject moves between auxiliary and participle.
- *com' è vinto* ‘as it is surpassed’ → “as it hath been passed”: a present passive describing a state becomes a perfect passive expressing the completed action.
- *nel montar su* ‘in the going up’ → “in the uprising”: a noun replaces the infinitive and its directional adverb.
- *così sarà* ‘so it will be’ → “shall be”: the answering demonstrative adverb is omitted.

> 112 Bellincion Berti vid' io andar cinto  
> 113 di cuoio e d' osso, e venir dallo specchio  
> 114 la donna sua senza il volto dipinto;  

Bellincion Berti have I seen go girt with bone and leather, and his dame come from her mirror with unpainted face;

- *vid' io* ‘saw I’ → “have I seen”: the simple past becomes the present perfect, with the subject between auxiliary and participle.
- *di cuoio e d' osso* ‘with leather and with bone’ → “with bone and leather”: the materials change places and share one preposition.
- *e venir dallo specchio la donna sua* ‘and come from the mirror his lady’ → “and his dame come from her mirror”: the person seen moves before the infinitive, with the possessive before its noun; the mirror also receives a possessive in place of the definite article.
- *senza il volto dipinto* ‘without the face painted’ → “with unpainted face”: the negative shifts from the preposition to the adjective, which moves before the noun; the article disappears.

> 115 e vidi quel del Nerlo e quel del Vecchio  
> 116 esser contenti alla pelle scoperta,  
> 117 e le sue donne al fuso ed al pennecchio.  

I have seen him of the Nerlo, and him of the Vecchio, content with the skin jerkin and nought over it, and their dames at the spindle and the flax.

- *e vidi* ‘and I saw’ → “I have seen”: the conjunction is omitted and the simple past becomes the present perfect.
- *quel … e quel* ‘that one … and that one’ → “him … and him”: personal object pronouns replace the demonstratives.
- *esser contenti* ‘to be content’ → “content”: the infinitive is omitted, leaving a predicate adjective.
- *alla pelle scoperta* ‘with the uncovered skin’ → “with the skin jerkin and nought over it”: the skin is identified as a garment, and the adjective is expanded into a separate phrase.
- *al fuso ed al pennecchio* ‘at the spindle and at the tuft of fibre’ → “at the spindle and the flax”: the preposition is not repeated, and the prepared tuft is named by its material instead.

## Lines 118–148 — Old Florence and Cacciaguida’s Life

> 118 O fortunate! ciascuna era certa  
> 119 della sua sepoltura, ed ancor nulla  
> 120 era per Francia nel letto deserta.  

Oh happy they, each one of them secure of her burial-place, and none yet deserted in her couch because of France.

- *O fortunate* ‘O fortunate ones’ → “Oh happy they”: the feminine plural adjective becomes a predicate with an explicit pronoun.
- *ciascuna era certa* ‘each was certain’ → “each one of them secure”: “of them” makes the reference explicit, the finite verb drops out, and ‘certain’ becomes “secure”.
- *sepoltura* ‘burial’ → “burial-place”: the English specifies the place rather than the burial itself.
- *ancor nulla era* ‘as yet none was’ → “none yet”: the negative pronoun comes first, and the finite verb again drops out.
- *per Francia nel letto deserta* ‘because of France in the bed deserted’ → “deserted in her couch because of France”: the participle comes first and the cause last; ‘the bed’ becomes “her couch”.

> 121 L' una vegghiava a studio della culla,  
> 122 e consolando usava l' idioma  
> 123 che pria li padri e le madri trastulla;  

The one kept watch in minding of the cradle, and soothing spake that speech which first delighteth fathers and mothers;

- *a studio della culla* ‘in care of the cradle’ → “in minding of the cradle”: the noun for attentive care becomes a gerund.
- *usava l’ idioma* ‘used the language’ → “spake that speech”: using a language becomes speaking it, and the definite article becomes demonstrative.
- *li padri e le madri trastulla* ‘the fathers and the mothers delights’ → “delighteth fathers and mothers”: the verb precedes its objects, and the generic nouns lose their articles.

> 124 l' altra traendo alla rocca la chioma,  
> 125 favoleggiava con la sua famiglia  
> 126 dei Troiani, di Fiesole e di Roma.  

another, as she drew its locks from the distaff, would tell her household about the Trojans, and Fiesole, and Rome.

- *l’ altra* ‘the other’ → “another”: the definite counterpart of the first woman becomes indefinite.
- *traendo alla rocca la chioma* ‘drawing from the distaff the tress’ → “as she drew its locks from the distaff”: the gerund becomes a finite temporal clause; the object comes before the source, and the singular with a definite article becomes a possessive plural.
- *favoleggiava con la sua famiglia* ‘told tales with her household’ → “would tell her household”: “would” marks the habitual imperfect; the idea of tales is no longer explicit, and the household becomes the audience of the telling rather than the companion in it.
- *dei Troiani, di Fiesole e di Roma* ‘of the Trojans, of Fiesole and of Rome’ → “about the Trojans, and Fiesole, and Rome”: one “about” governs the whole series, and a conjunction is supplied before its second member.

> 127 Saria tenuta allor tal maraviglia,  
> 128 una Cianghella, un Lapo Salterello,  
> 129 qual or saria Cincinnato o Corniglia.  

Then a Cianghella, or a Lapo Salterello, would have been as great a marvel as now would Cincinnatus or Cornelia.

- *Saria tenuta allor tal maraviglia* ‘would be held then such a marvel’ → “Then” … “would have been as great a marvel”: “Then” is moved to the front, while “would have been as great a marvel” follows the subject. The conditional passive of judging becomes a perfect conditional of being, and ‘such’ becomes a comparison of degree.
- *una Cianghella, un Lapo Salterello* ‘a Cianghella, a Lapo Salterello’ → “a Cianghella, or a Lapo Salterello”: the alternative is made explicit, and the two names precede their predicate.
- *qual or saria* ‘such as now would be’ → “as now would”: the comparison is completed by “as”, with the infinitive ‘be’ left understood.

> 130 A così riposato, a così bello  
> 131 viver di cittadini, a così fida  
> 132 cittadinanza, a così dolce ostello,  
> 133 Maria mi diè, chiamata in alte grida,  

To so reposeful and so fair a life among the citizens, to so faithful cityhood, to so sweet abode, Mary — with deep wailings summoned — gave me;

- *A così riposato, a così bello viver* ‘to so restful, to so beautiful a living’ → “To so reposeful and so fair a life”: “and” replaces the repeated preposition, and the substantival infinitive becomes the noun “life”.
- *di cittadini* ‘of citizens’ → “among the citizens”: an ‘of’ relation becomes one of association, and an article is supplied.
- *a così fida cittadinanza* ‘to so faithful a citizenry’ → “to so faithful cityhood”: the collective noun is given the more abstract rendering “cityhood”.
- *Maria mi diè* ‘Mary me gave’ → “Mary” and “gave me”: the object follows the verb, and the intervening participial phrase delays the verb.
- *chiamata in alte grida* ‘called in loud cries’ → “with deep wailings summoned”: the participle is moved to the end of the phrase; “with” replaces ‘in’, and loud cries are interpreted as “deep wailings”.

> 134 e nell' antico vostro Batisteo  
> 135 insieme fui Cristiano e Cacciaguida.  

and, in your ancient Baptistery, at once a Christian I became and Cacciaguida.

- *antico vostro Batisteo* ‘ancient your Baptistery’ → “your ancient Baptistery”: the possessive precedes the adjective.
- *insieme fui Cristiano e Cacciaguida* ‘at the same time I was Christian and Cacciaguida’ → “at once a Christian I became and Cacciaguida”: ‘was’ is read as a change of state, and the first predicate is made a count noun and placed before the verb.

> 136 Moronto fu mio frate ed Eliseo;  

Moronto was my brother and Eliseo;

- Literal.

> 137 mia donna venne a me di val di Pado,  
> 138 e quindi il soprannome tuo si feo.  

my wife came to me from Po valley, and from her was thy surname derived.

- *mia donna* ‘my lady’ → “my wife”: the relationship is specified.
- *di val di Pado* ‘from the valley of the Po’ → “from Po valley”: the river name becomes a modifier before “valley”.
- *quindi* ‘thence’ → “from her”: the English identifies the wife, rather than explicitly the place, as the source.
- *il soprannome tuo si feo* ‘the surname of thine formed itself’ → “was thy surname derived”: the possessive precedes the noun, and the reflexive construction becomes a passive of derivation, with the subject between auxiliary and participle.

> 139 Poi seguitai lo imperador Currado,  
> 140 ed ei mi cinse della sua milizia,  
> 141 tanto per bene oprar gli venni in grado.  

Then followed I the Emperor Conrad, who girt me with his knighthood, so much by valiant work did I advance me in his grace.

- *ed ei* ‘and he’ → “who”: a coordinate clause becomes a relative clause.
- *mi cinse* ‘me girded’ → “girt me”: the object follows the verb.
- *della sua milizia* ‘of his knighthood’ → “with his knighthood”: English uses “with” for that with which one is girded.
- *per bene oprar* ‘through acting well’ → “by valiant work”: the infinitive becomes a noun, and the adverb of manner becomes an adjective specifying valour.
- *gli venni in grado* ‘to him I came into favour’ → “did I advance me in his grace”: the dative becomes a possessive, coming into favour becomes advancing oneself in it, and the fronted expression of degree is followed by auxiliary inversion.

> 142 Retro gli andai incontro alla nequizia  
> 143 di quella legge, il cui popolo usurpa,  
> 144 per colpa dei pastor, vostra giustizia.  

In his train I marched against the infamy of that Law whose people doth usurp, shame to the pastors, what is yours by right.

- *Retro gli andai* ‘behind him I went’ → “In his train I marched”: following behind becomes membership in a retinue, and the general verb of motion becomes specifically military.
- *nequizia* ‘wickedness’ → “infamy”: the English chooses a term of disgrace.
- *il cui popolo* ‘the people of which’ → “whose people”: the relative possessive precedes the noun and takes the place of its article.
- *per colpa dei pastor* ‘through the fault of the pastors’ → “shame to the pastors”: an expression of cause becomes a parenthetical reproach.
- *vostra giustizia* ‘your right’ → “what is yours by right”: the noun phrase becomes a relative clause distinguishing the thing possessed from the right to it.

> 145 Quivi fu' io da quella gente turpa  
> 146 disviluppato dal mondo fallace,  
> 147 il cui amor molte anime deturpa,  

There by that foul folk was I unswathed of the deceitful world, whose love befouleth many a soul,

- *fu’ io da quella gente turpa disviluppato* ‘was I by that folk foul disentangled’ → “by that foul folk was I unswathed”: the agent precedes the auxiliary, the adjective precedes its noun, and “unswathed” gives the release the particular image of being unwrapped.
- *dal mondo fallace* ‘from the world deceitful’ → “of the deceitful world”: the preposition changes with the participle, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *il cui amor* ‘the love of which’ → “whose love”: the relative possessive replaces the article and genitive construction.
- *molte anime deturpa* ‘many souls disfigures’ → “befouleth many a soul”: the verb precedes its object and suggests defilement rather than disfigurement; the plural becomes a distributive singular.

> 148 e venni dal martiro a questa pace.”  

and came from martyrdom unto this peace.”

- *dal martiro* ‘from the martyrdom’ → “from martyrdom”: English uses the abstract noun without the definite article.

## Summary — Close in Image, Freer in Construction

Wicksteed often keeps Dante’s images and repetitions while changing the order of their parts. In *l’ombra d’Anchise*, the name follows the noun; in “Anchises’ shade” it precedes it. In *per la lista radial trascorse*, the path comes before the verb; in “coursed along the radial line” it comes after. Yet Wicksteed does not consistently prefer ordinary English order: “darted a star” keeps the subject after the verb, as in *corse un astro*. The bow and target remain a bow and a target, and the repeated *suoni* finds an exact rhythmic counterpart in the repeated “sound forth”.

Changes of construction can either compress the Italian or expand it. The causative *fece quietar*, ‘made fall quiet’, becomes simply “stilled”; conversely, *m’ asseta*, ‘makes me thirsty’, becomes “setteth me athirst”. The participle in *traendo* gives way to a finite clause, “as she drew”, while the finite verb in *e pare* becomes “seeming”. Sometimes the grammatical change also shifts the image: in *che drittamente spira*, ‘that breathes rightly’, the act of breathing becomes a property in “that hath the right perfume”.

Wicksteed often chooses a more specific word or names what the Italian leaves implicit. *Radice*, ‘root’, becomes “taproot”; *pelle scoperta*, ‘uncovered skin’, becomes “the skin jerkin and nought over it”. He supplies “spake” in *Così quel lume*, but elsewhere leaves out a word whose sense is clear, as when *mi disse*, ‘said to me’, becomes “said”. Two routine differences underlie these more particular choices: English normally supplies a subject pronoun where the Italian verb ending identifies the subject, and drops the Italian definite article before a possessive, as in *la mia risposta*, “my answer”.
