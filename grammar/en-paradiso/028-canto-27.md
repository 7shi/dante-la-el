# Paradiso 27

## Lines 1–15 — Paradise Sings Glory and a Torch Brightens

> 1 “Al Padre, al Figlio, allo Spirito Santo”  
> 2 cominciò “Gloria” tutto il Paradiso,  

All Paradise took up the strain, “To the Father, to the Son, to the Holy Spirit, glory!”

- *cominciò … tutto il Paradiso* ‘began … all Paradise’ → “All Paradise took up the strain”: the subject moves before the verb, and both move ahead of the quoted words instead of interrupting them. ‘Began’ becomes “took up the strain”, with a noun supplied for what was begun.
- *allo Spirito Santo* ‘to the Spirit Holy’ → “to the Holy Spirit”: the adjective precedes the noun.

> 3 sì che m' inebbriava il dolce canto.  

so that the sweet song intoxicated me.

- *m' inebbriava il dolce canto* ‘me was intoxicating the sweet song’ → “the sweet song intoxicated me”: the subject moves before the verb and the object after it; the simple past does not mark the ongoing action of the imperfect.

> 4 Ciò ch' io vedeva, mi sembiava un riso  
> 5 dell' universo; per che mia ebbrezza  

Meseemed I was beholding a smile of the universe;

- *Ciò ch' io vedeva, mi sembiava* ‘that which I saw seemed to me’ → “Meseemed I was beholding”: instead of making the thing seen the subject of ‘seemed’, the translation makes ‘seemed’ impersonal and gives it a clause in which the speaker is the subject. The progressive “was beholding” renders the imperfect of ‘see’; the smile is now the object of beholding rather than what the sight seemed to be.

> 6 entrava per l' udire e per lo viso.  

wherefore my intoxication entered both by hearing and by sight.

- *per che* ‘for which reason’ → “wherefore”: a single connective replaces the prepositional phrase.
- *per l' udire e per lo viso* ‘through the hearing and through the sight’ → “both by hearing and by sight”: “by” replaces ‘through’, the articles drop out, and “both” is supplied.

> 7 O gioia! o ineffabile allegrezza!  

O joy! O gladness unspeakable!

- *ineffabile allegrezza* ‘unspeakable gladness’ → “gladness unspeakable”: the adjective follows the noun.

> 8 o vita intera d' amore e di pace!  

O life compact of love and peace!

- *intera* ‘entire’ → “compact”: the adjective is taken in the sense of ‘wholly composed’ rather than rendered by ‘entire’.
- *e di pace* ‘and of peace’ → “and peace”: the English lets the preceding preposition serve for both nouns.

> 9 o senza brama sicura ricchezza!  

O wealth secure that hath no longing!

- *senza brama* ‘without longing’ → “that hath no longing”: the prepositional phrase becomes a relative clause and moves to the end.
- *sicura ricchezza* ‘secure wealth’ → “wealth secure”: the adjective follows the noun.

> 10 Dinanzi agli occhi miei le quattro face  
> 11 stavano accese, e quella che pria venne  

Before mine eyes the four torches stood enkindled,

- *Dinanzi agli occhi miei* ‘before the eyes of mine’ → “Before mine eyes”: the English uses a possessor before the noun rather than after it, and “Before” alone does the work of the Italian prepositional construction.

> 12 incominciò a farsi più vivace;  

and the one which had first approached me began to grow more living;

- *quella* ‘that one’ → “the one”: the definite article replaces the demonstrative.
- *pria venne* ‘first came’ → “had first approached me”: the simple past becomes a past perfect; ‘came’ is construed as an approach, with its object “me” supplied.
- *a farsi più vivace* ‘to make itself more lively’ → “to grow more living”: an intransitive verb of becoming replaces the reflexive construction, and ‘lively’ is rendered by “living”.

> 13 e tal nella sembianza sua divenne,  
> 14 qual diverrebbe Giove, s' egli e Marte  
> 15 fossero augelli, e cambiassersi penne.  

and such became in semblance as would Jupiter if he and Mars were birds and should exchange their plumage.

- *nella sembianza sua divenne* ‘in its semblance it became’ → “became in semblance”: the verb moves ahead of the prepositional phrase, and the possessive is omitted.
- *qual diverrebbe Giove* ‘as would become Jupiter’ → “as would Jupiter”: the auxiliary stands alone instead of repeating the verb of becoming.
- *cambiassersi penne* ‘were to exchange feathers with each other’ → “should exchange their plumage”: “should” renders the imperfect subjunctive; the reciprocal pronoun has no separate equivalent, while “their” makes possession explicit and the collective singular “plumage” replaces the plural ‘feathers’.

## Lines 16–48 — Saint Peter Denounces the Abuse of His Office

> 16 La provvidenza, che quivi comparte  
> 17 vice ed officio, nel beato coro  
> 18 silenzio posto avea da ogni parte,  

The providence which there assigneth function and office had imposed silence on the blessed choir on every side,

- *comparte vice ed officio* ‘allots turn and office’ → “assigneth function and office”: “assigneth” loses the sense of sharing out, and “function” gives the duty rather than the turn to perform it.
- *nel beato coro* ‘in the blessed choir’ → “on the blessed choir”: the place in which silence is established becomes the body on which it is imposed; the phrase moves after “silence”.
- *silenzio posto avea* ‘silence set had’ → “had imposed silence”: the auxiliary precedes the participle, the object follows the verb, and ‘set’ becomes “imposed”.
- *da ogni parte* ‘from every side’ → “on every side”: the preposition expresses position rather than direction.

> 19 quando io udi': “Se io mi trascoloro,  
> 20 non ti maravigliar; chè, dicend' io,  
> 21 vedrai trascolorar tutti costoro.  

when I heard: “If I transform my hue, marvel thou not; for, as I speak, thou shalt see all of these transform it too.

- *mi trascoloro* ‘I change colour’ → “I transform my hue”: the reflexive verb becomes a transitive verb with an explicit object.
- *non ti maravigliar* ‘do not marvel’ → “marvel thou not”: the negative infinitive used as a command becomes an imperative with the negative after it; the reflexive construction disappears.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *dicend' io* ‘I speaking’ → “as I speak”: the gerund with its own following subject becomes a finite clause.
- *vedrai trascolorar tutti costoro* ‘thou shalt see change colour all these’ → “thou shalt see all of these transform it too”: the subject of the infinitive moves before it; “of” expands ‘all these’, “it” supplies an object referring back to “my hue”, and “too” makes the parallel explicit.

> 22 Quegli ch' usurpa in terra il loco mio,  
> 23 il loco mio, il loco mio che vaca  
> 24 nella presenza del figliuol di Dio,  

He who usurpeth upon earth my place, my place, my place, which in the presence of the Son of God is vacant,

- *che vaca nella presenza del figliuol di Dio* ‘which stands vacant in the presence of the Son of God’ → “which in the presence of the Son of God is vacant”: the single verb becomes a copula and adjective, and the phrase of place moves before them.

> 25 fatto ha del cimitero mio cloaca  
> 26 del sangue e della puzza, onde il perverso,  
> 27 che cadde di quassù, laggiù si placa.”  

hath made my burial-ground a conduit for that blood and filth, whereby the apostate one who fell from here above, is soothed down there below.”

- *fatto ha del cimitero mio cloaca* ‘made has of the burial-ground of mine a sewer’ → “hath made my burial-ground a conduit”: the auxiliary moves before the participle and the possessive before the noun; the burial-ground becomes the direct object rather than the complement of ‘of’, and “conduit” is less specific than ‘sewer’.
- *del sangue e della puzza* ‘of the blood and of the stench’ → “for that blood and filth”: “for” replaces ‘of’, “that” replaces the first article, and the second preposition and article are not repeated; “filth” names the foul matter rather than its smell.
- *il perverso* ‘the perverse one’ → “the apostate one”: the rendering specifies a kind of perversity.
- *laggiù si placa* ‘down there is appeased’ → “is soothed down there below”: the reflexive verb is rendered by a passive, and the adverb moves to the end and is expanded by “below”.

> 28 Di quel color, che per lo sole avverso  
> 29 nube dipinge da sera e da mane,  
> 30 vid' io allora tutto il ciel cosperso:  

With that colour which painteth a cloud at even or at morn by the opposing sun, did I then see all heaven o’erfused;

- *Di quel color* ‘of that colour’ → “With that colour”: English uses “With” for what heaven is suffused with.
- *per lo sole avverso* ‘by the sun opposite’ → “by the opposing sun”: the adjective becomes a participial modifier before the noun, and the whole phrase moves to the end of the relative clause.
- *nube dipinge* ‘a cloud it paints’ → “painteth a cloud”: the object moves after the verb.
- *da sera e da mane* ‘at evening and at morning’ → “at even or at morn”: the two times are presented as alternatives rather than joined by ‘and’.
- *vid' io allora* ‘saw I then’ → “did I then see”: the simple past is expressed with an auxiliary, leaving the main verb after the subject and adverb.
- *tutto il ciel* ‘all the heaven’ → “all heaven”: the article is dropped.
- *cosperso* ‘besprinkled’ → “o’erfused”: the image of sprinkling becomes one of colour spread over the whole.

> 31 e, come donna onesta, che permane  
> 32 di sè sicura, e, per l' altrui fallanza,  
> 33 pure ascoltando, timida si fane,  
> 34 così Beatrice trasmutò sembianza;  

and as a modest dame who remaineth sure of herself, yet at another’s fault, though only hearing it, feeleth all timid, so Beatrice changed her semblance;

- *donna onesta* ‘a woman honourable’ → “a modest dame”: “modest” selects one sense of the adjective and precedes the noun; “dame” gives ‘woman’ a more formal register.
- *di sè sicura* ‘of herself sure’ → “sure of herself”: the adjective precedes its complement.
- *e, per l' altrui fallanza* ‘and, because of another’s fault’ → “yet at another’s fault”: “yet” makes the contrast explicit, and “at” presents the fault as the occasion of the feeling.
- *pure ascoltando* ‘merely listening’ → “though only hearing it”: “though” makes the concession explicit; “hearing” replaces ‘listening’ and takes the supplied object “it”.
- *timida si fane* ‘timid she becomes’ → “feeleth all timid”: becoming is rendered as feeling, the adjective follows the verb, and “all” intensifies it.
- *trasmutò sembianza* ‘changed semblance’ → “changed her semblance”: the possessive is supplied.

> 35 e tal eclissi credo che in ciel fue,  
> 36 quando patì la suprema possanza.  

and such, I take it, was the eclipse in heaven when the supreme Might suffered.

- *tal eclissi credo che in ciel fue* ‘such an eclipse I believe that in heaven there was’ → “such, I take it, was the eclipse in heaven”: ‘such’ becomes a separate predicate of the definite “the eclipse”; ‘I believe that’ becomes a parenthetical expression with “it”, and the verb moves before the noun and the phrase of place.
- *patì la suprema possanza* ‘suffered the supreme Might’ → “the supreme Might suffered”: the subject moves before the verb.

> 37 Poi procedetter le parole sue  
> 38 con voce tanto da sè trasmutata,  
> 39 che la sembianza non si mutò piùe:  

Then his discourse proceeded, with voice so far transmuted from itself, that his semblance had not altered more:

- *procedetter le parole sue* ‘proceeded the words of his’ → “his discourse proceeded”: the plural ‘words’ becomes the singular collective “discourse”, the possessive precedes the noun, and the subject precedes the verb.
- *tanto da sè trasmutata* ‘so much from itself transmuted’ → “so far transmuted from itself”: degree is expressed by “so far”, and the complement follows the participle.
- *la sembianza non si mutò piùe* ‘the semblance did not change more’ → “his semblance had not altered more”: the possessive replaces the article, and the simple past of the reflexive verb becomes the pluperfect of an intransitive verb.

> 40 “Non fu la sposa di Cristo allevata  
> 41 del sangue mio, di Lin, di quel di Cleto,  
> 42 per essere ad acquisto d' oro usata;  

“The spouse of Christ was not reared upon my blood, and that of Linus and of Cletus, that she might then be used for gain of gold;

- *Non fu la sposa di Cristo allevata* ‘not was the spouse of Christ reared’ → “The spouse of Christ was not reared”: the subject moves before the auxiliary, and the negative follows it.
- *del sangue mio, di Lin, di quel di Cleto* ‘of my own blood, of Linus, of that of Cletus’ → “upon my blood, and that of Linus and of Cletus”: “upon” gives the blood as that on which the spouse was reared; the possessive moves before ‘blood’, conjunctions link the bloods, and the substitute “that” appears before Linus rather than before Cletus.
- *per essere ad acquisto d' oro usata* ‘in order to be for acquisition of gold used’ → “that she might then be used for gain of gold”: the purpose infinitive becomes a finite clause with an explicit subject and modal; “then” is supplied, “used” moves before its purpose phrase, and “gain” renders ‘acquisition’.

> 43 ma per acquisto d' esto viver lieto  
> 44 e Sisto e Pio e Calisto ed Urbano  
> 45 sparser lo sangue dopo molto fleto.  

but ’twas for gain of this glad life that Sixtus and Pius, Calixtus and Urban shed their blood after many a tear.

- *ma per acquisto d' esto viver lieto* ‘but for acquisition of this living glad’ → “but ’twas for gain of this glad life that”: the purpose phrase is emphasized by the supplied cleft ‘it was … that’; “gain” renders ‘acquisition’, the substantivized infinitive becomes “life”, and the adjective precedes it.
- *e Sisto e Pio e Calisto ed Urbano* ‘both Sixtus and Pius and Calixtus and Urban’ → “Sixtus and Pius, Calixtus and Urban”: the repeated conjunction is omitted before the first and third names, grouping them in pairs.
- *sparser lo sangue* ‘shed the blood’ → “shed their blood”: a possessive replaces the article.
- *dopo molto fleto* ‘after much weeping’ → “after many a tear”: the act of weeping becomes the countable “tear”, with “many a” in place of ‘much’.

> 46 Non fu nostra intenzion ch' a destra mano  
> 47 dei nostri successor parte sedesse,  
> 48 parte dall' altra, del popol Cristiano;  

It was not our purpose that on the right hand of our successors one part of the Christian folk should sit, and one part on the other;

- *Non fu nostra intenzion* ‘not was our intention’ → “It was not our purpose”: English introduces anticipatory “It” for the following clause, places the negative after the verb, and renders ‘intention’ by “purpose”.
- *ch' a destra mano dei nostri successor* ‘that at the right hand of our successors’ → “that on the right hand of our successors”: “on” replaces ‘at’, and the article before “right hand” is supplied.
- *parte sedesse* ‘a part might sit’ → “one part” … “should sit”: “one” marks the division explicitly, and the imperfect subjunctive after ‘intention’ is rendered with “should”.
- *parte dall' altra* ‘a part on the other’ → “and one part on the other”: the conjunction is supplied, and “one” parallels the first “one part”.
- *del popol Cristiano* ‘of the folk Christian’ → “of the Christian folk”: the phrase moves from the end to the first “one part”, and the adjective precedes the noun.

## Lines 49–81 — Peter’s Rebuke and Dante’s Downward Gaze

> 49 nè che le chiavi, che mi fur concesse,  
> 50 divenisser segnacolo in vessillo,  
> 51 che contra i battezzati combattesse;  

nor that the keys given in grant to me should become the ensign on a standard waging war on the baptised;

- *che mi fur concesse* ‘which to me were granted’ → “given in grant to me”: the finite passive relative clause becomes a participial phrase; the verb of granting is expanded into a verb and noun, and the recipient comes last.
- *segnacolo in vessillo* ‘ensign on standard’ → “the ensign on a standard”: both nouns acquire articles.
- *che contra i battezzati combattesse* ‘that against the baptised should fight’ → “waging war on the baptised”: the relative clause with a subjunctive verb becomes a participial phrase; fighting is expressed by verb and noun, and the opponent follows rather than precedes the action.

> 52 nè ch' io fossi figura di sigillo  
> 53 ai privilegi venduti e mendaci,  
> 54 ond' io sovente arrosso e disfavillo.  

nor that I should become the head upon the seal to sold and lying privileges, whereat I often blush and shoot forth flames.

- *fossi* ‘should be’ → “should become”: being is rendered as a change of state.
- *figura di sigillo* ‘figure of a seal’ → “the head upon the seal”: the figure is specified as a head, the relation to the seal is made spatial, and definite articles are supplied.
- *ai privilegi venduti e mendaci* ‘to the privileges sold and lying’ → “to sold and lying privileges”: the article is dropped and both modifiers precede the noun.
- *disfavillo* ‘I spark’ → “shoot forth flames”: a single verb becomes a verb phrase with an explicit object, flames rather than sparks.

> 55 In vesta di pastor lupi rapaci  
> 56 si veggion di quassù per tutti i paschi.  

In garb of pastors ravening wolves are seen from here above in all the pastures.

- *pastor* ‘shepherd’ → “pastors”: the singular becomes plural, and the ecclesiastical sense is explicit.
- *lupi rapaci* ‘wolves ravenous’ → “ravening wolves”: the modifier comes first and takes participial form.
- *si veggion* ‘one sees’ → “are seen”: the Italian *si* construction is rendered by an English passive.
- *per tutti i paschi* ‘throughout all the pastures’ → “in all the pastures”: a preposition of distribution becomes one of location.

> 57 O difesa di Dio, perchè pur giaci?  

Succour of God! oh wherefore liest thou prone?

- *O difesa di Dio* ‘O defence of God’ → “Succour of God! oh”: defence is rendered as aid, and the interjection moves from the address to the question.
- *perchè pur giaci* ‘why dost thou still lie’ → “wherefore liest thou prone”: the continuative adverb is not rendered; instead the translation specifies the posture.

> 58 Del sangue nostro Caorsini e Guaschi  
> 59 s' apparecchian di bere. O buon principio,  
> 60 a che vil fine convien che tu caschi!  

Cahorsines and Gascons make ready to drink our blood. Oh fair beginning, to what vile ending must thou fall!

- *Del sangue nostro Caorsini e Guaschi s' apparecchian di bere* ‘of our blood Cahorsines and Gascons prepare themselves to drink’ → “Cahorsines and Gascons make ready to drink our blood”: the fronted partitive becomes the direct object after the infinitive, the possessive precedes its noun, and the reflexive verb becomes the non-reflexive idiom “make ready”.
- *buon* ‘good’ → “fair”: the adjective is rendered by one of praise rather than its direct equivalent.
- *convien che tu caschi* ‘it is necessary that thou fall’ → “must thou fall”: an impersonal verb governing a subjunctive clause becomes a modal governing a bare infinitive.

> 61 Ma l' alta provvidenza, che con Scipio  
> 62 difese a Roma la gloria del mondo,  
> 63 soccorrà tosto, sì com' io concipio.  

But the lofty Providence, which with Scipio defended the glory of the world for Rome, will soon bring succour, as I deem.

- *difese a Roma la gloria del mondo* ‘defended for Rome the glory of the world’ → “defended the glory of the world for Rome”: the beneficiary is moved after the direct object.
- *soccorrà tosto* ‘will succour soon’ → “will soon bring succour”: the verb becomes a verb and noun, and the adverb moves inside the verb phrase.
- *sì com' io concipio* ‘so as I conceive’ → “as I deem”: the demonstrative adverb is absorbed into the conjunction, and conceiving is rendered as judging.

> 64 E tu, figliuol, che per lo mortal pondo  
> 65 ancor giù tornerai, apri la bocca,  

And thou, my son, who, for thy mortal weight, shalt return below once more, open thy mouth

- *figliuol* ‘son’ → “my son”: a possessive is supplied in the address.
- *per lo mortal pondo* ‘for the mortal weight’ → “for thy mortal weight”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *ancor giù tornerai* ‘again below thou shalt return’ → “shalt return below once more”: both adverbs move after the verb and reverse their relative order; the adverb of repetition becomes a phrase.
- *la bocca* ‘the mouth’ → “thy mouth”: English marks the possessor of the body part explicitly.

> 66 e non asconder quel ch' io non ascondo.”  

and hide thou not the thing which I not hide.”

- *non asconder* ‘do not hide’ → “hide thou not”: the infinitive used for a negative command becomes an imperative followed by the pronoun and the negation.
- *quel ch'* ‘that which’ → “the thing which”: the demonstrative becomes an article and noun.
- *io non ascondo* ‘I do not hide’ → “I not hide”: the negative finite verb is rendered without the auxiliary usual in English.

> 67 Sì come di vapor gelati fiocca  
> 68 in giuso l' aer nostro, quando il corno  
> 69 della Capra del ciel col sol si tocca;  

As our atmosphere raineth down in flakes the frozen vapours when the horn of the heavenly Goat is touched by the sun;

- *Sì come* ‘just as’ → “As”: the conjunction alone introduces the comparison.
- *di vapor gelati fiocca in giuso l' aer nostro* ‘with frozen vapours snows downward the air of ours’ → “our atmosphere raineth down in flakes the frozen vapours”: the subject moves from last to first, with a preceding possessive; air becomes atmosphere. The intransitive verb of snowing becomes a verb of raining qualified by “in flakes”, and the initial prepositional phrase becomes a final direct object with a definite article and a preceding adjective.
- *della Capra del ciel* ‘of the Goat of heaven’ → “of the heavenly Goat”: the second possessive phrase becomes a preceding adjective.
- *col sol si tocca* ‘with the sun comes into contact’ → “is touched by the sun”: contact expressed with *si* and a prepositional complement becomes a passive with the sun as agent, placed after the verb.

> 70 in su vid' io così l' etere adorno  
> 71 farsi, e fioccar di vapor trionfanti,  
> 72 che fatto avean con noi quivi soggiorno.  

so did I see the ether adorn itself and rain upward the flakes of the triumphant flashes, which had made sojourn there with us.

- *in su vid' io così l' etere adorno farsi* ‘upward saw I thus the ether fair make itself’ → “so did I see the ether adorn itself”: the direction is deferred to “upward” after “rain”, while the comparative adverb moves to the front. English uses auxiliary inversion, and the adjective with a reflexive infinitive becomes the single reflexive action “adorn itself”.
- *e fioccar di vapor trionfanti* ‘and snow with triumphant vapours’ → “and rain upward the flakes of the triumphant flashes”: snowing is again expressed as raining flakes, here with the flakes as direct object; vapours become flashes, with a definite article and a preceding adjective.
- *fatto avean con noi quivi soggiorno* ‘made had with us there sojourn’ → “had made sojourn there with us”: the auxiliary precedes the participle, the noun immediately follows the verb, and place precedes company.

> 73 Lo viso mio seguiva i suoi sembianti,  
> 74 e seguì in fin che il mezzo, per lo molto,  
> 75 gli tolse il trapassar del più avanti.  

My sight was following their semblance, and followed till the medium, by excess, deprived it of the power to pierce more far.

- *Lo viso mio* ‘the sight of mine’ → “My sight”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *seguiva* ‘followed’ → “was following”: the English progressive marks the ongoing action of the imperfect.
- *i suoi sembianti* ‘their semblances’ → “their semblance”: the plural object becomes a collective singular, with the possessive before the noun.
- *in fin che* ‘up to the point that’ → “till”: the phrase is condensed to a conjunction.
- *per lo molto* ‘because of the great amount’ → “by excess”: the substantivised adjective becomes a noun denoting an excessive amount.
- *gli tolse il trapassar del più avanti* ‘took from it the penetrating farther on’ → “deprived it of the power to pierce more far”: the indirect object becomes the direct object of a verb of deprivation; the nominalised infinitive becomes an infinitive dependent on the supplied noun “power”.

> 76 Onde la donna, che mi vide assolto  
> 77 dell' attendere in su, mi disse: “Adima  
> 78 il viso, e guarda come tu sei volto.”  

Whereat the Lady, who saw me now absolved from straining upward, said to me: “Down plunge thy sight and see how thou hast rolled.”

- *che mi vide assolto* ‘who me saw released’ → “who saw me now absolved”: the object follows the verb, and a temporal adverb is supplied.
- *dell' attendere in su* ‘from the attending upward’ → “from straining upward”: the nominalised infinitive becomes a gerund without an article, and attending is rendered as an effort.
- *mi disse* ‘to me said’ → “said to me”: the recipient follows the verb.
- *Adima il viso* ‘lower the sight’ → “Down plunge thy sight”: the downward direction inherent in the verb is expressed separately with a stronger verb of motion; a possessive replaces the article.
- *guarda come tu sei volto* ‘look how thou art turned’ → “see how thou hast rolled”: looking becomes seeing, and a construction with ‘be’ and a participle of turning becomes an active perfect of motion.

> 79 Dall' ora ch' io avea guardato prima,  
> 80 io vidi mosso me per tutto l' arco  
> 81 che fa dal mezzo al fine il primo clima;  

From the hour at which I had before looked down, I saw that I had moved through the whole arc which the first Climate makes from middle unto end;

- *ch' io avea guardato prima* ‘that I had looked before’ → “at which I had before looked down”: the temporal relative acquires a preposition, the adverb precedes the participle, and the direction of looking is supplied.
- *io vidi mosso me* ‘I saw moved myself’ → “I saw that I had moved”: the participial construction with an object pronoun becomes a finite content clause, with the pronoun as subject of an active pluperfect.
- *che fa dal mezzo al fine il primo clima* ‘which makes from the middle to the end the first Climate’ → “which the first Climate makes from middle unto end”: the subject moves from the end to before its verb, and the nouns marking the limits lose their articles.

## Lines 82–114 — From Earth to the Swiftest Heaven

> 82 sì ch' io vedea di là da Gade il varco  
> 83 folle d' Ulisse, e di qua presso il lito  
> 84 nel qual si fece Europa dolce carco.  

so that I saw beyond Cadiz the mad way which Ulysses took, and on this side, hard by, the shore whereon Europa made herself a sweet burden.

- *vedea* ‘was seeing’ → “saw”: the Italian imperfect is rendered by the English simple past.
- *il varco folle d' Ulisse* ‘the passage mad of Ulysses’ → “the mad way which Ulysses took”: the adjective moves before the noun; the phrase identifying the passage as Ulysses’ becomes a relative clause with the supplied verb “took”.
- *nel qual* ‘in which’ → “whereon”: the preposition and relative pronoun become a relative adverb, with the sense of ‘on’ rather than ‘in’.
- *si fece Europa dolce carco* ‘made herself Europa sweet burden’ → “Europa made herself a sweet burden”: the subject moves before the verb, the reflexive after it, and the predicate noun acquires an article.

> 85 E più mi fora discoperto il sito  
> 86 di questa aiuola; ma il sol procedea,  
> 87 sotto i miei piedi, un segno e più partito.  

And further had the site of this thrashing-floor been unfolded to me, save that the sun was in advance beneath my feet, severed by a Sign and more from me.

- *più mi fora discoperto il sito di questa aiuola* ‘more would have been uncovered to me the site of this little thrashing-floor’ → “further had the site of this thrashing-floor been unfolded to me”: “further” expresses degree; the counterfactual passive is recast with “had” before the subject and “been unfolded” after it. The recipient moves to the end, and the diminutive is not expressed.
- *ma* ‘but’ → “save that”: the adversative conjunction becomes a phrase introducing an exception.
- *procedea* ‘was advancing’ → “was in advance”: motion becomes relative position.
- *un segno e più partito* ‘a sign and more separated’ → “severed by a Sign and more from me”: the participle moves before the measure of separation; “by” introduces that measure, and “from me” supplies the unexpressed point of reference.

> 88 La mente innamorata, che donnea  
> 89 con la mia donna sempre, di ridure  
> 90 ad essa gli occhi più che mai ardea:  

My enamoured mind, which held amorous converse ever with my Lady, burned more than ever to bring back my eyes to her;

- *La mente innamorata* ‘the mind enamoured’ → “My enamoured mind”: the definite article becomes a possessive, and the adjective moves before the noun.
- *donnea con la mia donna sempre* ‘woos with my lady always’ → “held amorous converse ever with my Lady”: the single verb of courtship becomes a phrase and changes from present to past; the adverb moves before the phrase naming the companion.
- *di ridure ad essa gli occhi più che mai ardea* ‘to bring back to her the eyes more than ever burned’ → “burned more than ever to bring back my eyes to her”: the finite verb comes first, followed by its adverbial comparison and then its infinitive complement; the eyes are made explicitly the speaker’s, and their destination moves after them.

> 91 e se natura od arte fe' pasture  
> 92 da pigliare occhi, per aver la mente,  
> 93 in carne umana o nelle sue pitture,  

and whatsoever food nature or art e’er made, to catch the eyes and so possess the mind, be it in human flesh, be it in pictures,

- *se natura od arte fe' pasture* ‘if nature or art made pastures’ → “whatsoever food nature or art e’er made”: the conditional opening becomes a free relative, with the object first; the plural image becomes a mass noun, and “e’er” is supplied.
- *da pigliare occhi* ‘fit to catch eyes’ → “to catch the eyes”: the qualifying infinitive becomes an infinitive of purpose, and an article is supplied.
- *per aver la mente* ‘in order to have the mind’ → “and so possess the mind”: the second purpose is coordinated with the first and presented as its consequence; ‘have’ becomes “possess”.
- *in carne umana o nelle sue pitture* ‘in flesh human or in the pictures of it’ → “be it in human flesh, be it in pictures”: the adjective moves before the noun; the alternatives are marked by repeated “be it” rather than ‘or’, and the possessive linking the pictures to human flesh is omitted.

> 94 tutte adunate parrebber niente  
> 95 ver lo piacer divin che mi rifulse,  
> 96 quando mi volsi al suo viso ridente.  

if all united, would seem nought towards the divine delight which glowed upon me when that I turned me to her smiling face.

- *tutte adunate* ‘all gathered together’ → “if all united”: the participial phrase is explicitly marked as conditional; the feminine plural referring back to *pasture* is no longer marked.
- *ver lo piacer divin* ‘beside the delight divine’ → “towards the divine delight”: the preposition of comparison is rendered directionally, and the adjective moves before the noun.
- *che mi rifulse* ‘which shone resplendent on me’ → “which glowed upon me”: the verb of radiance becomes “glowed”, and the pronoun is expressed by a prepositional phrase after it.
- *quando mi volsi* ‘when myself I turned’ → “when that I turned me”: “that” is supplied after the conjunction, and the reflexive follows the verb.
- *al suo viso ridente* ‘to her face smiling’ → “to her smiling face”: the participial adjective moves before the noun.

> 97 E la virtù, che lo sguardo m' indulse,  
> 98 del bel nido di Leda mi divelse,  
> 99 e nel ciel velocissimo m' impulse.  

And the power of which that look made largess to me, from the fair nest of Leda plucked me forth, and into the swiftest heaven thrust me.

- *che lo sguardo m' indulse* ‘which the look bestowed on me’ → “of which that look made largess to me”: the direct-object relative becomes “of which” with the noun “largess” in place of the verb of giving; the article becomes a demonstrative, and the recipient follows the verbal phrase.
- *mi divelse* ‘me it tore away’ → “plucked me forth”: the object follows the verb, and “forth” separately expresses removal.
- *nel ciel velocissimo* ‘into the heaven swiftest’ → “into the swiftest heaven”: the superlative moves before the noun.
- *m' impulse* ‘me it thrust’ → “thrust me”: the object moves after the verb.

> 100 Le parti sue vivissime ed eccelse  
> 101 sì uniformi son ch' io non so dire  
> 102 qual Beatrice per loco mi scelse.  

Its parts most living and exalted are so uniform that I know not to tell which Beatrice chose for my position.

- *eccelse* ‘loftiest’ → “exalted”: the adjective of height is rendered by a participial adjective without a separate superlative marker.
- *sì uniformi son* ‘so uniform are’ → “are so uniform”: the copula moves before its complement.
- *non so dire* ‘I do not know how to say’ → “I know not to tell”: negation follows the finite verb, and the Italian construction for knowing how to do something is rendered by “know” with a bare *to*-infinitive.
- *per loco mi scelse* ‘as a place for me chose’ → “chose for my position”: the verb moves forward; the personal pronoun becomes a possessive modifying “position”.

> 103 Ma ella, che vedeva il mio disire,  
> 104 incominciò, ridendo tanto lieta  
> 105 che Dio parea nel suo volto gioire:  

But she, who saw my longing, smiling began—so glad that God seemed joying in her countenance—

- *vedeva* ‘was seeing’ → “saw”: the imperfect is rendered by the simple past.
- *il mio disire* ‘my desire’ → “my longing”: the noun is rendered by a more specifically affective term.
- *incominciò, ridendo tanto lieta* ‘began, smiling so glad’ → “smiling began—so glad”: the participle moves before the finite verb, separating it from the description of her gladness.
- *parea nel suo volto gioire* ‘seemed in her face to rejoice’ → “seemed joying in her countenance”: the infinitive becomes a participle, and the phrase locating the joy moves after it.

> 106 “La natura del mondo, che quieta  
> 107 il mezzo, e tutto l' altro intorno move,  
> 108 quinci comincia come da sua meta.  

“The nature of the universe which stilleth the centre and moveth all the rest around, hence doth begin as from its starting point.

- *del mondo* ‘of the world’ → “of the universe”: the noun is rendered in its cosmological sense.
- *il mezzo* ‘the middle’ → “the centre”: a more specifically spatial term is used.
- *tutto l' altro intorno move* ‘all the rest around moves’ → “moveth all the rest around”: the verb moves before its object and adverb.
- *comincia* ‘begins’ → “doth begin”: the simple present is expanded with an auxiliary.
- *da sua meta* ‘from its boundary’ → “from its starting point”: the limit is interpreted specifically as the point from which the beginning is made.

> 109 E questo cielo non ha altro dove  
> 110 che la mente divina, in che s'accende  
> 111 l'amor che il volge e la virtù ch' ei piove.  

And this heaven hath no other *where* than the divine mind wherein is kindled the love which rolleth it and the power which it sheddeth.

- *non ha altro dove* ‘has not another where’ → “hath no other *where*”: negation is transferred from the verb to the determiner.
- *la mente divina* ‘the mind divine’ → “the divine mind”: the adjective moves before the noun.
- *in che s'accende* ‘in which is kindled’ → “wherein is kindled”: the preposition and relative pronoun are combined in a relative adverb, and the reflexive construction is rendered as a passive.
- *che il volge* ‘which it turns’ → “which rolleth it”: the object pronoun moves after the verb, and turning is rendered as rolling.
- *la virtù ch' ei piove* ‘the power that it rains down’ → “the power which it sheddeth”: “sheddeth” replaces the image of raining.

> 112 Luce ed amor d' un cerchio lui comprende,  
> 113 sì come questo gli altri, e quel precinto  
> 114 colui che il cinge solamente intende.  

Light and love grasp it in one circle, as doth it the others, and this engirdment he only who doth gird it understandeth.

- *d' un cerchio lui comprende* ‘with a circle it encompasses’ → “grasp it in one circle”: the means of enclosure becomes a phrase with “in” and moves to the end; the object follows the verb. “Grasp” replaces ‘encompasses’ and agrees in the plural with the paired subject where Italian has a singular verb.
- *sì come questo gli altri* ‘just as this one the others’ → “as doth it the others”: the emphatic ‘just’ is not separately expressed, the omitted verb is represented by “doth”, and the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun.
- *quel precinto* ‘that enclosure’ → “this engirdment”: the distant demonstrative becomes a near one, and the noun is rendered by one formed from the verb used for girding.
- *colui che il cinge solamente intende* ‘he who it girds alone understands’ → “he only who doth gird it understandeth”: “only” is placed immediately after “he”, making its scope explicit; the simple relative-clause verb takes an auxiliary, and its object follows it.

## Lines 115–148 — Time, Greed, and the Promise of True Fruit

> 115 Non è suo moto per altro distinto;  

Its movement by no other is marked out;

- *Non è suo moto per altro distinto* ‘not is its movement by another distinguished’ → “Its movement by no other is marked out”: the subject comes first, the negative passes from the verb to “no other,” and the two parts of the passive come together at the end; ‘distinguished’ becomes “marked out.”

> 116 ma gli altri son misurati da questo,  
> 117 sì come dieci da mezzo e da quinto.  

but by it all the rest are measured, as ten by half and fifth.

- *gli altri* ‘the others’ → “all the rest”: “all” is supplied, and a collective expression replaces the plural.
- *son misurati da questo* ‘are measured by this’ → “by it all the rest are measured”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun, and the phrase denoting the measure moves ahead of the subject.
- *sì come* ‘just as’ → “as”: the emphatic *sì* has no separate equivalent.
- *e da quinto* ‘and by a fifth’ → “and fifth”: the second preposition is not repeated.

> 118 E come il tempo tenga in cotal testo  
> 119 le sue radici e negli altri le fronde,  
> 120 omai a te puot' esser manifesto.  

And how Time in this same vessel hath its roots, and in the rest its leaves, may now be manifest to thee.

- *tenga in cotal testo le sue radici* ‘holds in such a pot its roots’ → “in this same vessel hath its roots”: “this same” identifies rather than characterizes the vessel; “vessel” is less specific than ‘pot.’ The place phrase moves before the verb, and the subjunctive of ‘hold’ in the indirect question becomes the indicative of ‘have.’
- *negli altri le fronde* ‘in the others the leaves’ → “in the rest its leaves”: “the rest” replaces the plural, and the possessive replaces the article.
- *omai a te puot' esser manifesto* ‘by now to thee can be manifest’ → “may now be manifest to thee”: “now” follows the modal, ‘can’ becomes “may,” and the phrase naming the person comes last.

> 121 O cupidigia, che i mortali affonde  
> 122 sì sotto te, che nessuno ha potere  
> 123 di trarre gli occhi fuor delle tue onde!  

O greed, who so dost abase mortals below thee, that not one hath power to draw his eyes forth from thy waves!

- *che i mortali affonde sì sotto te* ‘that sinkest the mortals so far beneath thee’ → “who so dost abase mortals below thee”: “who” personalizes the relative; “so” moves from the phrase of depth to the verb. ‘Sink’ becomes “abase,” with auxiliary “dost,” and the object loses its article and follows the verb.
- *nessuno* ‘no one’ → “not one”: the negative is expressed separately.
- *gli occhi* ‘the eyes’ → “his eyes”: a possessive replaces the article.

> 124 Ben fiorisce negli uomini il volere;  

’Tis true the will in men hath vigour yet;

- *Ben fiorisce negli uomini il volere* ‘indeed flourishes in men the will’ → “’Tis true the will in men hath vigour yet”: the emphatic adverb becomes an introductory concession, the subject moves ahead of the place phrase, and ‘flourishes’ becomes “hath vigour,” losing the flower image. “yet” makes continuance explicit.

> 125 ma la pioggia continua converte  
> 126 in bozzacchioni le susine vere.  

but the continuous drench turneth true plum fruits into cankered tubers.

- *la pioggia continua* ‘the rain continuous’ → “the continuous drench”: the adjective precedes the noun, and “drench” stresses the soaking rather than the rain itself.
- *converte in bozzacchioni le susine vere* ‘turns into blighted plums the plums true’ → “turneth true plum fruits into cankered tubers”: the object moves before the result phrase, loses its article, and takes its adjective first. “plum fruits” expands ‘plums’; “cankered tubers” describes the spoiled growths rather than naming them as defective plums.

> 127 Fede ed innocenza son reperte  
> 128 solo nei parvoletti; poi ciascuna  
> 129 pria fugge che le guance sien coperte.  

Faith and innocence are found only in little children; then each of them fleeth away before the cheeks are covered.

- *nei parvoletti* ‘in the little children’ → “in little children”: English omits the generic article.
- *ciascuna* ‘each’ → “each of them”: the group referred to is made explicit.
- *pria fugge che le guance sien coperte* ‘sooner flees than the cheeks be covered’ → “fleeth away before the cheeks are covered”: “away” expands the verb, and the separated *pria … che* becomes the single conjunction “before” after the verb. The subjunctive becomes indicative.

> 130 Tale, balbuziendo ancor, digiuna,  
> 131 che poi divora, con la lingua sciolta,  
> 132 qualunque cibo per qualunque luna;  

Many a still lisping child observeth fast, who after, when his tongue is free, devoureth every food in every month;

- *Tale, balbuziendo ancor* ‘such a one, lisping still’ → “Many a still lisping child”: the indefinite singular becomes distributive, “child” is supplied, and the circumstantial gerund becomes an attributive participle with the adverb before it.
- *digiuna* ‘fasts’ → “observeth fast”: a verb becomes a verb and its object.
- *divora, con la lingua sciolta* ‘devours, with the tongue loosened’ → “when his tongue is free, devoureth”: the accompanying circumstance becomes a finite temporal clause placed before the verb; a possessive replaces the article, and “free” replaces the participle.
- *qualunque cibo per qualunque luna* ‘whatever food in whatever moon’ → “every food in every month”: unrestricted choice becomes distributive “every” in both places, and the lunar period is called a “month.”

> 133 e tal, balbuziendo, ama ed ascolta  
> 134 la madre sua, che, con loquela intera,  
> 135 disira poi di vederla sepolta.  

and many a lisping child loveth and hearkeneth to his mother, who after, when his speech is full, longeth to see her buried.

- *tal, balbuziendo* ‘such a one, lisping’ → “many a lisping child”: the indefinite pronoun becomes a distributive expression with “child” supplied, and the gerund becomes an attributive participle.
- *ascolta* ‘listens to’ → “hearkeneth to”: the English verb suggests heed as well as hearing and takes a preposition where the Italian takes a direct object.
- *la madre sua* ‘the mother his’ → “his mother”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *con loquela intera* ‘with speech complete’ → “when his speech is full”: a prepositional phrase becomes a finite temporal clause; the possessor is specified and the adjective becomes predicative.
- *disira poi di vederla sepolta* ‘desires afterward to see her buried’ → “after, when his speech is full, longeth to see her buried”: the time adverb moves ahead of the intervening clause, and “longeth” is stronger than ‘desires.’

> 136 Così si fa la pelle bianca, nera,  
> 137 nel primo aspetto, della bella figlia  
> 138 di quei ch'apporta mane e lascia sera.  

So blackeneth at the first aspect the white skin of his fair daughter who bringeth morn and leaveth evening.

- *si fa la pelle bianca, nera, nel primo aspetto* ‘becomes the skin white, black, at the first aspect’ → “blackeneth at the first aspect the white skin”: the reflexive verb of becoming and its predicate ‘black’ are compressed into an intransitive verb; ‘white’ moves before the noun, and the phrase of time moves from after the predicate adjective to before the subject.
- *della bella figlia di quei* ‘of the fair daughter of him’ → “of his fair daughter”: the possessor following ‘daughter’ becomes a possessive preceding “fair daughter”; the following relative still refers to the possessor.

> 139 Tu, perchè non ti facci maraviglia,  
> 140 pensa che in terra non è chi governi;  
> 141 onde sì svia l'umana famiglia.  

And thou, lest thou make marvel at it, reflect that there is none to govern upon earth, wherefore the human household so strayeth from the path.

- *Tu* ‘thou’ → “And thou”: the connective is supplied.
- *perchè non ti facci maraviglia* ‘in order that thou make not wonder for thyself’ → “lest thou make marvel at it”: “lest” incorporates the negative purpose, the reflexive has no separate equivalent, and “at it” specifies the object of wonder.
- *in terra non è chi governi* ‘on earth there is not one who governs’ → “there is none to govern upon earth”: the place phrase moves to the end; the negative existential is recast with “none,” and the subjunctive relative clause becomes an infinitive.
- *sì svia l'umana famiglia* ‘so strays the human family’ → “the human household so strayeth from the path”: the subject precedes the verb, ‘family’ becomes “household,” and “from the path” brings out the sense implicit in *svia*.

> 142 Ma prima che gennaio tutto si sverni,  
> 143 per la centesma ch' è laggiù negletta,  
> 144 ruggiran sì questi cerchi superni  

But, ere that January be all unwintered by that hundredth part neglected upon earth, so shall these upper circles roar

- *tutto si sverni* ‘wholly winters itself out’ → “be all unwintered”: the reflexive verb for passing out of winter is recast with “be” and a participle.
- *per la centesma* ‘through the hundredth’ → “by that hundredth part”: a demonstrative replaces the article, and “part” supplies the noun implicit in the fraction.
- *ch' è laggiù negletta* ‘which is down there neglected’ → “neglected upon earth”: the finite relative clause becomes a participial phrase, and the place indicated by ‘down there’ is named after the participle.
- *ruggiran sì questi cerchi superni* ‘will roar so these circles upper’ → “so shall these upper circles roar”: the degree adverb comes first; the future is expressed by an auxiliary before the subject and the main verb after it. The adjective precedes its noun.

> 145 che la fortuna, che tanto s'aspetta,  
> 146 le poppe volgerà u' son le prore,  
> 147 sì che la classe correrà diretta;  

that the fated season so long awaited shall turn round the poops where are the prows, so that the fleet shall have straight course;

- *la fortuna* ‘the fortune’ → “the fated season”: the noun is interpreted as an appointed time rather than rendered as ‘fortune.’
- *che tanto s'aspetta* ‘which is so much awaited’ → “so long awaited”: the relative clause becomes a participial phrase, and degree is interpreted as duration.
- *le poppe volgerà* ‘the poops will turn’ → “shall turn round the poops”: the object moves after the verb, and “round” makes the reversal explicit.
- *correrà diretta* ‘will run straight’ → “shall have straight course”: the verb of motion becomes “have” with the supplied noun “course”; the adjective describing the fleet's movement now qualifies that noun.

> 148 e vero frutto verrà dopo il fiore.”  

and true fruit shall follow on the flower.”

- *verrà dopo il fiore* ‘will come after the flower’ → “shall follow on the flower”: the relation expressed by ‘come after’ is recast with the verb “follow” and the preposition “on.”

## Summary — English Syntax and Italian Emphasis

Wicksteed often changes the order of a clause while keeping its emphasis. In *m’ inebbriava il dolce canto*, the song comes last and the person affected comes first; in “the sweet song intoxicated me” their positions are reversed. An adjective likewise moves before its noun in *mente divina*, “divine mind”. Yet Wicksteed does not invariably prefer ordinary English order: for *ineffabile allegrezza* he chooses “gladness unspeakable”, and he keeps the threefold repetition of *il loco mio* as “my place, my place, my place”.

Changes of construction often make a relation more explicit. The gerund and its subject in *dicend’ io*, ‘I speaking’, become the finite clause “as I speak”; the phrase *senza brama*, ‘without longing’, becomes “that hath no longing”. The reverse change occurs when *che mi fur concesse*, ‘which were granted to me’, is shortened to the participial phrase “given in grant to me”. Two routine adjustments generally pass unmarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where Italian gives the person by the verb ending, and drops the article before a possessive, as in *la mia donna*, “my Lady”.

Word choice can change the force of an image without changing the construction: *cloaca*, ‘sewer’, becomes the less specific “conduit”, while *fiorisce*, ‘flowers’, loses its link with the later flower and fruit in “hath vigour yet”. Elsewhere a small addition or omission shifts the emphasis: “both” underscores the pairing of hearing and sight, while the repeated *di* in *d’ amore e di pace* needs only one “of” in “of love and peace”. Where the Italian turns on a striking choice of word, however, Wicksteed can be exact even at the cost of unfamiliar English. In *altro dove*, ‘other where’, he keeps “other *where*” rather than substituting an ordinary noun for place.
