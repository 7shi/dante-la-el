# Paradiso 19

## Lines 1–15 — The Eagle Speaks in the Singular

> 1 Parea dinanzi a me con l' ali aperte  
> 2 la bella image, che, nel dolce *frui*  
> 3 liete, facevan l' anime conserte.  

With outstretched wings appeared before me the fair image which those enwoven souls, rejoicing in their sweet fruition, made.

- *Parea dinanzi a me con l’ ali aperte* ‘appeared before me with the wings open’ → “With outstretched wings appeared before me”: the phrase describing the wings comes first; the article is dropped, and the adjective moves before the noun and specifies extension rather than simply openness.
- *che, nel dolce frui liete, facevan l’ anime conserte* ‘which, in the sweet enjoyment glad, made the souls interwoven’ → “which those enwoven souls, rejoicing in their sweet fruition, made”: the subject comes before the verb, with a demonstrative for the article and “enwoven” before its noun. The adjective becomes the participle “rejoicing” and precedes its complement; the infinitive used as a noun becomes “fruition”, with a possessive for the article.

> 4 Parea ciascuna rubinetto, in cui  
> 5 raggio di sole ardesse sì acceso,  
> 6 che nei miei occhi rifrangesse lui.  

Each one appeared as a ruby whereon the sun’s ray should burn, enkindled so as to re-cast it on mine eyes.

- *Parea ciascuna rubinetto* ‘appeared each a little ruby’ → “Each one appeared as a ruby”: the subject moves before the verb, “as” makes the comparison explicit, and the diminutive is lost.
- *in cui* ‘in which’ → “whereon”: the relation changes from ‘in’ to ‘on’.
- *raggio di sole ardesse* ‘a ray of sun burned’ → “the sun’s ray should burn”: the indefinite ray with a following complement becomes a definite possessive phrase; “should burn” renders the imperfect subjunctive.
- *sì acceso, che nei miei occhi rifrangesse lui* ‘so enkindled that into my eyes it refracted him’ → “enkindled so as to re-cast it on mine eyes”: “so” moves after the participle and introduces an infinitive of result in place of the finite subjunctive clause. “re-cast” renders the turning back of the light; the object becomes neuter and comes before the phrase for the eyes, with “on” for ‘in’ or ‘into’.

> 7 E quel che mi convien ritrar testeso,  
> 8 non portò voce mai, nè scrisse inchiostro,  
> 9 nè fu per fantasia giammai compreso;  

And that which I must now retrace, nor ever voice conveyed, nor ink did write, nor ere by fantasy was comprehended;

- *mi convien ritrar testeso* ‘it behoves me to depict just now’ → “I must now retrace”: the impersonal verb becomes a personal modal; the adverb moves before the main verb, and depicting becomes retracing.
- *non portò voce mai* ‘no voice ever carried’ → “nor ever voice conveyed”: the simple negative becomes the first “nor” in a series, “ever” moves forward, and the postverbal subject precedes “conveyed”.
- *nè scrisse inchiostro* ‘nor wrote ink’ → “nor ink did write”: the subject comes before the verb, and the simple past is expanded with “did”.
- *nè fu per fantasia giammai compreso* ‘nor was by imagination ever comprehended’ → “nor ere by fantasy was comprehended”: “ere” takes the place of ‘ever’; it and the phrase for the imagining faculty precede the whole passive verb rather than standing between auxiliary and participle.

> 10 ch' io vidi, ed anche udii parlar lo rostro,  
> 11 e sonar nella voce ed *io* e *mio*,  
> 12 quand' era nel concetto *noi* e *nostro*.  

for I saw and eke I heard the beak discourse and utter in its voice both *I* and *Mine*, when in conception it was *We* and *Our*.

- *parlar lo rostro* ‘speak the beak’ → “the beak discourse”: the subject of the infinitive moves before it, and “discourse” renders the plainer verb ‘speak’.
- *e sonar nella voce ed io e mio* ‘and sound in the voice both I and my’ → “and utter in its voice both *I* and *Mine*”: on a reading in which the two words are what sound, they become the objects of the beak’s act of uttering. The article becomes a possessive, and “*Mine*” gives the possessive word in its independent English form.
- *quand’ era nel concetto noi e nostro* ‘when it was in the conception we and our’ → “when in conception it was *We* and *Our*”: the phrase for conception moves before the subject and verb and loses its article.

> 13 E cominciò: “Per esser giusto e pio  
> 14 son io qui esaltato a quella gloria,  
> 15 che non si lascia vincere a disio;  

And it began: “In that I was just and duteous am I here exalted to this glory which suffereth not itself to be surpassed by longing;

- *Per esser giusto e pio* ‘for being just and pious’ → “In that I was just and duteous”: the causal preposition and present infinitive become a finite causal clause in the past; “duteous” takes *pio* in the sense of fulfilling a duty.
- *a quella gloria* ‘to that glory’ → “to this glory”: the demonstrative changes from distant to near.
- *che non si lascia vincere a disio* ‘which does not let itself be overcome by desire’ → “which suffereth not itself to be surpassed by longing”: permission is expressed by “suffereth”, with the negative after the verb; the active infinitive with passive sense becomes explicitly passive, and overcoming is rendered as surpassing.

## Lines 16–48 — One Voice and the Limits of Understanding

> 16 ed in terra lasciai la mia memoria  
> 17 sì fatta, che le genti lì malvage  
> 18 commendan lei, ma non seguon la storia.”  

and upon earth have I left a memory, so fashioned that there the evil folk commend it, though they follow not the tale.”

- *lasciai la mia memoria* ‘I left my memory’ → “have I left a memory”: the simple past becomes a present perfect, with the subject after the auxiliary; the possessive gives way to an indefinite article.
- *le genti lì malvage* ‘the folk there evil’ → “there the evil folk”: the adverb moves before the whole subject, and the adjective before the noun.
- *lei* ‘her’ → “it”: English uses a neuter pronoun for the grammatically feminine antecedent.
- *ma non seguon* ‘but do not follow’ → “though they follow not”: coordination becomes concession, and the negative follows the verb.

> 19 Così un sol calor di molte brage  
> 20 si fa sentir, come di molti amori  
> 21 usciva solo un suon di quella image;  

So do we feel one glow from many coals as from those many loves there issued forth one only sound out of that image.

- *un sol calor di molte brage si fa sentir* ‘a single heat from many embers makes itself felt’ → “do we feel one glow from many coals”: the Italian subject becomes the object of an active verb with a new subject, “we”; the reflexive causative disappears. “One” does duty for article and adjective, and “glow” and “coals” replace ‘heat’ and ‘embers’.
- *di molti amori* ‘from many loves’ → “from those many loves”: the demonstrative is supplied.
- *usciva* ‘was coming out’ → “there issued forth”: a simple past renders the imperfect, and introductory “there” precedes the verb.
- *solo un suon* ‘only one sound’ → “one only sound”: “only” follows “one”.

> 22 ond' io appresso: “O perpetui fiori  
> 23 dell' eterna letizia, che pur uno  
> 24 parer mi fate tutti i vostri odori,  

Whereon straightway I: “O perpetual flowers of the eternal gladness, ye who make all your odours seem to me but one,

- *io appresso* ‘I next’ → “straightway I”: the adverb comes before the pronoun and suggests immediacy.
- *che* ‘who’ → “ye who”: the relative is given an explicit pronominal antecedent in addition to the vocative.
- *pur uno parer mi fate tutti i vostri odori* ‘but one seem to me you make all your odours’ → “make all your odours seem to me but one”: English puts the causative verb first, followed by its object, the infinitive, and finally the complement that Italian puts first.

> 25 solvetemi, spirando, il gran digiuno  
> 26 che lungamente m' ha tenuto in fame,  
> 27 non trovandogli in terra cibo alcuno.  

solve, as ye breathe, the great fast which long hath held me hungering, because on earth I found no food for it.

- *solvetemi* ‘loose for me’ → “solve”: the verb takes the form ordinarily used of resolving a difficulty, and the dative pronoun is not rendered.
- *spirando* ‘breathing’ → “as ye breathe”: a gerund becomes a finite temporal clause.
- *m' ha tenuto in fame* ‘me has held in hunger’ → “hath held me hungering”: the object follows the verb, and a participle replaces the prepositional phrase.
- *non trovandogli in terra cibo alcuno* ‘not finding for it on earth food any’ → “because on earth I found no food for it”: the gerund becomes an explicitly causal clause in the past tense. The place phrase moves before the verb, the dative to the end, and the verbal negative combines with the indefinite adjective as “no”.

> 28 Ben so io che, se in cielo altro reame  
> 29 la divina giustizia fa suo specchio,  
> 30 che 'l vostro non l' apprende con velame.  

Well do I know that if the divine justice maketh any other realm of heaven its mirror, yours apprehendeth it without a veil.

- *Ben so io* ‘well know I’ → “Well do I know”: English supplies the auxiliary for the inversion.
- *in cielo altro reame* ‘in heaven another realm’ → “any other realm of heaven”: “any” is supplied in the conditional, and the location becomes an *of*-phrase after the noun.
- *la divina giustizia fa suo specchio* ‘the divine justice makes its mirror’ → “the divine justice maketh” … “its mirror”: the subject and verb move ahead of the object, while the object complement stays after it.
- *che 'l vostro* ‘that yours’ → “yours”: the repeated introductory conjunction after the conditional clause is not repeated in English.
- *non l' apprende con velame* ‘does not apprehend it with a veil’ → “apprehendeth it without a veil”: negation passes from the verb to the preposition; the object pronoun follows the verb, and an indefinite article is supplied.

> 31 Sapete come attento io m' apparecchio  
> 32 ad ascoltar; sapete quale è quello  

Ye know how eager I prepare me to hearken;

- *attento* ‘attentive’ → “eager”: an adjective of readiness or desire replaces one of attention.
- *m' apparecchio* ‘myself I prepare’ → “I prepare me”: the reflexive pronoun follows the verb.

> 33 dubbio, che m' è digiun cotanto vecchio.”  

ye know what is that question which hath been to me a fast of so long date.”

- *sapete quale è quello dubbio* ‘you know what is that doubt’ → “ye know what is that question”: the doubt is expressed as a question. The opening words of this clause occur at the end of line 32; its noun opens line 33.
- *che m' è digiun cotanto vecchio* ‘which to me is a fast so old’ → “which hath been to me a fast of so long date”: the continuing present becomes a present perfect, the dative follows the verb, and an *of*-phrase expressing duration replaces the adjective of age.

> 34 Qual il falcon, ch' uscendo del cappello  
> 35 move la testa e coll' ali si plaude,  
> 36 voglia mostrando e facendosi bello,  

As the falcon issuing from the hood shaketh head and clappeth wings, showing his will and making himself beauteous,

- *Qual il falcon, ch' uscendo del cappello* ‘like the falcon that, coming out of the hood’ → “As the falcon issuing from the hood”: English makes the comparison a clause introduced by “As”, dispensing with the relative pronoun.
- *move la testa* ‘moves the head’ → “shaketh head”: the motion is made more specific and the article dropped.
- *coll' ali si plaude* ‘with its wings it applauds itself’ → “clappeth wings”: the instrument becomes the direct object, without article, and the reflexive disappears.
- *voglia mostrando* ‘desire showing’ → “showing his will”: the object follows the participle and receives a possessive.

> 37 vid' io farsi quel segno, che di laude  
> 38 della divina grazia era contesto,  
> 39 con canti quai si sa chi lassù gaude.  

such did I see that ensign which was woven of the praises of divine grace, with songs such as be known to whoso up there rejoiceth.

- *vid' io farsi quel segno* ‘I saw that sign make itself [such]’ → “such did I see that ensign”: the implied complement is set first, the reflexive infinitive disappears, and English uses an auxiliary for the inversion. “Ensign” specifies a kind of sign.
- *di laude della divina grazia era contesto* ‘of praise of the divine grace was woven’ → “was woven of the praises of divine grace”: the verb moves before its complement, singular ‘praise’ becomes a definite plural, and ‘divine grace’ loses its article.
- *quai si sa chi lassù gaude* ‘such as one knows who up there rejoices’ → “such as be known to whoso up there rejoiceth”: the Italian construction with impersonal *si* gives way to a passive, with the knower introduced by “to”.

> 40 Poi cominciò: “Colui che volse il sesto  
> 41 all' estremo del mondo, e dentro ad esso  
> 42 distinse tanto occulto e manifesto,  

Then it began: “He who rolled the compass round the limit of the universe, and within it marked out so much both hidden and revealed,

- *volse il sesto all' estremo del mondo* ‘turned the compass at the extremity of the world’ → “rolled the compass round the limit of the universe”: verb and preposition express the sweep around the boundary; ‘world’ is taken in the sense of ‘universe’.
- *distinse* ‘distinguished’ → “marked out”: a phrase of delineation replaces the single verb.
- *tanto occulto e manifesto* ‘so much hidden and manifest’ → “so much both hidden and revealed”: “both” is supplied, and ‘manifest’ becomes “revealed”.

> 43 non potè suo valor sì fare impresso  
> 44 in tutto l' universo, che il suo verbo  
> 45 non rimanesse in infinito eccesso.  

could not so stamp his worth on all the universe but that his word remained in infinite excess.

- *suo valor sì fare impresso* ‘his worth so cause to be imprinted’ → “so stamp his worth”: the causative and participle become a single active verb, with its object after it.
- *in tutto l' universo* ‘in all the universe’ → “on all the universe”: the preposition changes with the choice of “stamp”.
- *che il suo verbo non rimanesse* ‘that his word should not remain’ → “but that his word remained”: the negative result clause with imperfect subjunctive becomes an affirmative clause with indicative after “but that”.

> 46 E ciò fa certo che il primo superbo,  
> 47 che fu la somma d' ogni creatura,  
> 48 per non aspettar lume, cadde acerbo:  

And this is certified by that first proud being, who was the summit of all creation, because he would not wait for light, falling unripe;

- *ciò fa certo che il primo superbo* ‘this the fact makes certain, that the first proud one …’ → “this is certified by that first proud being”: *ciò*, the object of *fa certo*, becomes the passive subject. Italian presents the fact of the fall as the proof; English makes the being the agent of “certified”, supplying a noun for the substantivized adjective.
- *d' ogni creatura* ‘of every creature’ → “of all creation”: a distributive expression becomes a collective one.
- *per non aspettar lume* ‘for not waiting for light’ → “because he would not wait for light”: the causal infinitive becomes a finite clause, with “would not” suggesting unwillingness.
- *cadde acerbo* ‘fell unripe’ → “falling unripe”: the finite verb of the Italian *che*-clause becomes a participle.

## Lines 49–81 — The Limits of Sight in Judging Divine Justice

> 49 e quinci appar ch' ogni minor natura  
> 50 è corto recettacolo a quel bene  
> 51 che non ha fine, e sè con sè misura.  

and hence it is apparent that each lesser nature is a receptacle too scant for that good which hath not end, and itself measureth with itself.

- *appar* ‘appears’ → “it is apparent”: the impersonal verb becomes a phrase with an anticipatory “it” and a predicate adjective.
- *corto recettacolo* ‘scant receptacle’ → “a receptacle too scant”: the article and the degree word “too” are supplied, and the adjective follows the noun.
- *non ha fine* ‘not hath end’ → “hath not end”: the negative follows the verb.
- *sè con sè misura* ‘itself with itself measureth’ → “itself measureth with itself”: the prepositional phrase moves after the verb.

> 52 Dunque nostra veduta, che conviene  
> 53 essere alcun dei raggi della mente  
> 54 di che tutte le cose son ripiene,  

Wherefore our sight, which needs must be one of the rays of that mind whereby all things are filled,

- *che conviene essere alcun* ‘which it is necessary should be some one’ → “which needs must be one”: the impersonal expression of necessity becomes a modal phrase with “which” as subject; the indefinite *alcun* is reduced to “one”.
- *della mente* ‘of the mind’ → “of that mind”: a demonstrative replaces the article.
- *di che tutte le cose son ripiene* ‘of which all the things are full’ → “whereby all things are filled”: the complement of fullness becomes an expression of agency or means, ‘full’ becomes “filled”, and the article before *cose* drops out.

> 55 non può da sua natura esser possente  
> 56 tanto che suo principio non discerna  
> 57 molto di là, da quel che l' è parvente.  

cannot of its nature have so great power but that its principle should discern far beyond that which unto it appeareth.

- *non può da sua natura esser possente tanto* ‘cannot by its nature be powerful to such a degree’ → “cannot of its nature have so great power”: “of” replaces ‘by’; being powerful becomes having power, and the degree word, rather than following the adjective, precedes “great”.
- *che suo principio non discerna* ‘that it should not discern its principle’ → “but that its principle should discern”: on the reading in which sight remains the subject, *suo principio* is the object placed before the verb. Wicksteed instead makes “its principle” the subject; “but that” takes up the negative construction.
- *molto di là, da quel* ‘much farther than that’ → “far beyond that”: the comparative expression is recast with the preposition “beyond”.
- *che l' è parvente* ‘which to it is apparent’ → “which unto it appeareth”: the copula and predicate adjective become a single verb.

> 58 Però nella giustizia sempiterna  
> 59 la vista che riceve il vostro mondo,  
> 60 com' occhio per lo mar, dentro s' interna;  

Wherefore in the eternal justice such sight as your world doth receive, like the eye in the ocean, is absorbed;

- *nella giustizia sempiterna* ‘in the justice everlasting’ → “in the eternal justice”: “eternal” precedes the noun.
- *la vista che riceve il vostro mondo* ‘the sight which receives your world’ → “such sight as your world doth receive”: the article and relative become the correlative “such … as”; *il vostro mondo*, the postverbal subject of *riceve*, precedes the verb in English, and “doth” is supplied.
- *com' occhio per lo mar* ‘like an eye through the sea’ → “like the eye in the ocean”: English supplies a definite article for the generic noun, changes ‘through’ to the locative “in”, and uses “ocean” for ‘sea’.
- *dentro s' interna* ‘inward it enters’ → “is absorbed”: the inward motion of the pronominal verb becomes a passive, with no separate word for *dentro*.

> 61 chè, benchè dalla proda veggia il fondo,  
> 62 in pelago nol vede, e non di meno  
> 63 è lì, ma cela lui l' esser profondo.  

for, albeit it can see the bottom by the shore, in the open sea it seeth it not, and none the less ’tis there, but the depth it hath concealeth it.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a coordinating conjunction replaces the subordinating one.
- *dalla proda veggia il fondo* ‘from the shore it see the bottom’ → “it can see the bottom by the shore”: the viewing point becomes a location and moves to the end; “can” adds the sense of ability to the subjunctive governed by *benchè*.
- *in pelago* ‘in open sea’ → “in the open sea”: a single noun is expanded into adjective and noun, with an article.
- *nol vede* ‘not it seeth’ → “it seeth it not”: both the object pronoun and the negative move after the verb.
- *cela lui l' esser profondo* ‘conceals it the being deep’ → “the depth it hath concealeth it”: the subject moves from last to first, and the nominalized infinitive with its adjective becomes a noun qualified by a clause of possession.

> 64 Lume non è, se non vien dal sereno  
> 65 che non si turba mai, anzi è tenebra,  
> 66 od ombra della carne, o suo veleno.  

There is no light unless from that serene which never is disturbed, else is it darkness or shadow of the flesh or else its poison.

- *Lume non è* ‘light is not’ → “There is no light”: an existential construction replaces the initial noun, and the negative modifies “light” rather than the verb.
- *se non vien dal sereno* ‘if it does not come from the clear sky’ → “unless from that serene”: “unless” combines condition and negation, the verb of coming drops out, and a demonstrative replaces the article before the substantivized “serene”.
- *che non si turba mai* ‘which does not grow troubled ever’ → “which never is disturbed”: the negative and *mai* become a single preverbal adverb, and the pronominal verb becomes passive.
- *anzi è tenebra* ‘nay rather it is darkness’ → “else is it darkness”: the corrective conjunction becomes an expression of an alternative, with the verb before its subject.
- *o suo veleno* ‘or its poison’ → “or else its poison”: “else” is supplied.

> 67 Assai t' è mo aperta la latebra,  
> 68 che t' ascondeva la giustizia viva,  
> 69 di che facei question cotanto crebra;  

Enough is opened to thee now the labyrinth which hid from thee the living justice of which thou hast made question so incessantly;

- *t' è mo aperta* ‘to thee is now opened’ → “is opened to thee now”: the indirect object moves after the participle, and “now” follows it.
- *la latebra* ‘the hiding place’ → “the labyrinth”: a place of concealment is rendered as a maze.
- *t' ascondeva* ‘from thee was hiding’ → “hid from thee”: the pronoun moves after the verb in a prepositional phrase, and the imperfect becomes a simple past.
- *la giustizia viva* ‘the justice living’ → “the living justice”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *di che facei question cotanto crebra* ‘of which thou wast making question so frequent’ → “of which thou hast made question so incessantly”: the imperfect becomes a present perfect, and the adjective describing the frequency of the questioning becomes an adverb modifying “hast made”.

> 70 chè tu dicevi: ‘Un uom nasce alla riva  
> 71 dell' Indo, e quivi non è chi ragioni  
> 72 di Cristo, nè chi legga, nè chi scriva;  

for thou didst say: ‘A man is born upon the bank of Indus and there is none to tell of Christ, nor none to read, nor none to write;

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a coordinating conjunction replaces the subordinating one.
- *tu dicevi* ‘thou wast saying’ → “thou didst say”: the imperfect becomes a simple past formed with “didst”.
- *alla riva dell' Indo* ‘at the bank of the Indus’ → “upon the bank of Indus”: “upon” replaces ‘at’, and the article before the river name drops out.
- *e quivi non è chi ragioni di Cristo* ‘and there is not anyone who may speak of Christ’ → “and there is none to tell of Christ”: the locative *quivi* is not distinguished from existential “there”; “none” takes up the negation, and an infinitive replaces the relative subjunctive.
- *nè chi legga, nè chi scriva* ‘nor anyone who may read, nor anyone who may write’ → “nor none to read, nor none to write”: each indefinite relative with a subjunctive becomes “none” with an infinitive; the negative is expressed in the pronoun as well as in “nor”.

> 73 e tutti i suoi voleri ed atti buoni  
> 74 sono, quanto ragione umana vede,  
> 75 senza peccato in vita o in sermoni.  

and all his volitions and his deeds are good so far as human reason seeth, sinless in life or in discourse.

- *i suoi voleri ed atti* ‘his volitions and deeds’ → “his volitions and his deeds”: the possessive shared by the two nouns is repeated.
- *buoni sono* ‘good are’ → “are good”: the predicate adjective follows the copula.
- *quanto ragione umana vede* ‘as much as reason human sees’ → “so far as human reason seeth”: extent becomes “so far as”, and the adjective precedes its noun.
- *senza peccato* ‘without sin’ → “sinless”: the prepositional phrase becomes an adjective.
- *o in sermoni* ‘or in utterances’ → “or in discourse”: a singular collective replaces the plural.

> 76 More non battezzato e senza fede;  

He dieth unbaptised and without faith;

- *non battezzato* ‘not baptised’ → “unbaptised”: a negative prefix replaces the separate negative.

> 77 ov' è questa giustizia che il condanna?  

where is that justice which condemneth him?

- *questa giustizia* ‘this justice’ → “that justice”: the demonstrative changes from near to remote.
- *il condanna* ‘him condemns’ → “condemneth him”: the object pronoun follows the verb.

> 78 ov' è la colpa sua, s' egli non crede?’  

where is his fault, in that he not believes?’

- *la colpa sua* ‘the fault his’ → “his fault”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *s' egli non crede* ‘if he does not believe’ → “in that he not believes”: the condition becomes a clause specifying what the fault would consist in.

> 79 Or tu chi sei, che vuoi sedere a scranna  
> 80 per giudicar da lungi mille miglia  
> 81 con la veduta corta d' una spanna?  

Now who art thou who wouldst sit upon the seat to judge at a thousand miles away with the short sight that carries but a span?

- *tu chi sei* ‘thou who art’ → “who art thou”: the interrogative comes first, and the subject follows the verb.
- *che vuoi sedere a scranna* ‘who wishest to sit on a judgment seat’ → “who wouldst sit upon the seat”: the verb of willing becomes a modal; the definite article is supplied, and the specifically judicial sense of *scranna* is left to the context.
- *per giudicar da lungi mille miglia* ‘to judge from afar a thousand miles’ → “to judge at a thousand miles away”: “at” replaces the preposition of vantage point, and the distance precedes “away”.
- *con la veduta corta d' una spanna* ‘with the sight short of a span’ → “with the short sight that carries but a span”: the adjective moves before the noun, and the phrase of measure becomes a relative clause with an added verb of reach and a restrictive “but”.

## Lines 82–114 — Divine Will and Eternal Judgment

> 82 Certo a colui che meco s' assottiglia,  
> 83 se la scrittura sopra voi non fosse,  
> 84 da dubitar sarebbe a maraviglia.  

Truly to him who goeth subtly to work with me, were not the Scripture over you, there were marvellous ground for questioning.

- *meco s' assottiglia* ‘with me sharpens his wits’ → “goeth subtly to work with me”: the reflexive verb becomes a verbal phrase in which an adverb expresses the idea of subtlety; the phrase of accompaniment moves to the end.
- *se la scrittura sopra voi non fosse* ‘if the Scripture over you were not’ → “were not the Scripture over you”: inversion takes the place of the conditional conjunction, and the verb and negative precede the subject.
- *da dubitar sarebbe a maraviglia* ‘for doubting it would be to a marvel’ → “there were marvellous ground for questioning”: the conditional becomes an existential subjunctive with the noun “ground” supplied; the closing noun of degree becomes an adjective, and the infinitive of doubting becomes a gerund at the end.

> 85 O terreni animali, o menti grosse!  

O animals of earth, minds gross!

- *terreni animali* ‘earthly animals’ → “animals of earth”: the preceding adjective becomes a following prepositional phrase.
- *o menti* ‘O minds’ → “minds”: the second vocative particle is omitted.

> 86 la prima volontà, ch' è per sè buona,  
> 87 da sè, che è sommo ben, mai non si mosse.  

the primal Will, good in itself, never departed from its own self which is the highest good.

- *ch' è per sè buona* ‘which is of itself good’ → “good in itself”: the relative and copula drop out; the adjective moves before its qualifying phrase, and ‘of’ becomes “in”.
- *da sè, che è sommo ben, mai non si mosse* ‘from itself, which is highest good, never did it move’ → “never departed from its own self which is the highest good”: the negative verb moves before its complement; the reflexive verb of motion becomes “departed”, and the Italian negative pair is expressed by “never” alone. The emphatic “its own self” expands the reflexive pronoun, and “the” is supplied before the superlative.

> 88 Cotanto è giusto, quanto a lei consuona;  

All is just which doth harmonise with it;

- *Cotanto è giusto, quanto* ‘so much is just, as’ → “All is just which”: the correlatives of extent become a universal pronoun and a relative.
- *a lei consuona* ‘with it accords’ → “doth harmonise with it”: the complement follows the verb, which is expressed with the auxiliary “doth”.

> 89 nullo creato bene a sè la tira,  
> 90 ma essa, radiando, lui cagiona.”  

no created good draweth it to itself, but it by raying forth giveth rise to it.”

- *a sè la tira* ‘to itself it draws’ → “draweth it to itself”: the verb moves before its object and the phrase of direction comes last.
- *radiando* ‘radiating’ → “by raying forth”: “by” explicitly marks means, and “forth” expresses the outward direction.
- *lui cagiona* ‘it causes’ → “giveth rise to it”: the transitive verb becomes a verbal phrase, with the object following a preposition instead of preceding the verb.

> 91 Quale sopr' esso il nido si rigira  
> 92 poi che ha pasciuto la cicogna i figli,  
> 93 e come quei ch' è pasto la rimira;  

As right above her nest the stork sweepeth when she hath fed her brood, and as the one which she hath fed looketh up to her;

- *sopr' esso il nido* ‘right above the nest’ → “right above her nest”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *si rigira* ‘circles round’ → “sweepeth”: a verb of sweeping motion replaces the reflexive verb of turning.
- *poi che ha pasciuto la cicogna i figli* ‘after the stork has fed the young’ → “the stork sweepeth when she hath fed her brood”: the delayed subject, shared by the two verbs, is placed before the main verb and resumed by “she” in the time clause. ‘After’ becomes “when”, and the plural with a definite article becomes a collective singular with a possessive.
- *quei ch' è pasto* ‘the one that is fed’ → “the one which she hath fed”: the passive becomes an active perfect; the relative becomes the object and the agent is made explicit.
- *la rimira* ‘gazes at her’ → “looketh up to her”: the upward direction is supplied, and the preceding object becomes a following prepositional complement.

> 94 cotal si fece, e sì levai li cigli,  
> 95 la benedetta imagine, che l' ali  
> 96 movea sospinta da tanti consigli.  

so did (and so did I uplift my brow) the blessed image, which plied its wings driven by so many counsels.

- *cotal si fece* ‘such did it make itself’ → “so did”: the reflexive construction with a predicate adjective becomes an adverb and a verb standing for the preceding action.
- *e sì levai li cigli* ‘and so I raised the brows’ → “and so did I uplift my brow”: the auxiliary precedes the subject, and the definite plural becomes a possessive singular.
- *che l' ali movea* ‘which the wings moved’ → “which plied its wings”: the object follows the verb, ‘moved’ becomes “plied”, and the definite article becomes a possessive.

> 97 Roteando cantava, e dicea: “Quali  
> 98 son le mie note a te, che non le intendi,  
> 99 tal è il giudizio eterno a voi mortali.”  

Wheeling it sang, and said: “As are my notes to thee who understandest them not, such is the eternal judgment to you mortals.”

- *Quali son* ‘of what sort are’ → “As are”: the qualitative adjective becomes the comparative “As”, paired with “such”.
- *non le intendi* ‘not them understandest’ → “understandest them not”: both object and negative move after the verb.
- *il giudizio eterno* ‘the judgment eternal’ → “the eternal judgment”: the adjective precedes the noun.

> 100 Poi si quetaron quei lucenti incendi  
> 101 dello Spirito Santo ancor nel segno,  
> 102 che fe' i Romani al mondo reverendi,  

When those glowing flames of the Holy Spirit were stilled, yet in the ensign which gained the Romans reverence from all the world,

- *Poi si quetaron* ‘then grew quiet’ → “When those glowing flames of the Holy Spirit were stilled”: “When” makes the clause subordinate to the next action; the reflexive verb becomes passive in form and follows its subject.
- *quei lucenti incendi* ‘those shining fires’ → “those glowing flames”: “glowing” replaces ‘shining’, and “flames” renders the fires.
- *Spirito Santo* ‘Spirit Holy’ → “Holy Spirit”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *ancor nel segno* ‘still in the sign’ → “yet in the ensign”: “yet” has the temporal sense of ‘still’; “ensign” gives the sign a specifically emblematic sense.
- *che fe' i Romani al mondo reverendi* ‘which made the Romans to the world worthy of reverence’ → “which gained the Romans reverence from all the world”: the Romans become recipients rather than the object of ‘made’; the predicate adjective becomes the noun “reverence”, and the world becomes its source, with “all” supplied.

> 103 esso ricominciò: “A questo regno  
> 104 non salì mai chi non credette in Cristo,  
> 105 nè pria, nè poi ch' ei si chiavasse al legno.  

it began again: “To this realm ne’er rose one who believed not in Christ, neither before nor after he was nailed unto the tree.

- *ricominciò* ‘began anew’ → “began again”: a separate adverb does the work of the verbal prefix.
- *non salì mai chi* ‘did not ever ascend whoever’ → “ne’er rose one who”: the negative pair becomes the single adverb “ne’er” before the verb, and the free relative is expanded into antecedent and relative.
- *non credette* ‘did not believe’ → “believed not”: the negative follows the verb.
- *nè pria, nè poi ch'* ‘neither before, nor after that’ → “neither before nor after”: the clause that follows is introduced without a separate equivalent of ‘that’.
- *ei si chiavasse al legno* ‘he should be nailed to the wood’ → “he was nailed unto the tree”: the passive expressed with *si* and an imperfect subjunctive becomes an English past indicative passive; the material, ‘wood’, is rendered as “tree”.

> 106 Ma, vedi, molti gridan ‘Cristo, Cristo,’  
> 107 che saranno in giudizio assai men *prope*  
> 108 a lui, che tal che non conosce Cristo;  

But see, many cry Christ, Christ, who at the judgment shall be far less near to him than such as know not Christ;

- *che saranno in giudizio* ‘who will be in judgment’ → “who at the judgment shall be”: the prepositional phrase moves before the verb and takes a definite article; “at” replaces ‘in’.
- *assai men prope a lui* ‘much less near to him’ → “far less near to him”: “far” gives the degree of the comparative, and the Latin *prope* is rendered in English.
- *tal che non conosce Cristo* ‘one who does not know Christ’ → “such as know not Christ”: the singular becomes plural, “as” introduces the relative clause, and the negative follows the verb.

> 109 e tai Cristiani dannerà l' Etiope,  
> 110 quando si partiranno i due collegi,  
> 111 l' uno in eterno ricco, e l' altro inope.  

and such Christians the Ethiop shall condemn when the two colleges shall dispart, the one for ever rich, the other stripped.

- *dannerà l' Etiope* ‘shall condemn the Ethiop’ → “the Ethiop shall condemn”: the subject, which follows the verb in Italian, precedes it in English.
- *si partiranno i due collegi* ‘shall part the two colleges’ → “the two colleges shall dispart”: the subject moves before the verb, and the pronominal verb becomes an intransitive verb without a pronoun.
- *e l' altro inope* ‘and the other needy’ → “the other stripped”: the conjunction is omitted, and a participle suggesting deprivation replaces the adjective of want.

> 112 Che potran dir li Persi ai vostri regi,  
> 113 come vedranno quel volume aperto,  
> 114 nel qual si scrivon tutti i suoi dispregi?  

What may the Persians say unto your kings when they shall see that volume opened wherein are their dispraises all recorded?

- *potran dir li Persi* ‘will be able to say the Persians’ → “may the Persians say”: the future of ability becomes the modal “may”, and the subject moves between the modal and the main verb.
- *nel qual si scrivon tutti i suoi dispregi* ‘in which are written all their acts of contempt’ → “wherein are their dispraises all recorded”: the preposition and relative become “wherein”; the passive expressed with *si* becomes “are … recorded”, with the subject between auxiliary and participle. “All” follows rather than precedes the noun phrase, and “their dispraises” takes the final noun as things recorded to their discredit rather than as acts of scorning.

## Lines 115–148 — The Record of Kings’ Misdeeds

> 115 Lì si vedrà tra l' opere d' Alberto  
> 116 quella che tosto moverà la penna,  
> 117 per che il regno di Praga fia deserto.  

There shall be seen amidst the deeds of Albert that one which soon shall move its wing to make the realm of Prague a desert.

- *si vedrà* ‘will one see’ → “shall be seen”: the Italian *si* construction becomes an English passive.
- *quella* ‘that’ → “that one”: the feminine demonstrative stands for a deed; English supplies “one”.
- *la penna* ‘the pen’ → “its wing”: the translation takes the word in its sense of a feather or wing rather than a writing pen, and makes possession explicit.
- *per che il regno di Praga fia deserto* ‘whereby the realm of Prague will be deserted’ → “to make the realm of Prague a desert”: a relative clause of consequence becomes a causative infinitive; the Italian subject becomes its object, and the predicate adjective becomes a noun with an article.

> 118 Lì si vedrà il duol che sopra Senna  
> 119 induce, falseggiando la moneta,  
> 120 quei che morrà di colpo di cotenna.  

There shall be seen the woe which he is bringing on the Seine by making false the coinage, who by the wild boar’s stroke shall die.

- *si vedrà* ‘will one see’ → “shall be seen”: English uses the passive.
- *che sopra Senna induce* ‘which upon the Seine brings’ → “which he is bringing on the Seine”: the verb moves before the place phrase and becomes progressive; “he” anticipates the subject that Italian withholds until the next line but one.
- *falseggiando la moneta* ‘falsifying the coinage’ → “by making false the coinage”: “by” specifies means, and the single verb is unpacked into a verb and an adjective.
- *quei che morrà di colpo di cotenna* ‘he who will die of a blow of hide’ → “who by the wild boar’s stroke shall die”: the demonstrative subject has been anticipated, leaving only the relative here. The cause precedes the verb; the hide stands for the animal, which English names outright, and the second *di* phrase becomes a possessive.

> 121 Lì si vedrà la superbia ch' asseta,  
> 122 che fa lo Scotto e l' Inghilese folle,  
> 123 sì che non può soffrir dentro a sua meta.  

There shall be seen the pride which maketh athirst and doth the Scot and Englishman so madden they may not abide within their proper bound.

- *si vedrà* ‘will one see’ → “shall be seen”: English uses the passive.
- *ch’ asseta* ‘which thirsts’ in the causative sense ‘which causes thirst’ → “which maketh athirst”: a single transitive verb becomes a verb with a predicative adjective.
- *che fa lo Scotto e l’ Inghilese folle* ‘which makes the Scot and the Englishman mad’ → “and doth the Scot and Englishman so madden”: coordination replaces the second relative; the causative verb and adjective become one lexical verb with an auxiliary, the second article disappears, and the two objects come between auxiliary and infinitive.
- *sì che* ‘so that’ → “so”: the degree word moves back to modify the preceding verb, and the result clause follows without an expressed conjunction.
- *non può soffrir* ‘cannot endure’ → “they may not abide”: the singular construction becomes plural, with the negation after the modal; enduring is expressed as remaining in place.
- *dentro a sua meta* ‘within his limit’ → “within their proper bound”: one English preposition renders the compound expression; the distributive singular possessive becomes plural, with “proper” added to stress the limit belonging to each.

> 124 Vedrassi la lussuria e il viver molle  
> 125 di quel di Spagna, e di quel di Buemme,  
> 126 che mai valor non conobbe, nè volle.  

The lechery shall be seen and life effeminate of him of Spain, and him of Bohemia, who knew not ever worthiness, nor willed it.

- *Vedrassi la lussuria e il viver molle* ‘one will see the lechery and the soft living’ → “The lechery shall be seen and life effeminate”: the first member of the compound subject moves before the English passive. The substantivized infinitive becomes a noun without its article, and softness is interpreted as effeminacy.
- *e di quel di Buemme* ‘and of him of Bohemia’ → “and him of Bohemia”: the first preposition is not repeated; the preceding “of” serves both members.
- *che mai valor non conobbe* ‘who never worth knew’ → “who knew not ever worthiness”: the verb precedes the negative and the object; the Italian negative concord becomes “not ever”, and worth is expressed as a quality of character.
- *nè volle* ‘nor willed’ → “nor willed it”: English supplies the object left understood in Italian.

> 127 Vedrassi al Ciotto di Jerusalemme  
> 128 segnata con un *i* la sua bontate,  
> 129 quando il contrario segnerà un *emme*.  

For the cripple of Jerusalem shall be seen marked with an I, his excellence, whereas an M shall mark the countercharge.

- *Vedrassi al Ciotto di Jerusalemme* ‘one will see for the cripple of Jerusalem’ → “For the cripple of Jerusalem shall be seen”: the dative phrase moves before the verb, and English uses the passive.
- *segnata con un i la sua bontate* ‘marked with an I his goodness’ → “marked with an I, his excellence”: moral goodness is expressed as excellence.
- *quando* ‘when’ → “whereas”: a formally temporal link becomes explicitly contrastive.
- *il contrario segnerà un emme* ‘the contrary will mark an M’ in surface order → “an M shall mark the countercharge”: English puts the marking letter first as subject and the thing marked last as object; the contrary of goodness is given the more specific sense of an adverse entry.

> 130 Vedrassi l' avarizia e la viltate  
> 131 di quel che guarda l' isola del foco,  
> 132 dove Anchise finì la lunga etate;  

The avarice and baseness shall be seen of him who hath in ward the Isle of Fire where Anchises ended his long life;

- *Vedrassi l’ avarizia e la viltate* ‘one will see the avarice and the baseness’ → “The avarice and baseness shall be seen”: the two nouns precede the English passive and share one article.
- *guarda* ‘guards’ → “hath in ward”: a simple verb becomes a phrase expressing custody.
- *finì la lunga etate* ‘finished the long span of years’ → “ended his long life”: the span of years is expressed as a life, and the article becomes a possessive.

> 133 ed a dare ad intender quanto è poco,  
> 134 la sua scrittura fien lettere mozze,  
> 135 che noteranno molto in parvo loco.  

and to give to understand how great his paltriness, his record shall be kept in stunted letters which shall note much in little space.

- *quanto è poco* ‘how little he is’ → “how great his paltriness”: littleness becomes the abstract noun, and it is the greatness of that quality that is measured; the copula is left unexpressed.
- *la sua scrittura fien lettere mozze* ‘his writing will be clipped letters’ → “his record shall be kept in stunted letters”: the writing is interpreted as a record; the plural copula agreeing with the letters gives way to a singular passive construction about the record. The letters become the medium, and the adjective precedes the noun.

> 136 E parranno a ciascun l' opere sozze  
> 137 del barba e del fratel, che tanto egregia  
> 138 nazione e due corone han fatte bozze.  

And plain to all shall be revealed the foul deeds of his uncle and his brother which have made so choice a family, and two crowns, cuckold.

- *parranno a ciascun* ‘will appear to each’ → “plain to all shall be revealed”: appearance becomes passive revelation with “plain” added; the recipient precedes the verb, and the distributive singular becomes collective.
- *l’ opere sozze* ‘the deeds foul’ → “the foul deeds”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *del barba e del fratel* ‘of the uncle and of the brother’ → “of his uncle and his brother”: the articles become possessives, and the first preposition serves both nouns.
- *tanto egregia nazione* ‘so distinguished a lineage’ → “so choice a family”: “choice” renders distinction and “family” gives the kinship sense of the noun.
- *han fatte bozze* ‘have made cuckolded’ → “have made” … “cuckold”: the verb moves before its two objects, while the predicate stays after them; English has no counterpart to the feminine plural agreement.

> 139 E quel di Portogallo e di Norvegia  
> 140 lì si conosceranno, e quel di Rascia  
> 141 che mal ha visto il conio di Vinegia.  

And he of Portugal and he of Norway there shall be known, and he of Rascia, who in ill hour saw the coin of Venice.

- *e di Norvegia* ‘and of Norway’ → “and he of Norway”: the demonstrative understood in the second member is supplied.
- *si conosceranno* ‘one will recognize them’ → “shall be known”: English uses the passive and expresses recognition as being known.
- *mal ha visto* ‘to his harm has seen’ → “in ill hour saw”: the adverb becomes a phrase of ill-fated time, and the compound perfect becomes a simple past.
- *il conio* ‘the die’ → “the coin”: the product struck stands in place of the instrument that strikes it.

> 142 O beata Ungheria, se non si lascia  
> 143 più malmenare! E beata Navarra,  
> 144 se s' armasse del monte che la fascia!  

O happy Hungary, if she suffereth herself to be mauled no more! And happy Navarre, were she to arm herself with the mount that fringeth her!

- *non si lascia più malmenare* ‘no longer lets herself be roughly handled’ → “suffereth herself to be mauled no more”: the reflexive follows the finite verb, the active Italian infinitive takes passive form in English, and the negative expression moves to the end.
- *se s’ armasse* ‘if she armed herself’ → “were she to arm herself”: inversion replaces the conditional conjunction, and the imperfect subjunctive becomes a construction with an infinitive.
- *del monte* ‘of the mount’ → “with the mount”: English marks what one arms oneself with by a different preposition.
- *che la fascia* ‘that girds her’ → “that fringeth her”: the image of encircling becomes one of bordering, and the object follows the verb.

> 145 E creder dee ciascun che già, per arra  
> 146 di questo, Nicosia e Famagosta  
> 147 per la lor bestia si lamenti e garra,  
> 148 che dal fianco dell' altre non si scosta.”  

And all should hold that ’tis in pledge of this that Nicosia and Famagosta already wail and shriek by reason of their beast, who doth not part him from beside the others.”

- *creder dee ciascun* ‘believe ought each’ → “all should hold”: the distributive singular becomes a plural subject before the modal; the infinitive follows it, and belief is expressed as holding something to be so.
- *che già, per arra di questo* ‘that already, as a pledge of this’ → “that ’tis in pledge of this that” … “already”: English introduces a cleft to emphasize the pledge and moves the adverb to the verbs it modifies.
- *Nicosia e Famagosta per la lor bestia si lamenti e garra* ‘Nicosia and Famagosta on account of their beast lament and cry out’ → “Nicosia and Famagosta already wail and shriek by reason of their beast”: the causal phrase moves after the verbs; singular subjunctives after the verb of belief become plural indicatives, and lamenting and crying out are rendered by more specifically vocal verbs.
- *che dal fianco dell’ altre non si scosta* ‘that from the flank of the others does not draw away’ → “who doth not part him from beside the others”: the relative is personal; the reflexive is retained as “him” after the verb, and the phrase of separation follows rather than precedes it. The noun for the flank becomes the relational “beside”.

## Summary — Close in Phrasing, Freer in Construction

Wicksteed often keeps Dante’s words while rearranging them within the clause. A delayed subject moves forward in *Parea ciascuna* ‘appeared each’ → “Each one appeared”; an object pronoun moves after the verb in *il condanna* → “condemneth him”. Yet he does not consistently prefer ordinary English order: “am I here exalted” keeps the subject after the verb as in *son io qui esaltato*, and “minds gross” keeps the adjective after its noun as in *menti grosse*. Two routine changes generally pass unremarked: English supplies subject pronouns where Italian gives the person in the verb ending alone, and drops the article before a possessive, as in *le mie note* → “my notes”.

Changes of construction sometimes alter the emphasis. In *un sol calor … si fa sentir* ‘a single heat … makes itself felt’, the heat is the subject; in “do we feel one glow from many coals”, it is the object, and the perceivers become the subject. The gerund *spirando* becomes the temporal clause “as ye breathe”, whereas *non trovandogli* is expanded into a clause beginning with “because”: the relation left implicit in the Italian is made explicit in different ways. Elsewhere the change is smaller: *senza peccato* ‘without sin’ becomes “sinless”, and *non l’ apprende con velame* becomes “apprehendeth it without a veil”, transferring the negation from the verb to its complement.

Word choice can sharpen a movement, as when *move* ‘moves’ becomes “shaketh”, or substitute an image, as when *latebra* ‘hiding place’ becomes “labyrinth”. Small additions and omissions also qualify otherwise close renderings: “as” makes the comparison with the ruby explicit, but “ruby” loses the diminutive of *rubinetto*; “solve” retains the verb in *solvetemi* but leaves its dative ‘for me’ unexpressed. At the larger scale, the prose often follows the Italian closely. It preserves the contrast of singular and plural in *io* and *mio*, *noi* and *nostro*, and answers the repeated *Lì si vedrà* with the same repeated opening, “There shall be seen”.
