# Paradiso 9

## Lines 1–15 — Charles’s Warning and Another Light

> 1 Da poi che Carlo tuo, bella Clemenza,  
> 2 m' ebbe chiarito, mi narrò gl' inganni  
> 3 che ricever dovea la sua semenza;  

When thy Charles, fair Clemence, had enlightened me, he told me of the frauds his seed was destined to encounter;

- *Da poi che* ‘after’ → “When”: the conjunction marks time without expressly saying ‘after’.
- *Carlo tuo* ‘Charles thy’ → “thy Charles”: the possessive comes before the name.
- *m' ebbe chiarito* ‘me he had enlightened’ → “had enlightened me”: the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *mi narrò gl' inganni* ‘to me he recounted the frauds’ → “he told me of the frauds”: the personal object follows the verb, and what was recounted becomes the object of “of”.
- *che ricever dovea la sua semenza* ‘which receive was to his seed’ → “his seed was destined to encounter”: the object relative is left unexpressed, the subject moves from last to first, and the infinitive follows the expression of futurity. “Was destined” makes that futurity more emphatic; the frauds are encountered rather than received.

> 4 ma disse: “Taci, e lascia volger gli anni”;  

but added: “Hold thy peace, and let the years revolve”;

- *disse* ‘he said’ → “added”: the verb marks the speech as a continuation.
- *Taci* ‘be silent’ → “Hold thy peace”: a single imperative becomes an idiom with a possessive and a noun.
- *lascia volger gli anni* ‘let revolve the years’ → “let the years revolve”: the subject of the infinitive precedes it.

> 5 sì ch' io non posso dir, se non che pianto  
> 6 giusto verrà di retro ai vostri danni.  

so that I can say naught, save that wailing well-deserved shall track your wrongs.

- *io non posso dir* ‘I cannot say’ → “I can say naught”: the negation moves from the verb to its object.
- *pianto giusto* ‘weeping just’ → “wailing well-deserved”: “wailing” intensifies ‘weeping’, and ‘just’ is taken in the sense of deserved.
- *verrà di retro ai vostri danni* ‘will come behind your injuries’ → “shall track your wrongs”: a transitive verb replaces the verb of motion and its phrase of direction; “wrongs” gives the injuries a moral sense.

> 7 E già la vita di quel lume santo  
> 8 rivolta s' era al sol che la riempie,  
> 9 come quel ben ch' ad ogni cosa è tanto.  

And already the life of that sacred light had turned to the sun that filleth it, as to the good ample for all things.

- *lume santo* ‘light holy’ → “sacred light”: the adjective is rendered by “sacred” and precedes the noun.
- *rivolta s' era* ‘turned itself had’ → “had turned”: the auxiliary precedes the participle, and the reflexive becomes an intransitive verb.
- *la riempie* ‘it filleth’ → “filleth it”: the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *come quel ben* ‘as that good’ → “as to the good”: “to” is carried over from the preceding construction, and the demonstrative becomes a definite article.
- *ch' ad ogni cosa è tanto* ‘that for each thing is enough’ → “ample for all things”: an adjective replaces the relative clause, and the distributive singular becomes a collective plural.

> 10 Ahi, anime ingannate, e fatture empie,  
> 11 che da sì fatto ben torcete i cori,  
> 12 drizzando in vanità le vostre tempie!  

Ah! souls deceived, ah! creatures impious, who from such good wry-twist your hearts, squaring your temples unto vanity!

- *e* ‘and’ → “ah!”: the opening interjection is repeated in place of the conjunction.
- *da sì fatto ben* ‘from a good so constituted’ → “from such good”: “such” condenses the expression of kind and precedes the noun.
- *torcete i cori* ‘you twist the hearts’ → “wry-twist your hearts”: the compound elaborates the verb, and a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *drizzando in vanità le vostre tempie* ‘directing toward vanity your temples’ → “squaring your temples unto vanity”: “squaring” gives the act of directing a more particular shape, and the object precedes the phrase of direction.

> 13 Ed ecco un altro di quelli splendori  
> 14 ver me si fece, e il suo voler piacermi  
> 15 significava nel chiarir di fuori.  

And lo, another of those splendours drew him towards me, and signified his will to pleasure me, by brightening outwardly.

- *ver me si fece* ‘towards me he made himself’ → “drew him towards me”: the idiom of motion is recast with “drew” and the reflexively used “him”; the phrase of direction comes last.
- *il suo voler piacermi significava* ‘his willing to please me he signified’ → “signified his will to pleasure me”: the finite verb comes before its object, the substantivized infinitive becomes the noun “will”, and ‘please’ is rendered by the verb “pleasure”.
- *nel chiarir di fuori* ‘in the brightening on the outside’ → “by brightening outwardly”: the preposition explicitly marks means, the gerund takes no article, and an adverb replaces the final phrase.

## Lines 16–48 — Cunizza Speaks of Love, Fame, and Padua

> 16 Gli occhi di Beatrice, ch' eran fermi  
> 17 sopra me, come pria, di caro assenso  
> 18 al mio disio certificato fermi.  

Beatrice’s eyes, fixed on me as before, of dear assent to my desire assured me.

- *Gli occhi di Beatrice* ‘the eyes of Beatrice’ → “Beatrice’s eyes”: the possessor comes first in the English possessive construction.
- *ch' eran fermi sopra me* ‘which were fixed upon me’ → “fixed on me”: the relative clause is reduced to a participial phrase.
- *certificato fermi* ‘made me assured’ → “assured me”: the causative verb and its complement become a single verb, followed by its object.

> 19 “Deh metti al mio voler tosto compenso,  
> 20 beato spirto, dissi, e fammi prova  
> 21 ch' io possa in te rifletter quel ch' io penso.”  

“Nay! make swift counterpoise unto my will,” I said, “thou blessed spirit, and give proof that I can cast reflection upon thee of what I think.”

- *Deh* ‘pray’ → “Nay!”: an exclamation of entreaty is rendered by one of insistence.
- *metti al mio voler tosto compenso* ‘put to my will straightway a counterweight’ → “make swift counterpoise unto my will”: “make” replaces ‘put’; the adverb of time becomes an adjective modifying “counterpoise,” and the phrase naming what is to be counterbalanced moves to the end.
- *beato spirto, dissi* ‘blessed spirit, I said’ → “I said, “thou blessed spirit”: the reporting clause comes before the vocative, to which “thou” is added.
- *fammi prova* ‘give me proof’ → “give proof”: the indirect object is omitted.
- *ch' io possa in te rifletter quel ch' io penso* ‘that I may in thee reflect what I think’ → “that I can cast reflection upon thee of what I think”: the subjunctive of ability becomes “can”; the infinitive becomes a verb and noun, with “upon thee” following rather than preceding them, and the object of ‘reflect’ is attached to “reflection” by “of.”

> 22 Onde la luce che m' era ancor nuova,  
> 23 del suo profondo, ond' ella pria cantava,  
> 24 seguette, come a cui di ben far giova:  

Whereat the light which was new to me, from out its depth, wherein it first was singing, went on as one rejoicing to do well:

- *che m' era ancor nuova* ‘which to me was still new’ → “which was new to me”: the indirect object moves after the adjective, and *ancor* is omitted.
- *del suo profondo* ‘from its depth’ → “from out its depth”: “out” makes the movement from within explicit.
- *ond' ella pria cantava* ‘whence it sang before’ → “wherein it first was singing”: origin becomes location, ‘before’ becomes “first,” and the imperfect is expressed by the past progressive.
- *come a cui di ben far giova* ‘as one to whom doing well gives pleasure’ → “as one rejoicing to do well”: the person to whom the action gives pleasure becomes the one rejoicing; the infinitive now follows the expression of pleasure, with the adverb after the verb.

> 25 “In quella parte della terra prava  
> 26 Italica, che siede tra Rialto  
> 27 e le fontane di Brenta e di Piava,  

“In that region of the depraved Italian land which sitteth ’twixt Rialto and the springs of Brenta and Piave,

- *parte* ‘part’ → “region”: a specifically geographical noun is used.
- *della terra prava Italica* ‘of the land depraved, Italian’ → “of the depraved Italian land”: both adjectives precede the noun.
- *di Brenta e di Piava* ‘of Brenta and of Piave’ → “of Brenta and Piave”: one preposition serves both names.

> 28 si leva un colle, e non surge molt' alto,  
> 29 là donde scese già una facella,  
> 30 che fece alla contrada un grande assalto.  

riseth a hill, lifted to no great height, whence erst came down a firebrand that made a dire assault upon the country.

- *si leva* ‘raises itself’ → “riseth”: an intransitive verb replaces the reflexive construction.
- *e non surge molt' alto* ‘and does not rise very high’ → “lifted to no great height”: a passive participial phrase replaces the coordinated finite clause; degree and negation are expressed within a noun phrase.
- *là donde* ‘there from where’ → “whence”: one relative adverb does the work of both words.
- *una facella* ‘a little torch’ → “a firebrand”: a different term for the burning object is used, without the diminutive.
- *fece alla contrada un grande assalto* ‘made upon the country a great assault’ → “made a dire assault upon the country”: the object precedes the phrase naming the target, and “dire” characterizes the assault rather than giving its magnitude.

> 31 D' una radice nacqui ed io ed ella;  

Out of one root spring I with it;

- *nacqui ed io ed ella* ‘were born both I and she’ → “spring I with it”: the past becomes present, with a verb suited to “root”; the second member of the paired subject becomes a companion phrase, and the feminine pronoun becomes “it.”

> 32 Cunizza fui chiamata, e qui refulgo,  
> 33 perchè mi vinse il lume d' esta stella.  

Cunizza was I called, and here I glow because the light of this star overcame me.

- *refulgo* ‘shine resplendently’ → “glow”: the verb of radiance is rendered by a less emphatic one.
- *mi vinse il lume d' esta stella* ‘me overcame the light of this star’ → “the light of this star overcame me”: the subject moves before the verb and the object after it.

> 34 Ma lietamente a me medesma indulgo  
> 35 la cagion di mia sorte, e non mi noia,  
> 36 che parria forse forte al vostro vulgo.  

But joyously I grant myself indulgence for the occasion of my lot, nor doth it grieve me, which would seem, mayhap, hard saying to your common herd.

- *a me medesma indulgo la cagion di mia sorte* ‘to my very self I pardon the cause of my lot’ → “I grant myself indulgence for the occasion of my lot”: the verb becomes “grant” with the noun “indulgence”; the recipient follows the verb, and what is pardoned becomes the occasion for the indulgence.
- *e non mi noia* ‘and it does not trouble me’ → “nor doth it grieve me”: conjunction and negation combine in “nor,” with auxiliary inversion; “grieve” replaces ‘trouble,’ and the object follows the verb.
- *parria forse forte* ‘would seem perhaps hard’ → “would seem, mayhap, hard saying”: “saying” is supplied for the adjective to qualify.
- *al vostro vulgo* ‘to your common people’ → “to your common herd”: a figurative collective replaces the term for the populace.

> 37 Di questa luculenta e cara gioia  
> 38 del nostro cielo, che più m' è propinqua,  
> 39 grande fama rimase, e, pria che moia,  
> 40 questo centesim' anno ancor s' incinqua.  

Of this shining and dear gem of our heaven, which most doth neighbour me, great fame remaineth, and ere it shall perish this centenary year shall be five times repeated.

- *che più m' è propinqua* ‘which is nearest to me’ → “which most doth neighbour me”: the comparative adjective with the copula becomes a verb modified by “most,” and the pronoun follows as its object.
- *rimase* ‘remained’ → “remaineth”: past becomes present.
- *pria che moia* ‘before it die’ → “ere it shall perish”: the present subjunctive in the temporal clause becomes an explicit future, with “perish” for ‘die.’
- *questo centesim' anno* ‘this hundredth year’ → “this centenary year”: an adjective denoting the hundred-year mark replaces the ordinal.
- *ancor s' incinqua* ‘yet makes itself fivefold’ → “shall be five times repeated”: the reflexive verb formed from ‘five’ becomes a passive phrase of repetition; futurity is expressed by “shall,” with no separate equivalent for *ancor*.

> 41 Vedi se far si dee l' uomo eccellente,  
> 42 sì ch' altra vita la prima relinqua!  

See if a man should make himself excel, so that the first life leave another after!

- *far si dee l' uomo eccellente* ‘make himself ought the man excellent’ → “a man should make himself excel”: the generic definite article becomes indefinite, the subject and modal precede the infinitive, and a verb replaces the predicate adjective.
- *altra vita la prima relinqua* ‘another life the first may leave’ → “the first life leave another after”: subject and object exchange places, “life” is expressed with “the first” rather than with “another,” and “after” makes succession explicit.

> 43 E ciò non pensa la turba presente,  
> 44 che Tagliamento ed Adice richiude,  
> 45 nè per esser battuta ancor si pente.  

And of this thinketh not the present crowd that Tagliamento and Adige enclose; the which, though smitten, yet repenteth not.

- *ciò non pensa* ‘this it does not think’ → “of this thinketh not”: the object becomes a prepositional phrase, and the negative follows the verb.
- *la turba presente* ‘the crowd present’ → “the present crowd”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *richiude* ‘encloses’ → “enclose”: English uses a plural verb with the two river names.
- *nè per esser battuta ancor si pente* ‘nor for being beaten does it yet repent’ → “the which, though smitten, yet repenteth not”: the coordinated negative clause becomes a relative clause with final “not”; the prepositional infinitive is read concessively and reduced to a participle after “though,” and the pronominal Italian verb becomes intransitive.

> 46 Ma tosto fia che Padova al palude  
> 47 cangerà l' acqua che Vicenza bagna,  
> 48 per esser al dover le genti crude.  

But soon shall come to pass that Padua at the pool shall change the water that doth bathe Vicenza, because the folk are stubborn against duty.

- *fia* ‘it will be’ → “shall come to pass”: a verb of being becomes a phrase for an event taking place.
- *al palude* ‘at the marsh’ → “at the pool”: a different term for the body of water is used.
- *bagna* ‘bathes’ → “doth bathe”: the simple present is expressed with an auxiliary.
- *per esser al dover le genti crude* ‘because of being toward duty the peoples harsh’ → “because the folk are stubborn against duty”: the causal infinitive becomes a finite clause with its subject first; the plural noun becomes collective, and harshness toward duty is interpreted as stubborn opposition to it.

## Lines 49–81 — Judgment, Heavenly Joy, and an Unspoken Wish

> 49 E dove Sile e Cagnan s' accompagna,  
> 50 tal signoreggia e va con la testa alta,  
> 51 che già per lui carpir si fa la ragna.  

And where Sile meets Cagnano, one holdeth sway and goeth with uplifted head to catch whom even now the net is being woven.

- *Sile e Cagnan s' accompagna* ‘Sile and Cagnan join together’ → “Sile meets Cagnano”: the two names joined by ‘and’ become the subject and object of a transitive verb.
- *tal* ‘such a one’ → “one”: the indefinite reference is retained without ‘such’.
- *con la testa alta* ‘with the head high’ → “with uplifted head”: the article drops out, and the adjective following the noun becomes a participle preceding it.
- *che già per lui carpir* ‘that already, in order him to catch’ → “to catch whom even now”: the connective and the object pronoun give way to the relative “whom”; the infinitive precedes its object, and the time expression follows the purpose phrase.
- *si fa la ragna* ‘is being made the net’ → “the net is being woven”: the subject follows the verb in Italian; the English puts it first and specifies the manner of making.

> 52 Piangerà Feltro ancora la diffalta  
> 53 dell' empio suo pastor, che sarà sconcia  
> 54 sì che per simil non s' entrò in Malta.  

A wail shall yet arise from Feltro for the trespass of its impious pastor, which shall be so foul that for the like none ever entered Malta.

- *Piangerà Feltro ancora la diffalta* ‘Feltro will yet bewail the fault’ → “A wail shall yet arise from Feltro for the trespass”: the act of weeping becomes a noun and the subject of “shall yet arise”; Feltro changes from the one that weeps to the source of the wail, and the direct object becomes the reason for it.
- *dell' empio suo pastor* ‘of the impious its pastor’ → “of its impious pastor”: the possessive precedes rather than follows the adjective.
- *che sarà sconcia sì* ‘which will be foul so’ → “which shall be so foul”: the degree adverb precedes the adjective it qualifies.
- *per simil* ‘for like’ → “for the like”: English makes the adjective substantival with an article.
- *non s' entrò in Malta* ‘one did not enter into Malta’ → “none ever entered Malta”: the impersonal negative becomes a negative subject, “ever” is supplied, and “entered” takes the place directly as its object.

> 55 Troppo sarebbe larga la bigoncia  
> 56 che ricevesse il sangue Ferrarese,  
> 57 e stanco chi il pesasse ad oncia ad oncia,  

Too ample were the charger which should receive Ferrara’s blood, and weary who should weigh it ounce by ounce,

- *Troppo sarebbe larga* ‘too would be broad’ → “Too ample were”: the degree adverb and adjective are brought together before the verb; the conditional becomes a hypothetical “were”.
- *la bigoncia* ‘the vat’ → “the charger”: a different kind of receptacle is named.
- *il sangue Ferrarese* ‘the Ferrarese blood’ → “Ferrara’s blood”: the adjective following the noun becomes a possessive place-name preceding it.
- *il pesasse* ‘it should weigh’ → “should weigh it”: the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *ad oncia ad oncia* ‘ounce at a time, ounce at a time’ → “ounce by ounce”: the repeated preposition gives way to a single linking “by”.

> 58 che donerà questo prete cortese,  

which this obliging priest shall give

- *donerà questo prete cortese* ‘shall give this priest courteous’ → “this obliging priest shall give”: the subject moves before the verb, and its adjective before the noun; ‘courteous’ becomes “obliging”.

> 59 per mostrarsi di parte; e cotai doni  
> 60 conformi fieno al viver del paese.  

to prove himself a partisan; and such-like gifts shall suit the country’s way of life.

- *per mostrarsi di parte* ‘in order to show oneself of a party’ → “to prove himself a partisan”: the infinitive alone expresses purpose, ‘show’ becomes “prove”, and a noun naming the person replaces the phrase expressing affiliation.
- *conformi fieno* ‘conforming will be’ → “shall suit”: the predicate adjective and copula become a transitive verb.
- *al viver del paese* ‘to the living of the country’ → “the country’s way of life”: the complement becomes a direct object; the substantivized infinitive is expanded to “way of life”, with the possessive placed first.

> 61 Su sono specchi, voi dicete Troni,  
> 62 onde rifulge a noi Dio giudicante,  
> 63 sì che questi parlar ne paion buoni.”  

Aloft are mirrors,—ye name them Thrones,—whence God in judgment shineth upon us so that these words approve themselves to us.”

- *voi dicete Troni* ‘ye say Thrones’ → “ye name them Thrones”: “name” makes the sense of the verb explicit and takes the supplied object “them”.
- *rifulge a noi Dio giudicante* ‘shines forth to us God judging’ → “God in judgment shineth upon us”: the subject comes first, the participle becomes a prepositional phrase, and shining forth ‘to’ someone becomes shining “upon” someone.
- *questi parlar* ‘these utterances’ → “these words”: the noun for acts of speaking is rendered by one for what is spoken.
- *ne paion buoni* ‘to us seem good’ → “approve themselves to us”: seeming good becomes a reflexive expression of acceptability, and the reference to ‘us’ moves to the end.

> 64 Qui si tacette, e fecemi sembiante  
> 65 che fosse ad altro volta, per la rota  
> 66 in che si mise, com' era davante.  

Here she was silent, and to me her semblance was of one who turneth him to other heeding, judging as by the wheel whereto she gave herself, like as she was before.

- *si tacette* ‘fell silent’ → “was silent”: the change into silence is rendered as a state.
- *fecemi sembiante* ‘made me a show’ → “to me her semblance was”: what she showed becomes the subject of a clause with “was”; the recipient moves to the front and “her” is supplied.
- *che fosse ad altro volta* ‘that she was turned to something else’ → “of one who turneth him to other heeding”: the subjunctive clause describing how she appeared becomes a description of a generic “one”; a turned state becomes a present reflexive action, and “heeding” specifies the kind of turning.
- *per la rota* ‘by the wheel’ → “judging as by the wheel”: “judging as” makes explicit that the wheel is the basis for the impression.
- *in che si mise* ‘into which she put herself’ → “whereto she gave herself”: entry into something becomes giving oneself to it.
- *com' era davante* ‘as she was before’ → “like as she was before”: “like” reinforces the comparison.

> 67 L' altra letizia, che m' era già nota  
> 68 preclara cosa, mi si fece in vista  
> 69 qual fin balascio in che lo sol percota.  

The other joy, noted already to me as a thing illustrious, shone in my sight like a fine ruby that the sun should strike.

- *che m' era già nota preclara cosa* ‘which to me was already known for an illustrious thing’ → “noted already to me as a thing illustrious”: a participial phrase replaces the relative clause; ‘known’ becomes “noted”, the reference to ‘me’ moves after “already”, and “as a” introduces the characterization, with the adjective now after the noun.
- *mi si fece in vista* ‘made itself to me in sight’ → “shone in my sight”: the reflexive expression for appearing becomes a verb of shining, and the dative becomes possessive.
- *qual fin balascio* ‘like fine balas ruby’ → “like a fine ruby”: the article is supplied and the particular kind of ruby is not distinguished.
- *in che lo sol percota* ‘upon which the sun should strike’ → “that the sun should strike”: the prepositional relative becomes a direct object.

> 70 Per letiziar lassù folgor s' acquista,  
> 71 sì come riso qui; ma giù s' abbuia  
> 72 l' ombra di fuor, come la mente è trista.  

By joy up there brightness is won, just as a smile on earth; but down below darkeneth the shade externally as the mind saddeneth.

- *Per letiziar* ‘by rejoicing’ → “By joy”: a noun replaces the infinitive denoting the activity.
- *folgor s' acquista* ‘radiance is acquired’ → “brightness is won”: the flash or radiance becomes “brightness”, and acquisition is phrased as winning.
- *sì come riso qui* ‘just as laughter here’ → “just as a smile on earth”: “a smile” selects a quieter sense of *riso*, and “on earth” identifies the place indicated by ‘here’.
- *giù* ‘below’ → “down below”: “down” reinforces the direction.
- *di fuor* ‘on the outside’ → “externally”: the phrase becomes a single adverb.
- *è trista* ‘is sad’ → “saddeneth”: a state becomes a change of state.

> 73 “Dio vede tutto, e tuo veder s' inluia,  
> 74 diss' io, beato spirto, sì che nulla  
> 75 voglia di sè a te puote esser fuia.  

“God seeth all, and into him thy seeing sinketh,” said I, “blessed spirit, so that no wish may steal itself from thee.

- *tuo veder s' inluia* ‘thy seeing ins itself in him’ → “into him thy seeing sinketh”: the coined reflexive verb incorporates the idea of ‘him’; English brings that element out as a phrase, puts it first, and supplies the image of sinking.
- *di sè a te puote esser fuia* ‘of itself to thee can be thief’ → “may steal itself from thee”: being a thief of itself becomes the act of stealing itself; the person affected becomes the complement of “from”, placed last.

> 76 Dunque la voce tua, che il ciel trastulla  
> 77 sempre, col canto di quei fochi pii  
> 78 che di sei ali fannosi cuculla,  
> 79 perchè non satisface ai miei disii?  

Then wherefore doth thy voice, which gladdeneth Heaven ceaselessly,—together with the singing of those Flames devout, which make themselves a cowl with the six wings,— not satisfy my longings?

- *perchè non satisface* ‘why does not satisfy’ → “wherefore doth” … “not satisfy”: Italian postpones the interrogative and verb until after the long subject phrase; English puts the interrogative and auxiliary before the subject, leaving the negation and main verb after it.
- *che il ciel trastulla sempre* ‘which Heaven delights always’ → “which gladdeneth Heaven ceaselessly”: the object moves after the verb; delighting becomes gladdening, and ‘always’ becomes “ceaselessly”.
- *col canto* ‘with the singing’ → “together with the singing”: “together” makes the accompaniment explicit.
- *di quei fochi pii* ‘of those devout fires’ → “of those Flames devout”: “Flames” replaces ‘fires’; the English retains the Italian placement of the adjective after the noun.
- *che di sei ali fannosi cuculla* ‘which of six wings make themselves a cowl’ → “which make themselves a cowl with the six wings”: the material phrase moves to the end, “with” replaces ‘of’, and “the” is supplied.
- *ai miei disii* ‘to my desires’ → “my longings”: “satisfy” takes a direct object, and “longings” gives the desires a stronger cast.

> 80 Già non attenderei io tua domanda,  
> 81 s' io m' intuassi, come tu t' immii.”  

Not till now had I awaited thy demand, were I in thee even as thou art in me.”

- *Già non attenderei io* ‘certainly I would not await’ → “Not till now had I awaited”: “Not till now” gives the opening a temporal rather than an emphatic sense, and the conditional present becomes an inverted past perfect.
- *tua domanda* ‘thy question’ → “thy demand”: asking is rendered as a demand.
- *s' io m' intuassi* ‘if I were to put myself into thee’ → “were I in thee”: inversion replaces ‘if’, and a state expressed by “were” and a preposition replaces the coined reflexive verb.
- *come tu t' immii* ‘as thou puttest thyself into me’ → “even as thou art in me”: “even” strengthens the comparison, and the corresponding reflexive coinage likewise becomes a state.

## Lines 82–114 — Folco Speaks of His Home and Love

> 82 “La maggior valle in che l' acqua si spanda,  

“The greatest valley in which water stretcheth,”

- *l' acqua si spanda* ‘the water spreads itself’ → “water stretcheth”: the article is dropped, the reflexive becomes an intransitive verb, and the descriptive subjunctive becomes indicative.

> 83 incominciaro allor le sue parole,  

then began his words,

- *incominciaro allor* ‘began then’ → “then began”: the adverb is moved before the verb.

> 84 fuor di quel mar che la terra inghirlanda,  
> 85 tra i discordanti liti, contra il sole  
> 86 tanto sen va che fa meridiano  
> 87 là dove l' orizzonte pria far suole.  

“forth from that sea which garlandeth the earth, betwixt opposing shores, against the sun, goeth so far that it meridian maketh of what was first horizon.

- *la terra inghirlanda* ‘the earth it garlands’ → “garlandeth the earth”: the object is moved after the verb.
- *tra i discordanti liti* ‘between the discordant shores’ → “betwixt opposing shores”: the article is dropped, and “opposing” renders the adjective of disagreement.
- *tanto sen va* ‘so far it goes its way’ → “goeth so far”: the expression of extent follows the verb, and the pronominal turn is reduced to a simple verb of motion.
- *che fa meridiano / là dove l' orizzonte pria far suole* ‘that it makes a meridian there where it is wont first to make the horizon’ → “that it meridian maketh of what was first horizon”: “meridian” precedes the verb; the clause of place becomes an “of what” complement, and the habitual present with an infinitive becomes a simple past of being. The article before “horizon” drops out.

> 88 Di quella valle fu' io littorano  
> 89 tra Ebro e Macra, che, per cammin corto,  
> 90 lo Genovese parte dal Toscano.  

Of this valley was I a shoresman, midway ’twixt the Ebro and the Macra, which, with short course, parteth the Genoese and Tuscan.

- *Di quella valle* ‘of that valley’ → “Of this valley”: the demonstrative changes from distant to near.
- *littorano* ‘shore-dwelling’ → “a shoresman”: the predicate is expressed by a noun with an indefinite article.
- *tra Ebro e Macra* ‘between Ebro and Macra’ → “midway ’twixt the Ebro and the Macra”: “midway” specifies a position not specified by the preposition alone, and each river name receives an article.
- *cammin corto* ‘course short’ → “short course”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *lo Genovese parte dal Toscano* ‘the Genoese it parts from the Tuscan’ → “parteth the Genoese and Tuscan”: the verb precedes its object, and the two terms are coordinated under one article rather than joined by a preposition of separation.

> 91 Ad un occaso quasi e ad un orto  
> 92 Buggea siede e la terra ond' io fui,  
> 93 che fe' del sangue suo già caldo il porto.  

Almost alike for sunset and for sunrise the site of Bougiah and of the place I spring from, which with its blood once made the harbour warm.

- *Ad un occaso quasi e ad un orto* ‘at one sunset almost and at one sunrise’ → “Almost alike for sunset and for sunrise”: the repeated ‘one’ is recast as a comparison with “alike”; “Almost” moves to the front, and “for” replaces the preposition of position.
- *Buggea siede e la terra ond' io fui* ‘Bougiah sits and the land whence I was’ → “the site of Bougiah and of the place I spring from”: the finite verb of situation becomes a noun governing both names with “of”; ‘land’ becomes “place”, and the past of being with a relative adverb becomes a present verb of origin with a trailing preposition.
- *fe' del sangue suo già caldo il porto* ‘made with blood its once warm the harbour’ → “with its blood once made the harbour warm”: the possessive precedes its noun, the instrumental phrase and adverb precede the verb, and the object precedes its predicate adjective.

> 94 Folco mi disse quella gente, a cui  
> 95 fu noto il nome mio, e questo cielo  
> 96 di me s'imprenta, com' io fei di lui;  

Folco they called me to whom my name was known, and this heaven is stamped by me, as I was stamped by it;

- *mi disse quella gente* ‘me called that people’ → “they called me”: the singular collective subject becomes a plural pronoun before the verb, and the object follows it.
- *fu noto il nome mio* ‘was known the name my’ → “my name was known”: the subject precedes the verb and the possessive precedes the noun.
- *di me s'imprenta* ‘with me imprints itself’ → “is stamped by me”: the reflexive becomes a passive, with the prepositional phrase following the verb and expressing agency.
- *com' io fei di lui* ‘as I did of it’ → “as I was stamped by it”: the past of ‘do’, standing in for the preceding verb, is expanded into an explicit passive; “by” again marks agency.

> 97 chè più non arse la figlia di Belo,  
> 98 noiando ed a Sicheo ed a Creusa,  
> 99 di me, in fin che si convenne al pelo;  

for Belus’ daughter, wronging alike Sichæus and Creüsa, did not more burn than I, so long as it consorted with my locks;

- *più non arse la figlia di Belo … di me* ‘more did not burn the daughter of Belus … than me’ → “Belus’ daughter, … did not more burn than I”: the subject moves before the verb, the genitive before its noun, and the comparative adverb after the negation; the pronoun of comparison becomes nominative, as for an understood verb.
- *noiando ed a Sicheo ed a Creusa* ‘giving hurt both to Sichæus and to Creüsa’ → “wronging alike Sichæus and Creüsa”: a transitive verb takes the two names directly, and “alike” conveys the force of the repeated conjunction.
- *in fin che si convenne al pelo* ‘until it was fitting to the hair’ → “so long as it consorted with my locks”: the limit is expressed as duration, fittingness as agreement “with”, and the collective singular with a definite article as a possessive plural.

> 100 nè quella Rodopeia, che delusa  
> 101 fu da Demofoonte, nè Alcide  
> 102 quando Iole nel cor ebbe richiusa.  

nor yet the Rhodopeian maid who was deluded by Demophoön, neither Alcides when he had shut Iole in his heart.

- *nè quella Rodopeia* ‘nor that Rhodopeian’ → “nor yet the Rhodopeian maid”: “yet” is added, the demonstrative becomes an article, and “maid” supplies a noun for the feminine designation.
- *delusa / fu* ‘deluded was’ → “was deluded”: the auxiliary precedes the participle.
- *nè Alcide* ‘nor Alcides’ → “neither Alcides”: the repeated negative conjunction is varied to “neither”.
- *Iole nel cor ebbe richiusa* ‘Iole in the heart he had shut’ → “he had shut Iole in his heart”: the verb precedes its object and the phrase of place, and the definite article becomes a possessive.

> 103 Non però qui si pente, ma si ride,  
> 104 non della colpa, ch' a mente non torna,  
> 105 ma del valor ch' ordinò e provide.  

Yet here we not repent, but smile; not at the sin, which cometh not again to mind, but at the Worth that ordered and provided.

- *Non però qui si pente* ‘not on that account does one here repent’ → “Yet here we not repent”: the negated inference becomes an opening adversative, the negation moves next to the verb, and the impersonal construction becomes first-person plural.
- *si ride* ‘one laughs’ → “smile”: the impersonal verb shares the preceding personal subject in English, and the verb is softened.
- *della colpa* ‘of the fault’ → “at the sin”: the preposition fits the English verb of smiling, and “sin” renders ‘fault’.
- *ch' a mente non torna* ‘which to mind does not return’ → “which cometh not again to mind”: ‘return’ is expressed by “cometh … again”, and the phrase of destination follows the verb.
- *del valor* ‘of the worth’ → “at the Worth”: the preposition again changes to suit the English verb.

> 106 Qui si rimira nell' arte che adorna  
> 107 cotanto effetto, e discernesi il bene  
> 108 per che al mondo di su quel di giù torna.  

Here gaze we on the Art that beautifieth its so great effect, and here discern the Good which bringeth back the world below unto the world above.

- *si rimira nell' arte* ‘one gazes into the art’ → “gaze we on the Art”: the impersonal becomes first-person plural, with the subject after the verb, and “on” replaces ‘into’.
- *adorna* ‘adorns’ → “beautifieth”: the verb expresses the result of adorning.
- *cotanto effetto* ‘so great an effect’ → “its so great effect”: the possessive explicitly relates the effect to the art.
- *e discernesi il bene* ‘and discerned is the good’ → “and here discern the Good”: the construction with *si* becomes active, sharing the preceding subject, and “here” is repeated.
- *per che al mondo di su quel di giù torna* ‘through which to the world above that below returns’ → “which bringeth back the world below unto the world above”: the relative expressing means becomes the subject of a causative verb; the returning subject becomes its object, with “world” repeated for the demonstrative, and the destination moves to the end.

> 109 Ma perchè le tue voglie tutte piene  
> 110 ten porti, che son nate in questa spera,  
> 111 procedere ancor oltre mi conviene.  

But that thou mayst bear away full satisfied all the desires born within this sphere, needs must I yet proceed.

- *le tue voglie tutte piene / ten porti* ‘thy desires all full thou mayst carry away with thee’ → “thou mayst bear away full satisfied all the desires”: the verb moves before its object, without a separate equivalent for ‘with thee’; “full satisfied” expresses fulfilment, “all” quantifies “the desires”, and the possessive becomes a definite article.
- *che son nate in questa spera* ‘which have been born in this sphere’ → “born within this sphere”: the finite relative clause becomes a participial modifier, and “within” renders the preposition.
- *procedere ancor oltre mi conviene* ‘to proceed still further is requisite for me’ → “needs must I yet proceed”: the impersonal verb with a dative becomes a personal modal construction; the infinitive moves to the end, and “yet” conveys continuation without a separate ‘further’.

> 112 Tu vuoi saper chi è in questa lumiera,  
> 113 che qui appresso me così scintilla,  
> 114 come raggio di sole in acqua mera.  

Thou wouldst know who is within that light which here by me so sparkleth as the sun’s ray in pure water.

- *vuoi saper* ‘wish to know’ → “wouldst know”: present volition is expressed by a modal with a bare infinitive.
- *in questa lumiera* ‘in this light’ → “within that light”: the preposition is made more explicit, and the near demonstrative becomes distant.
- *qui appresso me così scintilla, / come* ‘here beside me thus sparkles, like’ → “here by me so sparkleth as”: the manner adverb and comparison are rendered as the paired “so … as”.
- *raggio di sole* ‘ray of sun’ → “the sun’s ray”: the genitive precedes its noun and receives a definite article.
- *acqua mera* ‘water pure’ → “pure water”: the adjective precedes the noun.

## Lines 115–142 — Rahab’s Peace and Rome’s Adultery

> 115 Or sappi che là entro si tranquilla  
> 116 Raab, ed a nostr' ordine congiunta  
> 117 di lei nel sommo grado si sigilla.  

Now know that there within hath Rahab peace; and when she joined our order, it stamped itself with her in the highest grade.

- *si tranquilla Raab* ‘rests at peace Rahab’ → “hath Rahab peace”: the reflexive verb becomes a verb of possession with a noun; the subject now stands between the two.
- *ed a nostr' ordine congiunta* ‘and joined to our order’ → “and when she joined our order”: the participle becomes a finite temporal clause; Rahab is the subject of an active verb, and the order its direct object.
- *di lei nel sommo grado si sigilla* ‘with her in the highest grade seals itself’ → “it stamped itself with her in the highest grade”: the verb and its reflexive object move before both prepositional phrases; sealing becomes stamping, and the present becomes past.

> 118 Da questo cielo, in cui l' ombra s' appunta  
> 119 che il vostro mondo face, pria ch' altr' alma  
> 120 del trionfo di Cristo fu assunta.  

By this heaven,—touched by the shadow’s point which your world casteth,—ere other soul was she uptaken from Christ’s triumph.

- *Da questo cielo* ‘from this heaven’ → “By this heaven”: the heaven is construed as the agent of the passive verb rather than the point of departure.
- *in cui l' ombra s' appunta* ‘in which the shadow ends in a point’ → “touched by the shadow’s point”: the relative clause becomes a passive participial phrase modifying the heaven; the point expressed by the Italian verb becomes a noun.
- *che il vostro mondo face* ‘which your world makes’ → “which your world casteth”: making a shadow becomes casting one.
- *pria ch' altr' alma* ‘before any other soul’ → “ere other soul”: the Italian comparison introduced by *pria che* is condensed to a preposition.
- *del trionfo di Cristo fu assunta* ‘from the triumph of Christ was taken up’ → “was she uptaken from Christ’s triumph”: the passive precedes the phrase of origin, the possessive precedes its noun, and the expressed subject stands between auxiliary and participle.

> 121 Ben si convenne lei lasciar per palma  
> 122 in alcun cielo dell' alta vittoria,  
> 123 che s' acquistò con l' una e l' altra palma;  

And soothly it beseemed to leave her as a trophy, in some heaven, of the lofty victory which was achieved with the one and the other palm;

- *Ben si convenne* ‘well did it befit’ → “And soothly it beseemed”: the adverb of fitness becomes an affirmation, and a connective is supplied.
- *lei lasciar* ‘her to leave’ → “to leave her”: the object follows the infinitive.
- *per palma* ‘for a palm’ → “as a trophy”: the emblem is rendered by what it signifies, with an indefinite article supplied.
- *che s' acquistò* ‘which was gained’ → “which was achieved”: the Italian *si* construction becomes an English passive, and gaining becomes achieving.

> 124 perch' ella favorò la prima gloria  
> 125 di Josuè in su la Terrasanta,  
> 126 che poco tocca al papa la memoria.  

because she favoured Joshua’s first glory in the Holy Land, which little toucheth the Papal memory.

- *la prima gloria di Josuè* ‘the first glory of Joshua’ → “Joshua’s first glory”: the possessive precedes the noun and takes the place of its article.
- *in su la Terrasanta* ‘upon the Holy Land’ → “in the Holy Land”: the compound preposition becomes a simple preposition of place.
- *al papa la memoria* ‘in the pope the memory’ → “the Papal memory”: the pope, expressed separately as the person affected, becomes an adjective modifying the direct object.

> 127 La tua città, che di colui è pianta  
> 128 che pria volse le spalle al suo Fattore,  
> 129 e di cui è la invidia tanto pianta,  

Thy city,—of his planting who first turned his shoulders on his Maker, and from whose envy hath such wailing sprung,—

- *che di colui è pianta* ‘which of him is a plant’ → “of his planting”: the relative clause loses its subject and copula; the plant becomes a planting, and the demonstrative pronoun a possessive.
- *volse le spalle al suo Fattore* ‘turned the shoulders to his Maker’ → “turned his shoulders on his Maker”: English uses a possessive for the body part and changes the preposition.
- *e di cui è la invidia tanto pianta* ‘and whose envy is so much bewailed’ → “and from whose envy hath such wailing sprung”: the envy changes from the subject of a passive verb to the source of the wailing; the present passive becomes a perfect of arising, and the degree of the bewailing becomes a modifier of the noun.

> 130 produce e spande il maledetto fiore  
> 131 ch' ha disviate le pecore e gli agni,  
> 132 però che fatto ha lupo del pastore.  

maketh and spreadeth that accursed flower which hath set sheep and lambs astray, for it hath turned the shepherd to a wolf.

- *produce* ‘produces’ → “maketh”: the more general verb replaces the verb of production.
- *il maledetto fiore* ‘the accursed flower’ → “that accursed flower”: the article becomes demonstrative.
- *ha disviate* ‘has led astray’ → “hath set sheep and lambs astray”: the single participle becomes a verb with a result word placed after the objects.
- *le pecore e gli agni* ‘the sheep and the lambs’ → “sheep and lambs”: both articles are dropped.
- *però che* ‘because’ → “for”: a coordinating conjunction replaces the subordinating one.
- *fatto ha lupo del pastore* ‘made has a wolf of the shepherd’ → “hath turned the shepherd to a wolf”: the auxiliary comes first; the shepherd becomes the direct object and the wolf the result of the change.

> 133 Per questo l' Evangelio e i dottor magni  
> 134 son derelitti, e solo ai Decretali  
> 135 si studia sì che pare ai lor vivagni.  

Therefore it is the Gospel and great Doctors are deserted, and only the Decretals are so studied, as may be seen upon their margins.

- *Per questo* ‘for this’ → “Therefore it is”: the prepositional phrase becomes an adverb, with an emphatic introductory construction supplied.
- *e i dottor magni* ‘and the doctors great’ → “and great Doctors”: the second article is dropped and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *solo ai Decretali si studia sì* ‘only to the Decretals does one apply oneself so’ → “only the Decretals are so studied”: the impersonal construction with a prepositional complement becomes a personal passive; the adverb of degree precedes the participle.
- *che pare ai lor vivagni* ‘that it is apparent at their edges’ → “as may be seen upon their margins”: a result clause becomes a clause of comparison; being apparent becomes the possibility of being seen, and the edges are specified as margins.

> 136 A questo intende il papa e i cardinali:  
> 137 non vanno i lor pensieri a Nazzarette,  
> 138 là dove Gabriello aperse l' ali.  

Thereon the Pope and Cardinals are intent; ne’er wend their thoughts to Nazareth, where Gabriel spread his wings.

- *A questo intende il papa e i cardinali* ‘to this attends the pope and the cardinals’ → “Thereon the Pope and Cardinals are intent”: the opening phrase becomes a pronominal adverb; the singular verb preceding the compound subject becomes a plural copula and adjective following it, and the second article is dropped.
- *non vanno i lor pensieri* ‘their thoughts do not go’ → “ne’er wend their thoughts”: simple negation becomes temporal negation, and going becomes wending; the English retains the Italian verb-before-subject order.
- *là dove* ‘there where’ → “where”: the separate adverb of place is omitted.
- *aperse l' ali* ‘opened the wings’ → “spread his wings”: opening becomes spreading, and the article becomes a possessive.

> 139 Ma Vaticano e l' altre parti elette  
> 140 di Roma, che son state cimiterio  
> 141 alla milizia che Pietro seguette,  
> 142 tosto libere fien dell' adulterio.”  

But Vatican, and the other parts elect of Rome, the cemetery of the soldiery that followed Peter, shall soon be freed from the adultery.”

- *che son state cimiterio* ‘which have been a cemetery’ → “the cemetery”: the relative clause becomes an appositive, losing the tense of the verb and gaining a definite article.
- *alla milizia* ‘for the soldiery’ → “of the soldiery”: the relation expressed by the preposition changes.
- *che Pietro seguette* ‘that Peter followed’ (in Italian word order, with Peter as object) → “that followed Peter”: the object moves after the verb.
- *tosto libere fien dell' adulterio* ‘soon free shall be of the adultery’ → “shall soon be freed from the adultery”: the adverb moves after the auxiliary, and the predicate adjective becomes a passive participle with a preposition of release.

## Summary — Italian Order and English Idiom

Wicksteed often moves a subject before its verb, an object pronoun after it, or an adjective before its noun. Thus *mi vinse il lume d' esta stella* becomes “the light of this star overcame me”, and *lascia volger gli anni* becomes “let the years revolve”. Yet he keeps the Italian order where English can bear the inversion: *si leva un colle* is “riseth a hill”, and *le parti elette* retains its postposed adjective in “parts elect”. More generally, English supplies subjects that Italian leaves to the verb ending, and omits articles before possessives, as in *la tua città*, “Thy city”.

A change of construction often lets the English put the emphasis elsewhere. In *Piangerà Feltro*, Feltro is the subject of ‘will bewail’; in “A wail shall yet arise from Feltro”, the wail becomes the subject and Feltro its source. Elsewhere Wicksteed condenses rather than expands: *che son nate in questa spera* loses its finite verb in “born within this sphere”. The impersonal *si* can also give way to a personal subject, as when *si rimira* becomes “gaze we”. Dante’s coined verbs call for a different solution: *s' inluia* gains an image of motion in “into him thy seeing sinketh”, whereas *m' intuassi* becomes the simple state “were I in thee”.

The choice of a word can sharpen, soften, or narrow the Italian: *pianto* becomes “wailing”, *si ride* becomes “smile”, and the net in *si fa la ragna* is not merely made but “woven”. Small omissions and additions are common: *ancor* has no counterpart in “which was new to me”, while “midway” specifies more than *tra* alone. Yet Wicksteed often follows an image closely even while rearranging its words. In *raggio di sole in acqua mera*, both the genitive and the adjective change position, but “the sun’s ray in pure water” preserves each element of the comparison.
