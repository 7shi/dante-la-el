# Paradiso 32

## Lines 1–18 — The Women of the Rose

> 1 Affetto al suo piacer, quel contemplante  
> 2 libero ufficio di dottore assunse,  
> 3 e cominciò queste parole sante:  

With his love fixed on his Delight, that contemplating saint took the free office of the teacher on him, and began these sacred words:

- *Affetto al suo piacer* ‘attached in love to his delight’ → “With his love fixed on his Delight”: the participle describes the contemplative himself; the English makes his love the thing fixed and introduces the phrase with “With”.
- *quel contemplante* ‘that contemplative’ → “that contemplating saint”: the participle used as a noun becomes a modifier of the supplied “saint”.
- *libero ufficio di dottore assunse* ‘free office of teacher he assumed’ → “took the free office of the teacher on him”: the verb moves before its object and is expanded into “took … on him”; both definite articles are supplied.
- *queste parole sante* ‘these words holy’ → “these sacred words”: the adjective moves before the noun, and ‘holy’ becomes “sacred”.

> 4 “La piaga, che Maria richiuse ed unse,  
> 5 quella ch' è tanto bella da' suoi piedi  
> 6 è colei che l'aperse e che la punse.  

“The wound which Mary closed and anointed, she who is so beauteous at her feet opened and thrust.

- *quella ch' è tanto bella* ‘that one who is so beautiful’ → “she who is so beauteous”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun.
- *da' suoi piedi* ‘down by her feet’ → “at her feet”: the English uses a simple preposition of position.
- *è colei che l'aperse e che la punse* ‘is she who opened it and who pricked it’ → “opened and thrust”: the identifying construction becomes a direct predication. “The wound” serves as the fronted object, so neither object pronoun is repeated; “thrust” gives the action rather than its effect on the wound.

> 7 Nell' ordine, che fanno i terzi sedi,  
> 8 siede Rachel di sotto da costei  
> 9 con Beatrice, sì come tu vedi.  

In the order which the third rank maketh sitteth below her, Rachael with Beatrice, even as thou seest.

- *che fanno i terzi sedi* ‘which the third seats make’ → “which the third rank maketh”: the plural seats become a single collective “rank”; the subject moves before the verb, which becomes singular.
- *siede Rachel di sotto da costei* ‘sits Rachel below this one’ → “sitteth below her, Rachael”: the phrase of position moves before the subject, and the demonstrative becomes “her”.

> 10 Sara, Rebecca, Judit, e colei  
> 11 che fu bisava al cantor, che, per doglia  
> 12 del fallo, disse: *Miserere mei*,  

Sarah, Rebecca, Judith, and her from whom, third in descent, the singer came who for grief at his sin cried out *have pity on me!*

- *colei che fu bisava al cantor* ‘her who was great-grandmother to the singer’ → “her from whom, third in descent, the singer came”: the relationship is stated from the descendant’s side, making the singer the subject rather than the woman.
- *del fallo* ‘of the fault’ → “at his sin”: “at” marks the occasion of the grief, “his” replaces the article, and “sin” is more specific than ‘fault’.
- *disse* ‘said’ → “cried out”: the English adds force to the verb of speaking.
- *Miserere mei* ‘have mercy on me’ → “have pity on me!”: the Latin prayer is translated rather than retained, with “pity” for ‘mercy’.

> 13 puoi tu veder così di soglia in soglia  
> 14 giù digradar, com' io ch' a proprio nome  
> 15 vo per la rosa giù di foglia in foglia.  

these mayst thou see from rank to rank descending; even as I, naming their proper names, go down the rose petal by petal.

- *puoi tu veder così* ‘canst thou see thus’ → “these mayst thou see”: “these” is supplied to resume the preceding list; ‘thus’ has no separate rendering.
- *di soglia in soglia* ‘from threshold to threshold’ → “from rank to rank”: the architectural term becomes one denoting a row.
- *giù digradar* ‘downward to descend by degrees’ → “descending”: the infinitive after the verb of seeing becomes a participle; the separate indication of downward motion is absorbed into it.
- *com' io ch' a proprio nome* ‘as I who by proper name’ → “even as I, naming their proper names”: the relative construction is dropped. The phrase of manner becomes a participial phrase with a supplied verb and possessive, and the singular ‘name’ becomes plural.
- *vo per la rosa giù* ‘go through the rose downward’ → “go down the rose”: “down” moves next to the verb and takes “the rose” as its complement; ‘through’ is not separately expressed.
- *di foglia in foglia* ‘from leaf to leaf’ → “petal by petal”: “petal” specifies the part of the flower, and “by” replaces the ‘from … to’ construction.

> 16 E dal settimo grado in giù, sì come  
> 17 infino ad esso, succedono Ebree,  
> 18 dirimendo del fior tutte le chiome;  

And down from the seventh onward, even as thereto, follow Hebrew dames, disparting all the flower’s locks;

- *dal settimo grado in giù* ‘from the seventh step downward’ → “down from the seventh onward”: ‘step’ is left understood; the direction is placed first, with “onward” marking continuation.
- *infino ad esso* ‘up to it’ → “thereto”: a single adverb replaces the phrase, leaving the sense of a limit implicit.
- *Ebree* ‘Hebrew women’ → “Hebrew dames”: the feminine plural noun is rendered by an adjective and the more elevated “dames”.
- *del fior tutte le chiome* ‘of the flower all the locks’ → “all the flower’s locks”: the *of*-phrase becomes a possessive and moves after “all”.

## Lines 19–51 — The Two Divisions of the Sacred Rose

> 19 perchè, secondo lo sguardo che fee  
> 20 la fede in Cristo, queste sono il muro  
> 21 a che si parton le sacre scalee.  

because, accordant with the way faith looked to Christ, these are the partition-wall whereat the sacred steps are parted.

- *secondo lo sguardo che fee la fede in Cristo* ‘according to the gaze that faith directed toward Christ’ → “accordant with the way faith looked to Christ”: the noun for the gaze and the verb for directing it become an expression of manner and the verb “looked”; “faith” moves from after the verb to before it.
- *il muro* ‘the wall’ → “the partition-wall”: the function of the wall is made explicit in the compound.
- *a che* ‘at which’ → “whereat”: the preposition and relative pronoun become a single relative adverb.
- *si parton le sacre scalee* ‘divide themselves the sacred steps’ → “the sacred steps are parted”: the pronominal verb becomes passive, and its subject moves before it.

> 22 Da questa parte, onde il fior è maturo  
> 23 di tutte le sue foglie, sono assisi  
> 24 quei che credettero in Cristo venturo.  

On this side, wherein the flower is mature in all its petals, are seated who believed in Christ to come.

- *Da questa parte* ‘from this side’ → “On this side”: English uses a preposition of position.
- *onde* ‘whence’ → “wherein”: the relative is taken in a locative rather than a directional sense.
- *di tutte le sue foglie* ‘of all its leaves’ → “in all its petals”: “in” specifies the respect in which the flower is mature, and “petals” narrows the sense of ‘leaves’.
- *quei che* ‘those who’ → “who”: the demonstrative antecedent is omitted.
- *Cristo venturo* ‘Christ about to come’ → “Christ to come”: an infinitive does the work of the future participle.

> 25 Dall' altra parte, onde sono intercisi  
> 26 di voti, in semicircoli si stanno  
> 27 quei ch' a Cristo venuto ebber li visi.  

On the other side, where they are broke by empty seats, abide in semi-circles such as had their sight turned to-wards Christ come.

- *Dall' altra parte* ‘from the other side’ → “On the other side”: the preposition again expresses position in English.
- *onde* ‘whence’ → “where”: the relative is again taken as locative.
- *sono intercisi di voti* ‘are interrupted by gaps’ → “they are broke by empty seats”: “broke” renders the participle, and the substantivized *voti* (‘empty spaces’) is expanded to specify what is empty.
- *in semicircoli si stanno* ‘in semicircles they stay’ → “abide in semi-circles”: the pronominal verb becomes a simple intransitive verb and precedes the phrase of position.
- *quei ch' a Cristo venuto ebber li visi* ‘those who toward Christ come had their eyes’ → “such as had their sight turned to-wards Christ come”: “such as” replaces the demonstrative and relative; the plural becomes the singular faculty “their sight”; “turned” is supplied, and the phrase indicating direction moves to the end.

> 28 E come quinci il glorioso scanno  
> 29 della donna del cielo, e gli altri scanni  
> 30 di sotto lui cotanta cerna fanno,  

And as on the one side the glorious seat of the Lady of heaven and the other seats below it make so great partition,

- *quinci* ‘here on this side’ → “on the one side”: the demonstrative adverb becomes a phrase anticipating the opposite side.
- *il glorioso scanno* ‘the glorious throne’ → “the glorious seat”: the more general noun is used.
- *gli altri scanni* ‘the other thrones’ → “the other seats”: the same substitution is made in the plural.
- *di sotto lui* ‘underneath it’ → “below it”: the compound expression of position becomes a simple preposition.
- *cotanta cerna fanno* ‘so great a division make’ → “make so great partition”: the verb moves before its object, and “partition” is used without an article.

> 31 così di contra quel del gran Giovanni,  
> 32 che sempre santo il diserto e il martiro  
> 33 sofferse, e poi l' Inferno da due anni;  

so, over against her, doth the seat of that great John who ever holy endured the desert and the martyr death and thereafter Hell for two years’ space;

- *di contra* ‘opposite’ → “over against her”: the person opposite is made explicit.
- *quel del gran Giovanni* ‘that of the great John’ → “doth the seat of that great John”: “doth” supplies the verb understood from the preceding clause; the demonstrative standing for the seat is replaced by the noun itself, while John receives a demonstrative in place of the definite article.
- *il diserto e il martiro sofferse* ‘the desert and the martyrdom endured’ → “endured the desert and the martyr death”: the verb moves before its objects, and the single noun for martyrdom becomes a phrase specifying death.
- *poi* ‘then’ → “thereafter”: the succession is expressed explicitly in relation to what precedes.
- *da due anni* ‘for some two years’ → “for two years’ space”: the duration is given without qualification, and “space” is supplied.

> 34 e sotto lui così cerner sortiro  
> 35 Francesco, Benedetto ed Augustino,  
> 36 ed altri sin quaggiù di giro in giro.  

and beneath him the making of such severance hath been assigned to Francis, Benedict and Augustine, and others down to here from circle unto circle.

- *così cerner sortiro* ‘to divide thus they received by lot’ → “the making of such severance hath been assigned to”: the infinitive becomes a noun phrase, with the adverb ‘thus’ becoming the modifier “such”; the active preterite becomes a present perfect passive, and the persons who received the task become the objects of “to”. The suggestion of allotment by lot is not separately rendered.
- *sin quaggiù* ‘as far as here below’ → “down to here”: the downward direction is placed first.
- *di giro in giro* ‘from circle to circle’ → “from circle unto circle”: “unto” marks the successive destinations.

> 37 Or mira l'alto provveder divino,  
> 38 chè l' uno e l' altro aspetto della fede  
> 39 egualmente empierà questo giardino.  

Now marvel at the deep divine provision; for either aspect of the faith, in equal measure shall fill full this garden.

- *mira* ‘behold’ → “marvel at”: an injunction to look becomes an injunction to wonder.
- *l'alto provveder divino* ‘the high divine foreseeing’ → “the deep divine provision”: height becomes depth; the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun, and the adjective meaning ‘divine’ moves before it.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *l' uno e l' altro aspetto* ‘the one and the other aspect’ → “either aspect”: a single distributive word replaces the paired expressions.
- *egualmente* ‘equally’ → “in equal measure”: the adverb becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *empierà* ‘will fill’ → “shall fill full”: “full” makes the result explicit.

> 40 E sappi che dal grado in giù, che fiede  
> 41 a mezzo il tratto le due discrezioni,  
> 42 per nullo proprio merito si siede,  

And know that, downward from the rank which in mid line cleaveth the two divisions, in virtue of no merit of their own they have their seats,

- *dal grado in giù* ‘from the step downward’ → “downward from the rank”: the direction moves first, and the step is rendered as a tier or “rank”.
- *che fiede a mezzo il tratto le due discrezioni* ‘which strikes the two divisions halfway along their extent’ → “which in mid line cleaveth the two divisions”: “cleaveth” gives the effect of the stroke; the expression of position moves before the verb, and the extent traversed is expressed as a “line”.
- *per nullo proprio merito* ‘through no merit of their own’ → “in virtue of no merit of their own”: the simple preposition becomes a phrase specifying the ground of entitlement.
- *si siede* ‘one sits’ → “they have their seats”: the impersonal singular becomes a definite plural, and the verb of posture becomes an expression of possession.

> 43 ma per l'altrui, con certe condizioni;  
> 44 chè tutti questi son spiriti assolti  
> 45 prima ch' avesser vere elezioni.  

but by another’s, under fixed conditions; for these are spirits all released ere they had exercised true choice.

- *con certe condizioni* ‘with certain conditions’ → “under fixed conditions”: the preposition is changed, and *certe* is taken in the sense of determinate rather than unspecified.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *tutti questi son spiriti assolti* ‘all these are spirits released’ → “these are spirits all released”: “all” moves from the demonstrative subject to the phrase describing the spirits.
- *prima ch' avesser vere elezioni* ‘before they had true choices’ → “ere they had exercised true choice”: the imperfect subjunctive of possession becomes a pluperfect of action; “exercised” is supplied, and the plural acts of choosing become singular “choice”.

> 46 Ben te ne puoi accorger per li volti,  
> 47 ed anco per le voci puerili,  
> 48 se tu li guardi bene e se gli ascolti.  

Well mayst thou perceive it by their faces, and also their child voices if thou look aright and if thou listen.

- *Ben te ne puoi accorger* ‘well canst thou become aware of it’ → “Well mayst thou perceive it”: the pronominal construction with *ne* becomes a transitive verb with a direct object after it; ability is expressed by “mayst”.
- *per li volti* ‘by the faces’ → “by their faces”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *per le voci puerili* ‘by the voices childlike’ → “their child voices”: the repeated preposition is omitted, a possessive replaces the article, and the adjective becomes an attributive noun before “voices”.
- *se tu li guardi bene* ‘if thou look at them well’ → “if thou look aright”: the object is omitted, and “aright” gives the sense of looking properly.
- *se gli ascolti* ‘if thou listen to them’ → “if thou listen”: the object is again omitted.

> 49 Or dubbi tu, e dubitando sili;  

Now thou art perplexed, and in perplexity thou keepest silence;

- *dubbi tu* ‘doubtest thou’ → “thou art perplexed”: the subject moves before the verb, and the act of doubting becomes a state.
- *dubitando* ‘doubting’ → “in perplexity”: the gerund becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *sili* ‘art silent’ → “keepest silence”: a single verb becomes a verb with a noun object.

> 50 ma io ti solverò il forte legame,  
> 51 in che ti stringon li pensier sottili.  

but I will loose the hard knot for thee wherein thy subtle thoughts are binding thee.

- *ti solverò il forte legame* ‘for thee I shall loosen the strong bond’ → “will loose the hard knot for thee”: the beneficiary moves after the object; the general ‘bond’ becomes a “knot”, and its strength is rendered as hardness.
- *in che* ‘in which’ → “wherein”: preposition and relative pronoun become a single relative adverb.
- *ti stringon li pensier sottili* ‘thee constrain the thoughts subtle’ → “thy subtle thoughts are binding thee”: the subject moves before the verb and the object after it; a possessive replaces the article, the adjective precedes its noun, and the simple present becomes progressive.

## Lines 52–84 — Grace and the Ranks of Children

> 52 Dentro all' ampiezza di questo reame  
> 53 casual punto non puote aver sito,  
> 54 se non come tristizia, o sete, o fame;  

Within this kingdom’s amplitude no chance point may have place, no more than sadness may nor thirst, nor hunger;

- *Dentro all' ampiezza di questo reame* ‘within the amplitude of this kingdom’ → “Within this kingdom’s amplitude”: the possessive precedes the noun in place of the following *di* phrase.
- *casual punto non puote aver sito* ‘a chance point cannot have place’ → “no chance point may have place”: the negation passes from the verb to the subject; *puote* is rendered by “may” in the sense of possibility.
- *se non come tristizia, o sete, o fame* ‘except as sadness, or thirst, or hunger’ → “no more than sadness may nor thirst, nor hunger”: the exception becomes a negative comparison, the modal verb is repeated, and the alternatives are joined by “nor”.

> 55 chè per eterna legge è stabilito  
> 56 quantunque vedi, sì che giustamente  
> 57 ci si risponde dall' anello al dito.  

because established by eternal law is whatsoe’er thou seest, so that the correspondence is exact between the ring and finger.

- *per eterna legge è stabilito* ‘by eternal law is established’ → “established by eternal law is”: the participle is moved ahead of both the prepositional phrase and the auxiliary.
- *giustamente ci si risponde dall' anello al dito* ‘exactly there is correspondence here from the ring to the finger’ → “the correspondence is exact between the ring and finger”: the impersonal verb becomes a noun with a copula, and the adverb becomes a predicate adjective. Locative *ci* has no separate equivalent; the directional pair ‘from … to’ becomes “between … and”, with one article for both nouns.

> 58 E però questa festinata gente  
> 59 a vera vita non è *sine causa*  
> 60 intra sè qui più e meno eccellente.  

Wherefore this swift-sped folk to the true life is here, not without cause, more or less excellent in mutual order.

- *E però* ‘and therefore’ → “Wherefore”: the connection is expressed by a single inferential word.
- *festinata* ‘hastened’ → “swift-sped”: the participle is rendered by a compound.
- *a vera vita* ‘to true life’ → “to the true life”: a definite article is supplied.
- *non è sine causa intra sè qui più e meno eccellente* ‘is not without cause among themselves here more and less excellent’ → “is here, not without cause, more or less excellent in mutual order”: *qui* moves forward to follow “is”; the Latin phrase is translated, and the negative is grouped with “without cause” rather than with the copula. The reflexive phrase *intra sè* becomes a phrase naming an order, placed after “excellent”; “or” replaces the conjunction ‘and’.

> 61 Lo rege, per cui questo regno pausa  
> 62 in tanto amore ed in tanto diletto,  
> 63 che nulla volontà è di più ausa,  

The King through whom this realm resteth in so great love and in so great delight that never will hath daring for aught more,

- *in tanto amore ed in tanto diletto* ‘in so much love and in so much delight’ → “in so great love and in so great delight”: quantity is expressed as greatness in both phrases.
- *nulla volontà è di più ausa* ‘no will has dared more’ → “never will hath daring for aught more”: the negative determiner becomes the adverb “never”; the compound verb becomes “hath” with a noun for its object, and *di più* is expanded to “for aught more”.

> 64 le menti tutte nel suo lieto aspetto  
> 65 creando, a suo piacer di grazia dota  
> 66 diversamente; e qui basti l'effetto.  

as he createth all minds in his own glad sight, doth at his pleasure with grace endow them diversely; and here let the effect suffice.

- *le menti tutte nel suo lieto aspetto creando* ‘all the minds in his glad sight creating’ → “as he createth all minds in his own glad sight”: the gerund following its complements becomes a finite clause introduced by “as”; the verb precedes its object, “all” precedes “minds”, the definite article disappears, and “own” is supplied.
- *a suo piacer di grazia dota diversamente* ‘at his pleasure with grace endows diversely’ → “doth at his pleasure with grace endow them diversely”: the simple verb is divided into auxiliary and infinitive around the two prepositional phrases; “them” resumes the minds already named.
- *basti l'effetto* ‘may the effect suffice’ → “let the effect suffice”: the exhortative subjunctive becomes a construction with “let”, and “the effect” precedes “suffice”.

> 67 E ciò espresso e chiaro vi si nota  
> 68 nella scrittura santa in quei gemelli,  
> 69 che nella madre ebber l'ira commota.  

And this, express and clear, is noted unto you in Holy Writ, anent those twins whose wrath was stirred within their mother’s womb.

- *vi si nota* ‘to you is noted’ → “is noted unto you”: the passive construction with *si* becomes an English passive auxiliary and participle; the dative follows the verb as a prepositional phrase.
- *nella scrittura santa* ‘in the writing holy’ → “in Holy Writ”: the adjective precedes the noun, the article drops out, and “Writ” renders *scrittura*.
- *in quei gemelli* ‘in those twins’ → “anent those twins”: the preposition is interpreted as ‘concerning’.
- *che nella madre ebber l'ira commota* ‘who in the mother had their wrath stirred’ → “whose wrath was stirred within their mother’s womb”: the subject relative becomes possessive, and wrath becomes the subject of a passive in place of the object of ‘had’. The phrase of place moves to the end; “their mother’s womb” specifies what is expressed simply as *la madre*.

> 70 Però, secondo il color dei capelli  
> 71 di cotal grazia, l'altissimo lume  
> 72 degnamente convien che s' incappelli.  

Wherefore accordant to the colour of the locks of such grace, needs must the lofty light enchaplet them after their worth.

- *secondo il color* ‘according to the colour’ → “accordant to the colour”: an adjectival turn replaces the preposition.
- *dei capelli* ‘of the hairs’ → “of the locks”: the collective image is rendered by “locks” rather than individual hairs.
- *l'altissimo lume* ‘the highest light’ → “the lofty light”: the superlative becomes a positive adjective; the noun phrase follows “needs must” instead of preceding the expression of necessity.
- *degnamente convien che s' incappelli* ‘worthily it is fitting that one be chapleted’ → “needs must” … “enchaplet them after their worth”: the impersonal verb and its subjunctive clause become a modal construction. Wicksteed makes the light the agent of a transitive verb and supplies “them” as its object, rather than retaining the *si* construction; the initial adverb becomes a final phrase explicitly referring worth to the recipients.

> 73 Dunque, senza mercè di lor costume,  
> 74 locati son per gradi differenti,  
> 75 sol differendo nel primiero acume.  

Wherefore, without reward for their own ways, they are placed in different ranks, differing only in their primal keenness.

- *Dunque* ‘therefore’ → “Wherefore”: a different inferential connective is used.
- *senza mercè di lor costume* ‘without reward of their conduct’ → “without reward for their own ways”: *di* is interpreted as giving the grounds for reward; singular *costume* becomes plural “ways”, and “own” is added.
- *locati son* ‘placed are’ → “they are placed”: the auxiliary precedes the participle.
- *per gradi differenti* ‘by ranks different’ → “in different ranks”: “in” replaces distributive *per*, and the adjective precedes its noun.
- *sol differendo* ‘only differing’ → “differing only”: the limiting adverb follows the participle.
- *nel primiero acume* ‘in the first keenness’ → “in their primal keenness”: the definite article becomes a possessive, and “primal” renders *primiero* as indicating an original quality.

> 76 Bastava sì nei secoli recenti  
> 77 con l'innocenza, per aver salute,  
> 78 solamente la fede dei parenti;  

Thus, in the new-born ages the parents’ faith alone sufficed, with innocence, to secure salvation;

- *Bastava sì* ‘sufficed thus’ → “Thus, in the new-born ages the parents’ faith alone sufficed”: “Thus” is moved to the front, and the verb is postponed until after its subject.
- *nei secoli recenti* ‘in the recent ages’ → “in the new-born ages”: *recenti* is taken in the sense of newly begun rather than recent from the speaker’s standpoint.
- *con l'innocenza* ‘with the innocence’ → “with innocence”: the article is omitted.
- *per aver salute* ‘to have salvation’ → “to secure salvation”: possession is expressed as attainment.
- *solamente la fede dei parenti* ‘only the faith of the parents’ → “the parents’ faith alone”: the possessive replaces the following *di* phrase, and “alone” follows the subject instead of introducing it.

> 79 poichè le prime etadi fur compiute,  
> 80 convenne ai maschi all' innocenti penne,  
> 81 per circoncidere, acquistar virtute.  

when the first ages were complete male children behoved to gather power to their innocent wings by circumcision.

- *poichè* ‘after’ → “when”: the temporal conjunction is rendered without explicit ‘after’.
- *fur compiute* ‘were completed’ → “were complete”: the participle of completion becomes an adjective of state.
- *convenne ai maschi* ‘it was necessary for the males’ → “male children behoved”: the dative becomes the subject of a personal verb, and “children” specifies *maschi*.
- *all' innocenti penne* ‘to the innocent feathers’ → “to their innocent wings”: the article becomes a possessive, “wings” takes the feathers as standing for the whole, and the phrase follows the infinitive and its object.
- *per circoncidere* ‘by circumcising’ → “by circumcision”: the infinitive becomes a noun, and the phrase moves to the end.
- *acquistar virtute* ‘to acquire power’ → “to gather power”: “gather” replaces ‘acquire’.

> 82 Ma poichè il tempo della grazia venne,  
> 83 senza battesmo perfetto di Cristo  
> 84 tale innocenza laggiù si ritenne.  

But when the time of grace had come, then without perfect baptism of Christ such innocence was held back there below.

- *poichè il tempo della grazia venne* ‘when the time of the grace came’ → “when the time of grace had come, then”: the article before ‘grace’ disappears, the simple past becomes a past perfect, and “then” is supplied to introduce the main clause.
- *senza battesmo perfetto* ‘without baptism perfect’ → “without perfect baptism”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *laggiù si ritenne* ‘there below was retained’ → “was held back there below”: the passive with *si* becomes an English passive with a phrasal verb, and the place adverb follows it.

## Lines 85–117 — Mary’s Face and Gabriel’s Greeting

> 85 Riguarda omai nella faccia ch' a Cristo  
> 86 più si somiglia, chè la sua chiarezza  
> 87 sola ti può disporre a veder Cristo.”  

Look now upon the face which is most likened unto Christ; for its brightness, and no other, hath power to fit thee to see Christ.”

- *Riguarda omai nella faccia* ‘look now into the face’ → “Look now upon the face”: the direction of the gaze is expressed with “upon” rather than ‘into’.
- *ch' a Cristo più si somiglia* ‘which to Christ most resembles itself’ → “which is most likened unto Christ”: the pronominal verb of resemblance becomes a passive, and the comparison with Christ moves to the end.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *sola* ‘alone’ → “and no other”: the adjective qualifying the brightness is expanded into a separate expression of exclusion.
- *ti può disporre* ‘thee can dispose’ → “hath power to fit thee”: the modal verb becomes a phrase with a noun, ‘dispose’ is rendered as “fit”, and the object follows the infinitive.

> 88 Io vidi sopra lei tanta allegrezza  
> 89 piover, portata nelle menti sante  
> 90 create a trasvolar per quella altezza,  

I saw rain down upon that face such joyance (borne on the sacred minds created for flying through that lofty region),

- *sopra lei tanta allegrezza piover* ‘upon her such joy to rain’ → “rain down upon that face such joyance”: the infinitive moves forward and gains “down”; “that face” replaces the personal pronoun.
- *portata nelle menti sante* ‘borne in the minds holy’ → “borne on the sacred minds”: “on” replaces ‘in’, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *create a trasvolar per quella altezza* ‘created to fly across through that height’ → “created for flying through that lofty region”: the infinitive of purpose becomes a gerund after “for”; “flying through” gathers up the motion across and the preposition, and the abstract ‘height’ becomes a region qualified as “lofty”.

> 91 che quantunque io avea visto davante,  
> 92 di tanta ammirazion non mi sospese,  
> 93 nè mi mostrò di Dio tanto sembiante.  

that all which I had seen before held me not in suspense of so great marvelling, nor showed me so great semblance of God.

- *di tanta ammirazion non mi sospese* ‘in such great wonder did not suspend me’ → “held me not in suspense of so great marvelling”: the verb becomes “held … in suspense”; the object follows “held”, the negative follows the object, and the phrase expressing wonder moves from first to last.
- *mi mostrò* ‘to me showed’ → “showed me”: the indirect object follows the verb.
- *di Dio tanto sembiante* ‘of God so great semblance’ → “so great semblance of God”: the dependent phrase follows its noun.

> 94 E quell'amor che primo lì discese,  
> 95 cantando: *Ave, Maria, gratia plena,*  
> 96 dinanzi a lei le sue ali distese.  

And that Love which first descended to her, singing: *Hail, Mary, full of grace* now spread his wings before her.

- *lì discese* ‘there descended’ → “descended to her”: the indication of place becomes a personal destination and follows the verb.
- *gratia plena* ‘with grace full’ → “full of grace”: the adjective precedes its complement, which English introduces with “of”.
- *dinanzi a lei le sue ali distese* ‘before her his wings he spread’ → “now spread his wings before her”: the verb comes first, the phrase of position comes last, and “now” is supplied.

> 97 Rispose alla divina cantilena  
> 98 da tutte parti la beata corte,  
> 99 sì ch' ogni vista sen fe' più serena.  

The divine canticle was answered from every side by the blest Court, so that every face thereby gathered serenity.

- *Rispose alla divina cantilena* ‘answered to the divine canticle’ → “The divine canticle was answered”: the active verb becomes passive, with the object of the Italian preposition as its subject.
- *da tutte parti* ‘from all parts’ → “from every side”: the plural becomes a distributive singular.
- *la beata corte* ‘the blest Court’ → “by the blest Court”: the postposed subject becomes the agent of the passive.
- *ogni vista* ‘every countenance’ → “every face”: the outward look is rendered by the more concrete noun.
- *sen fe' più serena* ‘made itself thereby more serene’ → “thereby gathered serenity”: the reflexive verb and predicate adjective become a verb of acquisition and its noun object; the increase expressed by the comparative is implicit in “gathered”.

> 100 “O santo Padre, che per me comporte  
> 101 l'esser quaggiù lasciando il dolce loco  
> 102 nel qual tu siedi per eterna sorte,  

“O holy Father, who for my sake acceptest being here below, leaving the sweet place wherein thou sittest by eternal lot,

- *per me comporte* ‘for me dost endure’ → “for my sake acceptest”: the prepositional phrase is expanded, and bearing something is expressed as accepting it.
- *l'esser quaggiù* ‘the being down here’ → “being here below”: the substantivized infinitive becomes an articleless gerund, and the elements of the place expression are reversed.
- *nel qual* ‘in which’ → “wherein”: the preposition and relative are combined into one adverb.
- *per eterna sorte* ‘by eternal allotment’ → “by eternal lot”: the noun is rendered by the shorter term for what is allotted.

> 103 qual è quell' angel, che con tanto gioco  
> 104 guarda negli occhi la nostra Regina,  
> 105 innamorato sì che par di foco?”  

what is that angel who with such delight looketh our Queen in the eyes, enamoured so he seemeth all aflame?”

- *qual è* ‘which is’ → “what is”: the interrogative asking which one is meant becomes “what”.
- *con tanto gioco* ‘with so much joy’ → “with such delight”: a phrase of degree becomes one with “such”, and joy is rendered as delight.
- *guarda negli occhi la nostra Regina* ‘looks in the eyes our Queen’ → “looketh our Queen in the eyes”: the personal object moves before the phrase specifying where he looks.
- *innamorato sì che par di foco* ‘enamoured so that he seems of fire’ → “enamoured so he seemeth all aflame”: there is no separate ‘that’, and the phrase of substance becomes a description of being aflame, intensified by the supplied “all”.

> 106 Così ricorsi ancora alla dottrina  
> 107 di colui ch'abbelliva di Maria,  
> 108 come del sole stella mattutina.  

So did I turn again unto his teaching who drew beauty from Mary, as from the sun the morning star.

- *Così ricorsi ancora* ‘thus I had recourse again’ → “So did I turn again”: having recourse becomes turning, with auxiliary inversion after “So”.
- *alla dottrina di colui* ‘to the teaching of him’ → “unto his teaching”: the possessor becomes a possessive adjective before the noun, leaving the following relative to refer back to “his”.
- *ch'abbelliva di Maria* ‘who grew beautiful from Mary’ → “who drew beauty from Mary”: the verb expressing a gain in beauty becomes a transitive verb with “beauty” as its object; the imperfect is rendered by the simple past.
- *stella mattutina* ‘star morning’ → “the morning star”: the modifier precedes the noun, and a definite article is supplied.

> 109 Ed egli a me: “Baldezza e leggiadria,  
> 110 quanta esser può in Angelo ed in alma,  
> 111 tutta è in lui, e sì volem che sia,  

And he to me, “Exultancy and winsomeness as much as there may be in angel or in soul, is all in him; and we would have it so,

- *Baldezza e leggiadria* ‘boldness and grace’ → “Exultancy and winsomeness”: the first noun is taken in the sense of joyful assurance, the second in that of personal charm.
- *quanta esser può* ‘as much as be can’ → “as much as there may be”: the modal comes before the infinitive, ‘can’ becomes “may”, and existential “there” is supplied.
- *in Angelo ed in alma* ‘in angel and in soul’ → “in angel or in soul”: the conjunction becomes disjunctive.
- *tutta è in lui* ‘all is in him’ → “is all in him”: “all” moves after the verb.
- *e sì volem che sia* ‘and so we will that it be’ → “and we would have it so”: willing followed by a subjunctive clause becomes “would have” with an object and complement; “so” moves to the end.

> 112 perch' egli è quegli che portò la palma  
> 113 giù a Maria, quando il figliuol di Dio  
> 114 carcar si volle della nostra salma.  

for he it is who brought down the palm to Mary, when the Son of God willed to load him with our burden.

- *perch'* ‘because’ → “for”: the causal subordinator becomes a coordinating conjunction.
- *egli è quegli che* ‘he is the one who’ → “he it is who”: the demonstrative antecedent gives way to an emphatic construction with “it”.
- *portò la palma giù* ‘brought the palm down’ → “brought down the palm”: the directional adverb precedes the object.
- *carcar si volle* ‘to load himself he willed’ → “willed to load him”: the finite verb comes first, and the reflexive is rendered by the simple pronoun “him” after the infinitive.

> 115 Ma vieni omai con gli occhi, sì com' io  
> 116 andrò parlando, e nota i gran patrici  
> 117 di questo imperio giustissimo e pio.  

But come now with thine eyes even as I shall traverse in discourse, and note the great patricians of this most just and pious empire.

- *sì com' io andrò parlando* ‘just as I shall go speaking’ → “even as I shall traverse in discourse”: the construction with ‘go’ and a gerund is recast as a verb of motion qualified by a prepositional phrase; speaking becomes “discourse”.
- *di questo imperio giustissimo e pio* ‘of this empire most just and pious’ → “of this most just and pious empire”: both modifiers move before the noun, and the superlative suffix of the first adjective is expressed by “most”.

## Lines 118–151 — The Roots of the Rose and the Turn to Prayer

> 118 Quei due che seggon lassù più felici,  
> 119 per esser propinquissimi ad Augusta,  
> 120 son d' esta rosa quasi due radici.  

Those two who sit up there, most blest by being nearest to the Empress, are as two roots of this our rose.

- *più felici* ‘happier’ → “most blest”: the comparative is taken in a superlative sense, and happiness is expressed as blessedness.
- *per esser propinquissimi ad Augusta* ‘because of being very near to Augusta’ → “by being nearest to the Empress”: cause is expressed by “by”; the superlative is taken as relative to the other seats, and the imperial title is translated with a definite article.
- *son d' esta rosa quasi due radici* ‘are of this rose as it were two roots’ → “are as two roots of this our rose”: the phrase naming the rose is moved after “roots”; “as” makes the comparison directly, and “our” is added.

> 121 Colui che da sinistra le s'aggiusta,  
> 122 è il padre, per lo cui ardito gusto  
> 123 l'umana specie tanto amaro gusta.  

He who neighboureth her upon the left is that Father because of whose audacious tasting the human race tasteth such bitterness.

- *da sinistra le s'aggiusta* ‘on the left places himself beside her’ → “neighboureth her upon the left”: the reflexive construction becomes a transitive verb of position, and the phrase of place moves to the end.
- *il padre* ‘the father’ → “that Father”: a demonstrative replaces the article.
- *per lo cui ardito gusto* ‘because of whose bold taste’ → “because of whose audacious tasting”: the verbal noun “tasting” specifies the act rather than its sensation.
- *l'umana specie* ‘the human species’ → “the human race”: “race” replaces ‘species’.
- *tanto amaro gusta* ‘so much bitter tastes’ → “tasteth such bitterness”: the object follows the verb; the substantivized adjective becomes an abstract noun, and quantity is expressed instead by “such”.

> 124 Dal destro vedi quel padre vetusto  
> 125 di santa Chiesa, cui Cristo le chiavi  
> 126 raccomandò di questo fior venusto.  

On the right, look upon that ancient Father of Holy Church to whom Christ commended the keys of this lovesome flower.

- *Dal destro* ‘from the right [side]’ → “On the right”: the preposition expresses position rather than direction from a side.
- *vedi* ‘see’ → “look upon”: seeing is rendered as a direction to look.
- *padre vetusto* ‘father ancient’ → “ancient Father”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *le chiavi raccomandò* ‘the keys commended’ → “commended the keys”: the object follows the verb.
- *fior venusto* ‘flower lovely’ → “lovesome flower”: the adjective precedes the noun.

> 127 E quei che vide tutt' i tempi gravi,  
> 128 pria che morisse, della bella sposa  
> 129 che s'acquistò con la lancia e coi chiavi,  

And he who, ere he died, saw all the grievous seasons of that fair spouse who with the lance and with the nails was won,

- *vide tutt' i tempi gravi* ‘saw all the times grievous’ → “saw all the grievous seasons”: “seasons” renders ‘times’, and the adjective precedes its noun.
- *pria che morisse* ‘before he should die’ → “ere he died”: the time clause moves ahead of “saw”; the Italian imperfect subjunctive becomes an English simple past.
- *della bella sposa* ‘of the fair spouse’ → “of that fair spouse”: a demonstrative replaces the article.
- *che s'acquistò con la lancia e coi chiavi* ‘who was acquired with the lance and with the nails’ → “who with the lance and with the nails was won”: the passive construction with *si* becomes a passive with “was”; “won” renders ‘acquired’, and the instruments come before the verb.

> 130 siede lungh' esso; e lungo l'altro posa  
> 131 quel duca, sotto cui visse di manna  
> 132 la gente ingrata, mobile e ritrosa.  

sitteth by his side; and by the other resteth that leader under whom was fed by manna the folk ungrateful, fickle and mutinous.

- *lungh' esso* ‘beside him’ → “by his side”: the pronoun becomes a possessive qualifying the supplied “side”.
- *visse di manna* ‘lived on manna’ → “was fed by manna”: the active verb of subsistence becomes a passive of feeding, with “by” in place of ‘on’.
- *mobile e ritrosa* ‘changeable and refractory’ → “fickle and mutinous”: the first adjective specifies inconstancy, the second more strongly expresses active resistance.

> 133 Di contro a Pietro vedi sedere Anna,  
> 134 tanto contenta di mirar sua figlia,  
> 135 che non move occhi per cantare Osanna.  

Over against Peter see Anna sit, so satisfied to gaze upon her daughter that she removeth not her eyes to sing Hosanna.

- *vedi sedere Anna* ‘see sit Anna’ → “see Anna sit”: the subject of the infinitive comes before it.
- *tanto contenta di mirar* ‘so content in gazing at’ → “so satisfied to gaze upon”: contentment in the act is expressed by “satisfied” with an infinitive.
- *non move occhi* ‘does not move eyes’ → “she removeth not her eyes”: “removeth” specifies moving away, “not” follows the verb, and “her” is supplied.

> 136 E contro al maggior padre di famiglia  
> 137 siede Lucia, che mosse la tua donna,  
> 138 quando chinavi, a ruinar, le ciglia.  

And o’er against the greatest of housefathers sitteth Lucy who moved thy Lady when thou wert stooping down thy brows to thy destruction.

- *al maggior padre di famiglia* ‘the greatest father of a household’ → “the greatest of housefathers”: the singular noun with its complement becomes a plural compound in a partitive construction.
- *chinavi, a ruinar, le ciglia* ‘wert lowering, to fall, the brows’ → “thou wert stooping down thy brows to thy destruction”: the progressive conveys the imperfect; “down” makes the direction explicit, and “thy” specifies whose brows. The intervening infinitive moves to the end and becomes a noun phrase with a second “thy”; the fall is interpreted as destruction.

> 139 Ma perchè il tempo fugge, che t'assonna,  
> 140 qui farem punto, come buon sartore  
> 141 che, com' egli ha del panno, fa la gonna;  

But since the time that doth entrance thee fleeth, here let us make a stop, like to the careful tailor who to the cloth he hath cutteth the garment;

- *il tempo fugge, che t'assonna* ‘the time flees, which lulls thee to sleep’ → “the time that doth entrance thee fleeth”: the relative clause comes directly after “time”, before its verb; inducing sleep is rendered as entrancing.
- *farem punto* ‘we shall make a stop’ → “let us make a stop”: the future becomes an exhortation.
- *come buon sartore* ‘like a good tailor’ → “like to the careful tailor”: “to” is supplied, the tailor takes a definite article, and general excellence is specified as care.
- *com' egli ha del panno* ‘according as he has cloth’ → “to the cloth he hath”: the clause of proportion becomes a phrase headed by “to”, with a relative clause modifying “cloth”; the partitive becomes definite.
- *fa la gonna* ‘makes the gown’ → “cutteth the garment”: making is narrowed to cutting, while “garment” is less specific than ‘gown’.

> 142 e drizzeremo gli occhi al primo amore,  
> 143 sì che, guardando verso lui, penetri,  
> 144 quant' è possibil, per lo suo fulgore.  

and let us turn our eyes to the Primal Love, so that gazing toward him thou mayst pierce as far as may be into his shining.

- *drizzeremo gli occhi* ‘we shall direct the eyes’ → “let us turn our eyes”: the future again becomes an exhortation, directing is expressed as turning, and a possessive replaces the article.
- *primo amore* ‘first love’ → “Primal Love”: “Primal” gives ‘first’ the sense of original or primary.
- *penetri* ‘thou penetrate’ → “thou mayst pierce”: the purpose-clause subjunctive is expressed with the modal “mayst”.
- *quant' è possibil* ‘as much as is possible’ → “as far as may be”: degree is expressed as distance, possibility by a modal rather than an adjective.
- *per lo suo fulgore* ‘through his radiance’ → “into his shining”: “into” marks penetration rather than passage through; “shining” renders the noun of brilliance.

> 145 Veramente (nè forse tu t'arretri  
> 146 movendo l'ali tue, credendo oltrarti)  
> 147 orando grazia convien che s' impetri,  

But—lest perchance thou backward fall as thou dost ply thy wings, thinking to forward thee,—by prayer behoveth grace to be acquired,

- *Veramente* ‘truly’ → “But”: the adverb is taken adversatively, as a qualification rather than an affirmation.
- *nè forse tu t'arretri* ‘lest perchance thou draw thyself back’ → “lest perchance thou backward fall”: *nè* is taken in the preventive sense ‘lest’; the reflexive verb becomes an intransitive verb with a directional adverb.
- *movendo l'ali tue* ‘moving the wings of thine’ → “as thou dost ply thy wings”: the gerund becomes a finite time clause, “ply” renders ‘move’, and the possessive precedes the noun.
- *credendo oltrarti* ‘believing thyself to advance’ → “thinking to forward thee”: belief is expressed as thinking, and advancing as forwarding oneself.
- *orando* ‘by praying’ → “by prayer”: a noun replaces the gerund.
- *grazia convien che s' impetri* ‘grace it is needful that one obtain by entreaty’ → “behoveth grace to be acquired”: “grace” moves after the verb of necessity; the finite subjunctive construction becomes a passive infinitive, and “acquired” does not itself express entreaty.

> 148 grazia da quella che può aiutarti;  

grace from her who hath power to aid thee;

- *può aiutarti* ‘can aid thee’ → “hath power to aid thee”: the modal verb is expanded into a phrase naming the power.

> 149 e tu mi segui con l'affezione,  
> 150 sì che dal dicer mio lo cor non parti.”  

and do thou follow me with such affection that from my words thy heart be severed not.”

- *tu mi segui* ‘thou me follow’ → “do thou follow me”: the imperative takes emphatic “do”, and the object follows the verb.
- *con l'affezione, sì che* ‘with affection, so that’ → “with such affection that”: the definite article disappears, and the force of *sì* is carried back into “such”.
- *dal dicer mio* ‘from the speaking of mine’ → “from my words”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a plural noun for what is said, with the possessive before it.
- *lo cor non parti* ‘the heart thou separate not’ → “thy heart be severed not”: the active verb addressed to ‘thou’ becomes passive, its object becomes its subject, and “thy” replaces the article.

> 151 E cominciò questa santa orazione.  

And he began this holy prayer.

- Literal.

## Summary — Word Order and Idiom

Wicksteed often follows the order of Dante’s phrases while rearranging the words within them. In *le chiavi raccomandò*, ‘the keys’ precedes the verb; in “commended the keys” it follows. The adjective in *fior venusto* moves before its noun in “lovesome flower”, and the possessive in *l’ampiezza di questo reame* moves forward in “this kingdom’s amplitude”. Yet Wicksteed does not make every inversion disappear: “sitteth below her, Rachael” postpones the subject even longer than the Italian does. He also keeps close to Dante’s repetitions, notably in “from circle unto circle” for *di giro in giro* and in the echo of “tasting” and “tasteth” for *gusto* and *gusta*.

His choice of words sometimes makes a relation explicit that the Italian leaves to context. Thus *muro*, ‘wall’, becomes “partition-wall”, and *foglia*, ‘leaf’, becomes “petal”. Elsewhere the emphasis shifts: *mira*, ‘behold’, becomes “marvel at”, and the height suggested by *alto* becomes depth in “the deep divine provision”. Wicksteed supplies “these” to gather up a preceding list, but leaves *così*, ‘thus’, without a separate equivalent there. More routinely, he supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending identifies the subject, and omits the Italian article before a possessive: *le sue ali* becomes “his wings”.

Changes of construction can preserve a relation while reversing the grammatical roles. *Rispose alla divina cantilena* becomes “The divine canticle was answered”: what follows a preposition in Italian becomes the subject of an English passive. In *si siede*, ‘one sits’, impersonal posture becomes personal possession: “they have their seats”. Likewise *dubitando*, ‘doubting’, becomes “in perplexity”, though the English still echoes the preceding “perplexed” as the Italian echoes *dubbi*. Such recasting sits beside passages of almost word-for-word correspondence: at the close, *cominciò questa santa orazione* gives “began this holy prayer” without a change of order or image.
