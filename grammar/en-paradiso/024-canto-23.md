# Paradiso 23

## Lines 1–15 — Beatrice Waits Like a Bird for Dawn

> 1 Come l' augello, intra l' amate fronde,  
> 2 posato al nido dei suoi dolci nati  
> 3 la notte che le cose ci nasconde,  

As the bird amidst the loved foliage who hath brooded on the nest of her sweet offspring through the night which hideth things from us,

- *fronde* ‘boughs’ → “foliage”: a collective singular replaces the plural.
- *posato al nido* ‘settled at the nest’ → “who hath brooded on the nest”: the participle becomes a relative clause with a perfect verb; resting is rendered more specifically as brooding, and ‘at’ becomes “on”.
- *suoi dolci nati* ‘its sweet young ones’ → “her sweet offspring”: English makes the bird female where the Italian possessive does not specify the possessor’s sex, and uses a collective for the plural.
- *la notte* ‘the night’ → “through the night”: a preposition makes the duration explicit.
- *le cose ci nasconde* ‘the things from us hides’ → “hideth things from us”: the verb comes first, the article is dropped, and a prepositional phrase renders the clitic *ci*.

> 4 che, per veder gli aspetti disiati,  

who, to look upon their longed-for aspect

- *veder* ‘see’ → “look upon”: perception is rendered as an act of looking.
- *gli aspetti disiati* ‘the aspects desired’ → “their longed-for aspect”: the article becomes a possessive, the plural becomes singular, and the participial adjective precedes the noun.

> 5 e per trovar lo cibo onde li pasca,  

and to find the food wherewith to feed them,

- *onde li pasca* ‘with which she may feed them’ → “wherewith to feed them”: an infinitive replaces the finite subjunctive, and the object follows the verb.

> 6 in che i gravi labor gli sono aggrati,  

wherein her heavy toils are pleasant to her,

- *i gravi labor* ‘the heavy toils’ → “her heavy toils”: a possessive replaces the article.
- *gli sono aggrati* ‘to it are pleasing’ → “are pleasant to her”: the dative becomes a prepositional phrase after the predicate, with a feminine pronoun for the bird.

> 7 previene il tempo in su l' aperta frasca,  

foreruns the time, upon the open spray,

- *in su* ‘up on’ → “upon”: one preposition renders the compound expression of position.

> 8 e con ardente affetto il sole aspetta,  

and with glowing love awaiteth the sun,

- *ardente affetto* ‘burning affection’ → “glowing love”: “glowing” varies the image of burning, and “love” specifies the affection.
- *il sole aspetta* ‘the sun awaits’ → “awaiteth the sun”: the object follows the verb.

> 9 fiso guardando pur che l' alba nasca;  

fixedly gazing for the dawn to rise;

- *pur che l' alba nasca* ‘only for the dawn to be born’ → “for the dawn to rise”: the emphasis of *pur* is not separately rendered; the subordinate clause with a subjunctive becomes a for-infinitive, and rising replaces the image of birth.

> 10 così la donna mia si stava eretta  
> 11 ed attenta, rivolta in ver la plaga,  
> 12 sotto la quale il sol mostra men fretta,  

so was my Lady standing, erect and eager, turned toward the region beneath which the sun showeth least speed;

- *la donna mia si stava eretta* ‘my lady remained erect’ → “was my Lady standing, erect”: the possessive moves before the noun, and the pronominal imperfect becomes a progressive of posture, with the auxiliary before the subject.
- *attenta* ‘attentive’ → “eager”: readiness of attention is rendered as eagerness.
- *mostra men fretta* ‘shows less haste’ → “showeth least speed”: a superlative replaces the comparative, and “speed” renders ‘haste’.

> 13 sì che veggendola io sospesa e vaga,  

so that, as I looked on her in her suspense and longing,

- *veggendola io* ‘seeing her I’ → “as I looked on her”: the gerund becomes a finite temporal clause, the expressed subject moves before the verb, and seeing becomes looking on.
- *sospesa e vaga* ‘in suspense and desirous’ → “in her suspense and longing”: two adjectives predicated of the object become nouns governed by a preposition, with a possessive supplied.

> 14 fecimi quale è quei, che disiando  
> 15 altro vorria e sperando s' appaga.  

I became like him who, desiring, would fain have other than he hath, and payeth him with hope.

- *fecimi* ‘made myself’ → “I became”: an intransitive verb replaces the reflexive.
- *quale è quei* ‘such as is he’ → “like him”: a preposition with an object pronoun replaces the comparison with its own finite verb.
- *altro vorria* ‘something else he would want’ → “would fain have other than he hath”: the object follows the verb; willingness to have renders the conditional of wanting, and the clause “than he hath” makes the contrast explicit.
- *sperando s' appaga* ‘hoping, he contents himself’ → “payeth him with hope”: the gerund becomes a phrase of means after the verb; “payeth” uses the sense of giving satisfaction, with “him” used reflexively.

## Lines 16–48 — Christ’s Triumph and Beatrice’s Smile

> 16 Ma poco fu tra uno ed altro quando,  
> 17 del mio attender, dico, e del vedere  
> 18 lo ciel venir più e più rischiarando.  

But short the space ’twixt one and the other *when*, of fixing my attent I mean, and of seeing the heaven grow brilliant more and more.

- *poco fu* ‘little was [the interval]’ → “short the space”: the noun for the interval is supplied, the adjective expresses duration, and the verb is omitted.
- *del mio attender, dico* ‘of my attending, I say’ → “of fixing my attent I mean”: the possessive moves from the substantivized infinitive to the object of the new verb “fixing”; ‘I say’ becomes an expression of clarification.
- *del vedere lo ciel* ‘of the seeing the heaven’ → “of seeing the heaven”: the English gerund takes no article.
- *venir più e più rischiarando* ‘come more and more brightening’ → “grow brilliant more and more”: the verb of becoming takes an adjective instead of a gerund, and the expression of degree comes last.

> 19 E Beatrice disse: “Ecco le schiere  
> 20 del trionfo di Cristo, e tutto il frutto  
> 21 ricolto del girar di queste spere.”  

And Beatrice said: “Behold the hosts of Christ’s triumph, and all the fruit gathered by the circling of these spheres.”

- *del trionfo di Cristo* ‘of the triumph of Christ’ → “of Christ’s triumph”: the second *di* phrase becomes a possessive before the noun.
- *del girar* ‘of the circling’ → “by the circling”: the preposition makes the circling the means of gathering.

> 22 Pareami che il suo viso ardesse tutto,  
> 23 e gli occhi avea di letizia sì pieni  
> 24 che passar mi convien senza costrutto.  

Meseemed her countenance was all aglow, and her eyes so full of gladness, that I must needs pass it unconstrued by.

- *Pareami che il suo viso ardesse tutto* ‘it seemed to me that her face burned all over’ → “Meseemed her countenance was all aglow”: “Meseemed” incorporates the pronoun; the clause loses its introductory conjunction, and a verb of burning in the subjunctive becomes a description with “was”.
- *e gli occhi avea di letizia sì pieni* ‘and the eyes she had of gladness so full’ → “and her eyes so full of gladness”: possession is expressed by “her” instead of a verb; “so full” precedes its complement.
- *passar mi convien senza costrutto* ‘to pass on is necessary for me without an account’ → “I must needs pass it unconstrued by”: impersonal necessity becomes a personal modal construction; “pass … by” takes a supplied object, and “unconstrued” describes that object instead of saying how the speaker must pass on.

> 25 Quale nei plenilunii sereni  
> 26 Trivia ride tra le ninfe eterne,  
> 27 che dipingono il ciel per tutti i seni,  

As in the calm full moons Trivia smileth amongst the eternal nymphs who paint the heaven in each recess,

- *nei plenilunii sereni* ‘in the full moons serene’ → “in the calm full moons”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *le ninfe eterne* ‘the nymphs eternal’ → “the eternal nymphs”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *per tutti i seni* ‘through all the recesses’ → “in each recess”: a preposition of extent becomes one of place, and the plural is made distributive and singular.

> 28 vid' io, sopra migliaia di lucerne,  
> 29 un sol che tutte quante l' accendea,  
> 30 come fa il nostro le viste superne;  

I saw, thousands of lamps surmounting, one sun which all and each enkindled, as doth our own the things we see above;

- *vid' io* ‘saw I’ → “I saw”: the explicit subject precedes the verb.
- *sopra migliaia di lucerne* ‘above thousands of lamps’ → “thousands of lamps surmounting”: the preposition becomes a participle following its object.
- *un sol* ‘a sun’ → “one sun”: the numeral force of the article is made explicit.
- *che tutte quante l' accendea* ‘which lit them all’ → “which all and each enkindled”: the emphatic totality is expressed distributively as well as collectively; the object still precedes the verb, but is not resumed by a pronoun.
- *le viste superne* ‘the sights above’ → “the things we see above”: the noun becomes the object of a supplied relative clause with “we” as subject.

> 31 e per la viva luce trasparea  
> 32 la lucente sustanzia tanto chiara  
> 33 nel viso mio, che non la sostenea.  

and through the living light outglowed the shining substance so bright upon my vision that it endured it not.

- *per la viva luce trasparea* ‘through the living light shone through’ → “through the living light outglowed”: the verb expresses the emergence of a glow rather than repeating the notion of shining through.
- *tanto chiara nel viso mio* ‘so bright in the sight of mine’ → “so bright upon my vision”: the preposition changes, and the possessive precedes the noun.
- *non la sostenea* ‘did not sustain it’ → “it endured it not”: ‘sustain’ becomes ‘endure’; the object follows the verb, and the negative comes last.

> 34 O Beatrice, dolce guida e cara!  

Oh Beatrice, sweet guide and dear!

- Literal.

> 35 Ella mi disse: “Quel che ti sopranza  
> 36 è virtù, da cui nulla si ripara.  

She said to me: “That which o’ercometh thee is power against which nought hath defence.

- *mi disse* ‘to me said’ → “said to me”: the indirect object follows the verb.
- *ti sopranza* ‘thee surpasses’ → “o’ercometh thee”: the verb expresses overpowering rather than exceeding, and the object follows it.
- *da cui nulla si ripara* ‘from which nothing shelters itself’ → “against which nought hath defence”: the reflexive verb becomes a construction with “hath” and a noun; “against” replaces the preposition of separation.

> 37 Quivi è la sapienza e la possanza  
> 38 ch' aprì le strade intra il cielo e la terra,  
> 39 onde fu già sì lunga disianza.”  

Therein is the wisdom and the might which oped the pathways betwixt heaven and earth, for which there erst had been so long desire.”

- *Quivi* ‘there’ → “Therein”: the English makes the relation one of inclusion.
- *intra il cielo e la terra* ‘between the heaven and the earth’ → “betwixt heaven and earth”: both articles are dropped.
- *onde fu già sì lunga disianza* ‘of which there was formerly so long a longing’ → “for which there erst had been so long desire”: “for” introduces the object of desire, and the simple past becomes a pluperfect.

> 40 Come foco di nube si disserra  
> 41 per dilatarsi sì che non vi cape,  
> 42 e fuor di sua natura in giù s' atterra;  

Even as fire is unbarred from the cloud, because it so dilateth that it hath not space within, and counter to its nature dasheth down to earth,

- *Come* ‘as’ → “Even as”: the comparison is reinforced.
- *foco di nube si disserra* ‘fire from cloud unbars itself’ → “fire is unbarred from the cloud”: the pronominal verb becomes passive, the source follows the verb, and the cloud receives an article.
- *per dilatarsi sì* ‘through expanding itself so’ → “because it so dilateth”: a causal infinitive becomes a finite clause; the verb is intransitive rather than reflexive, and the degree word precedes it.
- *non vi cape* ‘it does not fit there’ → “it hath not space within”: fitting is recast as having space, and the locative pronoun becomes “within”.
- *fuor di sua natura* ‘outside its nature’ → “counter to its nature”: the spatial expression becomes one of opposition.
- *in giù s' atterra* ‘downward it casts itself to earth’ → “dasheth down to earth”: a verb of forceful motion replaces the reflexive verb; the direction follows it, and the destination is expressed separately.

> 43 la mente mia così, tra quelle dape  
> 44 fatta più grande, di sè stessa uscio,  
> 45 e, che si fesse, rimembrar non sape. . . .  

so my mind, grown greater ’mid these feasts, forth issued from itself, and what it then became knoweth not to recall.

- *la mente mia così* ‘the mind of mine thus’ → “so my mind”: the correlative adverb comes first, and the possessive precedes the noun.
- *tra quelle dape fatta più grande* ‘amid those feasts made greater’ → “grown greater ’mid these feasts”: the participial phrase precedes the phrase of place; ‘made’ becomes the intransitive “grown”, and the demonstrative changes from distant to near.
- *di sè stessa uscio* ‘from itself it went out’ → “forth issued from itself”: the source follows the verb, and “forth” reinforces the motion out.
- *che si fesse* ‘what it became’ → “what it then became”: “then” is supplied; the Italian pronominal subjunctive in the indirect question is represented by a simple past.
- *rimembrar non sape* ‘to remember it knows not’ → “knoweth not to recall”: the infinitive follows the finite verb and its negative.

> 46 “Apri gli occhi e riguarda qual son io;  

. . . “Open thine eyes and look on what I am;

- *gli occhi* ‘the eyes’ → “thine eyes”: English marks the possessor of the body part explicitly.
- *riguarda qual son io* ‘look what manner of thing am I’ → “look on what I am”: the verb takes “on”; “what” renders the qualitative interrogative, and the explicit subject precedes the verb.

> 47 tu hai vedute cose, che possente  
> 48 sei fatto a sostener lo riso mio.”  

thou hast seen things by which thou art made mighty to sustain my smile.”

- *che possente sei fatto* ‘such that mighty thou art made’ → “by which thou art made mighty”: the result clause is read instrumentally as a relative clause, and the predicative adjective follows “made”.

## Lines 49–81 — The Sacred Smile and the Flowering Garden

> 49 Io era come quei, che si risente  
> 50 di vision obblita, e che s' ingegna  
> 51 indarno di ridurlasi alla mente,  

I was as one who cometh to himself from a forgotten vision, and doth strive in vain to bring it back unto his mind,

- *quei, che* ‘he who’ → “one who”: the demonstrative gives way to an indefinite pronoun.
- *si risente* ‘reawakens’ → “cometh to himself”: the reflexive verb is rendered by a phrase for recovering consciousness.
- *di vision obblita* ‘from vision forgotten’ → “from a forgotten vision”: English supplies the indefinite article and puts the participle before the noun.
- *e che s' ingegna* ‘and who strives’ → “and doth strive”: the relative pronoun is not repeated; the reflexive verb becomes a simple verb with auxiliary “doth”.
- *di ridurlasi alla mente* ‘to bring it back to mind for himself’ → “to bring it back unto his mind”: the object attached to the infinitive follows “bring”; the reflexive dative is expressed by making the mind “his”.

> 52 quando io udi' questa profferta, degna  
> 53 di tanto grado, che mai non si estingue  
> 54 del libro che il preterito rassegna.  

when I heard this proffer, worthy of so great gratitude, as never to be blotted from the book that doth record the past.

- *di tanto grado, che mai non si estingue* ‘of so much gratitude that it is never extinguished’ → “of so great gratitude, as never to be blotted”: the finite result clause becomes an infinitive with “as”; English uses the single negative “never” and substitutes an image of erasure for one of extinction.
- *che il preterito rassegna* ‘that the past records’ → “that doth record the past”: the object moves after the verb, which takes the auxiliary “doth”.

> 55 Se mo sonasser tutte quelle lingue,  
> 56 che Polinnia con le suore fero  
> 57 del latte lor dolcissimo più pingue,  

If now there were to sound all of those tongues which Polyhymnia with her sisters made richest with their sweetest milk,

- *sonasser* ‘should sound’ → “there were to sound”: the hypothetical imperfect subjunctive becomes a construction with introductory “there” and an infinitive.
- *tutte quelle lingue* ‘all those tongues’ → “all of those tongues”: English inserts “of”.
- *con le suore* ‘with the sisters’ → “with her sisters”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *fero … più pingue* ‘made … more fat’ → “made richest”: the comparative is read in a superlative sense, and figurative fatness is expressed as richness; the complement moves up beside “made”.
- *del latte lor dolcissimo* ‘of the milk their sweetest’ → “with their sweetest milk”: “with” marks the means of enrichment; possessive and adjective both precede the noun.

> 58 per aiutarmi, al millesmo del vero  
> 59 non si verria, cantando il santo riso,  
> 60 e quanto il santo aspetto facea mero.  

it would not mount, in aiding me, unto the thousandth of the truth, hymning the sacred smile, and how deep-clear it made the sacred aspect.

- *per aiutarmi* ‘in order to help me’ → “in aiding me”: purpose becomes an accompanying circumstance, placed after the main verb.
- *al millesmo del vero* ‘to the thousandth of the true’ → “unto the thousandth of the truth”: the substantivized adjective becomes an abstract noun; the phrase follows rather than precedes the verb of reaching.
- *non si verria* ‘one would not come’ → “it would not mount”: the impersonal construction is recast with “it”, and coming to a point becomes mounting to it.
- *cantando il santo riso* ‘singing the holy smile’ → “hymning the sacred smile”: “hymning” gives the singing a specifically devotional character; “sacred” renders ‘holy’.
- *e quanto il santo aspetto facea mero* ‘and how much it made the holy aspect pure’ → “and how deep-clear it made the sacred aspect”: the final predicative adjective moves up beside the degree word; the compound “deep-clear” interprets the purity as clarity, and the object follows the verb.

> 61 E così, figurando il Paradiso,  
> 62 convien saltar lo sacrato poema,  
> 63 come chi trova suo cammin reciso.  

And therefore, figuring Paradise, needs must the sacred poem make a leap, as who should find his pathway intercepted.

- *E così* ‘and so’ → “And therefore”: the connection is made explicitly inferential.
- *convien saltar lo sacrato poema* ‘it is necessary for the consecrated poem to leap’ → “needs must the sacred poem make a leap”: impersonal necessity becomes a modal construction with the poem as subject; the infinitive becomes “make” with a noun, and “sacred” gives the state rather than the participial ‘consecrated’.
- *come chi trova* ‘like one who finds’ → “as who should find”: the indicative present becomes a hypothetical construction.
- *suo cammin reciso* ‘his path cut off’ → “his pathway intercepted”: the image of cutting is replaced by one of obstruction.

> 64 Ma chi pensasse il ponderoso tema,  
> 65 e l' omero mortal che se ne carca,  
> 66 nol biasmerebbe, se sott' esso trema.  

But whoso thinketh of the weighty theme and of the mortal shoulder which hath charged itself therewith, will think no blame if under it it trembleth.

- *chi pensasse* ‘whoever should consider’ → “whoso thinketh of”: the imperfect subjunctive becomes a present indicative, and the English verb takes “of” rather than a direct object.
- *e l' omero mortal* ‘and the shoulder mortal’ → “and of the mortal shoulder”: “of” is repeated for the second complement, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *che se ne carca* ‘that loads itself with it’ → “which hath charged itself therewith”: the present becomes a present perfect.
- *nol biasmerebbe* ‘would not blame it’ → “will think no blame”: the conditional becomes a future; the object pronoun disappears, and the negative qualifies the noun “blame” rather than the verb.
- *se sott' esso trema* ‘if beneath it it trembles’ → “if under it it trembleth”: English distinguishes the object of the preposition from the subject of the verb by using “it” twice.

> 67 Non è pileggio da picciola barca  
> 68 quel che fendendo va l' ardita prora,  
> 69 nè da nocchier ch' a sè medesmo parca.  

It is no voyage for a little barque, that which my daring keel cleaveth as it goeth, nor for a helmsman who doth spare himself.

- *Non è pileggio* ‘is not a voyage’ → “It is no voyage”: anticipatory “It” stands for the postponed subject, and the negative moves to the noun phrase.
- *da picciola barca* ‘suited to a small boat’ → “for a little barque”: “for” expresses the fitness conveyed by *da*; “barque” is more specific than ‘boat’.
- *quel che fendendo va l' ardita prora* ‘that which the daring prow goes cleaving’ → “that which my daring keel cleaveth as it goeth”: the subject moves before the verb, the definite article becomes “my”, and keel replaces prow. Cleaving becomes the main action and going the subordinate one, reversing their Italian constructions.
- *nè da nocchier* ‘nor for helmsman’ → “nor for a helmsman”: English supplies the indefinite article.
- *ch' a sè medesmo parca* ‘who should be sparing to his very self’ → “who doth spare himself”: the subjunctive becomes indicative; the emphatic indirect object becomes a direct reflexive object after the verb.

> 70 “Perchè la faccia mia sì t' innamora,  
> 71 che tu non ti rivolgi al bel giardino  
> 72 che sotto i raggi di Cristo s' infiora?  

“Wherefore doth my face so enamour thee that thou turnest thee not to the fair garden which flowereth beneath the rays of Christ?

- *Perchè la faccia mia sì t' innamora* ‘why the face of mine so thee enamours’ → “Wherefore doth my face so enamour thee”: the question takes an auxiliary before its subject; the possessive precedes the noun, and the object follows the verb.
- *che tu non ti rivolgi* ‘that thou not thyself turn’ → “that thou turnest thee not”: both reflexive and negative follow the verb.
- *che sotto i raggi di Cristo s' infiora* ‘which beneath the rays of Christ bedecks itself with flowers’ → “which flowereth beneath the rays of Christ”: an intransitive verb replaces the reflexive one and moves before the prepositional phrase.

> 73 Quivi è la Rosa, in che il Verbo divino  
> 74 carne si fece; quivi son li Gigli,  
> 75 al cui odor si prese il buon cammino.”  

There is the Rose wherein the Word Divine made itself flesh; there are the Lilies at whose odour the good path was taken.”

- *in che* ‘in which’ → “wherein”: preposition and relative are combined in one word.
- *carne si fece* ‘flesh itself made’ → “made itself flesh”: the verb precedes the reflexive, and the predicative noun comes last.
- *al cui odor* ‘at the odour of which’ → “at whose odour”: a possessive relative precedes the noun.
- *si prese il buon cammino* ‘one took the good path’ → “the good path was taken”: English chooses the passive rendering of the *si* construction and places the path before the verb.

> 76 Così Beatrice; ed io, ch' a' suoi consigli  
> 77 tutto era pronto, ancora mi rendei  
> 78 alla battaglia dei debili cigli.  

So Beatrice: and I, who to her counsels was all eager, again surrendered me to the conflict of the feeble brows.

- *tutto era pronto* ‘wholly was ready’ → “was all eager”: the intensifier moves after the verb, and readiness is interpreted as eagerness.
- *mi rendei* ‘myself I surrendered’ → “surrendered me”: the reflexive follows the verb and takes the simple form “me”.
- *alla battaglia* ‘to the battle’ → “to the conflict”: “conflict” replaces the more concrete ‘battle’.
- *dei debili cigli* ‘of the weak eyelashes’ → “of the feeble brows”: “brows” gives a different anatomical sense to *cigli*.

> 79 Come a raggio di sol, che puro mei  
> 80 per fratta nube, già prato di fiori  
> 81 vider, coperti d' ombra, gli occhi miei;  

As under the sun’s ray, which issueth pure through a broken cloud, ere now mine eyes have seen a meadow full of flowers, when themselves covered by the shade;

- *a raggio di sol* ‘at a ray of sun’ → “under the sun’s ray”: the preposition becomes “under”, and the source of the ray becomes a preceding definite possessive.
- *che puro mei* ‘which pure may stream’ → “which issueth pure”: the subjunctive becomes indicative, and the predicate adjective follows the verb.
- *per fratta nube* ‘through broken cloud’ → “through a broken cloud”: English supplies the indefinite article.
- *già … vider … gli occhi miei* ‘already … saw … the eyes of mine’ → “ere now mine eyes have seen”: the simple past becomes a present perfect; the delayed subject moves before the verb, with possessive before noun.
- *prato di fiori* ‘meadow of flowers’ → “a meadow full of flowers”: the object moves after the verb; English supplies an article and makes abundance explicit with “full”.
- *coperti d' ombra* ‘covered with shade’ → “when themselves covered by the shade”: the participial phrase is made explicitly temporal; “themselves” links it back to the eyes, and shade becomes the definite agent of the covering.

## Lines 82–114 — The Crowning of Mary

> 82 vid' io così più turbe di splendori  
> 83 folgorati di su da raggi ardenti,  
> 84 senza veder principio dei fulgori.  

so beheld I many a throng of splendours, glowed on from above by ardent rays, beholding not the source whence came the glowings.

- *vid' io così* ‘beheld I thus’ → “so beheld I”: the adverb moves to the beginning.
- *più turbe* ‘several throngs’ → “many a throng”: a plural becomes a distributive singular.
- *folgorati* ‘flashed upon’ → “glowed on”: the passive construction is kept, but flashing becomes glowing.
- *raggi ardenti* ‘rays ardent’ → “ardent rays”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *senza veder* ‘without seeing’ → “beholding not”: a preposition with an infinitive becomes a negated participle.
- *principio dei fulgori* ‘source of the flashes’ → “the source whence came the glowings”: the source takes a definite article, the flashes become glowings, and the genitive becomes a relative clause with a verb of motion.

> 85 O benigna virtù che sì gl' imprenti,  

O benign power which dost so imprint them!

- *sì gl' imprenti* ‘so them thou imprintest’ → “dost so imprint them”: the verb is formed with an auxiliary, and the object pronoun moves after the main verb.

> 86 su t' esaltasti per largirmi loco  
> 87 agli occhi lì, che non eran possenti.  

thou hadst thyself uplifted to yield place there for mine eyes that lacked in power.

- *su t' esaltasti* ‘upward thou didst raise thyself’ → “thou hadst thyself uplifted”: the simple past becomes a pluperfect, the reflexive precedes the participle, and the direction is incorporated in the verb.
- *per largirmi loco* ‘in order to grant me room’ → “to yield place”: the infinitive alone marks purpose, granting becomes yielding, and the personal recipient is not separately expressed.
- *agli occhi lì* ‘to the eyes there’ → “there for mine eyes”: the adverb moves forward, and the eyes are marked as the beneficiary and explicitly as the speaker’s.
- *non eran possenti* ‘were not powerful’ → “lacked in power”: a negated copula and adjective become a verb of deficiency and a noun.

> 88 Il nome del bel fior, ch' io sempre invoco  
> 89 e mane e sera, tutto mi ristrinse  
> 90 l' animo ad avvisar lo maggior foco.  

The name of the beauteous flower which I ever invoke, morning and evening, drew all my mind together to look upon the greatest flame.

- *e mane e sera* ‘both morning and evening’ → “morning and evening”: the first member of the paired conjunction is not expressed.
- *tutto mi ristrinse l' animo* ‘wholly drew my mind tight’ → “drew all my mind together”: the verb is expanded into a verb and particle; “all” goes directly before “my mind”, and the personal dative becomes a possessive.
- *ad avvisar* ‘to gaze intently at’ → “to look upon”: the verb of directed attention becomes a verb with a preposition.
- *lo maggior foco* ‘the greatest fire’ → “the greatest flame”: the fire is rendered by its flame.

> 91 E come ambo le luci mi dipinse  
> 92 il quale e il quanto della viva stella,  
> 93 che lassù vince, come quaggiù vinse,  

And when on both mine eyes had been depicted the quality and greatness of the living star which conquereth up there, e’en as down here it conquered,

- *come* ‘as soon as’ → “when”: the temporal conjunction does not expressly mark immediacy.
- *ambo le luci mi dipinse* ‘painted both my lights’ → “on both mine eyes had been depicted”: the lights are named directly as eyes, the personal dative becomes a possessive, and the eyes, formerly the object of painting, become its surface. The active simple past becomes a passive pluperfect.
- *il quale e il quanto* ‘the what-sort and the how-much’ → “the quality and greatness”: the substantivized interrogatives become abstract nouns, with one article for both; measure is interpreted as greatness.
- *lassù vince* ‘up there conquers’ → “conquereth up there”: the place adverb moves after the verb.
- *come quaggiù vinse* ‘as down here she conquered’ → “e’en as down here it conquered”: the comparison is strengthened, and the grammatically feminine star is referred to by a neuter pronoun.

> 94 per entro il cielo scese una facella,  
> 95 formata in cerchio a guisa di corona,  
> 96 e cinsela, e girossi intorno ad ella.  

from within the heaven descended a torch circle-formed, in fashion of a crown, and girt her and wheeled round her.

- *facella* ‘little torch’ → “torch”: the diminutive is not separately rendered.
- *formata in cerchio* ‘formed in a circle’ → “circle-formed”: the prepositional phrase becomes the first element of a compound adjective.
- *a guisa di corona* ‘in the manner of a crown’ → “in fashion of a crown”: the comparison is recast with “fashion”, and the bare Italian noun for crown takes an article.
- *girossi intorno ad ella* ‘turned itself around her’ → “wheeled round her”: an intransitive verb expresses the circular motion without a reflexive pronoun.

> 97 Qualunque melodia più dolce suona  
> 98 quaggiù, e più a sè l' anima tira,  
> 99 parrebbe nube che squarciata tuona,  

Whatever melody soundeth sweetest here below, and most doth draw the soul unto itself, would seem a rent cloud thundering,

- *più dolce suona* ‘more sweetly sounds’ → “soundeth sweetest”: the modifier moves after the verb, and the comparative form is given superlative force.
- *più a sè l' anima tira* ‘more to itself the soul draws’ → “most doth draw the soul unto itself”: the degree word again becomes a superlative; the verb, formed with an auxiliary, precedes its object, and the directional phrase comes last.
- *nube che squarciata tuona* ‘a cloud that, rent, thunders’ → “a rent cloud thundering”: the past participle moves before the noun, and the finite relative clause becomes a present participle.

> 100 comparata al sonar di quella lira,  
> 101 onde si coronava il bel zaffiro,  
> 102 del quale il ciel più chiaro s' inzaffira.  

compared unto the sound of that lyre whereby was crowned the beauteous sapphire by which the brightest heaven is ensapphired.

- *al sonar* ‘to the sounding’ → “unto the sound”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun for the sound produced.
- *onde si coronava* ‘with which was being crowned’ → “whereby was crowned”: the Italian *si* construction becomes an English passive, without an explicit mark of the ongoing action of the imperfect.
- *del quale* ‘of which’ → “by which”: the relation is made instrumental.
- *il ciel più chiaro* ‘the heaven most bright’ → “the brightest heaven”: the degree phrase becomes an inflected superlative before the noun.
- *s' inzaffira* ‘sapphires itself’ → “is ensapphired”: the reflexive form becomes passive.

> 103 “Io sono amore angelico, che giro  
> 104 l' alta letizia che spira del ventre,  
> 105 che fu albergo del nostro disiro;  

“I am the angelic love who circle the lofty gladness that doth breathe from out the womb which was the hostelry of our desire;

- *amore angelico* ‘angelic love’ → “the angelic love”: the predicate noun takes a definite article, and the adjective moves before it.
- *spira del ventre* ‘breathes from the womb’ → “doth breathe from out the womb”: the verb is formed with an auxiliary, and “out” makes emergence explicit.
- *fu albergo* ‘was lodging’ → “was the hostelry”: the predicate noun becomes a definite noun phrase, with a word specifically denoting a place of lodging.

> 106 e girerommi, donna del ciel, mentre  
> 107 che seguirai tuo figlio, e farai dia  
> 108 più la spera suprema, perchè gli entre.”  

and I will circle, Lady of heaven, until thou followest thy son, and dost make yet more divine the supreme sphere in that thou enterest it.”

- *girerommi* ‘I shall turn myself’ → “I will circle”: an intransitive verb conveys the circular motion without a reflexive pronoun.
- *mentre che seguirai* ‘while thou wilt follow’ → “until thou followest”: the conjunction is read as marking a limit rather than a duration, and English uses a present for the Italian future in the temporal clause.
- *farai dia più* ‘thou wilt make divine more’ → “dost make yet more divine”: the second future likewise becomes a present, formed with an auxiliary; the degree word moves before the adjective and is reinforced by “yet”.
- *la spera suprema* ‘the sphere supreme’ → “the supreme sphere”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *perchè gli entre* ‘because thou enter therein’ → “in that thou enterest it”: the causal link is recast, the Italian subjunctive becomes indicative, and the locative pronoun becomes a direct object.

> 109 Così la circulata melodia  
> 110 si sigillava, e tutti gli altri lumi  
> 111 facean sonar lo nome di Maria.  

Thus the circling melody impressed itself, and all the other lights made sound the name of Mary.

- *circulata* ‘circled’ → “circling”: a past participle becomes an active present participle.
- *si sigillava* ‘was sealing itself’ → “impressed itself”: the rendering uses the impression made by a seal rather than the verb of sealing; the reflexive follows the verb, and the simple past does not mark the imperfect aspect.
- *facean sonar* ‘were causing to resound’ → “made sound”: the causative is kept, but the simple past does not distinguish the Italian imperfect.

> 112 Lo real manto di tutti i volumi  
> 113 del mondo, che più ferve e più s' avviva  
> 114 nell' alito di Dio e nei costumi,  

The royal mantle of all the swathings of the universe which most doth burn and most is quickened in the breath and in the ways of God,

- *volumi* ‘rolls’ → “swathings”: the rendering emphasizes what is wrapped around rather than the rolled forms.
- *del mondo* ‘of the world’ → “of the universe”: the noun is given its comprehensive sense.
- *più ferve* ‘more seethes’ → “most doth burn”: the comparative form is read as superlative, and seething becomes burning, expressed with an auxiliary.
- *più s' avviva* ‘more comes alive’ → “most is quickened”: the degree is again rendered as superlative, and a change of state in reflexive form becomes passive.
- *nell' alito di Dio e nei costumi* ‘in the breath of God and in the ways’ → “in the breath and in the ways of God”: the genitive moves to the end, making it apply to both nouns.

## Lines 115–139 — Mary’s Ascent and the Triumph of the Blessed

> 115 avea sopra di noi l' interna riva  
> 116 tanto distante, che la sua parvenza  
> 117 là dov' io era ancor non m' appariva.  

above us had its inner shore so distant that its appearance, there where I was, not yet appeared to me.

- *avea sopra di noi* ‘had above us’ → “above us had”: the phrase of place is moved before the verb.
- *l' interna riva* ‘the inner shore’ → “its inner shore”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *ancor non* ‘as yet not’ → “not yet”: the negation comes before the adverb.
- *m' appariva* ‘to me was appearing’ → “appeared to me”: the indirect object follows the verb, and the imperfect is rendered by the simple past.

> 118 Però non ebber gli occhi miei potenza  
> 119 di seguitar la coronata fiamma,  
> 120 che si levò appresso sua semenza.  

Therefore mine eyes had not power to follow the crowned flame as she ascended after her own offspring.

- *non ebber gli occhi miei potenza* ‘had not the eyes of mine power’ → “mine eyes had not power”: the subject moves before the verb, and the possessive before the noun.
- *di seguitar* ‘of following’ → “to follow”: the complement of “power” takes the English *to*-infinitive.
- *che si levò* ‘which rose’ → “as she ascended”: a relative clause becomes a temporal clause with a personal pronoun; a simple intransitive verb renders the Italian pronominal verb.
- *sua semenza* ‘her seed’ → “her own offspring”: “offspring” gives the sense of *semenza* rather than its image, and “own” adds emphasis.

> 121 E come fantolin, che in ver la mamma  
> 122 tende le braccia poi che il latte prese,  
> 123 per l' animo che in fin di fuor s' infiamma;  

And as the infant who toward his mother stretcheth up his arms when he hath had the milk, because his mind flameth forth even into outward gesture;

- *fantolin* ‘a little child’ → “the infant”: English uses a definite article for the generic example and conveys smallness by the choice of noun rather than a diminutive.
- *in ver la mamma* ‘toward the mamma’ → “toward his mother”: the possessive replaces the definite article, and “mother” is less familiar than *mamma*.
- *tende le braccia* ‘stretches the arms’ → “stretcheth up his arms”: “up” supplies direction, and a possessive replaces the article before the part of the body.
- *poi che il latte prese* ‘after he took the milk’ → “when he hath had the milk”: “when” replaces the explicitly successive ‘after’; the simple past becomes a present perfect, with “had” for ‘took’ and the object after the verb.
- *per l' animo che* ‘on account of the spirit that’ → “because his mind”: a preposition governing a noun with a relative clause becomes a causal conjunction governing a finite clause; the noun becomes its subject, with a possessive for the definite article.
- *in fin di fuor s' infiamma* ‘even to the outside takes fire’ → “flameth forth even into outward gesture”: the verb comes first and loses its pronominal form; “forth” gives it an outward direction, and “gesture” specifies a form of outward expression not named in the Italian.

> 124 ciascun di quei candori in su si stese  
> 125 con la sua fiamma, sì che l' alto affetto,  
> 126 ch' egli aveano a Maria, mi fu palese.  

so each one of these glowings up-stretched with its flame, so that the deep love which they had for Mary was made plain to me.

- *ciascun* ‘each one’ → “so each one”: “so” is supplied to answer the earlier *come*.
- *di quei candori* ‘of those whitenesses’ → “of these glowings”: the demonstrative changes from distant to near, and a word for bright whiteness becomes one for the giving off of light.
- *in su si stese* ‘upward stretched itself’ → “up-stretched”: the direction is incorporated into the verb, with no separate reflexive.
- *l' alto affetto* ‘the high affection’ → “the deep love”: “deep” reverses the spatial image while retaining the sense of intensity; “love” renders *affetto*.
- *ch' egli aveano a Maria* ‘that they had toward Mary’ → “which they had for Mary”: “for” replaces the directional preposition.
- *mi fu palese* ‘to me was manifest’ → “was made plain to me”: English adds the idea of becoming plain through something done, and puts the indirect object last.

> 127 Indi rimaser lì nel mio cospetto,  
> 128 *Regina coeli* cantando sì dolce,  
> 129 che mai da me non si partì il diletto.  

Then they stayed there within my sight, singing *O Queen of heaven* so sweetly that ne’er hath parted from me the delight.

- *nel mio cospetto* ‘in my presence’ → “within my sight”: the phrase is rendered in terms of the bounds of vision.
- *Regina coeli cantando* ‘Queen of heaven singing’ → “singing *O Queen of heaven*”: the participle precedes its object; “O” makes the Latin vocative explicit.
- *sì dolce* ‘so sweet’ → “so sweetly”: the adjective used adverbially becomes an English adverb.
- *mai da me non si partì* ‘never from me did it depart’ → “ne’er hath parted from me”: the Italian negative concord is expressed by one negative; the simple past of the pronominal verb becomes a present perfect, and the phrase of separation follows the verb.

> 130 Oh quanta è l' ubertà che si soffolce  
> 131 in quell' arche ricchissime, che foro  
> 132 a seminar quaggiù buone bobolce!  

O how great the wealth crammed in those most rich chests, which here on earth were goodly acres for the seeding!

- *quanta è l' ubertà* ‘how great is the abundance’ → “how great the wealth”: the copula is omitted, and “wealth” gives a more specifically material turn to *ubertà*.
- *che si soffolce* ‘that is closely packed’ → “crammed”: a participle attached directly to “wealth” replaces the finite relative clause.
- *arche ricchissime* ‘chests most rich’ → “most rich chests”: the superlative precedes its noun.
- *che foro a seminar quaggiù buone bobolce* ‘which were, for sowing here below, good ploughlands’ → “which here on earth were goodly acres for the seeding”: the place phrase moves before the verb and identifies ‘below’ as “on earth”; the purpose phrase moves to the end, with a verbal noun and article for the infinitive. “Goodly acres” takes *bobolce* as measures of tillable land rather than as those who till it.

> 133 Quivi si vive e gode del tesoro  
> 134 che s' acquistò piangendo nell' esilio  
> 135 di Babilon, dove si lasciò l' oro.  

Here they have life and joy even in that treasure which was earned in weeping in the exile of Babylon, where gold was scorned.

- *Quivi* ‘there’ → “Here”: the point of reference changes from distant to near.
- *si vive e gode* ‘one lives and rejoices’ → “they have life and joy”: the impersonal construction acquires a definite plural subject, and two verbs become nouns governed by “have”.
- *del tesoro* ‘of the treasure’ → “even in that treasure”: “in” connects the treasure with both “life and joy”; “even” adds emphasis, and a demonstrative replaces the article.
- *s' acquistò* ‘was acquired’ → “was earned”: “earned” specifies how the treasure came to be possessed.
- *piangendo* ‘weeping’ → “in weeping”: a preposition is supplied before the verbal noun.
- *si lasciò l' oro* ‘one left the gold’ → “gold was scorned”: English makes the object the subject of a passive, drops the article, and interprets leaving gold behind as scorning it.

> 136 Quivi trionfa, sotto l' alto Filio  
> 137 di Dio e di Maria, di sua vittoria,  
> 138 e con l' antico e col nuovo concilio  
> 139 colui che tien le chiavi di tal gloria.  

Here triumphs under the lofty Son of God and Mary, in his victory, together with the ancient and new council, he who doth hold the keys of so great glory.

- *Quivi* ‘there’ → “Here”: the same change of point of reference recurs.
- *di Dio e di Maria* ‘of God and of Mary’ → “of God and Mary”: the preposition is not repeated.
- *di sua vittoria* ‘of his victory’ → “in his victory”: English uses “in” for the complement of “triumphs”.
- *e con l' antico e col nuovo concilio* ‘and with the ancient and with the new council’ → “together with the ancient and new council”: “together with” absorbs the initial conjunction, and the preposition and article are not repeated before “new”.
- *tien* ‘holds’ → “doth hold”: the simple verb is expanded with auxiliary “doth”.
- *di tal gloria* ‘of such glory’ → “of so great glory”: “so great” interprets *tal* as indicating degree.

## Summary — Italian Order and English Idiom

Wicksteed often keeps Dante’s sequence of clauses while changing the order within them. Objects move after their verbs (*il sole aspetta* becomes “awaiteth the sun”), and possessives and adjectives often move before their nouns. Yet he retains the inversion in “so beheld I” and the divided placement of the adjectives in “sweet guide and dear”. Two routine changes generally go unmarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where an Italian verb ending suffices, and omits the article before a possessive, as in *la donna mia*, “my Lady”.

Changes of construction can shift the emphasis even where the sense stays close. The gerund in *veggendola io* becomes a finite temporal clause, “as I looked on her”, while the finite subjunctive in *onde li pasca* gives way to an infinitive, “wherewith to feed them”. Wicksteed treats pronominal verbs according to their sense rather than reproducing each pronoun: *s’ infiora* becomes the intransitive “flowereth”, but *s’ inzaffira* becomes the passive “is ensapphired”. The latter change preserves the distinctive echo of *zaffiro* in the verb formed from it.

Wicksteed sometimes makes an implication explicit and sometimes leaves an emphasis unexpressed. In *altro vorria*, ‘would want something else’, he spells out the contrast by adding “than he hath”; in the bird’s watch for dawn, *pur*, ‘only’, has no separate equivalent. A choice of words can also preserve the force of a phrase while changing its image: *l’ alto affetto*, ‘the high affection’, becomes “the deep love”, reversing the spatial figure but keeping the intensity. Such departures stand beside notably close renderings: “foreruns the time” keeps both the verb and the object of *previene il tempo*, despite the unusual English phrasing.
