# Paradiso 5

## Lines 1–18 — Beatrice’s Radiance and the Question of Vows

> 1 “S' io ti fiammeggio nel caldo d' amore  
> 2 di là dal modo che in terra si vede  
> 3 sì che degli occhi tuoi vinco il valore,  

“If I flame on thee in the warmth of love, beyond the measure witnessed upon earth, and so vanquish the power of thine eyes,

- *ti fiammeggio* ‘on thee I flame’ → “flame on thee”: the Italian pronoun precedes the verb; the English prepositional phrase follows it.
- *di là dal modo* ‘beyond the manner’ → “beyond the measure”: Wicksteed takes *modo* as a degree of intensity.
- *che in terra si vede* ‘that on earth one sees’ → “witnessed upon earth”: the finite relative clause with *si* becomes a passive participial phrase, and the place phrase moves after the participle.
- *sì che* ‘so that’ → “and so”: the subordinate clause of result becomes a coordinated predicate.
- *degli occhi tuoi vinco il valore* ‘of the eyes of thine I overcome the power’ → “vanquish the power of thine eyes”: the possessor phrase moves from before the verb to after “the power”, and the possessive moves before “eyes”.

> 4 non ti maravigliar; chè ciò procede  
> 5 da perfetto veder, che come apprende,  
> 6 così nel bene appreso move il piede.  

marvel not; for this proceedeth from perfect vision, which, as it apprehendeth, so doth advance its foot in the apprehended good.

- *non ti maravigliar* ‘do not marvel’ → “marvel not”: the negative follows the verb, and the reflexive pronoun has no English counterpart.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *perfetto veder* ‘perfect seeing’ → “perfect vision”: a noun replaces the substantivized infinitive.
- *nel bene appreso move il piede* ‘in the good apprehended moves the foot’ → “doth advance its foot in the apprehended good”: the verb and its object move before the prepositional phrase; “doth advance” gives the simple verb a periphrastic form and a forward direction. The possessive replaces the definite article, and the participle moves before “good”.

> 7 Io veggio ben sì come già risplende  
> 8 nello intelletto tuo l' eterna luce,  
> 9 che, vista sola, sempre amore accende;  

Well do I note how in thine intellect already doth reglow the eternal light, which only seen doth ever kindle love;

- *Io veggio ben* ‘I see well’ → “Well do I note”: the adverb moves first, with auxiliary “do” before the subject; “note” replaces ‘see’.
- *sì come* ‘just how’ → “how”: the reinforcing *sì* has no separate equivalent.
- *già risplende nello intelletto tuo* ‘already shines in the intellect of thine’ → “in thine intellect already doth reglow”: the place phrase moves before the adverb and verb, and the possessive precedes the noun. The simple present becomes periphrastic, and “reglow” gives the verb a sense of answering radiance.
- *vista sola* ‘seen but alone’ → “only seen”: “only” precedes the participle and qualifies the seeing rather than standing as an adjective agreeing with the light.
- *sempre amore accende* ‘always love kindles’ → “doth ever kindle love”: the simple verb takes an auxiliary, and its object moves after the main verb.

> 10 e s' altra cosa vostro amor seduce,  
> 11 non è se non di quella alcun vestigio  
> 12 mal conosciuto, che quivi traluce.  

and if aught else seduce your love, naught is it save some vestige of this light, ill understood, that shineth through therein.

- *altra cosa* ‘another thing’ → “aught else”: an indefinite pronoun with a following modifier replaces the adjective and noun.
- *vostro amor seduce* ‘your love seduces’ → “seduce your love”: the object moves after the verb, and the English conditional clause uses a subjunctive where the Italian has an indicative.
- *non è se non* ‘it is not except’ → “naught is it save”: the first negative becomes a fronted pronoun, with the subject after the verb.
- *di quella alcun vestigio* ‘of that some vestige’ → “some vestige of this light”: the dependent phrase follows “vestige”; the demonstrative changes from distal to proximal, and its noun is made explicit.
- *mal conosciuto* ‘badly known’ → “ill understood”: understanding replaces knowing.
- *quivi traluce* ‘there shines through’ → “shineth through therein”: the place adverb follows the verb, and “therein” makes its inward reference explicit.

> 13 Tu vuoi saper, se con altro servigio,  
> 14 per manco voto, si può render tanto,  
> 15 che l' anima sicuri di litigio.”  

Thou wouldst know whether with other service reckoning may be paid for broken vow, so great as to secure the soul from process.”

- *vuoi saper* ‘wish to know’ → “wouldst know”: the present verb of wishing and its infinitive become a volitional auxiliary with a bare infinitive.
- *se* ‘if’ → “whether”: English marks the indirect question explicitly.
- *per manco voto* ‘for a deficient vow’ → “for broken vow”: “broken” interprets the deficiency as nonfulfilment, and the phrase moves after the verb of payment.
- *si può render tanto* ‘one can render so much’ → “reckoning may be paid”, “so great”: the impersonal construction becomes a passive with a supplied subject, “reckoning”; the idea of rendering is narrowed to payment, and the quantity expressed by *tanto* becomes a degree modifying the reckoning.
- *che l' anima sicuri* ‘that it may secure the soul’ → “as to secure the soul”: the finite subjunctive clause of result becomes an infinitive, with the object after the verb.
- *di litigio* ‘from litigation’ → “from process”: Wicksteed uses the legal sense of “process” for the threatened suit.

> 16 Sì cominciò Beatrice questo canto;  

So Beatrice began this chant,

- *cominciò Beatrice* ‘began Beatrice’ → “Beatrice began”: the subject moves before the verb.
- *questo canto* ‘this song’ → “this chant”: “chant” is the more specific English term.

> 17 e sì com' uom che suo parlar non spezza,  
> 18 continuò così il processo santo:  

and, as one who interrupteth not his speech, continued thus the sacred progress:

- *sì com' uom* ‘just as a man’ → “as one”: the reinforcing *sì* is not separately rendered, and the generic noun becomes an indefinite pronoun.
- *suo parlar non spezza* ‘his speaking does not break’ → “interrupteth not his speech”: the object moves after the verb, the negative follows it, and a noun replaces the substantivized infinitive; “interrupteth” renders the figurative breaking.
- *il processo santo* ‘the course holy’ → “the sacred progress”: the adjective moves before the noun; “progress” renders the onward course of the speaking.

## Lines 19–51 — The Vow and the Freedom of the Will

> 19 “Lo maggior don, che Dio per sua larghezza  
> 20 fesse creando, ed alla sua bontate  
> 21 più conformato, e quel ch' ei più apprezza,  

“The greatest gift God of his largess made at the creation, and the most conformed to his own excellence, and which he most prizeth,

- *che* ‘that’ → “gift God”: the relative pronoun, object of “made”, is left out between its antecedent and the subject.
- *per sua larghezza* ‘through his largess’ → “of his largess”: the preposition of cause becomes one of source.
- *fesse creando* ‘made in creating’ → “made at the creation”: the gerund becomes a noun with an article, and the act of creating becomes a time reference.
- *alla sua bontate più conformato* ‘to his goodness most conformed’ → “the most conformed to his own excellence”: the participle precedes its complement, the superlative takes an article, “own” adds emphasis, and “excellence” is broader than ‘goodness’.
- *quel ch’ ei più apprezza* ‘that which he most prizes’ → “which he most prizeth”: the demonstrative antecedent is dropped; the relative clause refers directly back to “gift”.

> 22 fu della volontà la libertate,  

was the will’s liberty,

- *della volontà la libertate* ‘of the will the liberty’ → “the will’s liberty”: the prepositional genitive becomes a possessive, which also serves in place of the article before ‘liberty’.

> 23 di che le creature intelligenti,  
> 24 e tutte e sole furo e son dotate.  

wherewith creatures intelligent, both all and only, were and are endowed.

- *di che* ‘with which’ → “wherewith”: preposition and relative are combined in a relative adverb.
- *e tutte e sole* ‘and all and only’ → “both all and only”: the repeated conjunction becomes the English correlative “both” and “and”.

> 25 Or ti parrà, se tu quinci argomenti,  
> 26 l' alto valor del voto, s' è sì fatto,  
> 27 che Dio consenta quando tu consenti;  

Now will appear to thee (if thence thou draw due inference) the high worth of the vow, if so made that God consent when thou consentest;

- *ti parrà* ‘to thee will appear’ → “will appear to thee”: the indirect object follows the verb.
- *se tu quinci argomenti* ‘if thou from here infer’ → “if thence thou draw due inference”: the adverb moves before the subject and points back with “thence”; the single verb becomes a verb with a noun as object, and “due” is supplied.
- *s’ è sì fatto* ‘if it is so made’ → “if so made”: the conditional clause is reduced to a participial construction.

> 28 chè, nel fermar tra Dio e l' uomo il patto,  
> 29 vittima fassi di questo tesoro,  
> 30 tal qual io dico, e fassi col suo atto.  

for in establishing the compact between God and man, the victim is made from out this treasure, such as I pronounce it, and made by its own act.

- *nel fermar tra Dio e l’ uomo il patto* ‘in making firm between God and man the pact’ → “in establishing the compact between God and man”: “establishing” renders the sense of making firm; the object moves immediately after it, before the phrase naming the parties.
- *vittima fassi di questo tesoro* ‘a victim is made of this treasure’ → “the victim is made from out this treasure”: the predicative noun becomes a definite subject, the reflexive form is rendered as a passive, and “from out” stresses the source.
- *tal qual io dico* ‘such as I say’ → “such as I pronounce it”: the verb becomes one of express declaration and takes an explicit object.
- *e fassi col suo atto* ‘and is made with its act’ → “and made by its own act”: the second participle shares the earlier auxiliary; “by” marks agency and “own” emphasizes the possessive.

> 31 Dunque che render puossi per ristoro?  

What may be rendered, then, as restoration?

- *Dunque che render puossi* ‘then what render can one’ → “What may be rendered, then”: the connective moves from the opening to a position after the verb phrase, the infinitive no longer precedes the modal, and the impersonal construction becomes a passive.
- *per ristoro* ‘for recompense’ → “as restoration”: the phrase says in what capacity the thing is rendered rather than for what end.

> 32 Se credi bene usar quel ch' hai offerto,  
> 33 di mal tolletto vuoi far buon lavoro.  

If thou think to make good use of that which thou hadst consecrated, thou wouldst do good works from evil gains.

- *bene usar* ‘well to use’ → “to make good use of”: the verb becomes a noun governed by “make”, the adverb becomes an adjective, and the object is introduced by “of”.
- *hai offerto* ‘hast offered’ → “hadst consecrated”: the perfect becomes a pluperfect, and “consecrated” specifies the kind of offering.
- *di mal tolletto* ‘of what was wrongly taken’ → “from evil gains”: the adverb and participle become an adjective and a plural noun, and the source phrase moves to the end.
- *vuoi far buon lavoro* ‘thou wishest to do good work’ → “thou wouldst do good works”: volition is rendered by a modal that also reads as conditional, and the singular becomes plural.

> 34 Tu se' omai del maggior punto certo;  

Thou art now assured as to the greater point;

- *omai* ‘by now’ → “now”: the temporal adverb loses the explicit sense of ‘by this time’.
- *del maggior punto certo* ‘of the greater point certain’ → “assured as to the greater point”: the predicate moves before its complement; “assured” replaces ‘certain’ and takes “as to” rather than ‘of’.

> 35 ma, perchè santa Chiesa in ciò dispensa,  
> 36 che par contra lo ver ch' io t' ho scoperto,  

but since holy Church granteth herein dispensations, which seemeth counter to the truth I have unfolded to thee,

- *in ciò dispensa* ‘in this dispenses’ → “granteth herein dispensations”: the verb is expanded into a verb and a plural object; the prepositional phrase becomes an adverb placed between them.
- *par contra lo ver* ‘seems against the true’ → “seemeth counter to the truth”: “counter to” renders the preposition, and the substantivized adjective becomes a noun.
- *ch’ io t’ ho scoperto* ‘that I to thee have uncovered’ → “I have unfolded to thee”: the object relative is omitted, the indirect object moves after the verb, and the image of uncovering becomes one of unfolding.

> 37 convienti ancor sedere un poco a mensa,  
> 38 però che il cibo rigido ch' hai preso  
> 39 richiede ancora aiuto a tua dispensa.  

it behoves thee still to sit a while at table, because the stubborn food which thou hast taken demandeth further aid for thy digestion.

- *convienti ancor sedere* ‘it behoves thee yet to sit’ → “it behoves thee still to sit”: “still” gives the adverb a continuative sense.
- *un poco* ‘a little’ → “a while”: the expression of amount is made explicitly temporal.
- *però che* ‘for the reason that’ → “because”: the causal expression becomes a single conjunction.
- *il cibo rigido* ‘the food hard’ → “the stubborn food”: the adjective moves before the noun and describes resistance rather than physical hardness.
- *richiede ancora aiuto* ‘requires yet help’ → “demandeth further aid”: “demandeth” strengthens the verb, and the adverb becomes an adjective modifying “aid”.
- *a tua dispensa* ‘for thy processing of it’ → “for thy digestion”: the noun is given its particular sense in the figure of food.

> 40 Apri la mente a quel ch' io ti paleso,  
> 41 e fermalvi entro; chè non fa scienza,  
> 42 senza lo ritenere, avere inteso.  

Open thy mind to that which I unfold to thee, and fix it there within; for to have understood without retaining maketh not knowledge.

- *la mente* ‘the mind’ → “thy mind”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *ti paleso* ‘to thee I make plain’ → “I unfold to thee”: the verb of making manifest is rendered figuratively, and the indirect object follows it.
- *fermalvi entro* ‘fix it therein within’ → “fix it there within”: the attached object and locative pronouns become separate words, with “there” and “within” together expressing the location.
- *non fa scienza, senza lo ritenere, avere inteso* ‘does not make knowledge, without retaining it, to have understood’ → “to have understood without retaining maketh not knowledge”: the infinitival subject moves from last to first, the qualification follows it, and the explicit object of ‘retaining’ is dropped.

> 43 Due cose si convengono all' essenza  
> 44 di questo sacrificio: l' una è quella  
> 45 di che si fa, l' altra è la convenenza.  

Two things pertain to the essence of this sacrifice: first, that whereof it is composed, and then the compact’s self.

- *si convengono all’ essenza* ‘belong to the essence’ → “pertain to the essence”: the pronominal verb is rendered by a simple intransitive verb.
- *l’ una è quella* ‘the one is that’ → “first, that”: the numbered subject becomes an ordering adverb, and the copula is omitted.
- *di che si fa* ‘of which it is made’ → “whereof it is composed”: preposition and relative are combined, and “composed” specifies the sense of the passive.
- *l’ altra è la convenenza* ‘the other is the compact’ → “and then the compact’s self”: the second subject and copula give way to a conjunction and an ordering adverb; “self” supplies emphasis.

> 46 Quest' ultima giammai non si cancella,  
> 47 se non servata, ed intorno di lei  
> 48 sì preciso di sopra si favella;  

This last can ne’er be cancelled save by being kept; and concerning this it is that the discourse above is so precise;

- *giammai non si cancella* ‘never is cancelled’ → “can ne’er be cancelled”: the emphatic Italian negative becomes a single negative adverb, and the translation adds a modal of possibility.
- *se non servata* ‘unless kept’ → “save by being kept”: the elliptical condition becomes an exception expressed as a means.
- *intorno di lei* ‘concerning it’ → “concerning this it is that”: the feminine pronoun referring back becomes a demonstrative, and a cleft construction gives the phrase emphasis.
- *sì preciso di sopra si favella* ‘so precisely above one speaks’ → “the discourse above is so precise”: the impersonal verb becomes a noun subject, “above” modifies that noun, and the adverbial description becomes a predicate adjective.

> 49 però necessità fu agli Ebrei  
> 50 pur l' offerere, ancor che alcuna offerta  
> 51 si permutasse, come saper dei.  

therefore it was imperative upon the Hebrews to offer sacrifice in any case, though the thing offered might sometimes be changed, as thou shouldst know.

- *necessità fu agli Ebrei* ‘a necessity it was for the Hebrews’ → “it was imperative upon the Hebrews”: a noun becomes a predicate adjective, with “upon” marking those under the obligation.
- *pur l’ offerere* ‘in any case the offering’ → “to offer sacrifice in any case”: the articulated infinitive becomes a verbal infinitive with “sacrifice” supplied as its object, and the qualification moves after it.
- *alcuna offerta si permutasse* ‘some offering were exchanged’ → “the thing offered might sometimes be changed”: the noun becomes a noun and participle, the indefinite determiner is represented by an adverb of frequency, and the subjunctive is expressed by “might”; “changed” is less specific than ‘exchanged’.
- *come saper dei* ‘as know thou oughtest’ → “as thou shouldst know”: the finite modal precedes the infinitive rather than following it.

## Lines 52–84 — Vows, Exchanges, and Rash Promises

> 52 L' altra, che per materia t' è aperta,  
> 53 puote bene esser tal, che non si falla,  
> 54 se con altra materia si converta.  

The other thing, which hath been unfolded to thee as the matter, may in sooth be such that there is no offence if it be interchanged with other matter.

- *L' altra* ‘the other’ → “The other thing”: the noun is supplied.
- *che per materia t' è aperta* ‘which as matter to thee is laid open’ → “which hath been unfolded to thee as the matter”: a present expression of state becomes a perfect passive; the recipient follows the verb, and the phrase identifying the matter comes last, with a definite article.
- *puote bene esser tal* ‘may well be such’ → “may in sooth be such”: the adverb qualifying the possibility becomes an affirmation of its truth.
- *che non si falla* ‘that one does no wrong’ → “that there is no offence”: the impersonal verb becomes an existential construction with a noun.
- *se con altra materia si converta* ‘if with other matter it be exchanged’ → “if it be interchanged with other matter”: the phrase naming the other party to the exchange moves after the passive verb.

> 55 Ma non trasmuti carco alla sua spalla  
> 56 per suo arbitrio alcun, senza la volta  
> 57 e della chiave bianca e della gialla;  

But let none shift the load upon his shoulder at his own judgment, without the turn both of the white and of the yellow key;

- *non trasmuti carco alla sua spalla per suo arbitrio alcun* ‘let not anyone shift the load upon his shoulder by his own decision’ → “let none shift the load upon his shoulder at his own judgment”: the negative is absorbed into the subject, which in Italian comes last; the phrase of discretion becomes one of judgment.
- *e della chiave bianca e della gialla* ‘both of the key white and of the yellow one’ → “both of the white and of the yellow key”: the noun, expressed with the first adjective and understood with the second in Italian, is deferred to the second in English.

> 58 ed ogni permutanza creda stolta,  
> 59 se la cosa dimessa in la sorpresa,  
> 60 come il quattro nel sei, non è raccolta.  

and let him hold all changing to be folly, unless the thing remitted be contained in that assumed in four to six proportion.

- *ogni permutanza creda stolta* ‘every exchange let him deem foolish’ → “let him hold all changing to be folly”: the verb precedes its object; a countable exchange becomes the activity of changing, and the predicative adjective becomes a noun with an explicit infinitive.
- *se ... non* ‘if ... not’ → “unless”: the conjunction incorporates the negation.
- *la cosa dimessa* ‘the thing laid aside’ → “the thing remitted”: the participle describes the thing as released rather than put aside.
- *in la sorpresa* ‘in the one taken on’ → “in that assumed”: a demonstrative replaces the article, and the phrase follows rather than precedes the main verb.
- *come il quattro nel sei* ‘as four in six’ → “in four to six proportion”: a comparison of containment becomes an explicit ratio and moves to the end.
- *è raccolta* ‘is gathered’ → “be contained”: the image of gathering becomes containment, and the indicative becomes subjunctive after “unless”.

> 61 Però qualunque cosa tanto pesa  
> 62 per suo valor, che tragga ogni bilancia,  
> 63 satisfar non si può con altra spesa.  

Wherefore what thing soe’er weigheth so heavy in virtue of its worth as to turn every scale, can never be made good by any other outlay.

- *qualunque cosa* ‘whatever thing’ → “what thing soe’er”: the indefinite force of the single modifier is distributed on either side of the noun.
- *tanto pesa* ‘so much weighs’ → “weigheth so heavy”: the expression of degree follows the verb and is put in terms of heaviness.
- *per suo valor* ‘by its worth’ → “in virtue of its worth”: the simple preposition is expanded.
- *che tragga ogni bilancia* ‘that it draw every balance’ → “as to turn every scale”: the finite subjunctive of result becomes an infinitive, and drawing a balance is rendered by the English idiom of turning a scale.
- *satisfar non si può* ‘satisfaction cannot be made’ → “can never be made good”: the thing itself becomes the subject of a passive idiom, and simple negation is strengthened to “never”.
- *con altra spesa* ‘with other expenditure’ → “by any other outlay”: the means is introduced by “by”, and “any” is supplied under the negative.

> 64 Non prendan li mortali il voto a ciancia:  

Let mortals never take the vow in sport;

- *Non prendan li mortali* ‘let not mortals take’ → “Let mortals never take”: the subject moves before the main verb, and the prohibition gains the temporal emphasis of “never”.
- *a ciancia* ‘as a jest’ → “in sport”: a phrase describing what one takes the vow for becomes one describing how one takes it.

> 65 siate fedeli, ed a ciò far non bieci;  
> 66 come Jeptè alla sua prima mancia,  

be loyal, and in doing this not squint-eyed; like as was Jephthah in his firstling vow;

- *siate fedeli* ‘be faithful’ → “be loyal”: fidelity is expressed as loyalty.
- *a ciò far* ‘in this to do’ → “in doing this”: the infinitive becomes a gerund, followed by its object.
- *come Jeptè* ‘like Jephthah’ → “like as was Jephthah”: the comparison is expanded into a clause with an explicit verb.
- *alla sua prima mancia* ‘at his first gift’ → “in his firstling vow”: the gift is interpreted as the vow, and the ordinal becomes the attributive “firstling”.

> 67 cui più si convenia dicer: *Mal feci*,  
> 68 che, servando, far peggio; e così stolto  

whom it had more become to say: *I did amiss*, than keep it and do worse;

- *cui più si convenia* ‘for whom it was more fitting’ → “whom it had more become”: the person for whom something was fitting becomes the direct object of “become” in the sense of ‘befit’; the imperfect is recast as a counterfactual past.
- *Mal feci* ‘ill I did’ → “I did amiss”: the adverb moves from before the verb to after it.
- *che, servando, far peggio* ‘than, by keeping, to do worse’ → “than keep it and do worse”: the gerund expressing how the worse deed is done becomes the first of two coordinated verbs, with an explicit object.

> 69 ritrovar puoi lo gran duca dei Greci,  

and in like folly mayst thou track the great chief of the Greeks,

- *e così stolto ritrovar puoi* ‘and just as foolish find thou canst’ → “and in like folly mayst thou track”: the adjective predicated of the chief becomes a phrase with an abstract noun; the modal precedes the infinitive, and finding becomes tracking.
- *lo gran duca* ‘the great leader’ → “the great chief”: the title of one who leads is rendered by a term of rank.

> 70 onde pianse Ifigenia il suo bel volto,  

wherefore Iphigenia wept that her face was fair,

- *pianse Ifigenia il suo bel volto* ‘wept Iphigenia for her fair face’ → “Iphigenia wept that her face was fair”: the subject precedes the verb, and the noun phrase giving the object of lament becomes a clause in which fairness is predicated of the face.

> 71 e fe' pianger di sè li folli e i savi,  
> 72 ch'udir parlar di così fatto colto.  

and made simple and sage to weep for her, hearing of such a rite.

- *fe' pianger di sè li folli e i savi* ‘made weep for herself the foolish and the wise’ → “made simple and sage to weep for her”: those made to weep move before the infinitive; the two substantivized adjectives lose their articles and are rendered by an alliterating pair. The reflexive referring to the causer becomes an ordinary pronoun.
- *ch'udir parlar di* ‘who heard tell of’ → “hearing of”: the finite past relative clause becomes a participial phrase, and the separate verb of telling disappears.
- *così fatto colto* ‘worship of such a kind’ → “such a rite”: the description of kind is compressed into a determiner, and worship is rendered as a particular observance.

> 73 Siate, Cristiani, a movervi più gravi,  

Ye Christians, be more sedate in moving,

- *Siate, Cristiani* ‘be, Christians’ → “Ye Christians, be”: the address moves before the imperative and gains an explicit “Ye”.
- *a movervi più gravi* ‘in moving yourselves more weighty’ → “more sedate in moving”: the comparative moves forward, figurative weight becomes sedateness, and the reflexive infinitive becomes an intransitive gerund.

> 74 non siate come penna ad ogni vento,  

not like a feather unto every wind;

- *non siate* ‘be not’ → “not”: the repeated imperative is left understood.
- *come penna* ‘like feather’ → “like a feather”: English supplies the indefinite article.

> 75 e non crediate ch' ogni acqua vi lavi.  

nor think that every water cleanseth you.

- *e non crediate* ‘and do not believe’ → “nor think”: conjunction and negation are combined, and belief becomes thinking.
- *vi lavi* ‘you wash’ → “cleanseth you”: the object moves after the verb, washing becomes cleansing, and the Italian subjunctive is rendered by an English indicative.

> 76 Avete il vecchio e il nuovo testamento,  
> 77 e il pastor della Chiesa che vi guida:  

Ye have the Old and the New Testament and the shepherd of the Church to guide you;

- *che vi guida* ‘who guides you’ → “to guide you”: the relative clause stating what the shepherd does becomes an infinitive expressing his function, with the object after the verb.

> 78 questo vi basti a vostro salvamento.  

let this suffice you, unto your salvation.

- *questo vi basti* ‘this to you may suffice’ → “let this suffice you”: “let” conveys the force of the exhortative subjunctive, and the personal complement follows the verb.

> 79 Se mala cupidigia altro vi grida,  
> 80 uomini siate, e non pecore matte,  
> 81 sì che il Giudeo di voi tra voi non rida.  

If sorry greed proclaim aught else to you, be men, not senseless sheep, lest the Jew in your midst should scoff at you.

- *mala cupidigia* ‘evil desire’ → “sorry greed”: moral badness becomes a term of disparagement, and desire is narrowed to greed.
- *altro vi grida* ‘something else to you cries’ → “proclaim aught else to you”: the verb moves before both complements; crying out becomes proclaiming, and the Italian indicative is rendered by the English conditional subjunctive.
- *uomini siate* ‘men be’ → “be men”: the predicate follows the imperative.
- *e non pecore matte* ‘and not sheep mad’ → “not senseless sheep”: the conjunction drops out in the contrast, and the adjective precedes its noun and expresses lack of sense.
- *sì che ... non* ‘so that ... not’ → “lest”: the negative purpose is compressed into one conjunction.
- *di voi* ‘of you’ → “at you”: the target of ridicule follows the verb and takes the English preposition.
- *tra voi* ‘among you’ → “in your midst”: the pronoun becomes a possessive in a phrase with a noun.
- *rida* ‘may laugh’ → “should scoff”: a modal renders the subjunctive, and laughter is made explicitly derisive.

> 82 Non fate come agnel che lascia il latte  
> 83 della sua madre, e semplice e lascivo  
> 84 seco medesmo a suo piacer combatte.”  

Do not ye as the lamb who leaves his mother’s milk, silly and wanton, fighting with himself for his disport.”

- *come agnel* ‘like a lamb’ → “as the lamb”: the article-less example is introduced with an English generic definite article.
- *il latte della sua madre* ‘the milk of his mother’ → “his mother’s milk”: the following prepositional complement becomes a preceding possessive.
- *e semplice e lascivo* ‘and simple and wanton’ → “silly and wanton”: the initial conjunction linking the two actions disappears, and simplicity is interpreted as foolishness.
- *seco medesmo a suo piacer combatte* ‘with himself for his pleasure he fights’ → “fighting with himself for his disport”: the second finite verb becomes a participle and moves before its complements; pleasure is rendered as recreation.

## Lines 85–117 — Beatrice and the Splendours of the Second Heaven

> 85 Così Beatrice a me, com' io scrivo;  

Thus Beatrice to me, as I write;

- Literal.

> 86 poi si rivolse tutta disiante  
> 87 a quella parte ove il mondo è più vivo.  

then turned her all in longing to that part where the world quickeneth most.

- *si rivolse* ‘herself she turned’ → “turned her”: the reflexive follows the verb and takes the form of an object pronoun.
- *tutta disiante* ‘all desiring’ → “all in longing”: a prepositional phrase replaces the participle.
- *è più vivo* ‘is most alive’ → “quickeneth most”: a verb replaces the copula and adjective, and the degree word follows it.

> 88 Lo suo tacere e il trasmutar sembiante  
> 89 poser silenzio al mio cupido ingegno,  
> 90 che già nuove questioni avea davante.  

Her ceasing and her transmuted semblance enjoined silence on my eager wit, which already had new questionings before it.

- *il trasmutar sembiante* ‘the changing of countenance’ → “her transmuted semblance”: the countenance becomes the head noun, qualified by a past participle; the possessive is repeated where the Italian has a definite article.
- *poser silenzio al* ‘put silence to’ → “enjoined silence on”: the verb of placing becomes one of injunction, with a corresponding change of preposition.
- *nuove questioni avea davante* ‘new questions had before [it]’ → “had new questionings before it”: the object follows the verb; “questionings” suggests the activity of inquiry, and “it” makes the reference of the final adverb explicit.

> 91 E sì come saetta, che nel segno  
> 92 percote pria che sia la corda queta,  
> 93 così corremmo nel secondo regno.  

And even as an arrow which smiteth the targe ere the cord be still, so fled we to the second realm.

- *saetta* ‘arrow’ → “an arrow”: English supplies the indefinite article.
- *nel segno percote* ‘in the mark strikes’ → “smiteth the targe”: the verb comes first, and a direct object replaces the prepositional phrase.
- *pria che sia la corda queta* ‘before that be the cord still’ → “ere the cord be still”: one conjunction replaces the Italian phrase, and the subject precedes the subjunctive.
- *corremmo nel secondo regno* ‘we ran into the second realm’ → “fled we to the second realm”: the verb of running becomes one of swift flight, with the subject after it; “to” marks the destination rather than entry into it.

> 94 Quivi la donna mia vid' io sì lieta,  
> 95 come nel lume di quel ciel si mise,  
> 96 che più lucente se ne fe' il pianeta.  

There I beheld my Lady so glad, when to the light of this heaven she committed her, that the planet’s self became the brighter for it.

- *la donna mia vid' io* ‘the lady mine saw I’ → “I beheld my Lady”: subject–verb–object replaces object–verb–subject; the possessive precedes the noun.
- *come nel lume di quel ciel si mise* ‘as into the light of that heaven herself she put’ → “when to the light of this heaven she committed her”: the conjunction is explicitly temporal; “to” goes with a verb of commitment rather than one of placing, the demonstrative changes from distal to proximal, and the reflexive follows the verb as “her”.
- *più lucente se ne fe' il pianeta* ‘brighter made itself thereby the planet’ → “the planet’s self became the brighter for it”: the subject moves to the front; reflexive making is rendered by “became”, while “self” gives the subject added emphasis. The comparative gains “the”, and the force of *ne* is expressed at the end.

> 97 E se la stella si cambiò e rise,  
> 98 qual mi fec' io, che pur di mia natura  
> 99 trasmutabile son per tutte guise!  

And if the star was changed and laughed, what then did I, who of my very nature am subjected unto change through every guise!

- *si cambiò* ‘changed’ → “was changed”: the Italian pronominal verb is rendered by a passive.
- *qual mi fec' io* ‘what did I become’ → “what then did I”: the question about the state into which the speaker was changed becomes an elliptical question with “did”; “then” is supplied to point the inference.
- *pur di mia natura* ‘just by my nature’ → “of my very nature”: the emphasis of the adverb is transferred to a modifier of the noun.
- *trasmutabile son* ‘changeable am’ → “am subjected unto change”: the predicate follows the copula, and a passive phrase replaces the adjective of capacity.
- *per tutte guise* ‘in all ways’ → “through every guise”: “through” replaces the preposition of manner, and a distributive singular replaces the plural.

> 100 Come in peschiera, ch' è tranquilla e pura,  
> 101 traggonsi i pesci a ciò che vien di fuori  
> 102 per modo che lo stimin lor pastura;  

As in a fish-pool still and clear, the fishes draw to aught that so droppeth from without as to make them deem it somewhat they may feed on,

- *in peschiera, ch' è tranquilla e pura* ‘in a fish-pool which is still and pure’ → “in a fish-pool still and clear”: English supplies the article but drops the relative pronoun and copula, attaching the adjectives directly to the noun; purity is rendered as clarity.
- *traggonsi i pesci* ‘draw themselves the fishes’ → “the fishes draw”: the subject precedes the verb, which is used intransitively without a reflexive.
- *a ciò che vien di fuori* ‘to that which comes from outside’ → “to aught that so droppeth from without”: the demonstrative becomes indefinite, and coming is specified as dropping; “so” anticipates the following expression of manner.
- *per modo che lo stimin lor pastura* ‘in such a way that they deem it their food’ → “as to make them deem it somewhat they may feed on”: the finite subjunctive clause becomes a causative infinitive construction; the possessive and noun become an indefinite pronoun qualified by a clause expressing possible use.

> 103 sì vid' io ben più di mille splendori  
> 104 trarsi ver noi, ed in ciascun s' udia:  
> 105 *Ecco chi crescerà li nostri amori.*  

so did I see more than a thousand splendours draw towards us, and in each one was heard: *Lo! one who shall increase our loves.*

- *vid' io* ‘saw I’ → “did I see”: inversion is effected with an auxiliary.
- *ben più di mille* ‘well over a thousand’ → “more than a thousand”: the intensifier is not separately rendered.
- *trarsi* ‘draw themselves’ → “draw”: the intransitive English verb needs no reflexive.
- *s' udia* ‘one heard’ → “was heard”: the impersonal construction is rendered by a passive.
- *chi* ‘he who’ → “one who”: English gives the relative an explicit indefinite antecedent.

> 106 E sì come ciascuno a noi venia,  
> 107 vedeasi l' ombra piena di letizia  
> 108 nel fulgor chiaro che da lei uscia.  

And as each one came up to us, the shade appeared full filled with joy, by the bright glow that issued forth of it.

- *sì come* ‘even as’ → “as”: the conjunction is not reinforced by a separate adverb.
- *a noi venia* ‘to us came’ → “came up to us”: the destination follows the verb, and “up” is supplied.
- *vedeasi l' ombra* ‘was seen the shade’ → “the shade appeared”: the subject moves first, and appearance replaces the passive expression of seeing.
- *piena di letizia* ‘full of joy’ → “full filled with joy”: a participle is added, with “with” rather than ‘of’.
- *nel fulgor chiaro* ‘in the glow bright’ → “by the bright glow”: the glow is construed as the means by which the joy appeared; the adjective precedes its noun.
- *da lei uscia* ‘from her came out’ → “issued forth of it”: the verb precedes the phrase of origin, and the pronoun referring to the grammatically feminine shade becomes neuter.

> 109 Pensa, lettor, se quel che qui s' inizia  
> 110 non procedesse, come tu avresti  
> 111 di più sapere angosciosa carizia;  

Think, reader, if what I now begin proceeded not, how thou would’st feel an anguished dearth of knowing more,

- *quel che qui s' inizia* ‘that which here is begun’ → “what I now begin”: the antecedent and relative coalesce in “what”; place becomes time, and the passive construction becomes active with the speaker as agent.
- *come tu avresti di più sapere angosciosa carizia* ‘how thou wouldst have, of knowing more, an anguished dearth’ → “how thou would’st feel an anguished dearth of knowing more”: feeling replaces having, the indefinite article is supplied, and the complement follows “dearth” rather than preceding it; within the complement, “more” follows the verb.

> 112 e per te vederai, come da questi  
> 113 m' era in disio d' udir lor condizioni,  
> 114 sì come agli occhi mi fur manifesti.  

and by thyself thou shalt perceive how it was in my longing to hear from these concerning their estate, soon as they were revealed unto my eyes.

- *vederai* ‘thou wilt see’ → “thou shalt perceive”: the verb of sight is rendered explicitly as one of apprehension.
- *da questi m' era in disio d' udir* ‘from these it was to me a desire to hear’ → “it was in my longing to hear from these”: the phrase naming those from whom the speaker wishes to hear moves from before the expression of desire to immediately after the verb of hearing; English supplies a formal subject and turns the dative of the person desiring into a possessive.
- *lor condizioni* ‘their conditions’ → “concerning their estate”: the direct object becomes a prepositional phrase, and the plural becomes a collective singular.
- *sì come* ‘even as’ → “soon as”: the relation is construed as one of immediate succession.
- *agli occhi mi fur manifesti* ‘to the eyes to me were manifest’ → “they were revealed unto my eyes”: a passive of disclosure replaces the copula and adjective; the phrase naming the eyes follows the predicate, and the personal dative becomes a possessive.

> 115 “O bene nato, a cui veder li Troni  
> 116 del trionfo eternal concede grazia,  
> 117 prima che la milizia s' abbandoni,  

“O happy-born, to whom grace concedeth to look upon the Thrones of the eternal triumph ere thou abandonest thy time of warfare,

- *bene nato* ‘well born’ → “happy-born”: the adverb is interpreted as denoting good fortune.
- *veder li Troni del trionfo eternal concede grazia* ‘to see the Thrones of the triumph eternal grants grace’ → “grace concedeth to look upon the Thrones of the eternal triumph”: the subject and verb move before the infinitive they govern, seeing becomes looking upon, and the adjective precedes “triumph”.
- *prima che la milizia s' abbandoni* ‘before the military service be left behind’ → “ere thou abandonest thy time of warfare”: a single conjunction introduces an active clause addressed to the hearer, in place of the passive subjunctive; service becomes a period of warfare, with a possessive in place of the article.

## Lines 118–139 — A Spirit Answers from Within Its Light

> 118 del lume che per tutto il ciel si spazia  
> 119 noi semo accesi: e però, se disii  
> 120 di noi chiarirti, a tuo piacer ti sazia.”  

by the light that rangeth through all heaven are we enkindled; and therefore if thou desire to draw light from us, sate thee at thine own will.”

- *del lume* ‘of the light’ → “by the light”: English marks the light as that by which the spirits are enkindled.
- *per tutto il ciel si spazia* ‘through all the heaven ranges abroad’ → “rangeth through all heaven”: the verb comes before the phrase of extent; the Italian pronominal verb becomes a simple intransitive verb, and the article drops out.
- *noi semo accesi* ‘we are enkindled’ → “are we enkindled”: the auxiliary precedes the subject.
- *di noi chiarirti* ‘about us to enlighten thyself’ → “to draw light from us”: the reflexive infinitive becomes a verb with a noun object; the spirits become the source of the light rather than the subject on which enlightenment is sought.
- *a tuo piacer ti sazia* ‘at thy pleasure thyself sate’ → “sate thee at thine own will”: the imperative comes first, the reflexive follows it, and the phrase of choice comes last; “will” replaces ‘pleasure’, with “own” added for emphasis.

> 121 Così da un di quelli spirti pii  
> 122 detto mi fu; e da Beatrice: “Di', di',  
> 123 sicuramente, e credi come a dii.”  

Thus by one of those devout spirits was said to me, and by Beatrice: “Speak, speak securely, and believe as thou would’st deities.”

- *spirti pii* ‘spirits devout’ → “devout spirits”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *detto mi fu* ‘said to me was’ → “was said to me”: the auxiliary precedes the participle, and the indirect object follows both.
- *Di', di'* ‘Say, say’ → “Speak, speak”: the imperative of ‘say’ is rendered by the verb for the act of speaking.
- *come a dii* ‘as to gods’ → “as thou would’st deities”: English expands the comparison into a clause with a conditional auxiliary and an understood verb of believing; ‘gods’ becomes “deities”, a direct rather than a prepositional object.

> 124 “Io veggio ben sì come tu t' annidi  
> 125 nel proprio lume, e che dagli occhi il traggi,  
> 126 perch' ei corruscan, sì come tu ridi;  

“Verily, I see how thou dost nestle in thine own light, and that thou dost draw it through thine eyes, because they sparkle as thou smilest;

- *Io veggio ben sì come* ‘I see well just how’ → “Verily, I see how”: the emphasis within the Italian phrase is transferred to an opening adverb of affirmation; there is no separate equivalent of *sì* before “how”.
- *tu t' annidi* ‘thou nestest thyself’ → “thou dost nestle”: an intransitive verb replaces the reflexive, and the simple present is expressed with an auxiliary.
- *nel proprio lume* ‘in the very light of one’s own’ → “in thine own light”: the possessor implicit in *proprio* is made explicit in the second person.
- *dagli occhi il traggi* ‘from the eyes it thou drawest’ → “thou dost draw it through thine eyes”: the verb, expressed with an auxiliary, precedes the object and the prepositional phrase; source becomes passage, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *sì come* ‘just as’ → “as”: the reinforcing *sì* has no separate equivalent.

> 127 ma non so chi tu sei, nè perchè aggi,  
> 128 anima degna, il grado della spera,  
> 129 che si vela ai mortal con altrui raggi.”  

but I know nor who thou art, nor why, O worthy soul, thou art graded in this sphere, which veileth it to mortals in another’s rays.”

- *non so chi tu sei, nè perchè* ‘I do not know who thou art, nor why’ → “I know nor who thou art, nor why”: instead of negating the main verb, English places the first of two matching negatives before the first indirect question.
- *aggi … il grado della spera* ‘thou hast … the rank of the sphere’ → “thou art graded in this sphere”: the verb, postponed until after the vocative, takes a passive form incorporating the sense of the noun *grado*; ‘of’ becomes locative “in”, and the article becomes demonstrative.
- *anima degna* ‘soul worthy’ → “O worthy soul”: the vocative particle is added, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *si vela* ‘veils itself’ → “veileth it”: the Italian reflexive is rendered by a simple pronoun used reflexively.
- *con altrui raggi* ‘with rays of another’ → “in another’s rays”: “in” replaces ‘with’, and the possessive is expressed by an English genitive before the noun.

> 130 Questo diss' io diritto alla lumiera  
> 131 che pria m' avea parlato, ond' ella fessi  
> 132 lucente più assai di quel ch' ell' era.  

This I said, turned towards the light which first had spoken to me; whereat it glowed far brighter yet than what it was before.

- *diss' io* ‘said I’ → “I said”: the expressed subject moves before the verb.
- *diritto alla lumiera* ‘directed to the luminary’ → “turned towards the light”: the description of orientation is recast with “turned towards”, and the luminous object is called a “light”.
- *m' avea parlato* ‘to me had spoken’ → “had spoken to me”: the indirect object follows the verb.
- *ond' ella fessi lucente più assai* ‘whereupon she made herself luminous much more’ → “whereat it glowed far brighter yet”: the feminine pronoun becomes neuter; a reflexive verb of becoming with a predicate adjective becomes an intransitive verb of shining with a comparative. The intensifier precedes the comparative, and “yet” reinforces it.
- *di quel ch' ell' era* ‘than what it was’ → “than what it was before”: the prior time of comparison is made explicit.

> 133 Sì come il sol, che si cela egli stessi  
> 134 per troppa luce, come il caldo ha rose  
> 135 le temperanze dei vapori spessi;  

Like as the sun which hideth him by excess of light when the heat hath gnawed away the tempering of the thick vapours,

- *Sì come* ‘just as’ → “Like as”: the reinforced comparison takes a different English form.
- *si cela egli stessi* ‘hides himself, he himself’ → “hideth him”: “him” does reflexive duty, without a separate rendering of the emphatic subject.
- *per troppa luce* ‘through too much light’ → “by excess of light”: the adjective of excess becomes a noun governing “of light”.
- *come* ‘as’ → “when”: the temporal sense is made explicit.
- *ha rose* ‘has gnawed’ → “hath gnawed away”: the particle makes the effect of the gnawing explicit.
- *le temperanze* ‘the moderations’ → “the tempering”: the plural noun becomes a singular noun of action.
- *dei vapori spessi* ‘of the vapours thick’ → “of the thick vapours”: the adjective precedes the noun.

> 136 per più letizia sì mi si nascose  
> 137 dentro al suo raggio la figura santa,  

so by access of joy the sacred figure hid him in his own rays,

- *per più letizia* ‘through greater joy’ → “by access of joy”: the comparative modifier becomes a noun meaning an increase, with “of joy” dependent on it.
- *sì mi si nascose* ‘so from me hid itself’ → “so by access of joy the sacred figure hid him”: the correlative “so” moves to the head of the clause; “him” serves as the reflexive, but the person from whom the figure hid is left unexpressed.
- *dentro al suo raggio* ‘within his ray’ → “in his own rays”: “in” replaces ‘within’, “own” reinforces the possessive, and the singular becomes plural.
- *la figura santa* ‘the figure holy’ → “the sacred figure”: the subject moves from the end of the clause to before the verb, and the adjective precedes the noun.

> 138 e così chiusa chiusa mi rispose  
> 139 nel modo che il seguente canto canta.  

and thus enclosed, enclosed, answered me in such fashion as chanteth the following chant.

- *mi rispose* ‘to me replied’ → “answered me”: the indirect object becomes a direct object and follows the verb.
- *nel modo che* ‘in the manner that’ → “in such fashion as”: the definite noun and relative are recast as a comparison with “such” and “as”.
- *il seguente canto canta* ‘the following canto sings’ → “chanteth the following chant”: the verb precedes its subject, and English uses the same root for verb and noun to reproduce the Italian verbal echo.

## Summary — Reordering and emphasis

Wicksteed often keeps Dante’s words but changes their order. In *la donna mia vid' io*, the object precedes the verb and the subject follows it; “I beheld my Lady” puts the subject first and the object last, with the possessive before its noun. English also regularly supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending gives the person, and omits the article that Italian uses before a possessive: *il suo bel volto* begins in English with “her face”, not ‘the face of hers’. Yet Wicksteed does not invariably prefer ordinary English order: “creatures intelligent” keeps the adjective after its noun as in *le creature intelligenti*.

A change of construction sometimes allows Wicksteed to keep a distinction without translating each word separately. The negative in *se ... non* (‘if ... not’) is absorbed into “unless”; the relative clause *che vi guida* (‘who guides you’) becomes “to guide you”, expressing function rather than action. Elsewhere he expands: *argomenti* (‘thou inferrest’) becomes “thou draw due inference”, with a noun carrying the sense of the verb and “due” supplied. By contrast, the intensifier *ben* in *ben più di mille* has no separate equivalent in “more than a thousand”.

The choice of a word can sharpen one sense at the expense of another. For *manco voto* (‘deficient vow’), “broken vow” specifies the deficiency; for *cibo rigido* (‘hard food’), “stubborn food” stresses resistance rather than hardness. Wicksteed stays particularly close where repetition matters: *chiusa chiusa* becomes “enclosed, enclosed”, and although he reverses subject and verb in *il seguente canto canta*, “chanteth the following chant” preserves the echo between noun and verb.
