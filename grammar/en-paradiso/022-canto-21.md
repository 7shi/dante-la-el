# Paradiso 21

## Lines 1–9 — Why My Lady Does Not Smile

> 1 Già eran gli occhi miei rifissi al volto  
> 2 della mia donna, e l' animo con essi,  
> 3 e da ogni altro intento s' era tolto;  

Already were mine eyes fixed on my Lady’s countenance again, and my mind with them, from all other intent removed;

- *gli occhi miei* ‘the eyes of mine’ → “mine eyes”: the possessive precedes the noun instead of following it.
- *rifissi al volto della mia donna* ‘fixed anew on the countenance of my Lady’ → “fixed on my Lady’s countenance again”: the force of the prefix *ri-* is expressed by a separate adverb placed after the whole phrase; the possessor precedes the thing possessed.
- *l' animo* ‘the mind’ → “my mind”: the possessive replaces the definite article.
- *e da ogni altro intento s' era tolto* ‘and from every other intent had withdrawn itself’ → “from all other intent removed”: the conjunction drops out, “all” replaces ‘every’, and a participle describing the mind’s state replaces the finite reflexive pluperfect.

> 4 e quella non ridea, ma: “S' io ridessi,  
> 5 mi cominciò, tu ti faresti quale  
> 6 fu Semelè, quando di cener fessi;  

and she smiled not, but: “Were I to smile,” she began, “thou wouldst be such as was Semele, when she turned to ashes;

- *quella* ‘that one’ → “she”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *non ridea* ‘did not smile’ → “smiled not”: the negative follows the verb, and the English simple past does not mark the Italian imperfect as such.
- *S' io ridessi* ‘if I smiled’ → “Were I to smile”: inversion replaces the conditional conjunction, and the imperfect subjunctive is expressed by “Were” with an infinitive.
- *mi cominciò* ‘she began to me’ → “she began”: the pronoun indicating the person addressed is omitted.
- *tu ti faresti quale fu Semelè* ‘thou wouldst become such as Semele was’ → “thou wouldst be such as was Semele”: “be” expresses the resulting state rather than the change expressed by reflexive *farsi*; the single relative *quale* is expanded to “such as”.
- *di cener fessi* ‘made herself of ash’ → “she turned to ashes”: the reflexive verb and phrase of material become a verb of change followed by its result; singular ‘ash’ becomes plural.

> 7 chè la bellezza mia, che per le scale  
> 8 dell' eterno palazzo più s' accende,  
> 9 com' hai veduto, quanto più si sale,  

for my beauty, which, along the steps of the eternal palace kindleth more, as thou hast seen, the higher the ascent,

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a coordinating conjunction replaces the subordinating one.
- *per le scale* ‘by way of the stairs’ → “along the steps”: the prepositional phrase is rendered as a course along the individual steps.
- *dell' eterno palazzo* ‘of the palace eternal’ → “of the eternal palace”: the adjective precedes the noun instead of following it.
- *più s' accende* ‘more is kindled’ → “kindleth more”: the pronominal verb becomes an English intransitive, and the comparative adverb follows it.
- *quanto più si sale* ‘in proportion as one climbs higher’ → “the higher the ascent”: the impersonal finite verb becomes a definite noun phrase, and the proportional relation is expressed by “the” with the comparative.

## Lines 10–42 — The Ladder in the Seventh Splendour

> 10 se non si temperasse, tanto splende,  
> 11 che il tuo mortal potere, al suo fulgore,  
> 12 sarebbe fronda che tuono scoscende.  

were it not tempered, so doth glow as that thy mortal power, at its flash, would be like foliage that the thunder shattereth.

- *se non si temperasse* ‘if it did not temper itself’ → “were it not tempered”: inversion replaces the conditional conjunction, and the reflexive becomes passive.
- *tanto splende, che* ‘shines so much that’ → “so doth glow as that”: the degree is expressed by “so … as that”, and the simple verb becomes “doth glow”.
- *al suo fulgore* ‘at its brilliance’ → “at its flash”: a word for radiance is rendered by one suggesting a sudden burst of light.
- *sarebbe fronda* ‘would be foliage’ → “would be like foliage”: “like” makes the comparison explicit instead of identifying the one thing with the other.
- *tuono scoscende* ‘thunder rends’ → “the thunder shattereth”: the article is supplied, and the verb of rending is rendered by one of breaking to pieces.

> 13 Noi sem levati al settimo splendore,  
> 14 che sotto il petto del Leone ardente  
> 15 raggia mo misto giù del suo valore.  

We have arisen to the seventh splendour, which, underneath the bosom of the glowing Lion, downrayeth now mingling with its power.

- *Noi sem levati* ‘we are raised’ → “We have arisen”: the construction with ‘be’ and a participle becomes an active perfect of an intransitive verb.
- *sotto il petto* ‘under the breast’ → “underneath the bosom”: the preposition is expanded and the noun is rendered by “bosom”.
- *del Leone ardente* ‘of the Lion burning’ → “of the glowing Lion”: “glowing” replaces ‘burning’ and precedes the noun.
- *raggia mo misto giù del suo valore* ‘rays now, mixed, down with its virtue’ → “downrayeth now mingling with its power”: the direction is taken forward into the verb; the past participle expressing a state becomes an active present participle, and ‘virtue’ in the sense of efficacy becomes “power”.

> 16 Ficca di retro agli occhi tuoi la mente,  
> 17 e fa di quelli specchi alla figura,  
> 18 che in questo specchio ti sarà parvente.”  

Fix thy mind after thine eyes, and make of them mirrors to the figure which in this mirror shall be shown unto thee.”

- *Ficca di retro agli occhi tuoi la mente* ‘plant behind the eyes of thine the mind’ → “Fix thy mind after thine eyes”: the object is brought next to the imperative and given a possessive; “after” renders the spatial ‘behind’, and the possessive of ‘eyes’ moves before its noun.
- *di quelli* ‘of those’ → “of them”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *ti sarà parvente* ‘to thee will be apparent’ → “shall be shown unto thee”: the predicative ‘apparent’ becomes a passive verb of showing, and the recipient follows it.

> 19 Chi sapesse qual era la pastura  
> 20 del viso mio nell' aspetto beato,  
> 21 quand' io mi trasmutai ad altra cura,  

Whoso should know what was the pasture of my sight in the blessed aspect when I changed me to another care,

- *Chi sapesse* ‘whoever knew’ → “Whoso should know”: “should” expresses the hypothesis carried by the imperfect subjunctive.
- *del viso mio* ‘of the sight of mine’ → “of my sight”: the postposed possessive moves before the noun.
- *nell' aspetto beato* ‘in the aspect blessed’ → “in the blessed aspect”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *io mi trasmutai* ‘I myself transformed’ → “I changed me”: the less emphatic “changed” renders the verb of transformation, and the reflexive pronoun follows it.

> 22 conoscerebbe quanto m' era a grato  
> 23 ubbidire alla mia celeste scorta,  
> 24 contrappesando l' un con l' altro lato.  

would recognise how much it was my joy to be obedient to my heavenly guide, weighing the one against the other side.

- *quanto m' era a grato* ‘how much it was pleasing to me’ → “how much it was my joy”: the experiencer becomes a possessive, and ‘pleasing’ is recast as the noun “joy”.
- *ubbidire alla mia celeste scorta* ‘to obey my celestial guide’ → “to be obedient to my heavenly guide”: the infinitive becomes ‘be’ with an adjective and a complement in “to”; “heavenly” renders ‘celestial’.
- *contrappesando l' un con l' altro lato* ‘counterweighing the one with the other side’ → “weighing the one against the other side”: the opposition conveyed by the prefix is expressed instead by “against” in place of ‘with’.

> 25 Dentro al cristallo, che il vocabol porta,  
> 26 cerchiando il mondo, del suo chiaro duce,  
> 27 sotto cui giacque ogni malizia morta,  

Within the crystal which doth bear the name, circling the world, of its illustrious leader, beneath whom every wickedness lay dead,

- *che il vocabol porta* ‘which the name bears’ → “which doth bear the name”: the verb moves before its object and takes the auxiliary “doth”.
- *del suo chiaro duce* ‘of its bright leader’ → “of its illustrious leader”: the adjective is taken in the sense of distinction rather than brightness.
- *sotto cui* ‘under whom’ → “beneath whom”: “beneath” renders ‘under’.
- *giacque ogni malizia morta* ‘lay every wickedness dead’ → “every wickedness lay dead”: the subject is moved ahead of the verb.

> 28 di color d' oro, in che raggio traluce,  
> 29 vid' io uno scaleo eretto in suso  
> 30 tanto, che nol seguiva la mia luce.  

coloured like gold which doth recast the ray, I saw a ladder erected upward so far that my sight might not follow it.

- *di color d' oro* ‘of colour of gold’ → “coloured like gold”: the phrase with a noun becomes participial, and “like” replaces the second ‘of’.
- *in che raggio traluce* ‘in which a ray shines through’ → “which doth recast the ray”: the prepositional relative becomes the subject; the ray, now definite, becomes the object, and shining through is interpreted as light being cast back.
- *vid' io* ‘saw I’ → “I saw”: the expressed subject moves before the verb.
- *tanto* ‘so much’ → “so far”: degree is made specifically a matter of distance.
- *nol seguiva la mia luce* ‘it followed not my light’ → “my sight might not follow it”: the postposed subject moves first, ‘light’ is interpreted as the faculty of sight, and the imperfect indicative becomes a modal expression of inability; the object pronoun moves after the verb.

> 31 Vidi anche per li gradi scender giuso  
> 32 tanti splendor, ch' io pensai ch' ogni lume  
> 33 che par nel ciel quindi fosse diffuso.  

I saw, moreover, descend upon the steps so many splendours that methought every light which shineth in the heaven had been thence poured down.

- *anche* ‘also’ → “moreover”: the additive adverb becomes a more marked connective.
- *per li gradi scender giuso* ‘along the steps descend down’ → “descend upon the steps”: the infinitive moves first, “upon” replaces the preposition of passage, and the separate ‘down’ is dropped.
- *io pensai* ‘I thought’ → “methought”: the personal verb with its expressed subject becomes an impersonal ‘it seemed to me’.
- *che par nel ciel* ‘which appears in the heaven’ → “which shineth in the heaven”: being visible is rendered as emitting light.
- *quindi fosse diffuso* ‘thence were diffused’ → “had been thence poured down”: the imperfect subjunctive passive is interpreted as a completed prior action; “thence” moves inside the verb phrase, and “poured down” adds a direction to the image of diffusion.

> 34 E come, per lo natural costume,  
> 35 le pole insieme, al cominciar del giorno,  
> 36 si movono a scaldar le fredde piume;  

And as, after their nature’s way, the daws at the beginning of the day set out in company to warm their chilled feathers;

- *per lo natural costume* ‘according to natural custom’ → “after their nature’s way”: a possessive is supplied, ‘natural’ becomes the genitive “nature’s”, and ‘custom’ becomes “way”.
- *insieme* ‘together’ → “in company”: the adverb between the subject and the time phrase becomes a phrase following the verb.
- *al cominciar del giorno* ‘at the commencing of the day’ → “at the beginning of the day”: the substantivised infinitive becomes a noun.
- *si movono* ‘move themselves’ → “set out”: the reflexive verb becomes a phrasal verb specifically denoting departure.
- *le fredde piume* ‘the cold feathers’ → “their chilled feathers”: a possessive replaces the article, and “chilled” renders the adjective as the result of cooling.

> 37 poi altre vanno via senza ritorno,  
> 38 altre rivolgon sè, onde son mosse,  
> 39 ed altre roteando fan soggiorno:  

then some go off without return, others come again to whence they started, and others make a wheeling sojourn;

- *altre* ‘others’ → “some”: the first of the repeated pronouns introduces a group without yet contrasting it with another.
- *vanno via* ‘go away’ → “go off”: “off” replaces ‘away’.
- *rivolgon sè* ‘turn themselves back’ → “come again”: the reflexive construction disappears, and the return is expressed as coming again rather than turning back.
- *onde son mosse* ‘whence they have set out’ → “to whence they started”: “to” marks the point of departure as the destination of the return, and the compound tense becomes a simple past.
- *roteando fan soggiorno* ‘wheeling, make a stay’ → “make a wheeling sojourn”: the gerund describing what they do while staying becomes a modifier of “sojourn”.

> 40 tal modo parve a me che quivi fosse  
> 41 in quello sfavillar che insieme venne,  
> 42 sì come in certo grado si percosse;  

such fashion, meseemed, was in that sparkling which came in company, soon as it smote upon a certain step,

- *parve a me che quivi fosse* ‘it seemed to me that there was’ → “meseemed, was”: the verb of seeming and its experiencer become a parenthesis; the subordinate subjunctive becomes the main verb, and ‘there’ is not separately rendered.
- *insieme venne* ‘together came’ → “came in company”: the adverb becomes a phrase and follows the verb.
- *sì come* ‘just as’ → “soon as”: the conjunction is read temporally, with emphasis on immediacy.
- *in certo grado si percosse* ‘against a certain step it struck itself’ → “it smote upon a certain step”: the reflexive form becomes intransitive, and the phrase naming the point of contact follows the verb.

## Lines 43–75 — The Silent Heaven and the Spirit’s Descent

> 43 e quel che presso più ci si ritenne,  
> 44 si fe' sì chiaro, ch' io dicea pensando:  

and the one which abode nighest to us became so bright that in my thought I said:

- *quel che presso più ci si ritenne* ‘the one which near more to us stayed itself’ → “the one which abode nighest to us”: the pronominal verb becomes the simple “abode” and moves before the expression of place; the comparison is expressed by a single superlative.
- *si fe' sì chiaro* ‘made itself so bright’ → “became so bright”: the reflexive construction becomes a verb of becoming.
- *io dicea pensando* ‘I was saying, thinking’ → “in my thought I said”: the gerund becomes a possessive noun phrase placed first, and the imperfect becomes a simple past.

> 45 “Io veggio ben l' amor che tu m' accenne.  

“I do perceive the love which thou art signalling unto me.

- *Io veggio ben* ‘I see well’ → “I do perceive”: “perceive” replaces ‘see’, and emphatic “do” carries the force of the adverb.
- *tu m' accenne* ‘thou dost indicate to me’ → “thou art signalling unto me”: a progressive construction suggests a signal in the making, and the recipient follows the verb.

> 46 Ma quella, ond' io aspetto il come e il quando  
> 47 del dire e del tacer, si sta, ond' io  
> 48 contra il disio fo ben ch' io non domando.”  

But she from whom I wait the how and when of speech and silence, pauses, and therefore I, counter to my desire, do well not to demand.”

- *quella* ‘that one’ → “she”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *il come e il quando* ‘the how and the when’ → “the how and when”: the two substantivized adverbs share one article.
- *del dire e del tacer* ‘of speaking and of keeping silent’ → “of speech and silence”: abstract nouns replace the substantivized infinitives, and the preposition is not repeated.
- *si sta* ‘stays still’ → “pauses”: the English suggests an interval of waiting rather than simply a continuing state.
- *ond' io* ‘whence I’ → “and therefore I”: the connective of consequence is expanded into a conjunction and an adverb.
- *contra il disio* ‘against the desire’ → “counter to my desire”: the possessive specifies whose desire it is.
- *fo ben ch' io non domando* ‘I do well in that I do not ask’ → “do well not to demand”: the finite clause becomes a negative infinitive, and “demand” renders the act of asking.

> 49 Perch' ella, che vedeva il tacer mio  
> 50 nel veder di colui che tutto vede,  
> 51 mi disse: “Solvi il tuo caldo disio.”  

Whereat she, who saw my silence in his sight who seeth all, said to me: “Loose thy warm desire.”

- *vedeva* ‘was seeing’ → “saw”: the simple past does not mark the continuing aspect of the imperfect.
- *il tacer mio* ‘the keeping-silent of mine’ → “my silence”: the substantivized infinitive becomes an abstract noun, with the possessive placed first.
- *nel veder di colui* ‘in the seeing of him’ → “in his sight”: another substantivized infinitive becomes a noun, and the possessor moves before it.
- *che tutto vede* ‘who all sees’ → “who seeth all”: the object follows the verb.
- *mi disse* ‘to me said’ → “said to me”: the recipient follows the verb.
- *caldo* ‘hot’ → “warm”: the English moderates the adjective.

> 52 Ed io incominciai: “La mia mercede  
> 53 non mi fa degno della tua risposta,  
> 54 ma per colei che il chieder mi concede,  

And I began: “My merit maketh me not worthy of thy response, but for her sake who granteth me to make request,

- *mercede* ‘desert’ → “merit”: the English selects the sense of worthiness rather than that of recompense.
- *non mi fa degno* ‘not me makes worthy’ → “maketh me not worthy”: the object follows the verb and the negative stands next to the adjective.
- *per colei* ‘for her’ → “for her sake”: “sake” makes the appeal explicit.
- *il chieder mi concede* ‘the asking to me grants’ → “granteth me to make request”: the verb comes first, and the substantivized infinitive becomes an infinitive with “request” as its object.

> 55 vita beata, che ti stai nascosta  
> 56 dentro alla tua letizia, fammi nota  
> 57 la cagion che sì presso mi t' ha posta;  

O blessed life, who abidest hidden in thy gladness, make known to me the cause which so nigh to me hath placed thee;

- *vita beata* ‘life blessed’ → “O blessed life”: the vocative is marked by “O”, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *ti stai nascosta* ‘stayest thyself hidden’ → “abidest hidden”: a simple intransitive verb replaces the pronominal one.
- *dentro alla* ‘within’ → “in”: the explicit sense of interiority is less marked in English.
- *fammi nota la cagion* ‘make to me known the cause’ → “make known to me the cause”: “known” comes directly after the verb rather than after the recipient.
- *mi t' ha posta* ‘to me thee has placed’ → “to me hath placed thee”: the direct object moves after the participle, which in English does not mark feminine agreement.

> 58 e di' perchè si tace in questa rota  
> 59 la dolce sinfonia di paradiso,  
> 60 che giù per l' altre sona sì devota.”  

and say, wherefore in this wheel the sweet symphony of Paradise keepeth silence, which below throughout the others soundeth so devoutly.”

- *si tace in questa rota la dolce sinfonia di paradiso* ‘keeps silent in this wheel the sweet symphony of Paradise’ → “in this wheel the sweet symphony of Paradise keepeth silence”: the place phrase comes first and the subject precedes the verb; the pronominal verb is rendered by a verb with a noun object.
- *per l' altre* ‘through the others’ → “throughout the others”: “throughout” stresses the extent of the sounding.
- *devota* ‘devout’ → “devoutly”: the adjective agreeing with the symphony becomes an adverb modifying its sounding.

> 61 “Tu hai l' udir mortal, sì come il viso,  
> 62 rispose a me; onde qui non si canta  
> 63 per quel che Beatrice non ha riso.  

“Thou hast the hearing, as the sight, of mortals,” he answered me; “wherefore here is no song for that same reason for which Beatrice hath not smiled.

- *l' udir mortal, sì come il viso* ‘hearing mortal, just as sight’ → “the hearing, as the sight, of mortals”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun; the adjective becomes a phrase with a plural noun, placed after both faculties, and the comparison is reduced to “as”.
- *rispose a me* ‘answered to me’ → “he answered me”: English makes the person answered a direct object.
- *qui non si canta* ‘here one does not sing’ → “here is no song”: an impersonal verb of action becomes a negative existential construction.
- *per quel che* ‘for that for which’ → “for that same reason for which”: “reason” supplies a noun for the demonstrative, and “same” makes the identity explicit.
- *non ha riso* ‘has not laughed’ → “hath not smiled”: the English chooses the gentler sense of the verb.

> 64 Giù per li gradi della scala santa  
> 65 discesi tanto, sol per farti festa  
> 66 col dire e con la luce che m' ammanta:  

Down by the steps of the sacred ladder I so far descended only to do thee joyance with speech and with the light which mantleth me;

- *scala santa* ‘ladder holy’ → “sacred ladder”: “sacred” renders the adjective, which precedes the noun.
- *discesi tanto* ‘I descended so far’ → “I so far descended”: the expression of extent precedes the verb.
- *per farti festa* ‘to make festivity for thee’ → “to do thee joyance”: the idiom of giving a welcome becomes one of giving joy.
- *col dire* ‘with the speaking’ → “with speech”: a noun without an article replaces the substantivized infinitive.
- *m' ammanta* ‘me mantles’ → “mantleth me”: the object follows the verb.

> 67 nè più amor mi fece esser più presta,  
> 68 chè più e tanto amor quinci su ferve,  
> 69 sì come il fiammeggiar ti manifesta;  

nor was it greater love that made me swifter; for more and so much love up there doth burn, as the flashing maketh plain to thee;

- *nè più amor mi fece esser più presta* ‘nor did more love make me be more prompt’ → “nor was it greater love that made me swifter”: a cleft construction singles out “greater love”; the object follows “made”, and a single comparative replaces the infinitive and its comparative phrase.
- *quinci su* ‘from here upward’ → “up there”: the English gives a location rather than a direction reckoned from here.
- *ferve* ‘seethes’ → “doth burn”: the heat of boiling becomes that of burning.
- *sì come* ‘just as’ → “as”: the strengthening adverb has no separate equivalent.
- *il fiammeggiar* ‘the flaming’ → “the flashing”: the English emphasizes the emission of light.
- *ti manifesta* ‘to thee makes manifest’ → “maketh plain to thee”: the single verb becomes a verb and adjective, followed by the recipient.

> 70 ma l' alta carità, che ci fa serve  
> 71 pronte al consiglio che il mondo governa,  
> 72 sorteggia qui, sì come tu osserve.”  

but the deep love which holdeth us prompt servants of the counsel which governeth the world, maketh assignment here as thou observest.”

- *l' alta carità* ‘the high charity’ → “the deep love”: depth replaces height, “love” renders ‘charity’, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *ci fa serve pronte al consiglio* ‘makes us handmaids ready for the counsel’ → “holdeth us prompt servants of the counsel”: “holdeth” expresses a maintained condition; the feminine noun becomes ungendered, the adjective moves before it, and “of the counsel” attaches to “servants” rather than to the expression of readiness.
- *il mondo governa* ‘the world governs’ → “governeth the world”: the object follows the verb.
- *sorteggia* ‘allots’ → “maketh assignment”: a verb and noun replace the single verb without retaining the association with lots.
- *sì come tu osserve* ‘just as thou observest’ → “as thou observest”: the strengthening adverb has no separate equivalent.

> 73 “Io veggio ben diss' io, sacra lucerna,  
> 74 come libero amore in questa corte  
> 75 basta a seguir la provvidenza eterna:  

“Yea, I perceive, O sacred lamp,” said I, “how free love in this court sufficeth to make follow the eternal providence;

- *Io veggio ben* ‘I see well’ → “Yea, I perceive”: “perceive” replaces ‘see’, and the force of the adverb is transferred to an opening affirmation.
- *diss' io, sacra lucerna* ‘said I, sacred lamp’ → “O sacred lamp,” said I”: the reporting clause moves after the address, and “O” marks the vocative.
- *a seguir* ‘to follow’ → “to make follow”: a causative verb is supplied; love suffices to bring about the following rather than itself being said to follow.
- *provvidenza eterna* ‘providence eternal’ → “eternal providence”: the adjective precedes the noun.

## Lines 76–108 — The Limits of Created Vision

> 76 ma quest' è quel ch' a cerner mi par forte,  

but this it is, which seemeth me hard to discern:

- *quest' è quel* ‘this is that’ → “this it is”: the demonstrative predicate gives way to “it”, placed before the verb.
- *a cerner mi par forte* ‘to discern seems to me hard’ → “seemeth me hard to discern”: the infinitive moves to the end, after the adjective, and the dative pronoun follows the verb.

> 77 perchè predestinata fosti sola  
> 78 a questo ufficio tra le tue consorte.”  

Wherefore thou alone amongst thy consorts wast predestined to this office.”

- *predestinata fosti sola* ‘predestined wast thou alone’ → “thou alone … wast predestined”: the participle follows the auxiliary, while “alone” goes with the subject before the verb.
- *a questo ufficio tra le tue consorte* ‘to this office amongst thy consorts’ → “amongst thy consorts wast predestined to this office”: the two prepositional phrases exchange places; the phrase of comparison now immediately follows “alone”.

> 79 Nè venni prima all' ultima parola,  
> 80 che del suo mezzo fece il lume centro,  
> 81 girando sè, come veloce mola.  

Nor had I come to the last word, ere the light made his mid point a centre, and whirled himself like to a swift millstone.

- *venni prima … che* ‘came sooner … than’ → “had I come to the last word, ere”: the simple past becomes a past perfect, inverted after the initial negative; the comparison with *prima* becomes a temporal clause introduced by “ere”.
- *del suo mezzo fece il lume centro* ‘of its middle made the light a centre’ → “the light made his mid point a centre”: the subject precedes the verb; the phrase with *di* becomes a direct object, with “point” making *mezzo* more explicit and “his” treating the light as personal.
- *girando sè* ‘turning itself’ → “and whirled himself”: a gerund becomes a coordinated finite verb; “whirled” is more specific than ‘turning’, and the reflexive is personal.
- *come veloce mola* ‘like a swift millstone’ → “like to a swift millstone”: “to” and the indefinite article are supplied.

> 82 Poi rispose l' amor che v' era dentro:  

Then answered the love that was therein:

- *v' era dentro* ‘there was within’ → “was therein”: the locative pronoun and *dentro*, on opposite sides of the verb, are gathered into one adverb after it.

> 83 “Luce divina sopra me s' appunta,  
> 84 penetrando per questa ond' io m' inventro;  

“The divine light doth focus it on me, piercing into that wherein I am embowelled;

- *Luce divina* ‘light divine’ → “The divine light”: the article is supplied and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *sopra me s' appunta* ‘upon me concentrates itself’ → “doth focus it on me”: the prepositional phrase follows the verb; the reflexive is rendered by “it” referring back to the light, with “doth” carrying the tense.
- *penetrando per questa* ‘penetrating through this’ → “piercing into that”: passage through becomes penetration into, and the near demonstrative becomes “that”.
- *ond' io m' inventro* ‘in which I enclose myself as in a belly’ → “wherein I am embowelled”: the reflexive verb becomes a passive, retaining the bodily image.

> 85 la cui virtù, col mio veder congiunta,  
> 86 mi leva sopra me tanto, ch' io veggio  
> 87 la somma essenza della quale è munta.  

the power whereof, conjoined unto my sight, uplifteth me above myself so far that I perceive the supreme essence whence it is milked.

- *la cui virtù* ‘whose power’ → “the power whereof”: the relative possessive follows its noun.
- *col mio veder congiunta* ‘with my seeing conjoined’ → “conjoined unto my sight”: the participle comes first, ‘with’ becomes “unto”, and the substantivized infinitive becomes the noun “sight”.
- *mi leva sopra me tanto* ‘me lifts above me so much’ → “uplifteth me above myself so far”: the object follows the verb, the second pronoun is explicitly reflexive, and degree is expressed as distance.
- *veggio* ‘see’ → “perceive”: a less specifically visual verb is used.
- *della quale* ‘from which’ → “whence”: an adverb replaces the preposition and relative pronoun.

> 88 Quinci vien l' allegrezza, ond' io fiammeggio;  

Thence cometh the joy wherewith I flame;

- Literal.

> 89 perchè alla vista mia, quant' ella è chiara,  
> 90 la chiarità della fiamma pareggio.  

for to my sight, even as it is clear, the brightness of the flame do I equate.

- *perchè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *alla vista mia* ‘to the sight of mine’ → “to my sight”: the postposed possessive moves before the noun.
- *quant' ella è chiara* ‘in the measure in which it is clear’ → “even as it is clear”: a comparison explicitly of degree becomes one with “as”, reinforced by “even”.
- *pareggio* ‘I match’ → “do I equate”: the lexical verb is rendered by “equate”, with an inverted auxiliary carrying the tense after the fronted complements.

> 91 Ma quell' alma nel ciel che più si schiara,  
> 92 quel Serafin che in Dio più l' occhio ha fisso,  
> 93 alla domanda tua non satisfara;  

But that soul in heaven which is most illuminated, that Seraph who hath his eye most fixed on God, had given no satisfaction to thy question;

- *più si schiara* ‘more brightens itself’ → “is most illuminated”: the comparative has superlative force in context; the reflexive verb becomes passive.
- *in Dio più l' occhio ha fisso* ‘on God more the eye has fixed’ → “hath his eye most fixed on God”: the verb precedes its object, the definite article becomes a possessive, and the phrase naming the object of vision moves to the end; “most” again makes the superlative sense explicit.
- *alla domanda tua* ‘to the question of thine’ → “to thy question”: the possessive precedes its noun, and the whole complement moves after the predicate.
- *non satisfara* ‘would not satisfy’ → “had given no satisfaction”: the old conditional form is rendered by a pluperfect with hypothetical force; the simple verb becomes a verb and noun, with the negative attached to the noun.

> 94 però che sì s' inoltra nell' abisso  
> 95 dell' eterno statuto quel che chiedi,  
> 96 che da ogni creata vista è scisso.  

because so far within the abyss of the eternal statute lieth the thing thou askest, that from all created vision it is cut off.

- *s' inoltra nell' abisso* ‘advances into the abyss’ → “within the abyss of the eternal statute lieth”: reflexive movement becomes a state of lying within; the verb moves after the description of the abyss.
- *quel che chiedi* ‘that which thou askest’ → “the thing thou askest”: the demonstrative becomes a noun with an article, and the object relative is unexpressed.
- *da ogni creata vista* ‘from every created sight’ → “from all created vision”: distributive ‘every’ becomes collective “all”, with “vision” used as a mass noun.

> 97 Ed al mondo mortal, quando tu riedi,  
> 98 questo rapporta, sì che non presuma  
> 99 a tanto segno più mover li piedi.  

And to the mortal world, when thou returnest, take this report, that it presume not more to move its feet to-ward so great a goal.

- *al mondo mortal* ‘to the world mortal’ → “to the mortal world”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *questo rapporta* ‘this report back’ → “take this report”: the object demonstrative becomes a determiner; the Italian verb supplies the English noun, with “take” as the new imperative.
- *sì che* ‘so that’ → “that”: the separate *sì* is not represented.
- *a tanto segno* ‘toward so great a mark’ → “to-ward so great a goal”: the mark is interpreted as a goal, and the phrase moves from before the infinitive to the end.
- *più mover li piedi* ‘any more to move the feet’ → “more to move its feet”: “more” renders the adverb under the preceding negative; the definite article for a body part becomes a possessive.

> 100 La mente che qui luce, in terra fuma;  

The mind which shineth here, on earth doth smoke,

- *qui luce* ‘here shines’ → “shineth here”: the adverb follows the verb.
- *in terra fuma* ‘on earth smokes’ → “on earth doth smoke”: the simple present is expanded with “doth”.

> 101 onde riguarda come può laggiùe  
> 102 quel che non puote, perchè il ciel l' assuma.”  

and therefore think how it should have power there below, which it hath not even though heaven take it to itself.”

- *onde riguarda* ‘whence consider’ → “and therefore think”: the connective becomes a conjunction and an adverb of consequence; the imperative is rendered by “think”.
- *come può laggiùe* ‘how it can down there’ → “how it should have power there below”: the indicative of ability becomes “should have power”, supplying a noun that the following relative can qualify.
- *quel che non puote* ‘that which it cannot do’ → “which it hath not”: instead of a free-standing object expressing what the mind cannot do, the relative refers back to “power”; inability becomes non-possession.
- *perchè il ciel l' assuma* ‘though heaven take it up’ → “even though heaven take it to itself”: Wicksteed takes *perchè* with the subjunctive concessively, reinforces it with “even”, and specifies the destination of the taking.

> 103 Sì mi prescrisser le parole sue,  
> 104 ch' io lasciai la questione, e mi ritrassi  
> 105 a domandarla umilmente chi fue.  

Such limits did his words impose on me, I left the question, and restrained me to demanding humbly who himself was.

- *Sì mi prescrisser le parole sue* ‘so set bounds to me the words of his’ → “Such limits did his words impose on me”: the degree adverb becomes a determiner of the supplied noun “limits”; the postposed subject, with possessive now before the noun, moves between auxiliary and infinitive, and the dative becomes a final prepositional phrase.
- *ch' io lasciai la questione* ‘that I left the question’ → “I left the question”: the conjunction marking the result of *sì* is omitted.
- *mi ritrassi* ‘myself I drew back’ → “restrained me”: withdrawal is rendered as self-restraint, and the object follows the verb.
- *a domandarla umilmente* ‘to ask her humbly’ → “to demanding humbly”: the Italian infinitive becomes a gerund after prepositional “to”; the object pronoun referring to the soul is omitted.
- *chi fue* ‘who he was’ → “who himself was”: the subject is made emphatic.

> 106 “Tra due liti d' Italia surgon sassi,  
> 107 e non molto distanti alla tua patria,  
> 108 tanto che i tuoni assai suonan più bassi,  

“’Twixt the two shores of Italy crags arise, and not far distant from thy fatherland, so high the thunders sound far lower down,

- *Tra due liti* ‘between two shores’ → “’Twixt the two shores”: the definite article is supplied.
- *surgon sassi* ‘rise rocks’ → “crags arise”: the subject precedes the verb, and “crags” is more specific than ‘rocks’.
- *molto distanti* ‘much distant’ → “far distant”: degree is expressed by the adverb of distance.
- *tanto che* ‘to such a degree that’ → “so high”: the dimension of height is supplied, while the result clause follows without an explicit ‘that’.
- *assai suonan più bassi* ‘much sound lower’ → “sound far lower down”: the intensifier moves next to the comparative after the verb; “down” makes the spatial sense of ‘lower’ explicit.

## Lines 109–142 — Peter Damian’s Rebuke and the Thunderous Cry

> 109 e fanno un gibbo, che si chiama Catria,  
> 110 di sotto al quale è consecrato un ermo,  
> 111 che suol esser disposto a sola latria.”  

and make a hump whose name is Catria, ’neath which a hermitage is consecrate, which erst was given only unto prayer.”

- *che si chiama Catria* ‘which is called Catria’ → “whose name is Catria”: the pronominal verb of naming becomes a possessive relative with a noun and copula.
- *di sotto al quale* ‘underneath which’ → “’neath which”: one English preposition does the work of the compound Italian expression.
- *è consecrato un ermo* ‘is consecrated a hermitage’ → “a hermitage is consecrate”: the subject of the passive moves before the verb.
- *suol esser disposto* ‘is wont to be devoted’ → “erst was given”: the present verb of custom becomes an adverb of former time with a past passive; ‘devoted’ becomes “given”.
- *a sola latria* ‘to divine worship alone’ → “only unto prayer”: “only” moves before the preposition, and “prayer” is narrower than ‘divine worship’.

> 112 Così ricominciommi il terzo sermo,  
> 113 e poi, continuando, disse: “Quivi  
> 114 al servigio di Dio mi fei sì fermo,  

So he began to me again the third discourse, and then continuing, said: “There in God’s service I became so rooted

- *ricominciommi* ‘began again to me’ → “he began to me again”: the sense of the prefix is expressed by a separate adverb, placed after the rendering of the attached pronoun.
- *poi, continuando, disse* ‘then, continuing, he said’ → “then continuing, said”: the reporting verb is coordinated with “began” under its earlier subject rather than given a subject of its own.
- *al servigio di Dio* ‘to the service of God’ → “in God’s service”: the preposition of attachment becomes one of position, and the possessor precedes the noun.
- *mi fei sì fermo* ‘I made myself so steadfast’ → “I became so rooted”: the reflexive construction becomes a verb of becoming, and steadfastness is expressed by the figure of taking root.

> 115 che pur con cibi di liquor d' ulivi,  
> 116 lievemente passava caldi e gieli,  
> 117 contento nei pensier contemplativi.  

that only with olive-juice viands I lightly traversed heat and cold, satisfied in thoughts contemplative.

- *che pur con cibi di liquor d' ulivi* ‘that only with foods of juice of olive trees’ → “that only with olive-juice viands”: the two postposed complements are compressed into a compound modifier before “viands”; the trees are represented by the name of their fruit.
- *passava caldi e gieli* ‘I used to pass through heats and frosts’ → “I lightly traversed heat and cold”: the imperfect becomes a simple past, and the plural nouns become mass nouns, with the more general “cold” for ‘frosts’.
- *contento nei pensier contemplativi* ‘content in the thoughts contemplative’ → “satisfied in thoughts contemplative”: “satisfied” replaces ‘content’, and the definite article is dropped.

> 118 Render solea quel chiostro a questi cieli  
> 119 fertilemente, ed ora è fatto vano,  
> 120 sì che tosto convien che si riveli.  

That cloister erst bore ample fruit unto these heavens, and is now become so futile, that ere long needs must it be revealed.

- *Render solea quel chiostro a questi cieli fertilemente* ‘yield was wont that cloister to these heavens fruitfully’ → “That cloister erst bore ample fruit unto these heavens”: the subject moves to the front; customary action becomes former action. The infinitive and the final adverb are recast together as “bore ample fruit”, before the recipient rather than on either side of it.
- *ed ora è fatto vano, sì che* ‘and now has become vain, so that’ → “and is now become so futile, that”: “now” follows the auxiliary; ‘vain’ becomes “futile”, and “so” is attached to the adjective rather than to the result clause.
- *tosto convien che si riveli* ‘soon it is necessary that it be revealed’ → “ere long needs must it be revealed”: ‘soon’ becomes an adverbial phrase; the impersonal verb and its subjunctive clause become a single clause with an emphatic modal and a passive infinitive.

> 121 In quel loco fu' io Pier Damiano;  
> 122 e Pietro peccator fui nella casa  
> 123 di Nostra Donna in sul lito Adriano.  

I, Peter of Damian, was in that same place; and I, Peter the Sinner, was in the house of Our Lady on the Adriatic shore.

- *In quel loco fu' io Pier Damiano* ‘in that place was I Peter Damian’ → “I, Peter of Damian, was in that same place”: the name becomes an apposition to the initial subject, with “of” making the relation between the names explicit; the place phrase moves to the end and gains “same”.
- *e Pietro peccator fui* ‘and Peter sinner was I’ → “and I, Peter the Sinner, was”: the name and epithet are again set in apposition to the subject, now placed first, and the epithet acquires an article.
- *in sul lito Adriano* ‘upon the shore Adriatic’ → “on the Adriatic shore”: the reinforced preposition is rendered by “on”, and the adjective precedes the noun.

> 124 Poca vita mortal m' era rimasa,  
> 125 quando fui chiesto e tratto a quel cappello,  
> 126 che pur di male in peggio si travasa.  

Little of mortal life was left to me when I was called and drawn unto the hat which doth but change from bad receptacle to worse.

- *Poca vita mortal* ‘little mortal life’ → “Little of mortal life”: the quantifier takes a partitive complement rather than modifying ‘life’ directly, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *m' era rimasa* ‘to me had remained’ → “was left to me”: the pluperfect of ‘remain’ becomes a passive expressing the resulting state, with the recipient after the verb.
- *fui chiesto* ‘I was asked for’ → “I was called”: the passive of asking becomes one of summoning.
- *a quel cappello* ‘to that hat’ → “unto the hat”: the demonstrative becomes a definite article.
- *pur di male in peggio si travasa* ‘only from bad into worse is poured from vessel to vessel’ → “doth but change from bad receptacle to worse”: the pronominal verb of transfer is replaced by “change”, moved before the complements; the vessel implicit in the verb is expressed as “receptacle” after “bad”.

> 127 Venne Cephas, e venne il gran vasello  
> 128 dello Spirito Santo, magri e scalzi,  
> 129 prendendo il cibo di qualunque ostello.  

Cephas came, and the great vessel of the Holy Spirit came, lean and unshod, taking their food from every hostelry.

- *Venne Cephas* ‘came Cephas’ → “Cephas came”: the subject precedes the verb.
- *e venne il gran vasello dello Spirito Santo* ‘and came the great vessel of the Spirit Holy’ → “and the great vessel of the Holy Spirit came”: the verb moves after the whole subject, and “Holy” precedes “Spirit”.
- *scalzi* ‘barefoot’ → “unshod”: the condition is expressed by the absence of shoes rather than by bare feet.
- *il cibo* ‘the food’ → “their food”: a possessive replaces the article.
- *di qualunque ostello* ‘from whatever lodging’ → “from every hostelry”: the indefinite of unrestricted choice becomes distributive, and “hostelry” gives ‘lodging’ the more specific sense of an inn.

> 130 Or voglion quinci e quindi chi rincalzi  
> 131 li moderni pastori, e chi li meni,  
> 132 tanto son gravi, e chi di retro gli alzi.  

Now the modern pastors must needs be buttressed on this side and on that, and have one to lead them on, so heavy are they, and one to hoist behind.

- *voglion quinci e quindi chi rincalzi li moderni pastori* ‘want on this side and on that someone who will prop them up, the modern pastors’ → “the modern pastors must needs be buttressed on this side and on that”: the subject moves from after the relative clause to before the main verb. Wanting someone to act becomes a necessity expressed in the passive; the person doing the propping disappears, and the indications of place follow the verb.
- *e chi li meni* ‘and someone who will lead them’ → “and have one to lead them on”: “have” is supplied to carry the construction on after the passive; the relative with a subjunctive becomes a pronoun with an infinitive, and “on” is added.
- *tanto son gravi* ‘so much are they heavy’ → “so heavy are they”: the adjective moves next to its degree modifier, before the verb.
- *e chi di retro gli alzi* ‘and someone who from behind will lift them’ → “and one to hoist behind”: the relative with a subjunctive again becomes a pronoun with an infinitive; the indication of place follows the verb, and its object ‘them’ is left understood.

> 133 Copron dei manti loro i palafreni,  
> 134 sì che due bestie van sott' una pelle:  
> 135 o pazienza, che tanto sostieni!”  

With their mantles they o’erspread their palfreys, so that two beasts travel beneath one hide; O patience, that so much endureth!”

- *Copron dei manti loro i palafreni* ‘they cover with the mantles of theirs the palfreys’ → “With their mantles they o’erspread their palfreys”: the instrumental phrase moves before the verb and its possessive before the noun; ‘cover’ becomes “o’erspread”, and a possessive replaces the article before ‘palfreys’.
- *van sott' una pelle* ‘go under one skin’ → “travel beneath one hide”: “travel” specifies the movement, and “hide” gives ‘skin’ its animal sense.
- *sostieni* ‘thou endurest’ → “endureth”: the Italian addresses patience in the second person; the English relative clause uses a third-person verb.

> 136 A questa voce vid' io più fiammelle  
> 137 di grado in grado scendere e girarsi,  
> 138 ed ogni giro le facea più belle.  

At this voice I saw more flames from step to step descend and whirl, and every whirl made them more beauteous.

- *vid' io* ‘saw I’ → “I saw”: the expressed subject moves before the verb.
- *più fiammelle* ‘more little flames’ → “more flames”: the diminutive is lost.
- *di grado in grado* ‘from step into step’ → “from step to step”: the repeated-noun construction takes the idiomatic English prepositions of progression.
- *girarsi* ‘turn themselves about’ → “whirl”: the reflexive becomes an intransitive verb suggesting rapid rotation.
- *ogni giro* ‘every turn’ → “every whirl”: the noun takes up the translator’s choice of verb.
- *le facea più belle* ‘them it was making more beautiful’ → “made them more beauteous”: the object follows the verb, and the imperfect of repeated action becomes a simple past.

> 139 Dintorno a questa vennero, e fermarsi,  

Around this one they came and stayed themselves

- *a questa* ‘to this one’ → “this one”: the Italian prepositional complement of *Dintorno* becomes the direct complement of “Around”.
- *fermarsi* ‘they stopped’ → “stayed themselves”: the contracted reflexive preterite is rendered with an explicitly reflexive use of “stayed”.

> 140 e fero un grido di sì alto suono,  
> 141 che non potrebbe qui assimigliarsi;  

and raised a cry of so deep sound that here it may not find similitude;

- *fero un grido* ‘they made a cry’ → “raised a cry”: the English changes the verb used with ‘cry’.
- *di sì alto suono* ‘of so high a sound’ → “of so deep sound”: “deep” interprets the force of *alto* rather than reproducing its adjective of height.
- *non potrebbe qui assimigliarsi* ‘it could not here be likened’ → “here it may not find similitude”: the place adverb moves first, conditional ability becomes “may not”, and the pronominal passive becomes an active verb with a noun for its object.

> 142 nè io lo intesi, sì mi vinse il tuono.  

nor did I understand it, so vanquished me the thunder.

- *nè io lo intesi* ‘nor I it understood’ → “nor did I understand it”: negative inversion requires an auxiliary before the subject, and the object pronoun follows the main verb.
- *sì mi vinse il tuono* ‘so me vanquished the thunder’ → “so vanquished me the thunder”: the object pronoun moves after the verb, while the subject remains last.

## Summary — Shifts of Order and Emphasis

Wicksteed often keeps Dante’s vocabulary while changing the order in which the words reach the reader. Subjects and objects move to their usual English positions: *surgon sassi* becomes “crags arise”, and *il mondo governa* becomes “governeth the world”. Possessives likewise move forward, as in *gli occhi miei*, “mine eyes”. Two routine adjustments generally go unmarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where Italian indicates the subject by the verb ending alone, and omits the article before a possessive, as in *la mia celeste scorta*, “my heavenly guide”.

Changes of construction can shift the emphasis even where the sense remains close. The impersonal act of climbing in *quanto più si sale* becomes a matter of height in “the higher the ascent”; similarly, *qui non si canta*, ‘here one does not sing’, becomes “here is no song”. Wicksteed sometimes supplies a word to make a relation explicit: “like” turns the identification in *sarebbe fronda* into a comparison. Elsewhere a word disappears because its sense is already present: *scender giuso* needs no separate ‘down’ in “descend upon the steps”. The reverse happens with *rifissi*, whose prefix reappears as “again” at the end of the phrase.

Individual choices range from a narrowing of sense, as when *latria*, ‘divine worship’, becomes “prayer”, to a change of image, as when *ferve*, ‘seethes’, becomes “doth burn”. Yet Wicksteed also preserves turns of phrase that a freer translation might smooth away. He retains the repeated mirror image of *specchi* and *specchio* in “mirrors” and “mirror”, and keeps both the balance and the verbs of *luce* and *fuma* in “shineth here” and “on earth doth smoke”. At the close he even leaves “the thunder” in the emphatic final position of *il tuono*.
