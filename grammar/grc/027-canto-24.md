# Inferno 24

## Lines 1–21 — The Frost and the Guide’s Brow

> 1 In quella parte del giovinetto anno,  
> 2 che il sole i crin sotto l' Aquario tempra  
> 3 e già le notti al mezzo dì sen vanno,  

In that part of the youthful year, when the Sun tempers his locks beneath Aquarius, and the nights already wane towards half the day,

> 1 Καθ᾽ ἣν ἐποχὴν τοῦ νεάζοντος ἔτους  
> (In that season of the youthful year in which)
>
> 2 Ἥλιος κόμην ἐν Ὑδροχόῳ λούει,  
> (The sun bathes his locks in Aquarius,)
>
> 3 Ἡ δὲ νὺξ ῥέπει πρὸς τὴν ἰσημερίαν,  
> (And the night inclines toward the equinox,)

- *Καθ᾽ ἣν ἐποχήν* ‘in which season’ draws *In quella parte … che* into one relative phrase: there is no separate demonstrative answering to *quella*. *ἣν* is the feminine accusative singular of *ὅς*, agreeing with *ἐποχήν* under temporal *κατά*. In *καθ᾽* the final vowel of *κατά* is elided and the *τ* aspirated before the rough breathing of *ἣν*.
- *τοῦ νεάζοντος ἔτους* ‘of the year that is still young’ depends on *ἐποχήν*. For the adjective *giovinetto*, Musurus uses the present active participle of *νεάζω*, genitive neuter singular in attributive position with *ἔτους* (from *ἔτος*).
- *Ἥλιος κόμην ἐν Ὑδροχόῳ λούει* keeps the order subject–object–prepositional phrase–verb. The Sun’s name has no article; *κόμην* is a collective singular, ‘hair’, for plural *i crin*, with possession understood. *ἐν* with the dative of *Ὑδροχόος* replaces *sotto*; *λούει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *λούω*) gives *tempra* the more specific sense ‘bathes’.
- *Ἡ δὲ νὺξ ῥέπει πρὸς τὴν ἰσημερίαν* ‘and the night inclines toward equality of day and night’: postpositive *δέ* coordinates the second clause within the temporal frame. Generic singular *νύξ* replaces *le notti*, and *ῥέπει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *ῥέπω*) replaces the pronominal motion verb *sen vanno*. *πρός* with the accusative gives the point approached: *ἰσημερία* interprets *al mezzo dì* in terms of the equinox. *già* has no equivalent.

> 4 quando la brina in su la terra assempra  
> 5 l' imagine di sua sorella bianca,  
> 6 ma poco dura alla sua penna tempra,  

when the hoar-frost copies his white sister's image on the ground, but short while lasts the temper of his pen,

> 4 Ἡ πάχνη δ᾽ ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς ἀπομιμεῖται  
> (And the hoarfrost upon the earth imitates)
>
> 5 Τῆς λευκῆς αὑτῆς ἀδελφῆς τὴν εἰκόνα,  
> (The likeness of its white sister,)
>
> 6 Μικρὸν τὴν αὐτὴν χροιὰν διατηροῦσα,  
> (Preserving for a little while the same hue,)

- *Ἡ πάχνη δ᾽* ‘and the frost’ continues the coordination with elided *δέ* rather than reopening the temporal construction with a word for *quando*. *ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς* is local *ἐπί* with the genitive, ‘on the surface of the earth’. *ἀπομιμεῖται* is the contracted 3rd sg. present middle indicative of *ἀπομιμέομαι*, ‘copies’, with active sense and the accusative object *τὴν εἰκόνα*.
- *Τῆς λευκῆς αὑτῆς ἀδελφῆς τὴν εἰκόνα* puts the entire possessive genitive before ‘the image’, reversing *l’ imagine di sua sorella bianca*. Within it *λευκῆς* qualifies *ἀδελφῆς*; *αὑτῆς*, with rough breathing, is the contracted feminine genitive singular of the reflexive *ἑαυτῆς*, ‘her own’, referring to feminine *πάχνη*.
- *Μικρὸν τὴν αὐτὴν χροιὰν διατηροῦσα* ‘retaining the same hue for a short while’ replaces the finite adversative *ma poco dura*. *Μικρόν* is an accusative of duration used adverbially; *διατηροῦσα* is a present active participle of *διατηρέω*, nominative feminine singular agreeing with *πάχνη*. The limitation is conveyed by *μικρόν*, without a conjunction for *ma*. Attributive *τὴν αὐτήν* means ‘the same’, not ‘her’; the hue is what lasts, with neither *penna* nor the second *tempra* reproduced.

> 7 lo villanello, a cui la roba manca,  
> 8 si leva e guarda, e vede la campagna  
> 9 biancheggiar tutta, ond' ei si batte l' anca;  

the peasant, whose fodder fails, rises, and looks, and sees the fields all white; whereat he smites his thigh,

> 7 Ὁ χωρίτης, ᾧ τὰ πρὸς χρείαν ἐλλείπει,  
> (The peasant, who lacks what is needful,)
>
> 8 Ἐγειρόμενος ἀτενίζει καὶ βλέπει  
> (Rising up, gazes intently and sees)
>
> 9 Τὴν πεδιάδα λευκήν, καὶ μηροὺς πλήττει·  
> (The plain white, and smites his thighs;)

- *Ὁ χωρίτης* ‘the countryman’ introduces the main subject after the temporal clauses, without the diminutive of *villanello*. *ᾧ τὰ πρὸς χρείαν ἐλλείπει* keeps the construction of *a cui la roba manca*: *ᾧ* (dative masculine singular of *ὅς*) names the person to whom something is lacking. *τὰ πρὸς χρείαν*, literally ‘the things for need’, makes an articular neuter plural out of a prepositional phrase; unlike “fodder”, it does not specify the kind of provisions. Its verb *ἐλλείπει* (present active indicative of *ἐλλείπω*) is singular, as regularly with a neuter plural subject.
- *Ἐγειρόμενος* (present middle/passive participle of *ἐγείρω*, nominative masculine singular, here intransitive ‘getting up’) subordinates *si leva* to the coordinated presents *ἀτενίζει καὶ βλέπει* (3rd sg. active indicatives of *ἀτενίζω* and *βλέπω*). The first means ‘looks intently’, more specific than *guarda*.
- *Τὴν πεδιάδα λευκήν* substitutes an object and predicate adjective, ‘the plain [as] white’, for *la campagna biancheggiar*, an object with an infinitive of perception. *λευκήν* is outside the article–noun group, not an attributive ‘the white plain’; *tutta* is unrepresented. *καὶ μηροὺς πλήττει* ‘and he strikes [his] thighs’ replaces consequential *ond’ ei* with simple coordination and singular *l’ anca* with an accusative plural, not a dual. The active *πλήττει* (3rd sg. present indicative of *πλήττω*, Attic *-ττ-*) needs no equivalent of possessive *si*.

> 10 ritorna in casa, e qua e là si lagna,  
> 11 come il tapin che non sa che si faccia,  

goes back into the house, and to and fro laments like a poor wight who knows not what to do;

> 10 Ἐπανακάμψας δ᾽ οἴκαδε σχετλιάζει,  
> (And returning homeward, he laments,)
>
> 11 Ὡς δυστυχής τις οὐκ ἔχων τί ποιήσει·  
> (Like some wretch not knowing what he shall do;)

- *Ἐπανακάμψας δ᾽ οἴκαδε σχετλιάζει* ‘and having turned back home, he laments’ replaces *ritorna … e … si lagna* with an anterior aorist active participle (*ἐπανακάμψας*, nominative masculine singular of *ἐπανακάμπτω*) and one finite verb (*σχετλιάζει*, 3rd sg. present active indicative of *σχετλιάζω*). Elided postpositive *δέ* links this to the preceding action. Directional *οἴκαδε* already means ‘homeward’; there is nothing for *qua e là*.
- *Ὡς δυστυχής τις* ‘like some unfortunate person’ uses comparative *ὡς* and an adjective made indefinite by enclitic *τις* (nominative singular), rather than the definite substantive *il tapin*. *οὐκ ἔχων τί ποιήσει* replaces the relative *che non sa* with a circumstantial present active participle of *ἔχω*, agreeing with *τις*: literally ‘not having [an answer to] what he will do’. *τί* is interrogative, the accusative object of *ποιήσει* (3rd sg. future active indicative of *ποιέω*). The indirect question thus has a future indicative where *che si faccia* has a subjunctive; the Greek is not an infinitive meaning simply ‘to do’.

> 12 poi riede e la speranza ringavagna,  
> 13 veggendo il mondo aver cangiata faccia  

then comes out again, and recovers hope, observing how the world has changed its face

> 12 Εἶτ᾽ ἀνακύψας ἐλπίδ᾽ ἀναλαμβάνει,  
> (Then looking up again, he recovers hope,)
>
> 13 Βλέπων τὸν κόσμον ὄψιν μεταβαλόντα  
> (Seeing that the world has changed its face)

- *Εἶτ᾽ ἀνακύψας* ‘then having raised his head’ has elided *εἶτα* and the nominative masculine singular aorist active participle of *ἀνακύπτω*. This changes both the sense and the rank of finite *riede*: the upward look, rather than the return outside, is preliminary to the main verb. *ἐλπίδ᾽ ἀναλαμβάνει* elides accusative singular *ἐλπίδα* before *ἀναλαμβάνει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *ἀναλαμβάνω*); ‘takes up hope again’ conveys *la speranza ringavagna* without its basket metaphor.
- *Βλέπων* (present active participle of *βλέπω*, nominative masculine singular) closely answers to the gerund *veggendo*. After this verb of perception, *τὸν κόσμον … μεταβαλόντα* is an accusative with a supplementary participle, in place of *il mondo aver cangiata*; *μεταβαλόντα* is the second aorist active participle of *μεταβάλλω*, accusative masculine singular agreeing with *κόσμον*. The change is viewed as accomplished when seen. *ὄψιν* ‘appearance’ is the participle’s accusative object, without article or possessive, as with *faccia*.

> 14 in poco d' ora, e prende suo vincastro,  
> 15 e fuor le pecorelle a pascer caccia :  

in little time; and takes his staff, and chases forth his lambs to feed:

> 14 Ἐν βραχεῖ χρόνῳ, καὶ τὴν ποιμνικὴν ῥάβδον  
> (In a short time, and his shepherd's staff)
>
> 15 Λαβὼν ἐκβαίνει πρὸς νομὴν τῶν προβάτων·  
> (Taking, he goes out to pasture the sheep:)

- *Ἐν βραχεῖ χρόνῳ* completes *μεταβαλόντα*, retaining *in poco d’ ora* as a prepositional phrase: *βραχεῖ* is the dative masculine singular of *βραχύς*, qualifying *χρόνῳ* under temporal *ἐν*.
- *καὶ τὴν ποιμνικὴν ῥάβδον λαβών* ‘and having taken the shepherd’s staff’ transposes *prende* after its object and subordinates it as *λαβών*, nominative masculine singular second aorist active participle of *λαμβάνω*. The attributive adjective *ποιμνικήν* specifies the function of the *vincastro*; the article leaves possession implicit instead of translating *suo*.
- *ἐκβαίνει πρὸς νομὴν τῶν προβάτων* ‘he goes out for the pasturing of the sheep’ makes the peasant’s own motion the finite action (*ἐκβαίνει*, 3rd sg. present active indicative of *ἐκβαίνω*). The outward direction of *fuor* survives in *ἐκ-*, but not the transitive force of *caccia*. Purpose is expressed by *πρός* with the accusative noun *νομήν*, not by an infinitive like *a pascer*; *τῶν προβάτων* (genitive plural of *πρόβατον*) depends on that noun rather than serving as object of the verb. There is no diminutive for *pecorelle*.

> 16 così mi fece sbigottir lo mastro  
> 17 quand' io gli vidi sì turbar la fronte,  

thus the Master made me despond, when I saw his brow so troubled;

> 16 Οὕτω με κατέπληξεν οὑμὸς Προστάτης  
> (Even so my Master dismayed me)
>
> 17 Τὴν ὄψιν ἰδόντ᾽ αὐτοῦ τεταραγμένην·  
> (When I saw his face troubled;)

- *Οὕτω με κατέπληξεν οὑμὸς Προστάτης* keeps the initial *così*, the weak object pronoun, and the delayed subject of *così mi fece sbigottir lo mastro*. One transitive verb replaces the causative and infinitive: *κατέπληξεν* ‘dismayed’ is the 3rd sg. first aorist active indicative of *καταπλήττω*, with augment after the preverb and movable *-ν*. *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, ‘my’; the possessive is supplied, and *Προστάτης* means ‘protector’ rather than literally ‘master’.
- *Τὴν ὄψιν ἰδόντ᾽ αὐτοῦ τεταραγμένην* renders temporal *quand’ io … vidi* by *ἰδόντ᾽*, elided *ἰδόντα* (second aorist active participle of *ὁράω*). Its accusative masculine singular agrees with *με*, not with the nominative *Προστάτης*: ‘[me] on seeing’. Within that construction *τὴν ὄψιν … αὐτοῦ* is ‘his face’ rather than *la fronte*; the possessive genitive replaces Italian *gli*. *τεταραγμένην* (perfect middle/passive participle of *ταράσσω*, accusative feminine singular) predicates a resulting state of *ὄψιν*, rather than rendering *turbar* with an infinitive. The degree word *sì* is not separately expressed.

> 18 e così tosto al mal giunse lo impiastro.  

and thus quickly to the sore the plaster came.

> 18 Οὕτω τε τάχος ἐπῆλθ᾽ ἡ θεραπεία.  
> (And just as swiftly came the remedy.)

- *Οὕτω τε τάχος* repeats *οὕτω* for the second *così*; enclitic *τε* links the two statements, and *τάχος*, a neuter singular accusative used adverbially, gives *tosto* as ‘swiftly’. *ἐπῆλθ᾽* is elided *ἐπῆλθε*, 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *ἐπέρχομαι*, with augmented *ἦλθε* after the preverb and aspiration of the final consonant before rough-breathed *ἡ*. As in *giunse lo impiastro*, the subject follows the verb, but *ἡ θεραπεία* generalizes the plaster to ‘the treatment’; there is no complement corresponding to *al mal*.

> 19 Chè, come noi venimmo al guasto ponte,  
> 20 lo duca a me si volse con quel piglio  
> 21 dolce, ch' io vidi prima a piè del monte.  

For when we reached the shattered bridge, my Guide turned to me with that sweet aspect which I saw [first] at the foot of the mountain.

> 19 Ὡς γὰρ εἰς τὸ ῥῆγμ᾽ ἤλθομεν τῆς γεφύρας,  
> (For when we came to the breach of the bridge,)
>
> 20 Ἡγήτωρ πρός μ᾽ ἔτρεψε τὸ γλυκὺ βλέμμα,  
> (My Guide turned toward me that sweet gaze,)
>
> 21 Ὃ πρῶτον εἶδον παρὰ πόδας τοῦ λόφου.  
> (Which I first saw at the foot of the hill.)

- *Ὡς γάρ* distributes *Chè, come* between temporal *ὡς* ‘when’ and explanatory, necessarily postpositive *γάρ* ‘for’. *εἰς τὸ ῥῆγμ᾽ ἤλθομεν τῆς γεφύρας* ‘we came to the breach of the bridge’ translates the damage denoted by adjectival *guasto* with a noun: *ῥῆγμ᾽* is elided *ῥῆγμα*, accusative neuter singular after directional *εἰς*, with its dependent genitive *τῆς γεφύρας* delayed until after the verb. *ἤλθομεν* is the 1st pl. second aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι*; the ending suffices for *noi*.
- *Ἡγήτωρ πρός μ᾽ ἔτρεψε τὸ γλυκὺ βλέμμα* ‘the leader turned the sweet gaze toward me’ uses anarthrous nominative *ἡγήτωρ* for *lo duca*, and *πρός* with elided accusative *με* for *a me*. *ἔτρεψε* (3rd sg. aorist active indicative of *τρέπω*) is transitive, not reflexive like *si volse*. The accompanying *con quel piglio dolce* consequently becomes the direct object *τὸ γλυκὺ βλέμμα*: the adjective is attributive, the look specifically a ‘gaze’, and the article stands in place of the demonstrative.
- *Ὃ πρῶτον εἶδον* closely follows *ch’ io vidi prima*: *ὅ* is accusative as object of *εἶδον*, neuter singular after *βλέμμα*, and *πρῶτον* is the adverbial neuter accusative ‘first’. *εἶδον* is the augmented second aorist active indicative used with *ὁράω*, here 1st sg. rather than the formally identical 3rd pl.; *io* has no independent equivalent. *παρὰ πόδας τοῦ λόφου* ‘at the feet of the hill’ retains the figurative body part of *a piè del monte* but uses accusative plural *πόδας* (from *πούς*) with local *παρά* and the dependent genitive of *λόφος*, ‘hill’.

## Lines 22–54 — The Climb and the Call to Rise

> 22 Le braccia aperse, dopo alcun consiglio  
> 23 eletto seco, riguardando prima  
> 24 ben la ruina, e diedemi di piglio.  

He opened his arms after having chosen some plan within himself, first looking well at the ruin, and took hold of me.

> 22 Τὰς ἀγκάλας ἤνοιξε μετὰ βραχεῖαν  
> (He opened his arms after brief)
>
> 23 Καθ᾽ αὑτὸν σκέψιν, προσεπιβλέπων πρῶτον  
> (Reflection within himself, first examining)
>
> 24 Καλῶς τὸν κρημνόν· εἶτα δ᾽ ἐλάβετό μου.  
> (Well the crag; and then he took hold of me.)

- *Τὰς ἀγκάλας ἤνοιξε* keeps the object-first order of *Le braccia aperse*. The article does the work of a possessive with the body part; *ἀγκάλας* (acc. pl. of *ἀγκάλη*) suggests arms extended to receive someone. *ἤνοιξε* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀνοίγω*.
- *μετὰ βραχεῖαν καθ᾽ αὑτὸν σκέψιν* means ‘after a brief consideration by himself’. *μετά* takes the accusative of the event after which he acts; *βραχεῖαν* (acc. sg. fem. of *βραχύς*) agrees across the line break with *σκέψιν*. Musurus makes the deliberation brief rather than indefinite (*alcun consiglio*) and does not separately render *eletto*. *καθ᾽ αὑτόν* renders *seco*: *κατά* loses its final vowel and its *τ* is aspirated before the rough breathing of the contracted reflexive *αὑτόν*.
- *προσεπιβλέπων πρῶτον καλῶς τὸν κρημνόν* matches the gerund *riguardando* with a present active participle (nom. sg. masc. of *προσεπιβλέπω*), and *prima ben* with the two adverbs in the same order. *κρημνόν* denotes a steep drop or crag rather than a mass of fallen material (*ruina*). *εἶτα δ᾽ ἐλάβετό μου* makes the succession explicit: ‘and then he took hold of me’. *ἐλάβετο* is 3rd sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *λαμβάνω*; the middle in this sense governs the genitive *μου*, whose enclisis accounts for the additional accent on *ἐλάβετό*.

> 25 E come quei che adopera ed estima,  
> 26 che sempre par che innanzi si provveggia:  

And as one who works, and calculates, always seeming to provide beforehand:

> 25 Ὡς δ᾽ ὅτ᾽ ἐν ταὐτῷ σκέπτεταί τις καὶ πράττει,  
> (And as one who at the same time considers and acts,)
>
> 26 Κᾀεὶ προνοεῖ τῶν μελλόντων ἐγκαίρως,  
> (And always provides in good time for what is to come,)

- *Ὡς δ᾽ ὅτ᾽* is the poetic simile opening ‘and as when’ (*δέ*, *ὅτε*, both elided), answered by *Οὕτως* in 27. Instead of the demonstrative and relative *quei che*, Greek has a finite clause with indefinite *τις*, ‘someone’, as subject. *ἐν ταὐτῷ* (*ταὐτῷ* by crasis from *τῷ αὐτῷ*) means ‘at the same time’; it expressly joins the two operations. *σκέπτεταί τις καὶ πράττει* reverses *adopera ed estima*: ‘someone considers and acts’ (3rd sg. pres. mid. of *σκέπτομαι* and pres. act. of *πράττω*).
- *Κᾀεί* is crasis of *καὶ ἀεί*, ‘and always’. *προνοεῖ* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind., contracted from *προνοέω*) is a third coordinated verb: what in *par che … si provveggia* appears to be done is asserted outright. The genitive *τῶν μελλόντων* (substantivized pres. act. participle of *μέλλω*, neut. pl.) supplies the object of forethought, ‘what is to come’; *ἐγκαίρως* gives *innanzi* the sense ‘in good time’.

> 27 così, levando me su ver la cima  
> 28 d’un ronchion, avvisava un’ altra scheggia,  

so, lifting me up towards the top of one big block, he looked out another splinter,

> 27 Οὕτως ὑψώσας ἄνω με πρὸς τὴν ἄκραν  
> (So, lifting me up toward the top)
>
> 28 Πέτρας, ἐξελέξατο δι᾽ αὑτὸν ἄλλην  
> (Of one rock, he chose for himself another,)

- *Οὕτως ὑψώσας ἄνω με* resumes the comparison, but the aorist active participle *ὑψώσας* (nom. sg. masc. of *ὑψόω*) presents the lift as a single act preliminary to the choice, rather than as the ongoing *levando*. *ἄνω* reinforces the upward direction as does *su*. *πρὸς τὴν ἄκραν πέτρας* has *πρός* with the accusative of direction and an anarthrous dependent genitive, ‘toward the top of a rock’.
- *ἐξελέξατο δι᾽ αὑτὸν ἄλλην* means ‘he chose another for his own sake’. The selection is completed (*ἐξελέξατο*, 3rd sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἐκλέγω*) rather than observed in progress (*avvisava*, imperfect). *δι᾽* is elided *διά* with the accusative reflexive, an addition to the Italian. *ἄλλην* (acc. sg. fem.) implies *πέτραν*: the distinction between *ronchion* and *scheggia* disappears.

> 29 dicendo: “Sopra quella poi t’ aggrappa;  
> 30 ma tenta pria s’ è tal ch’ ella ti reggia.”  

saying: “Now clamber over that, but try first if it will carry thee.”

> 29 Λέγων· “ Ἀντέχου σθεναρῶς πέτρας τῆσδε·  
> (Saying: “Cling firmly to this rock;)
>
> 30 „ Ἀλλ᾽ ὅρα πρῶτον, εἰ δύναταί σε φέρειν.”  
> („ But see first whether it can bear you.”)

- *Λέγων* (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *λέγω*) follows *dicendo*. *Ἀντέχου* (2nd sg. pres. mid. imperative of *ἀντέχομαι*) is ‘hold fast to’, governing the genitive *πέτρας τῆσδε*. Greek names the rock again and points to ‘this’ one (gen. sg. fem. of *ὅδε*) rather than *quella*. The adverb *σθεναρῶς*, ‘firmly’, specifies the manner of gripping, not the sequence conveyed by *poi*.
- *Ἀλλ᾽ ὅρα πρῶτον* has elided *ἀλλά* and the contracted 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *ὁράω*: ‘but see first’, rather than literally ‘test’. *εἰ δύναταί σε φέρειν* is an indirect yes-or-no question with present indicative *δύναται* (3rd sg. of *δύναμαι*) and complementary present active infinitive *φέρειν*. Thus *s’ è tal ch’ ella ti reggia*, with its description of the rock as being of the requisite kind, becomes simply ‘whether it can bear you’.

> 31 Non era via da vestito di cappa,  
> 32 chè noi a pena, ei lieve ed io sospinto,  
> 33 potevan su montar di chiappa in chiappa.  

It was no way for one clad with cloak of lead: for scarcely we, he light and I pushed on, could mount up from jagg to jagg.

> 31 Οὐκ ἦν ὁδὸς πρόσφορος μανδυοφόροις·  
> (It was no path suitable for wearers of cloaks;)
>
> 32 Μόλις γὰρ αὐτὸς κοῦφος κἀγὼ στηρίξει  
> (For scarcely he, light as he was, and I with support)
>
> 33 Ἠδυνάμεθα βαθμηδὸν ἀναβῆναι.  
> (Were able step by step to climb up.)

- *Οὐκ ἦν ὁδὸς πρόσφορος μανδυοφόροις* expands *via da* into a noun and predicate adjective, ‘a way suitable for’. *ἦν* is the 3rd sg. imperfect of *εἰμί*; *πρόσφορος* is feminine nominative singular despite its ending, agreeing with *ὁδός*. The dative plural of the substantivized compound *μανδυοφόρος*, ‘cloak-wearer’, replaces singular *vestito di cappa*. Like the Italian line, the Greek does not specify lead.
- *Μόλις γάρ* keeps *a pena* and causal *chè*. The separate subjects *αὐτὸς κοῦφος κἀγώ*, ‘he himself light, and I’, suffice without *noi*; *κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*. Where *sospinto* has a passive participle agreeing with *io*, *στηρίξει* is an instrumental dative singular of *στήριξις*, ‘with support’.
- *Ἠδυνάμεθα* is 1st pl. imperf. mid. ind. of *δύναμαι*, with the variant augment *ἠ-*; the verb is plural, not dual. It takes *ἀναβῆναι*, the second aorist active infinitive of *ἀναβαίνω*, viewing the ascent as a whole. *βαθμηδόν*, ‘step by step’, retains the successive movement of *di chiappa in chiappa* but not the individual projections underfoot.

> 34 E se non fosse che da quel precinto,  
> 35 più che dall’ altro, era la costa corta,  
> 36 non so di lui, ma io sarei ben vinto.  

And were it not that on that precinct the ascent was shorter than on the other, I know not about him, but I certainly had been defeated.

> 34 Εἰ μὴ δ᾽ ἦν ἐκ τοὐντεῦθεν κρημνώδους μέρους  
> (And were not, on this precipitous side,)
>
> 35 Ἡ κλιτὺς ἢ θἀτέρου συντομωτέρα,  
> (The slope shorter than on the other,)
>
> 36 Οὐκ οἶδ’, εἰ κεῖνος, ἀλλ’ ἔγωγ’ ἠδυνάτουν.  
> (I know not about him, but I surely would have failed.)

- *Εἰ μὴ δ᾽ ἦν … ἡ κλιτύς* replaces *se non fosse che* with a negative conditional protasis in the imperfect indicative: ‘but if the slope had not been …’. *δ᾽* is elided *δέ*. *ἐκ τοὐντεῦθεν κρημνώδους μέρους* specifies the side: *τοὐντεῦθεν* contracts *τοῦ ἐντεῦθεν*, the adverb qualifying *μέρους* inside the article group, ‘on the side here’. *κρημνώδους* (gen. sg. of *κρημνώδης*) adds ‘precipitous’ to the rendering of *precinto*.
- *ἡ κλιτὺς ἢ θἀτέρου συντομωτέρα* places the comparison inside the subject–predicate frame: ‘the slope shorter than [on] the other’. *συντομωτέρα* is nom. sg. fem. comparative of *σύντομος*. *ἤ* introduces the second term; *θἀτέρου* represents *τοῦ ἑτέρου* by Attic crasis, with aspiration, and leaves *μέρους* and the repeated preposition understood.
- *Οὐκ οἶδ᾽, εἰ κεῖνος* makes *non so di lui* an elliptical indirect question, ‘I do not know whether he [could not manage]’. *οἶδ᾽* is elided *οἶδα*, perfect in form but present in meaning; *κεῖνος* is the poetic short form of *ἐκεῖνος*. *ἀλλ᾽ ἔγωγ᾽* (elided *ἀλλά*, *ἔγωγε*) marks the contrast ‘but I at least’. *ἠδυνάτουν* is 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἀδυνατέω*, ‘I was unable’, not a passive corresponding to *sarei ben vinto*. Its counterfactual force comes from the condition; the usual Attic *ἄν* of an unreal apodosis is absent.

> 37 Ma perchè Malebolge in ver la porta  
> 38 del bassissimo pozzo tutta pende,  
> 39 lo sito di ciascuna valle porta  
> 40 che l’ una costa surge e l’ altra scende;  

But as Malebolge all hangs towards the entrance of the lowest well, the site of every valley imports that one side rises and the other descends;

> 37 Ἀλλ’ ὡς τὸ Κακόβοθρον ἐπὶ τὸ στόμα  
> (But since Evil-pit toward the mouth)
>
> 38 Τοῦ κατωτάτου φρέατος ὅλον κλίνει,  
> (Of the nethermost well wholly slopes downward,)
>
> 39 Ἡ θέσις ποιεῖ φάραγγος ἑκατέρας  
> (The lay of each valley causes)
>
> 40 Τὴν μὲν τῶν πλευρῶν ὑψοῦσθαι, τὴν δὲ νεύειν.  
> (One of its sides to rise high, and the other to dip.)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ ὡς* renders *Ma perchè* causally, ‘but since’. *τὸ Κακόβοθρον* recasts the name *Malebolge* as a neuter singular compound, ‘Evil-pit’; *ὅλον*, agreeing with it, renders feminine *tutta*. *ἐπὶ τὸ στόμα* uses *ἐπί* with the directional accusative, ‘toward the mouth’, for *in ver la porta*. *τοῦ κατωτάτου φρέατος* has the superlative *κατώτατος*, ‘lowest’, agreeing with the gen. sg. of *φρέαρ*. *κλίνει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *κλίνω*) is used intransitively, ‘inclines’.
- *Ἡ θέσις … φάραγγος ἑκατέρας* corresponds to *lo sito di ciascuna valle*, with the dependent genitive postponed past *ποιεῖ*. *φάραγγος* is gen. sg. of *φάραγξ*, ‘ravine’; *ἑκατέρας* agrees with it, though *ἑκάτερος* properly means ‘each of two’, more restricted than *ciascuna*.
- For *porta che* and finite verbs Greek uses causative *ποιεῖ* (contracted present of *ποιέω*) with accusative subjects and infinitives. *τὴν μὲν τῶν πλευρῶν … τὴν δέ* means ‘the one of the sides … the other’, with partitive genitive and correlating particles. *ὑψοῦσθαι* (pres. mid./pass. inf. of *ὑψόω*) is literally ‘to be raised’; *νεύειν* (pres. act. inf. of *νεύω*) is ‘to incline’, rather than strictly ‘to descend’.

> 41 noi pur venimmo alfine in su la punta  
> 42 onde l’ ultima pietra si scoscende.  

we, however, came at length to the point from which the last stone breaks off.

> 41 Ἡμεῖς τέλος ἤλθομεν ἐπὶ τῆς ἄκρας,  
> (We at last came to the summit,)
>
> 42 Ὅθεν ἡ τελευταία προκύπτει πέτρα.  
> (Whence the last rock juts out.)

- *Ἡμεῖς τέλος ἤλθομεν* preserves explicit *noi*; adverbial *τέλος* gives *alfine*, but no particle separately expresses concessive *pur*. *ἤλθομεν* is 1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*. *ἐπὶ τῆς ἄκρας* uses a genitive of place, ‘on the summit’, presenting the position attained rather than marking direction with an accusative.
- *Ὅθεν* is the relative adverb ‘from where’, matching *onde*. *ἡ τελευταία … πέτρα* encloses *προκύπτει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *προκύπτω*). The verb means ‘projects, juts out’: the Greek gives the stone’s outward position, not the breaking away expressed by *si scoscende*.

> 43 La lena m’ era del polmon sì munta  
> 44 quando fui su, ch’ io non potea più oltre,  
> 45 anzi mi assisi nella prima giunta.  

The breath was so exhausted from my lungs, when I was up, that I could no farther; nay, seated me at my first arrival.

> 43 Οὕτω δ’ ἡ πνοή μ’ ἔλειψε τῶν πνευμόνων,  
> (And so had the breath failed my lungs,)
>
> 44 Ὥστ’ ἀναβὰς οὐκ εἶχον ἰέναι πρόσω·  
> (That, having climbed up, I could go no farther;)
>
> 45 Καὶ δὴ παρευθὺς ἀνελθὼν ἐκαθέσθην.  
> (And indeed straightway upon ascending I sat down.)

- *Οὕτω δ᾽ ἡ πνοή μ᾽ ἔλειψε τῶν πνευμόνων* turns passive *La lena m’ era … munta* into ‘the breath … failed me’. *ἡ πνοή* is subject of *ἔλειψε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *λείπω*); elided *μ᾽* is accusative *με*, the person left without breath. *τῶν πνευμόνων* associates the breath with its source, following *del polmon* but using a plural. The degree adverb *οὕτω* is moved to the front to anticipate the result.
- *Ὥστ᾽* (elided *ὥστε*) introduces an actual result with the indicative. *ἀναβάς* (nom. sg. masc. second aor. act. participle of *ἀναβαίνω*), ‘having climbed up’, replaces finite *quando fui su*. *οὐκ εἶχον ἰέναι πρόσω* spells out the motion implicit in *non potea più oltre*: imperfect *εἶχον* (1st sg. of *ἔχω*) with an infinitive means ‘I was not able’; *ἰέναι* is the present infinitive of *εἶμι*, ‘go’, and *πρόσω* means ‘onward’.
- *Καὶ δή* is confirmatory, ‘and indeed’, rather than corrective like *anzi*. *παρευθὺς ἀνελθών*, ‘immediately upon coming up’, renders *nella prima giunta* with an adverb and another nom. sg. masc. second aorist active participle, this time of *ἀνέρχομαι*. *ἐκαθέσθην* is a 1st sg. aorist in passive form with intransitive sense, ‘I sat down’, for reflexive *mi assisi*.

> 46 “Omai convien che tu così ti spoltre,”  

“Now it behooves thee thus to free thyself from sloth,”

> 46 “Δέον σε λοιπὸν ἀποθέσθαι νωθρείαν·  
> (“It behoves you now to cast off sluggishness;)

- *Δέον σε λοιπὸν ἀποθέσθαι νωθρείαν* uses neuter singular *δέον*, the present participle of impersonal *δεῖ*, predicatively with *ἐστί* understood: ‘it is necessary for you to put off sluggishness’. *σε* is accusative subject of *ἀποθέσθαι* (second aor. mid. inf. of *ἀποτίθημι*). This infinitive construction replaces *convien che tu … ti spoltre*; the middle retains the subject’s involvement, but what is put off becomes an explicit accusative noun, *νωθρείαν*. Adverbial *λοιπόν*, ‘from now on’, gives *Omai*; *così* has no distinct equivalent.

> 47 disse il maestro; “chè, seggendo in piuma,  
> 48 in fama non si vien, nè sotto coltre;  

said the Master: “for sitting on down, or under coverlet, men come not into fame;

> 47 „Εὕδων τις,” εἶπεν ὁ Προστάτης, “ἐν πτίλοις  
> („One sleeping,” said my Master, “upon down)
>
> 48 „Ἐν χλανίσι τε λαμπραῖς οὐ κτᾶται φήμην,  
> („And beneath splendid coverlets wins no fame,)

- *Εὕδων τις … ἐν πτίλοις* precedes and surrounds the reporting clause *εἶπεν ὁ Προστάτης*. *εἶπεν* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*; *Προστάτης*, ‘protector, one who stands before another’, interprets *maestro* by function. There is no causal conjunction for *chè*. *Εὕδων* (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *εὕδω*) changes *seggendo* to ‘sleeping’ and agrees with indefinite *τις*, the personal subject supplied for impersonal *si*.
- *ἐν πτίλοις ἐν χλανίσι τε λαμπραῖς* coordinates two *ἐν* phrases with enclitic *τε*: ‘in feathers and in splendid wraps’. Both nouns are dative plural (*πτίλον*, *χλανίς*). Greek levels the distinction between *in* and *sotto*, replaces negative *nè* with coordination under a single negation, and adds *λαμπραῖς*, ‘splendid’.
- *οὐ κτᾶται φήμην* replaces motion into fame (*in fama non si vien*) with acquisition: *κτᾶται* is contracted 3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *κτάομαι*, ‘wins for himself’, with direct object *φήμην*.

> 49 senza la qual chi sua vita consuma,  
> 50 cotal vestigio in terra di sè lascia,  
> 51 qual fummo in aer ed in acqua la schiuma;  

without which whoso consumes his life, leaves such vestige of himself on earth, as smoke in air or foam in water;

> 49 „Ἧς ἄνευ καταλύει τὸν βίον, ἴχνος  
> („Without which he ends his life, a trace)
>
> 50 „Καταλείπων ἐπὶ γῆς, οἷον πομφόλυξ  
> („Leaving upon earth such as a bubble)
>
> 51 „Ἐν τοῖς ὕδασι καὶ καπνὸς ἐν ἀέρι.  
> („In the waters and smoke in the air.)

- *Ἧς ἄνευ* reverses *senza la qual*: the feminine relative refers to *φήμην* but is genitive because governed by postposed *ἄνευ*, ‘without’. The indefinite *τις* of 47 continues as subject; there is no new relative subject for *chi*. *καταλύει τὸν βίον* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *καταλύω*) is ‘ends his life’, not literally ‘uses it up’.
- Greek reverses the syntactic prominence of the two actions: *καταλύει* is finite, while *καταλείπων* (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *καταλείπω*) is ‘leaving’, for finite *lascia*. Its object *ἴχνος* stands before it; *ἐπὶ γῆς* uses the genitive of place for *in terra*. *di sè* is not separately expressed.
- *οἷον* (neut. sg. of *οἷος*) picks up *ἴχνος*, ‘a trace such as [that left by] …’, without a separate demonstrative for *cotal*. *πομφόλυξ ἐν τοῖς ὕδασι καὶ καπνὸς ἐν ἀέρι* reverses the Italian comparisons and makes *schiuma* a single ‘bubble’. *πομφόλυξ* and *καπνός* are nominatives in the elliptical comparison; *ὕδασι* is dat. pl. of *ὕδωρ*, *ἀέρι* dat. sg. of *ἀήρ*. *καί* itself corresponds to Italian *ed*, not to the English “or”.

> 52 e però leva su, vinci l’ ambascia  
> 53 con l’ animo che vince ogni battaglia,  
> 54 se col suo grave corpo non s’ accascia.  

and therefore rise! conquer thy panting with the soul, that conquers every battle, if with its heavy body it sinks not down.

> 52 „Ἀνάστα τοίνυν, καὶ τὴν δύσπνοιαν νίκα  
> („Rise up then, and conquer your shortness of breath)
>
> 53 „Δι’ εὐψυχίας πᾶσαν μάχην νικώσης,  
> („With stoutness of spirit, which wins every battle,)
>
> 54 „Ὅτ’ οὐχ ὑπείκει τῷ τοῦ σώματος βάρει.  
> („When it yields not to the weight of the body.)

- *Ἀνάστα τοίνυν* gives *e però leva su* with the postpositive inferential particle *τοίνυν* and the short 2nd sg. second aor. act. imperative of *ἀνίστημι*, intransitive ‘stand up’. *καὶ τὴν δύσπνοιαν νίκα* adds coordination between the commands and puts the object first. *δύσπνοιαν* interprets *ambascia* specifically as ‘shortness of breath’; *νίκα* is the contracted 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *νικάω*.
- *Δι᾽ εὐψυχίας* uses elided *διά* with the genitive of means: *animo* becomes the quality ‘courage, stoutness of spirit’. *πᾶσαν μάχην νικώσης* replaces relative *che vince* with a present active participle (*νικώσης*, gen. sg. fem. of *νικάω*) agreeing with *εὐψυχίας*. The repeated verb *νίκα … νικώσης* preserves *vinci … vince*; singular *πᾶσαν μάχην* is distributive, ‘every battle’.
- *Ὅτ᾽ οὐχ ὑπείκει* makes *se … non* temporal, ‘when it does not yield’, with elided *ὅτε* and the 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ὑπείκω*. *οὐχ* is the form of *οὐ* before a rough breathing. The verb governs the dative *τῷ … βάρει* (dat. sg. of *βάρος*): instead of sinking *with* its heavy body, the spirit yields *to* ‘the weight of the body’. *τοῦ σώματος* is a dependent genitive enclosed between article and noun; *grave* has thus become the head noun ‘weight’, and *suo* has no explicit possessive counterpart.

## Lines 55–87 — The Steeper Climb and the Throng of Serpents

> 55 Più lunga scala convien che si saglia:  

A longer ladder must be climbed:

> 55 „ Ἀναβῆναι δεῖ κλίμακα μακροτέραν·  
> („ A longer stair must be climbed;)

- *Ἀναβῆναι δεῖ κλίμακα μακροτέραν* ‘one must ascend a longer ladder’ puts the act of climbing first, where *Più lunga scala* puts the ladder and its length first. *Ἀναβῆναι* is the second aorist active infinitive of *ἀναβαίνω*: the ascent is taken as a whole, not referred to past time.
- Impersonal *δεῖ* ‘it is necessary’ takes the infinitive without an expressed subject, in place of *convien che* with impersonal *si saglia* in the subjunctive. *Κλίμακα* (acc. sg. of *κλῖμαξ*) denotes what one ascends; *μακροτέραν*, the feminine accusative singular comparative of *μακρός*, agrees with it and follows it.

> 56 non basta da costoro esser partito;  

to have quitted these is not enough;

> 56 „ Οὐκ ἀρκεῖ μόνον ἀπαλλαχθῆναι τούτων.  
> („ It suffices not merely to have got clear of these.)

- *Οὐκ ἀρκεῖ* follows *non basta* closely. The subject of *ἀρκεῖ* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἀρκέω*) is the infinitive *ἀπαλλαχθῆναι*; *μόνον* ‘merely’ makes explicit the limitation implied by *non basta*.
- *Ἀπαλλαχθῆναι* is aorist passive in form, from *ἀπαλλάσσω*, but means ‘to get clear, be rid of’. Its genitive of separation *τούτων* does the work of *da costoro*, without a preposition. The Greek aorist presents the separation as an accomplished act rather than reproducing the form of the Italian perfect infinitive *esser partito*.

> 57 se tu m' intendi, or fa sì che ti vaglia."  

if thou understandest me, now act so that it may profit thee."

> 57 „ Εἴ μ᾽ ἐνόησας, τὸ συμφέρον σοι πρᾶξον.”  
> („ If you have understood me, do what profits you.”)

- *Εἴ μ᾽ ἐνόησας* ‘if you understood me’ changes present *intendi* to *ἐνόησας* (2nd sg. aor. act. ind. of *νοέω*), as if the point has already been grasped. The personal ending suffices for *tu*; *μ᾽* is elided accusative *με*, the object of the verb.
- *Τὸ συμφέρον σοι πρᾶξον* ‘do what is advantageous to you’ compresses *or fa sì che ti vaglia*. *Τὸ συμφέρον* is the substantivized neuter singular present active participle of *συμφέρω*, accusative object of *πρᾶξον* (2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *πράσσω*); *σοι* is dative of the person advantaged. The Greek thus has a specified object of ‘do’ rather than a clause saying to what effect one should act; *or* has no separate equivalent.

> 58 Leva' mi allor, mostrandomi fornito  
> 59 meglio di lena ch' io non mi sentia;  
> 60 e dissi: "Va, ch' io son forte ed ardito."  

I then rose, showing myself better furnished with breath than I felt, and said: "Go on; for I am strong and confident."

> 58 Ἀνέστην τότε, μείζον’ ἐπιδεικνύων  
> (I rose up then, showing greater)
>
> 59 Ἐν ἐμοὶ πνοὴν ἢ πραγματικῶς εἶχον,  
> (Breath within me than I actually had,)
>
> 60 Εἶπον τ᾽· “Ἄγε δή, ῥώμην ἔχω καὶ θάῤῥος.”  
> (And said: “Come on, I have strength and courage.”)

- *Ἀνέστην τότε* retains the order of *Leva' mi allor*. *Ἀνέστην* is the intransitive second aorist active indicative, 1st sg., of *ἀνίστημι*: ‘I stood up’, without a reflexive pronoun.
- *Μείζον’ ἐπιδεικνύων ἐν ἐμοὶ πνοήν* means ‘displaying greater breath in myself’. The Greek shifts from showing **oneself** *fornito meglio di lena* to showing **breath** in greater measure. *Ἐπιδεικνύων* is a nominative masculine singular present active participle, in the thematic form in *-ύων*, simultaneous with *ἀνέστην*. Its object *πνοήν* is separated from its modifier *μείζον’* (elided *μείζονα*, acc. sg. fem. of *μείζων*, comparative of *μέγας*).
- *Ἢ πραγματικῶς εἶχον* ‘than I actually had’ substitutes actual possession for *ch' io non mi sentia*, the speaker’s sense of his strength. *Εἶχον* is 1st sg. imperfect active indicative of *ἔχω*, with ‘breath’ understood; comparative *ἤ* needs no counterpart to Italian comparative *non*.
- In *εἶπον τ᾽*, postpositive *τε*, elided before *ἄγε*, connects *εἶπον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind.) to *ἀνέστην*. *Ἄγε δή* renders *Va* by an exhortation, literally ‘come on, then’ (*ἄγε*, 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *ἄγω*). *Ῥώμην ἔχω καὶ θάῤῥος* ‘I have strength and courage’ replaces *son forte ed ardito* with two accusative nouns dependent on *ἔχω*; the explanatory *ch’* is left implicit. *Θάῤῥος* has the Attic double rho in place of *ρσ* in *θάρσος*, with the two rhos separately marked for breathing.

> 61 Su per lo scoglio prendemmo la via,  
> 62 ch' era ronchioso, stretto e malagevole,  
> 63 ed erto più assai che quel di pria.  

We took our way up the cliff, which was rugged, narrow, and difficult, and greatly steeper than the former.

> 61 Ἐπὶ τὸν κρημνὸν τότ’ ἠρξάμεθ’ ὁδεύειν,  
> (Upon the crag then we began to journey,)
>
> 62 Ὃς ἦν δύσβατος καὶ στενὸς καὶ πετρώδης,  
> (Which was hard to pass, and narrow, and rocky,)
>
> 63 Καὶ πολλῷ μᾶλλον ἀποῤῥὼξ τοῦ προτέρου.  
> (And far steeper than the former one.)

- *Ἐπὶ τὸν κρημνόν* puts the direction first, like *Su per lo scoglio*: *ἐπί* with the accusative marks movement onto the crag. *Τότ’ ἠρξάμεθ’ ὁδεύειν* ‘then we began to travel’ supplies a temporal connective (*τότε*, elided) and unpacks *prendemmo la via* into *ἠρξάμεθα* (1st pl. aor. mid. ind. of *ἄρχομαι*, with augment and elision) and the complementary present active infinitive *ὁδεύειν*.
- *Ὃς ἦν* corresponds to *ch' era*: the relative is masculine singular after *κρημνόν*, but nominative as subject of imperfect *ἦν*. The predicate adjectives *δύσβατος καὶ στενὸς καὶ πετρώδης* ‘hard to traverse and narrow and rocky’ reverse the order of the outer terms in *ronchioso, stretto e malagevole*: the difficulty of passage comes first, the character of the rock last.
- *Πολλῷ μᾶλλον ἀποῤῥώξ* ‘by much more precipitous’ keeps a positive adjective with a comparative adverb, like *erto più assai*. *Πολλῷ* is a dative of degree of difference modifying *μᾶλλον*; *ἀποῤῥώξ* is nominative singular, another predicate of *ὅς*, with doubled rho after the prefix. *Τοῦ προτέρου* is a genitive of comparison, ‘than the former [crag]’, in place of *che quel di pria*.

> 64 Parlando andava per non parer fievole,  

Speaking I went, that I might not seem faint;

> 64 Ἐβάδιζον ὁμιλῶν, οὐ δοκῶν ἔκπνους,  
> (I walked along talking, not to seem out of breath,)

- *Ἐβάδιζον ὁμιλῶν* ‘I was walking along talking’ puts the finite verb first, unlike *Parlando andava*. *Ἐβάδιζον* (1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *βαδίζω*) gives the continuing movement; *ὁμιλῶν* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *ὁμιλέω*) gives the accompanying action.
- *Οὐ δοκῶν ἔκπνους* attaches a second present participle to the same subject: literally ‘not seeming out of breath’. *Ἔκπνους* is a contracted nominative masculine singular predicate adjective. It narrows *fievole* to breathlessness. The context supplies the intention of *per non parer*: grammatically *οὐ* negates a description of how he appeared, not an explicit Greek purpose clause.

> 65 onde una voce uscìo dall' altro fosso,  
> 66 a parole formar disconvenevole.  

whereat a voice came from the other fosse, unsuitable for forming words.

> 65 Ὅτ’ ἐξῆλθ’ αἴφνης φωνὴ τῆς ἄλλης τάφρου,  
> (When suddenly a voice issued from the next trench,)
>
> 66 Ἐξ ἧς οὐκ ἦν συλλαβεῖν ἐνάρθρους λέξεις.  
> (From which it was impossible to catch articulate words.)

- *Ὅτ’ ἐξῆλθ’ αἴφνης φωνή* ‘when a voice suddenly came out’ interprets *onde* temporally rather than explicitly as ‘in consequence of which’. Both *ὅτε* and *ἐξῆλθε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἐξέρχομαι*) are elided; *αἴφνης* adds suddenness. *Τῆς ἄλλης τάφρου* is a genitive of source after the verb compounded with *ἐξ-*, corresponding to *dall' altro fosso*.
- *Ἐξ ἧς οὐκ ἦν συλλαβεῖν ἐνάρθρους λέξεις* shifts the difficulty in *a parole formar disconvenevole* from the formation of words to their apprehension: ‘from which [voice] it was not possible to catch articulate words’. *Ἧς* is feminine singular like *φωνή*, genitive after *ἐξ*. Impersonal imperfect *οὐκ ἦν* takes *συλλαβεῖν* (second aor. act. infinitive of *συλλαμβάνω*); its object is *λέξεις*, with the two-termination adjective *ἐνάρθρους* agreeing in the feminine accusative plural.

> 67 Non so che disse, ancor che sovra il dosso  
> 68 fossi dell' arco già, che varca quivi;  
> 69 ma chi parlava ad ira parea mosso.  

I know not what it said, though I already was on the ridge of the arch which crosses there; but he who spake seemed moved to anger.

> 67 Τί ποτ’ ἔλεγεν οὐκ οἶδα, καίπερ ἄνω  
> (What exactly it was saying I know not, although on top)
>
> 68 Ὢν τῆς γεφύρας τῆς ἐκεῖ περαιούσης·  
> (Of the bridge that crosses over there I was;)
>
> 69 Ἀλλ’ ὑπ’ ὀργῆς ὁ φωνῶν κινεῖσθ’ ἐδόκει.  
> (Yet the speaker seemed to be moved by anger.)

- *Τί ποτ’ ἔλεγεν οὐκ οἶδα* puts the indirect question before ‘I do not know’. Elided *ποτε* adds indefiniteness to interrogative *τί*, ‘what ever’; *ἔλεγεν* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *λέγω*) presents the speaking as in progress, unlike *disse*. *Οἶδα* is perfect in form but present in sense.
- *Καίπερ ἄνω ὢν τῆς γεφύρας* replaces *ancor che … fossi* with concessive *καίπερ* and *ὤν*, the nominative masculine singular present participle of *εἰμί*, agreeing with the subject of *οἶδα*. *Ἄνω* with the genitive gives ‘atop the bridge’; neither the distinct *dosso* of the *arco* nor *già* is separately expressed.
- In *τῆς γεφύρας τῆς ἐκεῖ περαιούσης* the repeated article puts the participle in attributive position, equivalent to *che varca quivi*. *Περαιούσης* (gen. fem. sg. pres. act. participle of *περαιόω*) agrees with ‘bridge’; *ἐκεῖ* modifies the crossing.
- *Ἀλλ’ ὑπ’ ὀργῆς* (elided *ἀλλά*, *ὑπό*) puts anger first and makes it the moving force, ‘by anger’, rather than the state **to** which the speaker seemed moved (*ad ira*). *Ὁ φωνῶν*, substantivized present active participle of *φωνέω*, renders *chi parlava*. *Κινεῖσθ’ ἐδόκει* has an elided present passive infinitive, *κινεῖσθαι*, with the personal imperfect *ἐδόκει* (3rd sg. of *δοκέω*): ‘the speaker seemed to be moved’.

> 70 Io era volto in giù; ma gli occhi vivi  
> 71 non potean ire al fondo per l' oscuro;  

I had turned myself downwards; but my living eyes could not reach the bottom for the darkness;

> 70 Ὄψεις ἔστρεψα κάτω, πλὴν ὄμμα ζῶντος  
> (I turned my eyes downward, yet a living eye)
>
> 71 Οὐ διορᾷ τὸν βυθὸν ὑπὸ τοῦ σκότους.  
> (Cannot pierce to the bottom because of the darkness.)

- *Ὄψεις ἔστρεψα κάτω* ‘I turned my eyes downward’ gives *ἔστρεψα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *στρέφω*) an accusative plural object, *ὄψεις* (from *ὄψις*). The eyes, mentioned in the next Italian clause, thus enter earlier; *Io era volto* describes an orientation where Greek describes the act of turning.
- *Πλὴν ὄμμα ζῶντος* ‘yet the eye of a living person’ changes *gli occhi vivi* from plural to generic singular. Adversative *πλήν* introduces *ὄμμα*, the nominative neuter subject; *ζῶντος* is a substantival genitive singular present active participle of *ζάω*, not an adjective agreeing with ‘eye’.
- *Οὐ διορᾷ τὸν βυθόν* ‘does not see the bottom distinctly’ changes past inability, *non potean ire al fondo*, to a generalizing present (*διορᾷ*, contracted 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *διοράω*) with a direct object. *Ὑπὸ τοῦ σκότους* is causal, ‘owing to the darkness’, for *per l' oscuro*.

> 72 per ch' io: "Maestro, fa che tu arrivi  
> 73 dall' altro cinghio, e dismontiam lo muro:  
> 74 chè com' i' odo quinci e non intendo,  
> 75 così giù veggio, e niente affiguro."  

wherefore I: "Master, see that thou get to the other belt, and let us dismount the wall: for as I hear from hence and do not understand, so I see down and distinguish nothing."

> 72 “Ὦ Προστάτα,” τότ’ εἶπον, “ἐλθεῖν ἐπείγου  
> (“O Master,” then I said, “hasten to go)
>
> 73 „ Ἐκεῖθεν, ὅπως καταβῶμεν τὸ τεῖχος.  
> („ Across to the other side, so that we may descend the wall.)
>
> 74 „ Ὡς γὰρ ἐνταῦθα μὴ συνιὼν ἀκούω,  
> („ For just as here I hear without understanding,)
>
> 75 „ Οὕτω ’κεῖ κάτω βλέπω μὴ διακρίνων.”  
> („ So down there I look without discerning.”)

- *Ὦ Προστάτα* renders *Maestro* as an address to a protector or leader (*προστάτα*, voc. sg. of *προστάτης*). *Τότ’ εἶπον* ‘then I said’ supplies the verb left understood after *per ch' io* and marks sequence rather than consequence.
- *Ἐλθεῖν ἐπείγου* ‘hasten to go’ uses *ἐπείγου* (2nd sg. pres. mid. imperative of *ἐπείγω*) with the second aorist active infinitive of *ἔρχομαι*, rather than *fa che* with the subjunctive *arrivi*. *Ἐκεῖθεν* stands alone for *dall' altro cinghio*: the noun disappears, and the adverb, ordinarily ‘from there’, is pressed into the sense ‘over on the other side’ with a verb of going.
- *Ὅπως καταβῶμεν τὸ τεῖχος* makes a purpose clause of the coordinated *e dismontiam lo muro*. *Καταβῶμεν* is 1st pl. second aor. act. subjunctive of *καταβαίνω*, not a separate exhortation; *τὸ τεῖχος* is accusative of the thing descended, close to the Italian construction.
- *Ὡς γὰρ … οὕτω* preserves the correlation *chè com’ … così*, with explanatory *γάρ* postpositive. *Ἐνταῦθα* ‘here’ renders *quinci* without its explicit sense of origin; *’κεῖ κάτω* ‘there below’ has prodelision of the initial vowel of *ἐκεῖ*.
- *Μὴ συνιὼν ἀκούω* and *βλέπω μὴ διακρίνων* balance two finite verbs of perception with two negated participles: ‘without understanding I hear’, ‘I look without distinguishing’. *Συνιών* (from *συνίημι*) and *διακρίνων* (from *διακρίνω*) are nominative masculine singular present active participles. The accompanying circumstance expressed by *μή* and the participle replaces Italian coordination; in particular *niente* is not translated by a negative object.

> 76 "Altra risposta," disse, "non ti rendo,  
> 77 se non lo far: chè la domanda onesta  
> 78 si dee seguir con l' opera tacendo."  

"Other answer I give thee not," he said, "than the deed: for a fit request should be followed with the work in silence."

> 76 —“Οὐκ ἄλλην,” εἶπεν, “ἀπόκρισίν σοι δώσω  
> (—“No other,” he said, “answer will I give you)
>
> 77 „ Ἢ πράττων, ὡς φῄς· αἴτησις γὰρ τιμία  
> („ Than by doing as you say; for an honourable request)
>
> 78 „ Ἐν σιγῇ καὶ δι᾽ ἔργων ἐκτελεστέα.”  
> („ Must be fulfilled in silence and by deeds.”)

- *Οὐκ ἄλλην, εἶπεν, ἀπόκρισίν σοι δώσω* retains the emphatic *Altra risposta* but interposes ‘he said’ between adjective and noun. *Ἄλλην* agrees with accusative singular *ἀπόκρισιν*, whose additional final accent in *ἀπόκρισίν σοι* is due to the enclitic dative *σοι*. *Δώσω* is 1st sg. future active indicative of *δίδωμι*, ‘I shall give’, for present *rendo*.
- *Ἢ πράττων, ὡς φῄς* ‘than by acting as you say’ answers *οὐκ ἄλλην* with comparative *ἤ*. Instead of the nominalized infinitive *lo far*, Greek has a participle of means, *πράττων* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. of Attic *πράττω*), referring to the giver of the answer. *Ὡς φῄς* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *φημί*) makes explicit whose words the action will follow.
- *Αἴτησις γὰρ τιμία* ‘for an honourable request’ uses an anarthrous noun for a general statement. *Ἐκτελεστέα* is the feminine nominative singular verbal adjective of necessity from *ἐκτελέω*, agreeing with *αἴτησις*, with *ἐστί* understood: ‘is to be carried out’. This replaces *si dee seguir*, without naming an agent.
- *Ἐν σιγῇ καὶ δι᾽ ἔργων* ‘in silence and by deeds’ turns *tacendo* into a prepositional phrase and puts it first. *Δι᾽* is elided *διά* with the genitive plural of *ἔργον*, expressing means in place of singular *con l' opera*.

> 79 Noi discendemmo il ponte dalla testa,  
> 80 dove s' aggiunge con l' ottava ripa,  
> 81 e poi mi fu la bolgia manifesta:  

We went down the bridge, at the head where it joins with the eighth bank; and then the chasm was manifest to me:

> 79 Κατέβημεν οὖν τὴν γέφυραν ἐκεῖθεν,  
> (We went down the bridge, then, at that end,)
>
> 80 Ἔνθα ζεύγνυται πρὸς τὴν ὀγδόην ὄχθην.  
> (Where it joins onto the eighth bank.)
>
> 81 Τότε δ᾽ ὁ βόθρος ἐμφανής μοι παρέστη·  
> (And then the pit appeared plainly before me;)

- *Κατέβημεν οὖν τὴν γέφυραν* closely follows *discendemmo il ponte*, with a direct accusative of the way traversed. *Κατέβημεν* is 1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *καταβαίνω*, with the augment after the prefix; there is no separate *Noi*. Postpositive *οὖν* ‘accordingly’ supplies a link to the preceding speech.
- *Ἐκεῖθεν, ἔνθα* ‘from there, where’ substitutes an adverb and its relative for *dalla testa, dove*, without naming the bridge-end. *Ζεύγνυται* (3rd sg. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *ζεύγνυμι*) means ‘is joined’ where Italian has *s' aggiunge*; *πρὸς τὴν ὀγδόην ὄχθην* uses *πρός* with the accusative for the point of junction.
- *Τότε δ᾽ ὁ βόθρος ἐμφανής μοι παρέστη* ‘then the pit stood manifest before me’ keeps the predicate adjective and the dative of the perceiver in *mi fu la bolgia manifesta*. But *βόθρος* is masculine, hence masculine nominative singular *ἐμφανής*; *παρέστη* is the intransitive second aorist active indicative, 3rd sg., of *παρίστημι*, more eventive than a simple copula. Elided *δέ* with *τότε* renders *e poi*.

> 82 e vidivi entro terribile stipa  
> 83 di serpenti, e di sì diversa mena,  
> 84 che la memoria il sangue ancor mi scipa.  

and I saw within it a fearful throng of serpents, and of so strange a look, that even now the recollection scares my blood.

> 82 Εἶδον δ᾽ ἐν αὐτῷ συνάθροισμα φρικῶδες  
> (And I saw within it a dreadful throng)
>
> 83 Ὄφεων οὕτω τὴν μορφὴν διαφόρων,  
> (Of serpents so diverse in form,)
>
> 84 Ὥστ᾽ ἔτι πήγνυσ᾽ αἷμα τοὐμὸν ἡ μνήμη.  
> (That the memory of it still curdles my blood.)

- *Εἶδον δ᾽ ἐν αὐτῷ* distributes *e vidivi entro* over a verb (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*), elided connective *δέ*, and a prepositional phrase. *Αὐτῷ* refers back to masculine *βόθρος*. *Συνάθροισμα φρικῶδες* ‘a dreadful gathering’ is the neuter accusative singular object, with its adjective following rather than preceding as in *terribile stipa*.
- *Ὄφεων* (gen. pl. of *ὄφις*) specifies what the gathering consists of. *Οὕτω τὴν μορφὴν διαφόρων* ‘so diverse in form’ makes *διαφόρων* agree directly with *ὄφεων*, dropping the coordination of *e di sì diversa mena*. *Τὴν μορφήν* is accusative of respect, defining the diversity as one of outward form.
- *Ὥστ᾽* (elided *ὥστε*) answers *οὕτω* as *che* answers *sì*. The indicative marks an actual result: *ἔτι πήγνυσ᾽ αἷμα τοὐμὸν ἡ μνήμη* ‘memory still congeals my blood’. *Πήγνυσ᾽* is elided *πήγνυσι*, 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *πήγνυμι*; it gives *scipa* the particular image of congealing. Greek delays the subject *ἡ μνήμη* to the end. *Τοὐμόν* is crasis for *τὸ ἐμόν*, the articulated possessive following accusative *αἷμα*: *τοὐμόν*, not a dative pronoun, expresses the possession conveyed by Italian *mi*.

> 85 Più non si vanti Libia con sua rena;  

Let Libya boast no longer with its sand;

> 85 Μηκέτ᾽ ἐπ᾽ ἄμμοις σαῖς ἐγκαυχῶ, Λιβύη·  
> (Boast no more of your sands, Libya;)

- *Μηκέτ᾽* (elided *μηκέτι*) retains the initial negative of *Più non*, but *ἐγκαυχῶ* is a direct prohibition in the 2nd sg. present middle imperative of *ἐγκαυχάομαι*. Where *si vanti Libia* has a third-person subjunctive with *Libia* as subject, *Λιβύη* is vocative, placed last.
- Accordingly *sua* becomes second-person *σαῖς*. *Ἐπ᾽ ἄμμοις σαῖς* (elided *ἐπί* with the dative plural) means ‘on account of your sands’, giving the ground of boasting rather than reproducing instrumental *con*; singular *rena* becomes plural.

> 86 chè, se chelidri, iaculi e faree  
> 87 produce, e cencri con amfisibena,  

for, though it engenders chelydri, jaculi and pareæ, and cenchres with amphisbæna,

> 86 Εἰ γὰρ ἀσπίδας, αἱμόῤῥους καὶ χελύδρους  
> (For if asps, haemorrhoi, and chelydri)
>
> 87 Παράγεις, πρὸς δὲ κέγχρους τε κἀμφισβαίνας,  
> (You bring forth, and besides cenchres and amphisbaenae,)

- *Εἰ γάρ* corresponds to *chè, se*: explanatory *γάρ* follows conditional *εἰ*. The concession is conveyed through the condition in context, not by a separate particle meaning ‘although’. *Παράγεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *παράγω*) continues the direct address in place of 3rd sg. *produce*; like the Italian verb, it comes after the first three names.
- *Ἀσπίδας, αἱμόῤῥους καὶ χελύδρους* are accusative plural objects of *παράγεις*. Musurus does not simply transliterate *chelidri, iaculi e faree*: he retains *χελύδρους* but moves it to third place, giving ‘asps’ and ‘haemorrhoi’ as the other two. *Ἀσπίδας* is the consonant-stem plural of *ἀσπίς*; *αἱμόῤῥους* shows the doubled rho in a compound.
- *Πρὸς δὲ κέγχρους τε κἀμφισβαίνας* means ‘and in addition both cenchres and amphisbaenae’. *Πρός* is adverbial, ‘besides’, not a preposition governing the names; these remain objects of *παράγεις*. Postpositive *τε* pairs with *καί*, present in the crasis *κἀμφισβαίνας* for *καὶ ἀμφισβαίνας*. Thus *cencri con amfisibena* becomes a coordinated pair of accusative plurals rather than a noun followed by a singular of accompaniment.

## Lines 88–120 — The Serpent Stroke and the Return from Ashes

> 88 nè tante pestilenzie nè sì ree  
> 89 mostrò giammai con tutta l' Etiopia,  
> 90 nè con ciò che di sopra il mar rosso ee.  

plagues so numerous or so dire it never shewed, with all Ethiopia, nor with the land that lies by the Red Sea.

> 88 Οὔποτε λύμας τόσας καὶ δεινὰς τόσον  
> (Never plagues so many nor so dire)
>
> 89 Ἔδειξας μετὰ πάσης Αἰθιοπίας,  
> (Did you show, even with all Ethiopia,)
>
> 90 Οὔτ᾽ ἐφ᾽ ἁπάσης τῆς Ἐρυθρᾶς θαλάσσης.  
> (Nor over all the Red Sea.)

- *Οὔποτε* brings *giammai* to the front and gives it its own negative, ‘never’. Instead of repeating *nè … nè* before the two descriptions, Musurus joins them positively under that negative: *λύμας τόσας καὶ δεινὰς τόσον*, ‘plagues so many and so terrible’. *Λύμας* is accusative plural of the feminine *λύμη*, not of the neuter *λῦμα*; *τόσας* agrees with it and expresses number, whereas *τόσον* is a neuter accusative used adverbially to express the degree of *δεινάς*.
- *Ἔδειξας* (2nd sg. aor. act. ind. of *δείκνυμι*) makes the subject an addressee, ‘you showed’, where *mostrò* is third person. *Μετὰ πάσης Αἰθιοπίας* keeps *con tutta l’ Etiopia*, with the genitive of accompaniment.
- *Οὔτ᾽ ἐφ᾽ ἁπάσης τῆς Ἐρυθρᾶς θαλάσσης* changes both the construction and its geographical reference: not *con ciò che … ee*, ‘with that which is’, but ‘nor over the whole Red Sea’. *Ἐπί* with the genitive expresses extent over a surface; *ἁπάσης* is outside the article, *Ἐρυθρᾶς* inside it. *Οὔτ᾽* is elided *οὔτε*; in *ἐφ᾽* the final vowel of *ἐπί* is lost and the *π* aspirated before the rough breathing of *ἁπάσης*.

> 91 Tra questa cruda e tristissima copia  
> 92 correvan genti nude e spaventate,  
> 93 senza sperar pertugio o elitropia.  

Amid this cruel and most dismal swarm were people running, naked and terrified, without hope of lurking hole or heliotrope.

> 91 Μέσον τοῦ σκληροῦ, μυσαχθοῦς σωροῦ τούτου  
> (Amid this cruel, loathsome swarm)
>
> 92 Ἔντρομοι, γυμναὶ ψυχαὶ τρέχουσιν ἄνευ  
> (Trembling, naked souls run without)
>
> 93 Ἐλπίδος φυγῆς οὐδ᾽ ἡλιοτροπίου.  
> (Hope of escape or of heliotrope.)

- *Μέσον* is a neuter accusative used prepositionally with the genitive, ‘in the midst of’. *Τοῦ σκληροῦ, μυσαχθοῦς σωροῦ τούτου* turns *questa … copia* into ‘this heap’: the two adjectives stand between article and noun, the demonstrative after the noun. *Σκληροῦ* gives *cruda* as ‘harsh’; *μυσαχθοῦς* (gen. sg. of *μυσαχθής*) gives *tristissima* as ‘loathsome’, without a superlative.
- *Ἔντρομοι, γυμναὶ ψυχαί* replaces *genti* with ‘souls’ and puts the adjectives first, reversing their Italian order. Both are nominative feminine plural; *ἔντρομοι* has the common masculine-feminine ending of *ἔντρομος*. *Τρέχουσιν* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *τρέχω*, with movable *ν*) makes *correvan* a vivid present.
- *Ἄνευ ἐλπίδος* changes *senza sperar* from preposition and infinitive to preposition and genitive noun. *Φυγῆς* and *ἡλιοτροπίου* are genitives dependent on *ἐλπίδος*: for the concrete *pertugio* Musurus gives the abstract *φυγή*, ‘escape’. *Οὐδέ* (elided *οὐδ᾽*) continues the negative sense of *ἄνευ*, ‘nor’, where Italian has *o*.

> 94 Con serpi le man dietro avean legate;  

They had their hands tied behind with serpents;

> 94 Ὄφεσιν ὄπισθ᾽ εἶχον δετὰς τὰς χεῖρας  
> (With serpents behind their backs they had their hands bound,)

- *Ὄφεσιν* (dat. pl. of *ὄφις*) puts the instrument first. *Ὄπισθ᾽* is elided *ὄπισθεν* in its shorter form *ὄπισθε*, ‘behind’; there is no Greek noun for ‘backs’.
- *Εἶχον δετὰς τὰς χεῖρας* closely follows *le man … avean legate*: *εἶχον* is here 3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *ἔχω*, and *δετάς* (acc. fem. pl. of the verbal adjective *δετός*, from *δέω*) predicates a condition of *τὰς χεῖρας*. It stands outside the article: ‘they had their hands bound’, not ‘they had the bound hands’.

> 95 quelle ficcavan per le ren la coda  
> 96 e il capo, ed eran dinanzi aggroppate.  

these through their loins fixed the tail and the head, and were coiled in knots before.

> 95 Κεφαλὴν κοὐρὰν τοῖς νεφροῖς ἐμπηγνῦσιν,  
> (Which thrust head and tail right through their loins,)
>
> 96 Ἐπίπροσθεν δὲ συμπεριπεπλεγμένοις.  
> (And were knotted together in front.)

- *Κεφαλὴν κοὐράν* reverses *la coda e il capo* and dispenses with the articles; *κοὐράν* is crasis of *καὶ οὐράν*. *Τοῖς νεφροῖς* (dat. pl. of *νεφρός*) gives the site of insertion, ‘in the loins’, for *per le ren*. *Ἐμπηγνῦσιν* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *ἐμπήγνυμι*) changes *ficcavan* to a present. Its understood subject is the serpents; there is no counterpart to the explicit *quelle*.
- *Ἐπίπροσθεν δέ* gives ‘and in front’, but *συμπεριπεπλεγμένοις* is not a finite equivalent of *eran … aggroppate*. It is a reduplicated perfect middle-passive participle of *συμπεριπλέκω*, ‘entwined together’, dative masculine plural in agreement with *ὄφεσιν* in 94. Thus the finite verb in 95 treats the serpents as its subject, while the participle reaches back past it to their earlier case.

> 97 Ed ecco ad un, ch' era da nostra proda,  
> 98 s'avventò un serpente, che il trafisse  
> 99 là dove il collo alle spalle s'annoda.  

And lo! at one, who was near our shore, sprang up a serpent, which transfixed him there where the neck is bound upon the shoulders.

> 97 Ἰδοὺ δ’ ἐπί τιν’, ὃς ἦν ἡμῶν πλησίον,  
> (And behold, at one who was near us,)
>
> 98 Ὥρμησεν ὄφις, αὐτὸν διατετρήνας,  
> (A serpent darted, piercing him through)
>
> 99 Ἔνθ’ ὁ τράχηλος πρὸς τοὺς ὤμους ἑνοῦται.  
> (Where the neck joins the shoulders.)

- *Ἰδοὺ δ’ ἐπί τιν᾽* gives *Ed ecco ad un*: *ἰδού*, originally a 2nd sg. aorist middle imperative of *ὁράω*, functions as a presentative; postpositive *δέ* and *τινά* (acc. sg. of indefinite *τις*) are both elided. *Ἐπί* with the accusative marks the target of *ὥρμησεν*. *Ὃς ἦν ἡμῶν πλησίον* reduces *da nostra proda* to ‘near us’: *ἦν* is 3rd sg. imperfect of *εἰμί*, and adverbial *πλησίον* governs the genitive.
- *Ὥρμησεν ὄφις* retains the verb–subject order of *s’avventò un serpente*, but uses the active intransitively (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ὁρμάω*, with temporal augment). *Αὐτὸν διατετρήνας* replaces *che il trafisse* with a circumstantial participle: *διατετρήνας* (nom. masc. sg. aor. act. of *διατετραίνω*) agrees with *ὄφις* and governs *αὐτόν*.
- *Ἔνθ᾽* (elided *ἔνθα*) alone does the work of *là dove*. *Πρὸς τοὺς ὤμους ἑνοῦται* gives ‘is joined to the shoulders’ for *alle spalle s’annoda*: *πρός* takes the accusative, and the contracted 3rd sg. present middle-passive of *ἑνόω* replaces the reflexive image of knotting.

> 100 Nè o sì tosto mai nè i si scrisse,  
> 101 com' ei s'accese ed arse, e cener tutto  
> 102 convenne che cascando divenisse;  

Neither “O” nor “I” was ever written so quickly as he took fire, and burnt, and dropt down all changed to ashes;

> 100 Οὔτ’ Ὄ οὔτ’ Ἴ τις οὕτω τάχος ἂν γράψαι,  
> (Neither O nor I could anyone write so swiftly,)
>
> 101 Ὡς αὐτὸς ἥφθη, κατεφλέχθη καὶ τέφρα  
> (As he caught fire, burned away, and into ashes)
>
> 102 Χαμᾶζε καταπεσὼν ὅλος κατέστη.  
> (Wholly turned, falling down to the ground.)

- *Οὔτ’ Ὄ οὔτ’ Ἴ* repeats elided *οὔτε* before the two letter names, treated as indeclinable objects. *Τις … ἂν γράψαι* turns *mai … si scrisse* into ‘could anyone write’: indefinite *τις* supplies a personal subject, and *γράψαι* is here 3rd sg. aor. act. optative of *γράφω* with potential *ἄν*, not an infinitive. There is no separate ‘ever’. *Οὕτω τάχος* uses the adverbial accusative of *τάχος*, ‘with such speed’, for *sì tosto*.
- *Ὡς* answers *οὕτω*, as *com’* answers *sì*. *Αὐτός* makes the subject explicit for *ei*; *ἥφθη* (from *ἅπτω*) and *κατεφλέχθη* (from *καταφλέγω*) are both 3rd sg. aor. pass. indicative, ‘was kindled, was burnt up’, for reflexive *s’accese* and intransitive *arse*.
- *Τέφρα … ὅλος κατέστη* renders *cener tutto … divenisse* directly, without *convenne che* or its note of necessity. *Κατέστη* is the intransitive second aorist active indicative, 3rd sg., of *καθίστημι*, ‘became’; *τέφρα* is its predicate nominative, while masculine *ὅλος* agrees with the man. *Χαμᾶζε*, a directional adverb ‘to the ground’, qualifies *καταπεσών* (nom. masc. sg. second aor. act. participle of *καταπίπτω*), a completed fall in place of the Italian gerund *cascando*.

> 103 e poi che fu a terra sì distrutto,  
> 104 la polver si raccolse per sè stessa,  
> 105 e in quel medesmo ritornò di butto:  

and after he was thus dissolved upon the ground, the [powder] reunited of [itself and] at once resumed the former shape:

> 103 Ἐπεὶ δ’ ἐπὶ γῆς οὕτως ἐξηφανίσθη,  
> (And when upon the earth he was thus destroyed,)
>
> 104 Ἀφ’ ἑαυτῆς ἡ τέφρα συνεφειλκύσθη,  
> (Of its own accord the ashes drew together,)
>
> 105 Αἴφνης τ’ ἐπανῆλθ’ εἰς τὸ πρότερον σχῆμα.  
> (And suddenly returned to his former shape.)

- *Ἐπεὶ δ᾽ … οὕτως ἐξηφανίσθη* keeps the temporal clause of *e poi che … sì distrutto*. *Ἐπί* with genitive *γῆς* now gives position on the ground, unlike directional *χαμᾶζε*. *Ἐξηφανίσθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ἐξαφανίζω*, with internal temporal augment) means ‘was made to disappear’, a single verb for *fu … distrutto*.
- *Ἀφ᾽ ἑαυτῆς* fronts *per sè stessa*: *ἀπό* is elided and aspirated before the feminine reflexive genitive referring to *ἡ τέφρα*. Musurus repeats ‘ash’ for *la polver*. *Συνεφειλκύσθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *συνεφέλκω*) gives ‘was drawn together’ for *si raccolse*; the reflexive phrase, rather than the verb alone, conveys spontaneity.
- *Αἴφνης* moves *di butto* to the front. Enclitic *τε*, elided in *τ᾽*, joins the clauses; *ἐπανῆλθ᾽* is elided *ἐπανῆλθε*, 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἐπανέρχομαι*. *Εἰς τὸ πρότερον σχῆμα* makes the elliptical *in quel medesmo* explicit as ‘into the former shape’, substituting priority for identity.

> 106 così per li gran savi si confessa,  
> 107 che la fenice more e poi rinasce,  
> 108 quando al cinquecentesimo anno appressa;  

thus by great sages 'tis confest the Phœnix dies, and then is born again, when it approaches the five-hundredth year;

> 106 Οὕτως ὁμολογοῦσι σοφοὶ μεγάλοι  
> (Even so great sages confess)
>
> 107 Τὸν φοίνικ’ ἀποθνήσκειν κἀναζῇν αὖθις,  
> (That the phoenix dies and lives again,)
>
> 108 Τὸ πεντακοσιοστὸν πληρώσαντ’ ἔτος.  
> (When it has completed its five hundredth year.)

- *Οὕτως ὁμολογοῦσι σοφοὶ μεγάλοι* keeps initial *così* but makes *li gran savi* the nominative subject rather than the agent introduced by *per*. *Ὁμολογοῦσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *ὁμολογέω*) introduces an accusative and infinitive instead of *si confessa, che* and finite verbs.
- *Τὸν φοίνικ᾽* (elided *φοίνικα*, acc. sg. of *φοῖνιξ*) is subject of both present active infinitives, *ἀποθνήσκειν* and *ἀναζῇν*. *Κἀναζῇν* is crasis of *καὶ ἀναζῇν*: ‘to live again’ shifts *rinasce* from birth to life. *Αὖθις* gives renewed life an additional ‘again’, rather than simply reproducing temporal *poi*.
- *Τὸ πεντακοσιοστὸν … ἔτος* is the accusative object of *πληρώσαντ᾽* (elided *πληρώσαντα*, acc. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *πληρόω*, agreeing with *φοίνικα*). A temporal participle replaces *quando … appressa*, and ‘having completed the five-hundredth year’ changes approaching the year to finishing it.

> 109 erba nè biado in sua vita non pasce,  
> 110 ma sol d'incenso lagrime ed amomo,  
> 111 e nardo e mirra son l' ultime fasce.  

in its life it eats no herb or grain, but only tears of incense and amomum; and nard and myrrh are its last swathings.

> 109 Ἕως ζῇ χόρτον καὶ κόκκους οὐκ ἐσθίει,  
> (While it lives, it eats neither herb nor grain,)
>
> 110 Ἀλλὰ δάκρυα λιβάνου τε κἀμώμου,  
> (But tears of frankincense and amomum,)
>
> 111 Σπάργαν’ ὕστατα νάρδον ἔχων καὶ σμύρναν.  
> (Having nard and myrrh as its final swaddling-bands.)

- *Ἕως ζῇ* expands *in sua vita* into ‘while it lives’; *ζῇ* is the contracted 3rd sg. present of *ζάω*, indicative in this factual description. The Greek returns to finite verbs with the phoenix as understood subject. *Χόρτον καὶ κόκκους οὐκ ἐσθίει* uses one negated present indicative (*ἐσθίει*, 3rd sg. of *ἐσθίω*) with coordinated accusative objects, ‘grass and grains’, rather than repeating the negatives of *erba nè biado … non pasce*.
- *Ἀλλὰ δάκρυα* supplies the contrasting object of the same verb, without a distinct word for *sol*. *Λιβάνου τε κἀμώμου* coordinates two genitives dependent on *δάκρυα*, ‘tears of both frankincense and amomum’; *κἀμώμου* is crasis of *καὶ ἀμώμου*. The Greek thus places both substances under ‘tears’, where *d’incenso lagrime ed amomo* can coordinate amomum with the tears.
- *Σπάργαν᾽ ὕστατα* (elided *σπάργανα*, acc. neut. pl., with its adjective) fronts *l’ ultime fasce*. For *nardo e mirra son* Musurus writes *νάρδον ἔχων καὶ σμύρναν*, ‘having nard and myrrh’: *ἔχων* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *ἔχω*) agrees with the understood phoenix, the two aromatics are its accusative objects, and *σπάργαν᾽ ὕστατα* is predicative, ‘as last swaddling bands’. No possessive is needed with ‘having’.

> 112 E qual è quei che cade, e non sa como,  
> 113 per forza di demon ch' a terra il tira,  
> 114 o d' altra oppilazion che lega l' uomo,  

And as one who falls, and knows not how, through force of Demon which drags him to the ground, or of other obstruction that fetters men;

> 112 Οἷος δ’ ὁ πεσών, ἀλλὰ πῶς, οὐ γινώσκων,  
> (And like one who falls, yet knows not how,)
>
> 113 Εἴτ’ αἰτίᾳ δαίμονος εἰς γῆν ὠθοῦντος,  
> (Whether by power of a demon hurling him to the ground,)
>
> 114 Εἴτ’ ἐκ πνιγμονῆς τοὺς ἀνθρώπους ναρκούσης,  
> (Or from a seizure that numbs men,)

- *Οἷος δ᾽ ὁ πεσών* opens a comparison answered by *τοιοῦτος* in 118. *Οἷος* is predicative, ‘of what sort’; *ὁ πεσών* (articular nom. masc. sg. second aor. act. participle of *πίπτω*) gives ‘the one who has fallen’ for *quei che cade*. There is no expressed equivalent of *è*. *Ἀλλὰ πῶς, οὐ γινώσκων* changes *e non sa como* to an adversative participial phrase: *γινώσκων* is present active, agreeing with *ὁ πεσών*, and interrogative *πῶς* is placed before ‘not knowing’.
- Repeated *εἴτ᾽* (elided *εἴτε*) presents the alternatives as ‘whether … or’. *Αἰτίᾳ* (dat. sg. of *αἰτία*) gives cause rather than the *forza* of *per forza di demon*. *Δαίμονος … ὠθοῦντος* depends on *αἰτίᾳ*: the genitive masculine singular present active participle of *ὠθέω* qualifies *δαίμονος* in place of the Italian relative clause. *Εἰς γῆν* gives the goal; the verb means ‘pushing’ rather than *tira*, and there is no expressed object for *il*.
- For the alternative cause Musurus changes from a bare dative to *ἐκ πνιγμονῆς*, ‘from a choking fit’; *altra* is not separately represented. *Ναρκούσης* (gen. fem. sg. pres. act. participle of *ναρκόω*) agrees with *πνιγμονῆς* and takes *τοὺς ἀνθρώπους* as object: ‘numbing people’ interprets *che lega l’ uomo*, with a generic plural instead of a generic singular.

> 115 quando si leva, che intorno si mira  
> 116 tutto smarrito dalla grande angoscia  
> 117 ch' egli ha sofferta, e guardando sospira:  

who, when he rises, looks fixtly round him, all bewildered by the great anguish he has undergone, and looking sighs:

> 115 Ἐγειρόμενος ἑαυτοῦ πέριξ βλέπει,  
> (Who upon rising looks around himself,)
>
> 116 Ὅλος τεταραγμένος ἐξ ἧς ὑπέστη  
> (Wholly bewildered by that which he has undergone,)
>
> 117 Μεγάλης ἀγωνίας, καὶ βλέπων στένει,  
> (Great anguish, and looking sighs:)

- *Ἐγειρόμενος* (nom. masc. sg. pres. middle-passive participle of *ἐγείρω*) turns *quando si leva* into ‘on rising’, with the same subject as *βλέπει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *βλέπω*). *Ἑαυτοῦ πέριξ* gives ‘around himself’: *πέριξ* follows its reflexive genitive. Greek thus attaches the reflexive to the spatial expression rather than to a verb corresponding to *si mira*.
- *Ὅλος τεταραγμένος* follows *tutto smarrito*: *ὅλος* agrees with the man, and *τεταραγμένος* (nom. masc. sg. perf. middle-passive participle of *ταράσσω*) describes his resulting state, ‘wholly disquieted’.
- *Ἐξ ἧς ὑπέστη μεγάλης ἀγωνίας* interweaves what Italian keeps in sequence, *dalla grande angoscia ch’ egli ha sofferta*. *Ἐξ* governs the genitive of cause, *μεγάλης ἀγωνίας*; the relative *ἧς* is attracted into that genitive although it represents the object of *ὑπέστη* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὑφίστημι*, here transitive, ‘underwent’). The antecedent is drawn into the relative construction and placed after the verb.
- *Καὶ βλέπων στένει* closely reproduces *e guardando sospira*: the Greek repeats the stem of *βλέπει* in the simultaneous present active participle *βλέπων*, and *στένει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *στένω*) gives ‘groans’ or ‘sighs’.

> 118 tal era il peccator levato poscia.  

such was the sinner when he rose.

> 118 Τοιοῦτος ἦν ὁ πάσχων ἀναστὰς πάλιν.  
> (Such was the sufferer when he stood up again.)

- *Τοιοῦτος ἦν* answers *οἷος* with the normal Greek qualitative correlative, closely following *tal era*. *Ὁ πάσχων* (articular nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *πάσχω*) means ‘the one suffering’, not *il peccator*. *Ἀναστὰς πάλιν* gives ‘having stood up again’ for *levato poscia*: *ἀναστάς* is the intransitive second aorist active participle of *ἀνίστημι*, and *πάλιν* marks return rather than merely subsequent time.

> 119 O potenzia di Dio, quant' è severa,  
> 120 che cotai colpi per vendetta croscia!  

[Power] of God! o how severe, that showers such blows in vengeance!

> 119 Ὡς εἶς αὐστηρά, Θεία Δικαιοσύνη,  
> (How stern you are, O Divine Justice,)
>
> 120 Τοιαύταις πληγαῖς τοὺς κακοὺς ἐκδικοῦσα!  
> (Taking vengeance on the wicked with such blows!)

- *Ὡς … αὐστηρά* puts exclamatory ‘how stern’ first, postponing the address that opens *O potenzia di Dio*. The printed *εἶς* functions as the 2nd sg. present indicative ‘you are’ (ordinary Attic *εἶ*): Musurus thus makes the address explicitly second person where *quant’ è severa* has a third-person verb. *Αὐστηρά* is predicate feminine singular.
- *Θεία Δικαιοσύνη*, ‘Divine Justice’, substitutes an adjective for *di Dio* and justice for *potenzia*. The feminine singular forms serve as a vocative without *ὦ*. *Ἐκδικοῦσα* (nom. fem. sg. pres. act. participle of *ἐκδικέω*, also identical in form to the vocative) refers to this addressee; instead of the finite relative *che … croscia*, the Greek has ‘taking vengeance’.
- *Τοιαύταις πληγαῖς* keeps the qualitative demonstrative of *cotai colpi* but makes the blows instrumental, ‘with such blows’, not the object of ‘showers’. *Per vendetta* is absorbed into *ἐκδικοῦσα*, and *τοὺς κακούς*, the substantivized acc. masc. pl. of *κακός*, supplies an object absent from the Italian: the participle is used in the punitive sense ‘punishing the wicked’.

## Lines 121–151 — Vanni Fucci’s Confession and Prophecy

> 121 Lo duca il domandò poi chi egli era;  

The Guide then asked him who he was;

> 121 Εἶτ᾿ αὐτόν, τίς ἔστ᾽, ἠρώτησ᾽ ὁ Προστάτης·  
> (Then my Master asked him who he was;)

- *Εἶτ᾿* (elided *εἶτα*) moves *poi* to the opening; *ὁ Προστάτης* ‘the protector, leader’ moves *Lo duca* to the end. *ἠρώτησ᾿* is *ἠρώτησε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐρωτάω*, with the augment retained and the final vowel elided before the article. Its personal object, *αὐτόν*, precedes the question.
- *τίς ἔστ᾽* ‘who he is’ has the nominative interrogative as predicate and the present indicative of *εἰμί* (elided *ἐστί*). After a past verb of asking, Greek can retain the tense and mood of the original question; there is no oblique optative, no counterpart to the imperfect *era*, and no expressed subject corresponding to *egli*.

> 122 per ch’ ei rispose: “Io piovvi di Toscana,  
> 123 poco tempo è, in questa gola fera.  

whereupon he answered: "I rained from Tuscany, short while ago, into this fierce gullet.

> 122 Ὁ δ᾽ ἀπεκρίθη· “Προσφάτως ἐκ Τουσκίας  
> (And he answered: “Recently from Tuscany)
>
> 123 „ Εἰς τόνδ᾽ ἔπεσον τὸν θηριώδη βρόγχον·  
> („ I fell into this savage throat;)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ ἀπεκρίθη* ‘and he answered’ replaces the connective *per ch’* with postpositive *δέ* (elided before the verb); *ὁ δέ* uses the article pronominally to mark the change of speaker. *ἀπεκρίθη* is the 3rd sg. aorist indicative of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, passive in form but not in sense.
- *Προσφάτως* ‘recently’ takes the place of the clause *poco tempo è* and stands first in the reply. *ἐκ Τουσκίας* (gen. of origin) is balanced by *εἰς τόνδ᾽ … τὸν θηριώδη βρόγχον* (acc. of goal). *τόνδ᾽* is elided *τόνδε*, the demonstrative in the regular position outside the article and noun; the verb intervenes between them.
- *ἔπεσον* is here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *πίπτω*. It gives the motion of *Io piovvi* without its image of raining. *θηριώδη* (acc. sg. masc. of *θηριώδης*) means ‘beastlike, savage’, qualifying *βρόγχον* ‘throat’ for *gola fera*.

> 124 Vita bestial mi piacque, e non umana,  
> 125 sì come a mul ch’ io fui; son Vanni Fucci  
> 126 bestia, e Pistoia mi fu degna tana.”  

Bestial life, not human, pleased me, mule that I was; I am Vanni Fucci, savage beast; and Pistoia was a fitting den for me."

> 124 „ Κτήνους, οὐκ ἀνθρώπινον, βίον εἱλόμην,  
> („ A beast's life, not a human one, I chose,)
>
> 125 „ Ἡμίονος ὤν· εἰμὶ δὲ Βάννης Φούκκης,  
> („ Being a mule; I am Vanni Fucci,)
>
> 126 „ Ὁ θήρ· Πιστωρία δ᾽ ἦν κευθμών μοι πρέπων.”  
> („ The beast; and Pistoia was a fitting lair for me.”)

- *Κτήνους, οὐκ ἀνθρώπινον, βίον εἱλόμην* ‘a beast’s life, not a human one, I chose’: *κτήνους* (contracted gen. sg. of *κτῆνος*) depends on *βίον*, whereas *ἀνθρώπινον* agrees with it. The two Italian adjectives thus receive grammatically different renderings. Instead of *vita* as subject of *mi piacque*, *βίον* is the object of *εἱλόμην*, 1st sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *αἱρέω*; the middle means ‘choose for oneself’.
- *Ἡμίονος ὤν* ‘being a mule’ reduces *sì come a mul ch’ io fui* to a circumstantial participle with a predicate nominative. *ὤν* (nom. sg. masc., pres. act. part. of *εἰμί*) is simultaneous with the choice, not an independent past-tense assertion.
- *εἰμὶ δὲ Βάννης Φούκκης, ὁ θήρ* gives both names Greek nominative endings and adds the article to the apposition *bestia*: ‘the beast’, with no separate adjective for “savage”. *Πιστωρία δ᾽ ἦν κευθμών μοι πρέπων* retains the imperfect *fu* in sense with *ἦν* ‘was’. *πρέπων*, pres. act. part. of *πρέπω*, agrees with the predicate noun *κευθμών* ‘hiding-place, lair’, not with *Πιστωρία*; *μοι* is the person for whom the lair was suited.

> 127 Ed io al duca: “Digli che non mucci,  
> 128 e domanda qual colpa quaggiù il pinse:  
> 129 ch’ io il vidi uomo di sangue e di crucci.”  

And I to the Guide: "Tell him not to budge; and ask what crime thrust him down here, for I saw him once a man of rage and blood."

> 127 Κἀγὼ Προστάτῃ· “Λέγ᾽ αὐτῷ μὴ κινεῖσθαι·  
> (And I to my Master: “Tell him not to stir;)
>
> 128 „ Πυνθάνου, τί πταῖσμ᾽ ἔῤῥιψ᾽ ἐν τόπῳ τῷδε·  
> („ Ask what crime cast him down into this place;)
>
> 129 „ Φίλαιμον καὶ φίλεριν αὐτὸν γὰρ ᾔδειν.”  
> („ For I knew him as a man of blood and strife.”)

- *Κἀγὼ Προστάτῃ* has crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ* and a dative of address in the sense of recipient, not a vocative. Like *Ed io al duca*, it leaves the verb of speaking understood. *Λέγ᾽* (elided *λέγε*, 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *λέγω*) takes *αὐτῷ* and the infinitive *μὴ κινεῖσθαι* ‘not to stir’ (pres. mid./pass. of *κινέω*). The infinitive with prohibitive *μή* replaces *che non mucci* ‘that he not slip away’.
- *Πυνθάνου* is the contracted 2nd sg. pres. mid. imperative of *πυνθάνομαι* ‘ask, ascertain’; it follows without the conjunction of *e domanda*. *τί πταῖσμ᾽* (elided *πταῖσμα*) is the neuter nominative subject of *ἔῤῥιψ᾽*, elided *ἔρριψε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ῥίπτω*, with doubled rho after the augment. Italian expresses the person thrust down (*il*); Greek leaves the object understood. *ἐν τόπῳ τῷδε* ‘in this place’ gives the resulting location, not the downward direction of *quaggiù*.
- Postpositive *γάρ* makes *ch’* explicitly causal. *ᾔδειν*, 1st sg. pluperfect of *οἶδα* with imperfect sense, gives ‘I knew’ rather than *io il vidi*. *Φίλαιμον καὶ φίλεριν* ‘blood-loving and strife-loving’ are predicate accusatives with *αὐτόν*, placed first for emphasis; the Greek expresses dispositions by compounds rather than by *uomo* with two dependent nouns.

> 130 E il peccator, che intese, non s’ infinse,  
> 131 ma drizzò verso me l’ animo e il volto,  
> 132 e di trista vergogna si dipinse;  

And the sinner who heard, feigned not; but directed towards me his mind and face, with a look of dismal shame;

> 130 Ἀκούσας μου δ᾽ ὁ πάσχων οὐχ ὑπεστάλη,  
> (And the sufferer, hearing me, did not shrink back,)
>
> 131 Ἀλλ᾽ ἔτρεψε πρὸς ἐμὲ νοῦν τε καὶ βλέμμα,  
> (But turned toward me both his mind and gaze,)
>
> 132 Ὑπὸ λυπηρᾶς καταληφθεὶς αἰσχύνης.  
> (Overtaken by bitter shame.)

- *Ἀκούσας μου δ᾽ ὁ πάσχων* substitutes two participles for *il peccator, che intese*. *ἀκούσας* (nom. sg. masc., aor. act. of *ἀκούω*) gives the prior act of hearing, with the speaker supplied in the genitive *μου*; the articular present active participle *ὁ πάσχων* means ‘the one suffering’, not ‘the sinner’. *οὐχ ὑπεστάλη* ‘did not draw back’ interprets *non s’infinse* in terms of his response rather than of pretence. *ὑπεστάλη* is 3rd sg. second aor. pass. ind. of *ὑποστέλλω*, used intransitively; *οὐχ* is the form before a rough breathing.
- *Ἀλλ᾽ ἔτρεψε πρὸς ἐμὲ νοῦν τε καὶ βλέμμα* keeps the order of *ma drizzò verso me l’animo e il volto*. *ἔτρεψε* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *τρέπω*; *τε καί* couples its two accusative objects, ‘both mind and gaze’. *βλέμμα* renders the look rather than the face itself.
- *Ὑπὸ λυπηρᾶς καταληφθεὶς αἰσχύνης* makes *e di trista vergogna si dipinse* subordinate: ‘overtaken by grievous shame’. *καταληφθείς* (nom. sg. masc., aor. pass. part. of *καταλαμβάνω*) agrees with *ὁ πάσχων*. The adjective and noun of the agent phrase *ὑπὸ λυπηρᾶς … αἰσχύνης* enclose the participle; the Italian image of colouring disappears.

> 133 poi disse: “Più mi duol che tu m’ hai colto  
> 134 nella miseria dove tu mi vedi,  
> 135 che quando fui dell’ altra vita tolto.  

then he said: "It pains me more that thou hast caught me in the misery wherein thou seest me, than when I was taken from the other life.

> 133 Εἶτ᾽ εἶπε· “Μᾶλλον λυποῦμ᾽ ἀπαντηθείς σοι  
> (Then he said: “It grieves me more to have been met by you)
>
> 134 „ Ἐν ᾗ με βλέπεις ἐνταῦθα δυστυχίᾳ,  
> („ In the misery in which you see me here,)
>
> 135 „ Ἤπερ ὅτ’ ἐκ τῆς ἄλλης ζωῆς ἡρπάσθην.  
> („ Than when I was snatched away from the other life.)

- *Εἶτ᾽ εἶπε* ‘then he said’ has elided *εἶτα* and the 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. used with *λέγω*. *Μᾶλλον λυποῦμ᾽* (elided *λυποῦμαι*, 1st sg. pres. pass. ind. of *λυπέω*) makes the sufferer the subject: ‘I am more grieved’. *ἀπαντηθείς σοι* (nom. sg. masc., aor. pass. part. of *ἀπαντάω*) gives the occasion of the grief instead of the finite clause *che tu m’hai colto*. The passive ‘met by you’ is unusual beside classical active *ἀπαντάω* ‘meet’ with a dative of the person encountered; Greek also loses the force of being *caught*.
- *Ἐν ᾗ με βλέπεις ἐνταῦθα δυστυχίᾳ* draws the antecedent *δυστυχίᾳ* ‘misery’ into the relative construction and postpones it to the end. *ᾗ* is dat. sg. fem., agreeing with the noun and governed by *ἐν*. *με βλέπεις* is present (‘you see me’), as in *tu mi vedi*; *ἐνταῦθα* supplies ‘here’.
- *Ἤπερ* ‘than’ answers *μᾶλλον* and introduces the comparison with *ὅτ᾽* (elided *ὅτε*) ‘when’. *ἐκ τῆς ἄλλης ζωῆς* uses *ἐκ* with the genitive of separation; *ἡρπάσθην* (1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ἁρπάζω*) preserves the passive of *fui … tolto* but strengthens ‘taken’ to ‘snatched’.

> 136 Io non posso negar quel che tu chiedi:  

I cannot deny thee what thou askest:

> 136 „ Οὐκ ἔχω σοι, πῶς ὃ ζητεῖς ἀρνηθῆναι.  
> („ I cannot refuse you what you ask.)

- *Οὐκ ἔχω … πῶς … ἀρνηθῆναι* is literally ‘I have no way to deny’, for *Io non posso negar*. The infinitive *ἀρνηθῆναι* is aorist passive in form, active in sense, from *ἀρνέομαι*. The fronted *σοι* supplies the person refused, not expressed as an indirect object in the Italian.
- *ὃ ζητεῖς* ‘what you seek’ is a free relative, object of the infinitive and placed between *πῶς* and that infinitive. Within the relative, *ὃ* is neut. acc. sg., object of *ζητεῖς* (contracted 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ζητέω*).

> 137 in giù son messo tanto, perch’ io fui  
> 138 ladro alla sacrestia de’ belli arredi;  
> 139 e falsamente già fu apposto altrui.  

I am put down so far, because I robbed the sacristy of its goodly furniture; and falsely once it was imputed to others.

> 137 „ Ἐβλήθην τόσον κάτω, κλέπτης ὑπάρξας  
> („ I was cast down so low because I was the thief)
>
> 138 „ Τῶν ἐν τῷ ναῷ πανσέπτων κοσμημάτων,  
> („ Of the all-revered ornaments in the church,)
>
> 139 „ Ἀντ’ ἐμοῦ ψευδῶς αἰτιαθέντος ἄλλου.  
> („ While another was falsely accused in my stead.)

- *Ἐβλήθην τόσον κάτω* ‘I was cast so far down’ gives an event rather than the present resulting state of *in giù son messo tanto*. *ἐβλήθην* is 1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *βάλλω*; *τόσον* is adverbial accusative of extent. *κλέπτης ὑπάρξας* ‘having been a thief’ renders *perch’ io fui ladro* by a causal participle: *ὑπάρξας* (nom. sg. masc., aor. act. of *ὑπάρχω*) has the predicate nominative *κλέπτης*.
- *Τῶν ἐν τῷ ναῷ πανσέπτων κοσμημάτων* is the genitive of the things stolen, dependent on *κλέπτης*. The location *ἐν τῷ ναῷ* stands inside the article–noun group: ‘the all-revered ornaments in the sanctuary’. It is less specific than *alla sacrestia*, while *πανσέπτων* stresses reverence rather than the beauty of *belli*.
- *Ἀντ’ ἐμοῦ ψευδῶς αἰτιαθέντος ἄλλου* ‘another having been falsely accused instead of me’ changes the subject from the imputed deed to the accused person. *ἄλλου* and *αἰτιαθέντος* (gen. sg. masc., aor. pass. part. of *αἰτιάομαι*) form a genitive absolute; *ἀντ᾽* (elided *ἀντί*) governs the separate genitive *ἐμοῦ*. Greek specifies one other person for *altrui*, adds ‘instead of me’, and has no temporal equivalent of *già*.

> 140 Ma perchè di tal vista tu non godi,  
> 141 se mai sarai di fuor de’ lochi bui,  
> 142 apri gli orecchi al mio annunzio, ed odi:  

But that thou mayest not joy in this sight, if ever thou escape the dark abodes, open thy ears and hear what I announce:

> 140 „ Ἀλλ’ ἵνα σὺ μὴ χαίρῃς ἐπὶ τῇ θέᾳ  
> („ But lest you rejoice at this sight,)
>
> 141 „ Τῇ δ’, ἤν ποτε τῶν τόπων τούτων ἐξέλθῃς,  
> („ If ever you go forth from these regions,)
>
> 142 „ Ὦτ’ ἄνοιξον κἄκουσον, ἃ προαγγέλλω.  
> („ Open your ears and hear what I foretell.)

- *Ἀλλ’ ἵνα σὺ μὴ χαίρῃς* preserves the negative purpose of *Ma perchè … tu non godi*: *ἵνα μή* with *χαίρῃς*, 2nd sg. pres. act. subj. of *χαίρω*. The explicit *σύ* precedes the negation. *ἐπὶ τῇ θέᾳ τῇ δ’* ‘at this sight’ gives the occasion of rejoicing by *ἐπί* with the dative. The printed *τῇ δ’* represents the demonstrative *τῇδε* with final vowel elided; it completes the phrase across the line break.
- *ἤν ποτε … ἐξέλθῃς* has conditional *ἤν* (= *ἐάν*) with *ποτε* ‘ever’ and the 2nd sg. second aor. act. subj. of *ἐξέρχομαι*. Greek makes *se mai sarai di fuor* an eventual act of leaving rather than a future state of being outside. *τῶν τόπων τούτων* is genitive of separation, ‘from these places’; the demonstrative replaces *bui* rather than translating it.
- *Ὦτ’ ἄνοιξον κἄκουσον* has elision of *ὦτα* (acc. **plural**, not dual, of *οὖς*) and crasis of *καὶ ἄκουσον*. Both verbs are 2nd sg. aor. act. imperatives, of *ἀνοίγω* and *ἀκούω*. Where *al mio annunzio* accompanies *apri gli orecchi*, Greek gives the second imperative its own object, *ἃ προαγγέλλω* ‘the things I foretell’ (*ἃ*, neut. acc. pl.; *προαγγέλλω*, 1st sg. pres. act. ind.).

> 143 Pistoia in pria di Negri si dimagra,  
> 144 poi Fiorenza rinnuova genti e modi.  

Pistoia first is thinned of Neri; then Florence renovates her people and her laws.

> 143 „ Πιστωρία Μελάνων πρῶτον γυμνοῦται·  
> („ Pistoia is first stripped of the Blacks;)
>
> 144 „ Φλωρεντία δ’ εἶτ’ ἀστοὺς νόμους τ’ ἀλλάσσει·  
> („ Then Florence changes her citizens and laws;)

- *Πιστωρία Μελάνων πρῶτον γυμνοῦται* closely follows the order of *Pistoia in pria di Negri si dimagra* except that the genitive precedes ‘first’. *Μελάνων* is gen. pl. of substantivized *μέλας* ‘black’, a genitive of deprivation with *γυμνοῦται* (contracted 3rd sg. pres. pass. ind. of *γυμνόω*). ‘Is stripped of’ replaces the reflexive image of growing lean.
- *Φλωρεντία δ’ εἶτ’ ἀστοὺς νόμους τ’ ἀλλάσσει* retains the present tense and correlates *πρῶτον* with *εἶτα* (elided). The accusative plurals *ἀστούς* ‘citizens’ and *νόμους* ‘laws, usages’ narrow *genti e modi*; the second has postpositive *τ᾽* (elided *τε*) ‘and’. *ἀλλάσσει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἀλλάσσω*) means ‘changes’, without the explicit idea of renewal in *rinnuova*.

> 145 Tragge Marte vapor di Val di Magra  
> 146 ch’ è di torbidi nuvoli involuto,  

Mars brings from Valdimagra a fiery vapour, which is wrapt in turbid clouds,

> 145 „ Ἄρης ἀτμὸν ἐξ ἄνω κατάξει Μάκρας,  
> („ Mars shall bring down a vapour from upper Magra,)
>
> 146 „ Συμπεφυρμένον θολοέσσαις νεφέλαις·  
> („ Wrapt round with turbid clouds;)

- *Ἄρης ἀτμὸν ἐξ ἄνω κατάξει Μάκρας* puts the subject first and changes present *Tragge* to *κατάξει*, 3rd sg. fut. act. ind. of *κατάγω* ‘bring down’. *ἀτμόν* ‘vapour’ is unqualified, as is *vapor* in the Italian. *ἐξ … Μάκρας* gives the source in the genitive, with adverbial *ἄνω* ‘above, up-country’ specifying upper Magra rather than translating *Val* by a noun for ‘valley’.
- *Συμπεφυρμένον* (acc. sg. masc., perf. mid./pass. part. of *συμφύρω*) agrees with *ἀτμόν*, replacing *ch’è … involuto* by a participle denoting a resulting state. Its sense is ‘mingled with’ rather than simply ‘enveloped in’; *θολοέσσαις νεφέλαις* is the associative dative plural. *θολοέσσαις* has the poetic feminine formation in *-εσσα* (*θολόεις*, ‘turbid’).

> 147 e con tempesta impetuosa ed agra  
> 148 sopra campo Picen fia combattuto;  

and with angry and impetuous storm [a battle] shall be [fought] on Piceno's field;

> 147 „ Ἐν θυέλλῃ δὲ σφοδρᾷ καὶ δεινῇ μάχῃ  
> („ And in a fierce tempest and terrible battle)
>
> 148 „ Ἐπὶ Πικηνοῦ γενήσεται πεδίου,  
> („ It shall come to pass upon the plain of Piceno,)

- *Ἐν θυέλλῃ δὲ σφοδρᾷ καὶ δεινῇ μάχῃ* gives two dative descriptions under *ἐν*: ‘in a vehement tempest and a terrible battle’. Musurus distributes the force of *impetuosa ed agra* between the two nouns. He supplies *μάχῃ*, but as a dative of circumstance, not a nominative subject ‘a battle’.
- *Ἐπὶ Πικηνοῦ γενήσεται πεδίου* has locative *ἐπί* with the genitive; *Πικηνοῦ* agrees with *πεδίου*, from which the verb separates it. *γενήσεται* is 3rd sg. fut. mid. ind. of *γίγνομαι* ‘come to be, take place’, not a passive ‘shall be fought’. The idea of fighting in *fia combattuto* is thus carried by *μάχῃ*; the Greek verb has no newly expressed subject.

> 149 ond’ ei repente spezzerà la nebbia,  
> 150 sì ch’ ogni Bianco ne sarà feruto.  

whence it suddenly shall rend the mist, so that every Bianco shall be wounded by it.

> 149 „ Ὅθεν ἐξαίφνης τὴν ὀμίχλην σκεδάσει,  
> („ Whence suddenly it shall scatter the mist,)
>
> 150 „ Ὥστε πᾶς Λευκὸς κεραυνόβλητος ἔσται.  
> („ So that every White shall be thunder-smitten.)

- *Ὅθεν ἐξαίφνης τὴν ὀμίχλην σκεδάσει* renders *ond’ ei repente spezzerà la nebbia* with the relative adverb *ὅθεν* ‘whence’ and no pronoun for *ei*. *σκεδάσει* (3rd sg. fut. act. ind. of *σκεδάζω*) takes *τὴν ὀμίχλην* as its object: the mist will be ‘dispersed’ rather than ‘broken’.
- *Ὥστε πᾶς Λευκὸς κεραυνόβλητος ἔσται* retains *sì ch’* as a result conjunction with a finite verb, not an infinitive: the result is asserted as future. *πᾶς Λευκός* is distributive singular, ‘every White’. The predicate adjective *κεραυνόβλητος* ‘struck by lightning’ with *ἔσται* (3rd sg. fut. ind. of *εἰμί*) replaces *sarà feruto*; the compound specifies the striking force instead of translating *ne* by a separate pronoun.

> 151 E detto l’ ho, perchè doler ti debbia.”  

And I have said this [so that] it [may] grieve thee."

> 151 „ Εἶπον δὲ τοῦτο· λυπήσειν γάρ σε μέλλει.”  
> („ And I have told you this, for it will grieve you.”)

- *Εἶπον δὲ τοῦτο* has postpositive *δέ* for *E*, and *τοῦτο* ‘this’ for the object *l’*. *εἶπον* is here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind., a simple past for the compound past *detto l’ho*.
- *λυπήσειν γάρ σε μέλλει* ‘for it is going to grieve you’ changes *perchè doler ti debbia* from a statement of purpose to a reason given as a prediction. *γάρ* is postpositive; *λυπήσειν*, fut. act. infinitive of *λυπέω*, depends on *μέλλει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *μέλλω*). *σε* is the accusative object of the transitive infinitive, not its subject: what was said is understood as the cause of the grief.

## Summary — Parallel Phrasing, Recast Clauses

Musurus often follows Dante’s syntax most closely where a phrase is repeated or answered. The two occurrences of *così* are matched by *οὕτω* … *οὕτω*; *qual* … *tal* becomes *οἷος* … *τοιοῦτος*. Even when the construction changes, a verbal echo can survive: *vinci* … *vince* reappears as *νίκα* … *νικώσης*, though the second verb is now a participle rather than the verb of a relative clause. The particles are less strictly matched. Postpositive *γάρ* can reproduce explanatory *chè* precisely, while *δέ* or *τε* often simply links successive actions. The choice can affect the logic: in the final line *γάρ* gives a reason, and *μέλλει* with the future infinitive *λυπήσειν* predicts an effect that Dante’s *perchè* presents as intended.

The participle is Musurus’s most versatile means of redistributing the work of a sentence. A present participle can follow an Italian gerund closely, as *βλέπων* does *veggendo*, but the same phrase also shows what changes: for *il mondo aver cangiata faccia*, Greek has *τὸν κόσμον* with the agreeing aorist participle *μεταβαλόντα*, presenting the change as already accomplished when seen. Elsewhere a finite action becomes preliminary to another: *λαβών*, ‘having taken’, subordinates *prende* to *ἐκβαίνει*, ‘he goes out’. Case can carry a relation for which Italian uses a whole clause. At the false accusation, the genitive absolute *αἰτιαθέντος ἄλλου*, ‘another having been accused’, both subordinates the event and shifts attention from the deed imputed to the person accused.

Musurus is often more literal about a relation than about the image that conveys it. *Ἐλπίδ᾽ ἀναλαμβάνει*, ‘takes up hope again’, keeps the recovery in *la speranza ringavagna* without the basket; *τῷ τοῦ σώματος βάρει*, ‘to the weight of the body’, keeps the bodily impediment but makes *grave* a noun and the weight something to which one yields. Conversely, the Greek can supply an image more specific than Dante’s: *κεραυνόβλητος*, ‘struck by lightning’, draws the storm into *sarà feruto* itself. The sharpest substitutions are not necessarily the most elaborate. *Ὁ πάσχων*, ‘the one suffering’, twice stands for *il peccator*, and *Θεία Δικαιοσύνη*, ‘Divine Justice’, for *potenzia di Dio*: in each case the phrasing remains compact, but the name Musurus chooses is already an interpretation.
