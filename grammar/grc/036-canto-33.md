# Inferno 33

## Lines 1–18 — Ugolino Begins to Speak

> 1 La bocca sollevò dal fiero pasto  
> 2 quel peccator, forbendola ai capelli  
> 3 del capo, ch’ egli avea di retro guasto.  

From the fell repast that sinner raised his mouth, wiping it upon the hair of the head he had laid waste behind.

> 1 Ὁ δ᾽ ἁμαρτωλὸς τὸ στόμ᾽ ἐκ τῆς φρικώδους  
> (And the sinner, his mouth from the dreadful)
>
> 2 Βορᾶς ἐξάρας, ἀπομαξάμενός τε  
> (Repast raising, and wiping it)
>
> 3 Ταῖς θριξὶν οὗπερ κατέδακνε κρανίου,  
> (On the hair of the skull which he was gnawing,)

- Italian puts the object *La bocca* first and postpones the subject *quel peccator* until line 2. Greek begins with the subject *ὁ δ᾽ ἁμαρτωλός* ‘and the sinner’: the article replaces the demonstrative, and postpositive *δέ*, elided before a vowel, links the action to what precedes. *Τὸ στόμ᾽* is *τὸ στόμα* (acc. sg. neut.), the object of *ἐξάρας*; as with Italian *la bocca*, the article suffices where English uses “his.”
- *Ἐκ τῆς φρικώδους βορᾶς* renders *dal fiero pasto* with *ἐκ* and the genitive of separation. *Φρικώδους* (gen. sg. fem. of *φρικώδης*) means ‘causing a shudder’; *βορά* is ‘food’ or ‘feeding’. *Ἐξάρας* (nom. sg. masc., aor. act. participle of *ἐξαίρω*) changes the finite *sollevò* into ‘having raised’: the Greek waits until *ἤρξατο* in line 4 for its main verb.
- *Ἀπομαξάμενός τε* ‘and having wiped’ (aor. mid. participle of *ἀπομάσσω*) is coordinated by enclitic *τε* with *ἐξάρας* and agrees with the same subject. Italian *forbendola* presents the wiping as accompanying the raising and explicitly resumes ‘mouth’ with *-la*; Greek presents both actions as preliminary to speaking, with no repeated object. *Ταῖς θριξίν* (dat. pl. of *θρίξ*) gives the means of wiping where *ai capelli* gives the surface.
- *Οὗπερ κατέδακνε κρανίου* puts *κρανίου* ‘skull’, the genitive dependent on ‘hair’, after its relative clause. *Οὗπερ* is attracted into the genitive of *κρανίου* instead of the accusative expected as object of *κατέδακνε*; *-περ* singles out the very skull in question. *Κατέδακνε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *καταδάκνω*, with augment after the preverb) gives the ongoing action ‘he was gnawing’ for the result expressed by *avea … guasto*; *di retro* has no equivalent.

> 4 Poi cominciò: “Tu vuoi ch' io rinnovelli  
> 5 disperato dolor che il cor mi preme,  
> 6 già pur pensando, pria ch' io ne favelli.  

Then he began: “Thou willest that I renew desperate grief, which wrings my heart, even at the very thought, before I tell thereof.

> 4 Ἤρξατο λέγων· “Θέλεις μ᾽ ἀνανεῶσαι  
> (Began speaking: “You wish me to renew)
>
> 5 „ Τὸ τὴν καρδίαν θλίβον μου δεινὸν ἄλγος  
> („ The dread sorrow that crushes my heart)
>
> 6 „ Τῇ μνήμῃ μόνῃ, πρὶν ἂν λόγοις ἐκφράσω.  
> („ By memory alone, before I utter it in words.)

- *Ἤρξατο λέγων* ‘he began speaking’ makes explicit what *cominciò* leaves to the direct speech. *Ἤρξατο* (3rd sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἄρχομαι*, with temporal augment) completes the construction begun in line 1; *λέγων* is a supplementary present active participle agreeing with its subject. There is no separate adverb for *Poi*.
- *Θέλεις μ᾽ ἀνανεῶσαι* replaces *Tu vuoi ch’ io rinnovelli*, a finite clause with the subjunctive, by an accusative and infinitive after ‘you wish’. Elided *μ᾽* (*με*) is the subject of *ἀνανεῶσαι* (aor. act. inf. of *ἀνανεόω*); the subject of *θέλεις* is expressed by the verbal ending alone.
- *Τὸ τὴν καρδίαν θλίβον μου δεινὸν ἄλγος* encloses the equivalent of *che il cor mi preme* between the article and *ἄλγος*. *Θλίβον* (acc. sg. neut., pres. act. participle of *θλίβω*) agrees with *ἄλγος* and takes *τὴν καρδίαν* as object; enclitic *μου* marks the heart as ‘mine’. *Δεινόν* ‘terrible’ renders *disperato* by the intensity of the pain rather than by hopelessness.
- *Τῇ μνήμῃ μόνῃ* ‘by recollection alone’ replaces *già pur pensando*: an instrumental dative with an agreeing adjective replaces the gerund, without a separate ‘already’. *Πρὶν ἂν … ἐκφράσω* ‘before I express it’ has *ἄν* with the 1st sg. aor. act. subjunctive of *ἐκφράζω* for an event still prospective, answering to *pria ch’ io ne favelli*. *Λόγοις* ‘in words’ is supplied as a dative of means; the grief is understood as object.

> 7 Ma se le mie parole esser den seme,  
> 8 che frutti infamia al traditor ch' io rodo,  
> 9 parlare e lagrimar vedrai insieme.  

But if my words are to be a seed, that may bear fruit of infamy to the traitor whom I gnaw, thou shalt see me speak and weep at the same time.

> 7 „ Ἀλλ᾽ εἰ μέλλουσ᾽ οἱ ’μοὶ λόγοι παρέξειν  
> („ Yet if my words are to yield)
>
> 8 „ Ὡς καρπὸν αἶσχος ᾧ βιβρώσκω προδότῃ,  
> („ As fruit infamy to the traitor whom I devour,)
>
> 9 „ Ἅμα λέγοντος καὶ θρηνοῦντος ἀκούσῃ.  
> („ You shall hear me speaking and weeping at once.)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ εἰ μέλλουσ᾽ οἱ ’μοὶ λόγοι παρέξειν* ‘but if my words are going to yield’ retains *Ma se* and the prospective force of *den*, but drops *esser … seme*. *Ἀλλ᾽* is elided *ἀλλά*; *μέλλουσ᾽* is elided *μέλλουσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *μέλλω*), governing *παρέξειν* (fut. act. inf. of *παρέχω*). In *οἱ ’μοὶ λόγοι*, ‘my words’, the initial vowel of possessive *ἐμοί* (nom. pl. masc. of *ἐμός*) is lost by prodelision after the article.
- *Ὡς καρπὸν αἶσχος* ‘disgrace as fruit’ preserves the fruit image without its relative clause *che frutti*. *Αἶσχος* (acc. sg. neut.) is the object of *παρέξειν*; *ὡς καρπόν* states in what capacity it is yielded. In *ᾧ βιβρώσκω προδότῃ*, *προδότῃ* is dative of the recipient, as in *al traditor*; the relative *ᾧ* precedes its noun and is attracted to its dative rather than standing in the accusative as object of *βιβρώσκω*. This present active means ‘I devour’, stronger than *io rodo* ‘I gnaw’.
- *Ἅμα* brings *insieme* to the front. *Ἀκούσῃ* (2nd sg. fut. mid. ind. of *ἀκούω*, active in meaning) substitutes hearing for *vedrai*. Accordingly *parlare e lagrimar* become *λέγοντος καὶ θρηνοῦντος*, gen. sg. masc. present active participles in the construction for hearing a person in the act. The understood person is ‘me’; *θρηνοῦντος* gives audible lament rather than simply shedding tears.

> 10 I' non so chi tu sei, nè per che modo  
> 11 venuto se' quaggiù; ma Fiorentino  
> 12 mi sembri veramente, quand' io t' odo.  

I know not who thou mayest be, nor by what mode thou hast come down here; but, when I hear thee, in truth thou seemest to me a Florentine.

> 10 „ Οὐκ οἶδα, τίς εἶς, ἀλλ᾽ οὔτε πῶς κατῆλθες  
> („ I know not who you are, nor yet how you came down)
>
> 11 „ Ἐνθάδ᾽· ὅμως μοι δοκεῖς ὢν Φλωρεντῖνος  
> („ Hither; yet to me you seem to be a Florentine)
>
> 12 „ Ὄντως, ὡς ἐκ λαλιᾶς τῆς σῆς εἰκάζω.  
> („ In truth, as I gather from your speech.)

- *Οὐκ οἶδα, τίς εἶς* closely follows *I’ non so chi tu sei*. *Οἶδα* is perfect in form but present in sense, ‘I know’; *τίς* introduces an indirect question retaining the interrogative and the indicative. *Εἶς* is a by-form of the usual *εἶ*, 2nd sg. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*, not the numeral *εἷς*.
- *Ἀλλ᾽ οὔτε πῶς κατῆλθες ἐνθάδ᾽* adds a second unknown, ‘but neither [do I know] how you came down here’. The single interrogative adverb *πῶς* replaces *per che modo*. *Κατῆλθες* (2nd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *κατέρχομαι*) puts the downward direction of *quaggiù* into the verb, while elided *ἐνθάδε* gives the destination ‘here’; the simple aorist replaces *venuto se’*.
- *Ὅμως μοι δοκεῖς ὢν Φλωρεντῖνος ὄντως* ‘nevertheless you seem to me to be truly Florentine’ renders *ma Fiorentino mi sembri veramente*. *Μοι* is the dative of the judging person; *ὤν* (nom. sg. masc., pres. participle of *εἰμί*) and predicate *Φλωρεντῖνος* agree with the subject of *δοκεῖς*. The participle is notable: for ‘you seem to be’, ordinary Attic would more naturally use the infinitive *εἶναι*. *Ὄντως*, unlike *ὤν*, is an adverb, answering to *veramente*.
- *Ὡς ἐκ λαλιᾶς τῆς σῆς εἰκάζω* ‘as I infer from your speech’ interprets temporal *quand’ io t’ odo* as the grounds for the judgment. *Εἰκάζω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.) names the inference rather than the hearing; *ἐκ* with the genitive names its source. The postposed possessive *τῆς σῆς* agrees with *λαλιᾶς*.

> 13 Tu dei saper ch' io fui Conte Ugolino,  

Thou hast to know that I was Count Ugolino,

> 13 „ Κόμητά μ᾽ Οὐγολῖνον ἴσθι ποτ᾽ ὄντα.  
> („ Know that I was once Count Ugolino,)

- *Κόμητά μ᾽ Οὐγολῖνον ἴσθι ποτ᾽ ὄντα* replaces *Tu dei saper* with the direct command *ἴσθι* ‘know’ (2nd sg. imperative of *οἶδα*). In place of finite *ch’ io fui*, the verb of knowing takes elided *με* with supplementary *ὄντα* (acc. sg. masc., pres. participle of *εἰμί*); *κόμητα* and *Οὐγολῖνον* are predicates in the same case. Greek fronts the title and name; *ποτ᾽* (elided *ποτέ*) ‘once’ supplies the past reference not expressed by the participle itself.

> 14 e questi è l' Arcivescovo Ruggieri;  

and this the Archbishop Ruggieri;

> 14 „ Ἀρχιεπίσκοπος δ᾽ ἔσθ᾽ ὅδε Ῥουγείρης·  
> („ And this is the Archbishop Ruggieri;)

- *Ἀρχιεπίσκοπος δ᾽ ἔσθ᾽ ὅδε Ῥουγείρης* puts the title first and the pointing subject *ὅδε* ‘this one here’ after the copula, reversing *questi è l’ Arcivescovo*. Postpositive *δέ* continues the identification with a change of subject. *Ἔσθ᾽* represents *ἐστί* before rough-breathed *ὅδε*: the final vowel is elided and *τ* becomes *θ*. Title and name are nominative singular; the predicate title has no article.

> 15 or ti dirò perchè i son tal vicino.  

now I will tell thee why I am such a neighbour to him.

> 15 „ Ἐρῶ σοι δὲ νῦν, πῶς ὧδ᾽ εἴμ᾽ αὐτῷ γείτων.  
> („ And now I will tell you how I am thus neighbour to him.)

- *Ἐρῶ σοι δὲ νῦν* ‘and I shall tell you now’ renders *or ti dirò* with *ἐρῶ* (1st sg. fut. act. ind., the future used with *λέγω*) and the dative of the addressee. *Πῶς* introduces an indirect question of manner, ‘how’, for causal *perchè*; *ὧδε* ‘thus’ likewise replaces adjectival *tal*.
- *Εἴμ᾽ αὐτῷ γείτων* has an elided 1st sg. present of *εἰμί* and a predicate nominative, ‘I am a neighbour to him’. *Αὐτῷ*, dative with *γείτων*, makes explicit who the neighbour is in relation to, where Italian *i son tal vicino* leaves that relation more compressed.

> 16 Che per l' effetto de' suo' ma' pensieri,  
> 17 fidandomi di lui, io fossi preso  
> 18 e poscia morto, dir non è mestieri.  

That by the effect of his ill devices I, confiding in him, was taken and thereafter put to death, it is not necessary to say.

> 16 „ Ὡς ταῖς πονηραῖς αὐτοῦ βουλαῖς ὑπείκων  
> („ How, yielding to his wicked counsels)
>
> 17 „ Καὐτῷ πίστιν δούς, συνελήφθην χὐπέστην  
> („ And trusting in him, I was seized and underwent)
>
> 18 „ Εἶτα θάνατον, λέγειν οὐκ ἔστι χρεία.  
> („ Death thereafter, there is no need to tell.)

- *Ὡς … συνελήφθην χὐπέστην … θάνατον* ‘how I was seized and underwent death’ gives the content of *λέγειν* before the expression of necessity, preserving the suspension of *Che … dir non è mestieri*. *Ὡς* presents the matter as ‘how’ rather than simply ‘that’; the Greek finite verbs are indicative where Italian has *fossi preso e poscia morto* under *che*.
- *Ταῖς πονηραῖς αὐτοῦ βουλαῖς ὑπείκων* ‘yielding to his wicked counsels’ interprets rather than reproduces *per l’ effetto de’ suo’ ma’ pensieri*. The devices are no longer simply the cause of the arrest: *βουλαῖς* is dative plural governed by *ὑπείκων* (nom. sg. masc., pres. act. participle of *ὑπείκω*), so that the speaker is represented as yielding to them. *Αὐτοῦ* is a possessive genitive.
- *Καὐτῷ πίστιν δούς* ‘and having put faith in him’ coordinates a second participle with *ὑπείκων*. *Καὐτῷ* is crasis of *καὶ αὐτῷ*; the dative marks the person trusted, while *πίστιν* is the direct object of *δούς* (nom. sg. masc., aor. act. participle of *δίδωμι*). The aorist presents *fidandomi di lui* as a prior act of trust.
- *Συνελήφθην* (1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *συλλαμβάνω*) directly answers to *fossi preso*. But *χὐπέστην εἶτα θάνατον* ‘and then I underwent death’ changes the second passive expression to an active one. *Χὐπέστην* is crasis of *καὶ ὑπέστην*, with aspiration of *κ* before the rough breathing; *ὑπέστην* is the 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὑφίστημι*, with *θάνατον* as its object. *Εἶτα* carries the temporal force of *poscia*.
- *Λέγειν οὐκ ἔστι χρεία* closely follows *dir non è mestieri*: *λέγειν* is the present active infinitive, and *οὐκ ἔστι χρεία* is literally ‘there is no need’, with *χρεία* the nominative subject.

## Lines 19–51 — The Dream and the Sealing of the Tower

> 19 Però quel che non puoi avere inteso,  
> 20 ciò è come la morte mia fu cruda,  
> 21 udirai, e saprai se m' ha offeso.  

But that which thou canst not have learnt, that is, how cruel was my death, thou shalt hear—and know if he has offended me.

> 19 „ Ἀλλ᾽ ὅπερ ἴσως οὐκ ἤκουσας εἰσέτι,  
> („ But what perhaps you have not yet heard,)
>
> 20 „ Ὅσον δὴ σκληρὸς ὑπῆρξ᾽ ὁ θάνατός μου,  
> („ How cruel indeed my death was,)
>
> 21 „ Λέξω, καὶ γνώσῃ, πόσον ἠδίκησέ με.  
> („ I will tell, and you shall know how greatly he wronged me.)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ ὅπερ* ‘but precisely what’ compresses *Però quel che*: the neuter relative *ὅπερ* (*ὅ* strengthened by *-περ*) includes its own antecedent. The whole relative clause stands before the verb of telling, as in Italian. *Ἀλλ᾽* is *ἀλλά* with elision.
- *ἴσως οὐκ ἤκουσας εἰσέτι* ‘perhaps you have not yet heard’ changes the force of *non puoi avere inteso*. Instead of a negated verb of ability with a perfect infinitive, Greek has *ἤκουσας* (2nd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀκούω*, with temporal augment), qualified by *ἴσως* ‘perhaps’ and *εἰσέτι* ‘as yet’.
- *Ὅσον δὴ σκληρὸς ὑπῆρξ᾽ ὁ θάνατός μου* ‘how cruel indeed my death was’ explains *ὅπερ* without an equivalent for *ciò è*. *Ὅσον* is adverbial accusative of degree; *δή* emphasizes it. The predicate adjective *σκληρός* is masculine nominative to agree with *ὁ θάνατος*, unlike feminine *cruda* with *la morte*. *Ὑπῆρξ᾽* is elided *ὑπῆρξε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ὑπάρχω*, used as a copula.
- *Λέξω* ‘I shall tell’ (1st sg. fut. act. ind. of *λέγω*) reverses the perspective of *udirai* ‘you shall hear’. *Γνώσῃ* (2nd sg. fut. mid. ind. of *γιγνώσκω*, active in sense) keeps the person of *saprai*, but *πόσον ἠδίκησέ με* makes *se m’ ha offeso* a question of degree, ‘how greatly he wronged me’, rather than ‘whether’. *Ἠδίκησε* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀδικέω*; its extra accent in *ἠδίκησέ με* is due to enclitic *με*.

> 22 Breve pertugio dentro dalla muda,  
> 23 la qual per me ha il titol della fame  
> 24 e in che convien ancor ch' altri si chiuda,  

A narrow hole within the mew, which from me has the title of Famine, and in which others yet must be shut up,

> 22 „ Μικρόν τι διαύγιον τῆς εἱρκτῆς ἔνδον,  
> („ A small loophole within the prison,)
>
> 23 „ Ἔκτοτ᾽ ἀπ᾽ ἐμοῦ καλουμένης τῆς πείνης,  
> („ Called since then after me that of Hunger,)
>
> 24 „ Ἔνθ᾽ ἔτι καθειρχθῆναι προσήκει κἄλλους,  
> („ Wherein others also are destined yet to be shut up,)

- *Μικρόν τι διαύγιον* ‘a certain small light-opening’ is nominative, the subject held over to *ἐπέτρεψε* in line 25. *Μικρόν* renders *Breve* as smallness; enclitic neuter *τι* marks indefiniteness. In *τῆς εἱρκτῆς ἔνδον*, *ἔνδον* follows its genitive, reversing *dentro dalla muda*; *εἱρκτή* directly names a prison rather than reproducing *muda*.
- *Ἔκτοτ᾽ ἀπ᾽ ἐμοῦ καλουμένης τῆς πείνης* turns *la qual … ha il titol* into a participial phrase. *Καλουμένης* (gen. sg. fem. pres. pass. ptcp. of *καλέω*) agrees with *εἱρκτῆς*, not *διαύγιον*. *Ἔκτοτ᾽* (*ἔκτοτε*) supplies ‘from then on’; *ἀπ᾽ ἐμοῦ* (*ἀπό* with the stressed personal pronoun) gives the source of the name. *Τῆς πείνης* retains the naming genitive of *della fame*, without a noun corresponding to *titol*.
- *Ἔνθ᾽* (elided *ἔνθα*, ‘where’) takes up *in che*. Impersonal *προσήκει* ‘it is fitting, falls to’ governs *καθειρχθῆναι* (aor. pass. inf. of *καθείργω*, ‘to be shut in’), with accusative subject *κἄλλους* (crasis of *καὶ ἄλλους*, ‘others also’). This replaces *convien … ch’ altri si chiuda*, a finite subordinate clause with a singular verb; *ἔτι* retains *ancor*.

> 25 m' avea mostrato per lo suo forame  
> 26 più lune già, quand' io feci il mal sonno,  
> 27 che del futuro mi squarciò il velame.  

had through its opening already shewn me several moons, when I slept the evil sleep that rent for me the curtain of the future.

> 25 „ Διὰ τῆς ὀπῆς ὁρᾷν ἐπέτρεψέ μοι  
> („ Allowed me to see through its opening)
>
> 26 „ Πολλὰς σελήνας, ὅτ᾽ εἶδον κακὸν ὄναρ,  
> („ Many moons, when I saw an evil dream,)
>
> 27 „ Τὸν τοῦ μέλλοντος διασχίσαν μοι πέπλον.  
> („ Which rent for me the veil of the future.)

- *Διὰ τῆς ὀπῆς* ‘through the opening’ renders *per lo suo forame* with *διά* and the genitive, leaving possession implicit. *Ὁρᾷν ἐπέτρεψέ μοι* ‘allowed me to see’ replaces *m’ avea mostrato*: the aperture remains the subject, but the Italian recipient becomes the understood subject of *ὁρᾷν* (contracted pres. act. inf. of *ὁράω*). *Ἐπέτρεψε* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐπιτρέπω*, not pluperfect; *μοι* is its dative of the person permitted.
- *Πολλὰς σελήνας* ‘many moons’ is the accusative object of seeing; *πολλάς* gives *più* an absolute rather than comparative sense. There is no separate *già*. *Ὅτ᾽ εἶδον κακὸν ὄναρ* ‘when I saw a bad dream’ substitutes seeing for *feci*. *Ὅτ᾽* is *ὅτε*; *εἶδον* is here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. serving *ὁράω*. *Ὄναρ* is neuter singular.
- *Τὸν τοῦ μέλλοντος διασχίσαν μοι πέπλον* replaces *che … mi squarciò* with *διασχίσαν* (acc. sg. neut. aor. act. ptcp. of *διασχίζω*), agreeing with *ὄναρ*. Despite the intervening participle and *μοι*, *τόν* belongs with masculine *πέπλον*, its object. Between article and noun stands *τοῦ μέλλοντος*, substantivized neuter participle of *μέλλω*, ‘of what is to come’; *μοι* marks the person for whom the veil was rent.

> 28 Questi pareva a me maestro e donno,  
> 29 cacciando il lupo e i lupicini al monte,  
> 30 per che i Pisan veder Lucca non ponno.  

This *man* seemed to me lord and master, chasing the wolf and his whelps, upon the mountain for which the Pisans cannot see Lucca.

> 28 „ Ἐφάνη μοί τις αὐθέντης καὶ δεσπότης  
> („ There appeared to me a certain lord and master,)
>
> 29 „ Θηρεύων λύκον καὶ λυκιδεῖς πρὸς ὄρος,  
> („ Hunting a wolf and wolf-cubs toward the mountain)
>
> 30 „ Ὅπερ κωλύει Πισαίους ὁρᾷν Λοῦκαν.  
> („ Which prevents the Pisans from seeing Lucca.)

- *Ἐφάνη μοί τις αὐθέντης καὶ δεσπότης* shifts *Questi pareva a me maestro e donno* toward ‘there appeared to me a certain lord and master’. Indefinite *τις* replaces demonstrative *Questi*. *Ἐφάνη* is 3rd sg. second aor. pass. ind. of *φαίνω*, intransitive in sense, where *pareva* is imperfect; *μοι* is the dative of the observer. Both titles are nominative.
- *Θηρεύων* (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. ptcp. of *θηρεύω*) agrees with the person appearing and preserves the simultaneous action of *cacciando*. Its objects *λύκον καὶ λυκιδεῖς* are anarthrous, ‘a wolf and wolf-cubs’; *λυκιδεῖς* is acc. pl. of *λυκιδεύς*. *Πρὸς ὄρος* takes *al monte* directionally, ‘toward a mountain’, with *πρός* and the accusative.
- *Ὅπερ* is nom. sg. neut., referring to *ὄρος*, and itself the subject of *κωλύει*. Thus *per che … non ponno* becomes a positive verb of prevention: *κωλύει Πισαίους ὁρᾷν Λοῦκαν* ‘prevents Pisans from seeing Lucca’. *Πισαίους* is the accusative of the persons prevented, also the understood subject of the infinitive; *Λοῦκαν*, an inflected place-name, is its object.

> 31 Con cagne magre, studiose e conte,  
> 32 Gualandi con Sismondi e con Lanfranchi  
> 33 s' avea messi dinanzi dalla fronte.  

With hounds meagre, keen, and dexterous, he had put in front of him Gualandi with Sismondi, and with Lanfranchi.

> 31 „ Κυσὶ καλῶς ἀχθείσαις, ἰσχναῖς καὶ λάβραις  
> („ With hounds well trained, lean and eager,)
>
> 32 „ Οἱ Γουαλάνδοι, Σισμόνδαι καὶ Λαμφράγκοι  
> („ The Gualandi, Sismondi, and Lanfranchi)
>
> 33 „ Ἔτρεχον διώκοντες ἐπὶ μετώπου.  
> („ Ran pursuing in the vanguard.)

- *Κυσί* (dat. pl. of *κύων*) renders *Con cagne* by a bare dative of accompaniment or means. The noun can denote either sex; feminine agreement in *ἀχθείσαις, ἰσχναῖς καὶ λάβραις* specifies the females. Musurus redistributes the epithets: *ἰσχναῖς* ‘lean’ gives *magre*, *λάβραις* ‘impetuous’ conveys the eagerness of *studiose*, and *καλῶς ἀχθείσαις* ‘well led, trained’ interprets the proficiency of *conte*. *Ἀχθείσαις* is dat. pl. fem. aor. pass. ptcp. of *ἄγω*.
- *Οἱ Γουαλάνδοι, Σισμόνδαι καὶ Λαμφράγκοι* treats the family names as Greek masculine nominative plurals, with a shared initial article. Italian makes them the object of *s’ avea messi* and links the latter two by repeated *con*; Greek makes all three the subject of *ἔτρεχον* (3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *τρέχω*), ‘were running’.
- *Διώκοντες* (nom. pl. masc. pres. act. ptcp. of *διώκω*) supplies what they were doing as they ran. *Ἐπὶ μετώπου*, *ἐπί* with genitive, gives their position ‘at the front’; the Italian pluperfect placement and its reference back to the person who placed them are both lost.

> 34 In picciol corso mi pareano stanchi  
> 35 lo padre e i figli, e con l' acute scane  
> 36 mi parea lor veder fender li fianchi.  

After short course, the father and his sons seemed to me weary; and methought I saw their flanks torn by the sharp teeth.

> 34 „ Μικρῷ δ’ ὕστερον ἐδόκουν κεκμηκότες  
> („ And a little later they seemed exhausted,)
>
> 35 „ Πατὴρ καὶ τέκνα, καὶ τοὺς ὀξεῖς ὀδόντας  
> („ Father and children, and the sharp fangs)
>
> 36 „ Ἔβλεπον αὐτῶν σχίζοντας τὰς λαγόνας.  
> („ I saw tearing their flanks.)

- *Μικρῷ δ’ ὕστερον* ‘and a little later’ makes *In picciol corso* temporal, dropping the noun for the course. *Μικρῷ* is a neuter dative of degree of difference with comparative adverb *ὕστερον*; *δ’* is elided postpositive *δέ*.
- *Ἐδόκουν κεκμηκότες Πατὴρ καὶ τέκνα* keeps the predicate before the subjects, as in *mi pareano stanchi / lo padre e i figli*. *Ἐδόκουν* is here 3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *δοκέω*; *κεκμηκότες* (nom. pl. masc. perf. act. ptcp. of *κάμνω*) expresses the resulting state, ‘worn out’. The masculine plural takes father and children together despite neuter *τέκνα*. There is no expressed equivalent of *mi*.
- In *τοὺς ὀξεῖς ὀδόντας ἔβλεπον … σχίζοντας τὰς λαγόνας* the teeth are not an instrument as in *con l’ acute scane*, but the perceived agents: ‘I saw the sharp teeth splitting the flanks’. *Ἔβλεπον* is here 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *βλέπω*; it takes an accusative with a supplementary participle, *σχίζοντας* (acc. pl. masc. pres. act. of *σχίζω*), agreeing with *ὀδόντας*. *Αὐτῶν* gives the possessors of *τὰς λαγόνας*. The second qualification *mi parea* disappears.

> 37 Quando fui desto innanzi la dimane,  
> 38 pianger senti' fra il sonno i miei figliuoli,  
> 39 ch' eran con meco, e domandar del pane.  

When I awoke before the dawn, I heard my sons [who were with me, weeping in their sleep, and] asking for bread.

> 37 „ Ὅτε δ’ ἠγέρθην ἐκ κοίτης πρὸ τῆς ἕω,  
> („ And when I awoke from my bed before the dawn,)
>
> 38 „ Κλαίοντ’ ἤκουσα τὰ πεφυλακισμένα  
> („ I heard weeping the imprisoned)
>
> 39 „ Μετ’ ἐμοῦ τέκν’ ἐν ὕπνοις, κἆρτον αἰτοῦντα.  
> („ Children with me in their sleep, and asking for bread.)

- *Ὅτε δ’ ἠγέρθην* ‘and when I arose’ renders *Quando fui desto* with the intransitive use of *ἠγέρθην* (1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ἐγείρω*). *Ἐκ κοίτης* ‘from bed’ is supplied; *πρὸ τῆς ἕω* renders *innanzi la dimane* with *πρό* and the Attic genitive singular *ἕω* of *ἕως* ‘dawn’.
- *Κλαίοντ’ ἤκουσα* preserves the emphatic order of *pianger senti’*, but replaces the infinitive by a participle of perception: *κλαίοντ’* is elided *κλαίοντα*, acc. pl. neut. pres. act. of *κλαίω*, agreeing with the delayed *τέκν’* (*τέκνα*). *Ἤκουσα* is 1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀκούω*. *Τὰ πεφυλακισμένα μετ’ ἐμοῦ τέκνα* ‘the children imprisoned with me’ expands *ch’ eran con meco* into an attributive perfect passive participle of *φυλακίζω*, marking a continuing state. The possessive in *i miei figliuoli* is not separately expressed.
- *Ἐν ὕπνοις* is a prepositional dative plural for singular *fra il sonno*. *Κἆρτον* is crasis of *καὶ ἄρτον*; the conjunction adds a second participial phrase, *ἄρτον αἰτοῦντα* ‘asking for bread’. *Αἰτοῦντα* (acc. pl. neut. contracted pres. act. ptcp. of *αἰτέω*) is parallel to *κλαίοντα*, and takes the thing requested directly in the accusative.

> 40 Ben se' crudel, se tu già non ti duoli,  
> 41 pensando ciò ch' al mio cor s' annunziava ;  
> 42 e se non piangi, di che pianger suoli?  

Thou art right cruel, if thou dost not grieve already at the thought of what my heart foreboded; and if thou weepest not, at what art thou used to weep?

> 40 „ Σκληρὸς ἂν εἴης, εἰ μή μοι δὴ συνάχθῃ,  
> („ Cruel would you be, if indeed you do not grieve with me,)
>
> 41 „ Σκοπῶν, οἷ’ ἐν καρδίᾳ συνῃσθανόμην·  
> („ Considering what I felt within my heart;)
>
> 42 „ Εἰ δὲ μὴ κλαίεις, πότ’ ἆρ’ εἴωθας κλαίειν;  
> („ And if you weep not, when then are you wont to weep?)

- *Σκληρὸς ἂν εἴης* ‘you would be cruel’ softens the assertion *Ben se’ crudel* into a potential optative: *εἴης* is 2nd sg. pres. opt. of *εἰμί*, with modal *ἄν*. *Εἰ μή μοι δὴ συνάχθῃ* presents the condition as a present one rather than repeating the optative. *Συνάχθῃ* is read here as 2nd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *συνάχθομαι*, ‘grieve with’; *μοι* is the companion in grief. The compound and dative make *ti duoli* explicitly sympathetic; emphatic *δή* is not a temporal equivalent of *già*.
- *Σκοπῶν* (nom. sg. masc. contracted pres. act. ptcp. of *σκοπέω*) corresponds closely to *pensando*, with the addressee as subject. In *οἷ’ ἐν καρδίᾳ συνῃσθανόμην*, *οἷ’* is elided *οἷα*, neuter plural object, ‘what things’; *συνῃσθανόμην* is 1st sg. imperf. mid. ind. of *συναισθάνομαι*, with augment on the verbal stem after the prefix. ‘What I was feeling in my heart’ makes the speaker the perceiver, rather than his heart the recipient of what *s’ annunziava*.
- *Εἰ δὲ μὴ κλαίεις* ‘but if you do not weep’ uses *μή* in the condition and postpositive *δέ* for *e*. *Πότ’ ἆρ’* (*πότε ἄρα*) asks ‘when, then?’ rather than *di che* ‘at what?’; this *ἄρα* marks an inference. *Εἴωθας* (2nd sg. perf. act. ind. of *ἔθω*, with present sense ‘you are accustomed’) governs *κλαίειν*, the present active infinitive, like *suoli* with *pianger*.

> 43 Già eran desti, e l'ora s' appressava  
> 44 che il cibo ne soleva essere addotto,  
> 45 e per suo sogno ciascun dubitava ;  

They were now awake, and the hour approaching at which our food used to be brought us, and each was anxious from his dream,

> 43 „ Ἀνηγέρθησαν ἤδη, κἀγγὺς ὑπῆρχεν  
> („ They were awake by now, and near was)
>
> 44 „ Ὁ καιρός, καθ’ ὃν ἔφερον τὰ πρὸς βρῶσιν·  
> („ The hour at which they used to bring our food;)
>
> 45 „ Ἕκαστος δ’ ἡμῶν τοὖναρ εἶχ’ ἐν νῷ τρέμων,  
> („ And each of us had his dream in mind, trembling,)

- *Ἀνηγέρθησαν ἤδη* ‘they had by now arisen’ renders the state *Già eran desti* by the event of waking: *ἀνηγέρθησαν* is 3rd pl. aor. pass. ind. of *ἀνεγείρω*, with internal augment. Conversely, *κἀγγὺς ὑπῆρχεν ὁ καιρός* gives the approach in *l’ora s’ appressava* as a state, ‘and the time was near’. *Κἀγγύς* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγγύς*; *ὑπῆρχεν* is 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ὑπάρχω*, its subject postponed to the next line.
- *Καθ’ ὃν* ‘at which’ is *κατά* with a masculine accusative relative referring to *καιρός*; elision before the rough breathing changes *τ* to *θ*. *Ἔφερον* (here 3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *φέρω*) leaves the bearers indefinite and expresses habitual action by tense alone, replacing *soleva essere addotto*. *Τὰ πρὸς βρῶσιν* ‘the things for eating’ turns singular *il cibo* into a substantivized prepositional phrase; the recipients in *ne* are not expressed.
- *Ἕκαστος δ’ ἡμῶν* adds a partitive genitive to *ciascun*, ‘each of us’. *Τοὖναρ* (crasis of *τὸ ὄναρ*) is the object of *εἶχ’* (elided *εἶχε*, 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἔχω*). ‘Kept the dream in mind, trembling’ expands *per suo sogno … dubitava*: the dream becomes the object of recollection rather than an explicit cause, and *τρέμων* (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. ptcp. of *τρέμω*) expresses the anxiety as an accompanying action. *Ἐν νῷ* has the contracted dative of *νοῦς*.

> 46 ed io sentii chiavar l' uscio di sotto  
> 47 all' orribile torre : ond' io guardai  
> 48 nel viso a' miei figliuoi senza far motto.  

and below I heard the outlet of the horrible tower locked up: whereat I looked into the faces of my sons, without uttering a word.

> 46 „ Ὅτ’ ἤκουσ’ ὑπ’ ἔμ’ ἡλουμένην τὴν θύραν  
> („ When I heard being nailed up below me the door)
>
> 47 „ Τοῦ φρικαλέου πύργου. Σιωπῶν τότε  
> („ Of the horrible tower. Silent then)
>
> 48 „ Εἶδον εἰς τὸ πρόσωπον τῶν ἐμῶν τέκνων·  
> („ I looked into the faces of my children;)

- *Ὅτ’ ἤκουσ’* (elided *ὅτε ἤκουσα*) makes *ed io sentii* a temporal clause, ‘when I heard’, with no emphatic pronoun. *Ὑπ’ ἔμ’* (*ὑπὸ ἐμέ*) gives *di sotto* an explicit reference point, ‘beneath me’.
- *Ἡλουμένην τὴν θύραν* takes *chiavar l’ uscio* in the sense ‘nailing up the door’, rather than simply “locked up”. *Ἡλουμένην* is acc. sg. fem. contracted pres. pass. ptcp. of *ἡλόω*, agreeing with *θύραν* in the perception construction after *ἤκουσα*: what he heard was the door in the process of being nailed. *Τοῦ φρικαλέου πύργου* attaches ‘of the horrible tower’ directly to ‘door’.
- *Σιωπῶν τότε* replaces consequential *ond’* by temporal *τότε* ‘then’ and moves *senza far motto* before the verb as a positive participle, ‘keeping silent’ (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. of *σιωπάω*). *Εἶδον εἰς τὸ πρόσωπον* ‘I looked into the face’ follows the direction of *guardai nel viso* rather than using *εἶδον* with a bare object. The singular *πρόσωπον* follows singular *viso* despite the plural possessors, *τῶν ἐμῶν τέκνων*, a possessive genitive for Italian *a’ miei figliuoi*.

> 49 Io non piangeva, sì dentro impietrai ;  

I did not weep: so stony grew I within;

> 49 „ Οὐκ ἔκλαιον, ἀλλ’ ἔνδον ἀπελιθώθην.  
> („ I wept not, but within I turned to stone.)

- *Οὐκ ἔκλαιον* keeps the imperfect of *Io non piangeva* (1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *κλαίω*), without the separate subject pronoun. *Ἀλλ’* (*ἀλλά*) makes a contrast where *sì* marks degree or consequence. *Ἔνδον ἀπελιθώθην* ‘within I turned to stone’ retains the adverb before the verb and sets a change of state against the imperfect: *ἀπελιθώθην* is 1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ἀπολιθόω*, passive in form for intransitive *impietrai*.

> 50 piangevan elli ; ed Anselmuccio mio  
> 51 disse : 'Tu guardi sì, padre, che hai?'  

they wept; and my little Anselm said: ‘Thou lookest so, father, what ails thee?’

> 50 „ Αὐτὰ δ’ ἔκλαιον· ὁ δ’ Ἀνσελμούκιός μου  
> („ And they wept; and my little Anselm)
>
> 51 „ Εἶπε· ‘“Πῶς βλέπεις οὕτω, πάτερ; τί πάσχεις; ”’  
> („ Said: ‘“Why do you look so, father? what ails you?”’)

- *Αὐτὰ δ’ ἔκλαιον* fronts the contrasting pronoun which Italian postpones in *piangevan elli*. *Αὐτά* is nom. pl. neut., taking up *τέκνα*. The plural verb agrees with the children as persons rather than following the frequent Attic construction of a neuter plural subject with a singular verb. *Ἔκλαιον*, now 3rd pl., is identical in form to the preceding 1st sg.; the pronoun makes the switch explicit.
- *Ὁ δ’ Ἀνσελμούκιός μου* gives *ed Anselmuccio mio* a Greek article, a masculine nominative ending, and a postposed enclitic possessive, which accounts for the additional accent on the name. The Italian diminutive is carried over in the name rather than translated by an adjective. *Εἶπε* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. serving *λέγω*, corresponding to *disse*.
- *Πῶς βλέπεις οὕτω, πάτερ; τί πάσχεις;* divides the speech into two explicit questions. *Πῶς* adds ‘how?’ to *Tu guardi sì*, while *οὕτω* still represents *sì* ‘thus’. *Βλέπεις* is 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *βλέπω*; the ending suffices for *Tu*. *Πάτερ* is the vocative singular of *πατήρ*. *Τί πάσχεις;* ‘what are you suffering?’ substitutes *πάσχω* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind.) with neuter interrogative object *τί* for the idiom of possession in *che hai?*

## Lines 52–84 — Ugolino’s Silence and the Curse on Pisa

> 52 Però non lagrimai, nè rispos’ io  
> 53 tutto quel giorno, nè la notte appresso,  
> 54 infin che l’ altro sol nel mondo uscio.  

But I shed no tear, nor answered all that day, nor the next night, till another sun came forth upon the world.

> 52 „ Οὐ μὴν ἐδάκρυσ᾽, ἀλλ᾽ οὔτ᾽ ἀπεκρινάμην  
> („ Yet I shed no tear, nor did I answer)
>
> 53 „ Ἡμέραν ὅλην, οὔτ᾽ ἐπιοῦσαν νύκτα,  
> („ All that day, nor the following night,)
>
> 54 „ Μέχρις ἥλιος ἐπανέτειλ᾽ ἐν κόσμῳ.  
> („ Until the sun rose again upon the world.)

- *Οὐ μὴν ἐδάκρυσ᾽* ‘yet I did not weep’ gives *Però non lagrimai* an adversative turn. *μήν* is an asseverative particle, not a second negative. *ἐδάκρυσ᾽* is elided *ἐδάκρυσα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *δακρύω*); the augment is retained, but the emphatic subject *io* has no separate equivalent.
- *ἀλλ᾽ οὔτ᾽ ἀπεκρινάμην … οὔτ᾽ ἐπιοῦσαν νύκτα* distributes the negatives much as *nè rispos’ … nè la notte appresso* does. *ἀλλ᾽* and both occurrences of *οὔτ᾽* are elisions, of *ἀλλά* and *οὔτε*. *ἀπεκρινάμην* is 1st sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, with the augment after the prefix; the middle means ‘answered’, not ‘was answered’.
- *ἡμέραν ὅλην* and *ἐπιοῦσαν νύκτα* are accusatives of duration. The Greek has ‘a whole day’, without a demonstrative for *quel*; *ἐπιοῦσαν* (acc. fem. sg. pres. act. participle of *ἔπειμι*, ‘come next’) makes *appresso* an attribute of ‘night’.
- *Μέχρις ἥλιος ἐπανέτειλ᾽ ἐν κόσμῳ* uses *μέχρις* directly as ‘until’, with the indicative of an event that took place. *ἐπανέτειλ᾽* is elided *ἐπανέτειλε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐπανατέλλω*): the sun ‘rose again’, rather than ‘another sun came forth’. *ἐν κόσμῳ* gives a place, not a direction.

> 55 Come un poco di raggio si fu messo  
> 56 nel doloroso carcere, ed io scorsi  
> 57 per quattro visi il mio aspetto stesso,  

When a small ray was sent into the doleful prison, and I discerned in their four faces the aspect of my own,

> 55 „ Μικρᾶς δ᾽ ἀκτῖνος τότ᾽ ἔνδον παρεισδύσης  
> („ And when a faint ray then slipped inside)
>
> 56 „ Τῆς φρικτῆς εἱρκτῆς, εἶδον ἐν τοῖς προσώποις  
> („ The dreadful prison, I saw in the faces)
>
> 57 „ Τῶν τεσσάρων τὴν ἐμὴν ἀθλίαν ὄψιν.  
> („ Of the four my own wretched countenance.)

- *Μικρᾶς δ᾽ ἀκτῖνος … παρεισδύσης* converts the finite temporal clause *Come un poco di raggio si fu messo* into a genitive absolute: *παρεισδύσης* is gen. fem. sg. second aor. act. participle of *παρεισδύω*, ‘slip in’, agreeing with *ἀκτῖνος* (from *ἀκτίς*). The ray itself enters; *un poco di raggio* becomes an adjective and noun, ‘a small ray’. *δ᾽* is connective *δέ*, and *τότ᾽* is elided *τότε*, ‘then’.
- *ἔνδον … τῆς φρικτῆς εἱρκτῆς* ‘inside the dreadful prison’ uses *ἔνδον* with the genitive; the verb, rather than the preposition, conveys entry. *φρικτῆς* ‘causing a shudder’ shifts the adjective away from *doloroso*.
- *εἶδον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. serving *ὁράω*) is now the main verb, with no equivalent of *ed*: ‘when the ray had slipped in, I saw’. *ἐν τοῖς προσώποις τῶν τεσσάρων* is ‘in the faces of the four’, not literally *per quattro visi*: the numeral is substantival and genitive. *τὴν ἐμὴν ἀθλίαν ὄψιν* ‘my own wretched countenance’ adds *ἀθλίαν*; the possessive *ἐμήν* bears the emphasis of *mio … stesso* without a separate intensive pronoun.

> 58 ambo le mani per dolor mi morsi.  

I bit on both my hands for grief.

> 58 „ Ἐκ λύπης ἔδακόν μου τὰς χεῖρας ἄμφω·  
> („ In grief I bit both my hands;)

- *Ἐκ λύπης* fronts the cause that Italian places between object and verb. *ἔδακον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *δάκνω*) takes the hands as direct object. Enclitic *μου*, placed against the verb, supplies possession in place of reflexive *mi*; its presence accounts for the additional accent in *ἔδακόν μου*.
- *τὰς χεῖρας ἄμφω* reverses *ambo le mani*. *ἄμφω* is the nom./acc. dual ‘both’, here attached to a plural accusative, *τὰς χεῖρας* (from *χείρ*), not to a dual noun.

> 59 Ed ei, pensando ch’ io ’l fessi per voglia  
> 60 di manicar, di subito levorsi,  

And they, thinking that I did it from desire of eating, of a sudden rose up,

> 59 „ Οἱ δ᾽ ἐμοὶ παῖδες ὑπολαβόντες τοῦτο  
> („ And my sons, supposing this)
>
> 60 „ Ὡς πείνης ὁρμὴν ἀνέστησαν ἐξαίφνης,  
> („ To be an impulse of hunger, rose up suddenly,)

- *Οἱ δ᾽ ἐμοὶ παῖδες* supplies ‘my children’ for pronominal *Ed ei*. *ὑπολαβόντες* (nom. masc. pl. second aor. act. participle of *ὑπολαμβάνω*) agrees with them: ‘having taken this to be …’. Where *pensando ch’ io ’l fessi* has a gerund and a finite clause with imperfect subjunctive, Greek has an antecedent act of interpretation and a demonstrative object, *τοῦτο*.
- *ὡς πείνης ὁρμήν* ‘as an impulse of hunger’ is predicative to *τοῦτο*; *ὡς* marks the character they attribute to the act. The genitive *πείνης* replaces *di manicar*: hunger, rather than eating, defines the impulse. *ἀνέστησαν* (3rd pl. intransitive second aor. act. ind. of *ἀνίστημι*) needs no reflexive for *levorsi*; *ἐξαίφνης* ‘suddenly’ follows rather than precedes it.

> 61 e disser: ‘Padre, assai ci fia men doglia,  
> 62 se tu mangi di noi: tu ne vestisti  
> 63 queste misere carni, e tu le spoglia.’  

and said: 'Father, it will give us much less pain, if thou wilt eat of us: thou didst put upon us this miserable flesh, and do thou strip it off.'

> 61 „ Λέγοντες· ‘“Ἧττον ἀλγεινὸν ἡμῖν ἔσται,  
> („ Saying: ‘“It will be less painful for us,)
>
> 62 ‚ „ Ἢν φάγῃς ἡμῶν· σὺ γὰρ ὁ δοὺς τὰς σάρκας  
> (‚ „ If you eat of us; for you who gave the flesh,)
>
> 63 ‚ „ Τάσδ᾽ οἰκτρὰς ἡμῶν, σὺ λάβε ταύτας πάλιν.”’  
> (‚ „ This piteous one of ours, you take it back again.”’)

- *Λέγοντες* (nom. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *λέγω*) subordinates *e disser* to ‘rose up’: ‘saying’, not a second finite aorist. Musurus omits the vocative *Padre*. *Ἧττον ἀλγεινὸν ἡμῖν ἔσται* ‘it will be less painful to us’ converts *men doglia* into an impersonal neuter predicate adjective modified by comparative adverb *ἧττον*. *ἡμῖν* is dative of the persons affected; *ἔσται* is 3rd sg. fut. ind. of *εἰμί*. There is no intensifier for *assai*.
- *Ἢν φάγῃς ἡμῶν* ‘if you eat of us’ has *ἤν*, a contracted alternative to conditional *ἐάν*, with *φάγῃς* (2nd sg. second aor. act. subj., from the aorist *ἔφαγον* used for *ἐσθίω*). With the future in the main clause this is an open future condition, not an optative construction. *ἡμῶν* is partitive, closely matching *di noi*.
- *σὺ γὰρ ὁ δοὺς* ‘for you, the one who gave’ supplies explanatory *γάρ* and turns finite *tu ne vestisti* into an articular participle (*δούς*, nom. masc. sg. aor. act. of *δίδωμι*), in apposition to *σύ*. *τὰς σάρκας τάσδ᾽ οἰκτρὰς ἡμῶν* gives the object, ‘these pitiable pieces of flesh of ours’; *τάσδ᾽* is elided *τάσδε*, acc. fem. pl. of *ὅδε*. Possessive *ἡμῶν* attaches the flesh to the speakers instead of making them recipients of clothing.
- *σὺ λάβε ταύτας πάλιν* ‘you take these back’ preserves the repeated *tu* and the imperative (*λάβε*, 2nd sg. second aor. act. imperative of *λαμβάνω*). Feminine plural *ταύτας* resumes *σάρκας*. ‘Give’ and ‘take back’ replace the paired clothing verbs *vestisti … spoglia*.

> 64 Queta’ mi allor per non farli più tristi;  

Then I calmed myself, in order not to make them more unhappy;

> 64 „ Τότ᾽ ἐπραΰνθην, ὡς μὴ πλέον λυπήσω.  
> („ Then I calmed myself, so as not to grieve them more.)

- *Τότ᾽ ἐπραΰνθην* puts ‘then’ first and renders reflexive *Queta’ mi* by *ἐπραΰνθην* (1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *πραΰνω*), ‘I became calm’. The passive form expresses the change of state.
- *ὡς μὴ πλέον λυπήσω* replaces *per non farli più tristi* with a finite negative purpose clause. *λυπήσω* is 1st sg. aor. act. subj. of *λυπέω* here (identical in spelling to the future indicative): ‘that I might not grieve [them] more’. *μή* negates the purpose, *πλέον* is adverbial, and the object *li* is understood. The subjunctive retains the speaker’s purpose directly after a past tense; a shift to the optative is not obligatory.

> 65 lo dì e l’altro stemmo tutti muti.  

that day and the next we all were mute.

> 65 „ Ἦμεν σιγηλοὶ ᾽κείνην ἡμέραν κἄλλην.  
> („ We were silent that day and the next.)

- *Ἦμεν σιγηλοί* ‘we were silent’ brings the predicate forward. *ἦμεν* (1st pl. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*) presents a continuing state for *stemmo*; *σιγηλοί* is a predicate nominative plural. There is no separate *tutti*.
- *᾽κείνην ἡμέραν κἄλλην* gives accusatives of duration. *᾽κείνην* loses the initial vowel of *ἐκείνην* by prodelision after *σιγηλοί*; *κἄλλην* is crasis of *καὶ ἄλλην*, with *ἡμέραν* understood a second time. Literally ‘and another’, it takes its consecutive sense from *lo dì e l’altro*.

> 66 Ahi dura terra, perchè non t’ apristi?  

Ah, hard earth! why didst thou not open?

> 66 „ Αἴ! γῆ σκληρά, πῶς οὐκ ἀνεῴχθης τότε ;  
> („ Ah! hard earth, why did you not open then?)

- *Αἴ! γῆ σκληρά* keeps the interjection and the direct address, with feminine singular vocatives. *πῶς* is literally ‘how’, used for the reproachful *perchè*; *οὐκ* negates an indicative before a smooth-breathed vowel.
- *ἀνεῴχθης* (2nd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ἀνοίγω*) gives ‘were opened’ or ‘opened up’ for reflexive *t’ apristi*. *τότε* ‘then’ is supplied.

> 67 Poscia che fummo al quarto dì venuti,  
> 68 Gaddo mi si gittò disteso a’ piedi,  
> 69 dicendo: ‘Padre mio, chè non m’ aiuti?’  

When we had come to the fourth day, Gaddo threw himself stretched out at my feet, saying: 'My father! why don't you help me?'

> 67 „ Ἀνατειλάσης τῆς τετάρτης ἡμέρας,  
> („ When the fourth day had dawned,)
>
> 68 „ Γάδδος μοι πρὸ τῶν ποδῶν ἔπεσ᾽ ἐκτάδην,  
> („ Gaddo fell outstretched before my feet,)
>
> 69 „ Λέγων πικρῶς· ‘“Ὦ πάτερ, οὐ βοηθεῖς μοι;”’  
> („ Saying bitterly: ‘“O father, do you not help me?”’)

- *Ἀνατειλάσης τῆς τετάρτης ἡμέρας* ‘when the fourth day had dawned’ is a genitive absolute. *ἀνατειλάσης* (gen. fem. sg. aor. act. participle of *ἀνατέλλω*) is unaugmented, as befits a participle. The day becomes the subject; there is no first-person plural corresponding to *fummo … venuti*.
- *Γάδδος … ἔπεσ᾽ ἐκτάδην* replaces *mi si gittò disteso* with ‘fell at full stretch’: *ἔπεσ᾽* is elided *ἔπεσε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *πίπτω*), and poetic *ἐκτάδην* an adverb, not an agreeing participle. *μοι πρὸ τῶν ποδῶν* ‘before my feet’ retains an external possessor in the dative, while *πρό* takes the genitive.
- *Λέγων* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle) matches simultaneous *dicendo*; *πικρῶς* ‘bitterly’ is added. *Ὦ πάτερ* has the vocative of *πατήρ* with the particle of address, but no *mio*. *οὐ βοηθεῖς μοι;* ‘do you not help me?’ omits interrogative *chè*: *βοηθεῖς* is 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of contracted *βοηθέω*, governing a dative rather than a direct object.

> 70 Quivi morì; e come tu mi vedi,  
> 71 vid’ io cascar li tre ad uno ad uno  
> 72 tra il quinto dì e il sesto: ond’ io mi diedi  

There he died; and even as thou seest me, saw I the three fall one by one, between the fifth day and the sixth:

> 70 „ Ἀπέθαν᾽ ἐκεῖ, καί, καθὼς νῦν με βλέπεις,  
> („ He died there; and, even as you now see me,)
>
> 71 „ Εἶδον πεσόντας τοὺς τρεῖς ἄλλους καθ᾽ ἕνα  
> („ I saw the other three fall one by one)
>
> 72 „ Ἐντὸς τῆς πέμπτης καὶ τῆς ἕκτης ἡμέρας.  
> („ Between the fifth and the sixth day.)

- *Ἀπέθαν᾽ ἐκεῖ* reverses *Quivi morì*: *ἀπέθαν᾽* is elided *ἀπέθανε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἀποθνῄσκω*). *καθὼς νῦν με βλέπεις* closely follows *come tu mi vedi*, adding *νῦν* ‘now’ but leaving the subject of *βλέπεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind.) unexpressed.
- *Εἶδον πεσόντας τοὺς τρεῖς ἄλλους* renders *vid’ io cascar li tre* with the Greek supplementary participle after a verb of perception. *πεσόντας* (acc. masc. pl. second aor. act. participle of *πίπτω*) agrees with the persons seen; the aorist presents their falls as events seen entire. *ἄλλους* supplies ‘other’. *καθ᾽ ἕνα* ‘one at a time’ is distributive *κατά* with acc. masc. sg. of *εἷς*; elision before the rough breathing changes *τ* to *θ*.
- *Ἐντὸς τῆς πέμπτης καὶ τῆς ἕκτης ἡμέρας* takes two genitives with a shared noun. *ἐντός* properly gives ‘within’ a limit or period; it is less exact for *tra … e …* than a construction explicitly meaning ‘between’. Musurus ends the sentence here rather than beginning a consequence with *ond’ io mi diedi*.

> 73 già cieco a brancolar sopra ciascuno,  
> 74 e due dì li chiamai poi che fur morti;  

whence I betook me, already blind, to groping over each, and for [two] days called them, after they were dead;

> 73 „ Ἐψηλάφων ἕκαστον τυφλὸς ὢν ἤδη·  
> („ Already blind, I groped over each one;)
>
> 74 „ Ἐφ᾽ ἡμέρας τρεῖς θανόντας ἀνεκάλουν·  
> („ For three days after they were dead I called upon them;)

- *Ἐψηλάφων ἕκαστον* ‘I kept feeling each one’ absorbs *mi diedi … a brancolar* into a single imperfect (*ἐψηλάφων*, 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of contracted *ψηλαφάω*). The direct accusative replaces *sopra ciascuno*; no connective reproduces *onde*. *τυφλὸς ὢν ἤδη* ‘being blind already’ follows the object: *ὤν* is the nom. masc. sg. pres. participle of *εἰμί*, with predicate *τυφλός*.
- *Ἐφ᾽ ἡμέρας τρεῖς* gives duration with *ἐπί* and the accusative; elision before rough-breathed *ἡμέρας* produces *ἐφ᾽*. Musurus explicitly has *τρεῖς* ‘three’, not *due* or Carlyle–Oelsner’s “[two]”.
- *θανόντας ἀνεκάλουν* ‘I kept calling them, dead’ compresses *li chiamai poi che fur morti*. *θανόντας* (acc. masc. pl. second aor. act. participle of *θνῄσκω*) agrees with the understood object; its anterior action does the work of the temporal clause. *ἀνεκάλουν* (1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἀνακαλέω*, augmented after the prefix) makes the repeated calling explicit in aspect.

> 75 poscia, più che il dolor, potè il digiuno.”  

then fasting had more power than grief."

> 75 „ Ἡ πεῖν᾽ ἔπειτα κατίσχυσε τῆς λύπης.” —  
> („ Then hunger proved stronger than grief.” —)

- *Ἡ πεῖν᾽ ἔπειτα* puts the subject before ‘then’; *πεῖν᾽* is elided *πεῖνα*, ‘hunger’, the felt condition rather than *il digiuno*, ‘fasting’. *κατίσχυσε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *κατισχύω*) means ‘prevailed over’ and governs *τῆς λύπης*. Thus *più che il dolor, potè* becomes one verb of overcoming with a genitive complement, not a comparative adverb with a separate standard of comparison.

> 76 Quand’ ebbe detto ciò, con gli occhi torti  
> 77 riprese il teschio misero coi denti,  
> 78 che furo all’ osso, come d’ un can, forti.  

When he had spoken this, with eyes distorted he seized the miserable skull again with his teeth, which as a dog's were strong upon the bone.

> 76 Ταῦτ᾽ εἰπών, λοξοῖς ὄμμασι τὸ παντάλαν  
> (Having spoken thus, with askance eyes the wretched)
>
> 77 Κρανίον πάλιν ἔλαβεν, ἐπιδάκνων  
> (Skull again he seized, biting into)
>
> 78 Τοὐστοῦν ὀδάξ, ὅμοιος κυνὶ λυσσώδει.  
> (The bone with his teeth, like a mad dog.)

- *Ταῦτ᾽ εἰπών* ‘having said these things’ replaces *Quand’ ebbe detto ciò* with a participle sharing the subject of the main verb. *ταῦτ᾽* is elided neut. acc. pl. *ταῦτα*; *εἰπών* is the nom. masc. sg. second aor. act. participle belonging to *εἶπον*, used as aorist of *λέγω*. *λοξοῖς ὄμμασι* ‘with eyes askew’ gives the accompanying circumstance in the dative plural (*ὄμμα*, gen. *ὄμματος*), without a preposition.
- *τὸ παντάλαν κρανίον πάλιν ἔλαβεν* puts the object first, carrying its noun over the line ending. *παντάλαν* is the neut. acc. sg. of *παντάλας*, ‘utterly wretched’, stronger than *misero*. *πάλιν* with *ἔλαβεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λαμβάνω*) separates out the repetitive force of *riprese*.
- *ἐπιδάκνων τοὐστοῦν ὀδάξ* ‘biting into the bone with his teeth’ replaces *coi denti, che furo all’ osso … forti* with a simultaneous pres. act. participle (*ἐπιδάκνων*, nom. masc. sg. of *ἐπιδάκνω*). *τοὐστοῦν* is crasis of *τὸ ὀστοῦν*, the contracted neut. acc. sg. ‘the bone’; poetic *ὀδάξ* is an adverb, ‘with the teeth’.
- *ὅμοιος κυνὶ λυσσώδει* ‘like a rabid dog’ compares the grammatical subject, not his teeth, to the dog. *ὅμοιος* is nominative and takes the dative *κυνί* (from *κύων*); *λυσσώδει* is the agreeing dat. sg. of *λυσσώδης*. The qualification ‘rabid’ is added, and there is no independent equivalent of *forti*.

> 79 Ahi Pisa, vituperio delle genti  
> 80 del bel paese là dove il “sì” suona,  

Ah, Pisa! scandal to the people of the beauteous land where “_sì_” is heard,

> 79 Αἴ ! Πίσα τάλαιν᾽, ὄνειδος τῶν κατοίκων  
> (Ah, wretched Pisa! shame of the inhabitants)
>
> 80 Τῆς ὡραίας ἔνθ᾽ ἠχεῖ τὸ Σὶ δὴ χώρας !  
> (Of the fair land where the Sì indeed sounds!)

- *Αἴ! Πίσα τάλαιν᾽* adds a feminine vocative adjective, ‘wretched’, to *Ahi Pisa*; *τάλαιν᾽* is elided *τάλαινα*. *ὄνειδος* ‘reproach’ stands in apposition to the address, as *vituperio* does. *τῶν κατοίκων* ‘of the inhabitants’ particularizes *delle genti* and in turn takes the dependent genitive ‘of the land’.
- *Τῆς ὡραίας … χώρας* separates article and adjective from their genitive noun by enclosing the relative clause. *ἔνθ᾽* is poetic local relative *ἔνθα*, ‘where’, elided before *ἠχεῖ* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἠχέω*, ‘resounds’). *τὸ Σί* treats the quoted Italian word as a neuter substantive; it is the subject of ‘resounds’, as in *il “sì” suona*, not the object of a verb of hearing. *δή* adds emphasis without an Italian equivalent.

> 81 poi che i vicini a te punir son lenti,  

since thy neighbours are slow to punish thee,

> 81 Ὡς σῶν γειτόνων τιμωρεῖν σ᾽ ἀμελούντων,  
> (Since your neighbours neglect to punish you,)

- *Ὡς σῶν γειτόνων … ἀμελούντων* changes *poi che … son lenti* into a causal genitive absolute. *ὡς* introduces the reason as advanced by the speaker; *ἀμελούντων* (gen. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *ἀμελέω*) agrees with *σῶν γειτόνων*. The possessive adjective ‘your’ makes explicit the relation implicit in *i vicini*.
- *τιμωρεῖν σ᾽* is the complementary pres. act. infinitive of *τιμωρέω* with elided accusative *σε*. ‘Neglect to’ strengthens *son lenti … a*. The active infinitive with the person punished as direct object closely tracks *te punir*; in the usual Attic distinction the active means ‘help, avenge’ a wronged person, while the middle *τιμωρεῖσθαι* with the accusative means ‘take vengeance on’ someone.

> 82 movasi la Caprara e la Gorgona,  
> 83 e faccian siepe ad Arno in su la foce,  
> 84 sì ch’ egli anneghi in te ogni persona.  

let the _Caprara_ and _Gorgona_ move, and hedge up the _Arno_ at its mouth, that it may drown in thee every living soul.

> 82 Κινηθήτω Γοργόνη καὶ Καπραρία,  
> (Let Gorgona and Caprara move,)
>
> 83 Καὶ τὸ στόμιον ἐμφραξάτωσαν Ἄρνου,  
> (And let them dam up the mouth of the Arno,)
>
> 84 Ἵνα καταπνίξῃ σου κατοίκους πάντας.  
> (So that it may drown all your inhabitants.)

- *Κινηθήτω Γοργόνη καὶ Καπραρία* reverses the names but retains the preceding singular verb of *movasi la Caprara e la Gorgona*. *κινηθήτω* is 3rd sg. aor. pass. imperative of *κινέω*, ‘let [it] be moved’, in place of the Italian pronominal subjunctive of command. The singular agrees with the nearer of the coordinated subjects.
- *καὶ τὸ στόμιον ἐμφραξάτωσαν Ἄρνου* switches to the plural, as does *e faccian*. *ἐμφραξάτωσαν* is 3rd pl. aor. act. imperative of *ἐμφράσσω*, with the expanded imperative ending *-τωσαν*: ‘let them block’. The image expressed by the noun *siepe* becomes a verb; the location *in su la foce* becomes the direct object *τὸ στόμιον*, and *ad Arno* becomes the dependent genitive *Ἄρνου*, ‘of the Arno’.
- *Ἵνα καταπνίξῃ* expresses the intended result of *sì ch’ egli anneghi* as purpose, with *ἵνα* and the 3rd sg. aor. act. subj. of *καταπνίγω*. The change back to singular identifies the river as subject without an explicit *egli*. *σου κατοίκους πάντας* ‘all your inhabitants’ changes locative *in te* to a possessive genitive and distributive singular *ogni persona* to a plural object with *πάντας* in final position.

## Lines 85–117 — The Frost That Seals the Eyes

> 85 Chè se il Conte Ugolino aveva voce  
> 86 d’ aver tradita te delle castella,  
> 87 non dovei tu i figliuoi porre a tal croce:  

For if Count Ugolino had the fame of having betrayed thee [of] thy castles, thou oughtest not to have put his sons into such torture:

> 85 Εἰ γὰρ ἔνοχος ὁ Κόμης Οὐγολῖνος  
> (For if liable Count Ugolino)
>
> 86 Ἦν, ὡς ἀπίστως προδοὺς τὰ φρούριά σου,  
> (Was for having faithlessly betrayed your strongholds,)
>
> 87 Οὐκ ἔδει σ᾽ οὕτω κολάζειν αὐτοῦ παῖδας·  
> (You ought not so to have punished his children;)

- *Εἰ γὰρ ἔνοχος … ἦν* ‘for if he was guilty’ retains the condition and the explanatory *Chè* (*γάρ* is postpositive), but changes what is conditional: *aveva voce* speaks of a reputation, whereas *ἔνοχος*, a predicate adjective placed before the subject *ὁ Κόμης Οὐγολῖνος*, speaks of liability or guilt. The copula *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*) is held over to the next line.
- *ὡς ἀπίστως προδούς* ‘on the ground that he had faithlessly betrayed’ replaces *d’ aver tradita* with a circumstantial participle. *προδούς* (nom. sg. masc., second aorist active participle of *προδίδωμι*) agrees with the count; *ὡς* presents the betrayal as the ground alleged against him, rather than independently affirming it. *ἀπίστως* makes the breach of faith explicit. The Italian addresses the injured party in *te*; Greek makes the strongholds the direct object, *τὰ φρούριά σου* ‘your strongholds’, with an enclitic possessive genitive.
- *οὐκ ἔδει σ᾽ οὕτω κολάζειν* ‘you ought not to have punished them thus’ uses impersonal *ἔδει* (imperfect indicative of *δεῖ*) without *ἄν*, an ordinary construction for a past obligation that went unfulfilled. *σ᾽* is elided *σε*, accusative subject of the present active infinitive *κολάζειν*. *οὕτω* conveys *a tal*, but the image in *porre a tal croce* gives way to the simple verb ‘punish’; *αὐτοῦ παῖδας* ‘his children’ supplies the possessor of *i figliuoi*.

> 88 innocenti facea l’ età novella,  
> 89 novella Tebe, Uguccione e il Brigata,  
> 90 e gli altri due che il canto suso appella.  

their youthful age, thou modern Thebes! made innocent Uguccione and Brigata, and the other two whom my song above has named.

> 88 Ἐποίει γὰρ ἀθώους, ὦ νέαι Θῆβαι,  
> (For innocent made, O new Thebes,)
>
> 89 Ἡ νεότης Βριγάναν κ᾽ Οὐγουκιῶνα,  
> (Their youth Brigata and Uguccione,)
>
> 90 Τοὺς δύο τ᾽ ἄλλους, ὧν πρότερον ἐμνήσθην.  
> (And the other two, whom I mentioned before.)

- *ἐποίει γὰρ ἀθώους … ἡ νεότης* ‘for their youth made [them] innocent’: the delayed subject *ἡ νεότης* condenses *l’ età novella* into one noun. *ἐποίει* is the contracted 3rd sg. imperfect active indicative of *ποιέω*, matching *facea*; it takes the names as objects and *ἀθώους* as their predicate accusative. *γάρ* explicitly marks the explanation.
- *ὦ νέαι Θῆβαι* reproduces the apostrophe *novella Tebe* with the Greek plural place-name and a correspondingly plural vocative adjective. It interrupts the clause before the subject rather than after it. *Βριγάναν κ᾽ Οὐγουκιῶνα* reverses the Italian order of the names; both are accusative singular, and *κ᾽* is a shortened *καί* before a vowel.
- *τοὺς δύο τ᾽ ἄλλους* ‘and the other two’ uses the plural article and adjective, not the dual; postpositive *τε* is elided before *ἄλλους*. *ὧν πρότερον ἐμνήσθην* ‘whom I mentioned earlier’ substitutes a first-person verb for *il canto … appella*. *ἐμνήσθην* (1st sg. aorist passive in form, from *μιμνῄσκω*) has the middle sense ‘I called to mind, made mention of’ and governs genitive *ὧν*; *πρότερον* makes the spatial *suso* temporal.

> 91 Noi passamm’ oltre là ’ve la gelata  
> 92 ruvidamente un’ altra gente fascia,  
> 93 non volta in giù, ma tutta riversata.  

We went farther on, where the frost ruggedly inwraps another people, not bent [down]wards, but all reversed.

> 91 Πρόσω χωροῦντες ἤλθομεν ἔνθ᾽ ὁ πάγος  
> (Advancing onward we came where the ice)
>
> 92 Τραχέως ψυχὰς περιζώννυσιν ἄλλας,  
> (Roughly swathes other souls,)
>
> 93 Μὴ κυπτούσας κάτω, πλὴν οὔσας ὑπτίας.  
> (Not bent downward, but lying face upward.)

- *πρόσω χωροῦντες ἤλθομεν* ‘advancing onward, we came’ distributes *passamm’ oltre* between *πρόσω*, a present active participle (*χωροῦντες*, nom. pl. masc. of *χωρέω*) expressing movement in progress, and *ἤλθομεν* (1st pl. second aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι*) marking arrival. *ἔνθ᾽* is locative *ἔνθα* ‘where’, elided before *ὁ πάγος* ‘the ice’.
- *τραχέως … περιζώννυσιν* closely answers to *ruvidamente … fascia*: the verb (3rd sg. present active indicative of *περιζώννυμι*, with movable *ν*) means ‘girds about’. *ψυχὰς … ἄλλας* ‘other souls’ replaces collective singular *un’ altra gente*; noun and adjective enclose the verb.
- *μὴ κυπτούσας κάτω, πλὴν οὔσας ὑπτίας* ‘not stooping down, but lying face upward’ describes the accusative *ψυχάς* with two agreeing present active participles, of *κύπτω* and *εἰμί*. *μή* negates the first description; adversative *πλήν* introduces the second. Predicate *ὑπτίας* specifies the posture meant by *riversata*; there is no separate equivalent of *tutta*.

> 94 Lo pianto stesso lì pianger non lascia,  

The very weeping there allows *them* not to weep;

> 94 Ἐκεῖ τὰ δάκρυ᾽ οὐκ ἐπιτρέπει κλαίειν·  
> (There the tears do not allow them to weep;)

- *ἐκεῖ τὰ δάκρυ᾽ οὐκ ἐπιτρέπει κλαίειν* ‘there the tears do not permit weeping’ puts *lì* first and replaces *lo pianto stesso* with the physical tears, without a word for *stesso*. *δάκρυ᾽* is elided *δάκρυα*, neuter nominative plural, taking the singular verb *ἐπιτρέπει* in regular Attic fashion. The present active infinitive *κλαίειν* answers to *pianger*; the person denied permission is left unexpressed.

> 95 e il duol, che trova in su gli occhi rintoppo,  
> 96 si volve in entro a far crescer l’ ambascia:  

and the grief, which finds impediment upon their eyes, turns inward to increase the agony:

> 95 Ἡ λύπη δ᾽ ἐξ ὀμμάτων οὐ διιοῦσα,  
> (And the grief, not passing through from the eyes,)
>
> 96 Στρέφει πρὸς τἄνδον, καὔξει τὴν ἀγωνίαν.  
> (Turns inward, and increases the agony.)

- *ἡ λύπη δ᾽ ἐξ ὀμμάτων οὐ διιοῦσα* ‘and the grief, not passing out through the eyes’: *δ᾽* is postpositive *δέ* elided. The relative clause *che trova … rintoppo* becomes a negative participial description, *διιοῦσα* (nom. sg. fem., present participle of *δίειμι* ‘pass through’), agreeing with *λύπη*. Instead of naming an obstruction *in su gli occhi*, *ἐξ* with the genitive marks the outlet.
- *στρέφει πρὸς τἄνδον* ‘turns toward the inside’ uses active *στρέφει* intransitively for reflexive *si volve*. *τἄνδον* is crasis of *τὰ ἔνδον*, the article making the adverb ‘within’ a substantive. *καὔξει* is crasis of *καὶ αὔξει* ‘and increases’: a coordinated present indicative with direct object *τὴν ἀγωνίαν* replaces the dependent causative *a far crescer l’ ambascia*.

> 97 chè le lacrime prime fanno groppo,  
> 98 e sì come visiere di cristallo  
> 99 riempion sotto il ciglio tutto il coppo.  

for their first tears form a knot, and, like crystal vizors, fill up all the cavity beneath their eyebrows.

> 97 Συμπυκνοῦται γὰρ τὰ δάκρυα τὰ πρῶτα,  
> (For the first tears freeze solid,)
>
> 98 Καὶ δίκην κρυσταλλίνων διόπτρων ἅπαν  
> (And like crystal visors all)
>
> 99 Τὸ κάτω κενὸν ἐκπληροῖ τῶν ὀφρύων.  
> (The hollow beneath the eyebrows fill.)

- *συμπυκνοῦται γὰρ τὰ δάκρυα τὰ πρῶτα* ‘for the tears, the first ones, congeal’: *γάρ* gives the force of *chè*. *συμπυκνοῦται* (3rd sg. present middle/passive indicative of *συμπυκνόω*) expresses the tears becoming compact rather than making a separate *groppo*. The neuter plural subject again takes a singular verb; the repeated article places *τὰ πρῶτα* in attributive position.
- *δίκην κρυσταλλίνων διόπτρων* ‘after the manner of crystalline eye-pieces’ replaces comparative *sì come* with adverbial accusative *δίκην* governing a genitive. The material phrase *di cristallo* becomes an agreeing adjective; *διόπτρων* emphasizes an apparatus for looking through rather than the whole *visiere*.
- *ἅπαν τὸ κάτω κενὸν … τῶν ὀφρύων* ‘the whole hollow below the brows’ is the object of singular *ἐκπληροῖ* (present active indicative of *ἐκπληρόω*), still with the tears as subject. *ἅπαν* agrees with substantivized neuter *τὸ … κενόν*; the positional *κάτω* stands inside the article phrase, while its reference point, genitive plural *τῶν ὀφρύων*, is delayed until after the verb.

> 100 Ed avvegna che, sì come d’ un callo,  
> 101 per la freddura ciascun sentimento  
> 102 cessato avesse del mio viso stallo,  
> 103 già mi parea sentire alquanto vento;  

And although, as from a callus, through the cold all feeling had departed from my face, it now seemed to me as if I felt some wind;

> 100 Καί τοι δέ μου τὸ ψῦχος ἀποναρκῶσαν,  
> (And although the cold, having numbed me,)
>
> 101 Ὥσπερ ἐν τύλῳ νενεκρωμένῳ, πᾶσαν  
> (As in a deadened callus, all)
>
> 102 Αἴσθησιν ἀφῄρεσεν ἐκ τοῦ προσώπου,  
> (Feeling it took away from my face,)
>
> 103 Ὅμως αἰσθάνεσθ᾽ ἄνεμόν τιν᾽ ἐδόκουν.  
> (Yet I seemed to feel some wind.)

- *καί τοι … ὅμως* frames the concession ‘and yet … nevertheless’. Where *avvegna che* introduces a pluperfect subjunctive, Musurus has a finite indicative *ἀφῄρεσεν* followed by the resumptive *ὅμως*. The additional postpositive *δέ* makes the opening more heavily connective than a simple concessive clause.
- *τὸ ψῦχος ἀποναρκῶσαν* ‘the cold, having numbed [me]’: *per la freddura*, a cause in Italian, becomes the neuter singular subject, with an agreeing aorist active participle of *ἀποναρκόω*. Its neuter nominative ending *-σαν* is participial, not a plural personal ending. *ὥσπερ ἐν τύλῳ νενεκρωμένῳ* ‘as in a deadened callus’ makes *sì come d’ un callo* locative; the added perfect middle/passive participle of *νεκρόω* agrees with dative *τύλῳ* and marks a resulting state.
- *πᾶσαν αἴσθησιν ἀφῄρεσεν ἐκ τοῦ προσώπου* ‘took all sensation away from the face’ makes *ciascun sentimento* the object rather than the subject. *ἀφῄρεσεν* is 3rd sg. aorist active indicative of *ἀφαιρέω*, with internal augment; *ἐκ* marks removal. The anticipatory genitive *μου* identifies the person affected, separated by most of the clause from *τοῦ προσώπου*.
- *αἰσθάνεσθ᾽ ἄνεμόν τιν᾽ ἐδόκουν* ‘I seemed to feel a certain wind’ makes impersonal *mi parea* personal: *ἐδόκουν* (here 1st sg. imperfect active indicative of *δοκέω*) shares its subject with the present middle infinitive *αἰσθάνεσθαι*, shortened before the vowel. *τιν᾽* is elided accusative *τινα*, qualifying *ἄνεμον* rather than measuring the wind's strength as *alquanto* does. *già* has no distinct temporal equivalent.

> 104 per ch’ io: “ Maestro mio, questo chi move?  
> 105 Non è quaggiù ogni vapore spento? ”  

whereat I: "Master, who moves this? Is not all heat extinguished here below?"

> 104 Διό, “Προστάτα, τί τὸ κινοῦν τοῦτ᾽;” εἶπον.  
> (Wherefore, “Protector, what moves this?” I said.)
>
> 105 “Οὐκ ἐξέλιπε πᾶς ἀτμὸς ὧδε κάτω;”  
> (“Has not all vapour ceased here below?”)

- *διό … εἶπον* ‘wherefore … I said’ supplies the speech verb omitted in *per ch’ io*; *εἶπον* is 1st sg. second aorist active indicative. Vocative *προστάτα* (from *προστάτης*) means ‘protector’ or ‘leader’, not precisely *Maestro*, and drops *mio*.
- *τί τὸ κινοῦν τοῦτ᾽;* ‘what is it that moves this?’ replaces personal *chi* with neuter interrogative *τί*. *τὸ κινοῦν* is an articular present active participle of *κινέω*, ‘the thing moving’, in an understood copular question; *τοῦτ᾽* is shortened *τοῦτο*, its accusative object.
- *οὐκ ἐξέλιπε πᾶς ἀτμὸς ὧδε κάτω;* expects assent, like *Non è …?* *ἐξέλιπε* (3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *ἐκλείπω*) means ‘gave out, ceased’ and replaces the resultant state *è … spento*. *ἀτμός* follows *vapore* literally as ‘vapour’, not the prose rendering “heat”; *ὧδε κάτω* expands *quaggiù* into two adverbs.

> 106 Ond’ egli a me: “ Avaccio sarai dove  
> 107 di ciò ti farà l’ occhio la risposta,  
> 108 veggendo la cagion che il fiato piove.”  

Whence he to me: "Soon shalt thou be where thine eye *itself*, seeing the cause which rains the blast, shall answer thee in this."

> 106 Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ· “Ταχέως νῦν ἀφίξῃ  
> (And he to me: “Soon now you shall arrive)
>
> 107 „ Ἔνθ᾽ ἀπόκρισιν τὸ σὸν ὄμμα σοι δώσει,  
> („ Where your own eye shall give you the answer,)
>
> 108 „ Βλέποντι τὸ προξενοῦν ἄνεμον τόνδε.”  
> („ Seeing that which causes this wind.”)

- *ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ* ‘and he to me’ reproduces the omission of a speech verb; *ὁ* has its pronominal use with *δέ*, which merely continues the exchange rather than translating *Onde* as ‘whence’. *ταχέως νῦν ἀφίξῃ* ‘you will soon now arrive’ adds *νῦν* and makes *sarai* a verb of arrival: *ἀφίξῃ* is 2nd sg. future middle indicative of *ἀφικνέομαι*.
- *ἔνθ᾽ ἀπόκρισιν τὸ σὸν ὄμμα σοι δώσει* ‘where your eye will give you an answer’ closely follows *dove … ti farà l’ occhio la risposta*. *ἔνθ᾽* is elided *ἔνθα*; *δώσει* (3rd sg. future active indicative of *δίδωμι*) takes the fronted accusative *ἀπόκρισιν* and recipient dative *σοι*. The possessive *σόν* makes the relation of the eye to the addressee explicit; *di ciò* is not separately rendered.
- *βλέποντι* ‘as you see’ (dat. sg. masc., present active participle of *βλέπω*) attaches *veggendo* to *σοι*, not grammatically to the nominative eye. Its object *τὸ προξενοῦν ἄνεμον τόνδε* ‘that which causes this wind’ is an articular neuter participle of *προξενέω* with its own accusative object. It compresses *la cagion che il fiato piove*, dropping the metaphor in *piove*.

> 109 Ed un de’ tristi della fredda crosta  
> 110 gridò a noi: “ O anime crudeli  
> 111 tanto che data v’ è l’ ultima posta,  

And one of the wretched *shadows* of the icy crust cried out to us: "O souls, so cruel that the last post of *all* is given to you!

> 109 Εἷς δὲ τῶν ἐν τῷ πάγῳ βεβυθισμένων  
> (And one of those sunken in the ice)
>
> 110 Ἔκραξ᾽· “Ὦ ψυχαὶ παντὸς ἐνδεεῖς οἴκτου,  
> (Cried out: “O souls devoid of all pity,)
>
> 111 „ Ὥστ᾽ ἐν ἐσχάτῳ παρεπέμφθητε βόθρῳ,  
> („ So that you have been consigned to the lowest pit,)

- *εἷς δὲ τῶν ἐν τῷ πάγῳ βεβυθισμένων* ‘and one of those submerged in the ice’ retains the partitive construction of *un de’ tristi*, but defines the group by position, not wretchedness. *βεβυθισμένων* (gen. pl. masc., perfect middle/passive participle of *βυθίζω*) is substantivized by *τῶν*; the perfect describes the continuing state. *ἐν τῷ πάγῳ* replaces *della fredda crosta* without a separate adjective or word for ‘crust’.
- *ἔκραξ᾽* is elided *ἔκραξε* (3rd sg. aorist active indicative of *κράζω*); *a noi* is not expressed. *ὦ ψυχαὶ παντὸς ἐνδεεῖς οἴκτου* ‘O souls lacking all pity’ replaces *crudeli* with vocative plural *ἐνδεεῖς* (from *ἐνδεής*), governing genitive *οἴκτου*, with *παντός* placed before the adjective.
- *ὥστ᾽ … παρεπέμφθητε* ‘so that you were consigned’ renders *tanto che* by elided *ὥστε* with the indicative of an actual result. *παρεπέμφθητε* (2nd pl. aorist passive indicative of *παραπέμπω*, internally augmented) makes the recipients of *data v’ è* into the grammatical subject. *ἐν ἐσχάτῳ … βόθρῳ* ‘in the lowest pit’ interprets *l’ ultima posta* as a location: *ἐν* with the dative stresses the assigned position rather than motion into it.

> 112 levatemi dal viso i duri veli,  
> 113 sì ch’ io sfoghi il dolor che il cor m’ impregna,  
> 114 un poco, pria che il pianto si raggeli.”  

remove the hard veils from my face, that I may vent the grief, which stuffs my heart, a little, ere the weeping freeze again."

> 112 „ Τὰς καλύπτρας ἐξ ἐμῶν ἄρατ᾽ ὀμμάτων,  
> („ Lift the coverings from my eyes,)
>
> 113 „ Ἵνα κουφισθῶ τῆς ἐν καρδίᾳ λύπης  
> („ That I may be relieved of the grief in my heart)
>
> 114 „ Μικρόν, πρὶν πάλιν καταπαγῇ τὸ δάκρυ.”—  
> („ A little, before the tear freezes again.”—)

- *τὰς καλύπτρας ἐξ ἐμῶν ἄρατ᾽ ὀμμάτων* ‘lift the coverings from my eyes’ fronts the object of *ἄρατ᾽*, elided *ἄρατε* (2nd pl. aorist active imperative of *αἴρω*). *ἐξ* governs *ἐμῶν … ὀμμάτων*, with the imperative interposed between possessive and noun. The eyes specify what *viso* leaves general; *duri* is omitted.
- *ἵνα κουφισθῶ τῆς ἐν καρδίᾳ λύπης* ‘in order that I may be relieved of the grief in my heart’ preserves the purpose of *sì ch’ io sfoghi* but changes active venting to passive relief. *κουφισθῶ* is 1st sg. aorist passive subjunctive of *κουφίζω*, with a genitive of that from which one is relieved. The attributive phrase *ἐν καρδίᾳ* between article and noun replaces the active relative clause *che il cor m’ impregna*.
- *μικρόν* is an adverbial neuter accusative, ‘a little’, delayed as is *un poco*. *πρὶν πάλιν καταπαγῇ τὸ δάκρυ* ‘before the tear freezes solid again’ uses the 3rd sg. second aorist passive subjunctive of *καταπήγνυμι* with intransitive sense. The subjunctive without *ἄν* after *πρίν* follows the prospective *pria che … si raggeli* more closely than the usual Attic *πρίν* with infinitive after an affirmative clause. Singular *τὸ δάκρυ* makes *il pianto* a physical tear.

> 115 Per ch’ io a lui: “ Se vuoi ch’ io ti sovvegna,  
> 116 dimmi chi sei; e, s’ io non ti disbrigo,  
> 117 al fondo della ghiaccia ir mi convegna.”  

Wherefore I to him: "If thou wouldst have me aid thee, tell me who thou art; and if I do not extricate thee, may I have to go to the bottom of the ice."

> 115 Κἀγώ· “Παρ᾽ ἐμοῦ βοήθειαν εἰ θέλεις,  
> (And I: “If you wish aid from me,)
>
> 116 „ Φράσον μοι, τίς εἶς· ἢν δὲ μή σ᾽ ἀπαλλάξω,  
> („ Tell me who you are; and if I do not free you,)
>
> 117 „ Ῥιφθείην ἐν τῷ βυθῷ τοῦ πάγου τοῦδε.”  
> („ May I be cast into the depth of this ice.”)

- *κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*, ‘and I’: the verbless reply omits both the causal force of *Per ch’* and *a lui*. *παρ᾽ ἐμοῦ βοήθειαν εἰ θέλεις* ‘if you want aid from me’ places the source (*παρά* with genitive) and the object *βοήθειαν* before *εἰ θέλεις* (2nd sg. present active indicative of *θέλω*). The noun takes the place of the whole subordinate clause *ch’ io ti sovvegna*.
- *φράσον μοι, τίς εἶς* ‘tell me who you are’ closely matches *dimmi chi sei*: *φράσον* is 2nd sg. aorist active imperative of *φράζω*, with recipient dative *μοι* and an interrogative clause as object. *τίς* is interrogative, not enclitic indefinite *τις*; *εἶς* is the poetic, epic-attested second-person singular present of *εἰμί*, rather than ordinary Attic *εἶ*.
- *ἢν δὲ μή σ᾽ ἀπαλλάξω* ‘but if I do not release you’ uses *ἤν*, a contracted equivalent of *ἐάν*, with the subjunctive in a prospective condition. *μή* negates *ἀπαλλάξω* (1st sg. aorist active subjunctive of *ἀπαλλάσσω*, identical in spelling to the future indicative); *σ᾽* is its elided accusative object.
- *ῥιφθείην* ‘may I be hurled’ is an independent wish, 1st sg. aorist passive optative of *ῥίπτω*, without *ἄν*. It replaces the impersonal obligation and infinitive of *ir mi convegna* with a personal passive. *ἐν τῷ βυθῷ τοῦ πάγου τοῦδε* uses a locative dative for the position reached, against directional *al fondo*; the genitive *τοῦ πάγου* is further specified by agreeing demonstrative *τοῦδε* ‘of this ice’.

## Lines 118–150 — Friar Alberigo on Souls That Fall Before Death

> 118 Rispose adunque: “Io son frate Alberigo,  
> 119 io son quel delle frutte del mal orto,  
> 120 che qui riprendo dattero per figo.”  

He answered [therefore]: “I am Friar Alberigo, I am he of the fruits from the ill garden, who here receive dates for my figs.”

> 118 Ὁ δ᾽ ὑπέλαβε· “Φράτηρ εἴμ᾽ Ἀλβερίγος,  
> (And he answered: “I am Friar Alberigo,)
>
> 119 „ Ὁ τῶν ὀπωρῶν τοῦ θανασίμου κήπου·  
> („ He of the fruits from the deadly garden;)
>
> 120 „ Λαμβάνω δ᾽ ὧδε φοίνικας ἀντὶ σύκων.”  
> („ And here I receive dates in return for figs.”)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ ὑπέλαβε* ‘and he replied’: the article used pronominally with connective *δέ* supplies the subject of *rispose*. *Ὑπέλαβε* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὑπολαμβάνω*, ‘take up’ another’s words, with the augment inside the compound. There is no distinct inferential particle for *adunque*.
- *Φράτηρ εἴμ᾽ Ἀλβερίγος* places the title before the verb and the name after it; both are nominative. *Φράτηρ* is used for the Italian title *frate*, not in its classical sense ‘member of a phratry’. *Εἴμ᾽* represents *εἰμί*, 1st sg. pres. ind., with final *-ι* elided; no separate *ἐγώ* renders *io*.
- *Ὁ τῶν ὀπωρῶν τοῦ θανασίμου κήπου* ‘the one of the fruits of the deadly garden’ stands in apposition to the name. The article with a dependent genitive suffices for *io son quel delle frutte*, without repeating the copula. *Τοῦ θανασίμου κήπου* in turn qualifies *ὀπωρῶν*; *θανάσιμος* makes *mal* specifically ‘death-dealing’.
- *Λαμβάνω δ᾽ ὧδε* ‘and I receive here’ replaces the relative *che … riprendo* with an independent present and postpositive *δέ*. *Φοίνικας* (acc. pl. of *φοῖνιξ*) is its object; *ἀντὶ σύκων* has the genitive required by *ἀντί* for an exchange. Both fruits are plural against the Italian singulars *dattero* and *figo*.

> 121 “O,” diss’io lui, “or sei tu ancor morto? ”  

“Hah!” said I to him, “then art thou dead [already]?”

> 121 Εἶπον αὐτῷ· “Πῶς; ἀπέθανες οὖν ἤδη;”  
> (I said to him: “What? are you then already dead?”)

- *Εἶπον αὐτῷ* precedes the whole speech, whereas *diss’io lui* interrupts it. *Εἶπον* is the suppletive second aor. act. ind., here 1st sg., of *λέγω*; *αὐτῷ* is the dative of the addressee.
- *Πῶς;* ‘how?’ makes *O* a separate incredulous question. *Ἀπέθανες* (2nd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἀποθνῄσκω*, with internal augment) asks about the event of dying rather than using a copula with *morto*. Postpositive *οὖν* marks the inference and *ἤδη* gives *ancor* the sense ‘already’.

> 122 Ed egli a me: “Come il mio corpo stea  
> 123 nel mondo su nulla scienza porto.  

And he to me: “How my body stands in the world above, I have no knowledge.

> 122 Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ· “Τί μου τὸ σῶμ᾽ ἀπέβη  
> (And he to me: “How my body has fared)
>
> 123 „ Ἐπὶ γῆς οὐδεμίαν αἴσθησιν ἔχω.  
> („ Upon earth I have no knowledge.)

- *Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ* ‘and he to me’ keeps *Ed egli a me* verbless, using *πρός* with the accusative. The indirect question *τί μου τὸ σῶμ᾽ ἀπέβη* likewise precedes the expression of ignorance: literally ‘what did my body turn out [to be]?’ *Μου*, the possessive genitive, attaches as an enclitic after the interrogative; *σῶμ᾽* is elided *σῶμα*, nom. sg.
- *Ἀπέβη* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἀποβαίνω*) asks about the outcome, against the present subjunctive *stea* in *come … stea*. *Ἐπὶ γῆς*, with the genitive of place, substitutes ‘on earth’ for *nel mondo su*. *Οὐδεμίαν αἴσθησιν ἔχω* ‘I have no perception’ retains the noun-and-verb construction of *nulla scienza porto*, but uses the ordinary verb of possession.

> 124 Cotal vantaggio ha questa Tolomea,  
> 125 che spesse volte l’anima ci cade  
> 126 innanzi ch’ Atropòs mossa le dea.  

Such privilege has this Ptolomæa, that oftentimes the soul falls down hither, ere Atropos impels it.

> 124 „ Προτέρημα τοῦτ᾽ ἔστι τῆς Πτολεμαίας,  
> („ Such privilege belongs to Ptolomæa,)
>
> 125 „ Ὅτι συχνάκις ἐνθάδ᾽ ἡ ψυχὴ πίπτει,  
> („ That often hither the soul falls,)
>
> 126 „ Πρὶν Ἄτροπος τὸ νῆμα τῆς ζωῆς τάμῃ.  
> („ Before Atropos cuts the thread of life.)

- *Προτέρημα τοῦτ᾽ ἔστι τῆς Πτολεμαίας* ‘this is a prerogative of Ptolomæa’ reverses the construction of *Cotal vantaggio ha questa Tolomea*: the possessor becomes a genitive, and elided *τοῦτο* points forward to the clause defining the prerogative instead of qualifying the place-name.
- *Ὅτι* introduces that explanatory clause. *Συχνάκις* gives a single frequency adverb for *spesse volte*; *ἐνθάδ᾽* is elided *ἐνθάδε*, ‘here, hither’. The order adverb–subject–verb in *ἐνθάδ᾽ ἡ ψυχὴ πίπτει* places *ci*’s equivalent before the noun.
- *Πρὶν Ἄτροπος τὸ νῆμα τῆς ζωῆς τάμῃ* makes explicit an action different from the literal *mossa le dea*: ‘before Atropos cuts the thread of life’. *Τάμῃ* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. subj. of *τέμνω*, with the poetic aorist stem *ταμ-*. The subjunctive presents the cut as still prospective; the bare *πρίν* with a finite subjunctive, without *ἄν*, is not the usual Attic prose construction after an affirmative main clause.

> 127 E perchè tu più volentier mi rade  
> 128 le invetriate lagrime dal volto,  
> 129 sappi che tosto che l’ anima trade,  

And that thou more willingly mayest rid the glazen tears from off my face, know that forthwith, when the soul betrays,

> 127 „ Ἀλλ᾽ ἵνα μ᾽ ἀπαλλάξῃς προθυμοτέρως  
> („ And that you may more willingly relieve me)
>
> 128 „ Τῶν πεπηγότων δακρύων τοῦ προσώπου,  
> („ Of the frozen tears upon my face,)
>
> 129 „ Ἴσθ᾽ ὅτι καθ᾽ ἣν στιγμὴν προδῷ ψυχή τις,  
> („ Know that at the moment any soul betrays,)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ ἵνα* ‘but in order that’ changes connective *E* to adversative *ἀλλά* and makes *perchè* unequivocally final. *Ἀπαλλάξῃς* (2nd sg. aor. act. subj. of *ἀπαλλάσσω*) takes the person relieved in the accusative, elided *μ᾽* for *με*, and the thing removed in the genitive. Thus *mi rade … dal volto* is recast as ‘rid me of’; *προθυμοτέρως* is the comparative adverb ‘more willingly’.
- *Τῶν πεπηγότων δακρύων τοῦ προσώπου* ‘of the congealed tears of the face’: *πεπηγότων* is the gen. pl. neut. perfect active participle of *πήγνυμι*, from the intransitive perfect *πέπηγα*. It expresses the resulting solidity rather than the glass image of *invetriate*. *Τοῦ προσώπου* depends on *δακρύων* rather than marking motion from the face.
- *Ἴσθ᾽ ὅτι* ‘know that’ has elided *ἴσθι*, 2nd sg. imperative of the perfect *οἶδα* with present sense. Within the content clause, *καθ᾽ ἣν στιγμήν* ‘at the moment at which’ conveys the immediacy of *tosto che*; *καθ᾽* is *κατά* elided and aspirated before the rough breathing. *Προδῷ* is 3rd sg. aor. act. subj. of *προδίδωμι*, used without an expressed object or *ἄν* in this general temporal clause. Enclitic *τις* in *ψυχή τις* makes the generic *l’anima* ‘any given soul’.

> 130 come fec’io, il corpo suo l’ è tolto  
> 131 da un demonio, che poscia il governa  
> 132 mentre che il tempo suo tutto sia volto.  

as I did, her body is taken from her by a Demon who thereafter rules it, till its time has all revolved.

> 130 „ Ὡς ἔπραξ᾽ ἐγώ, ταύτης τὸ σῶμα δαίμων  
> („ As I did, its body a demon)
>
> 131 „ Ἁρπάζει, φέρων αὐτὸ μέχρις ὁ χρόνος  
> („ Seizes, governing it until the time)
>
> 132 „ Αὐτοῦ συνεκπληρωθῇ παντελῶς ὅλος·  
> („ Of it be wholly fulfilled in full;)

- *Ὡς ἔπραξ᾽ ἐγώ* ‘as I did’ retains the explicit emphatic pronoun of *come fec’io*; *ἔπραξ᾽* is elided *ἔπραξα*, 1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *πράσσω*. *Ταύτης* (gen. sg. fem. of *οὗτος*) resumes *ψυχή τις*: ‘this soul’s body’. It expresses the possessive *suo*, without a separate equivalent for the dative *l’* in *l’è tolto*.
- *Ταύτης τὸ σῶμα δαίμων ἁρπάζει* ‘her body a demon seizes’ keeps the body first but makes it the accusative object and makes *δαίμων* the nominative agent. *Φέρων αὐτό* ‘bearing it’ is a pres. act. participle, nom. sg. masc., agreeing with *δαίμων*; it subordinates the continued handling of the body instead of translating *che poscia il governa* with a relative verb and an adverb of succession.
- *Μέχρις ὁ χρόνος αὐτοῦ συνεκπληρωθῇ* ‘until its time is fulfilled’ uses *μέχρις* as a conjunction with the prospective aor. pass. subj., 3rd sg. of *συνεκπληρόω*, without *ἄν*. *Αὐτοῦ* refers to the body. The adverb *παντελῶς* modifies the verb, while the postponed adjective *ὅλος* agrees with *χρόνος*: both stress the completeness expressed once by *tutto*.

> 133 Ella ruina in sì fatta cisterna ;  

She falls rushing to this cistern;

> 133 „ Αὐτὴ δ᾽ ἐν τοιῷδ᾽ ἐγκυλίεται λάκκῳ.  
> („ While the soul itself is rolled down into such a pit.)

- *Αὐτὴ δ᾽* ‘but she herself’ emphatically returns to the feminine subject *ψυχή*, as *Ella* does. *Ἐγκυλίεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *ἐγκυλίω*) gives *ruina* the sense ‘rolls, is rolled’. *Ἐν τοιῷδ᾽ … λάκκῳ* ‘in such a pit’, with the verb between demonstrative and noun, substitutes a locative dative for the Italian direction of motion. *Τοιῷδ᾽* is elided *τοιῷδε*, dat. sg. masc. of *τοιόσδε*.

> 134 e forse pare ancor lo corpo suso  
> 135 dell’ ombra che di qua retro mi verna.  

and perhaps the body of this *other shade*, which winters here behind me, is still apparent *on the earth above*.

> 134 „ Ἴσως δ᾽ ἔτ᾽ ἄνω φαίνεται ζῶν τὸ σῶμα  
> („ And perchance the body still appears alive above)
>
> 135 „ Τῆς φρικιώσης σκιᾶς ὧδ᾽ ὄπισθέν μου.  
> („ Of the shivering shade here behind me.)

- *Ἴσως δ᾽ ἔτ᾽ ἄνω* gathers *e forse*, *ancor*, and *suso* before *φαίνεται*; *ἔτ᾽* is elided *ἔτι*. *Φαίνεται* is 3rd sg. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *φαίνω*, ‘appears’. Predicative *ζῶν* (nom. sg. neuter, pres. act. participle of *ζάω*, agreeing with *τὸ σῶμα*) supplies the sense in which the body is apparent, ‘as living’.
- *Τῆς φρικιώσης σκιᾶς* makes *che … verna* an attributive participle within the genitive dependent on *σῶμα*. *Φρικιώσης* (gen. sg. fem., pres. act. of *φρικιάω*) means ‘shivering’, interpreting rather than reproducing *verna*. *Ὧδ᾽ ὄπισθέν μου* ‘here behind me’ renders *di qua retro mi*; *ὄπισθεν* governs the genitive and takes an additional accent before enclitic *μου*.

> 136 Tu il dei saper, se tu vien pur mo giuso:  
> 137 egli è Ser Branca d’ Oria, e son più anni  
> 138 poscia passati ch’ ei fu sì racchiuso.”  

Thou must know [it], if thou art but now come down: it is Ser Branca d'Oria; and many years have passed since he was thus shut up.”

> 136 „ Δεῖ δέ σ᾽ εἰδέναι τοῦτ᾽, εἴ γ᾽ ἄνωθεν ἥκεις·  
> („ And you must know this, if indeed you come from above;)
>
> 137 „ Κὺρ-Βράγκας ἐστὶ Δορίας, κοὐ πόλλ᾽ ἔτη  
> („ It is Ser Branca d'Oria, and not many years)
>
> 138 „ Παρῆλθ᾽ ἀφ᾽ ὅτου τῷδ᾽ ἐν βόθρῳ καθεῖρκται.”—  
> („ Have passed since he was shut up in this pit.”—)

- *Δεῖ δέ σ᾽ εἰδέναι τοῦτ᾽* uses impersonal *δεῖ* with an accusative subject (*σ᾽* = *σε*) and the perfect active infinitive *εἰδέναι* of *οἶδα*, present in meaning. *Τοῦτ᾽* supplies the demonstrative object for *il*. *Εἴ γ᾽ ἄνωθεν ἥκεις* ‘if indeed you have come from above’ uses restrictive *γε* for *pur*; *ἥκεις*, 2nd sg. pres. ind. of *ἥκω*, denotes arrival already achieved. *Ἄνωθεν* specifies the point of departure rather than the direction *giuso*, and *mo* has no distinct equivalent.
- *Κὺρ-Βράγκας ἐστὶ Δορίας* adapts *Ser* with the later Greek title *Κύρ* and gives the personal and family names Greek nominative endings, with the copula between them. *Κοὐ* is crasis of *καὶ οὐ*: *κοὐ πόλλ᾽ ἔτη* therefore means ‘and not many years’, the reverse of the supplied Italian *e son più anni*. *Πόλλ᾽* is elided *πολλά*, nom. pl. neut. with *ἔτη*.
- *Παρῆλθ᾽* is elided *παρῆλθε*, 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *παρέρχομαι*; the singular verb with neuter plural subject is regular Greek. *Ἀφ᾽ ὅτου* ‘since when’ marks the starting point (elision and aspiration of *ἀπό*). *Καθεῖρκται*, 3rd sg. perf. mid./pass. ind. of *καθείργω*, expresses the continuing result of *fu … racchiuso*. *Τῷδ᾽ ἐν βόθρῳ* places the demonstrative before the preposition governing its noun: ‘in this pit’ makes *sì* concrete.

> 139 “Io credo,” dissi lui, “che tu m’ inganni:  
> 140 chè Branca d’ Oria non morì unquanche,  
> 141 e mangia e bee e dorme e veste panni.”  

“I believe,” said I to him, “that thou deceivest me: for Branca d'Oria never died; and eats, and drinks, and sleeps, and puts on clothes.”

> 139 — “Δοκεῖς μοι θέλων,” εἶπον, “ἐξαπατᾷν με,  
> (—“You seem to me,” I said, “to wish to deceive me,)
>
> 140 „ Οὐ γὰρ ἔθαν᾽, ἀλλὰ ζῇ Βράγκας Δορίας,  
> („ For Branca d'Oria has not died, but lives,)
>
> 141 „ Τρώγων, πίνων, ὑπνώττων, φορῶν ἐσθῆτας.”—  
> („ Eating, drinking, sleeping, and wearing clothes.”—)

- *Δοκεῖς μοι … ἐξαπατᾶν με* ‘you seem to me … to deceive me’ reverses the perspective of *Io credo … che tu m’inganni*. *Δοκεῖς* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *δοκέω*) has a personal subject, a dative of the judge, and a present active infinitive with the same understood subject. *Θέλων*, nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *θέλω*, adds intention: ‘wishing’, or ‘deliberately’. The reporting *εἶπον* is parenthetic; *lui* is not repeated.
- *Οὐ γὰρ ἔθαν᾽, ἀλλὰ ζῇ* ‘for he did not die, but lives’: postpositive *γάρ* renders causal *chè*. *Ἔθαν᾽* is elided *ἔθανε*, the poetic second aor. act. ind., 3rd sg., of *θνῄσκω*; the simple negative does not separately express *unquanche*. *Ἀλλὰ ζῇ* supplies an explicit positive counterpart, followed by the subject *Βράγκας Δορίας*.
- *Τρώγων, πίνων, ὑπνώττων, φορῶν* changes four coordinated finite presents into an asyndetic series of nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participles describing the subject of *ζῇ*. *Τρώγω* gives *mangia* the concrete sense ‘chew, eat’; *ὑπνώττων*, from *ὑπνώττω*, has Attic *-ττ-* for *-σσ-*. Contracted *φορῶν* from *φορέω* takes *ἐσθῆτας* (acc. pl. of *ἐσθής*): ‘wearing garments’, rather than an act of putting them on.

> 142 “Nel fosso su,” diss’ ei, “di Malebranche,  
> 143 là dove bolle la tenace pece,  
> 144 non era giunto ancora Michel Zanche,  

“In the ditch above, of the Malebranche,” said he, “there where the tenacious pitch is boiling, Michel Zanche had not yet arrived,

> 142 Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπεν· “Ἐν τῷ βόθρῳ τῶν Κακωνύχων  
> (And he said: “In the pit of the Evil-claws)
>
> 143 „ Ὕπερθεν, ἔνθα βράζει γλοιώδης πίσσα,  
> („ Above, where the sticky pitch boils,)
>
> 144 „ Οὐκ ἦν ἔτ᾽ ἀφιγμένος Μιχαὴλ Ζάγκης,  
> („ Michel Zanche had not yet arrived,)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπεν* moves the reporting clause to the beginning. *Ἐν τῷ βόθρῳ τῶν Κακωνύχων* retains the place noun and dependent genitive of *nel fosso … di Malebranche*. *Κακωνύχων* (gen. pl.) renders the name by a Greek compound, ‘those with evil claws’; the local adverb *ὕπερθεν*, ‘above’, follows the whole phrase.
- *Ἔνθα βράζει γλοιώδης πίσσα* ‘where sticky pitch boils’ compresses *là dove* into the relative adverb *ἔνθα*. *Γλοιώδης* (nom. sg. fem., a two-ending adjective) specifies the adhesive quality of *tenace*; the subject *πίσσα* follows the verb without an article.
- *Οὐκ ἦν ἔτ᾽ ἀφιγμένος* ‘he had not yet arrived’ matches *non era giunto ancora* with a periphrastic pluperfect: imperfect *ἦν* plus the nom. sg. masc. perfect middle participle of deponent *ἀφικνέομαι*. *Ἔτ᾽* = *ἔτι*. The postponed subject has an indeclinable first name *Μιχαήλ* and a Greek nominative *Ζάγκης*.

> 145 che questi lasciò il diavolo in sua vece  
> 146 nel corpo suo, e d’ un suo prossimano  
> 147 che il tradimento insieme con lui fece.  

when this *man* left a *Devil* in his stead in the body of himself, and of one of his kindred who did the treachery along with him.

> 145 „ Ὅτ᾽ ἀνθ᾽ ἑαυτοῦ δαίμον᾽ οὗτος ἀφῆκεν  
> („ When this man left a demon in his own stead)
>
> 146 „ Ἐν ἑαυτοῦ σώματι, συγγενοῦς τ᾽ ἄλλου,  
> („ In his own body, and in that of another kinsman,)
>
> 147 „ Προδοσίαν μετ᾽ αὐτοῦ κατεργασθέντος.  
> („ Who wrought the treachery along with him.)

- *Ὅτ᾽* (elided *ὅτε*) makes temporal the Italian *che* after the negative pluperfect. *Ἀνθ᾽ ἑαυτοῦ* ‘instead of himself’ moves *in sua vece* before the object; *ἀντί* loses its final vowel and its *τ* becomes *θ* before the rough breathing. *Δαίμον᾽* is elided accusative *δαίμονα*; demonstrative *οὗτος* alone renders *questi*. *Ἀφῆκεν* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀφίημι*, ‘let go, left’.
- *Ἐν ἑαυτοῦ σώματι* puts the reflexive possessive genitive before the dative noun: ‘in his own body’. *Συγγενοῦς τ᾽ ἄλλου* ‘and of another relative’ keeps the ellipsis of a second body in *e d’un suo prossimano*. *Τ᾽* is connective *τε* elided; *συγγενοῦς* is the gen. sg. of substantival *συγγενής*, with *ἄλλου* in agreement rather than a possessive for *suo*.
- *Προδοσίαν μετ᾽ αὐτοῦ κατεργασθέντος* substitutes a genitive participle agreeing with the kinsman for the finite relative *che … fece*. *Κατεργασθέντος* is gen. sg. masc. aor. participle of *κατεργάζομαι*, passive in form but here with active sense and the direct object *προδοσίαν*. *Μετ᾽ αὐτοῦ* (*μετά* with genitive) suffices for *insieme con lui*.

> 148 Ma distendi oramai in qua la mano,  
> 149 aprimi gli occhi” ; ed io non gliele apersi,  
> 150 e cortesia fu in lui esser villano.  

But reach hither thy hand: open my eyes”; and I opened them not for him: and to be rude to him was courtesy.

> 148 „ Ἀλλ᾽ ἄγε δὴ νῦν ἔκτεινόν μοι τὴν χεῖρα·  
> („ But come now, stretch out your hand to me;)
>
> 149 „ Ὄμματ᾽ ἄνοιξον. . .” Οὐκ ἀνέῳξα μέντοι·  
> („ Open my eyes...” Yet I did not open them;)
>
> 150 Περιφρονῶν γὰρ αὐτὸν πρόφρων ἐδόκουν.—  
> (For in despising him I seemed courteous.—)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ ἄγε δὴ νῦν* ‘but come now, now indeed’: *ἄγε*, formally 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *ἄγω*, serves as an exhortation; *δή* lends urgency and *νῦν* renders *oramai*. *Ἔκτεινόν μοι τὴν χεῖρα* has the 2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *ἐκτείνω*, with an additional accent before enclitic *μοι*. The dative ‘to me’ gives the endpoint of *in qua*; *χεῖρα* is acc. sg. of *χείρ*.
- *Ὄμματ᾽ ἄνοιξον* puts the object first, with no possessive: *ὄμματ᾽* is elided *ὄμματα*, acc. pl. neut.; *ἄνοιξον* is 2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *ἀνοίγω*. *Οὐκ ἀνέῳξα μέντοι* ‘I did not open [them], however’ answers the imperative with the 1st sg. aor. act. indicative, showing both internal *-ε-* and lengthened *ῳ* in the augmented form. Neither the emphatic *io* nor the two pronominal objects of *gliele* are expressed; postpositive *μέντοι* marks the contrast.
- *Περιφρονῶν γὰρ αὐτὸν πρόφρων ἐδόκουν* ‘for in despising him I seemed well-disposed’ turns the impersonal equation *cortesia fu … esser villano* into a personal judgment. *Περιφρονῶν* is a circumstantial nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *περιφρονέω*, with the person despised as accusative object rather than *in lui*. Predicate *πρόφρων* agrees with the unexpressed subject of *ἐδόκουν* (here 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *δοκέω*). The participle and adjective echo one another in sound while opposing one another in sense; explanatory *γάρ*, rather than additive *e*, makes the judgment a reason for the refusal.

## Lines 151–157 — The Genoese Rebuked

> 151 Ahi Genovesi, uomini diversi  
> 152 d’ ogni costume, e pien d’ ogni magagna,  

Ah, Genoese! men estranged from all morality, and full of all corruption,

> 151 Αἴ! Γενεάται, γενεὰ διαστρόφων  
> (Ah, Genoese! race of perverse)
>
> 152 Ἠθῶν, καὶ πάσης κακίας ὄντες πλήρεις !  
> (Manners, and full of every wickedness!)

- *Αἴ!* answers *Ahi* with an interjection of lament. *Γενεάται* is vocative plural of *Γενεάτης*, answering *Genovesi* as a direct address. Musurus follows it with the similar-sounding *γενεά* ‘race, stock’, a feminine singular vocative in apposition, in place of the plural *uomini*.
- *γενεὰ διαστρόφων ἠθῶν* means ‘a race of perverse ways’. The genitive plural *ἠθῶν* (from neuter *ἦθος* ‘custom, disposition’) is a genitive of quality; its adjective *διαστρόφων* (from *διάστροφος* ‘twisted, perverse’) precedes it across the line break. In *uomini diversi d’ ogni costume*, *diversi* characterizes the men as estranged from every good custom; in Greek it is their ways that are perverse, and the first *ogni* has no separate equivalent.
- *καὶ πάσης κακίας ὄντες πλήρεις* ‘and being full of every wickedness’ retains *e pien d’ ogni magagna*. *πλήρεις* (masc. pl. of *πλήρης*) takes the genitive of that with which one is full, *πάσης κακίας* (fem. gen. sg. of *πᾶς* and *κακία*). Both this genitive and the supplied *ὄντες* (pres. act. participle of *εἰμί*) precede *πλήρεις*. The masculine plurals refer back to the men addressed, not to the singular *γενεά*.

> 153 perchè non siete voi del mondo spersi?  

why are ye not scattered from the earth?

> 153 Πῶς οὐκ ἐστὲ τοῦ κόσμου δεδιωγμένοι ;  
> (Why are you not driven out of the world?)

- *Πῶς οὐκ* ‘how is it that … not?’ renders *perchè non* as a question about an actual state of affairs, with indicative negation *οὐκ* before the vowel of *ἐστέ*. The ending of *ἐστέ* (2nd pl. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*) conveys the person of *siete voi* without an emphatic subject pronoun.
- *ἐστὲ τοῦ κόσμου δεδιωγμένοι* ‘are in the state of having been driven out of the world’ keeps the copula and participle of *siete … spersi*, but substitutes expulsion for scattering. *δεδιωγμένοι* is the reduplicated perfect middle-passive participle of *διώκω*, passive in sense and nominative masculine plural; with *ἐστέ* it makes a periphrastic perfect. *τοῦ κόσμου*, placed between auxiliary and participle like *del mondo* in Italian, is a genitive of separation without a preposition.

> 154 chè col peggiore spirto di Romagna  
> 155 trovai un tal di voi, che per sua opra  
> 156 in anima in Cocito già si bagna,  

for with the worst spirit of Romagna, found I one of ye, who for his deeds even now in soul bathes in Cocytus,

> 154 Σὺν πνεύματι γὰρ κακίστῳ Ῥωμανίας  
> (For with the worst spirit of Romagna)
>
> 155 Εὗρον ἐξ ὑμῶν τινα διὰ κάκ’ ἔργα  
> (I found one of you, who for his evil deeds)
>
> 156 Τὴν ψυχὴν ἔχοντ’ ἐν τῷ Κωκυτοῦ πάγῳ,  
> (Has his soul in the ice of Cocytus,)

- *Σὺν πνεύματι γὰρ κακίστῳ Ῥωμανίας* keeps the companion before the verb, as in *col peggiore spirto di Romagna*. Explanatory *γάρ* answers *chè* but is postpositive, following *σὺν πνεύματι*. *σύν* governs the dative singular *πνεύματι* (neut. *πνεῦμα*); *κακίστῳ*, agreeing with it, is the superlative of *κακός*, rendering the superlative force of the articulated comparative *col peggiore*. *Ῥωμανίας* is a singular genitive of origin corresponding to *di Romagna*.
- *Εὗρον* ‘I found’ is the 1st sg. second aorist active indicative of *εὑρίσκω*, here with *ευ-* rather than the augmented *ηυ-* of *ηὗρον*. *ἐξ ὑμῶν τινα* reverses the order of *un tal di voi*: *ἐξ* with the genitive of the second-person plural pronoun marks the group from which the one is taken; indefinite *τινα* (masc. acc. sg. of *τις*) is the object of *εὗρον*.
- *διὰ κάκ’ ἔργα* ‘because of evil deeds’ renders *per sua opra* by causal *διά* with the accusative. *κάκ’* is elided *κακά*, neuter accusative plural agreeing with *ἔργα* (from *ἔργον*): the Greek pluralizes *opra* and explicitly calls the deeds evil, leaving the possessive *sua* implicit.
- *τὴν ψυχὴν ἔχοντ’* ‘having his soul’ replaces the finite relative construction *che … in anima … si bagna*. *ἔχοντ’* is elided *ἔχοντα*, the present active participle of *ἔχω*, masculine accusative singular agreeing with *τινα*: ‘I found one of you having …’. Its present stem presents the state as concurrent with the finding. *τὴν ψυχήν* is its direct object, with the article rather than a possessive for the man’s own soul.
- *ἐν τῷ Κωκυτοῦ πάγῳ* ‘in the ice of Cocytus’ supplies a static location in place of the image of bathing. *ἐν* governs *πάγῳ* (dat. sg. of *πάγος* ‘ice’); the genitive singular *Κωκυτοῦ* (from *Κωκυτός*) stands between article and noun. Neither the reflexive verb *si bagna* nor the adverb *già* has a separate Greek equivalent.

> 157 ed in corpo par vivo ancor di sopra.  

and above on earth still seems alive in body.

> 157 Οὗ τὸ σῶμ’ ἔτι φαίνεται ζῶν ἐν κόσμῳ.  
> (While his body still appears alive in the world.)

- *Οὗ τὸ σῶμ’* ‘whose body’ makes a possessive relative clause where *ed in corpo* continues the preceding clause by coordination. *οὗ* is the masculine genitive singular of the relative pronoun *ὅς*, referring to *τινα*; *σῶμ’* is *σῶμα* with final *-α* elided before *ἔτι*. The body itself is now the neuter singular subject, rather than *in corpo* qualifying what is said of the man.
- *ἔτι φαίνεται ζῶν* ‘still appears alive’ brings *ancor* forward before the verb. *φαίνεται* (3rd sg. pres. middle-passive ind. of *φαίνω*, here ‘appears’) takes the predicative participle *ζῶν* in place of the adjective *vivo*. Despite its resemblance to the masculine form, *ζῶν* here is neuter nominative singular, the contracted present active participle of *ζάω*, agreeing with *σῶμα*. Final *ἐν κόσμῳ* ‘in the world’ (*ἐν* with the dative singular of *κόσμος*) interprets *di sopra* as a location rather than translating ‘above’ directly.

## Summary — Dante’s Verbs in Greek Dress

Musurus often preserves Dante’s order of perception while changing the grammar that carries it. In *pianger senti’* and *κλαίοντ’ ἤκουσα*, the weeping is heard before the hearer is named by the verb; the Greek, however, replaces the infinitive with a participle agreeing with the children. Participles also let Musurus alter the relation between successive acts. At the opening, finite *sollevò* and accompanying *forbendola* become the two aorist participles *ἐξάρας* and *ἀπομαξάμενος*: both acts are now preliminary to *ἤρξατο* ‘he began’. Elsewhere a genitive absolute allows a change of subject: where Dante says that ‘we had come’ to the fourth day, *ἀνατειλάσης* ‘having dawned’ makes the day itself the subject.

Greek case and particles often do work assigned in Italian to a preposition, a clause, or word order. A bare dative gives the means of wiping in *ταῖς θριξίν* ‘with the hair’; impersonal *δεῖ* with an accusative and infinitive replaces personal *tu … dei saper*. Postpositive *δέ* carries a change of speaker, *γάρ* makes an explanation explicit, and *ἵνα* with the subjunctive marks a purpose that Italian introduces with *perchè* or *sì che*. Yet the recasting is not uniform. Musurus keeps the delayed judgment of *dir non è mestieri* in *λέγειν οὐκ ἔστι χρεία* ‘there is no need to tell’, and his prospective *πρίν* with a bare subjunctive follows Italian *pria che* more closely than ordinary Attic prose would.

Musurus sometimes preserves a verbal pattern more closely than its image. The repeated *tu* survives as repeated *σύ* in the children’s appeal, but *vestisti … spoglia* ‘clothed … strip’ becomes *δούς … λάβε πάλιν* ‘having given … take back’. Conversely, a single Greek word can retain a distinction obscured in English: *ἡλουμένην* ‘being nailed up’ gives *chiavar* a more specific sense than the prose translation’s ‘locked up’. Two discrepancies go beyond such choices of idiom. Musurus has *τρεῖς* ‘three’ where Oelsner prints *due* ‘two’, and *κοὐ πόλλ’ ἔτη* ‘and not many years’ introduces a negative absent from *e son più anni*.
