# Inferno 7

## Lines 1–15 — Plutus Silenced and Felled

> 1 “Pape Satan, pape Satan aleppe,”  
> 2 cominciò Pluto colla voce chioccia ;  

“Pape Satan! pape Satan, aleppe!” began Plutus, with clucking voice;

> 1 “Ἆ! παπαὶ Σατάν, παπαὶ Σατάν, Ἀλέππε!”  
> ("Ah! alas, Satan, alas, Satan, Aleppe!")
>
> 2 Κράζειν ἤρξατο Πλούτων φωνῇ βραγχώδει.  
> (Plutus began to cry out with a hoarse voice.)

- Musurus keeps the repeated address and the final *Ἀλέππε*, but interprets *Pape* by the Greek interjection *παπαί*, an exclamation of surprise or distress. He also supplies an initial *Ἆ*. The indeclinable *Σατάν* stands in direct address both times.
- Where *cominciò Pluto* leaves the act of speaking implicit, Greek puts it first: *Κράζειν ἤρξατο Πλούτων* ‘to cry out Plutus began’. *Κράζειν* is the present active infinitive of *κράζω*, complementary to *ἤρξατο* (3rd sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἄρχομαι* ‘begin’, with temporal augment). The subject *Πλούτων* follows the verb as *Pluto* does; the Greek name is nominative *Πλούτων*, not *Πλοῦτος*.
- *φωνῇ βραγχώδει* ‘with a hoarse voice’ renders *colla voce chioccia* by a dative of manner without preposition or article. *βραγχώδει* (dat. sg. fem. of *βραγχώδης*) characterizes the roughness of the sound rather than imitating a cluck.

> 3 e quel Savio gentil, che tutto seppe,  
> 4 disse per confortarmi : “Non ti noccia  

and that gentle Sage, who knew all, said, comforting me: “Let not thy fear hurt thee:

> 3 Ὁ δὲ χαρίεις σοφός, ὃς τὰ πάντ’ οἶδε,  
> (And the gracious sage, who knows all things,)
>
> 4 Θαῤῥύνων μ’ εἶπε· “Μὴ βλάψῃ σ’ ὁ σὸς φόβος·  
> (Encouraging me, said: "Let not your fear harm you;)

- *Ὁ δὲ χαρίεις σοφός* ‘and the gracious sage’ uses the article rather than a demonstrative for *quel*; postpositive *δέ* marks the change of speaker. *χαρίεις* (nom. sg. masc., stem *χαριεντ-*) stands before the substantival *σοφός*, reversing *Savio gentil*. In *ὃς τὰ πάντ’ οἶδε*, the relative *ὅς* is the subject and *τὰ πάντ’* (for *τὰ πάντα*) the neuter plural accusative object. *οἶδε* is the 3rd sg. perfect active indicative of *οἶδα*, with present sense ‘knows’, against the past *seppe*.
- *Θαῤῥύνων μ’ εἶπε* ‘encouraging me, he said’ moves the comforting before the verb of speech and makes *per confortarmi* an attendant action rather than an explicit purpose. *Θαῤῥύνων* is the nom. sg. masc. present active participle of *θαρρύνω*, agreeing with the sage; *ῤῥ* is the older typography for doubled rho. Elided *μ’* (*με*) is its object; *εἶπε* is the 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *λέγω*.
- *Μὴ βλάψῃ σ’ ὁ σὸς φόβος* closely follows *Non ti noccia la tua paura*, even to the postponed subject. *μή* with *βλάψῃ* (3rd sg. aor. act. subj. of *βλάπτω*) expresses a negative injunction. Italian *ti* is an indirect object of *noccia*, but Greek *σ’* (*σε*) is the direct accusative object of *βλάψῃ*. The attributive possessive *σός* agrees with *φόβος*.

> 5 la tua paura, chè, poder ch’ egli abbia,  
> 6 non ti torrà lo scender questa roccia.”  

for, whatever power he have, he shall not hinder [thee] from descending this rock.”

> 5 „Ὁποιονδήποτε γὰρ κράτος ἂν ἔχῃ,  
> (For whatever power he may possess,)
>
> 6 „Οὐ κωλύσει σε τοῦ κρημνοῦ καταβῆναι.”  
> (He shall not hinder you from descending the crag.")

- Postpositive *γάρ* takes up explanatory *chè*. For *poder ch’ egli abbia*, Greek has *Ὁποιονδήποτε … κράτος ἂν ἔχῃ* ‘whatever power he may have’: the indefinite relative adjective *ὁποιονδήποτε* agrees with *κράτος* (acc. sg. neut.), object of *ἔχῃ*. The subject *egli* is left implicit. *ἄν* with the 3rd sg. present active subjunctive of *ἔχω* gives the relative clause indefinite, here concessive, force.
- *Οὐ κωλύσει σε … καταβῆναι* ‘he will not prevent you from descending’ recasts *non ti torrà lo scender*: instead of taking the descent away from someone, Greek *κωλύω* takes the person prevented in the accusative and the action as an infinitive. *κωλύσει* is 3rd sg. future active indicative; *σε* is also the understood subject of *καταβῆναι*, the root-aorist **active** infinitive of *καταβαίνω*. *τοῦ κρημνοῦ* ‘from the crag’ is genitive of the place descended from; the article replaces the demonstrative of *questa roccia*.

> 7 Poi si rivolse a quella enfiata labbia,  
> 8 e disse : “Taci, maledetto lupo!  

Then he turned himself to that inflated visage, and said: “Peace, cursed Wolf!

> 7 Ἔπειτα στραφεὶς πρὸς τᾠδηκότα χείλη  
> (Then turning toward those swollen lips,)
>
> 8 Εἶπ’ αὐτῷ· “Σῖγα, κατηραμένε λύκε·  
> (He said to him: "Silence, accursed wolf;)

- *Ἔπειτα στραφεὶς … Εἶπ’ αὐτῷ* ‘then, having turned …, he said to him’ makes *si rivolse* subordinate to *disse*, so that no conjunction is needed. *στραφείς* (nom. sg. masc., second aor. pass. part. of *στρέφω*) is passive in form but intransitive in sense. *Εἶπ’* is *εἶπε* elided before the supplied dative of the addressee, *αὐτῷ*.
- *πρὸς τᾠδηκότα χείλη* ‘toward the swollen lips’ renders *a quella enfiata labbia*. Musurus takes the facial expression more narrowly as the lips, substituting a neuter plural for the Italian feminine singular. *πρός* governs the accusative; *τᾠδηκότα* is crasis of *τὰ ᾠδηκότα*, article and perfect active participle of *οἰδέω* ‘swell’. The participle agrees with *χείλη* (acc. pl. of *χεῖλος*) and denotes the resulting swollen state.
- *Σῖγα* ‘be silent’ is the contracted 2nd sg. present active imperative of *σιγάω*, corresponding directly to *Taci*. Both words of *κατηραμένε λύκε* are vocative singular: *κατηραμένε* is the perfect middle-passive participle of *καταράομαι*, used passively like *maledetto*, and *λύκε* is from *λύκος*.

> 9 consuma dentro te con la tua rabbia.  

consume thyself internally with thy greedy rage.

> 9 „Χώνευσον καταπιὼν τὴν σαυτοῦ λύσσαν.  
> (Swallow and digest your own fury.)

- *Χώνευσον καταπιὼν τὴν σαυτοῦ λύσσαν* is literally ‘digest your own fury, having swallowed it’. Musurus changes the object of *consuma*: the wolf is not told to consume itself *con la tua rabbia*; it is to consume the fury. Swallowing supplies the inward direction of *dentro te* without a separate expression of place.
- *Χώνευσον* is the 2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *χωνεύω*; *καταπιών* is the nom. sg. masc. second aor. act. participle of *καταπίνω*, denoting the act preliminary to digestion. *τὴν … λύσσαν* (acc. sg.) serves both verbs. Possessive *σαυτοῦ*, the shortened form of the reflexive genitive *σεαυτοῦ*, stands between article and noun: ‘your own’.

> 10 Non è senza cagion l’ andare al cupo:  

Not without cause is our journey to the deep:

> 10 „Οὐκ ἄνευ λόγου τὸ καταβῆν’ ἐνθάδε.  
> (Not without reason is the descent hither.)

- *Οὐκ ἄνευ λόγου* ‘not without reason’ closely follows *Non è senza cagion*, but leaves the copula understood; *ἄνευ* governs genitive *λόγου*. *τὸ καταβῆν’ ἐνθάδε* ‘the descending hither’ is the subject: the neuter singular article nominalizes *καταβῆναι*, as *l’* does *andare*. The spelling *καταβῆν’* shows poetic elision of infinitival *-αι* before the vowel. *ἐνθάδε* says ‘hither’, not literally *al cupo*; the downward motion is in the verb.

> 11 vuolsi nell’ alto, là dove Michele  
> 12 fe’ la vendetta del superbo strupo.”  

it is willed on high, [there] where Michael took vengeance of the proud adultery.”

> 11 „Τοῦτ’ ἄνω θέλουσ’, ἔνθα Μιχαὴλ δίκην  
> (They will it on high, where Michael vengeance)
>
> 12 „Ἔλαβέ ποτε τῆς ἀλαζόνος βίας.”  
> (Took once for the arrogant violence.")

- *Τοῦτ’ ἄνω θέλουσ’* ‘this they will above’ turns impersonal *vuolsi* into an active verb with an unspecified plural subject. *Τοῦτ’* is elided *τοῦτο*, the accusative object pointing back to the descent; *ἄνω* is an adverb for *nell’ alto*. *θέλουσ’* is *θέλουσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *θέλω*) with final *-ι* elided before *ἔνθα*. The single relative adverb *ἔνθα* ‘where’ does the work of *là dove*.
- *Μιχαὴλ δίκην ἔλαβέ ποτε* replaces *Michele fe’ la vendetta* with the Greek idiom *δίκην λαμβάνειν* ‘take satisfaction, exact punishment’. The indeclinable name is the subject; accusative *δίκην* precedes *ἔλαβε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λαμβάνω*). Musurus adds enclitic *ποτε* ‘once’, which accounts for the additional acute on the final syllable of *ἔλαβέ*.
- *τῆς ἀλαζόνος βίας* is the genitive of the offence for which satisfaction was taken. *ἀλαζόνος* (gen. sg. fem. of *ἀλαζών* ‘boastful, arrogant’) agrees with *βίας*. In rendering *del superbo strupo*, Musurus chooses *βία* ‘violence, force’, rather than the specifically sexual interpretation “adultery”.

> 13 Quali dal vento le gonfiate vele  
> 14 caggiono avvolte, poichè l’ alber fiacca :  
> 15 tal cadde a terra la fiera crudele.  

As sails, swelled by the wind, fall entangled when the mast breaks: so fell that cruel monster to the ground.

> 13 Ὡς ἐξ ἀνέμου τἄρμεν’ ἐξογκωθέντα  
> (As sails puffed out by the wind)
>
> 14 Συγκαταπίπτει, τῶν ἱστῶν ἐαγέντων,  
> (Collapse together when the masts are broken,)
>
> 15 Οὕτω πέπτωκε τἀνηλεὲς θηρίον.  
> (So fell the pitiless beast.)

- Adverbial *Ὡς … Οὕτω* ‘as … so’ replaces *Quali … tal*, without adjectives agreeing with the things compared. *ἐξ ἀνέμου* ‘from wind’ expresses the cause of the swelling and precedes the noun, as *dal vento* does. *τἄρμεν’* represents *τὰ ἄρμενα* ‘the tackle’, here ‘the sails’, with crasis at the beginning and elision of final *-α* before *ἐξογκωθέντα*. That participle (nom. pl. neut., aor. pass. of *ἐξογκόω* ‘inflate’) agrees with the sails and renders *gonfiate*.
- *Συγκαταπίπτει* ‘fall down together’ is 3rd **singular** present active indicative of *συγκαταπίπτω*, the regular Attic agreement with a neuter plural subject. The compound conveys a collective collapse, without a separate equivalent for *avvolte*. *τῶν ἱστῶν ἐαγέντων* ‘the masts having been broken’ replaces the finite *poichè l’ alber fiacca* with a temporal genitive absolute and makes the single mast plural. *ἐαγέντων* (gen. pl. masc., second aor. pass. part. of *ἄγνυμι* ‘break’) agrees with *ἱστῶν*; its initial *ἐ-* belongs to the *ἐαγ-* form of the aorist stem even in the participle.
- *Οὕτω πέπτωκε* ‘so has fallen’ substitutes the resultative perfect for narrative *tal cadde*. *πέπτωκε* is 3rd sg. perfect active indicative of *πίπτω*; there is no separate *a terra*. The subject again follows the verb: *τἀνηλεὲς θηρίον* ‘the pitiless beast’, with *τἀνηλές* by crasis for *τὸ ἀνηλές* (nom. sg. neut. of *ἀνηλεής*). The article replaces *la* in *la fiera crudele*, and the adjective, now attributive before the noun, specifies lack of pity.

## Lines 16–48 — The Avaricious and the Prodigal

> 16 Così scendemmo nella quarta lacca,  
> 17 prendendo più della dolente ripa,  
> 18 che il mal dell' universo tutto insacca.  

[Thus] we descended into the fourth concavity, taking in more of the dismal bank, which shuts up all the evil of the universe.

> 16 Εἰς βυθὸν κατέβημεν τέταρτον οὕτω,  
> (Into the fourth depth we thus descended,)
>
> 17 Βαίνοντες πρόσω πρὸς τὴν ὄχθην ἀβύσσου  
> (Advancing onward toward the bank of the abyss)
>
> 18 Τῆς βροχθιζούσης τοῦ Παντὸς κακὰ πάντα.  
> (That swallows down all the evils of the Universe.)

- *Εἰς βυθὸν κατέβημεν τέταρτον οὕτω* puts the destination first and *οὕτω* ‘thus’ last, reversing the emphasis of *Così scendemmo*. The verb separates *βυθόν* ‘depth’ from its agreeing ordinal *τέταρτον* (both acc. sg. masc. after *εἰς*). *Κατέβημεν* is the 1st pl. root aorist active indicative of *καταβαίνω*.
- *Βαίνοντες πρόσω* ‘going onward’ retains the participial construction of *prendendo* (*βαίνοντες*, pres. act. ptcp., nom. pl. masc. of *βαίνω*, agreeing with the subject of *κατέβημεν*), but renders *più* as further progress, not as a greater amount taken in. *Πρὸς τὴν ὄχθην ἀβύσσου* ‘toward the bank of the abyss’ substitutes a goal for *della ... ripa* and a dependent genitive for *dolente*.
- *Τῆς βροχθιζούσης* ‘which gulps down’ is an articular present active participle (gen. sg. fem. of *βροχθίζω*). Its case attaches it to *ἀβύσσου*, not to accusative *ὄχθην*: what swallows in Greek is the abyss. *Τοῦ Παντός* ‘of the Whole’ substantivizes *πᾶς* in the neuter singular; *κακὰ πάντα* ‘evils, all of them’ distributes the collective singular *il mal ... tutto* into an accusative plural object.

> 19 Ahi giustizia di Dio! tante chi stipa  
> 20 nuove travaglie e pene, quante io viddi?  

Ah, Justice Divine! who shall tell in few the many fresh pains and travails that I saw?

> 19 Θεία Δικαιοσύνη ! Τίς συσσωρεῦσαι  
> (O Divine Justice! Who could heap together)
>
> 20 Τόσα δεινὰ δύναιτ’ ἄν, ὅσ’ ἐκεῖθ’ εἶδον ;  
> (So many terrors as I saw there?)

- *Θεία Δικαιοσύνη* is a direct address, with adjective for the genitive *di Dio* and no separate word for *Ahi*. *Τίς συσσωρεῦσαι τόσα δεινὰ δύναιτ᾽ ἄν;* asks ‘who could heap together so many terrible things?’ Thus Musurus takes *stipa* in the sense of piling up, not in the sense suggested by “tell in few.” *Συσσωρεῦσαι* (aor. act. inf. of *συσσωρεύω*) is placed well before its governing *δύναιτ᾽ ἄν*, elided *δύναιτο ἄν* (3rd sg. pres. middle optative of *δύναμαι* with potential *ἄν*). The Italian present question becomes one about what anyone could do.
- *Τόσα ... ὅσα* reproduces the correlation *tante ... quante*. Substantival *δεινά* (acc. pl. neut.) condenses *nuove travaglie e pene*, without distinguishing the two nouns or rendering *nuove*. Relative *ὅσ᾽* is the object of *εἶδον* (here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*); the personal ending suffices for *io*. Poetic *ἐκεῖθ᾽* (elided *ἐκεῖθι*) supplies ‘there’.

> 21 e perchè nostra colpa sì ne scipa?  

and why does guilt of ours thus waste us?

> 21 Πῶς οὕτως ἡμᾶς τὰ πταίσματα σπαράττει ;  
> (Why do our offences thus tear us apart?)

- *Πῶς οὕτως ἡμᾶς τὰ πταίσματα σπαράττει;* asks literally ‘how do the offences rend us so?’ *Πῶς* changes the causal question *perchè* to one of manner; *οὕτως* retains *sì*, but the opening *e* and possessive *nostra* have no separate equivalents. *Ἡμᾶς* is the accusative object corresponding to *ne*. Plural *τὰ πταίσματα* ‘the missteps, offences’ replaces singular *colpa* but takes a singular verb in ordinary Greek neuter-plural syntax: *σπαράττει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *σπαράττω*) is more specifically physical than *scipa*.

> 22 Come fa l' onda là sovra Cariddi,  
> 23 che si frange con quella in cui s' intoppa:  
> 24 così convien che qui la gente riddi.  

As does the surge, there above Charybdis, that breaks itself against the surge wherewith it meets: so have the people here to counter-dance.

> 22 Ὡς ἄνω Χαρύβδεως ὁρμῶν τὸ κῦμα  
> (As above Charybdis the rushing wave)
>
> 23 Κατὰ κύματος, ὑπὸ τούτου συνθλᾶται,  
> (Against another wave is shattered by it,)
>
> 24 Οὕτω συγκρούεσθ’ αὐτοὺς ὧδε συμβαίνει.  
> (So here it befalls them to clash together.)

- *Ὡς ... οὕτω* preserves *Come ... così*, but instead of *fa* followed by a relative clause Greek gives the wave a single finite predicate. *Ἄνω Χαρύβδεως* ‘above Charybdis’ uses the local adverb with a genitive, without a separate *là*. *Ὁρμῶν* is neuter nominative singular, not masculine here: it is the contracted present active participle of *ὁρμάω*, agreeing with the following *τὸ κῦμα*. *Κατὰ κύματος* ‘against a wave’ completes the motion of the participle.
- *Ὑπὸ τούτου συνθλᾶται* ‘it is crushed by that one’ makes the second wave, resumed by genitive neuter *τούτου*, the agent of an explicit passive (3rd sg. pres. ind. of *συνθλάω*). This replaces the reflexive or intransitive *si frange* and the relative expression *con quella in cui s’intoppa*.
- *Συγκρούεσθ᾽ αὐτοὺς ὧδε συμβαίνει* ‘it befalls them to collide here’ puts an accusative-and-infinitive construction before impersonal *συμβαίνει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *συμβαίνω*). *Αὐτούς*, the subject of *συγκρούεσθαι* (pres. mid. inf. of *συγκρούω*, with poetic elision of final *-αι*), replaces collective *la gente*. The middle expresses mutual collision rather than the dancing of *riddi*; *συμβαίνει* expresses occurrence rather than the necessity of *convien*.

> 25 Qui vidi gente più che altrove troppa,  
> 26 e d' una parte e d' altra, con grandi urli,  
> 27 voltando pesi per forza di poppa;  

Here saw I too many more than elsewhere, both on the one side and on the other, with loud howlings, rolling weights by force of chests;

> 25 Πλείους ἤπερ ἀλλαχοῦ ψυχὰς ὧδ’ εἶδον,  
> (More souls than elsewhere I saw here,)
>
> 26 Ἔνθεν κἀκεῖθεν μετ’ ὠρυγμῶν μεγάλων  
> (On this side and on that with loud howlings)
>
> 27 Φορτία κυλιούσας διὰ τοῦ στήθους.  
> (Rolling weights with their breasts.)

- *Πλείους ἤπερ ἀλλαχοῦ ψυχὰς ὧδ᾽ εἶδον* fronts the comparison: ‘more souls than elsewhere I saw here’. *Πλείους* is the contracted acc. pl. fem. of comparative *πλείων*, agreeing with *ψυχάς*, the object of *εἶδον*. *Ἤπερ* marks the standard of comparison more emphatically than bare *ἤ*; *ὧδ᾽* is elided *ὧδε*. Musurus specifies ‘souls’ for *gente* and gives no separate equivalent for the excess expressed by *troppa*.
- *Ἔνθεν κἀκεῖθεν* ‘from here and from there’ gives the two sides by paired adverbs of source; *κἀκεῖθεν* is crasis for *καὶ ἐκεῖθεν*. *Μετ᾽ ὠρυγμῶν μεγάλων* uses elided *μετά* with the genitive of accompaniment for *con grandi urli*.
- *Φορτία κυλιούσας* ‘rolling loads’ has an accusative object and a present active participle (acc. pl. fem. of *κυλίω*). Unlike the invariant gerund *voltando*, the participle agrees with the souls seen, not the person seeing. *Διὰ τοῦ στήθους* ‘by means of the breast’ expresses *per forza di poppa* instrumentally, leaving *forza* implicit and using a generic singular.

> 28 percotevansi incontro, e poscia pur lì  
> 29 si rivolgea ciascun, voltando a retro,  
> 30 gridando: "Perchè tieni?" e "Perchè burli?"  

they smote against each other, and then each wheeled round just there, rolling aback, shouting, “Why holdest thou?” and “Why throwest thou away?”

> 28 Συνεπαίοντ’ ἀλλήλαις· εἶτα δ’ ἑκάστη  
> (They dashed against one another; and then each)
>
> 29 Περιεστρέφετ’, ἀπεχώρει τ’ ὀπίσω,  
> (Turned round and retreated backward,)
>
> 30 Κράζουσαι· “ Πρὸς τί τηρεῖς ; ” καὶ “ πρὸς τί ῥίπτεις ; ”  
> (Crying out: "Why do you keep?" and "Why do you cast away?")

- *Συνεπαίοντ᾽ ἀλλήλαις* ‘they struck against one another’ combines an imperfect middle (3rd pl., *συνεπαίοντο*, elided before the vowel) with the dative plural feminine of the reciprocal pronoun, referring back to *ψυχάς*. It thus makes the reciprocity of *percotevansi incontro* explicit in a separate word.
- *Εἶτα δ᾽ ἑκάστη περιεστρέφετ᾽* ‘and then each turned round’ follows the Italian change to distributive singular *ciascun*, but makes it feminine after *ψυχάς*. *Περιεστρέφετ᾽* is elided *περιεστρέφετο* (3rd sg. imperf. mid. of *περιστρέφω*, with the augment inside the compound). *Ἀπεχώρει τ᾽ ὀπίσω* ‘and went back’ adds a coordinated finite imperfect (3rd sg. act. of *ἀποχωρέω*; *τ᾽* = *τε*) for *voltando a retro*: the souls retreat, with no further mention of rolling. *Pur lì* is not separately rendered.
- *Κράζουσαι* (pres. act. ptcp., nom. pl. fem. of *κράζω*) returns from singular *ἑκάστη* to the plurality understood in *gridando*. Repeated *πρὸς τί* ‘to what end?’ renders *Perchè* in each question. *Τηρεῖς* (contracted from *τηρέω*) and *ῥίπτεις* (from *ῥίπτω*) are both 2nd sg. pres. act. indicatives without expressed objects; the latter interprets *burli* as casting away.

> 31 Così tornavan per lo cerchio tetro,  
> 32 da ogni mano all' opposito punto,  
> 33 gridandosi anche loro ontoso metro.  

Thus they returned along the gloomy circle, on either hand, to the opposite point, [again] shouting [at each other] their reproachful measure.

> 31 Οὕτως ἐπανήρχοντο διὰ τοῦ κύκλου  
> (Thus they returned along the circle)
>
> 32 Ἑκασταχόθεν πρὸς τἀπέναντι μέρος,  
> (From either side toward the opposite part,)
>
> 33 Ἐπᾴδουσαι τἄτιμον αὐτῶν τοῦτ’ ᾆσμα.  
> (Chanting again this shameful song of theirs.)

- *Οὕτως ἐπανήρχοντο* closely follows *Così tornavan*: *ἐπανήρχοντο* is the 3rd pl. imperfect middle indicative of *ἐπανέρχομαι*, with internal augment and active sense. *Διὰ τοῦ κύκλου* ‘through the circle’ gives the route by *διά* with the genitive; *tetro* drops out.
- *Ἑκασταχόθεν* ‘from each quarter’ compresses *da ogni mano* into an adverb in *-θεν*. *Πρὸς τἀπέναντι μέρος* gives the destination as ‘toward the opposite part’, less exact than *punto*; *τἀπέναντι* is crasis for *τὸ ἀπέναντι*, the adverb standing attributively with *μέρος*.
- *Ἐπᾴδουσαι* (pres. act. ptcp., nom. pl. fem. of *ἐπᾴδω*) makes *gridandosi* a chanting of a refrain rather than shouting. There is no reciprocal pronoun. *Τἄτιμον αὐτῶν τοῦτ᾽ ᾆσμα* ‘this dishonourable song of theirs’ gives the participle an accusative object: *τἄτιμον* is crasis for *τὸ ἄτιμον*, *αὐτῶν* is possessive, and *τοῦτ᾽* is elided neuter *τοῦτο*. The added demonstrative points back to the words just quoted; *ontoso metro* becomes a song characterized by dishonour.

> 34 Poi si volgea ciascun, quand' era giunto,  
> 35 per lo suo mezzo cerchio, all' altra giostra.  

Then every one, when he had reached it, turned through his half-circle towards the other joust.

> 34 Εἶτ᾽ ἐπέστρεφ᾽ ἑκάστη διαδραμοῦσα  
> (Then each turned back, having run through)
>
> 35 Ἡμικύκλιον αὑτῆς πρὸς κροῦσιν ἄλλην.  
> (Her half-circle, toward another clash.)

- *Εἶτ᾽ ἐπέστρεφ᾽ ἑκάστη* ‘then each turned back’ again distributes the action by a feminine singular. *Εἶτ᾽* is *εἶτα*; *ἐπέστρεφ᾽* is elided *ἐπέστρεφε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἐπιστρέφω*), here intransitive for *si volgea*.
- *Διαδραμοῦσα ἡμικύκλιον αὑτῆς* ‘having run through her own half-circle’ replaces *quand’ era giunto* with an anterior participle (nom. sg. fem., second aor. act. of *διατρέχω*, stem *δραμ-*). Musurus attaches the space traversed to the running as its accusative object rather than giving the turning a separate *per*-phrase. *Αὑτῆς*, with rough breathing, is the contracted reflexive genitive *ἑαυτῆς*, rendering *suo*. *Πρὸς κροῦσιν ἄλλην* ‘toward another collision’ retains the direction of *all’altra giostra* but substitutes the impact for the joust.

> 36 Ed io che avea lo cor quasi compunto,  
> 37 dissi: "Maestro mio, or mi dimostra  
> 38 che gente è questa; e se tutti fur cherci  
> 39 questi chercuti alla sinistra nostra."  

And I, who felt my heart as it were stung, said: “My Master, now shew me what people these are; and whether all those tonsured on our left were of the clergy.”

> 36 Ἐγὼ δὲ συγκινηθεὶς ἐν τῇ καρδίᾳ  
> (And I, deeply moved in my heart,)
>
> 37 Εἶπον· “Προστάτα, νῦν δίδαξόν με, τίνες  
> (Said: "Master, now teach me who)
>
> 38 „Οὗτοί γ᾽ εἰσιν, εἴ τ᾽ ἦσαν ἱερεῖς πάντες,  
> (These indeed are, and whether all were priests)
>
> 39 „Οὓς μεσοκούρους ἐπ᾽ ἀριστερὰ βλέπω.“  
> (Whom I see tonsured on the left.")

- *Ἐγὼ δὲ συγκινηθεὶς ἐν τῇ καρδίᾳ εἶπον* ‘and I, moved in my heart, said’ retains the emphatic personal subject but replaces *che avea lo cor quasi compunto* with an aorist passive participle (nom. sg. masc. of *συγκινέω*). The person, not the heart, undergoes the movement; *ἐν* with the dative identifies its seat. Neither the qualification *quasi* nor the image of pricking is retained. *Εἶπον* is 1st sg. second aor. act. ind.
- *Προστάτα* (voc. sg. of *προστάτης*) addresses a protector or guide rather than translating *Maestro mio* as a teacher with a possessive. The teaching is in *νῦν δίδαξόν με* ‘now teach me’: *δίδαξον* is the 2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *διδάσκω*, with the additional acute before enclitic *με*. *Τίνες οὗτοί γ᾽ εἰσιν* ‘who these are’ is an indirect question with a plural interrogative predicate instead of singular *che gente*. Elided *γε* focuses the demonstrative.
- *Εἴ τ᾽ ἦσαν ἱερεῖς πάντες* coordinates a second indirect question by postpositive *τε*: *εἰ* means ‘whether’, not ‘if’ introducing a condition. *Ἦσαν* is the 3rd pl. imperfect of *εἰμί*; predicate *ἱερεῖς* ‘priests’ is narrower than *cherci*. *Οὓς μεσοκούρους ἐπ᾽ ἀριστερὰ βλέπω* ‘whom I see shorn in the middle on the left’ replaces the demonstrative phrase *questi chercuti* with a relative clause. *Οὕς* is the object of the supplied present *βλέπω*, *μεσοκούρους* its predicate adjective (acc. pl. masc.); *ἐπ᾽ ἀριστερά* is adverbial, without *nostra*.

> 40 Ed egli a me: "Tutti quanti fur guerci  
> 41 sì della mente in la vita primaia,  
> 42 che con misura nullo spendio ferci.  

And he to me: “In their first life, all were so squint-eyed in mind, that they made no expenditure in it with moderation.

> 40 Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπε· “Πάντες ὑπῆρξαν παραβλῶπες  
> (And he said: "All of them were squinting)
>
> 41 „Τὸν νοῦν τοσοῦτον ἐν τῷ προτέρῳ βίῳ,  
> (In mind so much during their former life,)
>
> 42 „Ὥστ᾽ οὐδὲν εἶχον μέτρον ἐν ταῖς δαπάναις.  
> (That they kept no measure in their spending.)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπε* ‘and he said’ uses the article pronominally with *δέ*, supplies the verb of speech (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind.), and omits *a me*. *Πάντες ὑπῆρξαν παραβλῶπες* renders *Tutti quanti fur guerci*: *ὑπῆρξαν* (3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *ὑπάρχω*) serves as copula to the nominative plural predicate *παραβλῶπες* ‘squint-eyed’.
- *Τὸν νοῦν* is an accusative of respect, ‘as regards the mind’, for *della mente*. *Τοσοῦτον* is adverbial (‘to such a degree’), answering to *sì* and anticipating the result clause. *Ἐν τῷ προτέρῳ βίῳ* uses the comparative *πρότερος* ‘former’ rather than an ordinal for *primaia*.
- *Ὥστ᾽ οὐδὲν εἶχον μέτρον ἐν ταῖς δαπάναις* ‘so that they had no measure in their expenditures’ uses elided *ὥστε* with the indicative for an actual result. *Εἶχον* is here 3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *ἔχω*. The Italian construction is inverted: *misura* becomes the object *μέτρον*, negated by agreeing *οὐδέν*, while *spendio* becomes the sphere specified by *ἐν* with the plural dative. There is no separate locative corresponding to *-ci* in *ferci*.

> 43 Assai la voce lor chiaro l' abbaia,  
> 44 quando vengono a' duo punti del cerchio,  
> 45 ove colpa contraria li dispaia.  

Most clearly do their voices bark out this, when they come to the two points of the circle, where contrary guilt divides them.

> 43 „Τόδ᾽ ὁ τούτων ὑλαγμὸς σαφῶς δεικνύει,  
> (This their barking clearly shows,)
>
> 44 „Ὅτ᾽ ἔρχοντ᾽ εἰς τὰ δύο μέρη τοῦ κύκλου,  
> (When they come to the two parts of the circle,)
>
> 45 „Ἔνθ᾽ ἀντίθετα πταίσματ᾽ αὐτοὺς χωρίζει.  
> (Where opposite faults divide them.)

- *Τόδ᾽ ὁ τούτων ὑλαγμὸς σαφῶς δεικνύει* ‘this their barking clearly shows’ fronts the object *τόδε*, corresponding to *l’* in *l’abbaia*. The action of *abbaia* becomes the subject noun *ὑλαγμός*, displacing *la voce*; *τούτων* ‘of these’ renders *lor* inside the article–noun group. The new verb *δεικνύει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *δεικνύω*) makes the evidential sense explicit. *Σαφῶς* renders *chiaro*, without separately intensifying it for *assai*.
- *Ὅτ᾽ ἔρχοντ᾽ εἰς τὰ δύο μέρη τοῦ κύκλου* follows *quando vengono* with temporal *ὅτε* and a present indicative, not an indefinite temporal clause with the subjunctive. *Ἔρχοντ᾽* is elided *ἔρχονται* (3rd pl. pres. mid. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*). *Εἰς* marks the goal; as at line 32, *μέρη* ‘parts’ stands for *punti*. Despite *δύο*, the article and noun are accusative plural, not dual.
- *Ἔνθ᾽* is elided relative adverb *ἔνθα* ‘where’. *Ἀντίθετα πταίσματ᾽* (for *πταίσματα*) makes singular *colpa contraria* plural, ‘opposed offences’, but again takes a singular verb, *χωρίζει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *χωρίζω*). *Αὐτούς* is its personal accusative object, as *li* is of *dispaia*.

> 46 Questi fur cherci, che non han coperchio  
> 47 piloso al capo, e Papi e Cardinali,  
> 48 in cui usa avarizia il suo soperchio."  

These were Priests, that have not hairy covering on their heads, and Popes and Cardinals, in whom avarice does its utmost.”

> 46 „Οὗτοι δ᾽, ὧν τὴν κεφαλὴν θρὶξ οὐ καλύπτει,  
> (And these, whose heads no hair covers,)
>
> 47 „Ἦσαν ἱερεῖς, Πάπαι καὶ Καρδινάλεις,  
> (Were priests, Popes, and Cardinals,)
>
> 48 „Ὧν ὑπέρμετρος ἦν ἡ φιλαργυρία.“  
> (Whose avarice was beyond measure.")

- *Οὗτοι δ᾽, ὧν τὴν κεφαλὴν θρὶξ οὐ καλύπτει* inserts the relative clause between subject *οὗτοι* and copula *ἦσαν*. Instead of making the persons possess a *coperchio piloso*, Greek makes *θρίξ* ‘hair’ (nom. sg., collective here) the subject of *οὐ καλύπτει* ‘does not cover’ and *τὴν κεφαλήν* its object. Relative *ὧν* ‘whose’ is a possessive genitive; the singular head is distributive.
- *Ἦσαν ἱερεῖς, Πάπαι καὶ Καρδινάλεις* gives three nominative plural predicates, with *καί* only before the last. As at line 38, *ἱερεῖς* translates *cherci* as priests; *Πάπαι* and *Καρδινάλεις* give the titles Greek plural endings.
- *Ὧν ὑπέρμετρος ἦν ἡ φιλαργυρία* ‘whose love of money was beyond measure’ changes locative *in cui* into a possessive relative genitive depending on *ἡ φιλαργυρία*. The Italian present *usa* becomes imperfect *ἦν*, in keeping with *ἦσαν*. Rather than render *il suo soperchio* as something avarice exercises, Musurus puts *ὑπέρμετρος* first as a predicate adjective (nom. sg. fem.) of *ἡ φιλαργυρία*, recalling the noun *μέτρον* of line 42.

## Lines 49–81 — Unrecognisable Souls and Fortune’s Office

> 49 Ed io : " Maestro, tra questi cotali  
> 50 dovrei io ben riconoscere alcuni,  
> 51 che furo immondi di cotesti mali."  

And I: "Master, among this set, I surely ought to recognise some that were defiled by these evils."

> 49 Κἀγώ· “Προστάτα, μεταξὺ τούτων ἔδει  
> (And I: "Master, among these it would be fitting)
>
> 50 „Εἰδέναι κἀμέ τινας, οἵπερ τοιούτοις  
> (For me also to recognize some, who with such)
>
> 51 „Ἐλαττώμασιν ἦσαν ἐῤῥυπωμένοι.“  
> (Vices were defiled.")

- *Κἀγώ* is crasis for *καὶ ἐγώ*; like *Ed io*, it introduces direct speech without a verb of saying. *Προστάτα*, vocative singular of *προστάτης*, renders *Maestro* as ‘protector’ or ‘leader’. *Μεταξὺ τούτων* uses *μεταξύ* with the genitive, ‘among these’, without a separate equivalent for *cotali*.
- *Ἔδει εἰδέναι κἀμέ τινας* is literally ‘it was to be expected that I too should know some’. *Ἔδει* is the imperfect indicative of impersonal *δεῖ*: rather than reproducing the conditional of *dovrei*, the imperfect expresses an expectation not borne out. *Εἰδέναι* is the perfect active infinitive of *οἶδα*, with present sense ‘know’, not specifically ‘recognise again’. *Κἀμέ* (*καὶ ἐμέ*) is its accusative subject, *τινας* its indefinite accusative plural object; the emphasis is ‘I too’, rather than the assurance conveyed by *ben*.
- *Οἵπερ* is the nominative masculine plural relative with strengthening *-περ*, referring back to *τινας*. *Ἦσαν ἐῤῥυπωμένοι* combines the imperfect of *εἰμί* with the nominative plural perfect passive participle of *ῥυπόω*: a periphrastic pluperfect, ‘had been soiled’, presenting the state expressed by *furo immondi* as the result of an action. The written *ἐῤῥ-* is the perfect formation of an initial *ῥ-* verb, with the consonant doubled. *Τοιούτοις ἐλαττώμασιν*, dative plural of means or cause, makes *cotesti mali* ‘faults of this kind’.

> 52 Ed egli a me: "Vani pensieri aduni:  

And he to me: "Vain thoughts combinest thou:

> 52 Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ· “Κενὴν ἐλπίδα τρέφεις·  
> (And he to me: "You nourish a vain hope;)

- *Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ* matches the elliptical *Ed egli a me*: *ὁ* has pronominal force, postpositive *δέ* marks the change of speaker, and *πρός* with the accusative indicates the person addressed. *Κενὴν ἐλπίδα τρέφεις* retains the fronted adjective and object but changes both noun and metaphor: ‘an empty hope you nourish’ for *Vani pensieri aduni*. *Τρέφεις* is second singular present active indicative of *τρέφω*.

> 53 la sconoscente vita, che i fe' sozzi,  
> 54 ad ogni conoscenza or li fa bruni.  

their undiscerning life, which made them sordid, now makes them too obscure for any recognition.

> 53 „Ὁ βεβηλώσας αὐτοὺς ἀφανὴς βίος  
> (The obscure life that sullied them)
>
> 54 „ Ἀφανεῖς κἀγνώστους νῦν καθίστησ᾽ ὧδε.  
> (Now renders them obscure and unrecognizable here.)

- *Ὁ βεβηλώσας αὐτοὺς ἀφανὴς βίος* encloses the modifiers between the article and *βίος*. The relative clause *che i' fe' sozzi* becomes an attributive aorist active participle, *βεβηλώσας* (nominative masculine singular of *βεβηλόω*), with *αὐτούς* as object: ‘the obscure life that defiled them’. The single verb replaces ‘made’ plus its predicate adjective.
- *Ἀφανής* means ‘unseen, obscure’, not ‘undiscerning’: Musurus anticipates *bruni* in his rendering of *sconoscente*. He then repeats the adjective in *ἀφανεῖς κἀγνώστους*, ‘obscure and unknown’ (*κἀγνώστους* = *καὶ ἀγνώστους*). Both are predicate accusatives plural with causative *καθίστησ᾽* (elided *καθίστησι*, third singular present active of *καθίστημι*); the object ‘them’ is understood from *αὐτούς*. Thus *ad ogni conoscenza* becomes a second adjective; *νῦν* answers to *or*, while *ὧδε* adds ‘here’.

> 55 In eterno verranno agli due cozzi;  

To all eternity they shall continue butting one another;

> 55 „ Ἐλεύσοντ᾽ ᾀεὶ πρὸς τὰς δύο συγκρούσεις·  
> (Forever shall they come to the two clashes:)

- *Ἐλεύσοντ᾽* is elided *ἐλεύσονται*, third plural future middle in form, the future of *ἔρχομαι*. With *ᾀεί*, a variant of *ἀεί*, it gives ‘they will come perpetually’, close to *In eterno verranno*. *Πρὸς τὰς δύο συγκρούσεις*, ‘to the two collisions’, preserves *agli due cozzi* more closely than the English reciprocal wording: *δύο* stands with an accusative plural, not a dual.

> 56 questi risurgeranno del sepulcro  
> 57 col pugno chiuso, e questi co' crin mozzi.  

these shall arise from their graves with closed fists; and these with hair shorn off.

> 56 „ Οἱ μὲν τὴν πυγμὴν ἀναστήσοντ᾽ ἐκ τάφου  
> (These shall rise from the tomb with fist)
>
> 57 „ Κλειστὴν ἔχοντες, οἱ δὲ ξυραίαν κόμην.  
> (Held closed, and those with shaven hair.)

- *Οἱ μέν … οἱ δέ* turns the repeated *questi … questi* into a contrast, ‘the one group … the other’. *Ἀναστήσοντ᾽* is elided *ἀναστήσονται*, third plural future middle of *ἀνίστημι*, intransitive ‘will rise’. *Ἐκ τάφου* uses the genitive of source and keeps the singular of *del sepulcro*.
- *Τὴν πυγμὴν … κλειστὴν ἔχοντες* expands *col pugno chiuso*: the present active participle *ἔχοντες* agrees with *οἱ μέν*, and *κλειστήν* is predicative, ‘having the fist closed’. In *οἱ δὲ ξυραίαν κόμην* both the finite verb and *ἔχοντες* are understood. *Ξυραίαν* ‘shaven’ agrees with collective singular *κόμην* ‘hair’, for plural *crin mozzi*.

> 58 Mal dare, e mal tener lo mondo pulcro  
> 59 ha tolto loro, e posti a questa zuffa;  
> 60 qual ella sia, parole non ci appulcro.  

Ill-giving, and ill-keeping, has deprived them of the bright world, and put them to this conflict; what a conflict it is, I adorn no words to tell.

> 58 „ Κακῶς διδόντες καὶ τηροῦντες τοῦ κόσμου  
> (By ill giving and ill keeping, of the world)
>
> 59 „ Ἐστέρηνται τοῦ καλοῦ, καὶ νῦν εἰς μάχην  
> (They are deprived—the fair one—and now in strife)
>
> 60 „ Εἰσίν, ἣν λόγοις οὐκ ἔχω διαγράψαι.  
> (They are, which I cannot depict in words.)

- Dante makes the substantival infinitives *Mal dare, e mal tener* the subject of *ha tolto* and *posti*. Musurus makes the persons the subject: *κακῶς διδόντες καὶ τηροῦντες*, ‘giving and keeping badly’, are causal present active participles, nominative masculine plural of *δίδωμι* and *τηρέω*. The one adverb *κακῶς* serves both.
- *Ἐστέρηνται* is third plural perfect middle-passive indicative of *στερέω*, passive in sense: ‘they stand deprived’. It governs a genitive of deprivation, *τοῦ κόσμου … τοῦ καλοῦ*; the repeated article makes *τοῦ καλοῦ* attributive despite the intervening verb, ‘of the fair world’ (*lo mondo pulcro*).
- *Καὶ νῦν εἰς μάχην εἰσίν* replaces *e posti a questa zuffa* with a coordinated finite clause: ‘and now they are in conflict’. *Νῦν* is supplied and the demonstrative dropped. Directional *εἰς* with the accusative sits unusually with static *εἰσίν*, retaining something of *posti a*. The direct relative *ἣν* (accusative feminine singular, referring to *μάχην*) replaces *qual ella sia*. *Λόγοις οὐκ ἔχω διαγράψαι* means ‘I cannot delineate in words’: *ἔχω* plus the aorist active infinitive of *διαγράφω* expresses ability, and *λόγοις* is instrumental rather than the object of a verb of adornment.

> 61 Or puoi, figliuol, veder la corta buffa  
> 62 de' ben, che son commessi alla Fortuna,  
> 63 per che l' umana gente si rabbuffa.  

But thou, my Son, mayest [now] see the brief mockery of the goods that are committed unto Fortune, for which the human kind contend with one another.

> 61 „ Νῦν βλέπεις, τέκνον, ὅσον ἐστὶ βραχείας  
> (Now you see, my son, how much it is of brief)
>
> 62 „ Διαρκείας τἀγαθὰ πάντα τῆς Τύχης,  
> (Duration, all the goods of Fortune,)
>
> 63 „ Δι᾽ ἅπερ τἀνθρώπινον κυκᾶται γένος.  
> (For which the human race is thrown into turmoil.)

- *Νῦν βλέπεις, τέκνον* renders *Or puoi, figliuol, veder* with a simple present indicative, ‘now you see’, and a neuter singular vocative. Instead of making *la corta buffa* the object, Musurus uses an indirect exclamation, *ὅσον ἐστὶ βραχείας διαρκείας*, ‘of how short a duration [they] are’. *Ὅσον* marks degree; *βραχείας διαρκείας* is a predicate genitive of quality. The shortness survives, the mockery does not.
- *Τἀγαθά* (*τὰ ἀγαθά*), ‘the good things’, is the neuter plural subject of singular *ἐστί*, normal Greek agreement. *Πάντα* adds ‘all’; possessive *τῆς Τύχης* replaces the whole relative *che son commessi alla Fortuna*, without expressing entrustment.
- *Δι᾽ ἅπερ* (*διά* with accusative neuter plural relative *ἅπερ*) resumes the goods: ‘on account of which’. *Τἀνθρώπινον … γένος* (crasis of *τὸ ἀνθρώπινον*) surrounds *κυκᾶται*, third singular present middle-passive of *κυκάω*. The collective singular matches *l' umana gente*, but ‘is thrown into turmoil’ replaces the reciprocal sense of *si rabbuffa*.

> 64 Chè tutto l'oro, ch' è sotto la luna,  
> 65 e che già fu, di queste anime stanche  
> 66 non poterebbe farne posar una.  

For all the gold that is beneath the moon, or ever was, could not give rest to a single one of these weary souls."

> 64 „ Πᾶς γὰρ ὁ νῦν ὑπάρχων ὑπὸ σελήνην  
> (For all that now exists beneath the moon)
>
> 65 „ Χρυσὸς καὶ πρὶν ὑπάρξας ταῖς κεκμηκυίαις  
> (Of gold, and that existed before, to the weary)
>
> 66 „ Ψυχαῖς ταῖσδ᾽ οὐκ ἂν μικρὰν ἄνεσιν δῴη.”  
> (Souls, these here, could not give even a slight respite.")

- *Πᾶς γὰρ ὁ νῦν ὑπάρχων ὑπὸ σελήνην χρυσός* postpones the noun *χρυσός* until after its modifiers. Postpositive *γάρ* renders *Chè*; the articular present active participle *ὑπάρχων* replaces *ch' è*, with *νῦν* making the present explicit. *Ὑπό* with the accusative locates the gold ‘beneath the moon’. *Καὶ πρὶν ὑπάρξας* coordinates an aorist active participle of the same verb, *ὑπάρχω*: ‘and [all that] existed formerly’, for *e che già fu*. Both participles agree with the singular *χρυσός*.
- *Ταῖς κεκμηκυίαις ψυχαῖς ταῖσδ᾽* is dative plural, the recipients of relief. *Κεκμηκυίαις* is the feminine perfect active participle of *κάμνω*, ‘having grown weary’; *ταῖσδ᾽* is elided *ταῖσδε*, from *ὅδε*, in the normal demonstrative position outside the article–noun group.
- *Οὐκ ἂν … δῴη* is a potential optative: *δῴη*, third singular aorist active optative of *δίδωμι*, with *ἄν*, renders conditional *non poterebbe*. Musurus shifts the minimum from recipient to gift: not *farne posar una*, ‘make one of them rest’, but *μικρὰν ἄνεσιν* ‘a slight respite’ as direct object, with all the souls in the dative.

> 67 " Maestro," dissi lui, "or mi di' anche:  
> 68 questa Fortuna, di che tu mi tocche,  
> 69 che è, che i ben del mondo ha sì tra branche? "  

"Master," I said to him, "now tell me also: this Fortune, of which thou hintest to me; what is she, that has the good things of the world thus within her clutches?"

> 67 Κἀγώ· “Προστάτα, νῦν λέγε μοι καὶ τοῦτο·  
> (And I: "Master, now tell me this also:)
>
> 68 „ Ἡ Τύχη τίς ἆρ᾽ ἔστιν, ἧς ποιῇ μνείαν ;  
> (Who then is Fortune, of whom you make mention?)
>
> 69 „ Καὶ πῶς ἐν χερσὶ τοῦ κόσμου τἀγάθ᾽ ἔχει ;”  
> (And how holds she in her hands the goods of the world?")

- *Κἀγώ* again introduces the speech without a verb, this time replacing explicit *dissi lui*. After *Προστάτα*, *νῦν λέγε μοι καὶ τοῦτο* uses the present active imperative singular of *λέγω*, enclitic dative *μοι* and an anticipatory object: ‘now tell me this too’. The demonstrative is attached to what is to be told, not to Fortune as in *questa Fortuna*.
- *Ἡ Τύχη τίς ἆρ᾽ ἔστιν;* gives the question its predicate at once: ‘who, then, is Fortune?’ *Τίς* is the personal interrogative; elided *ἆρα* marks the question. The relative follows rather than interrupts it: *ἧς ποιῇ μνείαν*, ‘of whom you make mention’. *Ἧς* is objective genitive with *μνείαν*; *ποιῇ* is second singular present middle indicative of *ποιέω* in the idiom *μνείαν ποιεῖσθαι*. There is no second ‘to me’ corresponding to *mi*.
- *Καὶ πῶς … ἔχει;* turns *che … ha sì* into a second direct question, ‘and how does she hold …?’ *Ἐν χερσί*, locative *ἐν* with dative plural of *χείρ*, gives ‘in [her] hands’ rather than *tra branche*. *Τοῦ κόσμου* precedes its head *τἀγάθ᾽*, the crasis *τὰ ἀγαθά* with final vowel also elided before *ἔχει*.

> 70 E quegli a me: " O creature sciocche,  
> 71 quanta ignoranza è quella che vi offende!  

And he to me: "O foolish creatures, how great is this ignorance that falls upon ye!

> 70 Ὁ δ᾽ ὑπέλαβεν· “Ὦ φρενῶν ἄμοιρ᾽ ὄντα!  
> (And he replied: "O senseless beings!)
>
> 71 „ Ὦ τῆς ἀποτυφλούσης ὑμᾶς ἀγνοίας !  
> (O the ignorance that blinds you!)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ ὑπέλαβεν* supplies the verb missing in *E quegli a me*: *ὑπέλαβεν*, third singular second aorist active indicative of *ὑπολαμβάνω*, has the conversational sense ‘took up the word, replied’. There is no expressed addressee. *Ὦ φρενῶν ἄμοιρ᾽ ὄντα* gives *creature sciocche* as ‘O beings without understanding’: elided *ἄμοιρα* governs genitive plural *φρενῶν* and agrees with vocative neuter plural *ὄντα*, the substantivised present participle of *εἰμί*.
- *Ὦ τῆς … ἀγνοίας* is an exclamatory genitive, replacing the finite exclamation *quanta ignoranza è quella*. Within the article–noun group, *ἀποτυφλούσης*, genitive feminine singular present active participle of *ἀποτυφλόω*, takes *ὑμᾶς* as direct object. The relative *che vi offende* thus becomes an attribute with a more specific verb: ‘the ignorance that blinds you’.

> 72 Or vo' che tu mia sentenza ne imbocche.  

Now I wish thee to receive my judgment of her.

> 72 „ Ἄκουε νῦν ὅ,τι σοι βούλομαι φράζειν.  
> (Listen now to what I wish to tell you.)

- *Ἄκουε νῦν* ‘listen now’ is a second singular present active imperative of *ἀκούω*. In *Or vo' che tu … ne imbocche*, wishing is the main verb and receiving its dependent action; Musurus makes listening the command and moves the wish into its object clause.
- *Ὅ,τι σοι βούλομαι φράζειν* is a free relative, ‘what I wish to tell you’. *Ὅ,τι*, neuter singular of *ὅστις*, is the object of *φράζειν* (present active infinitive of *φράζω*), and *σοι* its indirect object. *Βούλομαι* is first singular present middle indicative, with the same subject understood for the infinitive. Neither *mia sentenza* nor the reference to Fortune in *ne* is separately rendered.

> 73 Colui, lo cui saver tutto trascende,  
> 74 fece li cieli, e diè lor chi conduce,  
> 75 sì ch' ogni parte ad ogni parte splende,  
> 76 distribuendo ugualmente la luce;  

He whose wisdom is transcendent over all, made the heavens and gave them guides, so that every part shines to every part, equally distributing the light;

> 73 „ Ἐκεῖνος, ὃς σοφίᾳ πάντων προέχει,  
> (He who in wisdom surpasses all,)
>
> 74 „Ὁ τοὺς οὐρανοὺς κτίσας καὶ διευθύνων  
> (Who created the heavens and directs them)
>
> 75 „Οὕτως, ὥστε πᾶν μέρος πρὸς πᾶν προσλάμπει,  
> (So that every part shines toward every other part,)
>
> 76 „Τὸ φῶς ἐξ ἴσου τοῖς πᾶσι διανέμων,  
> (Distributing the light equally to all,)

- *Ἐκεῖνος, ὃς σοφίᾳ πάντων προέχει* changes *lo cui saver tutto trascende* from a possessive relative to a personal one: ‘he who surpasses all in wisdom’. *Ὅς* is nominative subject of *προέχει* (third singular present active of *προέχω*), *σοφίᾳ* dative of respect, *πάντων* genitive of comparison.
- *Ὁ τοὺς οὐρανοὺς κτίσας καὶ διευθύνων* is an articular participial phrase in apposition to *ἐκεῖνος*, ‘he who created the heavens and directs [them]’. Both participles are nominative masculine singular active, but *κτίσας* (*κτίζω*) is aorist, *διευθύνων* (*διευθύνω*) present. For Dante’s two finite past actions *fece … e diè lor chi conduce*, Greek gives completed creation and continuing direction by the creator himself; there is no separate ‘one who conducts’. The main verb belonging to *ἐκεῖνος* is deferred to line 77.
- *Οὕτως, ὥστε … προσλάμπει* renders *sì ch' … splende* with a consecutive clause and the indicative of actual result. *Πᾶν μέρος πρὸς πᾶν* has nominative subject and accusative after directional *πρός*, with the second *μέρος* understood: ‘every part shines towards every [part]’.
- *Τὸ φῶς ἐξ ἴσου τοῖς πᾶσι διανέμων* retains the participial construction of *distribuendo ugualmente la luce*, but the nominative masculine singular *διανέμων* (present active of *διανέμω*) explicitly links the action to *ἐκεῖνος*, not to neuter *μέρος*. Adverbial *ἐξ ἴσου* means ‘equally’; the dative *τοῖς πᾶσι* supplies ‘to all’.

> 77 similemente agli splendor mondani  
> 78 ordinò general ministra e duce,  

in like manner, for worldly splendours, he ordained a general minister and guide,

> 77 „Ὁμοίως διώρισεν ἐν τοῖς γηΐνοις  
> (Likewise ordained for earthly)
>
> 78 „Ἀξιώμασιν ὑπουργοὺς κἀρχηγέτας,  
> (Dignities ministers and guides,)

- *Ὁμοίως διώρισεν* takes up the subject of line 73: ‘likewise he appointed’. *Διώρισεν* is third singular aorist active indicative of *διορίζω*, with the augment inside the compound. *Ἐν τοῖς γηΐνοις ἀξιώμασιν* specifies a sphere, ‘in earthly dignities’, using *ἐν* with the dative rather than reproducing the relation of *agli*; *ἀξιώματα* makes *splendor mondani* honours or offices rather than splendours.
- *Ὑπουργοὺς κἀρχηγέτας* (*καὶ ἀρχηγέτας*) are accusative masculine plurals, ‘ministers and leaders’, objects of *διώρισεν*. This is a marked departure from *general ministra e duce*, two singular titles for one minister; *general* has no separate equivalent. The plural is not carried through into the following verb.

> 79 che permutasse a tempo li ben vani,  
> 80 di gente in gente, e d' uno in altro sangue,  
> 81 oltre la difension de' senni umani:  

to change betimes the vain possessions, from people to people, and from one kindred to another, beyond the hindrance of human wisdom:

> 79 „Ἵνα τὰ φροῦδ᾽ ἀγαθὰ χρόνῳ μετάγῃ  
> (So that she might in time transfer the fleeting goods)
>
> 80 „Ἐκ λαοῦ ᾽ς λαόν, ἐκ φυλῆς ἐς ἑτέραν,  
> (From people to people, from one race to another,)
>
> 81 „Ὅσην κἂν προμήθειαν βροτοὶ ποιῶνται.  
> (Whatever foresight mortals may exercise.)

- *Ἵνα … μετάγῃ* makes *che permutasse* explicitly final: ‘in order that [she] may transfer’. *Μετάγῃ* is third singular present active subjunctive of *μετάγω*; retaining the subjunctive after a past main verb is permissible in a Greek purpose clause. Its singular accords with Dante’s *ministra*, but not with Musurus’ immediately preceding plural appointees; Fortune must be understood as subject.
- *Τὰ φροῦδ᾽ ἀγαθά* has elided *φροῦδα*, neuter plural of *φροῦδος*, ‘gone, soon gone’: *li ben vani* become ‘the fleeting goods’. *Χρόνῳ*, a bare temporal dative, renders *a tempo* as ‘in time’. *Μετάγω* expresses conveyance from one holder to another rather than exchange.
- *Ἐκ λαοῦ ᾽ς λαόν, ἐκ φυλῆς ἐς ἑτέραν* balances genitives of source after *ἐκ* with accusatives of destination. *᾽ς* is the reduced form of *ἐς* (equivalent to *εἰς*); the second destination has the full *ἐς*. *Λαός* renders *gente*, while *φυλή* ‘stock, tribe’ interprets the figurative *sangue*. *Ἑτέραν* agrees with an understood *φυλήν*; no numeral reproduces *uno*.
- *Ὅσην κἂν προμήθειαν βροτοὶ ποιῶνται* replaces *oltre la difension de' senni umani* with an indefinite relative clause of concessive force: ‘however much forethought mortals may exercise’. Accusative feminine singular *ὅσην* qualifies *προμήθειαν*; *κἄν* combines *καί* and modal *ἄν*, here with *ποιῶνται*, third plural present middle subjunctive of *ποιέω*. *Βροτοί*, the poetic ‘mortals’, makes the human possessors the subject, and *προμήθειαν ποιεῖσθαι* interprets their *difension* as taking forethought rather than offering resistance.

## Lines 82–114 — Fortune’s Changes and the Wrathful in Styx

> 82 per ch' una gente impera, e l' altra langue,  
> 83 seguendo lo giudicio di costei,  
> 84 che è occulto, come in erba l' angue.  

hence one people commands, another languishes; obeying her sentence, which is hidden like the serpent in the grass.

> 82 „Ἐθνῶν γὰρ τὸ μὲν ἄρχει, τὸ δ᾽ ὑποκύπτει,  
> (For of nations, one rules and another bows down,)
>
> 83 „Κατὰ τὴν ἀπόφασιν μόνον τῆς Τύχης  
> (According to the decree alone of Fortune,)
>
> 84 „Τῆς κρυπτομένης, ὡς ὄφις ὑπὸ χόρτον.  
> (Who is hidden, like a serpent beneath the grass.)

- *Ἐθνῶν γὰρ τὸ μὲν ἄρχει, τὸ δ᾽ ὑποκύπτει* ‘for of nations the one rules, the other bows down’: the fronted partitive genitive plural *ἐθνῶν* allows Musurus to leave *ἔθνος* understood twice with the neuter singular articles. *μέν … δέ* (the latter elided before *ὑποκύπτει*) marks the contrast of *una gente … l’altra*. Postpositive *γάρ* makes the statement explanatory rather than consequential as in *per ch’*. Both verbs are present active indicatives, 3rd singular; *ὑποκύπτει* expresses submission rather than the wasting or flagging of *langue*.
- *Κατὰ τὴν ἀπόφασιν μόνον τῆς Τύχης* ‘solely in accordance with the decision of Fortune’ replaces the participial *seguendo lo giudicio di costei* with *κατά* and the accusative. *μόνον* is adverbial, not an adjective agreeing with feminine *ἀπόφασιν*; the restriction is supplied. The possessive genitive *τῆς Τύχης* names the person designated by *costei*.
- *Τῆς κρυπτομένης* is a second-attributive-position participle (gen. fem. sg., pres. middle/passive of *κρύπτω*), agreeing with *Τύχης*: ‘Fortune, who lies hidden’. Where masculine *occulto* refers back to *giudicio*, the Greek makes Fortune herself hidden. *ὡς ὄφις ὑπὸ χόρτον* has an anarthrous nominative *ὄφις* in the simile, not a genitive attracted into the participial construction; *ὑπό* with the accusative places the serpent ‘beneath the grass’ rather than simply *in erba*.

> 85 Vostro saver non ha contrasto a lei :  

Your knowledge cannot understand her:

> 85 „Ἡ σὴ σύνεσις κατ᾽ αὐτῆς οὐκ ἰσχύει·  
> (Your understanding has no power against her:)

- *Ἡ σὴ σύνεσις* ‘your understanding’ gives *Vostro saver* an abstract noun in place of the substantivized infinitive. The possessive adjective *σή* (nom. fem. sg. of *σός*) agrees with *σύνεσις* and addresses one person, narrowing the plural reference of *Vostro*.
- *κατ᾽ αὐτῆς οὐκ ἰσχύει* ‘has no force against her’ keeps the opposition in *non ha contrasto a lei*, rather than making her the object of understanding. *κατ᾽* is elided *κατά*, adversative with the genitive; *ἰσχύει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἰσχύω*) replaces the Italian verb-plus-noun construction. The negative is *οὐκ* before a smooth-breathed vowel.

> 86 ella provvede, giudica, e persegue  
> 87 suo regno, come il loro gli altri Dei.  

she provides, judges, and maintains her kingdom, as the other Gods do theirs.

> 86 „Αὕτη προνοεῖ, κρίνει καὶ διεξάγει  
> (She foresees, judges, and administers)
>
> 87 „Τὸ κράτος αὑτῆς, ὡς πᾶσ᾽ ἄλλη θεότης.  
> (Her own dominion, like every other deity.)

- *Αὕτη προνοεῖ, κρίνει καὶ διεξάγει* reproduces the sequence of three present verbs, with the demonstrative *αὕτη* ‘she, this one’ emphatically resuming the subject. *προνοεῖ* (contracted from *προνοέει*, of *προνοέω*) admits both forethought and provision; *διεξάγει* (of *διεξάγω*) takes up *persegue* in the administrative sense ‘conducts, carries on’.
- *Τὸ κράτος αὑτῆς* ‘her own dominion’ is the accusative object of *διεξάγει*; the rough breathing distinguishes the contracted reflexive genitive *αὑτῆς* (for *ἑαυτῆς*) from *αὐτῆς*. *ὡς πᾶσ᾽ ἄλλη θεότης* substitutes the distributive singular ‘as every other deity’ for *come il loro gli altri Dei*. *πᾶσ᾽* is elided feminine *πᾶσα*, agreeing with nominative *θεότης*; both the verb and the corresponding object, expressed by *il loro* in Italian, are left to be supplied.

> 88 Le sue permutazion non hanno triegue ;  

Her permutations have no truce;

> 88 „Τῶν μεταβολῶν αὐτῆς οὐκ ἔστι παῦλα·  
> (Of her changes there is no cessation;)

- *Τῶν μεταβολῶν αὐτῆς οὐκ ἔστι παῦλα* ‘of her changes there is no cessation’ turns the Italian subject *Le sue permutazion* into a genitive plural dependent on *παῦλα*; *αὐτῆς* is possessive. The singular noun *παῦλα* ‘cessation, respite’ is the postponed nominative subject of existential *ἔστι* (3rd sg. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*), replacing plural *triegue* as the object of *non hanno*.

> 89 necessità la fa esser veloce ;  
> 90 sì spesso vien chi vicenda consegue.  

necessity makes her be swift; [thus he comes] oft [who doth a] change [obtain].

> 89 „Ἀνάγκη ποιεῖ ταχεῖαν αὐτὴν εἶναι,  
> (Necessity makes her to be swift,)
>
> 90 „Ἐναλλὰξ ἄλλων συχνῶς ἀνταπαιτούντων.  
> (As others in turn continually claim their share.)

- *Ἀνάγκη ποιεῖ ταχεῖαν αὐτὴν εἶναι* closely follows *necessità la fa esser veloce*: *ποιεῖ* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ποιέω*) governs an accusative and infinitive. *αὐτήν* is the subject of *εἶναι* (pres. inf. of *εἰμί*), and the predicate adjective *ταχεῖαν* (acc. fem. sg. of *ταχύς*) agrees with it but precedes both pronoun and infinitive.
- *Ἐναλλὰξ ἄλλων συχνῶς ἀνταπαιτούντων* ‘as others often claim in their turn’ is a genitive absolute: *ἄλλων* is the subject of *ἀνταπαιτούντων* (gen. masc. pl., pres. act. participle of *ἀνταπαιτέω*, ‘claim in return’). Italian *vien chi … consegue* has a finite verb of coming and a relative subject who obtains something; the Greek has neither the coming nor an expressed object of the claim. It interprets *vicenda* adverbially in *ἐναλλάξ* ‘by turns’; *συχνῶς* gives the frequency of *spesso* without a separate degree word for *sì*.

> 91 Quest' è colei, ch' è tanto posta in croce  
> 92 pur da color, che le dovrian dar lode,  
> 93 dandole biasmo a torto e mala voce.  

This is she, who is so much reviled, even by those who ought to praise her, *when* blaming her wrongfully, and with evil words.

> 91 „Ἥδ᾽ ἐστίν, ἥπερ ἐπὶ σταυροῦ κρεμᾶται  
> (She it is who is hung upon a cross)
>
> 92 „Ὑπ᾽ ἐκείνων, οὓς ἐπαινεῖν αὐτὴν ἔδει·  
> (By those who ought to give her praise,)
>
> 93 „Καὶ δὴ κακολογοῦσιν αὐτὴν ἀδίκως·  
> (And instead speak evil of her unjustly;)

- *Ἥδ᾽ ἐστίν, ἥπερ* ‘this is she who indeed’ answers to *Quest’ è colei, ch’*. *ἥδ᾽* is elided feminine *ἥδε*; *ἥπερ* is the corresponding nominative relative *ἥ* strengthened by *-περ*. *ἐπὶ σταυροῦ κρεμᾶται* retains the concrete image of *posta in croce*: *ἐπί* with the genitive gives the place, and *κρεμᾶται* (3rd sg. pres. middle/passive of *κρεμάννυμι*) means ‘hangs, is suspended’. There is no separate equivalent of *tanto*.
- *Ὑπ᾽ ἐκείνων* (elided *ὑπό* with gen. pl.) gives the personal agent, *da color*. In *οὓς ἐπαινεῖν αὐτὴν ἔδει*, the relative *οὕς* is accusative as subject of *ἐπαινεῖν* (pres. act. inf. of *ἐπαινέω*), with *αὐτήν* its object. Impersonal *ἔδει* (imperf. ind. of *δεῖ*) expresses the unfulfilled obligation of *dovrian* without a conditional verb or an optative; *dar lode* becomes simply ‘praise’.
- *Καὶ δὴ κακολογοῦσιν αὐτὴν ἀδίκως* ‘and in fact they speak ill of her unjustly’ gives the people a new finite verb, *κακολογοῦσιν* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *κακολογέω*), instead of the Italian gerund *dandole*. *καὶ δή* emphasizes what they actually do; there is no *pur* attached to the phrase naming the agents. *biasmo … e mala voce* are gathered into the single verb, with an accusative of the person in place of the Italian dative clitic *-le*; *ἀδίκως* answers to *a torto*.

> 94 Ma ella s' è beata, e ciò non ode :  

But she is in bliss, and hears it not:

> 94 „Ἀλλ’ ἔστ’ εὐδαίμων καὶ μομφὰς οὐκ ἀκούει.  
> (But she is blessed and hears not their reproaches.)

- *Ἀλλ’ ἔστ’ εὐδαίμων* ‘but she is blessed’ has successive elisions, of *ἀλλά* and of *ἔστι* before *εὐδαίμων*. The adjective is nominative singular, of the same form for masculine and feminine; the subject *ella* remains implicit, and the pronominal element in *s’è beata* is not reproduced.
- *καὶ μομφὰς οὐκ ἀκούει* makes *ciò* specific: ‘and she does not hear reproaches’. *μομφάς* (acc. pl. of *μομφή*) is the thing heard with *ἀκούει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἀκούω*).

> 95 con l' altre prime creature lieta  
> 96 volve sua spera, e beata si gode.  

with the other Primal Creatures joyful, she wheels her sphere, and tastes her blessedness.

> 95 „Μετὰ τῶν ἄλλων πρώτων κτισμάτων εὔφρων,  
> (Joyful among the other primal creatures,)
>
> 96 „Στρέφει τὴν σφαῖραν, εὐδαίμων τ’ οὖσα χαίρει.  
> (She turns her sphere, and being blessed rejoices.)

- *Μετὰ τῶν ἄλλων πρώτων κτισμάτων εὔφρων* follows the Italian order closely. *μετά* governs the genitive of accompaniment; *κτισμάτων* is gen. neut. pl. of *κτίσμα*, a thing created. *εὔφρων* ‘joyful’ is a nominative singular predicative adjective referring to the subject of *στρέφει*, as *lieta* does to that of *volve*, not an epithet of the creatures.
- *Στρέφει τὴν σφαῖραν* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *στρέφω* with acc. sg.) gives *volve sua spera*, with the definite article rather than an explicit possessive. *εὐδαίμων τ’ οὖσα χαίρει* ‘and being blessed she rejoices’ makes *beata* into the predicate of *οὖσα* (nom. fem. sg. pres. participle of *εἰμί*). Postpositive *τ’* is elided connective *τε*; active *χαίρει* (of *χαίρω*) needs no reflexive corresponding to *si gode*.

> 97 Or discendiamo omai a maggior pieta ;  

*But* let us now descend to greater misery;

> 97 „Καταβῶμεν νῦν εἰς μείζους ἀγωνίας·  
> (Let us now descend into greater agonies;)

- *Καταβῶμεν* is the hortatory 1st pl. second-aorist active subjunctive of *καταβαίνω*, on the root-aorist stem of *κατέβην*, without the indicative augment. It gives the force of *discendiamo* as a proposal, not a statement; *νῦν* alone does duty for *Or … omai*.
- *εἰς μείζους ἀγωνίας* ‘into greater agonies’ has directional *εἰς* with the accusative, changing singular *pieta* into plural experiences of distress. *μείζους* is the contracted acc. fem. pl. form (beside *μείζονας*) of comparative *μείζων*, agreeing with *ἀγωνίας*.

> 98 già ogni stella cade, che saliva  
> 99 quando mi mossi, e il troppo star si vieta."  

already every star is falling, that was ascending when [I set out] and to stay too long is not permitted.”

> 98 „Ἤδη κλίνει πᾶς ἀστήρ, ὃς ἀνυψοῦτο  
> (Already every star sinks that was rising)
>
> 99 „Ὅτ’ ἦλθον· πλέον δ’ ἀπηγόρευται μένειν.”  
> (When I set out; and to linger longer is forbidden.")

- *Ἤδη κλίνει πᾶς ἀστήρ* ‘already every star inclines’ retains *già* and the distributive singular *ogni stella* but puts the verb before its subject. *κλίνει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *κλίνω*) is used intransitively rather than with its common transitive sense ‘make lean’. *ὃς ἀνυψοῦτο* has a masculine nominative relative agreeing with *ἀστήρ* and an imperfect middle/passive, 3rd singular of *ἀνυψόω*: literally ‘which was being raised’, for intransitive *che saliva*.
- *Ὅτ’ ἦλθον* has elided temporal *ὅτε* and the 1st sg. second-aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι* (augmented stem *ἦλθ-*), ‘when I came/went’; unlike *mi mossi*, the verb does not itself specify the beginning of movement.
- *πλέον δ’ ἀπηγόρευται μένειν* ‘and to remain longer has been forbidden’: *πλέον* is the neuter comparative used adverbially with *μένειν* (pres. act. inf. of *μένω*), giving duration rather than a literal adjective for *troppo*. Elided *δέ* links the clauses; *ἀπηγόρευται* (3rd sg. perf. middle/passive ind. of *ἀπαγορεύω*) is impersonal and passive, giving a prohibition now in force for present *si vieta*. The infinitive supplies what is forbidden, without an article corresponding to *il*.

> 100 Noi ricidemmo il cerchio all' altra riva  
> 101 sopra una fonte, che bolle, e riversa  
> 102 per un fossato che da lei diriva.  

We crossed the circle, to the other bank, near a fount, that boils and pours down through a cleft, which it has formed.

> 100 Διέβημεν εἰς κύκλου τὴν ἄλλην ὄχθην  
> (We crossed over to the other bank of the circle)
>
> 101 Ἐπὶ ζεούσης πηγῆς, καταῤῥεούσης  
> (Above a boiling spring, flowing down)
>
> 102 Εἰς βόθρον ἐκ τῶν ῥοῶν διορυχθέντα·  
> (Into a trench hollowed out by its streams;)

- *Διέβημεν* (1st pl. root-aorist act. ind. of *διαβαίνω*, augmented after the preverb) incorporates the subject *Noi*. Instead of making *il cerchio* the object crossed, *εἰς κύκλου τὴν ἄλλην ὄχθην* makes *κύκλου* a dependent genitive, ‘to the circle’s other bank’. Its placement between *εἰς* and the articular accusative governed by the preposition is a conspicuous separation.
- *Ἐπὶ ζεούσης πηγῆς, καταῤῥεούσης* replaces *sopra una fonte, che bolle, e riversa* with local *ἐπί* and a genitive noun qualified by two present active participles (gen. fem. sg. of *ζέω* and *καταρρέω*). The first precedes *πηγῆς*, the second follows it and takes the directional complement in the next line. The spelling *ῤῥ* marks doubled rho with smooth and rough breathings.
- *Εἰς βόθρον ἐκ τῶν ῥοῶν διορυχθέντα* ‘into a trench dug out by the streams’: *εἰς* gives the destination rather than the course denoted by *per*. *διορυχθέντα* (acc. masc. sg., aor. pass. participle of *διορύσσω*) agrees with *βόθρον*. Where *che da lei diriva* could describe the ditch as leading from the spring, Musurus interprets the relation as one of formation: *ἐκ* with gen. pl. *ῥοῶν* gives the flows as the cause of the excavation.

> 103 L' acqua era buia assai vie che persa ;  

The water was darker far than perse;

> 103 Τὸ δ’ ὕδωρ ἦν θολερόν, μᾶλλον δὲ μέλαν.  
> (And the water was turbid, or rather black.)

- *Τὸ δ’ ὕδωρ ἦν θολερόν* ‘and the water was turbid’ adds connective *δέ* (elided); *ἦν* is 3rd sg. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*. *θολερόν* and the following *μέλαν* (neut. sg. of *μέλας*) are both predicate nominatives agreeing with *ὕδωρ*.
- *μᾶλλον δὲ μέλαν* ‘or rather black’ turns the comparison *buia assai vie che persa* into a correction of the first adjective by a second. Comparative adverb *μᾶλλον* works with postpositive *δέ*; there is no expressed standard of comparison or distinct equivalent for the colour term *persa*.

> 104 e noi, in compagnia dell' onde bige,  
> 105 entrammo giù per una via diversa.  

and we, accompanying the dusky waves, entered down by a strange path.

> 104 Ἡμεῖς δ’ ἐν μέσῳ βορβορωδῶν κυμάτων  
> (And we, amidst the miry waves,)
>
> 105 Ἤλθομεν κάτω δι’ ὁδοῦ τινος ἄλλης.  
> (Came down by another way.)

- *Ἡμεῖς δ’ ἐν μέσῳ βορβορωδῶν κυμάτων* keeps the explicit subject *noi* and connective *δέ* but makes *in compagnia* spatial: ‘in the midst of’. *ἐν* takes dative *μέσῳ*, used substantivally, with dependent gen. pl. *κυμάτων* (of *κῦμα*). *βορβορωδῶν* (gen. neut. pl. of *βορβορώδης*) gives the waves a muddy consistency rather than the grey colour of *bige*.
- *Ἤλθομεν κάτω* ‘we went down’ combines the 1st pl. second-aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι* with an adverb, matching *entrammo giù* without a specific verb of entry. *δι’ ὁδοῦ τινος ἄλλης* has elided *διά* with the genitive of the route traversed; enclitic indefinite *τινος* qualifies feminine *ὁδοῦ*, and *ἄλλης* interprets *diversa* simply as ‘other’.

> 106 Una palude fa, che ha nome Stige,  
> 107 questo tristo ruscel, quando è disceso  
> 108 al piè delle maligne piagge grige.  

This dreary streamlet makes a Marsh, that is named Styx, when it has descended to the foot of the grey malignant shores.

> 106 Ἕλος τὸ λεγόμενον Στὺξ σχηματίζει  
> (A marsh called Styx is formed by)
>
> 107 Ὁ λυπρὸς οὗτος ῥύαξ, ὅταν κατέλθῃ  
> (This dismal stream, when it descends)
>
> 108 Εἰς τῶν λυμωδῶν κἀμαυρῶν ἀκτῶν πόδας.  
> (To the foot of the malignant and dark banks.)

- *Ἕλος τὸ λεγόμενον Στὺξ σχηματίζει* retains the Italian object-first order of *Una palude fa*. Neuter *ἕλος* is accusative, not the subject of *σχηματίζει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *σχηματίζω*, ‘forms’). The postposed articular qualifier *τὸ λεγόμενον* (acc. neut. sg., pres. pass. participle of *λέγω*) replaces *che ha nome*; the name *Στύξ* stands in its naming or citation form, rather than being attracted into the accusative.
- *Ὁ λυπρὸς οὗτος ῥύαξ* is the delayed nominative subject. The demonstrative is outside the article–adjective group, with the noun delayed after it: ‘this dismal stream’. *λυπρός* renders *tristo*; *ῥύαξ* is not formally diminutive like *ruscel*. *ὅταν κατέλθῃ* replaces the Italian perfect indicative *quando è disceso* with *ὅταν* (*ὅτε* + *ἄν*) and the 3rd sg. second-aorist active subjunctive of *κατέρχομαι*: an indefinite temporal clause, ‘when it comes down’, viewing the descent as a whole.
- *Εἰς τῶν λυμωδῶν κἀμαυρῶν ἀκτῶν πόδας* puts the whole dependent genitive between *εἰς* and its accusative *πόδας* (pl. of *πούς*), pluralizing *al piè*. *λυμωδῶν* ‘baneful’ and *ἀμαυρῶν* ‘dim, dark’ both modify gen. fem. pl. *ἀκτῶν* ‘banks’; the Greek brings the two epithets together where *maligne piagge grige* separates them. *κἀμαυρῶν* shows crasis of *καί* and *ἀμαυρῶν*, with the coronis over the fused vowel.

> 109 Ed io, che di mirar mi stava inteso,  
> 110 vidi genti fangose in quel pantano,  
> 111 ignude tutte, e con sembiante offeso.  

And I, who stood intent on looking, saw muddy people in that bog, all naked and with a look of anger.

> 109 Ἐγὼ δ’ ἐκεῖ θεωρῶν ἀτενῶς εἶδον  
> (And I, gazing intently there, saw)
>
> 110 Ἐν τῷ βορβόρῳ ’κείνῳ σκιὰς ἐμπήλους,  
> (In that mire mud-covered shades,)
>
> 111 Παγγύμνους καὶ τὴν ὄψιν ἠγριωμένας.  
> (All naked and savage of aspect.)

- *Ἐγὼ δ’ ἐκεῖ θεωρῶν ἀτενῶς εἶδον* ‘and I, looking intently there, saw’ replaces *che di mirar mi stava inteso* with a circumstantial present active participle *θεωρῶν* (nom. masc. sg. of *θεωρέω*) and a manner adverb *ἀτενῶς*. The looking is simultaneous with *εἶδον* (1st sg. second-aorist act. ind. serving *ὁράω*), brought forward from line 110. Locative *ἐκεῖ* is supplied.
- *Ἐν τῷ βορβόρῳ ’κείνῳ* ‘in that mire’ precedes the object instead of following it. *’κείνῳ* shows prodelision, loss of the initial vowel of *ἐκείνῳ* after the preceding vowel; the demonstrative stands after the articular noun and agrees in the dative singular. *σκιὰς ἐμπήλους* ‘mud-covered shades’ substitutes *σκιάς* (acc. fem. pl. of *σκιά*) for the less specific *genti*; *ἐμπήλους* (of the two-termination adjective *ἔμπηλος*) is feminine by agreement.
- *Παγγύμνους* ‘wholly naked’ incorporates the force of *tutte* in an intensive compound (with *γγ* at the junction), shifting from ‘all of them’ to the completeness of their nakedness. *τὴν ὄψιν ἠγριωμένας* ‘grown fierce in aspect’ replaces *con sembiante offeso* with an accusative of respect and a participle. *ἠγριωμένας* (acc. fem. pl., perf. middle/passive participle of *ἀγριόω*) agrees with *σκιάς*, not *ὄψιν*; the perfect presents the resulting expression.

> 112 Questi si percotean non pur con mano,  
> 113 ma con la testa, e col petto, e co' piedi,  
> 114 troncandosi coi denti a brano a brano.  

They were smiting each other, not with hands only, but with head, and with chest, and with feet; *maiming* one another with their teeth, piece by piece.

> 112 Αἵδ’ ἔτυπτον οὐ μόνον χερσὶν ἀλλήλας,  
> (These struck one another not only with their hands,)
>
> 113 Ἀλλὰ κεφαλῇ καὶ ποσί τε καὶ στήθει,  
> (But with head and with feet and with breast,)
>
> 114 Ὀδοῦσί τ᾽ ἀπέτεμνον μεληδὸν σάρκας.  
> (And with their teeth tore off their flesh piecemeal.)

- *Αἵδ’ ἔτυπτον* ‘these were striking’ has elided *αἵδε* (nom. fem. pl. of *ὅδε*), feminine after *σκιάς* rather than masculine like *Questi*. *ἔτυπτον* (3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *τύπτω*) retains the continuing action of *percotean*; *ἀλλήλας* (acc. fem. pl. reciprocal pronoun) makes explicit the reciprocal force of *si*.
- *οὐ μόνον χερσὶν … ἀλλὰ κεφαλῇ καὶ ποσί τε καὶ στήθει* answers to *non pur … ma* with *οὐ μόνον … ἀλλά*. Bare instrumental datives replace repeated *con*: *χερσίν* (pl. of *χείρ*) for singular *mano*, *κεφαλῇ* (sg. of *κεφαλή*), *ποσί* (pl. of *πούς*), and *στήθει* (sg. of *στῆθος*). Feet precede breast, reversing the last two Italian terms; postpositive *τε* in *ποσί τε καὶ στήθει* links those two within the longer coordination.
- *Ὀδοῦσί τ᾽ ἀπέτεμνον μεληδὸν σάρκας* ‘and with teeth they cut off flesh piecemeal’ changes gerund *troncandosi* into a second finite imperfect, *ἀπέτεμνον* (3rd pl. act. ind. of *ἀποτέμνω*, augmented after the preverb), coordinated by elided *τε*. *ὀδοῦσι* (dat. pl. of *ὀδούς*) continues the instrumental series; its additional final accent is occasioned by the following enclitic. The reciprocal is not repeated: *σάρκας* (acc. pl. of *σάρξ*) supplies the concrete object instead. Distributive adverb *μεληδόν* ‘limb by limb’ compresses the repeated *a brano a brano* into one word.

## Lines 115–130 — The Wrathful and the Sullen in the Mire

> 115 Lo buon maestro disse: “ Figlio, or vedi  
> 116 l' anime di color cui vinse l' ira;  

The kind Master said: “Son, now see the souls of those whom anger overcame;

> 115 Ὁ δ᾽ Ἡγήτωρ εἶπέ μοι· “Νῦν, τέκνον, εἶδες  
> (And the Guide said to me: "Now, my son, you see)
>
> 116 „ Τὰς ψυχὰς τῶν ὑπ᾽ ὀργῆς κυριευθέντων.  
> (The souls of those mastered by wrath.)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ Ἡγήτωρ εἶπέ μοι* ‘and the Guide said to me’ renders *Lo buon maestro disse*. The agent noun *ἡγήτωρ* (nom. sg.) names the speaker by his function as guide rather than teacher; *buon* has no counterpart. Musurus adds connective *δέ* (postpositive, elided before *Ἡγήτωρ*) and the recipient *μοι* (enclitic dat. sg. of the first-person pronoun). The enclitic accounts for the additional accent on *εἶπέ*, third-person singular second aorist active indicative of *λέγω*.
- *Νῦν, τέκνον, εἶδες* moves *or* before the vocative *τέκνον* ‘child’, which does not specify sex as *Figlio* does. Where *vedi* admits an imperative or a present-indicative reading, *εἶδες* is neither: it is the second-person singular second aorist active indicative of *ὁράω*, ‘you saw’ or, with *νῦν*, ‘you have now seen’.
- *Τὰς ψυχὰς τῶν ὑπ᾽ ὀργῆς κυριευθέντων* keeps *l’anime* as the accusative object but recasts *di color cui vinse l’ira*. The article and participle *τῶν … κυριευθέντων* (gen. masc. pl., aor. pass. of *κυριεύω*) together mean ‘of those who were mastered’; they depend on *ψυχάς*. The subject of the Italian active verb becomes the agent of the Greek passive, *ὑπ᾽ ὀργῆς* (*ὑπό* elided, with the genitive).

> 117 ed anche vo' che tu per certo credi,  

and also I would have thee to believe for certain,

> 117 „ Τοῦτο δὲ σ᾽ ὡς βέβαιον βούλομ᾽ εἰδέναι,  
> (And this too I would have you know for certain,)

- *Τοῦτο δὲ σ᾽ ὡς βέβαιον βούλομ᾽ εἰδέναι* ‘and this I wish you to know as certain’ puts anticipatory *τοῦτο* first: it is the object of *εἰδέναι*, and the following *ὅτι* clause supplies its content. Postpositive *δέ* continues the speech without separately expressing *anche*.
- Instead of *vo’ che tu … credi*, Greek uses *βούλομ᾽* (first-person singular present middle indicative of *βούλομαι*, with final *-αι* elided) and an accusative subject *σ᾽* (elided *σε*) with an infinitive. *Εἰδέναι* is the perfect active infinitive of *οἶδα*, present in sense: ‘know’, not merely ‘believe’. *Ὡς βέβαιον* follows *per certo* closely, but *βέβαιον* is a neuter singular predicate adjective agreeing with *τοῦτο*: ‘as a certain thing’.

> 118 che sotto l' acqua ha gente che sospira,  
> 119 e fanno pullular quest' acqua al summo,  
> 120 come l' occhio ti dice u' che s' aggira.  

that there are people underneath the water, who sob, and make it bubble at the surface; as thy eye may tell thee, whichever way it turns.

> 118 „ Ὅτι πληθὺς ὧδ᾽ ὑπόβρυχα στενάζει,  
> (That a multitude here sighs beneath the water,)
>
> 119 „ Τὰς ἐπιπολῆς πομφόλυγας ποιοῦσα,  
> (Making the bubbles on the surface,)
>
> 120 „ Ὡς ὁθένποθεν στρεφόμενος νῦν βλέπεις.  
> (As wherever you turn you now behold.)

- *Ὅτι πληθὺς ὧδ᾽ ὑπόβρυχα στενάζει* replaces existential *ha gente* and relative *che sospira* with a single subject and verb: ‘that a multitude sighs here under water’. *Πληθύς* is nominative feminine singular, hence singular *στενάζει* (pres. act. ind. of *στενάζω*). *Ὧδ᾽* is elided *ὧδε* ‘here’; *ὑπόβρυχα* is adverbial, doing the work of the whole phrase *sotto l’acqua*.
- *Τὰς ἐπιπολῆς πομφόλυγας ποιοῦσα* ‘making the bubbles on the surface’ subordinates *e fanno* as a simultaneous present active participle of *ποιέω*. Its nominative feminine singular follows the grammatical number of *πληθύς*, whereas *fanno* follows the sense of collective *gente*. Instead of a causative with infinitive, *fanno pullular quest’acqua*, Greek makes the bubbles themselves the accusative object; the locative adverb *ἐπιπολῆς*, between article and noun, renders *al summo*. There is no second mention of the water.
- *Ὡς ὁθένποθεν στρεφόμενος νῦν βλέπεις* ‘as, turning this way or that, you now see’ makes the addressee, not *l’occhio*, the subject. *Βλέπεις* (second-person singular present active indicative of *βλέπω*) replaces the eye’s figurative *ti dice*, and *νῦν* is supplied. *Στρεφόμενος* (nom. masc. sg., pres. mid./pass. participle of *στρέφω*) has intransitive sense and agrees with the unexpressed ‘you’. The indefinite local adverb *ὁθένποθεν*, literally ‘from some quarter or other’, conveys the varying direction of *u’ che s’aggira* without a separate relative clause.

> 121 Fitti nel limo dicon: ‘Tristi fummo  
> 122 nell' aer dolce che dal sol s' allegra,  
> 123 portando dentro accidioso fummo;  

Fixed in the slime, they say: ‘Sullen were we in the sweet air, that is gladdened by the Sun, carrying lazy smoke within our hearts;

> 121 „ Πεπηγότες ἐν πηλῷ κράζουσιν· ‘“Ἦμεν  
> (Fixed in the mud they cry: 'We were)
>
> 122 ‚ „ Ἐν αἰθέρι τάλανες ἡλιοχάρμῳ,  
> (Wretches in the air that rejoices in the sun,)
>
> 123 ‚ „ Καπνὸν ἔνδοθι φέροντες ἀκηδίας·  
> (Bearing within us the smoke of sullenness;)

- *Πεπηγότες ἐν πηλῷ* closely matches *Fitti nel limo*. *Πεπηγότες* (nom. masc. pl., second perfect active participle of *πήγνυμι*) is active in form but intransitive and stative in sense: ‘stuck fast’. *Ἐν* takes the dative of place. The masculine plural now treats the members of *πληθύς* individually, as does *κράζουσιν* (third-person plural present active indicative of *κράζω*): ‘they cry out’, stronger than *dicon*.
- *Ἦμεν ἐν αἰθέρι τάλανες ἡλιοχάρμῳ* separates both the noun from its adjective and the copula from its predicate. Italian puts *Tristi* first; Greek opens with *ἦμεν* (first-person plural imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*), then inserts *ἐν αἰθέρι* before predicate *τάλανες* (nom. pl. of *τάλας*), ‘wretched’ rather than specifically ‘sullen’. The poetic compound *ἡλιοχάρμῳ* ‘sun-gladdened’ agrees with *αἰθέρι* (dat. sg. of *αἰθήρ*) and compresses *che dal sol s’allegra* into an epithet. *Dolce* is not separately rendered.
- *Καπνὸν ἔνδοθι φέροντες ἀκηδίας* retains the participial construction of *portando dentro*, with *φέροντες* (pres. act. participle of *φέρω*, nom. masc. pl.) referring to the speakers of *ἦμεν*. *Ἔνδοθι* simply means ‘within’; as in the Italian, no noun for ‘hearts’ is expressed. The object *καπνόν* ‘smoke’ comes first, its dependent genitive *ἀκηδίας* (from *ἀκηδία*, ‘listlessness’) last: the quality expressed by the adjective in *accidioso fummo* becomes ‘smoke of listlessness’.

> 124 or ci attristiam nella belletta negra.'  

now lie we sullen here in the black mire.’

> 124 ‚ „ Νῦν δὲ πάσχομεν ἐν μέλανι βορβόρῳ.”’  
> (But now we suffer in the black mire.')

- *Νῦν δὲ πάσχομεν* ‘but now we suffer’ marks the contrast with *ἦμεν* by postpositive *δέ*. *Πάσχομεν* (first-person plural present active indicative of *πάσχω*) is nonreflexive and less specific than *ci attristiam*; it does not reproduce the verbal echo of *Tristi*. *Ἐν μέλανι βορβόρῳ* closely renders *nella belletta negra*, with the adjective before the noun: *μέλανι* (dat. masc. sg. of *μέλας*) agrees with *βορβόρῳ* after locative *ἐν*.

> 125 Quest' inno si gorgoglian nella strozza,  
> 126 chè dir nol posson con parola integra.  

This hymn they gurgle in their throats, for they cannot speak it in full words.”

> 125 „ Ἐπῳδὴν τήνδε κορκορυγοῦσ᾽ ἐν βρόγχοις,  
> (This chant they gurgle in their throats,)
>
> 126 „ Οὐ δυνάμενοι λέξεις προφέρειν ὅλας.”  
> (Unable to utter whole words.")

- *Ἐπῳδὴν τήνδε* ‘this chant’ retains the fronted object of *Quest’inno*, but uses *ἐπῳδή*, ‘chant’ or ‘incantation’, rather than ‘hymn’. *Τήνδε* (acc. fem. sg. of *ὅδε*) follows its noun. The imitative *κορκορυγοῦσ᾽* (elided *κορκορυγοῦσι*, third-person plural present active indicative of *κορκορυγέω*) is used transitively, ‘gurgle out’, on the model of *gorgoglian*. *Ἐν βρόγχοις* is locative, ‘in [their] throats’: the plural distributes the throats among the speakers, in place of Italian singular *nella strozza*, without a separate equivalent for *si*.
- *Οὐ δυνάμενοι* ‘being unable’ turns causal *chè … non posson* into a circumstantial participle (nom. masc. pl., pres. mid. of *δύναμαι*). *Οὐ* negates a stated fact; the causal relation is inferred without a conjunction. *Λέξεις προφέρειν ὅλας* makes ‘whole words’ the direct object of *προφέρειν* (pres. act. inf. of *προφέρω*, complement of *δυνάμενοι*). *Ὅλας* agrees across the infinitive with accusative feminine plural *λέξεις*. Thus *con parola integra*, a singular phrase of manner, becomes a plural object, and the pronoun in *dir nol posson* is not retained.

> 127 Così girammo della lorda pozza  
> 128 grand' arco, tra la ripa secca e il mezzo,  
> 129 con gli occhi volti a chi del fango ingozza;  

Thus, between the dry bank and the putrid fen, we compassed a large arc of that loathly slough, with eyes turned towards those that swallow of its filth;

> 127 Οὕτως οὖν διήλθομεν ἕλους δυσώδους  
> (Thus then we traversed of the foul marsh)
>
> 128 Τὴν μέσον τούτου καὶ τῆς ὄχθης ἁψῖδα,  
> (The arc between it and the bank,)
>
> 129 Ἀτενίζοντες πρὸς τοὺς βορβοροφάγους·  
> (Gazing intently at those who swallow the mire;)

- *Οὕτως οὖν διήλθομεν* ‘thus, then, we traversed’ renders *Così girammo* with a resumptive *οὖν* and a verb of passage rather than turning. *Διήλθομεν* is first-person plural second aorist active indicative of *διέρχομαι*, with the augment internal to the compound. Its accusative object, like the object of *girammo*, is delayed to the next line; *ἕλους δυσώδους* ‘of a foul-smelling marsh’ comes first, in the position of *della lorda pozza*. Both words are genitive singular (*ἕλος*, neuter, and *δυσώδης*); the adjective specifies smell rather than dirt.
- *Τὴν μέσον τούτου καὶ τῆς ὄχθης ἁψῖδα* ‘the arc between it and the bank’ encloses the specification of place between the article and *ἁψῖδα* (acc. sg. of *ἁψίς*, ‘arc’). *Μέσον* is adverbial, used prepositionally with the two genitives; it is not an adjective agreeing with feminine *ἁψῖδα*. Neuter *τούτου* refers back to *ἕλος*. In rendering *tra la ripa secca e il mezzo*, Greek names the marsh first and the bank second, without separately naming the middle as a landmark. Neither *grand’* nor *secca* has an equivalent.
- *Ἀτενίζοντες πρός* ‘gazing intently towards’ replaces *con gli occhi volti a* with a simultaneous present active participle (nom. masc. pl. of *ἀτενίζω*) agreeing with the subject of *διήλθομεν*. No noun for the eyes is needed. After *πρός*, accusative plural *τοὺς βορβοροφάγους* ‘the mire-eaters’ compresses *chi del fango ingozza* into an articular compound, reusing the stem of *βόρβορος* from line 124.

> 130 Venimmo al piè d' una torre al dassezzo.  

we came to the foot of a tower at last.

> 130 Ἤλθομεν δ᾽ ἐπὶ τέλους εἰς πόδας πύργου.  
> (And at length we came to the foot of a tower.)

- *Ἤλθομεν δ᾽ ἐπὶ τέλους* ‘and at last we came’ adds connective *δέ*, elided before *ἐπί*. *Ἤλθομεν* (first-person plural second aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι*) answers to *Venimmo* and repeats the simple verbal stem of *διήλθομεν*. *Ἐπὶ τέλους*, *ἐπί* with the genitive singular of *τέλος* in a temporal phrase, brings *al dassezzo* forward from the end.
- *Εἰς πόδας πύργου* marks the goal with *εἰς* and the accusative. Greek retains the bodily image of *al piè d’una torre* but pluralizes it: *πόδας* ‘feet’ (acc. pl. of *πούς*), with dependent *πύργου* (gen. sg. of *πύργος*). The anarthrous genitive represents Italian *d’una torre* without a separate indefinite determiner.

## Summary — Close Parallels and Greek Recastings

Musurus often keeps the order of Dante’s thought while changing which words carry it. The paired *ὡς … οὕτω* ‘as … so’ preserves the frame of the Italian similes, and *τόσα … ὅσα* ‘so many … as’ closely follows *tante … quante*. Particles can sharpen or alter the connection: *οἱ μὲν … οἱ δέ* ‘the one group … the other’ makes a contrast explicit where Italian repeats *questi*, while explanatory *γάρ* ‘for’ at line 82 reverses the direction of inference in *per ch’* ‘hence’. Musurus also follows Dante in delaying a subject or object across a line ending, but Greek inflection lets him separate words that Italian keeps together: in *βυθὸν … τέταρτον* ‘a fourth depth’, noun and ordinal agree across the intervening verb.

The participle is Musurus’ most versatile means of recasting syntax. It can preserve an Italian gerund, as *κυλιούσας* ‘rolling’ does *voltando*, while its feminine plural ending identifies the souls, not the observer, as the ones rolling. Elsewhere *στραφείς* ‘having turned’ subordinates the finite *si rivolse* to the following verb of speech, and the genitive absolute *τῶν ἱστῶν ἐαγέντων* ‘when the masts were broken’ replaces a finite temporal clause. Case alone often carries a relation expressed by an Italian preposition: *τὸν νοῦν* ‘as regards the mind’ renders *della mente* by an accusative of respect, and bare instrumental datives replace the repeated *con* in the catalogue of blows. Changes of tense can be equally pointed: *πέπτωκε* ‘has fallen’ gives the resulting state where *cadde* narrates the fall.

Some departures are matters of interpretation rather than grammatical convenience. Musurus hears in *Pape* the Greek exclamation *παπαί*, but leaves *Ἀλέππε* unexplained. He renders *superbo strupo* as *ἀλαζόνος βίας* ‘of arrogant violence’, and chooses *συγκρούεσθαι* ‘to collide’ for *riddi*, giving the impact rather than the dance. In the passage on Fortune, agreement reveals a less conspicuous shift: *τῆς κρυπτομένης* ‘who lies hidden’ qualifies Fortune herself, whereas masculine *occulto* qualifies her judgment. Near the close, lexical compression is especially effective: *ἡλιοχάρμῳ* ‘sun-gladdened’ gathers a relative clause into an epithet, and *βορβοροφάγους* ‘mire-eaters’ turns an action into a name.
