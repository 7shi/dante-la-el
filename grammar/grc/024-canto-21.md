# Inferno 21

## Lines 1–18 — From Bridge to Boiling Pitch

> 1 Così di ponte in ponte altro parlando,  
> 2 che la mia Commedìa cantar non cura,  
> 3 venimmo, e tenevamo il colmo, quando  

THus from bridge to bridge we came, with other talk which my Comedy cares not to recite ; and held the summit, when

> 1 Ἀπὸ γεφύρας εἰς γέφυραν ἰόντες,  
> (Going from bridge to bridge,)
>
> 2 Ἄλλα λέγοντες, ἃ τῇ ᾽μῇ κωμῳδίᾳ  
> (Speaking of other things, of which my Comedy)
>
> 3 Οὐ μέλει ψάλλειν, ἀνέβημεν τὴν ἄκραν.  
> (Cares not to sing, we ascended the summit.)

- *Ἀπὸ γεφύρας εἰς γέφυραν* ‘from a bridge to a bridge’ expands *di ponte in ponte* into two directional phrases: *ἀπό* takes the genitive of departure, *εἰς* the accusative of destination. Both nouns are anarthrous. There is no separate equivalent of the opening *Così*.
- *ἰόντες* ‘going’ (nom. masc. pl., pres. act. participle of *εἶμι*) takes up the motion of *venimmo*, but makes it attendant on the main verb. Parallel *ἄλλα λέγοντες* ‘saying other things’ closely follows *altro parlando*: *ἄλλα* is the substantival neuter accusative plural of *ἄλλος*, the object of *λέγοντες* (from *λέγω*). Both participles agree with the implicit ‘we’.
- *ἃ τῇ ᾽μῇ κωμῳδίᾳ οὐ μέλει ψάλλειν* ‘which it is no concern to my Comedy to sing’ retains *che ... cantar non cura* as a relative clause but makes the construction impersonal. *ἅ* is neuter accusative plural, agreeing in gender and number with *ἄλλα* and serving as object of *ψάλλειν* (pres. act. infinitive of *ψάλλω*). With *μέλει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *μέλω*), the infinitive expresses the activity in question, and the Italian subject *la mia Commedìa* becomes the dative of the party concerned. *τῇ ᾽μῇ* shows prodelision of the initial vowel of *ἐμῇ*, the possessive agreeing with *κωμῳδίᾳ*.
- *ἀνέβημεν τὴν ἄκραν* ‘we ascended the summit’ replaces the arrival and continuing position of *venimmo, e tenevamo il colmo* with one completed ascent. *ἀνέβημεν* is the 1st pl. root aor. act. ind. of *ἀναβαίνω*, with augment after the preverb; *τὴν ἄκραν* is the accusative of the height ascended. Musurus ends the sentence here instead of carrying *quando* forward as a conjunction.

> 4 ristemmo per veder l' altra fessura  
> 5 di Malebolge, e gli altri pianti vani;  

we stood still to see the other cleft of Malebolge and [the] other vain lamentings;

> 4 Ἐνθάδ᾽ ἔστημεν ὀψόμενοι τοὺς ἄλλους  
> (Here we stood to look upon the other)
>
> 5 Κλαυθμοὺς καὶ τἄλλο χάσμα τοῦ Κακοβόθρου,  
> (Lamentations and the next chasm of Evil-pit,)

- *Ἐνθάδ᾽ ἔστημεν* ‘here we stopped’ begins an independent clause: the local adverb *ἐνθάδε*, with final *-ε* elided, takes the place of the temporal link. *ἔστημεν* (1st pl. intransitive root aor. act. ind. of *ἵστημι*) renders *ristemmo* as coming to a stand, not causing something to stand.
- *ὀψόμενοι* ‘in order to see’ is a future middle participle of *ὁράω*, nominative masculine plural with the subject of *ἔστημεν*. The future participle of purpose gives the force of *per veder*; the middle form has active sense.
- *τοὺς ἄλλους κλαυθμοὺς καὶ τἄλλο χάσμα* reverses the two accusative objects, putting *gli altri pianti* before *l’ altra fessura* and omitting *vani*. *τἄλλο* is crasis for *τὸ ἄλλο*, neuter accusative singular with *χάσμα*. *τοῦ Κακοβόθρου* is a dependent genitive translating *di Malebolge* by a compound singular, ‘of the evil pit’.

> 6 e vidila mirabilmente oscura.  

and I found it marvellously dark.

> 6 Ὅπερ ὑπερβολικῶς σκοτεινὸν εἶδον.  
> (Which I beheld exceedingly dark.)

- *Ὅπερ ... εἶδον* ‘which indeed I saw’ changes *e vidila*, coordination with an enclitic object pronoun, into a relative clause. *ὅπερ* is neuter accusative singular referring to *χάσμα*, with strengthening *-περ*; *εἶδον* is here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*, preserving the Italian change of person and number.
- *ὑπερβολικῶς σκοτεινόν* ‘exceedingly dark’ retains the adverb and predicate adjective of *mirabilmente oscura*, but expresses degree rather than wonder. *σκοτεινόν* agrees with the relative object and is consequently neuter, not feminine like *oscura*.

> 7 Quale nell' arzanà de' Viniziani  
> 8 bolle l' inverno la tenace pece  
> 9 a rimpalmar li lor legni non sani,  

As in the arsenal of the Venetians boils the clammy pitch in winter, to caulk their damaged ships,

> 7 Ὥσπερ ἐν τῶν Ἐνετῶν τῷ ναυπηγείῳ  
> (As in the shipyard of the Venetians)
>
> 8 Γλοιώδης πίσσα βράζει χειμῶνος ὥρᾳ  
> (Viscous pitch boils in the winter season)
>
> 9 Πρὸς ἐπίπλασιν τῶν βεβλαμμένων πλοίων  
> (For caulking the damaged ships)

- *Ὥσπερ* opens the comparison of *Quale*, to be answered by *Οὕτως* in line 16. In *ἐν τῶν Ἐνετῶν τῷ ναυπηγείῳ* ‘in the Venetians’ shipyard’, *ἐν* governs the dative singular *τῷ ναυπηγείῳ*; the intervening possessive genitive plural *τῶν Ἐνετῶν* thus precedes the noun, unlike *de’ Viniziani* after *arzanà*.
- *Γλοιώδης πίσσα βράζει* ‘sticky pitch boils’ moves the subject before the verb, unlike *bolle ... la tenace pece*. *γλοιώδης* is nominative feminine singular, agreeing with the anarthrous material noun *πίσσα*; *βράζει* is 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *βράζω*, used intransitively. *χειμῶνος ὥρᾳ* ‘in the season of winter’ follows the verb: *ὥρᾳ* is a temporal dative, *χειμῶνος* the dependent genitive of *χειμών*.
- *πρὸς ἐπίπλασιν* ‘for a coating’ replaces the infinitive of purpose *a rimpalmar* with *πρός* and an accusative action noun. The Italian direct object becomes its objective genitive, *τῶν βεβλαμμένων πλοίων*. *βεβλαμμένων* (gen. neut. pl., perfect middle-passive participle of *βλάπτω*, passive in sense) expresses the resulting state of damage positively, in place of *non sani*. *πλοίων* names the ships directly rather than using *legni* ‘timbers’; *lor* has no separate equivalent.

> 10 che navicar non ponno, e in quella vece  
> 11 chi fa suo legno nuovo, e chi ristoppa  
> 12 le coste a quel che più viaggi fece;  

[which] they cannot navigate ; and, instead thereof, one builds his ship anew, one plugs the ribs of that which hath made many voyages

> 10 Καὶ μὴ πλωΐμων· συγχρόνως δ᾽ ὁ μὲν νέον  
> (And those unseaworthy; and at the same time one a new)
>
> 11 Κατασκευάζει πλοῖον, ὁ δ᾽ ἀποφράττει  
> (Vessel builds, while another stops up)
>
> 12 Τὰ πλευρὰ τοῦ πλοῦς πολλοὺς πεποιηκότος·  
> (The ribs of that which has made many voyages;)

- *καὶ μὴ πλωΐμων* ‘and not seaworthy’ continues the preceding genitive phrase: *πλωΐμων* (gen. neut. pl. of *πλώϊμος*) is a second attribute of *πλοίων*, under the same article. The diaeresis marks the vowels as separate. The relative construction *che navicar non ponno*, with its personal verb of ability and infinitive, becomes a quality negatively characterizing the ships, with *μή*.
- *συγχρόνως δ᾽* ‘and at the same time’ gives *e in quella vece* a temporal rather than a substitutive sense. *δ᾽* is elided postpositive *δέ*. *ὁ μὲν ... ὁ δ᾽* ‘one ... another’ uses the article pronominally with balancing particles to render *chi ... e chi*.
- *νέον κατασκευάζει πλοῖον* ‘builds a new ship’ separates adjective and noun (both neut. acc. sg.) by *κατασκευάζει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *κατασκευάζω*). *νέον* describes the vessel rather than modifying the act of building; the possessive *suo* is not expressed.
- *ἀποφράττει τὰ πλευρά* ‘stops up the ribs’ keeps *ristoppa le coste* as a present active verb (3rd sg. of *ἀποφράττω*, with Attic *-ττ-*) and its accusative object. *τοῦ ... πεποιηκότος* ‘of the one that has made’ replaces *a quel che ... fece* by a possessive genitive dependent on *πλευρά*. The articular perfect active participle of *ποιέω* is genitive neuter singular with *πλοίου* understood; its perfect presents the voyages as the vessel’s accumulated experience. Enclosed between article and participle, *πλοῦς πολλούς* ‘many voyages’ is its object: *πλοῦς* is the contracted accusative plural of *πλόος*, not a nominative singular here. *πολλούς* renders *più* as multiplicity, without comparison.

> 13 chi ribatte da proda, e chi da poppa;  

; some hammer at the prow, some at the stern

> 13 Οὗτος κόπτει τὴν πρώραν, αὐτὸς τὴν πρύμνην,  
> (This one hammers the prow, that one the stern,)

- *Οὗτος κόπτει τὴν πρώραν, αὐτὸς τὴν πρύμνην* makes the prow and stern direct objects of *κόπτει* ‘strikes’ (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *κόπτω*), in place of the local expressions *da proda ... da poppa*. The verb is understood again in the second half.
- The nominative masculine singulars *οὗτος* ‘this one’ and *αὐτός* ‘he himself’ occupy corresponding positions for *chi ... e chi*. Their contrast depends on the Italian distribution of tasks: nominative *αὐτός* is normally intensive, not the ordinary Classical correlative meaning ‘that other one’. The two Greek phrases are juxtaposed without *e*.

> 14 altri fa remi, ed altri volge sarte;  

; some make oars, and some twist ropes

> 14 Ἄλλος πελεκᾷ κώπας, ἢ στρέφει κάλως,  
> (Another hews oars, or twists ropes,)

- *Ἄλλος πελεκᾷ κώπας* ‘another hews oars’ makes *fa* more specific. *ἄλλος* is substantival nominative masculine singular; *πελεκᾷ* is the contracted 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *πελεκάω*, with feminine accusative plural *κώπας* as object.
- *ἢ στρέφει κάλως* ‘or twists ropes’ substitutes disjunctive *ἤ* for *ed* and supplies no second subject for the repeated *altri*: the same *ἄλλος* serves both verbs. *στρέφει* is 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *στρέφω*; *κάλως* is the accusative plural of Attic-declension *κάλως* ‘rope’, not the adverb *καλῶς*.

> 15 chi terzeruolo ed artimon rintoppa:  

; one mends the jib, and one the mainsail

> 15 Ἄλλος δ᾽ ὀθόνην κἀρτέμον᾽ ἀναῤῥάπτει·  
> (And another patches sail-cloth and foresail:)

- *Ἄλλος δ᾽ ... ἀναῤῥάπτει* ‘and another stitches up’ resumes the series with elided *δέ*. The single subject and final verb follow *chi ... rintoppa*: unlike the English, the Greek does not distribute the two sails between two workers. *ἀναῤῥάπτει* is 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἀναρράπτω*; the spelling marks the doubled rho at the junction of the prefix and *ῥάπτω* with smooth and rough breathings.
- *ὀθόνην κἀρτέμον᾽* coordinates two accusative singulars. *ὀθόνην* (from *ὀθόνη*) gives a general ‘sailcloth’ for *terzeruolo*, whereas *ἀρτέμων* ‘foresail’ resembles *artimon* in form but does not give the English “mainsail”. *κἀρτέμον᾽* combines crasis of *καὶ ἀ-* with elision of the final *-α* of accusative *ἀρτέμονα* before the verb.

> 16 tal, non per foco, ma per divina arte  
> 17 bollia laggiuso una pegola spessa  
> 18 che inviscava la ripa da ogni parte.  

: so, not by fire but by art Divine, a dense pitch boiled down there, and overglued the banks on every side.

> 16 Οὕτως οὐ διὰ πυρός, θείᾳ δὲ τέχνῃ,  
> (So, not by fire, but by divine art,)
>
> 17 Ἐκεῖσ᾽ ἔβραζεν ἄσφαλτός τις παχεῖα,  
> (Down there was boiling a thick pitch,)
>
> 18 Χρίουσα πανταχόθεν τὸ πέριξ χεῖλος.  
> (Smearing on every side the surrounding bank.)

- *Οὕτως* ‘so’ answers *Ὥσπερ* as *tal* answers *Quale*. *οὐ διὰ πυρός, θείᾳ δὲ τέχνῃ* differentiates the two occurrences of Italian *per*: *διά* with the genitive singular of *πῦρ* expresses agency or means, while *θείᾳ τέχνῃ* is a bare instrumental dative, adjective and noun both feminine singular. Postpositive *δέ* marks the affirmative contrast of *ma*.
- *Ἐκεῖσ᾽ ἔβραζεν* puts the place before the verb, reversing *bollia laggiuso*. *ἐκεῖσ᾽* is elided *ἐκεῖσε*, ordinarily directional ‘thither’, used here with a stationary process for ‘down there’. *ἔβραζεν* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *βράζω*, with syllabic augment) matches the duration of *bollia* and repeats the verb of line 8 in a past tense.
- *ἄσφαλτός τις παχεῖα* ‘a certain thick bitumen’ varies *πίσσα* as *pegola* varies *pece*. *ἄσφαλτος* is feminine despite its ending; the enclitic indefinite *τις* gives explicit indefiniteness to *una* and accounts for the additional accent on the noun’s final syllable. *παχεῖα* is the feminine nominative singular of *παχύς*.
- *Χρίουσα* ‘smearing’ replaces *che inviscava* with a present active participle of *χρίω*, nominative feminine singular agreeing with *ἄσφαλτος*. Its action is simultaneous with the imperfect, not independently present. *πανταχόθεν* ‘from every side’ renders *da ogni parte*; *τὸ πέριξ χεῖλος* ‘the surrounding rim’ keeps the singular of *la ripa* as the accusative object. The adverb *πέριξ* functions attributively between the article and *χεῖλος*.

## Lines 19–51 — The Demon on the Bridge

> 19 Io vedea lei, ma non vedeva in essa  
> 20 ma' che le bolle che il bollor levava,  
> 21 e gonfiar tutta, e riseder compressa.  

It I saw; but saw nought therein, except the bubbles which the boiling raised, and the heaving and compressed subsiding of the whole.

> 19 Ἔβλεπον αὐτήν· ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἔβλεπον ἔνδον,  
> (I saw it; yet I saw nothing within it,)
>
> 20 Εἰ μὴ τοὺς βρασμούς, οὓς ὑπῆρεν ἡ ζέσις,  
> (Save the bubbles which the boiling raised up,)
>
> 21 Οἰδαίνουσ᾽ αὐτὴν καὶ καθιεῖσα πάλιν.  
> (Swelling it and letting it sink back again.)

- *Ἔβλεπον αὐτήν· ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἔβλεπον ἔνδον* preserves both the repetition and the imperfect of *vedea lei, ma non vedeva*. *Ἔβλεπον* is 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *βλέπω*; the feminine accusative *αὐτήν* corresponds to *lei*. The adverb *ἔνδον* ‘within’ does duty for *in essa*, without repeating the pronoun. Greek has no expressed object corresponding to “nought”: *οὐκ … εἰ μή* gives ‘I did not see … except’.
- *Τοὺς βρασμούς* (acc. pl. of *βρασμός*) denotes boilings or bubblings rather than individual *bolle*. *Οὓς ὑπῆρεν ἡ ζέσις* retains the order of *che il bollor levava*: relative object, verb, subject. *Ὑπῆρεν* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ὑπαίρω* ‘raise up’, a perfective rendering of the Italian imperfect.
- *Οἰδαίνουσ᾽* (elided *οἰδαίνουσα*, from *οἰδαίνω*) and *καθιεῖσα* (from *καθίημι*) are present active participles, nom. fem. sg., agreeing with *ἡ ζέσις*. Where Dante sees the pitch itself *gonfiar … e riseder*, Musurus makes the boiling the agent, ‘swelling it and letting it down again’: *αὐτήν* is the object of both participles. *Πάλιν* brings out the reversal in *riseder*; neither *tutta* nor *compressa* has a separate equivalent.

> 22 Mentr' io laggiù fissamente mirava,  
> 23 lo duca mio, dicendo: “Guarda, guarda!”  
> 24 mi trasse a sè del loco dov' io stava.  

Whilst I was gazing fixtly down on it, my Guide, saying, "Take care, take care!" drew me to him from the place where I was standing.

> 22 Μεταξὺ δὲ θεωροῦντ᾽ ἀτενῶς κάτω,  
> (While I was gazing intently down below,)
>
> 23 Οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ· “ Πρόσχες, φύλαξαι!” λέγων  
> (My Guide, saying: “Look out, take care!”)
>
> 24 Εἵλκυσέ μ᾽ ἐκ τοῦ μέρους, ἔνθ᾽ εὑρισκόμην.  
> (Drew me away from the spot where I was standing.)

- *Μεταξὺ δὲ θεωροῦντ᾽ ἀτενῶς κάτω* replaces the finite *mentr’ io … mirava* with *μεταξύ* ‘meanwhile, in the midst of’ and a circumstantial present active participle. *Θεωροῦντ᾽* is elided *θεωροῦντα* (acc. masc. sg. of *θεωρέω*), agreeing in anticipation with *μ᾽*, the object of *εἵλκυσε*. *Ἀτενῶς* renders *fissamente*; *κάτω* gives the downward direction of *laggiù* without its demonstrative element.
- *Οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ* ‘my guide’ has crasis of *ὁ ἐμός* in *οὑμός*. *Λέγων* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *λέγω*) follows, rather than introduces, the quoted words. For the repeated *Guarda, guarda!* there are two different 2nd sg. imperatives: *πρόσχες* (aor. act. of *προσέχω*) ‘pay attention’ and *φύλαξαι* (aor. mid. of *φυλάσσω*) ‘guard yourself’.
- *Εἵλκυσέ μ᾽* is ‘he drew me’ (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἑλκύω*, with elided *με*); nothing corresponds to *a sè*. *Ἐκ τοῦ μέρους* gives the point of removal. *Ἔνθ᾽* is *ἔνθα* ‘where’, elided before *εὑρισκόμην*. The latter is 1st sg. imperf. mid./pass. of *εὑρίσκω*, literally ‘I was to be found’, for *io stava*.

> 25 Allor mi volsi come l' uom cui tarda  
> 26 di veder quel che gli convien fuggire,  
> 27 e cui paura subita sgagliarda,  
> 28 che per veder non indugia il partire;  

Then I turned round, like one who longs to see what he must shun, and who is dashed with sudden fear, so that he puts not off his flight to look;

> 25 Τότε δ᾽ ἐστράφην, ὡς ὁ ποθῶν ἐνθέρμως  
> (Then I turned round, like one who eagerly longs)
>
> 26 Ἰδεῖν ἅπερ ἂν αὐτῷ συμφέροι φεύγειν,  
> (To see that which it would be best for him to flee,)
>
> 27 Ἀλλὰ συσχεθεὶς ἐξαπιναίῳ φόβῳ,  
> (Yet, seized by a sudden fear,)
>
> 28 Ἅμ᾽ ἀτενίζων τὴν φυγὴν ἐπισπεύδει.  
> (While still gazing, hastens his flight.)

- *Τότε δ᾽ ἐστράφην* renders *Allor mi volsi* by an intransitive aorist passive (1st sg. of *στρέφω*). Instead of *l’ uom cui tarda di veder*, *ὁ ποθῶν ἐνθέρμως ἰδεῖν* is ‘the one eagerly longing to see’: article and pres. act. participle of *ποθέω*, intensified by an adverb and completed by the second aor. act. infinitive *ἰδεῖν* of *ὁράω*.
- *Ἅπερ ἂν αὐτῷ συμφέροι φεύγειν* is ‘the very things it would be to his advantage to flee’. *Ἅπερ* is neut. acc. pl. of the strengthened relative *ὅσπερ*, object of *φεύγειν*; its clause supplies the object of *ἰδεῖν*. Impersonal *συμφέροι* (3rd sg. pres. act. opt. of *συμφέρω*) with *ἄν* is potential, weaker than *gli convien*; *αὐτῷ* is the person whose interest is concerned, and the present infinitive *φεύγειν* specifies the advantageous action.
- *Ἀλλὰ συσχεθείς* ‘but having been seized’ (nom. masc. sg. aor. pass. participle of *συνέχω*) reverses *cui paura subita sgagliarda*: the man is now the grammatical subject and *ἐξαπιναίῳ φόβῳ* ‘by sudden fear’ a dative of cause. The finite verb of the comparison is held back to *ἐπισπεύδει* (3rd sg. pres. act. of *ἐπισπεύδω*), ‘he hastens’, with *τὴν φυγήν* as object. Musurus turns *non indugia* into a positive and *per veder* into simultaneous action: *ἅμ᾽ ἀτενίζων* (elided *ἅμα* with pres. act. participle) ‘gazing at the same time’.

> 29 e vidi dietro a noi un diavol nero  
> 30 correndo su per lo scoglio venire.  

and behind us I saw a black Demon come running up the cliff.

> 29 Εἶδον δ᾽ ὄπισθεν ἡμῶν ἐπὶ τὴν ἄκραν  
> (And behind us upon the summit I saw)
>
> 30 Μέλανα δαίμον᾽ ἐρχόμενον δρομαίως.  
> (A black demon coming at a run.)

- *Εἶδον δ᾽ ὄπισθεν ἡμῶν* follows *e vidi dietro a noi*: *εἶδον* is 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*, and *ὄπισθεν* governs the genitive. *Ἐπὶ τὴν ἄκραν*, placed before the object of sight, uses the accusative of direction, ‘towards the height’, for the course *su per lo scoglio*.
- *Μέλανα δαίμον᾽* (acc. masc. sg. of *μέλας* and elided *δαίμονα* of *δαίμων*) takes a supplementary participle after the verb of perception: *ἐρχόμενον* (pres. mid. participle of *ἔρχομαι*) for the infinitive *venire*. Conversely, the Italian gerund *correndo* becomes the adverb *δρομαίως* ‘at a run’.

> 31 Ahi quanto egli era nell' aspetto fiero!  

Ah, how ferocious was his aspect!

> 31 Φεῦ ! ὢ πόσον ἦν τὴν θέαν θηριώδης !  
> (Alas! how savage he was in aspect!)

- *Φεῦ! ὢ πόσον* expands *Ahi quanto*: two interjections followed by adverbial neuter accusative *πόσον* ‘how very’. In *ἦν τὴν θέαν θηριώδης*, the demon, not his appearance, is the subject of *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. of *εἰμί*); *θηριώδης* ‘beastlike’ is predicate nominative, *τὴν θέαν* accusative of respect for *nell’ aspetto*.

> 32 e quanto mi parea nell' atto acerbo,  
> 33 con l' ale aperte, e sopra il piè leggiero!  

and how bitter he seemed to me in gesture, with his wings outspread, and light of foot!

> 32 Καὶ πόσον ὀξὺς ἐδόκει μοι τὴν τάσιν,  
> (And how fierce he seemed to me in bearing,)
>
> 33 Ἔχων ἀνοικτὰ πτερὰ κὠκεῖς τοὺς πόδας !  
> (Having outspread wings and swift feet!)

- *Καὶ πόσον ὀξὺς ἐδόκει μοι τὴν τάσιν* repeats the exclamatory accusative and the accusative of respect: *τὴν τάσιν* ‘in bearing, attitude’ renders *nell’ atto*. *Ὀξύς* ‘sharp, fierce’, predicate nominative, is advanced from the position of *acerbo*; *ἐδόκει* (3rd sg. imperf. act. of *δοκέω*) takes the judging person's dative *μοι*, as in *mi parea*.
- *Ἔχων* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *ἔχω*) supplies a single construction for *con* and *sopra*: ‘having wings open and feet swift’. *Ἀνοικτά* agrees with neut. pl. *πτερά*; *κὠκεῖς* is crasis for *καὶ ὠκεῖς*, acc. masc. pl. of *ὠκύς*, predicated of *τοὺς πόδας*. The singular *piè* and its lightness become plural feet and their speed.

> 34 L' omero suo, ch' era acuto e superbo,  
> 35 carcava un peccator con ambo l' anche,  
> 36 e quei tenea de' piè ghermito il nerbo.  

His shoulders that were sharp and high, a sinner with both haunches laded; and of each foot he held the sinew grasped.

> 34 Ἐπ’ ὤμου δὲ προμήκους καὶ γωνιώδους  
> (And upon a shoulder long and angular)
>
> 35 Ἔφερεν ἁμαρτωλὸν ἐκ τῆς ὀσφύος,  
> (He bore a sinner by the loins,)
>
> 36 Περιέσφιγγέ τ’ αὐτὸν τοῖς ποδῶν νεύροις.  
> (And held him tightly gripped by the sinews of his feet.)

- *Ἐπ᾽ ὤμου … προμήκους καὶ γωνιώδους* keeps the singular *omero*, despite the English “shoulders”. *Ἐπί* with the genitive locates the load ‘on a shoulder’; the two gen. sg. adjectives (from *προμήκης* and *γωνιώδης*) mean ‘elongated and angular’ rather than *acuto e superbo*. They replace the relative predicate *ch’ era*; *suo* is left implicit.
- Musurus makes the bearer subject of *ἔφερεν* (3rd sg. imperf. act. of *φέρω*) and *ἁμαρτωλόν* his direct object; in Italian *un peccator* is what loads the shoulder. *Ἐκ τῆς ὀσφύος* (*ὀσφύς*, gen. sg.) marks the point by which he is carried, ‘by the waist’, replacing *con ambo l’ anche* without a dual or a word for ‘both’.
- *Περιέσφιγγέ τ᾽ αὐτόν* ‘and he gripped him tight’ uses the internally augmented imperfect of *περισφίγγω* (3rd sg. act.), with elided connective *τε*. The person is the accusative object; *τοῖς ποδῶν νεύροις* puts ‘the sinews’ in the instrumental dative plural, with dependent gen. pl. *ποδῶν* ‘of feet’. Italian instead makes singular *il nerbo* the thing held *ghermito*.

> 37 “Del nostro ponte,” disse “o Malebranche,  

"Ye Malebranche of our bridge!" he said,

> 37 “Ὦ Κακώνυχες τῆς γεφύρας τῆσδ᾽,” εἶπεν,  
> (“O Evil-claws of this bridge,” he said,)

- *Ὦ Κακώνυχες* translates rather than transcribes *o Malebranche*: *ὦ* with voc. pl. of the compound *Κακώνυξ* ‘evil-clawed’. *Τῆς γεφύρας τῆσδ᾽* follows the address instead of preceding it; elided *τῆσδε* (gen. fem. sg. of *ὅδε*) gives ‘of this bridge’ for *del nostro ponte*. *Εἶπεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act., ‘he said’) is moved to the end.

> 38 ecco un degli anzian di santa Zita;  

"lo! one of Santa Zita's Elders;

> 38 “Ἰδοὺ τῶν γερόντων εἷς Ἁγίας Ζίτας.  
> (“Behold one of the elders of Santa Zita.)

- *Ἰδού* is the presentative particle ‘behold’ (historically the accented imperative *ἰδοῦ*). *Τῶν γερόντων* is the partitive gen. pl. of *γέρων*, placed before nominative *εἷς* ‘one’, reversing *un degli anzian*. *Ἁγίας Ζίτας* is gen. fem. sg., dependent on ‘elders’; the name in *-α* has genitive *-ας*.

> 39 mettetel sotto, ch' io torno per anche  
> 40 a quella terra ch' i' n' ho ben fornita:  

thrust him under, while I return for others to that city which [I have] provided well with them:

> 39 „Θέτ᾽ αὐτὸν ὑπὸ βυθόν· ἐγὼ δ᾽ ὑπάγω  
> („Thrust him beneath the depths; for I am going)
>
> 40 „Πάλιν δι᾽ ἄλλους, ὧν πλήρης ἔσθ᾽ ἡ χώρα.  
> („Back for others, of whom that land is full.)

- *Θέτ᾽ αὐτὸν ὑπὸ βυθόν* ‘put him down beneath the depth’ expands adverbial *sotto* with a noun. *Θέτ᾽* is elided *θέτε*, 2nd pl. aor. act. imperative of *τίθημι*; *ὑπό* with the accusative denotes motion.
- *Ἐγὼ δ᾽ ὑπάγω πάλιν δι᾽ ἄλλους* ‘I, on the other hand, go back for others’ replaces linking *ch’* with contrastive *δέ*. *Πάλιν* supplies the return implicit in *torno*, and *διά* with the accusative expresses the reason for going. Rather than attach a destination to the verb and retain *i’ n’ ho ben fornita*, Musurus writes *ὧν πλήρης ἔσθ᾽ ἡ χώρα* ‘of whom the land is full’: the relative *ὧν* refers to *ἄλλους* and is governed by *πλήρης*. *Ἔσθ᾽* is elided *ἐστί*, with aspiration before *ἡ*; the land is subject, and the speaker's agency disappears.

> 41 ognun v' è barattier, fuor che Bonturo;  
> 42 del no per li denar vi si fa ita.”  

every [one] there is a barrator, except Bonturo; there they make 'Ay' of 'No' for money."

> 41 „Πάντες εἴσ᾽ ἐκεῖ φένακες, πλὴν Βοντούρου·  
> („All there are cheats, except Bonturo;)
>
> 42 „Τὸ γὰρ Οὔ τρέπουσ᾽ εἰς Ναὶ χρημάτων χάριν.”  
> („For they turn No into Yes for the sake of money.”)

- *Πάντες εἴσ᾽ ἐκεῖ φένακες* replaces distributive singular *ognun … è barattier* with plural subject, verb and predicate: ‘all there are cheats’. *Εἴσ᾽* is elided *εἰσί* (3rd pl. pres. of *εἰμί*); *φένακες* (nom. pl. of *φέναξ*) is less specific than *barattier*. Exceptive *πλήν* governs the genitive *Βοντούρου*.
- *Τὸ γὰρ Οὔ τρέπουσ᾽ εἰς Ναί* makes the second assertion explanatory with postpositive *γάρ*. The article turns the quoted negative into an accusative object; *τρέπουσ᾽* (elided *τρέπουσι*, 3rd pl. pres. act. of *τρέπω*) makes the agents of impersonal *vi si fa* the understood subject. *Εἰς* marks the result, ‘into Yes’; there is no second equivalent of the locative *vi*. *Χρημάτων χάριν* ‘for money's sake’ has gen. pl. of *χρῆμα* before the postposition *χάριν*.

> 43 Laggiù il buttò, e per lo scoglio duro  
> 44 si volse, e mai non fu mastino sciolto  
> 45 con tanta fretta a seguitar lo furo.  

Down he threw him, then wheeled along the flinty cliff; and never was mastiff loosed with such a haste to follow thief.

> 43 Αὐτὸν δὲ κάτω ῥίψας, πρὸς τὴν κρημνώδη  
> (And hurling him down, toward the precipitous)
>
> 44 Ἄκραν ἐπέστρεψ᾽· οὔποτε δ᾽ ὠκυτέρως  
> (Crag he turned back; and never more swiftly)
>
> 45 Κύων ἀφεθεὶς ἐδίωξε τὸν κλέπτην.  
> (Did an unleashed hound pursue the thief.)

- *Αὐτὸν δὲ κάτω ῥίψας* puts the personal object first and makes the throw anterior to the turn: *ῥίψας* is nom. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *ῥίπτω*, in place of finite *buttò*. *Πρὸς τὴν κρημνώδη ἄκραν* ‘towards the precipitous height’ gives direction rather than a route *per lo scoglio* and steepness rather than hardness. *Ἐπέστρεψ᾽* is elided *ἐπέστρεψε*, internally augmented 3rd sg. aor. act. of *ἐπιστρέφω*, used intransitively for reflexive *si volse*.
- *Οὔποτε δ᾽ ὠκυτέρως* ‘and never more swiftly’ makes the implicit comparison in *mai … con tanta fretta* a comparative adverb. It modifies *ἐδίωξε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *διώκω*), not the release. *Κύων ἀφεθείς* ‘a hound let loose’ has the more general noun for *mastino* and a nom. masc. sg. aor. pass. participle of *ἀφίημι*. Thus finite passive *fu … sciolto* becomes the attendant circumstance, and purposive *a seguitar lo furo* the main assertion, with *τὸν κλέπτην* accusative object.

> 46 Quei s' attuffò, e tornò su convolto;  

The sinner plunged in, and came up again writhing convolved;

> 46 Ὁ δὲ βυθισθείς, σύγκαμπτος ἐπανέβη·  
> (And the other, having plunged in, rose up doubled over;)

- *Ὁ δέ* uses the article pronominally, ‘but the other’, for *quei*. *Βυθισθείς* (nom. masc. sg. aor. pass. participle of *βυθίζω*) turns reflexive *s’ attuffò* into an anterior circumstance, ‘having been submerged’. *Σύγκαμπτος* ‘bent double’ is a predicate adjective describing his posture for *convolto*, not a verb of writhing. *Ἐπανέβη* (3rd sg. root aor. act. of *ἐπαναβαίνω*, augmented in the verbal stem) combines the return and ascent of *tornò su*.

> 47 ma i demon, che del ponte avean coperchio,  
> 48 gridar: “Qui non ha loco il santo volto;  

but the Demons, who were under cover of the bridge, cried: "Here the Sacred Face besteads not;

> 47 Ἀλλ᾽ οἱ δαίμονες οἱ κάτωθεν γεφύρας  
> (But the demons who were beneath the bridge)
>
> 48 Ἔκραζον· “Ὧδ᾽ οὐκ ἔστιν εἰκὼν ἁγία.  
> (Shouted: “Here there is no Sacred Image.)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ οἱ δαίμονες οἱ κάτωθεν γεφύρας* replaces *che del ponte avean coperchio* with a second article introducing an attributive phrase, ‘the demons, those underneath the bridge’. *Κάτωθεν*, though *-θεν* can mark source, is local here and governs gen. sg. *γεφύρας*. The notion of cover is not separately expressed.
- *Ἔκραζον* is 3rd pl. imperf. act. of *κράζω*, presenting the cries as continuing rather than using an aorist for Italian preterite *gridar*. *Ὧδ᾽ οὐκ ἔστιν εἰκὼν ἁγία* gives *non ha loco* an existential reading: ‘here there is no holy image’, not “bestead[s] not”. *Ὧδ᾽* is elided local *ὧδε*; *ἔστιν* is accented as an existential, and *ἁγία* agrees with the feminine nominative *εἰκών*. ‘Image’ replaces the more specific *volto*.

> 49 qui si nuota altrimenti che nel Serchio:  

here swim ye otherwise than in the Serchio:

> 49 „Οὐ νήχοντ᾽ ἐνθάδ᾽ ὡς ἐν Αἴσαρος ῥείθροις.  
> („Here men do not swim as in the streams of Aesar.)

- *Οὐ νήχοντ᾽ ἐνθάδ᾽ ὡς* ‘they do not swim here as …’ renders affirmative *qui si nuota altrimenti che* by negating likeness. *Νήχοντ᾽* is elided *νήχονται*, 3rd pl. pres. mid. of *νήχομαι*, with an indefinite human subject, not a second-person address; *ἐνθάδ᾽* is elided *ἐνθάδε*.
- *Ἐν Αἴσαρος ῥείθροις* ‘in Aesar's streams’ puts the river name in the genitive dependent on poetic *ῥείθροις* (dat. pl. of *ῥεῖθρον*), rather than making the name itself the complement of *ἐν*. Musurus substitutes *Αἴσαρος* for *Serchio*.

> 50 però, se tu non vuoi de' nostri graffi,  
> 51 non far sopra la pegola soverchio.”  

therefore, unless thou wishest to make trial of our drags, come not out above the pitch."

> 50 „Εἰ θέλεις οὖν τὰς ἡμῶν φυγεῖν ἁρπάγας,  
> („If then you wish to escape our grappling-hooks,)
>
> 51 „Σκόπει μὴ πλέῃς ὕπερθεν τῆς ἀσφάλτου.”  
> („Take care not to float above the pitch.”)

- *Εἰ θέλεις οὖν … φυγεῖν* ‘if, then, you wish to escape’ transfers the consequence marked by *però* to postpositive *οὖν*. *Εἰ* introduces an open condition with *θέλεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *θέλω*). Greek removes the negative of *se tu non vuoi* and supplies the complementary second aor. act. infinitive *φυγεῖν* of *φεύγω*. Its object *τὰς ἡμῶν … ἁρπάγας* (acc. pl. of *ἁρπάγη* ‘grappling-hook’, with possessive gen. *ἡμῶν*) makes the implements explicit for *de’ nostri graffi*.
- *Σκόπει μὴ πλέῃς* ‘see to it that you do not float’ replaces the direct prohibition *non far … soverchio* with a positive 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *σκοπέω* and a preventive *μή*-clause. *Πλέῃς* is 2nd sg. pres. act. subj. of *πλέω*: the wording specifies the continuing action to avoid rather than an extent of bodily protrusion. *Ὕπερθεν* ‘above’ governs the gen. sg. *τῆς ἀσφάλτου* for *sopra la pegola*.

## Lines 52–84 — The Hooks and Virgil’s Parley

> 52 Poi l' addentar con più di cento raffi ;  
> 53 disser: “Coperto convien che qui balli,  
> 54 sì che, se puoi, nascosamente accaffi.”  

Then they struck him with more than a hundred prongs, *and* said: “Covered thou must dance thee here; so that, if thou canst, thou mayest pilfer privately.”

> 52 Εἶτα κεντοῦντες αὐτὸν ἑκατὸν ἅρπαις  
> (Then piercing him with a hundred hooks)
>
> 53 Εἶπον· “Δεῖ σε νῦν ὑπὸ σκέπην χορεύειν  
> (They said: “Now you must dance under cover)
>
> 54 „ Κρύβδην τε καπηλεύειν, εἰ δυνατόν σοι.”  
> („ And traffic in secret, if you can.”)

- *Εἶτα κεντοῦντες αὐτὸν … εἶπον* ‘then, pricking him … they said’ subordinates the first of the Italian’s two finite verbs to the second. *κεντοῦντες* is the contracted present active participle of *κεντέω*, nominative masculine plural: the pricking accompanies the speaking. It loses the image of biting in *l’addentar*. *εἶπον* is the second aorist active indicative, here 3rd plural, corresponding to *disser*.
- *ἑκατὸν ἅρπαις* ‘with a hundred hooks’ uses the instrumental dative plural of *ἅρπη* with an indeclinable numeral; *più di* has no equivalent. *δεῖ σε … χορεύειν* ‘you must dance’ replaces *convien che … balli* with impersonal *δεῖ* and a present active infinitive whose subject is accusative *σε*. *ὑπὸ σκέπην* ‘under cover’ replaces predicative *coperto*; Musurus supplies temporal *νῦν* ‘now’ rather than local *qui*.
- *κρύβδην τε καπηλεύειν* ‘and ply a covert trade’ coordinates a second infinitive under *δεῖ* by postpositive *τε*. Thus the purpose or consequence expressed by *sì che … accaffi* becomes part of the injunction. *κρύβδην* answers closely to *nascosamente*, but *καπηλεύειν* ‘deal as a huckster, make a trade of’ suggests dishonest dealing rather than the act of snatching. The condition moves to the end: *εἰ δυνατόν σοι* ‘if possible for you’, with neuter predicate adjective, understood *ἐστί*, and dative of the person concerned, for personal *se puoi*.

> 55 Non altrimenti i cuochi ai lor vassalli  
> 56 fanno atuffare in mezzo la caldaia  
> 57 la carne con gli uncin, perchè non galli.  

Not otherwise do the cooks make their vassals dip the flesh into the middle of the boiler with their hooks, to hinder it from floating.

> 55 Οὐκ ἄλλως οἱ μάγειροι τοῖς συνεργάταις  
> (Not otherwise the cooks to their helpers)
>
> 56 Διατάττουσιν εἰς μέσον τῶν λεβήτων  
> (Give orders into the middle of the cauldrons)
>
> 57 Ὠθεῖν κρεάγραις τἀπιπλέοντα κρέα.  
> (To thrust down with flesh-hooks the floating pieces of meat.)

- *Οὐκ ἄλλως οἱ μάγειροι* keeps the opening order of *Non altrimenti i cuochi*. But *τοῖς συνεργάταις διατάττουσιν … ὠθεῖν* is ‘they give their helpers orders to push’: the causative *fanno atuffare* becomes a verb of command (3rd plural present active indicative of *διατάττω*, with Attic *-ττ-*) and a present active infinitive of *ὠθέω*. The dative plural names the recipients of the order and supplies the understood agents of the pushing. *συνεργάταις* makes them fellow workers rather than *vassalli*; there is no separate possessive for *lor*.
- *εἰς μέσον τῶν λεβήτων* ‘into the midst of the cauldrons’ retains direction with *εἰς* and the accusative, but pluralizes *la caldaia*. Instrumental *κρεάγραις* (dative plural of *κρεάγρα*) specifies hooks for meat.
- *τἀπιπλέοντα κρέα* ‘the pieces of meat floating on top’ has crasis for *τὰ ἐπιπλέοντα*. The present active participle of *ἐπιπλέω* agrees with *κρέα*, accusative neuter plural of *κρέας*, the object of *ὠθεῖν*. Italian singular *la carne* becomes plural, and the negative purpose *perchè non galli* becomes a positive description of what the meat is doing when pushed.

> 58 Lo buon maestro: “Acciocchè non si paia  
> 59 che tu ci sii,” mi disse, “giù t' acquatta  
> 60 dopo uno scheggio che alcun schermo t' haia;  

The kind Master said to me: “That it may not be seen that thou art here, cower down behind a jagg, so that thou mayest have some screen for thyself;

> 58 Ὁ δ’ ἀγαθὸς Προστάτης· “Ἵνα μή σ’ ὧδε,”  
> (And the good Protector: “Lest you here,”)
>
> 59 Εἶπε, “Παρόντ’ αἴσθωνται, κάτω νῦν στῆθι  
> (He said, “be perceived present, stand down now)
>
> 60 „Ὄπισθεν πετρῶν, ὅπως ἀσφαλῶς ἔχῃς.  
> („Behind the rocks, so that you may be safe.)

- *Ὁ δ’ ἀγαθὸς Προστάτης … εἶπε* gives *lo buon maestro … mi disse* as ‘and the good protector … said’. Elided *δ’* (*δέ*) marks the transition; *προστάτης* stresses the role of one who stands before another rather than that of teacher. The addressee of *εἶπε* (3rd singular second aorist active indicative) is not separately expressed. As in Italian, the reporting verb interrupts the opening purpose clause.
- *ἵνα μή σ’ ὧδε … παρόντ’ αἴσθωνται* ‘so that they do not perceive you to be present here’ personalizes *acciocchè non si paia che tu ci sii*. *αἴσθωνται* is 3rd plural second aorist middle subjunctive of *αἰσθάνομαι*, after negative final *ἵνα μή*. The verb of perception takes *σ’* (*σε*) with the supplementary participle *παρόντ’* (*παρόντα*, present active participle of *πάρειμι*, accusative masculine singular). The two elisions occur before *ὧδε* and *αἴσθωνται* respectively.
- *κάτω νῦν στῆθι ὄπισθεν πετρῶν* ‘take your stand down below now, behind rocks’ gives a less specific posture than *giù t’acquatta*. *στῆθι* is the intransitive root-aorist active imperative, 2nd singular, of *ἵστημι*; *ὄπισθεν* governs the genitive plural, replacing *dopo uno scheggio* with no single projecting rock specified. *ὅπως ἀσφαλῶς ἔχῃς* ‘so that you may be safe’ renders *che alcun schermo t’haia* by a purpose clause with the 2nd singular present active subjunctive of *ἔχω*: adverb + *ἔχω* describes a state, not the possession of a screen.

> 61 e per nulla offension che mi sia fatta,  
> 62 non temer tu, ch' io ho le cose conte,  
> 63 perchè altra volta fui a tal baratta.”  

and whatever outrage may be done to me, fear not thou: for I know these matters, having once before been in the like affray.”

> 61 „ Περὶ βλάβης μοι τυχὸν γενησομένης  
> („ Concerning any harm that might befall me,)
>
> 62 „ Μὴ φοβοῦ· γινώσκω γὰρ τἀνθάδε πάντα  
> („ Do not fear; for I know all things here,)
>
> 63 „ Ὡς ἐντυχὼν ἄλλοτε τοιαύτῃ πάλῃ.”  
> („ Having met with such a struggle before.”)

- *περὶ βλάβης μοι τυχὸν γενησομένης μὴ φοβοῦ* ‘do not be afraid concerning harm that may perhaps befall me’ brings the object of concern forward as in *per nulla offension … non temer tu*. *βλάβης* is genitive after *περί*; *γενησομένης*, future middle participle of *γίγνομαι*, agrees with it, with dative *μοι* for the person affected. It replaces passive subjunctive *mi sia fatta* with an event yet to occur. Adverbial *τυχόν* ‘perhaps’ is in origin the neuter singular second aorist active participle of *τυγχάνω*; it does not agree with *βλάβης*. *μὴ φοβοῦ* is a prohibition with the contracted 2nd singular present middle imperative of *φοβέομαι*.
- *γινώσκω γὰρ τἀνθάδε πάντα* ‘for I know everything here’ replaces *ch’ io ho le cose conte* with a simple present active indicative, 1st singular, followed by explanatory *γάρ*. *τἀνθάδε* is crasis for *τὰ ἐνθάδε*: the article makes the local adverb substantival, and *πάντα* extends ‘the things here’ to ‘all the things here’.
- *ὡς ἐντυχὼν ἄλλοτε τοιαύτῃ πάλῃ* gives the speaker’s ground for his knowledge in place of finite *perchè … fui*. *ἐντυχών* is the nominative masculine singular second aorist active participle of *ἐντυγχάνω*, agreeing with the unexpressed ‘I’; *ὡς* presents the encounter as his stated reason. The verb takes a dative, *τοιαύτῃ πάλῃ* ‘with such a struggle’, where Italian has local *a tal baratta*. *ἄλλοτε* answers to *altra volta*.

> 64 Poscia passò di là dal co' del ponte,  
> 65 e com' ei giunse in su la ripa sesta,  
> 66 mestier gli fu d' aver sicura fronte.  

Then he passed beyond the head of the bridge; and when he arrived on the sixth bank, it was needful for him to have a steadfast front.

> 64 Ἐκεῖθεν εἶτα τὴν γέφυραν διέβη·  
> (Then from there he crossed over the bridge;)
>
> 65 Ὅτε δ’ ἀφίκετ’ ἐπὶ τὴν ἕκτην ὄχθην,  
> (And when he reached the sixth bank,)
>
> 66 Ἐδέησ’ αὐτὸν γενναῖον δεῖξαι στῆθος.  
> (He needed to show a stout heart.)

- *Ἐκεῖθεν εἶτα τὴν γέφυραν διέβη* ‘from there he then crossed the bridge’ changes the spatial construction: *ἐκεῖθεν* gives a starting point, and the whole bridge is the accusative object of *διέβη* (3rd singular root-aorist active indicative of *διαβαίνω*, with augment after the preverb). There is no noun for *co’* ‘head, end’, nor an exact equivalent of *di là dal*.
- *ὅτε δ’ ἀφίκετ’ ἐπὶ τὴν ἕκτην ὄχθην* closely follows temporal *e com’ ei giunse in su la ripa sesta*. *δ’* is elided *δέ*; *ἀφίκετ’* is elided *ἀφίκετο*, 3rd singular second aorist middle indicative of *ἀφικνέομαι*. *ἐπί* with the accusative marks the destination.
- *ἐδέησ’ αὐτὸν γενναῖον δεῖξαι στῆθος* ‘it was necessary for him to show a noble breast’ keeps the impersonal necessity of *mestier gli fu*. *ἐδέησ’* is elided *ἐδέησε*, the augmented 3rd singular aorist active indicative corresponding to *δεῖ*. Italian *gli* becomes the accusative subject *αὐτόν* of *δεῖξαι*, aorist active infinitive of *δείκνυμι*. Greek substitutes showing for having, and *γενναῖον … στῆθος* ‘a stout breast’ for *sicura fronte*.

> 67 Con quel furor e con quella tempesta  
> 68 ch' escono i cani addosso al poverello,  
> 69 che di subito chiede ove s' arresta  

With that fury and that storm, wherewith the dogs rush forth upon the poor man who where he stops suddenly seeks *alms*,

> 67 Μεθ’ ὁρμῆς καὶ μανίας, μεθ’ ἧς οἱ κύνες  
> (With the fury and frenzy with which dogs)
>
> 68 Ἐπιπίπτουσιν ἐπὶ πτωχὸν ἀλήτην  
> (Rush out upon a wandering beggar)
>
> 69 Αἴφνης ἐφιστάμενον καὶ προσαιτοῦντα,  
> (Who suddenly stops and asks for alms,)

- *μεθ’ ὁρμῆς καὶ μανίας, μεθ’ ἧς* ‘with impetuosity and frenzy, with which’ substitutes two states of feeling or motion for *quel furor e quella tempesta*, dropping both demonstratives. *μεθ’* is *μετά* before a rough breathing, with elision and aspiration of the final stop. The genitive feminine singular relative *ἧς* agrees immediately with *μανίας*, rather than resuming the pair in the plural; the repeated preposition makes explicit the force of Italian *ch’*.
- *οἱ κύνες ἐπιπίπτουσιν ἐπὶ πτωχὸν ἀλήτην* ‘the dogs fall upon a poor wanderer’ replaces *escono … addosso* with a compound verb of onset (3rd plural present active indicative of *ἐπιπίπτω*) reinforced by *ἐπί* with the accusative. Greek supplies *ἀλήτην* ‘wanderer’ and makes *πτωχόν* its modifier rather than reproducing the diminutive *poverello*.
- *αἴφνης ἐφιστάμενον καὶ προσαιτοῦντα* ‘suddenly stopping and begging’ turns the relative clause into two present participles agreeing with *ἀλήτην* in the accusative masculine singular: middle *ἐφιστάμενον* from *ἐφίστημι* and contracted active *προσαιτοῦντα* from *προσαιτέω*. Musurus attaches *αἴφνης* directly to the stopping, coordinates the begging with it, and leaves out local *ove*. The specific sense of *chiede* is carried by the verb ‘beg’, without a noun for alms.

> 70 usciron quei di sotto il ponticello,  
> 71 e volser contra lui tutti i roncigli;  
> 72 ma ei gridò: “Nessun di voi sia fello!  

rushed those *Demons* from beneath the bridge, and turned against him all their crooks; but he cried: “Be none of ye outrageous!

> 70 Ἐξῆλθον οὗτοι κάτωθεν τῆς γεφύρας,  
> (Out they came from beneath the bridge,)
>
> 71 Τὰς ἁρπάγας τρέψαντες κατ’ αὐτοῦ πάσας.  
> (Turning all their grappling-hooks against him.)
>
> 72 Αὐτὸς δ’ ἔκραξε· “Μηδεὶς ὑμῶν τολμάτω·  
> (But he cried out: “Let none of you dare!)

- *ἐξῆλθον οὗτοι κάτωθεν τῆς γεφύρας* ‘these came out from beneath the bridge’ resumes the main construction after the comparison. *ἐξῆλθον* is the 3rd plural second aorist active indicative of *ἐξέρχομαι*; postposed *οὗτοι* corresponds to pronominal *quei*, without a noun for “Demons”. *κάτωθεν* takes the genitive and expresses direction from below; *γεφύρας* does not reproduce the diminutive of *ponticello*.
- *τὰς ἁρπάγας τρέψαντες κατ’ αὐτοῦ πάσας* makes finite *e volser* an attendant aorist active participle (*τρέψαντες*, nominative masculine plural of *τρέπω*). *πάσας*, accusative feminine plural, is held back from *τὰς ἁρπάγας* ‘the grapnels’ until the end; the article suffices where Italian has *i* and English “their”. Elided *κατ’* (*κατά*) with the genitive expresses hostile direction.
- *αὐτὸς δ’ ἔκραξε* ‘but he himself cried out’ sets emphatic *αὐτός* against the plural subject, as *ma ei* does; *ἔκραξε* is the augmented 3rd singular aorist active indicative of *κράζω*. *μηδεὶς ὑμῶν τολμάτω* ‘let none of you dare’ retains the partitive genitive of *nessun di voi* but replaces *sia fello* with a verb of daring, its complement unexpressed. *τολμάτω* is the contracted 3rd singular present active imperative of *τολμάω*.

> 73 Innanzi che l' uncin vostro mi pigli,  
> 74 traggasi avanti alcun di voi che m'oda,  
> 75 e poi d' arroncigliarmi si consigli.”  

Before ye touch me with your forks, let one of you come forth to hear me, and then take counsel about hooking me.”

> 73 „Πρὸ τοῦ ψαῦσαί μου τὴν ὑμετέραν ἅρπην  
> („Before your hook touches me,)
>
> 74 „Ἀκούσων μού τις ἐξ ὑμῶν ὧδ᾽ ἐλθέτω,  
> („Let one of you come forward here to hear me,)
>
> 75 „Κἄπειτα τοῦ βάλλειν με σκέψις γενέσθω.”  
> („And then let there be counsel about striking me.”)

- *πρὸ τοῦ ψαῦσαί μου τὴν ὑμετέραν ἅρπην* ‘before your hook touches me’ keeps the singular hook as grammatical subject, as in *l’uncin vostro mi pigli*. *πρό* governs the genitive article of the articular infinitive *τοῦ ψαῦσαι* (aorist active of *ψαύω*); the infinitive has accusative subject *τὴν ὑμετέραν ἅρπην* and genitive object *μου*. The additional accent on *ψαῦσαί* precedes the enclitic. Touching is weaker than seizing.
- *ἀκούσων μού τις ἐξ ὑμῶν ὧδ’ ἐλθέτω* fronts the purpose of the motion: *ἀκούσων* is the future active participle of *ἀκούω*, nominative masculine singular agreeing with indefinite *τις*, ‘someone … with a view to hearing’. It replaces *che m’oda* and takes the genitive of the person heard; *μού* bears an accent before the next enclitic, *τις*. Partitive *ἐξ ὑμῶν* renders *di voi*. *ὧδ’* is elided *ὧδε* ‘hither’; *ἐλθέτω* is the 3rd singular second aorist active imperative of *ἔρχομαι*, a simple command to come for reflexive *traggasi avanti*.
- *κἄπειτα τοῦ βάλλειν με σκέψις γενέσθω* ‘and then let consideration of striking me take place’ has crasis in *κἄπειτα* for *καὶ ἔπειτα*. *τοῦ βάλλειν με* is an objective genitive, an articular present active infinitive dependent on *σκέψις*, with accusative object *με*. It preserves the order of *d’arroncigliarmi si consigli* but generalizes hooking to striking. The impersonal deliberation becomes a noun subject with *γενέσθω*, 3rd singular second aorist middle imperative of *γίγνομαι*.

> 76 Tutti gridaron: “Vada Malacoda ;”  

All cried: “Let Malacoda go”;

> 76 —“Ὕπαγε σύ, Κάκουρε,” κράζουσι πάντες.  
> (—“Go you, Evil-tail,” they all shout.)

- *ὕπαγε σύ, Κάκουρε* ‘go you, Evil-tail’ changes *vada Malacoda* from a third-person direction to a second-person command: *ὕπαγε* is the present active imperative singular of *ὑπάγω*, reinforced by explicit *σύ*. *Κάκουρε* is the vocative singular of *Κάκουρος*, a Greek compound translating the name rather than transcribing it (*κακός* ‘bad’, *οὐρά* ‘tail’).
- *κράζουσι πάντες* places the reporting clause after the cry and uses the historical present (3rd plural active indicative of *κράζω*) for past *tutti gridaron*.

> 77 per che un si mosse, e gli altri stetter fermi;  
> 78 e venne a lui dicendo: “Che gli approda?”  

thereat one moved himself, the others standing firm, and came to him, saying: “What will this avail him?”

> 77 Τῶν δ᾽ ἄλλων ἠρεμούντων, πρὸς τὸν Προστάτην  
> (While the rest stood still, toward the Protector)
>
> 78 Εἷς αὐτῶν ἐλθὼν ἠρώτα· “Τίνος δέῃ ;” —  
> (One of them came and asked: “What do you want?”—)

- *τῶν δ’ ἄλλων ἠρεμούντων* ‘while the others remained still’ moves the others to the front and subordinates *e gli altri stetter fermi* as a genitive absolute. *ἠρεμούντων* is the contracted present active participle, genitive masculine plural, of *ἠρεμέω*; the initial eta belongs to the stem, not to an augment. Connective *δ’* does not expressly render the consequential *per che*.
- *πρὸς τὸν Προστάτην εἷς αὐτῶν ἐλθὼν ἠρώτα* ‘one of them, having come to the protector, asked’ reduces *un si mosse … e venne a lui* to one participle of motion and identifies *lui* by title. *εἷς* is the nominative masculine numeral, with partitive genitive *αὐτῶν*; *ἐλθών* is the nominative masculine singular second aorist active participle of *ἔρχομαι*. The question becomes the main action: *ἠρώτα* is the augmented, contracted 3rd singular imperfect active indicative of *ἐρωτάω*, for participial *dicendo*.
- *τίνος δέῃ;* ‘what do you need?’ interprets *Che gli approda?* as a direct request to state one’s business, rather than a question about what will benefit *him*. *τίνος* is the genitive singular of interrogative *τίς*, governed by *δέῃ*, contracted 2nd singular present middle indicative of *δέομαι* ‘need’.

> 79 “Credi tu, Malacoda, qui vedermi  
> 80 esser venuto,” disse il mio maestro,  
> 81 “sicuro già da tutti vostri schermi,  
> 82 senza voler divino e fato destro?  

“Dost thou expect, Malacoda,” said my Master, “to find I have come here, secure already against all your hindrances, without will Divine and fate propitious?

> 79 —“Ὦ Κάκουρε, νομίζεις ἰδεῖν ἐνταῦθα  
> (—“O Evil-tail, do you think you see here)
>
> 80 „Ἀφιγμένον με,” τότ᾽ εἶπεν ὁ Προστάτης,  
> („Me arrived,” then said my Protector,)
>
> 81 „Ἐν ἀσφαλείᾳ τῶν ὑμετέρων ὅπλων,  
> („In safety from your weapons,)
>
> 82 „Ἄνευ τῆς θείας βουλῆς εὐμενοῦς τ᾽ αἴσης ;  
> („Without divine counsel and favouring destiny?)

- *ὦ Κάκουρε, νομίζεις ἰδεῖν ἐνταῦθα ἀφιγμένον με* ‘Evil-tail, do you think you see me here having arrived?’ moves the vocative before the verb and marks it with *ὦ*. *νομίζεις* (2nd singular present active indicative of *νομίζω*) takes *ἰδεῖν*, second aorist active infinitive of *ὁράω*, with the same understood subject. Within this construction *με* is the object seen, and *ἀφιγμένον*, perfect middle participle of *ἀφικνέομαι*, is predicative, accusative masculine singular. Thus *vedermi esser venuto* becomes perception of a person in the state resulting from arrival.
- *τότ’ εἶπεν ὁ Προστάτης* inserts the reporting clause after *με*: *τότ’* is elided *τότε*, an added ‘then’; *ὁ Προστάτης* again replaces *il mio maestro*, without *mio*. *ἐν ἀσφαλείᾳ* ‘in safety’ replaces the adjective *sicuro* with a prepositional phrase. *τῶν ὑμετέρων ὅπλων* is to be understood as a genitive of separation, ‘from your weapons’: its connection to *ἐν ἀσφαλείᾳ* closely tracks Italian *sicuro … da*. *ὅπλων* makes *schermi* concrete; neither *già* nor *tutti* is separately rendered.
- *ἄνευ τῆς θείας βουλῆς εὐμενοῦς τ’ αἴσης* retains the two coordinated terms of *senza voler divino e fato destro*, both in the genitive after *ἄνευ*. *βουλῆς* makes divine willing a ‘counsel’ or ‘decision’; poetic *αἴσης*, genitive singular of *αἶσα*, gives ‘allotted fate’. *εὐμενοῦς* is the contracted genitive feminine singular of the two-ending adjective *εὐμενής* ‘well-disposed’; postpositive *τ’* is *τε* elided before *αἴσης*.

> 83 Lasciami andar, chè nel cielo è voluto  
> 84 ch' io mostri altrui questo cammin silvestro.”  

Let me pass on: for it is willed in Heaven that I shew another this savage way.”

> 83 „Ἄφες μ᾽ ἰέναι· θέλημα γὰρ ἔστ᾽ ἄνω,  
> („Let me go on; for it is willed above,)
>
> 84 „Ὅπως τραχεῖαν τήνδ᾽ ὁδόν τινι δείξω.” —  
> („That I should show this rugged way to someone.”—)

- *ἄφες μ’ ἰέναι* closely matches *lasciami andar*: *ἄφες* is the 2nd singular aorist active imperative of *ἀφίημι*, with *μ’* (elided *με*) the person permitted and the understood agent of *ἰέναι*, present active infinitive of *εἶμι* ‘go’.
- *θέλημα γὰρ ἔστ’ ἄνω* ‘for there is a will above’ replaces passive *nel cielo è voluto* with an existential construction. *θέλημα* is the neuter singular subject; postpositive *γάρ* renders causal *chè*. *ἔστ’* is elided *ἔστι*, the accented existential present of *εἰμί*, 3rd singular. Local *ἄνω* ‘above’ stands for *nel cielo*.
- *ὅπως τραχεῖαν τήνδ’ ὁδόν τινι δείξω* specifies the content or object of that will in the form of a final clause. *δείξω* is here the 1st singular aorist active subjunctive of *δείκνυμι* after *ὅπως* (identical in spelling to the future indicative). The ending alone supplies Italian *io*. Musurus fronts the accusative object, ‘this rugged way’: *τραχεῖαν* is the feminine accusative singular of *τραχύς*, shifting *silvestro* from wildness to roughness; *τήνδ’* is elided *τήνδε*, the corresponding form of *ὅδε*. Dative singular indefinite *τινι* gives *altrui* as ‘to someone’, without explicitly contrasting that person with the speaker.

## Lines 85–117 — The Lowered Hooks and the Broken Bridge

> 85 Allor gli fu l' orgoglio sì caduto  
> 86 che si lasciò cascar l' uncino ai piedi,  

Then was his pride so fallen, that he let the hook drop at his feet,

> 85 Οὕτω τότ᾽ αὐτοῦ τὸ θράσος κατεβλήθη,  
> (So then was his arrogance cast down,)
>
> 86 Ὥστ᾽ ἀφῆκε τοῖς ποσὶ πεσεῖν τὴν ἅρπην·  
> (That he let his hook fall at his feet;)

- *Οὕτω τότ᾽ αὐτοῦ τὸ θράσος κατεβλήθη* ‘so then his insolence was cast down’ brings *sì* forward as *οὕτω*, anticipating *ὥστε*. *τότ᾽* is *τότε* elided before *αὐτοῦ*. The possessive genitive *αὐτοῦ*, placed before the article and noun, replaces the Italian possessive dative *gli*. *θράσος* suggests effrontery rather than simply *orgoglio*; *κατεβλήθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *καταβάλλω*) makes the pride something brought low, whereas in *fu … caduto* the pride itself has fallen.
- *Ὥστ᾽ ἀφῆκε* ‘so that he let’ has elided *ὥστε* with the indicative of an actual result. *ἀφῆκε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀφίημι*) takes *πεσεῖν* (2nd aor. act. inf. of *πίπτω*); the postponed *τὴν ἅρπην* ‘the hook’ is what he releases and the subject of ‘fall’. The Greek retains the two verbs of *si lasciò cascar* without a reflexive. *τοῖς ποσὶ* (dat. pl. of *πούς*) marks the place of the fall; as in *ai piedi*, the possessor of the feet is understood.

> 87 e disse agli altri: "Omai non sia feruto."  

and said to the others: "Now strike him not!"

> 87 Τοῖς δ᾽ ἄλλοις εἶπε· “Μηδεὶς αὐτὸν βαλέτω.”  
> (And to the others he said: “Let no one strike him.”)

- *Τοῖς δ᾽ ἄλλοις εἶπε* puts the recipients first; *δ᾽* is postpositive *δέ* elided, and *εἶπε* is the 3rd sg. 2nd aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*. *Μηδεὶς αὐτὸν βαλέτω* ‘let no one strike him’ recasts the passive jussive *non sia feruto* as an active command: *μηδείς* is its nominative subject, *αὐτόν* its accusative object, and *βαλέτω* the 3rd sg. 2nd aor. act. imperative of *βάλλω*. There is no separate rendering of *omai*.

> 88 E il duca mio a me: "O tu, che siedi  
> 89 tra gli scheggion del ponte quatto quatto,  
> 90 sicuramente omai a me ti riedi."  

And my Guide to me: "O thou that sittest cowering, cowering amongst the great splinters of the bridge, securely now return to me!"

> 88 Πρὸς ἐμὲ δ᾽ ὁ Προστάτης· “Ὦ σὺ νῦν, ὅστις  
> (And my Protector to me: “O you now, who)
>
> 89 „Ἐν μυχοῖς τῆς γεφύρας κάθησαι πτήσσων,  
> („Sit crouching in the recesses of the bridge,)
>
> 90 „Ἐπάνελθε πρός μ᾽ ἀσφαλῶς ἄνευ φόβου.”  
> („Come back to me safely without fear.”)

- *Πρὸς ἐμὲ δ᾽ ὁ Προστάτης* reverses *il duca mio a me*, retaining the absence of a verb of speaking. *πρός* takes the accusative of the person addressed; *ὁ Προστάτης* ‘the protector’ replaces *il duca mio*, without an expressed possessive. *Ὦ σὺ νῦν, ὅστις* gives the pronoun of address a fuller relative, *ὅστις* (nom. sg. masc.), whose verb, *κάθησαι*, is 2nd sg. present indicative of *κάθημαι*, agreeing in person with the addressee. *νῦν* anticipates the later *omai*.
- *Ἐν μυχοῖς τῆς γεφύρας* ‘in recesses of the bridge’ substitutes places of concealment for the pieces of rock in *tra gli scheggion del ponte*. *πτήσσων* (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *πτήσσω*) describes how the addressee sits; a single verb of cowering takes the place of doubled *quatto quatto*.
- *Ἐπάνελθε* (2nd sg. 2nd aor. act. imperative of *ἐπανέρχομαι*) expresses the return in *ti riedi* without a reflexive. *πρός μ᾽* is *πρός* with elided accusative *με*. Greek expands *sicuramente* into *ἀσφαλῶς ἄνευ φόβου* ‘safely, without fear’, with *ἄνευ* governing the genitive.

> 91 Per ch' io mi mossi, ed a lui venni ratto;  

Whereat I moved, and quickly came to him;

> 91 Τότε κινηθεὶς πρὸς αὐτὸν ἦλθον τάχος·  
> (Then moving forward I came to him swiftly;)

- *Τότε* ‘then’ makes *per ch’* temporal rather than explicitly consequential. *κινηθείς* (nom. sg. masc. aor. pass. participle of *κινέω*) has intransitive force, ‘having moved’, and subordinates *mi mossi* to *ἦλθον* (1st sg. 2nd aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*). *πρὸς αὐτόν* gives the goal; *τάχος*, a neuter singular accusative used adverbially, keeps the emphatic final position of *ratto*.

> 92 e i diavoli si fecer tutti avanti,  
> 93 sì ch' io temetti ch' ei tenesser patto.  

and the Devils all pressed forward, so that I feared they might not hold the compact.

> 92 Οἱ δαίμονες δ᾽ ὥρμησαν κατ᾽ ἐμοῦ πάντες,  
> (And all the demons rushed forward against me,)
>
> 93 Ὥστ᾽ ἐφοβήθην, μὴ πιστὰ παραβῶσιν.  
> (So that I feared they would break their pledged faith.)

- *Οἱ δαίμονες δ᾽ ὥρμησαν κατ᾽ ἐμοῦ πάντες* puts *πάντες* at the end, in nominative agreement with *δαίμονες*. *ὥρμησαν* (3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *ὁρμάω*, with temporal augment) gives *si fecer … avanti* the force of a rush; *κατ᾽ ἐμοῦ* (*κατά* with genitive), ‘against me’, supplies a hostile direction not explicit in *avanti*.
- *Ὥστ᾽ ἐφοβήθην* again uses *ὥστε* with an indicative of result. *ἐφοβήθην* is 1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *φοβέω*, in the sense ‘I grew afraid’. After it *μή* introduces the event feared, not a negation of that event. *παραβῶσιν* (3rd pl. 2nd aor. act. subj. of *παραβαίνω*) retains the subjunctive after a past main verb rather than shifting to the optative. *πιστά*, substantival neuter plural accusative, means ‘pledges’: Musurus interprets *temetti ch’ ei tenesser patto* as fear of their **breaking** faith, not fear of their keeping it.

> 94 E così vid' io già temer li fanti  
> 95 ch' uscivan patteggiati di Caprona,  
> 96 veggendo sè tra nimici cotanti.  

And thus once I saw the footmen, who marched out under treaty from Caprona, fear at seeing themselves among so many enemies.

> 94 Οὕτως εἶδον τρέμοντας τοὺς ὑποσπόνδως  
> (Thus I saw trembling those who under truce)
>
> 95 Ἐξελθόντας ποτ᾽ ἤδη φρουροὺς Καπρώνης,  
> (Once marched out of the garrison of Caprona,)
>
> 96 Ὅτ᾽ ἔβλεπον ἑαυτοὺς ἐν ἐχθρῶν μέσῳ.  
> (When they saw themselves in the midst of enemies.)

- *Οὕτως εἶδον τρέμοντας* ‘thus I saw … trembling’ uses the accusative participle after a verb of direct perception for Italian *vid’ … temer*. *εἶδον* is here 1st sg. 2nd aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*; *τρέμοντας* (acc. pl. masc. pres. act. participle of *τρέμω*) agrees with the eventual object *φρουρούς*. The Greek gives visible trembling for the Italian verb of fearing.
- *τοὺς ὑποσπόνδως ἐξελθόντας ποτ᾽ ἤδη φρουροὺς Καπρώνης* places the participial description between article and noun. *ἐξελθόντας* (acc. pl. masc. 2nd aor. act. participle of *ἐξέρχομαι*) presents the departure as accomplished, unlike imperfect *uscivan*; *ὑποσπόνδως* ‘under truce’ gives the condition *patteggiati* as an adverb. *ποτ᾽ ἤδη* (*ποτε* elided) shifts the indication of former time from the seeing in *vid’ io già* to the departure. *φρουροὺς Καπρώνης* ‘guards of Caprona’ makes *fanti* guards rather than specifically foot soldiers; the genitive can attach to *φρουρούς* rather than marking the point of departure as explicitly as *di Caprona*.
- *Ὅτ᾽ ἔβλεπον* ‘when they saw’ replaces the gerund *veggendo* with elided *ὅτε* and a 3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. *ἑαυτούς* is the direct reflexive object. *ἐν ἐχθρῶν μέσῳ* ‘in the midst of enemies’ puts the genitive *ἐχθρῶν* before substantival dative *μέσῳ*; it conveys surrounding enemies, but not explicitly *cotanti*.

> 97 Io m' accostai con tutta la persona  
> 98 lungo il mio duca, e non torceva gli occhi  
> 99 dalla sembianza lor ch' era non buona.  

I drew near my Guide with my whole body, and turned not away my eyes from the look of them, which was not good.

> 97 Προσεκολλήθην ὅλῳ τῷ σώματί μου  
> (I clung close with my whole body)
>
> 98 Πρὸς τὸν ἐμὸν Προστάτην, οὐ παρεκκλίνας  
> (To my Protector, not turning aside)
>
> 99 Τὰς ὄψεις ἐκ τῆς αὐτῶν ὑπόπτου θέας.  
> (My eyes from their suspicious aspect.)

- *Προσεκολλήθην* (1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *προσκολλάω*, augmented after the prefix) is stronger than *m’ accostai*: ‘I pressed close’, literally ‘I was glued to’. *ὅλῳ τῷ σώματί μου* is a dative of means or respect, ‘with my whole body’, for *con tutta la persona*. Greek supplies the possessive *μου*. *πρὸς τὸν ἐμὸν Προστάτην* marks the person approached rather than position *lungo il mio duca*; *ἐμόν* is the attributive possessive adjective.
- *οὐ παρεκκλίνας* replaces coordinated imperfect *e non torceva* with a negative attendant participle (nom. sg. masc. aor. act. of *παρεκκλίνω*), ‘without turning aside’. *τὰς ὄψεις*, literally ‘the sights’ or ‘eyes’, is its direct object; *ἐκ* with the genitive marks what the gaze does not leave.
- *τῆς αὐτῶν ὑπόπτου θέας* ‘their suspect appearance’ draws both possessive *αὐτῶν* and attributive *ὑπόπτου* into the noun phrase. Instead of the relative clause and understatement *ch’ era non buona*, the adjective identifies the appearance as grounds for suspicion.

> 100 Ei chinavan gli raffi, e "Vuoi che 'l tocchi,"  
> 101 diceva l' un con l' altro, "in sul groppone?"  
> 102 e rispondean: "Sì, fa che gliele accocchi."  

They lowered their drag-hooks, and kept saying to one another: "Shall I touch him on the rump?" and answering: "Yes, see thou nick it for him."

> 100 Ἔκλιναν ἅρπας, καὶ — “Βαλεῖν μ᾽ αὐτὸν βούλει,”  
> (They lowered their hooks, and—“Do you want me to strike him,”)
>
> 101 Ἔλεγον πρὸς ἀλλήλους, “ἐπὶ τῶν νώτων;”  
> (They said to one another, “upon the back?”)
>
> 102 Κἀπεκρίνοντο· “Ναί, διακοντισθήτω.” —  
> (And they answered: “Yes, let him be pierced through.”—)

- *Ἔκλιναν ἅρπας* ‘they lowered hooks’ omits both the subject pronoun *ei* and the article of *gli raffi*. *ἔκλιναν* is 3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *κλίνω*, presenting as a single action what *chinavan* gives in the imperfect. *ἅρπας* is the accusative plural corresponding to singular *ἅρπην* above.
- *Βαλεῖν μ᾽ αὐτὸν βούλει* ‘do you want me to strike him?’ fronts the 2nd aor. act. infinitive of *βάλλω* and postpones *βούλει* (2nd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *βούλομαι*). Elided *με* is the accusative subject of the infinitive and *αὐτόν* its object: an accusative and infinitive replaces *che ’l tocchi*, and ‘strike’ makes *tocchi* more explicit. *ἐπὶ τῶν νώτων*, with the genitive plural of *νῶτον*, names the back rather than specifically the rump.
- *Ἔλεγον πρὸς ἀλλήλους* (3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *λέγω* with reciprocal accusative) replaces singular *diceva l’ un con l’ altro*. *Κἀπεκρίνοντο* is crasis of *καὶ ἀπεκρίνοντο* (3rd pl. imperf. mid. ind. of *ἀποκρίνομαι*). After *ναί*, *διακοντισθήτω* ‘let him be pierced through’ is a 3rd sg. aor. pass. imperative of *διακοντίζω*: it makes the recipient the subject and suppresses the second-person agent and the periphrasis *fa che gliele accocchi*.

> 103 Ma quel demonio, che tenea sermone  
> 104 col duca mio, si volse tutto presto  
> 105 e disse: "Posa, posa, Scarmiglione."  

But that Demon, who [was speaking] with my Guide, turned instant round, and said: "Quiet, quiet, Scarmiglione!"

> 103 Ἀλλ᾽ ὁ δαίμων, ὃς μετὰ τοὐμοῦ Προστάτου  
> (But that demon, who with my Protector)
>
> 104 Συνδιελέχθη, στραφεὶς εὐθέως εἶπεν·  
> (Was conversing, turning straightway said:)
>
> 105 “Ἔχ᾽ ἀτρέμας, ἀτρέμα, Σκαρμιλιόνη.”  
> (“Hold still, still, Scarmiglione.”)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ ὁ δαίμων* gives elided *ἀλλά* and the definite article for *ma quel demonio*. In *ὃς μετὰ τοὐμοῦ Προστάτου συνδιελέχθη*, *ὅς* is nominative subject; *μετά* with the genitive marks the interlocutor. *τοὐμοῦ* is crasis of *τοῦ ἐμοῦ*. *συνδιελέχθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *συνδιαλέγομαι*, passive in form but meaning ‘conversed’) sums up the conversation instead of marking it as ongoing like *tenea sermone*.
- *στραφεὶς εὐθέως εἶπεν* subordinates *si volse* to *e disse*: *στραφείς* (nom. sg. masc. 2nd aor. pass. participle of *στρέφω*) means ‘having turned’. *εὐθέως* stands between participle and finite verb, so that the immediacy of *tutto presto* can apply to the response as a whole.
- *Ἔχ᾽ ἀτρέμας, ἀτρέμα* uses elided *ἔχε* (2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *ἔχω*) intransitively with an adverb, ‘keep still’. In place of the identical imperatives *posa, posa*, the adverb is repeated in its variant forms, with and without final *-ς*. *Σκαρμιλιόνη* is the name in direct address.

> 106 Poi disse a noi: "Più oltre andar per questo  
> 107 iscoglio non si può, però che giace  
> 108 tutto spezzato al fondo l' arco sesto;  

Then he said to us: "To go farther by this cliff will not be possible: for the sixth arch lies all in fragments at the bottom;

> 106 Εἶτ᾽ εἶπεν ἡμῖν· “Διὰ γεφύρας τῆσδε  
> (Then he said to us: “Over this bridge)
>
> 107 „ Προβῆναι πέραν οὐκ ἔξεστι, τῆς ἕκτης  
> („ It is not possible to go farther across, since the sixth)
>
> 108 „ Ἁψῖδος ὅλης εἰς βυθὸν συμπεσούσης.  
> („ Arch has fallen wholly into the depths.)

- *Εἶτ᾽ εἶπεν ἡμῖν* has elided *εἶτα* ‘then’ and a dative of the addressees. *Διὰ γεφύρας τῆσδε* ‘by way of this bridge’ names as a bridge the formation called *questo iscoglio*. *διά* governs the genitive; the postposed demonstrative *τῆσδε* (gen. sg. fem. of *ὅδε*) agrees with *γεφύρας*, which here has no article.
- *Προβῆναι πέραν οὐκ ἔξεστι* ‘to advance farther is not possible’ retains the impersonal construction and present tense of *andar … non si può*. *προβῆναι* is the 2nd aor. act. infinitive of *προβαίνω*, with adverbial *πέραν*; *ἔξεστι* is impersonal, ‘it is open to one’, here of practical possibility.
- *τῆς ἕκτης ἁψῖδος ὅλης εἰς βυθὸν συμπεσούσης* is a causal genitive absolute for *però che* with a finite verb. *συμπεσούσης* (gen. sg. fem. 2nd aor. act. participle of *συμπίπτω*) agrees with *ἁψῖδος* (gen. of *ἁψίς*); *ὅλης* describes the arch in its entirety. Greek gives a completed collapse *εἰς βυθόν* ‘into the depths’, not the resulting position *giace … al fondo* or explicitly the fragmentation *tutto spezzato*.

> 109 e se l' andare avanti pur vi piace,  
> 110 andatevene su per questa grotta;  
> 111 presso è un altro scoglio che via face.  

and if it please you still to go onward, go along this [ridge]: near at hand is another cliff which forms a path.

> 109 „ Ἀλλ᾽ εἴ γ᾽ ἰέναι βούλεσθε ποῤῥωτέρω,  
> („ But if indeed you wish to go farther on,)
>
> 110 „ Ὑπάγετ᾽ ἄνω διὰ σπηλαίου τοῦδε·  
> („ Go up through this cavern:)
>
> 111 „ Ἐγγὺς γέφυρα πρὸς ὁδόν ἐστιν ἄλλη.  
> („ Near at hand is another bridge that gives a way.)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ εἴ γ᾽ ἰέναι βούλεσθε ποῤῥωτέρω* ‘but if indeed you wish to go farther’ makes *e* adversative. Restrictive or emphatic *γε*, elided after *εἰ*, gives force to the proviso rather than translating *pur* by a temporal ‘still’. Personal *βούλεσθε* (2nd pl. pres. mid. ind. of *βούλομαι*) takes *ἰέναι* (pres. act. inf. of *εἶμι*, ‘go’), replacing the infinitival subject and dative experiencer of *l’ andare … vi piace*. *ποῤῥωτέρω* is comparative ‘farther’, with the traditional breathings on doubled rho.
- *Ὑπάγετ᾽ ἄνω* is elided *ὑπάγετε* (2nd pl. pres. act. imperative of *ὑπάγω*) with directional *ἄνω* for *su*. *διὰ σπηλαίου τοῦδε* takes *per questa grotta* as ‘through this cavern’, not in the sense of “along this [ridge]”. The postposed demonstrative is gen. sg. neut., agreeing with *σπηλαίου*.
- *Ἐγγὺς γέφυρα πρὸς ὁδόν ἐστιν ἄλλη* retains initial *presso* as adverbial *ἐγγύς*, but again translates *scoglio* by *γέφυρα*. *ἄλλη* agrees with *γέφυρα* across the intervening phrase. *πρὸς ὁδόν* ‘for passage’ gives the bridge’s use by *πρός* with the accusative, instead of the active relative clause *che via face*.

> 112 Ier, più oltre cinqu' ore che quest' otta,  
> 113 mille dugento con sessanta sei  
> 114 anni compiè che qui la via fu rotta.  

Yesterday, five hours later than this hour, [completed] a thousand two hundred and sixty-six years since the way here was broken.

> 112 „ Χθές, ὥρας πέντε μετὰ τὴν ὥραν ταύτην,  
> („ Yesterday, five hours later than this hour,)
>
> 113 „ Ἔτη χίλια πρὸς τοῖς διακοσίοις  
> („ Years one thousand two hundred)
>
> 114 „ Ἑξηκονταὲξ παρῆλθ’, ἀφ’ οὗ πᾶς πόρος  
> („ And sixty-six passed since every passage)

- *Χθές, ὥρας πέντε μετὰ τὴν ὥραν ταύτην* follows the Italian order closely. Accusative *ὥρας πέντε* measures the interval; temporal *μετά* with the accusative fixes its starting point. Together they give ‘five hours after this hour’ for the comparative *più oltre cinqu’ ore che quest’ otta*.
- *Ἔτη χίλια πρὸς τοῖς διακοσίοις ἑξηκονταὲξ παρῆλθ᾽* makes ‘years’ the subject. *χίλια* agrees with neuter plural *ἔτη* (from *ἔτος*); additive *πρός* with dative *τοῖς διακοσίοις* gives ‘in addition to the two hundred’, followed by the indeclinable *ἑξηκονταέξ* ‘sixty-six’, printed as one word. *παρῆλθ᾽* is elided *παρῆλθε* (3rd sg. 2nd aor. act. ind. of *παρέρχομαι*), ‘elapsed’, with the normal singular verb for a neuter plural subject, rather than a verb meaning *compiè* ‘completed’.
- *ἀφ᾽ οὗ* ‘since when’, idiomatically ‘ever since’, makes the temporal relation of *che* explicit; *ἀπό* elides and its final consonant is aspirated before the rough breathing. The subject *πᾶς πόρος* ‘every passage’ expands *la via*. Its predicate runs into Greek line 115: *ὧδε κέκλεισται* ‘here stands closed’. *κέκλεισται* (3rd sg. perf. mid./pass. ind. of *κλείω*, passive in sense) emphasizes the continuing closure instead of the past event *fu rotta*.

> 115 Io mando verso là di questi miei  
> 116 a riguardar s' alcun se ne sciorina;  

Thitherward I send some of these my men, to look if any one be out airing himself;

> 115 „ Ὧδε κέκλεισται. Τινὰς τῶν ἐμῶν πέμπω  
> („ Here was closed. Some of my band I am sending)
>
> 116 „ Ἐκεῖσ’, ὅπως ἴδωσι, μή τις ψυχάζῃ.  
> („ Thither, to see if any one is cooling himself.)

- After completing the preceding clause, Greek fronts the object *Τινὰς τῶν ἐμῶν* ‘some of my own’. *τινάς* is the acc. pl. of indefinite *τις*, with the partitive genitive of the substantivized possessive *οἱ ἐμοί*. This supplies a pronoun for the unexpressed ‘some’ in *di questi miei* but drops the demonstrative. *πέμπω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.) expresses *io mando* without a subject pronoun; *ἐκεῖσ᾽*, elided *ἐκεῖσε* ‘thither’, postpones the direction *verso là* until after the verb.
- *ὅπως ἴδωσι* replaces *a riguardar* with a purpose clause: *ἴδωσι* is 3rd pl. 2nd aor. act. subj. of *ὁράω*, its subject the people sent. *μή τις ψυχάζῃ* after ‘see’ gives a watchful ‘lest anyone …’ or ‘whether anyone may …’, less neutral than *s’ alcun*. *ψυχάζῃ* (3rd sg. pres. act. subj. of *ψυχάζω*) presents the ongoing act of taking the air or cooling off; there are no separate equivalents of the pronominal elements in *se ne sciorina*.

> 117 gite con lor, ch' ei non saranno rei."  

go with them, for they will not be treacherous."

> 117 „ Ἴτε σὺν αὐτοῖς· οὐ ποιήσουσι βλάβην.”  
> („ Go with them; they will do no harm.”)

- *Ἴτε σὺν αὐτοῖς* closely follows *gite con lor*: *ἴτε* is 2nd pl. pres. act. imperative of *εἶμι*, and *σύν* takes the associative dative.
- *οὐ ποιήσουσι βλάβην* ‘they will not do harm’ leaves causal *ch’* to be inferred from juxtaposition. *ποιήσουσι* (3rd pl. fut. act. ind. of *ποιέω*) preserves the future but replaces *saranno rei*, a prediction about what they will **be**, with a prediction about what they will **do**; accusative *βλάβην* names the harm without specifying treachery.

## Lines 118–139 — The Muster of the Ten and Their Signal

> 118 “Tratti avanti, Alichino e Calcabrina,”  
> 119 cominciò egli a dire, “e tu, Cagnazzo;  

"Draw forward, Alichino and Calcabrina," he then began to say, "and thou, Cagnazzo;

> 118 — “Πρόβα νῦν, Ἀλικῖνε καὶ Καλκαβρίνα,”  
> (—“Step forward now, Alichino and Calcabrina,”)
>
> 119 Οὗτος ἤρξατο λέγων, “καὶ σύ, Κογνάζε·  
> (He began saying, “and you, Cagnazzo;)

- *Πρόβα* is the 2nd sg. aor. active imperative of *προβαίνω*, ‘step forward’; the prefix *προ-* does the work of *avanti*. Musurus makes *Tratti* a direct command and adds *νῦν* ‘now’. The imperative is singular despite the two names joined by *καί*: it addresses the first by name and draws the second into the same command, rather than using a plural or dual.
- *Ἀλικῖνε* and *Κογνάζε* are vocatives in *-ε*, as of masculine names in *-ος*; *Καλκαβρίνα* retains the Italian final vowel in address. In *καὶ σύ, Κογνάζε*, *σύ* is the emphatic nominative subject of the understood imperative, distinct in construction from the vocative, just as in *e tu, Cagnazzo*.
- *Οὗτος ἤρξατο λέγων* puts the subject first: ‘this one began speaking’ for *cominciò egli a dire*. *ἤρξατο* is 3rd sg. aor. middle indicative of *ἄρχομαι*, with temporal augment; *λέγων* is the present active participle of *λέγω*, nom. masc. sg., supplementary to ‘began’ where the Italian has an infinitive.

> 120 e Barbariccia guidi la decina.  

and let Barbariccia lead the ten.

> 120 „ Ὁδήγει τὴν δεκάδα, Βαρβαρικία·  
> („ Lead the band of ten, Barbariccia;)

- Italian *e Barbariccia guidi la decina* has a subject and a 3rd sg. jussive subjunctive. Greek drops the connective and turns the subject into a final vocative, *Βαρβαρικία*: *Ὁδήγει* is the 2nd sg. present active imperative of the contract verb *ὁδηγέω*, ‘lead’. *τὴν δεκάδα* (acc. sg. of *δεκάς*, stem *δεκαδ-*) keeps the collective force of *la decina*: one body of ten, not simply the numeral ‘ten’.

> 121 Libicocco vegna oltre, e Draghignazzo,  
> 122 Ciriatto sannuto, e Graffiacane,  
> 123 e Farfarello, e Rubicante il pazzo.  

Let Libicocco come besides, and Draghignazzo, tusked Ciriatto, and Graffiacane, and Farfarello, and furious Rubicante.

> 121 „ Ἐλθέτω Λιβίκοκκος καὶ Δραγιγνάζος,  
> („ Let Libicocco come and Draghignazzo,)
>
> 122 „ Καριάττος προώδων καὶ Γραφικάνης  
> („ Tusked Ciriatto and Graffiacane,)
>
> 123 „ Καὶ Φαρφαρέλλος καὶ μωρὸς Ῥουβικάντης.  
> („ And Farfarello and mad Rubicante.)

- *Ἐλθέτω*, 3rd sg. second aor. active imperative of *ἔρχομαι*, matches the jussive *vegna* more closely than the preceding 2nd-person command matches *guidi*. There is no separate word for *oltre*. The singular verb precedes and agrees with the first of a series of nominative subjects; it can be understood afresh with each name.
- *Λιβίκοκκος*, *Δραγιγνάζος*, *Καριάττος* and *Φαρφαρέλλος* have nominatives in *-ος*, *Γραφικάνης* and *Ῥουβικάντης* in *-ης*. The repeated *καί* largely preserves the Italian coordination; initial *Ῥ-* in the last name has the conventional rough breathing of initial rho.
- *προώδων* ‘with projecting teeth’ is a nom. masc. sg. compound adjective agreeing with *Καριάττος* and following it like *sannuto*. By contrast, *μωρός* ‘foolish, senseless’ precedes *Ῥουβικάντης*, without an article: *il pazzo* becomes a descriptive adjective rather than an appended, articular epithet.

> 124 Cercate intorno le boglienti pane;  

Search around the boiling glue;

> 124 „ Περιέλθετε τὰς βραζούσας ἀσφάλτους·  
> („ Search round about the boiling pitch;)

- *Περιέλθετε* is the 2nd pl. second aor. active imperative of *περιέρχομαι*. Instead of a verb of searching qualified by *intorno*, the Greek says ‘go round’, with the place traversed in the accusative.
- *τὰς βραζούσας ἀσφάλτους* keeps the feminine plural of *le boglienti pane*. *βραζούσας* is the present active participle of *βράζω*, acc. fem. pl., attributive between article and noun: the pitch is itself boiling. *ἄσφαλτος* specifies pitch or bitumen rather than glue; the plural treats it as stretches or masses of the substance.

> 125 costor sien salvi insino all’ altro scheggio,  
> 126 che tutto intero va sopra le tane.”  

be these two safe [so far as] the other crag, which all unbroken goes across the dens."

> 125 „ Οὗτοι δ’ ἔστωσαν σῶοι μέχρι γεφύρας  
> („ And let these be safe as far as the bridge,)
>
> 126 „ Ἄλλης, ἐντελοῦς οὔσης ἐπὶ κευθμώνων.” —  
> („ Another one, which is unbroken over the chasms.”—)

- *Οὗτοι δ’ ἔστωσαν σῶοι* ‘and let these be safe’ follows *costor sien salvi*. *οὗτοι* is nom. masc. pl., not a dual or a numeral; *δ’* is postpositive *δέ* elided before *ἔστωσαν*. The 3rd pl. present imperative of *εἰμί* replaces the jussive subjunctive *sien*; *σῶοι* is the predicate adjective, nom. masc. pl. of *σῶος*.
- *μέχρι γεφύρας ἄλλης* ‘as far as another bridge’ has *μέχρι* governing the genitive, with *ἄλλης* delayed to the next line. For *all’ altro scheggio* Musurus names the crossing by its function, *γέφυρα*, rather than its material form, and uses no definite article.
- *ἐντελοῦς οὔσης ἐπὶ κευθμώνων* ‘being complete over hollows’ replaces *che tutto intero va sopra le tane*. *οὔσης*, the present participle of *εἰμί*, and its predicate *ἐντελοῦς* (from *ἐντελής*) are gen. fem. sg. in agreement with *γεφύρας*: this is a participial description of the governed noun, not an independent genitive absolute. Static ‘being’ replaces *va*; *ἐπί* with the gen. pl. of *κευθμών* locates the bridge above the recesses.

> 127 “O me! maestro, che è quel che io veggio?”  
> 128 diss’ io; “deh! senza scorta andiamci soli,  
> 129 se tu sai ir, ch’ io per me non la chieggio.  

"Oh me! Master, what is this that I see?" said I; "ah, without escort let us go alone, if thou knowest the way; for as to me, I seek it not!

> 127 “Οἴμοι, Προστάτα ! τί τόδ’ ἐστιν, ὃ βλέπω ;”  
> (“Alas, Protector! what is this that I see?”)
>
> 128 Εἶπον· “Ἄγωμεν ἄνευ συνόδων μόνοι·  
> (I said: “Let us go alone without escorts;)
>
> 129 „ Τὴν ὁδὸν οἶδας· οὐ ζητῶ δι’ ἔμ’ ἄλλους.  
> („ You know the way; I seek no others for myself.)

- *Οἴμοι* is the exclamation ‘alas for me’, containing the dative *μοι*. *Προστάτα*, voc. sg. of *προστάτης*, interprets *maestro* as ‘protector’. In *τί τόδ’ ἐστιν, ὃ βλέπω;* interrogative *τί* asks ‘what?’ of the demonstrative subject *τόδ’* (neut. sg. *τόδε*, elided). Greek points to ‘this’ rather than Italian *quel*. The relative *ὃ* is neut. acc. sg., object of *βλέπω*, 1st sg. present active indicative.
- *Εἶπον*, 1st sg. second aor. active indicative ‘I said’, needs no separate *io*. *Ἄγωμεν* is a 1st pl. present active hortatory subjunctive of *ἄγω*, used without an object, ‘let us go’; there is no separate *deh* or equivalent of the *-ci* in *andiamci*. *ἄνευ* governs *συνόδων*, gen. pl. of *σύνοδος* ‘companion’, replacing collective *scorta* with persons. *μόνοι* is predicative, nom. masc. pl., agreeing with the unexpressed ‘we’.
- *Τὴν ὁδὸν οἶδας* makes *se tu sai ir* an assertion, ‘you know the way’, with a noun object in place of *ir*. *οἶδας* is a 2nd sg. perfect indicative with present sense, the expanded form beside Attic *οἶσθα*. In *οὐ ζητῶ δι’ ἔμ’ ἄλλους*, *ζητῶ* is contracted from *ζητέω*; the two elisions are of *διά* before *ἐμέ* and of *ἐμέ* before *ἄλλους*. *δι’ ἔμ’* means ‘on my account’; *ἄλλους*, substantival acc. masc. pl., gives ‘others’ as the object instead of the pronoun *la* referring to the escort. The explanatory *ch’* is left to be understood from the juxtaposition.

> 130 Se tu sei sì accorto come suoli,  
> 131 non vedi tu ch’ ei digrignan li denti,  
> 132 e con le ciglia ne minaccian duoli?”  

If thou beest so wary, as thou art wont, dost thou not see how they grin[d] their teeth, and with their brows threaten mischief to us?"

> 130 „ Εἰ, καθάπερ εἴωθας, πρόνους τυγχάνεις,  
> („ If, as is your wont, you are watchful,)
>
> 131 „ Οὐχ ὁρᾷς τούτων τοὺς βρυγμοὺς τῶν ὀδόντων  
> („ Do you not see their gnashing of teeth)
>
> 132 „ Καὶ τὰς ἀπειλὰς καὶ νεύσεις τῶν ὀμμάτων ;”  
> („ And the threats and beckonings of their eyes?”)

- *Εἰ … πρόνους τυγχάνεις* retains an indicative condition: *τυγχάνεις* is 2nd sg. present active of *τυγχάνω*, here with the predicate adjective *πρόνους* ‘foreseeing, prudent’ (contracted nom. masc. sg. of *πρόνοος*), ‘if you prove to be prudent’. *καθάπερ εἴωθας* ‘just as you are accustomed’ is inserted between *εἰ* and its predicate. *εἴωθας* is 2nd sg. perfect active indicative of *ἔθω*, denoting an established habit. There is no separate degree word for *sì*.
- *Οὐχ ὁρᾷς* ‘do you not see?’ expects assent; *οὐχ* is the form of the negative before a rough-breathed vowel, and *ὁρᾷς* is 2nd sg. present active indicative of contracted *ὁράω*. Where *non vedi tu ch’ ei digrignan* introduces a finite clause, Greek gives ‘see’ accusative noun objects. *τούτων* ‘of these’ identifies the possessors; *τοὺς βρυγμοὺς τῶν ὀδόντων* turns the action into ‘the gnashings of the teeth’, with gen. pl. *ὀδόντων* instead of the Italian direct object.
- *τὰς ἀπειλὰς καὶ νεύσεις τῶν ὀμμάτων* adds ‘the threats and signals of the eyes’ as further objects of sight. The article serves both feminine accusative plurals. The instrumental *con le ciglia* becomes a genitive of the bodily source of the gestures, ‘of the eyes’ (*ὀμμάτων*, from *ὄμμα*), not ‘with the brows’. The threatened pains, *duoli*, and the persons threatened, *ne*, are not separately expressed.

> 133 Ed egli a me: “Non vo’ che tu paventi;  

And he to me: "I would not have thee be afraid;

> 133 Πρὸς ἐμέ δ᾽ αὐτός· “Οὐ θέλω σε φοβεῖσθαι·  
> (And he to me: “I do not wish you to fear;)

- *Πρὸς ἐμέ δ᾽ αὐτός* reverses *Ed egli a me*, putting the accented pronoun *ἐμέ* after *πρός* + accusative first, and the emphatic subject *αὐτός* last. *δ᾽* is elided *δέ*; as in Italian, the verb of answering is understood.
- *Οὐ θέλω σε φοβεῖσθαι* replaces *Non vo’ che tu paventi* with an accusative-and-infinitive complement after *θέλω*, 1st sg. present active indicative: *σε* is the subject of *φοβεῖσθαι*, present middle/passive infinitive of *φοβέω*, here in the sense of *φοβέομαι* ‘be afraid’.

> 134 lasciali digrignar pure a lor senno,  
> 135 ch’ ei fanno ciò per li lessi dolenti.”  

let them grin[d] on at their will: for they do it at the boiled wretches."

> 134 „Ἄφες, ὅσον βούλονται, βρύχειν ὀδόντας·  
> („Let them gnash their teeth as much as they will;)
>
> 135 „Ποιοῦσι τοῦτο πρὸς τοὺς ἀσφαλτοβράστους.”  
> („They do this at the pitch-boiled ones.”)

- *Ἄφες* is 2nd sg. aor. active imperative of *ἀφίημι*, ‘allow’. *βρύχειν* is the present active infinitive of *βρύχω*, denoting the continuing action permitted; its object *ὀδόντας* (acc. pl.) restores ‘teeth’ explicitly. The infinitive's personal subject, expressed by *-li* in *lasciali*, is understood.
- *ὅσον βούλονται* ‘as much as they wish’ replaces *pure a lor senno* with a clause of extent inserted before the infinitive. *ὅσον* is adverbial neut. acc. sg.; *βούλονται* is 3rd pl. present middle indicative of *βούλομαι*. There is no independent equivalent for permissive *pure*.
- *Ποιοῦσι τοῦτο* ‘they do this’ closely follows *ei fanno ciò*: *ποιοῦσι* is contracted 3rd pl. present active indicative of *ποιέω*, with subject implicit. Greek omits the causal connective *ch’*. *πρὸς τοὺς ἀσφαλτοβράστους* makes *per* directional, ‘towards, at’; the article turns the compound adjective into a designation of persons, acc. masc. pl. ‘the pitch-boiled’. The compound supplies what they are boiled in, but has no separate word for *dolenti*.

> 136 Per l’ argine sinistro volta dienno;  

By the sinister bank they turned;

> 136 Ἐπ᾽ ἀριστερὰν ἐτράπησαν τοῦ χείλους·  
> (Along the left of the bank they turned;)

- *Ἐπ᾽ ἀριστεράν* is *ἐπί* elided before the feminine accusative *ἀριστεράν*, used in the directional expression ‘to the left’ (understand ‘side’ or ‘hand’). Italian *sinistro* modifies *argine* directly; Greek detaches the direction from the noun and adds *τοῦ χείλους*, gen. sg. of neuter *χεῖλος* ‘lip, edge’, specifying the bank whose left side is meant.
- *ἐτράπησαν* is 3rd pl. second aor. passive indicative of *τρέπω*, with augment and the passive aorist in *-η-* rather than *-θη-*. It is intransitive in sense, ‘they turned’, a single verb for *volta dienno* ‘they made a turn’.

> 137 ma prima avea ciascun la lingua stretta  
> 138 coi denti, verso lor duca per cenno,  

but first each of them had pressed his tongue between the teeth toward their Captain, as a signal;

> 137 Ἀλλ᾽ ἕκαστος πρὶν ἔθλιψ᾽ ἐν τοῖς ὀδοῦσι  
> (Yet each one first pressed between his teeth)
>
> 138 Τὴν γλῶσσαν, ὡς σημεῖον πρὸς τὸν Δεκάρχην·  
> (His tongue, as a signal toward their Decurion;)

- *Ἀλλ᾽* is *ἀλλά* elided before *ἕκαστος*. The latter, nom. masc. sg. ‘each’, makes possession of the tongue and teeth clear without possessive pronouns. *πρίν* is adverbial ‘beforehand’, not a conjunction introducing another clause. *ἔθλιψ᾽* is elided *ἔθλιψε*, 3rd sg. aor. active indicative of *θλίβω* ‘press’, with syllabic augment. The aorist gives a single act for Italian *avea … stretta*; *πρίν* establishes its priority.
- *ἐν τοῖς ὀδοῦσι* (dat. pl. of *ὀδούς*) locates the tongue ‘among the teeth’ rather than giving them as the instrument, *coi denti*. The object *τὴν γλῶσσαν* is delayed until after this phrase, reversing the Italian order; *γλῶσσα* has the common *-σσ-* spelling beside Attic *γλῶττα*.
- *ὡς σημεῖον* ‘as a sign’ states the function of the gesture for *per cenno*. Musurus then places *πρὸς τὸν Δεκάρχην* ‘toward the commander of ten’ last. *Δεκάρχην* is acc. sg. of *δεκάρχης*: the title specifies the size of the command, unlike *duca*, and the definite article suffices without *lor*.

> 139 ed egli avea del cul fatto trombetta.  

and he of his — had made a trumpet.

> 139 Ὁ δ᾽ ἐχρῆτο τῷ πρωκτῷ σάλπιγγος δίκην.  
> (And he used his rump like a trumpet.)

- *Ὁ δ᾽* is the article used pronominally with postpositive *δέ*, ‘and he in turn’; *δέ* is elided before the augmented verb. *ἐχρῆτο*, 3rd sg. imperfect middle indicative of *χράομαι*, substitutes ‘was using’ for the completed making in *avea … fatto*. The verb governs the dative *τῷ πρωκτῷ* ‘his anus’: the article does duty for a possessive, and the Greek is explicit like *del cul*, not euphemistic like the English dash.
- *σάλπιγγος δίκην* ‘in the manner of a trumpet’ converts the result *fatto trombetta* into a comparison. *σάλπιγγος* is gen. sg. of *σάλπιγξ*; *δίκην*, originally acc. sg. of *δίκη*, is used postpositionally with that genitive. Greek thus distinguishes the thing used (dative) from the instrument it resembles (genitive), and does not reproduce the diminutive form of *trombetta*.

## Summary — Preserving the Pattern, Recasting the Clause

Musurus often preserves Dante’s larger construction while changing the grammar of its parts. The comparison opened by *Quale* and answered by *tal* keeps its span in *ὥσπερ* … *οὕτως*; within it, the repeated *chi* becomes the balanced *ὁ μὲν* … *ὁ δέ* (‘one’ … ‘another’). Even a repeated Italian preposition need not produce a repeated Greek one: *per foco* becomes *διὰ πυρός* (‘by fire’), but *per divina arte* becomes the instrumental dative *θείᾳ τέχνῃ* (‘by divine art’). Postpositive *δέ* marks the contrast without disturbing the balance.

The participle is Musurus’s most versatile means of recasting a clause. The future participles *ὀψόμενοι* (‘in order to see’) and *ἀκούσων* (‘in order to hear’) give the same Greek construction to Italian *per veder* and *che m’oda*. Elsewhere the choice of participial tense establishes the relation between actions that Dante coordinates: present *κεντοῦντες* (‘pricking’) makes the action of *addentar* simultaneous with *disser*, whereas aorist *ῥίψας* (‘having thrown’) makes *buttò* preliminary to *si volse*. A change of subject can prompt a genitive absolute: *τῶν δ᾽ ἄλλων ἠρεμούντων* (‘while the others remained still’) moves an independent Italian clause to the front and makes it the circumstance of the main action.

In direct speech Musurus is often close to the Italian: *ἄφες μ᾽ ἰέναι* (‘let me go’) follows *lasciami andar* almost word for word. Elsewhere he changes the angle of address, turning the third-person *guidi* into *ὁδήγει* (‘lead’), with the name in the vocative. Particles likewise register his reading of a connection rather than mechanically translating it: *γάρ* can make an explanation explicit where Dante merely juxtaposes two assertions, while an Italian causal *ch’* can be left unmarked. The clearest interpretive choice is *μὴ πιστὰ παραβῶσιν* (‘lest they break their pledges’): after a verb of fearing, *μή* introduces the feared event, and *παραβῶσιν* states it as the opposite of *tenesser patto* (‘keep the compact’).

The diction ranges from explanatory substitution to pointed compression. Recurrent *Προστάτης* (‘protector’) gives *duca* and *maestro* a common emphasis; *Κάκουρε* (‘Evil-tail’) translates the sense of *Malacoda*, whereas most of the names in the muster receive Greek endings rather than Greek meanings. The compound *ἀσφαλτοβράστους* (‘pitch-boiled’) compresses *lessi dolenti* by naming the substance but dropping the suffering. At the close, *πρωκτῷ* (‘anus’) is as explicit as *cul*, yet the change of construction matters: where Dante says that one thing has been made into another, *σάλπιγγος δίκην* (‘in the manner of a trumpet’) turns the identification into a comparison.
