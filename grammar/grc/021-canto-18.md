# Inferno 18

## Lines 1–15 — The Shape of Malebolge

> 1 Loco è in inferno detto Malebolge,  
> 2 tutto di pietra e di color ferrigno,  
> 3 come la cerchia che d' intorno il volge.  

There is a place in Hell called Malebolge, all of stone, and of an iron colour, like the barrier which winds round it.

> 1 Κακόβοθρον τοὔνομα τόπος ἐν ᾍδου  
> (A place in Hell, Evil-pit by name,)
>
> 2 Ἐστίν, ἅπας πέτρινος καὶ σιδηρόχρους,  
> (There is, all of stone and iron-coloured,)
>
> 3 Ὡς καὶ τὸ πέριξ αὐτοῦ κυκλωτὸν τεῖχος.  
> (Just as is the circular wall around it.)

- *Κακόβοθρον τοὔνομα* ‘Evil-pit by name’ comes first, where Dante begins with *Loco è*. Musurus translates the proper name *Malebolge* rather than transcribing it: the plural *bolge* becomes the singular compound *Κακόβοθρον*. There is no participle corresponding to *detto*: *τοὔνομα*, crasis of *τὸ ὄνομα*, is an accusative of respect, ‘as regards the name’. The name itself is neuter singular, though the place that bears it, *τόπος*, is masculine.
- *τόπος ἐν ᾍδου / Ἐστίν* keeps the subject *τόπος* indefinite, without an article, and delays the existential verb *ἔστιν* (3rd sg. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*) until the next line. In the idiom *ἐν ᾍδου*, *ᾍδου* is the genitive singular of *ᾍδης*, not a genitive governed by *ἐν*: the sense is ‘in [the house] of Hades’, with the dative noun of place understood.
- *ἅπας πέτρινος καὶ σιδηρόχρους* replaces *tutto di pietra e di color ferrigno* with three nominative masculine singular forms referring to *τόπος*. *ἅπας* gives the extent, ‘all of it’; *πέτρινος* expresses the material by an adjective, and *σιδηρόχρους* incorporates *color* into a compound adjective, ‘iron-coloured’.
- *ὡς καὶ τὸ πέριξ αὐτοῦ κυκλωτὸν τεῖχος* ‘as also [is] the circular wall around it’ has no finite verb. For *la cerchia che d’ intorno il volge*, Musurus supplies the concrete noun *τεῖχος* and replaces the active relative clause with two attributes inside the article–noun group: *πέριξ αὐτοῦ* ‘round it’ (*αὐτοῦ* gen. sg., referring to *τόπος*) and *κυκλωτόν* ‘circular’ (nom. neut. sg., agreeing with *τεῖχος*).

> 4 Nel dritto mezzo del campo maligno  
> 5 vaneggia un pozzo assai largo e profondo,  

Right in the middle of the malignant field yawns a well exceeding wide and deep,

> 4 Ὅλως ἐν μέσῳ τῆς λυγρᾶς πεδιάδος  
> (Right in the middle of the dismal plain)
>
> 5 Εὐρὺ καὶ βαθύτατον ὑπάρχει φρέαρ,  
> (There is a wide and deepest well,)

- *ὅλως ἐν μέσῳ* takes *dritto* as an intensifier: *ὅλως* ‘altogether’ modifies the expression of position, not the shape or direction of anything. *ἐν μέσῳ* has the substantival dative singular of *μέσος*, followed by the genitive of the whole, *τῆς λυγρᾶς πεδιάδος*. *πεδιάδος* is gen. sg. of *πεδιάς* ‘plain’; *λυγρᾶς* ‘dismal, grievous’ agrees with it, shifting the force of *maligno*.
- *εὐρὺ καὶ βαθύτατον ὑπάρχει φρέαρ* retains the delayed subject but substitutes *ὑπάρχει* ‘exists’ (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ὑπάρχω*) for the more graphic *vaneggia*. Both adjectives are nominative neuter singular with *φρέαρ*: *εὐρύ* is the positive of *εὐρύς*, whereas *βαθύτατον* is the superlative of *βαθύς*, used absolutely, ‘very deep’. Thus *assai largo e profondo* is not reproduced by a single adverb qualifying the two adjectives.

> 6 di cui suo loco dicerò l' ordigno.  

whose structure [I] shall tell [in] its own place.

> 6 Οὗπερ ἄλλοθι τὸ σχῆμα διαγράψω.  
> (Whose form I shall describe elsewhere.)

- *οὗπερ* is the genitive neuter singular relative *οὗ* with the emphasizing enclitic *-περ*. It refers to *φρέαρ* and depends on *τὸ σχῆμα* ‘the form’, the direct object: Greek makes explicit that *di cui* belongs with *l’ ordigno*.
- *ἄλλοθι* is a locative adverb, ‘elsewhere’, for *suo loco*: it does not retain the possessive or the implication ‘in its proper place’. *διαγράψω* (1st sg. fut. act. ind. of *διαγράφω*) preserves the future and the unexpressed subject of *dicerò*, but *τὸ σχῆμα διαγράψω* is ‘I shall delineate the form’ rather than ‘I shall tell the structure’.

> 7 Quel cinghio che rimane adunque è tondo,  
> 8 tra il pozzo e il piè dell' alta ripa dura,  

The border therefore that remains, between the well and the foot of the high rocky bank, is round;

> 7 Κυκλικὸς τοίνυν ἐστὶν ὁ μένων χῶρος  
> (Circular, then, is the remaining space)
>
> 8 Ἀπὸ φρέατος πρὸς τὴν ἀποῤῥωγάδα·  
> (From the well to the sheer cliff-wall;)

- *κυκλικὸς τοίνυν ἐστὶν ὁ μένων χῶρος* brings the predicate *κυκλικός* ‘circular’ to the front, where Italian begins with the subject and reserves *tondo* for the end. Postpositive *τοίνυν* gives the inference in *adunque*. *ὁ μένων χῶρος* is ‘the remaining space’: the attributive *μένων* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. ptcp. of *μένω*) replaces *che rimane*, and the article does duty for *quel* without a separate demonstrative. *χῶρος* itself means ‘space’, not ‘ring’ like *cinghio*; the circularity is expressed in the predicate.
- *ἀπὸ φρέατος πρὸς τὴν ἀποῤῥωγάδα* defines the extent of that space by two endpoints, ‘from the well to the precipice’, rather than by *tra … e*. *φρέατος* is gen. sg. of *φρέαρ* after *ἀπό*; *ἀποῤῥωγάδα* is acc. sg. of *ἀπορρωγάς* after directional *πρός*. The spelling *ῤῥ* marks the doubled rho in the compound. The single noun suggests a sheer face but does not separately translate *il piè*, *alta*, or *dura*.

> 9 ed ha distinto in dieci valli il fondo.  

and it has its bottom divided into ten valleys.

> 9 Τοὐμβαδὸν δὲ διῄρητ᾽ εἰς δέκ᾽ αὐλῶνας.  
> (And its area is divided into ten valleys.)

- *τοὐμβαδόν* is crasis of *τὸ ἐμβαδόν*, ‘the area’ or ‘the surface’. It is the nominative neuter singular subject: whereas the *cinghio* ‘has its bottom divided’ (*ha distinto … il fondo*), in Greek the area itself ‘stands divided’. Postpositive *δέ* continues the description in place of initial *ed*; there is no possessive.
- *διῄρητ᾽* is elided *διῄρηται* (3rd sg. perf. mid./pass. ind. of *διαιρέω*, passive in sense): the perfect denotes the resulting division, not the act of dividing. *εἰς* with the accusative gives the parts into which the area is divided; *δέκ᾽* is elided *δέκα*, and *αὐλῶνας* is acc. pl. of *αὐλών*, a channel or long hollow, for *valli*.

> 10 Quale, dove per guardia delle mura  
> 11 più e più fossi cingon li castelli,  
> 12 la parte dov' ei son rende figura :  

As is the form that ground presents, where to defend the walls successive ditches begird a castle:

> 10 Οἷον, ἔνθ᾽ εἰς ἄμυναν τῶν τειχῶν πλεῖσται  
> (Even as, where for the defence of walls very many)
>
> 11 Τάφροι περιζευγνῦσι τὰς πύργων ἄκρας,  
> (Moats encircle the heights of towers,)
>
> 12 Τὸ μέρος, ἐν ᾧ κεῖνται, παρέχει σχῆμα,  
> (The part in which they lie presents a figure,)

- *οἷον* ‘of what sort’ opens the comparison and must be taken with *σχῆμα* ‘shape’ two lines later (acc. neut. sg.). Musurus thus preserves the separation of *Quale* from *figura* by the intervening clause. *ἔνθ᾽* is *ἔνθα* ‘where’, elided before *εἰς*.
- *εἰς ἄμυναν τῶν τειχῶν* gives the purpose, ‘for defence of the walls’. *ἄμυναν* is acc. sg. after *εἰς*, and *τῶν τειχῶν* (gen. pl. of *τεῖχος*) designates what is defended. *πλεῖσται*, nom. fem. pl. of *πλεῖστος*, the superlative of *πολύς*, agrees across the line break with *τάφροι*. It gives *più e più* as ‘very many’, without repeating a comparative or explicitly marking succession.
- *τάφροι περιζευγνῦσι* ‘ditches gird about’ keeps the ditches as subject of an active verb: *περιζευγνῦσι* is 3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *περιζεύγνυμι*. For *li castelli* the object is *τὰς πύργων ἄκρας* ‘the heights of towers’: *ἄκρας* is acc. fem. pl. of *ἄκρα*, with *πύργων* (gen. pl. of *πύργος*) placed between article and noun.
- *τὸ μέρος, ἐν ᾧ κεῖνται, παρέχει σχῆμα* closely keeps the construction *la parte dov’ ei son rende figura*. *τὸ μέρος* is the nominative subject of *παρέχει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *παρέχω*, ‘presents’); *ἐν ᾧ* ‘in which’ has a dative neuter singular relative referring to *μέρος*. In *κεῖνται* (3rd pl. pres. ind. of *κεῖμαι*, middle in form), the understood subject is the ditches: *son* becomes the more specifically local ‘lie’.

> 13 tale imagine quivi facean quelli;  

such image these made here;

> 13 Εἰκόν᾽ ἐκεῖ τοιαύτην ἐποίουν κεῖνοι.  
> (Such an image did those make there.)

- *εἰκόν᾽ … τοιαύτην* ‘such an image’ answers *οἷον … σχῆμα*. *εἰκόν᾽* is elided *εἰκόνα*, acc. sg. of *εἰκών*, the object of *ἐποίουν*; the correlative adjective *τοιαύτην* agrees with it but is separated from it by *ἐκεῖ*. Where Carlyle has “here” for *quivi*, the Greek adverb is explicitly distal, ‘there’.
- *ἐποίουν κεῖνοι* preserves both the imperfect of *facean* and the postponed demonstrative subject *quelli*. *ἐποίουν* is the augmented, contracted 3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *ποιέω* (identical in form to the 1st sg.); *κεῖνοι* is the poetic shorter form of *ἐκεῖνοι*, nom. masc. pl. ‘those’. Its gender suits *αὐλῶνας* in line 9, not the feminine *τάφροι* of the intervening comparison.

> 14 e come a tai fortezze dai lor sogli  
> 15 alla ripa di fuor son ponticelli,  

and as, from the thresholds of the fortress, there are bridges to the outward bank:

> 14 Ὡς δ᾽ ἀπὸ βάθρων αὐτῶν ἕως τῆς πέραν  
> (And as from their foundations unto the farther)
>
> 15 Κρηπῖδος ἔχουσ᾽ οἱ πύργοι τὰς γεφύρας,  
> (Embankment the towers have their bridges,)

- *ὡς δ᾽* introduces the next comparison, with elided postpositive *δέ* for *e* and *ὡς* for *come*. *ἀπὸ βάθρων αὐτῶν* ‘from their foundations’ takes *sogli* as bases rather than thresholds. *βάθρων* is gen. pl. of *βάθρον* after *ἀπό*; possessive *αὐτῶν* corresponds to *lor* and refers to the towers.
- *ἕως τῆς πέραν κρηπῖδος* ‘as far as the farther embankment’ gives the other endpoint. *ἕως* governs the genitive *κρηπῖδος* (from *κρηπίς* ‘base, raised edge’); *πέραν* is an indeclinable adverb in attributive position, ‘on the far side’, for *di fuor*. The article and its attribute precede the line break, the noun follows it.
- *ἔχουσ᾽ οἱ πύργοι τὰς γεφύρας* recasts *a tai fortezze … son ponticelli* as a personal possessive construction, ‘the towers have the bridges’. *ἔχουσ᾽* is elided *ἔχουσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *ἔχω*), with the nominative subject *οἱ πύργοι* following it. The fortresses thus become the subject rather than the possessors expressed by *a*, and *tai* has no separate equivalent. *τὰς γεφύρας* is a definite accusative plural object; the ordinary noun *γέφυρα* does not reproduce the diminutive of *ponticelli*.

## Lines 16–48 — The First Ditch and a Familiar Face

> 16 così da imo della roccia scogli  
> 17 movien, che ricidean gli argini e fossi  
> 18 infino al pozzo, che i tronca e raccogli.  

so from the basis of the rock proceeded cliffs that crossed the embankments and the ditches, down to the well which truncates and collects them.

> 16 Οὕτως ἐκ ποδῶν τοῦ σιδηρόχρου τείχους  
> (So from the foot of the iron-coloured wall)
>
> 17 Σπιλάδες ἐφέροντο διὰ τῶν βόθρων  
> (Crags ran across through the pits)
>
> 18 Ἄχρι φρέατος, οὗ πέριξ συνηνοῦντο.  
> (As far as the well, around which they united.)

- *Οὕτως* takes up *così*; *ἐκ ποδῶν* ‘from the feet’ (gen. pl. of *πούς* after *ἐκ*) gives a concrete noun for *da imo* ‘from the bottom’. For *della roccia* Musurus has *τοῦ σιδηρόχρου τείχους* ‘of the iron-coloured wall’: *σιδηρόχρου* is the contracted gen. sg. neut. of *σιδηρόχρους*, agreeing with *τείχους*, not with *ποδῶν*.
- *Σπιλάδες ἐφέροντο* ‘crags ran’ puts the subject (*σπιλάδες*, nom. pl. of *σπιλάς*) at the head of the next line. *Ἐφέροντο* (3rd pl. imperf. mid./pass. ind. of *φέρω*) expresses the course the crags took. Where Italian adds a relative clause, *che ricidean gli argini e fossi*, Greek continues the one verb with *διὰ τῶν βόθρων* ‘through the pits’ (*διά* with gen.); the embankments have no separate equivalent.
- *Ἄχρι φρέατος* ‘as far as the well’ has the gen. sg. of *φρέαρ* after the preposition. In *οὗ πέριξ συνηνοῦντο* ‘around which they were joined’, *οὗ* is gen. sg. neut. with postposed *πέριξ*. Italian makes the well the subject of two present verbs, *tronca e raccogli*, and the crags their object (*i*); Greek keeps the crags as subject of one imperfect, *συνηνοῦντο* (3rd pl. mid./pass. of *συνενόω*, augmented after the prefix). It conveys convergence, not truncation.

> 19 In questo loco, dalla schiena scossi  
> 20 di Gerion, trovammoci; e il poeta  
> 21 tenne a sinistra, ed io retro mi mossi.  

In this place, shaken from the back of Geryon, we found ourselves ; and the Poet kept to the left, and I moved behind.

> 19 Εἰς τόπον τόνδε, σεισθέντες ἐπὶ νώτων  
> (Into this place, shaken off from the back)
>
> 20 Γηρυόνος, ἤλθομεν· οὑμὸς δ᾽ Ἡγήτωρ  
> (Of Geryon, we came; and my Guide)
>
> 21 Ἐπ᾽ ἀριστερᾶς ἔβαιν᾽, ἐγὼ δ᾽ εἱπόμην.  
> (Walked on the left, and I followed.)

- *Εἰς τόπον τόνδε … ἤλθομεν* ‘into this place … we came’ turns *in questo loco … trovammoci* into directed motion: *εἰς* takes the accusative, *τόνδε* is the near demonstrative, and *ἤλθομεν* is 1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*, not a reflexive.
- *Σεισθέντες* (nom. masc. pl. aor. pass. participle of *σείω*) agrees with the unexpressed ‘we’, like *scossi*. *Ἐπὶ νώτων Γηρυόνος* places the shaking ‘on the back of Geryon’ (*νῶτα* is plural, *Γηρυόνος* a possessive genitive); unlike *dalla schiena*, the preposition itself does not say ‘off’.
- *Οὑμὸς δ᾽ Ἡγήτωρ* ‘and my Guide’ replaces *il poeta*: *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, and *δέ* is postpositive. *Ἐπ᾽ ἀριστερᾶς* ‘on the left’ is *ἐπί* elided before a vowel, with a feminine genitive (understand ‘hand’). *Ἔβαιν᾽* is elided *ἔβαινε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *βαίνω*). The parallel *ἐγὼ δ᾽ εἱπόμην* ‘and I followed’ gives the contrasting subject explicitly; *εἱπόμην*, 1st sg. imperf. mid. ind. of *ἕπομαι*, includes in the verb the relation expressed by *retro mi mossi*. Both Greek imperfects present movement in progress.

> 22 Alla man destra vidi nuova pieta,  
> 23 nuovi tormenti e nuovi frustatori,  
> 24 di che la prima bolgia era repleta.  

On the right hand I saw new misery, new torments, and new tormenters, wherewith the first chasm was filled.

> 22 Εἶδον δ᾽ ἐπὶ δεξιᾷ νέας ὀδύνας,  
> (And on the right I saw new woes,)
>
> 23 Νέας βασάνους καὶ βασανιστὰς νέους,  
> (New torments and new tormentors,)
>
> 24 Ὧν ἦν ὅλος ἔμπλεως ὁ πρῶτος βόθρος.  
> (With which the first pit was wholly filled.)

- *Εἶδον δ᾽ ἐπὶ δεξιᾷ* ‘and I saw on the right’ moves the verb before the phrase for *alla man destra*. *Εἶδον* is 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*; *ἐπί* here has a locative dative, in contrast to the genitive at 21.
- *Νέας ὀδύνας* ‘new pains’ pluralizes *nuova pieta*. *Νέας βασάνους καὶ βασανιστὰς νέους* retains the threefold *nuova, nuovi, nuovi* but puts the last adjective after its noun. *Βασάνους* is acc. pl. feminine, *βασανιστάς* acc. pl. masculine: the pairing ‘torments / tormentors’ makes the agent noun echo the preceding noun rather than specifying scourging as *frustatori* does.
- *Ὧν ἦν ὅλος ἔμπλεως ὁ πρῶτος βόθρος* keeps *di che* as a relative genitive of contents, governed by *ἔμπλεως* ‘full’. *Ἦν* is 3rd sg. imperf. of *εἰμί*; *ὅλος* ‘whole’ and the predicate adjective *ἔμπλεως* are nom. masc. sg. with the postponed subject *ὁ πρῶτος βόθρος* ‘the first pit’. Italian *era repleta* thus becomes a copula with an adjective, with *ὅλος* added.

> 25 Nel fondo erano ignudi i peccatori;  
> 26 dal mezzo in qua ci venian verso il volto,  
> 27 di là con noi, ma con passi maggiori:  

In its bottom the sinners were naked; [on our side of the middle] they came facing us ; and, on the other side, along with us, but with larger steps:

> 25 Τοὺς δ᾽ ἁμαρτωλοὺς ἐν βάθει γυμνοὺς εἶδον,  
> (And at the bottom I saw the sinners naked,)
>
> 26 Τοὺς μὲν ἐκεῖθεν πρὸς ἡμᾶς ἐρχομένους,  
> (Some coming toward us from that side,)
>
> 27 Τοὺς δ᾽ ἐντεῦθεν μεθ᾽ ἡμῶν πλείονι τάχει,  
> (And others on this side with us at greater speed,)

- *Τοὺς δ᾽ ἁμαρτωλοὺς ἐν βάθει γυμνοὺς εἶδον* ‘and I saw the sinners naked in the depth’ substitutes another *εἶδον* for *erano*. The Italian subject becomes an accusative object, fronted ahead of *ἐν βάθει* (dat. sg. of *βάθος*); *γυμνούς* is its predicate adjective, not part of the articular noun phrase.
- *Τοὺς μὲν … τοὺς δέ* ‘the one group … the other’ distributes that object. *Ἐρχομένους* (acc. masc. pl. pres. mid. participle of *ἔρχομαι*) depends on the verb of seeing and is understood again with the second group. The simultaneous motion expressed by Italian finite *venian* thus becomes participial; *πρὸς ἡμᾶς* ‘towards us’ dispenses with *il volto*.
- The pointers are reversed: *ἐκεῖθεν* ‘from there’ describes those coming towards the observers, *ἐντεῦθεν* ‘from here’ those going *μεθ᾽ ἡμῶν* ‘with us’. Italian assigns the former to *dal mezzo in qua*, the latter to *di là*; Greek does not mention the middle. *Μεθ᾽* is *μετά* elided, with aspiration before the rough breathing. *Πλείονι τάχει* ‘at greater speed’ is a dative of manner, with comparative *πλείονι* agreeing with *τάχει* (dat. sg. neut. of *τάχος*): speed replaces *passi*, without a separate *ma*.

> 28 come i Roman, per l' esercito molto,  
> 29 l' anno del Giubbileo, su per lo ponte  
> 30 hanno a passar la gente modo colto,  

thus the Romans, because of the great throng, in the year of Jubilee, upon the bridge have taken means to pass the people over

> 28 Ὡς οἱ Ῥωμαῖοι διὰ τὸν πολὺν ὄχλον  
> (Just as the Romans because of the great throng)
>
> 29 Ἐπ᾽ ἔτους ἀφέσεως εἰς δύο τάξεις  
> (In the year of Jubilee in two files)
>
> 30 Διαβαίνουσι τὴν γέφυραν, εἰς τρόπον  
> (Cross over the bridge, in such a way)

- *Ὡς οἱ Ῥωμαῖοι* opens the comparison as *come i Roman* does. *Διὰ τὸν πολὺν ὄχλον* gives causal *διά* with the accusative, unlike *διά* with the genitive at 17; *πολύν* is acc. masc. sg. of *πολύς*, agreeing with *ὄχλον* ‘crowd’ for *esercito*.
- *Ἐπ᾽ ἔτους ἀφέσεως* ‘in a year of remission’ uses temporal *ἐπί* with the gen. sg. of *ἔτος*; *ἀφέσεως* (gen. sg. of *ἄφεσις*) defines the year in place of the name *Giubbileo*. *Εἰς δύο τάξεις* ‘in two files’ supplies the arrangement explicitly; *τάξεις* is acc. plural, not dual, after *δύο*.
- *Διαβαίνουσι τὴν γέφυραν* ‘they cross the bridge’ is 3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *διαβαίνω* with a direct object. In *hanno a passar la gente modo colto* the Romans have adopted a means for people to cross; in Greek the Romans themselves cross. *Εἰς τρόπον* ‘in such a manner’ keeps something of *modo* but looks forward to the result clause rather than serving as the object of ‘have taken’.

> 31 che dall' un lato tutti hanno la fronte  
> 32 verso il castello e vanno a Santo Pietro,  
> 33 dall' altra sponda vanno verso il monte.  

; so that, on the one side, all have their faces towards the Castle, and go to St Peter's ; at the other ledge, they go towards the Mount.

> 31 Ὥστ᾽ ἐντεῦθεν ἔχουσιν οἱ μὲν τὰς ὄψεις  
> (That on this side some have their faces)
>
> 32 Πρὸς τὸ φρούριον καὶ τὸν ἅγιον Πέτρον,  
> (Toward the castle and Saint Peter's,)
>
> 33 Οἱ δ᾽ ἑτέρωθεν βαδίζουσι πρὸς τοὖρος.  
> (And those on the other side walk toward the mount.)

- *Ὥστ᾽* is elided *ὥστε* ‘so that’, with finite indicatives for the actual result. *Ἐντεῦθεν … οἱ μέν … οἱ δ᾽ ἑτέρωθεν* ‘on this side the one group … and the others on the other side’ balances the groups as at 26–27, now in the nominative. There is no separate equivalent of *tutti* or noun for *sponda*.
- *Ἔχουσιν … τὰς ὄψεις* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *ἔχω*, with acc. pl. of *ὄψις*) closely follows *hanno la fronte*, except that the faces are plural. The one *πρός* governs both *τὸ φρούριον* and *τὸν ἅγιον Πέτρον*: both are objects towards which the faces are turned, whereas *vanno a Santo Pietro* has its own verb of motion.
- *Βαδίζουσι πρὸς τοὖρος* ‘they walk towards the mountain’ does give *vanno* a finite equivalent in the second limb (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *βαδίζω*). *Τοὖρος* is crasis of *τὸ ὄρος*, acc. sg. neut. after *πρός*.

> 34 Di qua, di là, su per lo sasso tetro  
> 35 vidi demon cornuti con gran ferze,  
> 36 che li battean crudelmente di retro.  

On this side, on that, along the hideous stone, I saw horned Demons with large scourges, who smote them fiercely from behind.

> 34 Ἔνθεν κἀκεῖθεν τῆς σιδηρόχρου πέτρας  
> (On this side and on that of the iron-coloured rock)
>
> 35 Εἶδον μάστιξι δαίμονας κερασφόρους  
> (I saw horned demons with scourges)
>
> 36 Αὐτοὺς σκληρῶς τύπτοντας ὀπισθιδίως.  
> (Cruelly striking them from behind.)

- *Ἔνθεν κἀκεῖθεν τῆς σιδηρόχρου πέτρας* ‘on this side and that of the iron-coloured rock’ combines *di qua, di là* with a defining genitive rather than retaining *su per* ‘along’. *Κἀκεῖθεν* is crasis of *καὶ ἐκεῖθεν*; *σιδηρόχρου*, here gen. sg. feminine, repeats the adjective of 16 rather than translating *tetro* directly.
- *Εἶδον μάστιξι δαίμονας κερασφόρους* puts *μάστιξι* (dat. pl. of *μάστιξ*) before the demons. The dative can be read instrumentally with *τύπτοντας* ‘striking with scourges’; there is no adjective for *gran*. *Κερασφόρους* ‘horn-bearing’ agrees with *δαίμονας* (acc. masc. pl.).
- *Αὐτοὺς σκληρῶς τύπτοντας ὀπισθιδίως* ‘striking them harshly from behind’ turns relative *che … battean* into a participial complement of *εἶδον*. *Τύπτοντας* is acc. masc. pl. pres. act. of *τύπτω*, agreeing with the demons, while *αὐτούς* is its object, corresponding to *li*. The two adverbs render *crudelmente* and *di retro*.

> 37 Ahi come facean lor levar le berze  
> 38 alle prime percosse! già nessuno  
> 39 le seconde aspettava nè le terze.  

Ah! how they made them lift their legs at the first strokes ! truly none waited for the second or the third.

> 37 Αἴ! πῶς τῇ πρώτῃ τύψει τὰς κνήμας ἦρον  
> (Ah! how at the first blow they lifted their shanks,)
>
> 38 Οἱ ταλαίπωροι, οὐδενὸς ἤδη τούτων  
> (The wretched ones, none of them now)
>
> 39 Περιμένοντος δευτέραν οὐδὲ τρίτην.  
> (Waiting for a second or a third.)

- *Αἴ! πῶς* preserves exclamatory *Ahi come*. *Τῇ πρώτῃ τύψει* ‘at the first blow’ uses a singular dative (*τύψει* from *τύψις*) for plural *alle prime percosse*. *Τὰς κνήμας ἦρον* ‘they lifted their shins’ removes the causative *facean lor levar*: *ἦρον* is 3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *αἴρω*, with the sufferers, not the tormentors, as subject. The delayed *οἱ ταλαίπωροι* ‘the wretched ones’ names that subject explicitly.
- *Οὐδενὸς ἤδη τούτων περιμένοντος* ‘none of these now waiting’ makes independent *già nessuno … aspettava* a genitive absolute of accompanying circumstance. *Οὐδενός* (gen. sg. masc. of *οὐδείς*) is its subject, *τούτων* a partitive gen. pl., *περιμένοντος* the agreeing gen. sg. pres. act. participle of *περιμένω*. The present stem conveys waiting contemporaneous with the lifting, not a subsequent action.
- *Δευτέραν οὐδὲ τρίτην* ‘a second nor a third’ supplies in thought acc. sg. feminine *τύψιν* as object of the participle, continuing the change to the singular. *Οὐδέ* continues the negative begun in *οὐδενός*.

> 40 Mentr' io andava, gli occhi miei in uno  
> 41 furo scontrati; ed io sì tosto dissi:  
> 42 "Di già veder costui non son digiuno."  

As I went on, my eyes were met by one, and instantly I said : “This one I have seen before.”

> 40 Ἐν ᾧ δ᾽ ἔβαινον, τὸ βλέμμα μου πρὸς ἕνα  
> (And as I walked, my gaze upon one)
>
> 41 Συνέπεσεν· ἐγὼ δὲ παραυτίκ᾽ εἶπον·  
> (Chanced to fall; and straightway I said:)
>
> 42 “ Οὐκ ἤδη πρῶτον αὐτὸν ἐθεασάμην.”  
> ("Not for the first time now have I beheld him.")

- *Ἐν ᾧ δ᾽ ἔβαινον* ‘and while I was walking’ renders *mentr’ io andava* by a temporal relative phrase (literally ‘in which [time]’) and 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *βαίνω*. *Τὸ βλέμμα μου* ‘my gaze’ replaces plural *gli occhi miei*; *μου* is the enclitic possessive genitive.
- *Πρὸς ἕνα συνέπεσεν* ‘fell in with one’ replaces passive *in uno furo scontrati* by an intransitive active verb. *Ἕνα* is acc. masc. sg. of *εἷς*, used substantivally after *πρός*; *συνέπεσεν* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *συμπίπτω*, with internal augment, agreeing with singular *βλέμμα*. *Ἐγὼ δὲ παραυτίκ᾽ εἶπον* gives explicit *io*, postpositive connective *δέ*, elided *παραυτίκα* ‘straightway’, and 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. *εἶπον* ‘I said’.
- *Οὐκ ἤδη πρῶτον αὐτὸν ἐθεασάμην* ‘it is not now for the first time that I have beheld him’ keeps the negative but discards the figurative predicate of *di già veder costui non son digiuno*. *Πρῶτον* is an adverbial neuter accusative; *ἐθεασάμην* is 1st sg. aor. mid. ind. of the deponent *θεάομαι*, with *αὐτόν* ‘him’ rather than demonstrative *costui*.

> 43 Perciò a figurarlo i piedi affissi;  
> 44 e il dolce duca meco si ristette,  
> 45 ed assentì ch' alquanto indietro gissi.  

I therefore stayed my feet to recognize him ; and the kind Guide stood still with me, and allowed me to go back a little.

> 43 Τότε βλέπων εἰς αὐτὸν ἀτενῶς ἔστην·  
> (Then looking intently at him I stopped;)
>
> 44 Ὁ δὲ γλυκὺς Ἡγήτωρ πράξας ὁμοίως  
> (And my sweet Guide, doing likewise,)
>
> 45 Συνῄνεσ᾽ ὅπως μικρὸν ὀπίσω μείνω.  
> (Consented that I stay behind a little.)

- *Τότε* ‘then’ makes the link temporal rather than inferential as in *perciò*. *Βλέπων εἰς αὐτὸν ἀτενῶς* ‘looking intently at him’ is a nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *βλέπω* with a directional phrase and adverb: it describes what the speaker was doing, rather than giving his purpose as *a figurarlo* does. *Ἔστην* ‘I stopped’ (1st sg. intransitive second aor. act. ind. of *ἵστημι*) needs no object corresponding to *i piedi*.
- *Ὁ δὲ γλυκὺς Ἡγήτωρ πράξας ὁμοίως* ‘and the sweet Guide, having done likewise’ keeps *dolce* but replaces *meco si ristette* by reference back to *ἔστην*. *Πράξας* is nom. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *πράσσω*, agreeing with *Ἡγήτωρ*: the Italian coordinate verb is subordinated to the act of consenting.
- *Συνῄνεσ᾽* is elided *συνῄνεσε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *συναινέω*, with the augment inside the compound. *Ὅπως … μείνω* gives the content of the consent in a finite clause, close to Italian *ch’ … gissi*, rather than by an infinitive. *Μείνω* is 1st sg. aor. act. subj. of *μένω* (not an optative despite the past main verb). *Μικρὸν ὀπίσω* ‘a little behind’ uses an adverbial accusative of extent; the consent is to **stay** behind, not, as in *indietro gissi*, to **go** back.

> 46 E quel frustato celar si credette  
> 47 bassando il viso, ma poco gli valse;  

And that scourged _spirit_ thought to hide himself, lowering his face ; but little it availed him,

> 46 Ὁ μαστιγίας ὅμως κρυφθῆναι θέλων,  
> (Yet that scourged wretch, wishing to hide himself,)
>
> 47 Κατένευσε τὴν ὄψιν, ἀλλὰ ματαίως.  
> (Bowed down his face, but in vain.)

- *Ὁ μαστιγίας* ‘the scourged wretch’ is an articular masculine agent- or person-noun in the nominative singular, not a participle like substantivized *quel frustato*. *Ὅμως* ‘however’ gives the transition an adversative force. *Κρυφθῆναι θέλων* ‘wishing to be hidden’ replaces *celar si credette*: *θέλων* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *θέλω*) expresses intention rather than belief, and its complementary aor. pass. infinitive *κρυφθῆναι* (from *κρύπτω*) gives passive form to the idea of concealing oneself.
- *Κατένευσε τὴν ὄψιν* ‘he bowed his face’ makes the action of Italian gerund *bassando* the main verb. *Κατένευσε* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *κατανεύω*, augmented after the prefix; the accusative specifies what he inclined, in close imitation of *il viso*, where the Greek verb can by itself mean ‘nod downwards’. *Ἀλλὰ ματαίως* ‘but in vain’ compresses *ma poco gli valse* into an adverb without a verb or personal indirect object.

> 48 ch' io dissi: "Tu che l' occhio a terra gette,  

for I said : “Thou, that dost cast thy eye upon the ground!

> 48 Εἶπον γὰρ αὐτῷ· “ Τί χαμαὶ κύπτεις βλέμμα;  
> (For I said to him: "Why do you bend your gaze to the ground?)

- *Εἶπον γὰρ αὐτῷ* ‘for I said to him’ interprets *ch’ io dissi* causally: postpositive *γάρ* links the speech to *ματαίως*. *Αὐτῷ* (dat. masc. sg.) supplies the addressee, while the subject is expressed by the ending of *εἶπον*.
- *Τί χαμαὶ κύπτεις βλέμμα;* ‘why do you bend your gaze earthward?’ changes the direct address and relative clause *Tu che l’ occhio a terra gette* into a complete question. *Τί* is the adverbial accusative of the interrogative *τίς*, ‘why?’; *χαμαί* is a local adverb. *Κύπτεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *κύπτω*) supplies the second person without *σύ*. Normally ‘stoop’ or ‘bend forward’, it is used here with bare acc. sg. *βλέμμα* after the pattern of Italian *l’ occhio … gette*: it is the gaze that is bent.

## Lines 49–81 — Venedico’s Confession and the View From the Bridge

> 49 se le fazion che porti non son false,  
> 50 Venedico se’ tu Caccianimico;  

If the features which thou wearest be not false, thou art Venedico Caccianimico;

> 49 „ Εἰ μὴ σῆς μορφῆς ὁ τύπος ἀπατᾷ με,  
> (If the features of your form do not deceive me,)
>
> 50 „ Σὺ Βενέδικος εἶ Κακιανιμῖκος.  
> (You are Venedico Caccianimico.)

- *Εἰ μὴ σῆς μορφῆς ὁ τύπος ἀπατᾷ με* ‘if the impress of your form does not deceive me’ turns the plural *le fazion* into the singular subject *ὁ τύπος*. The dependent genitive *σῆς μορφῆς* stands first; *σῆς* is the possessive adjective agreeing with *μορφῆς*. Possession thus replaces the relative clause *che porti*.
- For the predicate *non son false*, Musurus uses a transitive verb and supplies the person deceived: *ἀπατᾷ* is the contracted 3rd sg. present active indicative of *ἀπατάω*, with accusative *με*. *Εἰ μή* introduces a negative condition without implying that it is contrary to fact.
- *Σὺ Βενέδικος εἶ Κακιανιμῖκος* brings the explicit subject *σύ* to the front, whereas *tu* follows *se’*. Both parts of the name are predicate nominatives, separated by *εἶ*, 2nd sg. present indicative of *εἰμί*.

> 51 ma che ti mena a sì pungenti salse?”  

but what brings thee to such a biting pickle?”

> 51 „ Τίς δ᾽ εἰς τὰς δριμείας τάσδ᾽ ἤγαγέ σ᾽ ἅλμας;”  
> (But who has brought you to such bitter brines?")

- *Τίς δ᾽ … ἤγαγέ σ᾽* asks ‘but who brought you?’: nominative *τίς* asks for an agent rather than reproducing *che* ‘what’; postpositive *δέ* carries *ma*. *Ἤγαγε* is 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *ἄγω*, with the reduplicated aorist stem *ἀγαγ-* and initial augment. The completed act replaces present *mena*. The final accent in *ἤγαγέ* is additional before enclitic *σ᾽*, elided *σε*.
- *Εἰς τὰς δριμείας τάσδ᾽ … ἅλμας* ‘into these pungent brines’ retains the plural and makes *a* directional with *εἰς* and the accusative. *Δριμείας* is the feminine accusative plural of *δριμύς*; *τάσδ᾽* is elided *τάσδε*, from *ὅδε*. The demonstrative specifies ‘these’ rather than expressing the degree in *sì pungenti*. The noun is held back until after the verb and its object.

> 52 Ed egli a me: “Mal volentier lo dico;  

And he to me, “Unwillingly I tell it;

> 52 Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ· “ Λυπηρόν μοι τὸ λέγειν·  
> (And he to me: "It is painful for me to tell;)

- *Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ* matches the verbless *Ed egli a me*: *ὁ* has its pronominal use ‘he’ before connective *δέ*, and *πρός* takes the full accusative *ἐμέ*.
- *Λυπηρόν μοι τὸ λέγειν* ‘the telling is painful to me’ replaces *Mal volentier lo dico*. The article makes the present active infinitive of *λέγω* a neuter singular subject; *λυπηρόν* is its predicate, with the copula understood. The speaker is expressed by dative *μοι* rather than by a first-person finite verb; there is no separate object for *lo*.

> 53 ma sforzami la tua chiara favella,  
> 54 che mi fa sovvenir del mondo antico.  

but thy clear speech, that makes me recollect the former world, compels me.

> 53 „Ἀλλ’ ὁ σὸς ἐπείγει με καθαρὸς λόγος,  
> (But your clear speech urges me,)
>
> 54 „Ὃς ἀναμιμνήσκει με τὸν πάλαι κόσμον.  
> (Which reminds me of the former world.)

- *Ἀλλ’ ὁ σὸς ἐπείγει με καθαρὸς λόγος* ‘but your clear speech presses me’ retains the present and the personal object of *sforzami*. *Ἀλλ’* is elided *ἀλλά*; *ἐπείγει* is 3rd sg. present active indicative of *ἐπείγω*. The subject is discontinuous: the verb and its object intervene between *ὁ σός* and the rest of its noun phrase, *καθαρὸς λόγος*.
- *Ὃς ἀναμιμνήσκει με τὸν πάλαι κόσμον* uses a single causative verb for *mi fa sovvenir del mondo antico*. The masculine nominative relative *ὅς* agrees with *λόγος* and is the subject of *ἀναμιμνήσκει*, 3rd sg. present active indicative of *ἀναμιμνήσκω*. The person reminded and the thing recalled are both accusative here. *Πάλαι*, an adverb, stands attributively between article and noun: ‘the world of former times’.

> 55 Io fui colui, che la Ghisola bella  
> 56 condussi a far la voglia del Marchese,  
> 57 come che suoni la sconcia novella.  

It was I who led the fair Ghisola to do the Marquis' will, however the unseemly tale may sound.

> 55 „Ἔγωγ’ ἤμην, ὃς τὴν ὡραίαν Γισόλαν  
> (It was I who the fair Ghisola)
>
> 56 „Ἔπεισ’ ἐνδοῦναι τοῖς Μαρκίωνος πόθοις,  
> (Persuaded to yield to the Marquis's desires,)
>
> 57 „Κ’ εἰ τὸ ῥυπαρὸν ἱστόρημ’ ᾄδετ’ ἄλλως·  
> (Even though the foul story be told otherwise;)

- *Ἔγωγ’ ἤμην, ὃς* ‘I for my part was [the one] who’ renders *Io fui colui, che*. *Ἔγωγ’* is elided *ἔγωγε*, the pronoun strengthened by *γε*; there is no demonstrative corresponding to *colui*. *Ἤμην* is the later 1st sg. imperfect of *εἰμί*, rather than the usual Attic *ἦ*.
- *Τὴν ὡραίαν Γισόλαν ἔπεισ’ ἐνδοῦναι* ‘I persuaded the fair Ghisola to yield’ interprets *condussi a far* as persuasion. *Ἔπεισ’* is elided *ἔπεισα*, 1st sg. aorist active indicative of *πείθω*: the relative is the subject, but the verb takes the person of its antecedent *ἔγωγε*. *Γισόλαν* is both the object of persuasion and the understood subject of *ἐνδοῦναι*, aorist active infinitive of *ἐνδίδωμι*.
- *Τοῖς Μαρκίωνος πόθοις* makes singular *la voglia* plural, ‘to the desires’. The dative depends on ‘yield’, not on ‘persuaded’; genitive *Μαρκίωνος* ‘of the Marquis’ comes between the article and its noun.
- *Κ’ εἰ … ᾄδετ’ ἄλλως* ‘even if … it is sung otherwise’ gives *come che suoni* a specifically concessive interpretation and adds ‘otherwise’. *Κ’* is shortened *καί* before *εἰ*. *Τὸ ῥυπαρὸν ἱστόρημ’* ‘the foul tale’ is the neuter nominative subject (*ἱστόρημ’* for *ἱστόρημα*); *ᾄδετ’* is elided *ᾄδεται*, 3rd sg. present passive indicative of *ᾄδω*. The Italian subjunctive *suoni* becomes an indicative under *εἰ*, and the tale is something sung or reported, rather than something that itself sounds a certain way.

> 58 E non pur io qui piango Bolognese:  

And I am not the only Bolognese that weeps here:

> 58 „Οὐχὶ δὲ μόνος Βονωνὸς ὧδε κλαίω,  
> (Nor am I the only Bolognese weeping here,)

- *Οὐχὶ δὲ μόνος Βονωνὸς ὧδε κλαίω* keeps the finite first-person *piango* in *κλαίω*, present active indicative, and uses *ὧδε* locally, ‘here’. *Οὐχί* is the emphatic negative; *δέ* connects the statement to what precedes. Predicate nominatives *μόνος Βονωνός* render *pur io … Bolognese* without an explicit *ἐγώ*: ‘I do not weep here as the only Bolognese’.

> 59 anzi n’è questo loco tanto pieno,  
> 60 che tante lingue non son ora apprese  
> 61 a dicer ‘sipa’ tra Savena e Reno;  

nay, this place is so filled with us, that as many tongues are not now taught to say *sipa* between Savena and Reno;

> 59 „Ἀλλ’ ὁ τόπος ἔμπλεώς ἐστι τοσούτων,  
> (But the place is full of so many,)
>
> 60 „Ὅσαι γλῶσσαι νῦν οὐκ ἐξέμαθον λέγειν  
> (That not so many tongues have now learned to say)
>
> 61 „Σίπα μεταξὺ τῆς Σαυένης καὶ Ῥήνου.  
> ('Sipa' between the Savena and the Reno.)

- *Ἀλλ’ ὁ τόπος ἔμπλεώς ἐστι τοσούτων* ‘but the place is full of so many’ takes up *anzi* with adversative *ἀλλά*. Unlike *questo loco*, *ὁ τόπος* has no demonstrative. *Ἔμπλεως* is a masculine nominative singular predicate adjective governing the genitive *τοσούτων*, substantival ‘of so many [people]’; *ne* has no separate equivalent. The additional accent on *ἔμπλεώς* is occasioned by enclitic *ἐστι*.
- Greek recasts *tanto pieno, che tante lingue non …* as a comparison of number, *τοσούτων, ὅσαι γλῶσσαι … οὐκ* ‘of so many, as tongues have not …’. The negation occupies the relative clause much as in the Italian, rather than the comparison being reformulated with ‘more than’. Relative adjective *ὅσαι* agrees in the nominative feminine plural with its own noun *γλῶσσαι*, not in case with *τοσούτων*.
- *Νῦν οὐκ ἐξέμαθον λέγειν* turns *non son ora apprese a dicer* into active ‘have not now learned to say’. *Ἐξέμαθον* is 3rd pl. second aorist active indicative of *ἐκμανθάνω*, with the augment after the preverb; *λέγειν* is its complementary present active infinitive. *Σίπα* is the quoted, uninflected object. *Μεταξύ* takes the genitives *τῆς Σαυένης καὶ Ῥήνου* for *tra Savena e Reno*.

> 62 e se di ciò vuoi fede o testimonio,  
> 63 recati a mente il nostro avaro seno.”  

and if thou desirest assurance and testimony thereof, recall to thy memory our avaricious heart.”

> 62 „Πίστιν δ’ ἢ μαρτυρίαν πρὸς τοῦτ’ εἰ θέλεις,  
> (And if you wish proof or testimony of this,)
>
> 63 „Φέρ’ εἰς μνήμην τὴν ἡμῶν φιλαργυρίαν.”  
> (Call to mind our avarice.")

- *Πίστιν δ’ ἢ μαρτυρίαν πρὸς τοῦτ’ εἰ θέλεις* puts the accusative objects of *θέλεις* before *εἰ* ‘if’. *Δ’* is connective *δέ*; disjunctive *ἤ* keeps the Italian *o*. *Πρὸς τοῦτ’* (*τοῦτο*, neuter accusative singular) gives ‘in support of this’ for *di ciò*. *Θέλεις* is 2nd sg. present active indicative of *θέλω*.
- *Φέρ’ εἰς μνήμην* ‘bring to mind’ uses elided *φέρε*, 2nd sg. present active imperative of *φέρω*, with no reflexive corresponding to *-ti* in *recati*. Its object *τὴν ἡμῶν φιλαργυρίαν* ‘our love of money’ substitutes an abstract noun for *il nostro avaro seno*. The possessive genitive *ἡμῶν* stands inside the article–noun group.

> 64 Così parlando il percosse un demonio  
> 65 della sua scuriada, e disse: “Via,  
> 66 ruffian, qui non son femmine da conio.”  

And as he thus spake, a Demon smote him with his lash, and said: “Away! [pandar] there are no women here to coin.”

> 64 Ὡς ταῦτ’ ἔλεγεν, ἔτυψ’ αὐτόν τις Δαίμων  
> (As he was saying this, a Demon struck him)
>
> 65 Τῇ πανωδύνῳ μάστιγι λέγων· “Ἴθι,  
> (With his all-painful scourge, saying: "Begone!)
>
> 66 „Ὧδε, μαστροπέ, γυναῖκες οὐ πωλοῦνται.”  
> (Here, pander, women are not for sale.")

- *Ὡς ταῦτ’ ἔλεγεν* ‘as he was saying these things’ makes *Così parlando* a temporal clause. Elided *ταῦτα* is an accusative plural object rather than an adverb of manner; *ἔλεγεν*, 3rd sg. imperfect active indicative of *λέγω*, sets ongoing speech against the single stroke of *ἔτυψ’*, elided *ἔτυψε*, 3rd sg. aorist active indicative of *τύπτω*.
- *Αὐτόν τις Δαίμων* preserves the order object–subject in *il percosse un demonio* after moving the verb first. Enclitic indefinite *τις* qualifies *Δαίμων*. *Τῇ πανωδύνῳ μάστιγι* is an instrumental dative singular (*μάστιξ*, genitive *μάστιγος*); the article suffices without *sua*, and *πανωδύνῳ* adds ‘all-painful’.
- Conversely, *λέγων* makes finite *e disse* a present active participle of *λέγω*, nominative masculine singular agreeing with *Δαίμων*. *Ἴθι*, 2nd sg. present imperative of *εἶμι* ‘go’, renders *Via*. *Ὧδε* ‘here’ precedes vocative *μαστροπέ*, from *μαστροπός*, reversing *ruffian, qui*.
- *Γυναῖκες οὐ πωλοῦνται* ‘women are not sold’ changes existential *non son femmine da conio* into a passive assertion. *Πωλοῦνται* is the contracted 3rd pl. present passive indicative of *πωλέω*, agreeing with nominative *γυναῖκες*. Sale is Musurus’ interpretation of *da conio*; there is no Greek word for coining.

> 67 Io mi raggiunsi con la scorta mia;  

I rejoined my Escort;

> 67 Τότ’ ἐπανῆλθον ἔνθ’ ἦν οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ·  
> (Then I returned to where my Guide was;)

- *Τότ’ ἐπανῆλθον ἔνθ’ ἦν οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ* ‘then I returned to where my guide was’ expands *mi raggiunsi con la scorta mia* into motion to a place defined by a relative adverbial clause. *Τότ’* is elided *τότε*; *ἐπανῆλθον* is 1st sg. second aorist active indicative of *ἐπανέρχομαι*, with the augmented aorist of *ἔρχομαι* after the preverbs.
- *Ἔνθ’* is *ἔνθα* ‘where’ before *ἦν*, 3rd sg. imperfect of *εἰμί*. *Οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*; the rough breathing of the article is retained. Masculine agent noun *Ἡγήτωρ* ‘guide’ replaces feminine *scorta* and is the subject of *ἦν*.

> 68 poscia con pochi passi divenimmo  
> 69 là dove un scoglio della ripa uscia.  

then, with a few steps, we came to where a cliff proceeded from the bank.

> 68 Ἥκομεν δ’ εἶτα μετὰ βήματ’ ὀλίγα,  
> (And then after a few steps we came)
>
> 69 Ὅπου σπιλάς τις ἐξήρχετ’ ἐκ τῆς ὄχθης,  
> (Where a crag jutted forth from the bank,)

- *Ἥκομεν δ’ εἶτα μετὰ βήματ’ ὀλίγα* gives ‘and then after a few steps’. *Ἥκομεν* is 1st pl. of *ἥκω*, a verb whose present means ‘have come, be arrived’; the form also coincides with the imperfect. In the narrative it does the work of preterite *divenimmo*. *Μετά* with the accusative gives a temporal sequence in place of Italian *con*; *βήματ’* is elided *βήματα*, neuter plural, with agreeing *ὀλίγα*.
- *Ὅπου* ‘where’ suffices for *là dove*. *Σπιλάς τις* is nominative singular ‘a certain crag’, with enclitic *τις* for *un*. *Ἐξήρχετ’*, elided *ἐξήρχετο*, is 3rd sg. imperfect middle indicative of *ἐξέρχομαι*, internally augmented. The compound verb and *ἐκ τῆς ὄχθης* together express the projection *della ripa uscia*: the bank is the point of origin, hence the genitive after *ἐκ*.

> 70 Assai leggeramente quel salimmo,  
> 71 e volti a destra su per la sua scheggia,  
> 72 da quelle cerchie eterne ci partimmo.  

This we very easily ascended; and, turning to the right upon its jagged ridge, we quitted those eternal circles.

> 70 Ῥᾳδίως δ’ αὐτὴν ἀνέβημεν, κἀκεῖθεν  
> (And easily we climbed it, and thence)
>
> 71 Ἐπὶ δεξιᾷ στραφέντες τῆς σπιλάδος  
> (Turning to the right along the crag)
>
> 72 Ἐμακρύνθημεν γύρων τῶνδ’ αἰωνίων.  
> (We moved away from these eternal circles.)

- *Ῥᾳδίως δ’ αὐτὴν ἀνέβημεν* ‘and easily we climbed it’ has no separate intensifier for *assai*. The accusative *αὐτήν* refers back to feminine *σπιλάς*, as *quel* refers to masculine *scoglio*. *Ἀνέβημεν* is 1st pl. root aorist active indicative of *ἀναβαίνω*, with internal augment; the height ascended is its direct object.
- *Κἀκεῖθεν* is crasis of *καὶ ἐκεῖθεν* ‘and from there’, adding a point of departure. *Ἐπὶ δεξιᾷ στραφέντες* renders *volti a destra*: *δεξιᾷ* is a substantival feminine dative singular, ‘on the right’, and *στραφέντες* is the nominative masculine plural second aorist passive participle of *στρέφω*, intransitive in sense, ‘having turned’. *Τῆς σπιλάδος*, genitive of *σπιλάς*, specifies the crag whose right side is in question rather than naming a separate splinter or ridge for *scheggia*.
- *Ἐμακρύνθημεν* is 1st pl. aorist passive indicative of *μακρύνω*, again intransitive in sense, ‘we moved away’. Bare genitive *γύρων τῶνδ’ αἰωνίων* marks separation in place of *da quelle cerchie eterne*. *Τῶνδ’* is elided *τῶνδε*, genitive plural of *ὅδε*: ‘these’, not the distal *quelle*.

> 73 Quando noi fummo là, dov’ei vaneggia  
> 74 di sotto, per dar passo agli sferzati,  

When we reached the part where it yawns beneath to leave a passage for the scourged,

> 73 Ὅτε δ’ ἤλθομεν ἔνθα κάτωθεν πόρος  
> (And when we came where underneath a passage)
>
> 74 Ἐστὶ διδοὺς δίοδον τοῖς μαστιγίαις,  
> (Is, giving a thoroughfare to the scourged wretches,)

- *Ὅτε δ’ ἤλθομεν ἔνθα* ‘and when we came where’ expresses arrival by *ἤλθομεν*, 1st pl. second aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι*, in place of *fummo là*. *Ἔνθα* introduces the description without a separate antecedent for *là*.
- *Κάτωθεν πόρος ἐστὶ διδοὺς δίοδον* ‘underneath there is a passage giving a way through’ replaces *ei vaneggia di sotto* with existential *ἐστί* and a new nominative subject *πόρος*. *Διδούς* is the nominative masculine singular present active participle of *δίδωμι*, agreeing with *πόρος*: what the opening affords replaces the purpose infinitive *per dar*. Its direct object *δίοδον* is accusative singular of the feminine *δίοδος*.
- *Τοῖς μαστιγίαις* is dative plural of masculine *μαστιγίας*, the recipient with *διδούς*. Where *agli sferzati* substantivizes a passive participle, Greek uses a personal noun for one subjected to, or deserving of, the lash.

> 75 lo duca disse: “Attienti, e fa che feggia  
> 76 lo viso in te di questi altri mal nati,  
> 77 a’ quali ancor non vedesti la faccia,  
> 78 però che son con noi insieme andati.”  

my Guide said: “Stay, and let the look strike on thee of these other ill-born *spirits,* whose faces thou hast not yet seen, for they have gone along with us.”

> 75 Οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ εἶπε· “Μένε καὶ βλέπε  
> (My Guide said: "Stay and look)
>
> 76 „Ἀτενῶς τὰς δυσμοίρους ψυχὰς ἐκείνας,  
> (Intently at those ill-fated souls,)
>
> 77 „Ὧνπερ οὔπω σὺ τὴν ὄψιν ἐθεάσω,  
> (Whose face you have not yet beheld,)
>
> 78 „Ἅτε μεθ’ ἡμῶν ὁμοῦ προσερχομένων.”  
> (Inasmuch as they were advancing together with us.")

- *Οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ εἶπε* uses ‘my guide’ again for *lo duca*, supplying the possessive by crasis as in line 67. *Εἶπε* is 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative in the paradigm of *λέγω*. *Μένε καὶ βλέπε* gives two direct 2nd sg. present active imperatives, from *μένω* and *βλέπω*: ‘stay and look’. The second replaces the entire causative *fa che feggia lo viso in te*: the addressee is now the one looking.
- *Ἀτενῶς* ‘intently’ qualifies *βλέπε*, whose direct object is *τὰς δυσμοίρους ψυχὰς ἐκείνας*. Greek supplies *ψυχάς* ‘souls’ for the substantivized *mal nati* and makes their condition ‘ill-fated’, not literally ‘ill-born’. *Δυσμοίρους* is the feminine accusative plural of the two-termination adjective *δύσμοιρος*. Postnominal *ἐκείνας* is distal, ‘those’, for *questi*; *altri* is not separately rendered.
- *Ὧνπερ οὔπω σὺ τὴν ὄψιν ἐθεάσω* ‘whose countenance you have not yet beheld’ uses a possessive genitive relative for *a’ quali*. *Ὧνπερ* agrees in number with *ψυχάς* and adds strengthening *-περ*. *Τὴν ὄψιν* retains singular *la faccia*. *Σύ* makes the subject explicit; *ἐθεάσω* is 2nd sg. aorist middle indicative of *θεάομαι*, with middle form and active sense.
- *Ἅτε … προσερχομένων* makes finite causal *però che son … andati* participial: *ἅτε* presents the circumstance as an actual reason. *Προσερχομένων* is the genitive plural present middle participle of *προσέρχομαι*, agreeing with *ὧνπερ* and referring to the souls; its present stem presents motion in progress rather than completed motion. *Μεθ’ ἡμῶν ὁμοῦ* closely doubles *con noi insieme*: *μετά* takes the genitive and becomes *μεθ’* by elision and aspiration before the rough breathing of *ἡμῶν*.

> 79 Dal vecchio ponte guardavam la traccia,  
> 80 che venia verso noi dall’ altra banda,  
> 81 e che la ferza similmente scaccia.  

From the ancient bridge we viewed the train, who were coming towards us, on the other side, chased likewise by the scourge.

> 79 Ἐκ τῆς παλαιᾶς ἐβλέπομεν γεφύρας  
> (From the ancient bridge we watched)
>
> 80 Τὴν ἑτέρωθεν πρὸς ἡμᾶς ἐρχομένην  
> (The file coming toward us from the other side)
>
> 81 Σειρὰν μάστιγι τυπτομένην ἐπ’ ἴσης.  
> (Struck in like manner by the scourge.)

- *Ἐκ τῆς παλαιᾶς ἐβλέπομεν γεφύρας* keeps *Dal vecchio ponte* as the point of observation, with *ἐκ* and the genitive. The verb separates *τῆς παλαιᾶς* from *γεφύρας*. *Ἐβλέπομεν* is 1st pl. imperfect active indicative of *βλέπω*, matching imperfect *guardavam*.
- *Τὴν ἑτέρωθεν πρὸς ἡμᾶς ἐρχομένην σειράν* postpones the noun for *la traccia* until its motion has been specified. The article introduces the whole accusative object; *ἐρχομένην*, accusative feminine singular present middle participle of *ἔρχομαι*, agrees with *σειράν* ‘line, file’. The attributive participle replaces finite *che venia*. *Ἑτέρωθεν* incorporates both *dall’* and *altra banda* in one adverb, ‘from the other side’; *πρὸς ἡμᾶς* marks the direction towards the observers.
- *Μάστιγι τυπτομένην ἐπ’ ἴσης* adds a second participle agreeing with *σειράν*, this time after the noun and without a conjunction. *Τυπτομένην* is present passive of *τύπτω*: active *che la ferza … scaccia*, with the scourge as subject, becomes ‘being struck with a scourge’, with instrumental dative *μάστιγι*. The shift from ‘drives on’ to ‘strikes’ drops the implication of motion from this verb. *Ἐπ’ ἴσης*, elided *ἐπί* with a feminine genitive singular in a fixed adverbial expression, gives ‘equally’ for *similmente*.

## Lines 82–114 — Jason’s Deceit and the Second Ditch

> 82 Il buon maestro, senza mia domanda,  
> 83 mi disse: "Guarda quel grande che viene,  
> 84 e per dolor non par lagrima spanda:  

The kind Master, without my asking, said to me: “Look at that great *soul* who comes, and seems to shed no tear for pain:

> 82 Οὑμὸς δ’ Ἡγήτωρ, πρὶν αὐτὸν ἐρωτήσω,  
> (And my Guide, before I could ask him,)
>
> 83 Εἶπέ μοι· “Βλέπε τὸν μέγαν κεῖνον, ὅστις  
> (Said to me: "Look at that great one, who)
>
> 84 „Ἔρχεται δάκρυ λύπης οὐ δοκῶν χέειν.  
> (Comes seeming to shed no tear of grief.)

- *Οὑμὸς δ’ Ἡγήτωρ* ‘and my Guide’ has crasis of *ὁ ἐμός* in *οὑμός*, and elision of the postpositive connective *δέ*. The possessive, nominative masculine singular with *ἡγήτωρ* ‘leader’, makes the relation to the speaker explicit; there is no adjective for *buon*.
- *πρὶν αὐτὸν ἐρωτήσω* ‘before I ask him’ turns the noun phrase *senza mia domanda* into a temporal clause, supplying the personal object *αὐτόν*. *ἐρωτήσω* can in form be future indicative or aorist subjunctive active, first singular, of *ἐρωτάω*. The sense is of an act not yet performed, not a future assertion. The finite construction with bare *πρίν* is unlike the usual Attic *πρίν* with infinitive after an affirmative main clause.
- *Εἶπέ μοι* ‘said to me’ follows the intervening clause as does *mi disse*. *εἶπε* is the third singular second-aorist active indicative belonging to *λέγω*; its extra accent here is due to enclitic *μοι*, dative of the recipient. *Βλέπε* is a second singular present active imperative of *βλέπω*. In *τὸν μέγαν κεῖνον* ‘that great one’, *μέγαν* is substantival (accusative masculine singular of *μέγας*) and *κεῖνον* is the poetic short form of *ἐκεῖνον*. No Greek noun corresponds to “soul”.
- *ὅστις ἔρχεται* ‘who comes’ uses the compound relative, nominative as subject of the present middle-form *ἔρχεται*. Where Italian coordinates *viene, e … non par*, Greek subordinates the second predicate: *οὐ δοκῶν χέειν* ‘not seeming to shed’, with the nominative masculine singular present active participle of *δοκέω* and the uncontracted present active infinitive of *χέω*. *δάκρυ λύπης* ‘a tear of grief’ fronts the object of the infinitive and makes *per dolor* a genitive dependent on ‘tear’.

> 85 quanto aspetto reale ancor ritiene!  

what a *regal* aspect he yet retains!

> 85 „Ὡς ἔστ’ ἔτ’ αὐτοῦ βασίλειος ἡ θέα!  
> (How royal is his aspect still!)

- Exclamatory *ὡς* ‘how’ qualifies the predicate adjective *βασίλειος*, feminine nominative singular of a two-termination adjective agreeing with *ἡ θέα* ‘the aspect’. *ἔστ’ ἔτ’* elides *ἐστί* and *ἔτι* in succession. In place of *quanto aspetto … ritiene*, with Jason as subject, Musurus writes ‘how royal his aspect still is’, with possessive genitive *αὐτοῦ* and the grammatical subject last.

> 86 Quelli è Jason, che per core e per senno  
> 87 li Colchi del monton privati fene.  

That is Jason, who, by courage and by counsel, bereft the Colchians of the ram.

> 86 „Ἰάσων οὗτος, ὃς τόλμῃ καὶ συνέσει  
> (This is Jason, who by daring and wit)
>
> 87 „Ἐκ Κολχίδος τὸ χρυσοῦν ἥρπασε δέρας.  
> (Carried off the golden fleece from Colchis.)

- *Ἰάσων οὗτος* ‘Jason [is] this one’ puts the name first, uses a nearer demonstrative than *Quelli*, and leaves the copula unexpressed. Relative *ὃς* is nominative as subject of *ἥρπασε*; *τόλμῃ καὶ συνέσει* are instrumental datives singular of *τόλμα* ‘daring’ and *σύνεσις* ‘understanding’, interpreting *per core e per senno* as faculties exercised.
- *ἐκ Κολχίδος τὸ χρυσοῦν ἥρπασε δέρας* ‘from Colchis he snatched the golden fleece’ changes both objects of the Italian idiom. Instead of making the Colchians deprived of the ram (*li Colchi del monton privati fene*), the Greek gives the place of removal with *ἐκ* and the genitive, and the thing taken in the accusative. *χρυσοῦν*, contracted accusative neuter singular of *χρύσεος*, specifies a quality absent from the line; *ἥρπασε*, third singular aorist active indicative of *ἁρπάζω*, with temporal augment, separates adjective from noun.

> 88 Egli passò per l' isola di Lenno,  
> 89 poi che le ardite femmine spietate  
> 90 tutti li maschi loro a morte dienno.  

He passed, by the isle of Lemnos, after the bold merciless women had given all their males to death.

> 88 „Αὐτὸς διαβὰς διὰ τῆς νήσου Λήμνου,  
> (He, passing through the island of Lemnos,)
>
> 89 „Ἔνθ’ αἱ τολμηραὶ κἀνηλεεῖς γυναῖκες  
> (Where the bold and pitiless women)
>
> 90 „Ἀπηνῶς κατέκτειναν ἄρσενας πάντας,  
> (Ruthlessly slew all the males,)

- *Αὐτὸς διαβάς* ‘he himself, having passed’ replaces finite *Egli passò* with a nominative masculine singular second-aorist active participle of *διαβαίνω*: the sentence now waits for its main verb at 92. *διὰ τῆς νήσου Λήμνου* repeats the idea of passage in a preposition governing the genitive; *Λήμνου* names the island in apposition.
- *Ἔνθ’* (elided *ἔνθα*) ‘where’ replaces temporal *poi che*: the killings are located there rather than explicitly dated before the passage. *αἱ τολμηραὶ κἀνηλεεῖς γυναῖκες* keeps the two adjectives attributive; *κἀνηλεεῖς* has crasis of *καὶ ἀνηλεεῖς*, the adjective being nominative plural of *ἀνηλεής*.
- *ἀπηνῶς κατέκτειναν* ‘harshly they slew’ adds an adverb reinforcing *spietate* and condenses *a morte dienno* into a verb. *κατέκτειναν* is third plural aorist active indicative of *κατακτείνω*, with augment after the preverb and the liquid aorist in *-εινα*. *ἄρσενας πάντας* ‘all males’ is accusative plural, with *ἄρσην* used substantivally; *loro* has no equivalent.

> 91 Ivi con segni e con parole ornate  
> 92 Isifile ingannò, la giovinetta  
> 93 che prima avea tutte l'altre ingannate.  

There, with tokens and fair words, did he deceive the young Hypsipyle, who had before deceived all the rest.

> 91 „Μηχανεύμασι καὶ μειλιχίοις λόγοις  
> (With wiles and honeyed words)
>
> 92 „Ἐξηπάτησε τὴν νέαν Ὑψιπύλην  
> (Deceived the young Hypsipyle,)
>
> 93 „ Τὴν πρὶν τὰς ἄλλας ἀπατήσασαν πάσας,  
> (Who had before deceived all the others,)

- *Μηχανεύμασι καὶ μειλιχίοις λόγοις* supplies two instrumental datives plural: *μηχάνευμα* ‘contrivance’ gives *segni* a more pointedly deceptive sense, and *μειλίχιος* ‘soothing, winning’ interprets *ornate* by the effect of the words. There is no fresh adverb for *Ivi*; the preceding construction has already specified the place.
- *ἐξηπάτησε* ‘he deceived’ is the main verb after *διαβάς*: third singular aorist active indicative of *ἐξαπατάω*, with augment internal to the compound. *τὴν νέαν Ὑψιπύλην* makes *la giovinetta*, in apposition in Italian, an attributive adjective with the accusative name.
- *τὴν πρὶν τὰς ἄλλας ἀπατήσασαν πάσας* ‘the one who previously deceived all the others’ continues the accusative with a repeated article and attributive participle instead of *che … avea … ingannate*. *ἀπατήσασαν* is accusative feminine singular aorist active of *ἀπατάω*, a simpler verb than *ἐξαπατάω* for Jason’s act. Adverbial *πρίν* ‘previously’ makes the anteriority explicit; *πάσας* agrees across the participle with its feminine plural object *τὰς ἄλλας*.

> 94 Lasciolla quivi gravida e soletta:  

He left her there pregnant and forlorn:

> 94 „ Ἔγκυον αὐτὴν ἐκεῖ λιπὼν καὶ μόνην.  
> (Leaving her there pregnant and alone.)

- *ἔγκυον αὐτὴν ἐκεῖ λιπὼν καὶ μόνην* ‘leaving her there pregnant and alone’ attaches a further participle to the subject of *ἐξηπάτησε*, rather than starting a new finite clause as *Lasciolla* does. *λιπών* is nominative masculine singular second-aorist active of *λείπω*, without the indicative augment. The two predicate adjectives stand at opposite ends: *ἔγκυον* (feminine accusative of two-termination *ἔγκυος*) and *μόνην* both describe the condition of *αὐτήν*. The latter gives the sense of *soletta* without its diminutive form.

> 95 tal colpa a tal martiro lui condanna;  
> 96 ed anche di Medea si fa vendetta.  

such guilt condemns him to such torment; and also for Medea vengeance is taken.

> 95 „ Τοιοῦτο πταῖσμα τοιαύτην αὐτῷ φέρει  
> (Such a transgression brings him such)
>
> 96 „ Ποινὴν πρὸς ἐκδίκησιν καὶ τῆς Μηδείας·  
> (Punishment, in vengeance also for Medea;)

- *τοιοῦτο πταῖσμα τοιαύτην αὐτῷ φέρει ποινήν* ‘such a transgression brings him such a penalty’ preserves the echo *tal … tal*, inflecting the first demonstrative as nominative neuter singular with *πταῖσμα*, the second as accusative feminine singular with *ποινήν*. In place of *lui condanna*, *φέρει* (third singular present active indicative of *φέρω*) takes the penalty as direct object and the person affected in the dative.
- *πρὸς ἐκδίκησιν καὶ τῆς Μηδείας* ‘for the vindication also of Medea’ turns the separate impersonal *si fa vendetta* into a purpose phrase, *πρός* with the accusative. The genitive identifies the person avenged, and *καί* immediately precedes that person rather than introducing a second finite clause.

> 97 Con lui sen va chi da tal parte inganna;  

With him go all who practise the like deceit;

> 97 „ Σὺν αὐτῷ βαίνει πᾶς οὕτως ἀπατήσας.  
> (With him goes everyone who has deceived in this way.)

- *σὺν αὐτῷ βαίνει πᾶς οὕτως ἀπατήσας* ‘with him goes everyone who has thus deceived’ keeps the initial comitative phrase (*σύν* with dative) and the singular of *sen va chi*. *βαίνει* is third singular present active indicative of *βαίνω*; *πᾶς* and the nominative masculine singular aorist active participle *ἀπατήσας* make the indefinite relative *chi … inganna* a distributive subject defined by a completed act. *οὕτως* ‘in this way’ takes *da tal parte* as expressing manner.

> 98 e questo basti della prima valle  
> 99 sapere, e di color che in sè assanna."  

and let this suffice to know respecting the first valley, and those whom it devours.”

> 98 „ Ἅλις ἔχομεν τοῦ πρώτου νῦν αὐλῶνος  
> (We have had enough now of the first valley)
>
> 99 „ Κἀκείνων, ὅσους οὗτος περιλαμβάνει.”  
> (And of those whom it encloses.")

- *Ἅλις ἔχομεν … νῦν* ‘we have enough now’ substitutes a first plural present indicative for the third singular subjunctive of injunction *basti*. The Greek adds ‘now’, but has neither a demonstrative for *questo* nor an infinitive for *sapere*. The genitives *τοῦ πρώτου … αὐλῶνος* ‘of the first ravine’ and *κἀκείνων* (crasis of *καὶ ἐκείνων*) specify what they have had enough of.
- *ὅσους οὗτος περιλαμβάνει* ‘as many as this encloses’ uses accusative masculine plural *ὅσους* as object and nominative *οὗτος*, referring to masculine *αὐλών*, as subject. *περιλαμβάνει*, third singular present active indicative, conveys inclusion but not the biting force of *assanna*; the compound’s *περι-* does the work of a separate spatial expression like *in sè*.

> 100 Già eravam là 've lo stretto calle  
> 101 con l' argine secondo s' incrocicchia,  
> 102 e fa di quello ad un altro arco spalle.  

We had already come to where the narrow pathway crosses the second bank, and makes of it a buttress for another arch.

> 100 Ἤδη δ᾽ ἦμεν ἔνθ᾽ αὐλῶνα διασχίζει  
> (And already we were where the valley is crossed,)
>
> 101 Τὸν δεύτερον ἡ στενὴ τρίβος, καὶ τοῦτον  
> (The second, by the narrow path, and this)
>
> 102 Ὑποστύλωμα ποιεῖ γεφύρας ἄλλης.  
> (The abutment it makes of another bridge.)

- *Ἤδη δ’ ἦμεν ἔνθ’* ‘and already we were where’ is closer to the stative *Già eravam* than “had already come”: *ἦμεν* is first plural imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*. Elided *ἔνθα* combines the functions of *là ’ve*.
- *αὐλῶνα διασχίζει τὸν δεύτερον ἡ στενὴ τρίβος* ‘the narrow path cuts across the ravine, the second one’ makes the crossing transitive (third singular present active *διασχίζει*), with a delayed nominative subject. The accusative *αὐλῶνα* is specified by the postposed attributive *τὸν δεύτερον*. Musurus thus names a second **ravine**, repeating his word for *valle*, rather than translating *argine* as a bank; Italian *s’incrocicchia con* does not take the crossed feature as a direct object.
- *καὶ τοῦτον ὑποστύλωμα ποιεῖ γεφύρας ἄλλης* ‘and makes this a support of another bridge’ uses *ποιεῖ* (contracted third singular present active of *ποιέω*) with object *τοῦτον* and predicate accusative *ὑποστύλωμα*. The Italian *di quello* becomes the object, *spalle* a singular structural noun, and *ad un altro arco* a dependent genitive naming a bridge rather than an arch.

> 103 Quindi sentimmo gente, che si nicchia  
> 104 nell' altra bolgia e che col muso isbuffa,  
> 105 e sè medesma con le palme picchia.  

Here we heard people whining in the other chasm, and puffing with mouth and nostrils, and knocking on themselves with their palms.

> 103 Ἐκεῖθεν ἠκούσαμεν ψυχὰς εἰς βόθρον  
> (Thence we heard souls in a pit,)
>
> 104 Τὸν ἄλλον θρηνῳδούσας, δεινὸν φυσώσας  
> (The next one, lamenting, dreadfully snorting)
>
> 105 Τῷ στόματι, κἀλλήλας χερσὶ τυπτούσας.  
> (With their mouths, and beating one another with their hands.)

- *Ἐκεῖθεν ἠκούσαμεν* ‘from there we heard’ preserves the directional force of *Quindi*. *ἠκούσαμεν* is first plural aorist active indicative of *ἀκούω*, with temporal augment. In place of collective *gente, che* and singular verbs, *ψυχάς* ‘souls’ is an accusative plural object with three agreeing present active participles of perception: the souls are heard in the act.
- *εἰς βόθρον τὸν ἄλλον* ‘in the pit, the other one’ has a postposed articular adjective. Locative *εἰς* with accusative for *nell’ altra bolgia*, without a verb of entry, departs from the normal Classical distinction between motion into and rest in. *θρηνῳδούσας* (from *θρηνῳδέω*) gives *si nicchia* the sense ‘lamenting’; *δεινὸν φυσώσας* ‘blowing terribly’ adds an adverbial neuter accusative to the contracted participle of *φυσάω*. *τῷ στόματι* is instrumental singular, ‘with the mouth’, for *col muso*.
- *κἀλλήλας χερσὶ τυπτούσας* ‘and striking one another with their hands’ has crasis of *καὶ ἀλλήλας*. The feminine plural reciprocal agrees in gender with *ψυχάς* but changes the action: *sè medesma* is reflexive, not reciprocal. *χερσί*, dative plural of *χείρ*, specifies the instrument of *τυπτούσας* (from *τύπτω*) less narrowly than *con le palme*.

> 106 Le ripe eran grommate d' una muffa  
> 107 per l' alito di giù che vi si appasta,  
> 108 che con gli occhi e col naso facea zuffa.  

The banks were crusted over with a mould from the vapour below, which concretes upon them, which did battle with the eyes and with the nose.

> 106 Εὐρῶτι δ᾽ εἶχε τὰς πλευρὰς ἠλειμμένας,  
> (And it had its sides coated with mould,)
>
> 107 Ἐκ τοῦ δυσώδους βυθοῦ προερχομένῳ,  
> (Issuing from the foul-smelling depth,)
>
> 108 Κἀηδεστάτῳ τῇ τ᾽ ὄψει τῇ τ᾽ ὀσφρήσει.  
> (And most loathsome both to sight and to smell.)

- *Εὐρῶτι δ’ εἶχε τὰς πλευρὰς ἠλειμμένας* ‘and it had its sides coated with mould’ replaces *Le ripe eran grommate* with a singular possessor, implicitly the pit. *εἶχε* is third singular imperfect active indicative of *ἔχω*; its object *τὰς πλευράς* has a predicate participle *ἠλειμμένας*, accusative feminine plural perfect middle/passive of *ἀλείφω*, describing the resultant coating. The material stands first in the dative, *εὐρῶτι* (from *εὐρώς*, genitive *εὐρῶτος*).
- *ἐκ τοῦ δυσώδους βυθοῦ προερχομένῳ* ‘coming forth from the foul-smelling depth’ agrees with *εὐρῶτι*: *προερχομένῳ* is dative masculine singular present middle participle of *προέρχομαι*. The mould itself comes from below; *per l’alito … che vi si appasta* is not separately rendered as vapour that adheres. *δυσώδους*, genitive singular of *δυσώδης*, supplies an adjective of smell to *βυθοῦ*.
- *κἀηδεστάτῳ* (crasis of *καὶ ἀηδεστάτῳ*) ‘and most unpleasant’ is another dative masculine singular qualifying the mould, a superlative of *ἀηδής*. *τῇ τ’ ὄψει τῇ τ’ ὀσφρήσει* uses paired, elided *τε … τε* with datives of respect, ‘both to sight and to smell’. The senses replace the organs, and a quality of the mould replaces the personifying *facea zuffa*.

> 109 Lo fondo è cupo sì che non ci basta  
> 110 loco a veder senza montare al dosso  
> 111 dell' arco, ove lo scoglio più soprasta.  

The bottom is so deep, that we could see *it* nowhere without mounting to the ridge of the arch, where the cliff stands highest.

> 109 Τοιόνδ᾽ αὐτοῦ τὸ βάθος, ὥστ᾽ οὐδαμόθεν  
> (Such is its depth that from nowhere)
>
> 110 Θεαθῆναι δύναται, πλὴν τῆς γεφύρας  
> (Can it be seen, except from the bridge)
>
> 111 Ἄνωθεν, ἔνθ᾽ ἡ σπιλὰς ὑπερεξέχει.  
> (Above, where the crag overhangs most.)

- *Τοιόνδ’ αὐτοῦ τὸ βάθος* ‘such [is] its depth’ replaces subject *Lo fondo* and predicate *cupo sì* with an abstract subject and a demonstrative predicate. *τοιόνδ’* is elided *τοιόνδε*, nominative neuter singular of *τοιόσδε*, in agreement with *βάθος*; the copula is implicit. The demonstrative anticipates the result clause *ὥστ’* (elided *ὥστε*) with a finite indicative.
- *οὐδαμόθεν θεαθῆναι δύναται* ‘from nowhere can it be seen’ changes *non ci basta loco a veder*, with the observers explicitly affected, into a passive construction. *δύναται* is third singular present middle indicative of *δύναμαι*; its complement *θεαθῆναι* is the aorist passive infinitive of *θεάομαι*, passive in sense here. The ending of *οὐδαμόθεν* specifies a position **from which** one looks.
- *πλὴν τῆς γεφύρας ἄνωθεν* ‘except from above, on the bridge’ expresses an exception of vantage point, omitting the act of mounting and the distinct *dosso*. *ἄνωθεν* ‘from above’ follows the genitive *τῆς γεφύρας*. *ἔνθ’ ἡ σπιλὰς ὑπερεξέχει* ‘where the crag projects above’ uses elided *ἔνθα* and third singular present active *ὑπερεξέχει*: the compound expresses prominence without a separate comparative for *più*.

> 112 Quivi venimmo, e quindi giù nel fosso  
> 113 vidi gente attuffata in uno sterco,  
> 114 che dagli uman privati parea mosso.  

We got upon it; and [thence] in the ditch beneath, I saw a people dipped in excrement, that seemed as *it had* flowed from human privies.

> 112 Ἤλθομεν ἐκεῖ, κἀκεῖθεν πρὸς τὸν βόθρον  
> (We came there, and thence into the pit)
>
> 113 Κύψας βεβυθισμένους εἶδον ἐν κόπρῳ,  
> (Bending down I saw people plunged in dung,)
>
> 114 Ἐξ ἀνθρωπίνων συῤῥευσάσῃ λουτρώνων.  
> (That had flowed together from human privies.)

- *Ἤλθομεν ἐκεῖ* ‘we came there’ retains the plural and the adverb of *Quivi venimmo*; *ἤλθομεν* is first plural second-aorist active indicative belonging to *ἔρχομαι*. *κἀκεῖθεν* (crasis of *καὶ ἐκεῖθεν*) then marks the point from which the speaker looks, as does *e quindi*.
- *πρὸς τὸν βόθρον κύψας* ‘having bent down towards the pit’ introduces an act of stooping: *κύψας*, nominative masculine singular aorist active participle of *κύπτω*, marks the shift to a singular observer before *εἶδον* ‘I saw’ (first singular second-aorist active indicative belonging to *ὁράω*). *πρός* with accusative gives the direction of his movement rather than directly locating the people as *giù nel fosso* does.
- *βεβυθισμένους … ἐν κόπρῳ* ‘those submerged … in dung’ renders collective *gente attuffata* by a masculine plural perfect middle/passive participle of *βυθίζω*, accusative object of *εἶδον*. The reduplicated perfect presents their resulting state; *ἐν* with dative gives its location.
- *ἐξ ἀνθρωπίνων συῤῥευσάσῃ λουτρώνων* makes the flow an assertion about the dung, dropping the qualification *parea*. *συῤῥευσάσῃ* is dative feminine singular aorist active participle of *συρρέω*, agreeing back with feminine *κόπρῳ*: ‘having flowed together’. The spelling *ῤῥ* marks the doubled rho at the compound boundary. The participle separates *ἀνθρωπίνων* from *λουτρώνων*, the genitive plural governed by *ἐξ*; *λουτρών*, ordinarily ‘bathing-place’, stands here for *privati*.

## Lines 115–136 — Alessio and Thais Among the Flatterers

> 115 E mentre ch' io laggiù con l'occhio cerco,  
> 116 vidi un col capo sì di merda lordo,  
> 117 che non parea s' era laico o cherco.  

And whilst I was searching with my eyes, down amongst it, I beheld one with a head so smeared in filth, that it did not appear whether he was layman or clerk.

> 115 Ἐν ᾧ δὲ κάτω τοῖς ὄμμασιν ἠρεύνων,  
> (And while I was searching below with my eyes,)
>
> 116 Εἶδόν τιν’, ὃς ἔγκοπρον κεφαλὴν εἶχεν·  
> (I saw one who had a dung-caked head;)
>
> 117 Ἠπόρουν δ’, εἰ λαϊκὸς ἦν ἢ τοῦ κλήρου.  
> (And I was at a loss whether he was a layman or of the clergy.)

- *Ἐν ᾧ δὲ … ἠρεύνων* ‘and in the time in which I was searching’ renders *E mentre ch’ … cerco*. The relative *ᾧ* is dative singular after *ἐν*; together they introduce a temporal clause. Postpositive *δέ* carries the opening *E*. Where Dante uses the present *cerco* beside the past *vidi*, Musurus sets the ongoing search in the imperfect, *ἠρεύνων* (1st sg. impf. act. ind. of *ἐρευνάω*, with temporal augment and contraction), against the single perception in the aorist *εἶδον* (1st sg., from *ὁράω*).
- *κάτω* ‘below’ renders *laggiù* without a separate demonstrative; *τοῖς ὄμμασιν* (dat. pl. of *ὄμμα*) is instrumental, replacing singular *con l’occhio*. The emphatically expressed *io* has no separate Greek equivalent.
- *τιν’, ὃς ἔγκοπρον κεφαλὴν εἶχεν* ‘someone who had a dung-covered head’ expands *un col capo sì di merda lordo* into a relative clause. *τιν’* is elided *τινά*, accusative singular object of *εἶδον*; *ὃς* is nominative as subject of *εἶχεν* (3rd sg. impf. act. ind. of *ἔχω*). The compound adjective *ἔγκοπρον*, accusative feminine singular with *κεφαλήν*, absorbs both the soiling and its substance.
- The degree and result construction *sì … che non parea* disappears. *Ἠπόρουν δ’* ‘and I was at a loss’ makes the observer, rather than the appearance, the subject (*ἠπόρουν*, 1st sg. impf. act. ind. of *ἀπορέω*; *δ’* for *δέ*). *εἰ … ἢ …* introduces an indirect alternative question, retaining the indicative *ἦν* (3rd sg. impf. of *εἰμί*). Predicate nominative *λαϊκός* answers to *laico*, but *cherco* becomes the genitive of membership *τοῦ κλήρου* ‘of the clergy’, with no second *ἦν*.

> 118 Quei mi sgridò: “Perchè se’ tu sì ingordo  
> 119 di riguardar più me, che gli altri brutti?”  

He bawled to me: "Why art thou so eager in gazing at me, more than the others in their nastiness?"

> 118 Ἔκραξε δέ μοι· “Τί σύ μ’ οὕτως ἀπλήστως  
> (And he cried out to me: "Why do you at me so greedily)
>
> 119 „ Βλέπεις, καὶ πλέον ἢ τοὺς κοπρώδεις ἄλλους ;”  
> (Gaze, even more than at the other filthy ones?")

- *Ἔκραξε δέ μοι* ‘and he shouted to me’ renders *Quei mi sgridò*. The demonstrative subject drops out; *ἔκραξε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *κράζω*) takes the enclitic dative of the person addressed, *μοι*.
- *Τί σύ μ’ οὕτως ἀπλήστως βλέπεις;* ‘why do you look at me so insatiably?’ preserves the expressed *tu* in *σύ*. Interrogative *τί* is adverbial; *μ’* is elided *με*, direct object of *βλέπεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *βλέπω*). *οὕτως ἀπλήστως* turns *sì ingordo* from a predicate adjective into a modified adverb, while *di riguardar* supplies the finite verb in place of *se’*.
- *καὶ πλέον ἢ τοὺς κοπρώδεις ἄλλους* ‘and more than the other dung-covered ones’ adds *καί* before adverbial comparative *πλέον*. After *ἤ* the accusative matches *μ’*: the comparison is between objects of the gaze. *κοπρώδεις* (acc. masc. pl. of *κοπρώδης*) gives *brutti* a more specifically excremental sense.

> 120 Ed io a lui: “Perchè, se ben ricordo,  
> 121 già t' ho veduto coi capelli asciutti,  

And I to him: "Because, if I rightly recollect, I have seen thee before with thy hair dry;

> 120 Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν· “Διότι πάλαι σ’ εἶδον,  
> (And I to him: "Because I saw you formerly,)
>
> 121 „ Εἴπερ μέμνημ’ ἀκριβῶς, στεγνὸν τὴν κόμην.  
> (If I remember rightly, with dry hair.)

- *Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν* follows the verbless *Ed io a lui*: *κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*, and *πρός* with the accusative marks the addressee. *Διότι* introduces the reason, but Musurus places *πάλαι σ’ εἶδον* ‘I saw you formerly’ before the qualification *se ben ricordo*. *πάλαι* renders *già*, *σ’* is elided *σε*, and the simple aorist *εἶδον* takes the place of *ho veduto*.
- *Εἴπερ μέμνημ’ ἀκριβῶς* ‘if indeed I remember accurately’ inserts the qualification between *εἶδον* and the description of its object. *εἴπερ* strengthens the condition with *-περ*; *μέμνημ’* is elided *μέμνημαι* (1st sg. perf. mid. ind. of *μιμνήσκω*), a perfect denoting the present state of remembering. *ἀκριβῶς* specifies the sense of *ben*.
- *στεγνὸν τὴν κόμην* is literally ‘dry as to the hair’. Unlike *asciutti*, which agrees with *capelli*, *στεγνόν* is masculine accusative singular, predicated of *σ’*; *τὴν κόμην*, feminine accusative singular, is an accusative of respect, with a collective noun for the Italian plural.

> 122 e sei Alessio Interminei da Lucca:  
> 123 però t' adocchio più che gli altri tutti.”  

and thou art Alessio Interminei of Lucca: therefore do I eye thee more than all the rest."

> 122 „ Εἶς Ἀλέξις ἐκ Λούκης Ἰντερμινέλης·  
> (You are Alessio Interminei of Lucca;)
>
> 123 „ Διὸ κἀτενίζω σέ πλέον τῶν ἄλλων.”  
> (Therefore I also gaze intently at you more than at the others.")

- *Εἶς Ἀλέξις ἐκ Λούκης Ἰντερμινέλης* omits the connective *e* and places the indication of origin between the two nominative names. *Εἶς* functions as the 2nd sg. pres. ind. of *εἰμί* ‘you are’, corresponding to *sei*; the ordinary Attic form is *εἶ*. *ἐκ Λούκης* renders *da Lucca* with *ἐκ* and the genitive.
- *Διὸ κἀτενίζω σέ* ‘for which reason I also gaze intently at you’ answers to *però t’adocchio*. *διό* has connective force, and *κἀτενίζω* is crasis of *καὶ ἀτενίζω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.). The person looked at is the direct object, as in Italian. In *πλέον τῶν ἄλλων* the genitive of comparison replaces the *ἤ* construction of line 119; *tutti* has no separate equivalent.

> 124 Ed egli allor, battendosi la zucca:  

And he then, beating his pate:

> 124 Τότε τὴν αὑτοῦ κεφαλὴν τύψας εἶπεν·  
> (Then striking his own head he said:)

- *Τότε τὴν αὑτοῦ κεφαλὴν τύψας εἶπεν* ‘then, having struck his own head, he said’ retains *allor* but supplies a finite verb of speaking, *εἶπεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*), rather than an explicit equivalent of *egli*. *τύψας* (nom. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *τύπτω*) agrees with its subject and presents the blow as a whole rather than as ongoing accompaniment. The reflexive in *battendosi la zucca* becomes an attributive possessive genitive, *αὑτοῦ* (contracted *ἑαυτοῦ*); *κεφαλήν* is a neutral word for *zucca*.

> 125 “Quaggiù m' hanno sommerso le lusinghe,  
> 126 ond' io non ebbi mai la lingua stucca.”  

"Down to this, the flatteries wherewith my tongue was never weary have sunk me!"

> 125 “Ἐνθάδε μ’ ἐβύθισαν αἱ κολακεῖαι,  
> ("Down here the flatteries have plunged me,)
>
> 126 „ Ὧν ᾀείποτ’ ἦν ἀκάμας ἡ ’μὴ γλῶσσα.”  
> (Of which my tongue was ever unwearied.")

- *Ἐνθάδε μ’ ἐβύθισαν αἱ κολακεῖαι* closely follows the order of *Quaggiù m’hanno sommerso le lusinghe*: place, object, verb, delayed subject. *ἐνθάδε* itself means ‘here’; the downward movement is conveyed by *ἐβύθισαν* (3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *βυθίζω*). The simple aorist again renders an Italian compound perfect.
- *Ὧν ᾀείποτ’ ἦν ἀκάμας ἡ ’μὴ γλῶσσα* recasts *ond’ io non ebbi mai la lingua stucca* as ‘of which my tongue was ever unwearied’. Relative *ὧν* (gen. pl., referring to *κολακεῖαι*) specifies what the tongue did not tire of. The Italian possessor-subject *io* becomes a possessive adjective: *ἡ ’μή* represents *ἡ ἐμή*, with prodelision of the initial vowel after the article. *γλῶσσα* is now the subject of *ἦν*, and poetic *ἀκάμας* ‘untiring’ its nominative predicate. Perpetual continuance, *ᾀείποτ’* (final vowel elided before *ἦν*), and the negative prefix of *ἀκάμας* replace *non … mai* with *stucca*.

> 127 Appresso ciò lo duca: “Fa che pinghe,”  
> 128 mi disse, “il viso un poco più avante,  

Thereupon my Guide said to me: "Stretch thy face a little forwards,

> 127 Πρὸς ἐμὲ δ’ ἔπειτ’ εἶπεν οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ·  
> (And to me then my Guide said:)
>
> 128 “Ἀνάτεινον τὰς ὄψεις μικρόν τι πρόσω,  
> ("Stretch forward your eyes a little farther,)

- *Πρὸς ἐμὲ δ’ ἔπειτ’ εἶπεν οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ* ‘and to me then said my Guide’ gathers the reporting words together before the command, instead of interrupting it with *mi disse*. *πρὸς ἐμέ* puts the addressee first; *δ’ ἔπειτ’* represents *δὲ ἔπειτα*, with elision in both words. *ἔπειτα* renders *Appresso ciò* without an object corresponding to *ciò*. *οὑμός*, crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, makes the possessive explicit where Italian has *lo*; *Ἡγήτωρ* is a poetic masculine agent noun in the nominative.
- *Ἀνάτεινον τὰς ὄψεις* ‘stretch out your eyes’ replaces *Fa che pinghe … il viso* with a single direct command, *ἀνάτεινον* (2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *ἀνατείνω*). The plural *τὰς ὄψεις* shifts the emphasis from the face to the organs of sight; the article suffices for the possessor. *μικρόν τι* is a neuter accusative of extent, ‘a little’, with indefinite *τι*; directional *πρόσω* gives *più avante* without a grammatical comparative.

> 129 sì che la faccia ben con gli occhi attinghe  
> 130 di quella sozza e scapigliata fante,  
> 131 che là si graffia con l'unghie merdose,  
> 132 ed or s' accoscia, ed ora è in piede stante.  

that thy eyes may fully reach the visage of that unclean and dishevelled strumpet, who yonder with her filthy nails scratches herself, now cowering low, now standing on her feet.

> 129 „ Ἵν’ ἴδῃς καλῶς τὸ πρόσωπον ἐκείνης  
> (That you may clearly see the face of that)
>
> 130 „ Τῆς δεισαλέας ἀελλότριχος κόρης,  
> (Filthy, dishevelled wench,)
>
> 131 „ Τῆς ξεομένης ὄνυξι μεστοῖς κόπρου,  
> (Who scratches herself with dung-filled nails,)
>
> 132 „ Νῦν μὲν ὀκλαδόν, νῦν δ’ ὀρθῶς ἱσταμένης.  
> (Now crouching down, now standing upright.)

- *Ἵν’ ἴδῃς καλῶς τὸ πρόσωπον* ‘so that you may see the face clearly’ interprets *sì che … attinghe* as purpose: elided *ἵνα* takes *ἴδῃς* (2nd sg. aor. act. subj. of *ὁράω*). The metaphor of reaching and the instrumental *con gli occhi* give way to the verb of seeing with a direct object; *καλῶς* retains *ben*.
- *ἐκείνης τῆς δεισαλέας ἀελλότριχος κόρης* depends as a possessive genitive on *τὸ πρόσωπον*. In Greek the demonstrative stands outside the article–noun group. *δεισαλέας* and *ἀελλότριχος* are both genitive feminine singular with *κόρης*; the latter is the genitive of the poetic compound *ἀελλόθριξ*, literally ‘storm-haired’, for *scapigliata*. *κόρη* ‘girl’ does not by itself carry the depreciatory force of *fante* here.
- *Τῆς ξεομένης ὄνυξι μεστοῖς κόπρου* turns the relative clause *che là si graffia* into a second attributive group with repeated article. *ξεομένης* (gen. fem. sg. pres. mid. participle of *ξέω*) expresses the self-directed action by the middle voice. *λά* has no counterpart as a separate adverb in the Greek rendering of Italian *là*. Instrumental *ὄνυξι* (dat. pl. of *ὄνυξ*) takes the agreeing adjective *μεστοῖς*, itself governing genitive *κόπρου*: not simply ‘filthy’, but ‘full of dung’.
- *Νῦν μὲν ὀκλαδόν, νῦν δ’ ὀρθῶς ἱσταμένης* matches *ed or … ed ora* with correlated *νῦν μέν … νῦν δέ*. For *s’accoscia* there is only the adverb of posture *ὀκλαδόν* ‘squatting’; in the second member *ὀρθῶς* ‘upright’ and *ἱσταμένης* (gen. fem. sg. pres. mid. participle of *ἵστημι*, intransitive here) render *è in piede stante*. The genitive keeps the posture grammatically dependent on *κόρης*.

> 133 Taide è, la puttana che rispose  
> 134 al drudo suo, quando disse: ‘Ho io grazie  
> 135 grandi appo te?’ ‘Anzi, meravigliose.’  

It is Thais, the harlot, who answered her paramour, when he said: ‘Dost thou thank me much?’ ‘Nay, wondrously.’

> 133 „ Ἡ πόρνη Θαΐς ἐστιν, ἥτις συγκοίτῳ  
> (It is the harlot Thais, who to her paramour)
>
> 134 „ Ἀπεκρίθη λέγοντι· ‘“ Μεγάλας δή μοι  
> (Answered when he said: 'Great indeed to me)
>
> 135 „ Χάριτας ὄφλεις;”’—“ Ναὶ μὴν καὶ θαυμασίας ! ” —  
> (Are the thanks you owe?'—'Yes, and marvellous ones!'—)

- *Ἡ πόρνη Θαΐς ἐστιν* places the articular description first and the identifying name beside the copula, rather than beginning *Taide è*. *ἥτις* (nom. fem. sg. of the compound relative *ὅστις*) continues the identification with a descriptive relative clause; it has a definite antecedent here despite its indefinite formation.
- *συγκοίτῳ ἀπεκρίθη λέγοντι* ‘answered her bedfellow as he was speaking’ brings the addressee before the verb. *ἀπεκρίθη* is the 3rd sg. aor. passive in form of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, with the active sense ‘answered’; it governs the dative *συγκοίτῳ*. No possessive translates *suo*. *λέγοντι* (dat. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *λέγω*) agrees with the addressee: the separate past clause *quando disse* becomes a participle expressing attendant speech.
- *Μεγάλας δή μοι χάριτας ὄφλεις;* reverses the grammatical perspective of *Ho io grazie grandi appo te?*: ‘do you, then, owe me great thanks?’ The speaker becomes the dative creditor *μοι*, and the addressee the unexpressed subject of *ὄφλεις*, a 2nd sg. active indicative meaning ‘owe’. *μεγάλας*, brought to the front and emphasized by *δή*, agrees with the delayed accusative plural *χάριτας*.
- *Ναὶ μὴν καὶ θαυμασίας* ‘yes indeed, even marvellous ones’ makes *Anzi* an emphatic affirmation and intensification rather than a correction introduced by a negative. *μήν* reinforces *ναί*; *καί* marks the further degree. *θαυμασίας* (acc. fem. pl. of *θαυμάσιος*) picks up *χάριτας* without repeating noun or verb, as *meravigliose* picks up *grazie*.

> 136 E quinci sien le nostre viste sazie.”  

And herewith let our view rest sated."

> 136 Ἀλλὰ νῦν ἅλις ἡμῶν τὰ βλέμματ᾽ εἶδεν.  
> (But now our eyes have seen enough.")

- *Ἀλλὰ νῦν* ‘but now’ changes *E quinci* into an adversative and a temporal transition. *ἅλις* is an adverb, ‘enough’: where *sien … sazie* is a jussive subjunctive with a predicate adjective, Greek asserts a completed act, *ἅλις … εἶδεν* ‘have seen enough’.
- *ἡμῶν τὰ βλέμματ’* ‘our eyes’ gives *le nostre viste* a possessive genitive before the article. *βλέμματ’* is elided *βλέμματα*, nominative neuter plural of *βλέμμα*; its verb *εἶδεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*) is singular by ordinary Greek neuter-plural agreement.

## Summary — Recasting the Clause, Keeping the Echo

Musurus often preserves Dante’s word order even where he changes the construction. He separates *οἷον* ‘of what sort’ from *σχῆμα* ‘shape’ just as Dante separates *quale* from *figura*, then answers the comparison with *τοιαύτην* ‘such’. The echo of *tal … tal* likewise survives in *τοιοῦτο … τοιαύτην*, with different endings marking subject and object. Particles carry much of the connective work: postpositive *δέ* often serves for *e*, *τοίνυν* marks the inference in *adunque*, and *μέν … δέ* distinguishes groups that Dante sets against one another through expressions of place. Recurrent crasis in forms such as *οὑμός* ‘my’ and *κἀκεῖθεν* ‘and from there’ gives article and possessive, or conjunction and adverb, the compass of a single word.

The most persistent change is from finite verb to participle. After verbs of seeing and hearing, an accusative participle presents the action as it is perceived: *τύπτοντας* ‘striking’ replaces *che li battean*. The construction can also change the viewpoint. In *τυπτομένην* ‘being struck’, the file remains the object of sight, but the scourge, subject of *scaccia* ‘drives on’, becomes an instrumental dative. Musurus does not simply subordinate wherever Dante coordinates: in *ὡς ταῦτ’ ἔλεγεν* ‘as he was saying this’, he makes *parlando* finite, then turns *e disse* into *λέγων* ‘saying’. The distinction is between the speech already in progress when the blow falls and the words that accompany it.

Musurus’ lexical choices range from close imitation to explicit interpretation. *Κακόβοθρον* ‘Evil-pit’ translates *Malebolge* as a singular compound; *μαστιγίας* ‘scourged wretch’ repeatedly gives a personal noun for an Italian passive participle. Elsewhere an image yields to its inferred sense: *φιλαργυρία* ‘love of money’ replaces *avaro seno*, and *οὐ πωλοῦνται* ‘are not sold’ makes a commercial assertion out of *da conio*. Some departures change the relation itself, not just the idiom: *αὐλών* ‘ravine’ stands for *argine* ‘bank’, and reciprocal *ἀλλήλας* ‘one another’ replaces reflexive *sè medesma*. The closing turn is quieter but equally precise: *ἅλις* ‘enough’ retains the sense of *sazie*, while the aorist *εἶδεν* ‘saw’ changes a wish that the looking should cease into a judgment that it has gone far enough.
