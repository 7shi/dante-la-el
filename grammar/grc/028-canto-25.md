# Inferno 25

## Lines 1–21 — The Thief’s Defiance, the Serpents, and the Centaur

> 1 Al fine delle sue parole il ladro  
> 2 le mani alzò con ambedue le fiche,  
> 3 gridando : “Togli, Dio, chè a te le squadro.”  

At the conclusion of his words, the thief raised up his hands with both the figs, shouting: “Take them, God, for at thee I aim them!”

> 1 Περάνας δὲ τοὺς λόγους τούτους ὁ κλέπτης  
> (And having finished these words, the thief)
>
> 2 Ὕψωσ᾽ ἄμφω τὰς χεῖρας, καὶ σκιμαλίζων  
> (Raised both his hands, and making an insulting gesture)
>
> 3 Ἔκραξε· “Λάβε, Θεέ· τάδε σοι πέμπω.”  
> (Cried out: “Take them, God; these I send to you.”)

- *Περάνας δὲ τοὺς λόγους τούτους ὁ κλέπτης* turns the temporal phrase *Al fine delle sue parole* into a participial clause: ‘having finished these words, the thief …’. *Περάνας* (nom. sg. masc., aor. act. participle of *περαίνω*) agrees with *ὁ κλέπτης*; the aorist marks the finishing as prior to the raising of the hands. Postpositive *δέ* links the sentence to what precedes. The demonstrative *τούτους*, in the normal position outside article and noun, replaces possessive *sue*.
- *Ὕψωσ᾽* is elided *ὕψωσε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ὑψόω*. *Ἄμφω* is the dual numeral ‘both’, but *τὰς χεῖρας* (acc. pl. of *χείρ*) is plural, not dual. Musurus transfers *ambedue* from *le fiche* to the hands. Instead of naming the two gestures, he uses *σκιμαλίζων* (nom. sg. masc., pres. act. participle of *σκιμαλίζω*), ‘making an insulting finger gesture’.
- *Καὶ σκιμαλίζων ἔκραξε* changes the hierarchy of *alzò … gridando*: Greek coordinates two finite aorists, ‘he raised … and … cried out’, making the gesture simultaneous with the second verb. *Ἔκραξε* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *κράζω*, with syllabic augment.
- *Λάβε* is the 2nd sg. second-aorist act. imperative of *λαμβάνω*, without an expressed object, like *Togli*. *Θεέ* is vocative singular. *Τάδε σοι πέμπω* gives ‘these things I send to you’: neuter plural *τάδε* (acc. of *ὅδε*) points to what the speaker is doing, rather than resuming a feminine noun as *le* does. The recipient is expressed by the dative *σοι*; *πέμπω* substitutes sending for aiming, and the explanatory *chè* has no Greek conjunction to match it.

> 4 Da indi in qua mi fur le serpi amiche,  

From this time forth the serpents were my friends;

> 4 Ἀλλ᾽ ἔκτοτε τοὺς ὄφεις φιλεῖν ἠρξάμην·  
> (But from that time I began to love the serpents;)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ ἔκτοτε* adds an adversative, ‘but from then on’ (*ἀλλ᾽* elides *ἀλλά*), and condenses *Da indi in qua* into one adverb. *Τοὺς ὄφεις φιλεῖν ἠρξάμην* reverses the construction of *mi fur le serpi amiche*: the serpents become the accusative object, and the speaker the subject, ‘I began to love the serpents’. *Ἠρξάμην* is 1st sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἄρχω*, middle *ἄρχομαι* ‘begin’, with temporal augment; its complement *φιλεῖν* is the contracted pres. act. infinitive of *φιλέω*, presenting the affection as continuing.

> 5 perch’ una gli s’ avvolse allora al collo,  
> 6 come dicesse : “Io non vo’ che più diche”:  

for one of them then coiled itself about his neck, as if saying: “Thou shalt speak no farther!”

> 5 Εἷς γὰρ περὶ τὸν αὐτοῦ λαιμὸν εἰλίχθη,  
> (For one coiled round about his neck,)
>
> 6 Ὡς ἂν εἰ λέγων· “Πλέον λαλεῖν σ᾽ οὐ θέλω.”  
> (As if saying: “I do not wish you to speak further.”)

- *Εἷς γάρ* renders *perch’ una* by an explanatory main clause with postpositive *γάρ*. Masculine *εἷς* ‘one’ takes its gender from *ὄφις*, not from feminine *serpe*. *Περὶ τὸν αὐτοῦ λαιμόν* has *περί* with the accusative ‘round’ and a possessive genitive *αὐτοῦ* ‘of him’ inside the noun phrase: the Italian personal dative *gli* becomes an explicit possessor of the neck. *Εἰλίχθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ἑλίσσω*, also *εἱλίσσω*) has the sense ‘wound itself’; there is no separate *allora*.
- *Ὡς ἂν εἰ λέγων* renders *come dicesse*. Italian has a finite imperfect subjunctive; Greek has *λέγων*, a pres. act. participle agreeing with *εἷς*. *Ὡς* with a participle can itself present an action as ostensible; *ἂν εἰ* reinforces the hypothetical comparison, as though introducing ‘as it would if …’, but no finite conditional verb follows. There is no Greek optative here.
- *Πλέον λαλεῖν σ᾽ οὐ θέλω* retains the act of willing in *Io non vo’*, rather than turning it into a direct prohibition. *Θέλω* is 1st sg. pres. act. ind.; *σ᾽* (elided *σε*) is the accusative subject of *λαλεῖν* (pres. act. infinitive of *λαλέω*), replacing finite *che … diche*. Adverbial *πλέον* ‘more, further’ modifies the speaking; *οὐ* negates *θέλω*.

> 7 ed un’ altra alle braccia, e rilegollo,  
> 8 ribadendo sè stessa sì dinanzi,  
> 9 che non potea con esse dare un crollo.  

and another about his arms; and it tied him again, rivetting itself in front so firmly, that he could not give a jog with them.

> 7 Τοὺς βραχίονας συνεδέσμευσεν ἄλλος,  
> (Another bound fast his arms,)
>
> 8 Προσηλούμενος ἐν τοῖς ἔμπροσθεν οὕτως,  
> (Riveting itself in front in such a way,)
>
> 9 Ὥστ᾽ οὐχ οἷος τ᾽ ἦν διασεῖσαι τὰ μέλη.  
> (That he was not able to shake his limbs.)

- *Τοὺς βραχίονας συνεδέσμευσεν ἄλλος* brings the arms to the front as direct object and postpones the subject, ‘the arms another bound fast’. Masculine *ἄλλος* answers *εἷς*. The single finite verb *συνεδέσμευσεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *συνδεσμεύω*, augmented after the prefix) absorbs both the elliptical *un’ altra alle braccia* and *e rilegollo*: Greek binds the arms together rather than binding ‘him’, and does not separately say ‘again’.
- *Προσηλούμενος* (nom. sg. masc., contracted pres. mid./pass. participle of *προσηλόω*, ‘nail fast’) agrees with *ἄλλος*. Its middle/passive form conveys the fastening of the serpent itself, where *ribadendo sè stessa* has an active gerund and an explicit reflexive object. *Ἐν τοῖς ἔμπροσθεν* uses the article in the dative plural with the local adverb, ‘in the parts in front’.
- *Οὕτως, ὥστ᾽ … ἦν* gives *sì … che* its Greek result construction: *ὥστ᾽* is elided *ὥστε*, here with an indicative of actual result. *Οὐχ οἷος τ᾽ ἦν* is ‘he was not able’ (*οἷός τε εἰμί* with infinitive); *οὐχ* stands before the rough breathing, *τ᾽* is elided *τε*, and *ἦν* is 3rd sg. imperfect of *εἰμί*. *Διασεῖσαι* (aor. act. infinitive of *διασείω*) takes *τὰ μέλη* ‘the limbs’ as object: instead of giving a shake *with* the arms, he cannot shake the limbs.

> 10 Ahi Pistoia, Pistoia, che non stanzi  
> 11 d’ incenerarti, sì che più non duri,  
> 12 poi che in mal far lo seme tuo avanzi!  

Ah, Pistoia! Pistoia! why dost thou not decree to turn thyself to ashes, that thou mayest endure no longer since thou outgoest thy seed in evil-doing?

> 10 —Αἴ! Πιστωρία, Πιστωρία, πῶς τέφρα  
> (—Ah! Pistoia, Pistoia, why to ashes)
>
> 11 Γενέσθ᾽ οὐ σπεύδεις, καὶ μὴ πλέον ὑπάρχειν,  
> (Do you not hasten to turn, and to exist no more,)
>
> 12 Ἐπεὶ σπέρμα σὸν ἐν κακίαις προβαίνει; —  
> (Since your seed advances in wickedness?—)

- *Αἴ! Πιστωρία, Πιστωρία* keeps the interjection and repeated address, with the Greek place-name in the vocative. *Πῶς … οὐ σπεύδεις* makes *che non stanzi* a question, ‘how is it that you do not hasten …?’ *Σπεύδεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *σπεύδω*) substitutes hastening for decreeing.
- *Τέφρα γενέσθ᾽* is ‘to become ash’: *γενέσθ᾽* is *γενέσθαι*, second-aorist mid. infinitive of *γίγνομαι*, with poetic elision before *οὐ*. Its understood subject is the city addressed, so its predicate *τέφρα* is nominative. The change of state expressed by *γίγνομαι* replaces the reflexive *incenerarti*. *Καὶ μὴ πλέον ὑπάρχειν* coordinates a second infinitive under *σπεύδεις*, ‘and to exist no longer’, in place of the finite *sì che più non duri*. Hence infinitival *μή*, distinct from the *οὐ* negating the indicative.
- *Ἐπεὶ σπέρμα σὸν ἐν κακίαις προβαίνει* retains causal *poi che* but changes who advances. Italian *avanzi* is second person, with *lo seme tuo* its object; Greek *προβαίνει* is 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of intransitive *προβαίνω*, with *σπέρμα σόν* as its neuter singular subject, ‘your seed advances’. There is no longer an expressed comparison. *Ἐν κακίαις* ‘in vices’ (dat. pl. of *κακία*) replaces the verbal *in mal far*.

> 13 Per tutti i cerchi dell’ inferno oscuri  
> 14 non vidi spirto in Dio tanto superbo,  
> 15 non quel che cadde a Tebe giù da’ muri.  

Through all the dark circles of Hell, I saw no spirit against God so proud, not _even_ him who fell at Thebes down from the walls.

> 13 Ἐν πᾶσιν ᾍδου τοῖς ζοφώδεσι κύκλοις  
> (In all the gloomy circles of Hell)
>
> 14 Οὐκ εἶδον οὕτω θρασὺ πρὸς Θεὸν πνεῦμα,  
> (I saw not a spirit so insolent toward God,)
>
> 15 Οὔτε τὸν πεσόντ᾽ ἐκ τειχῶν τῶν Θηβαίων.  
> (Not even him who fell from the Theban walls.)

- *Ἐν πᾶσιν ᾍδου τοῖς ζοφώδεσι κύκλοις* uses local *ἐν* with the dative rather than directional ‘through’. *Πᾶσιν* stands outside the articular group, ‘in all the … circles’; *ζοφώδεσι* (dat. pl. of *ζοφώδης*) stands inside it as an attributive adjective. The dependent genitive *ᾍδου* ‘of Hades’ is brought forward between the quantifier and the article, unlike *dell’ inferno*.
- *Οὐκ εἶδον* is 1st sg. second-aorist act. ind. of *ὁράω*, from its suppletive aorist stem. *Οὕτω θρασὺ πρὸς Θεὸν πνεῦμα* puts the noun last: *θρασύ* (acc. sg. neut. of *θρασύς*) gives *superbo* the force ‘bold, insolent’; *οὕτω* is the degree adverb corresponding to *tanto*. *Πρὸς Θεόν*, with the accusative, gives the direction of the insolence in *in Dio*.
- *Οὔτε τὸν πεσόντ᾽* continues the negative, ‘nor [did I see] the one who fell’. Instead of *quel che cadde*, article and participle together designate the person. *Πεσόντ᾽* is elided *πεσόντα* (acc. sg. masc., second-aorist act. participle of *πίπτω*); the masculine refers to the individual, not grammatically to neuter *πνεῦμα*. *Ἐκ τειχῶν τῶν Θηβαίων* is ‘from the walls of the Thebans’: *ἐκ* governs genitive *τειχῶν*, while *τῶν Θηβαίων* is a dependent genitive plural. Locative *a Tebe* thus becomes an expression of possession; *giù* is not separately rendered.

> 16 Ei si fuggì, che non parlò più verbo ;  

He fled, speaking not another word;

> 16 Αὐτὸς δ᾽ ἔφυγεν, οὔτ᾽ εἶπε πλέον λέξιν.  
> (And he fled away, nor spoke a word more.)

- *Αὐτὸς δ᾽ ἔφυγεν* retains the expressed subject *Ei* with nominative *αὐτός*, followed by connective *δ᾽* (elided *δέ*). *Ἔφυγεν* is 3rd sg. second-aorist act. ind. of *φεύγω*; Greek needs no counterpart to Italian *si*. *Οὔτ᾽ εἶπε* (elided *οὔτε*, ‘nor’) coordinates a second finite verb instead of subordinating it with *che*. *Εἶπε* is the suppletive second-aorist active, 3rd sg., of *λέγω*; accusative *λέξιν* ‘a word’ renders *verbo*, with adverbial *πλέον* for *più*.

> 17 ed io vidi un Centauro pien di rabbia  
> 18 venir chiamando : “Ov’è, ov’è l’ acerbo ?”  

and I saw a Centaur, full of rage, come crying: “Where is, where is the surly one?”

> 17 Εἶδον δ᾽ ἥκοντα Κένταυρον πλήρη λύσσης  
> (And I saw coming a Centaur full of rage,)
>
> 18 Κράζοντα· “Ποῦ ’στιν ὁ κατάρατος, ποῦ ’στιν;”  
> (Crying out: “Where is the accursed one, where is he?”)

- *Εἶδον δ᾽ ἥκοντα Κένταυρον* renders *ed io vidi un Centauro … venir* with the Greek construction for direct perception: accusative object and agreeing supplementary participle. *Ἥκοντα* (acc. sg. masc., pres. act. participle of *ἥκω*, ‘be come, be here’) is placed before its noun; unlike *venir*, it characteristically views the arrival from its endpoint. *Πλήρη* (acc. sg. masc. of *πλήρης*) agrees with *Κένταυρον* and governs genitive *λύσσης* ‘of rage’.
- *Κράζοντα* (acc. sg. masc., pres. act. participle of *κράζω*) agrees with the same object, giving *chiamando* as an accompanying action. *Ποῦ ’στιν ὁ κατάρατος, ποῦ ’στιν;* places the subject between the two questions rather than after the repetition. *’Στιν* shows prodelision of the initial vowel of *ἐστίν* after interrogative *ποῦ* ‘where?’; final *-ν* is movable. The substantivized adjective *ὁ κατάρατος*, ‘the accursed one’, interprets rather than literally reproduces *l’acerbo*.

> 19 Maremma non cred’ io che tante n’ abbia,  
> 20 quante bisce egli avea su per la groppa,  
> 21 infin dove comincia nostra labbia.  

Maremma, I do believe, has not so many snakes as he had on his haunch, to where our human form begins.

> 19 Οὐκ οἴομαι Μαρέμμαν τοσούτους τρέφειν,  
> (I do not think Maremma breeds so many)
>
> 20 Ὅσους εἶχ᾽ αὐτὸς ὄφεις ἐπὶ τῶν νώτων,  
> (Serpents as he had upon his back,)
>
> 21 Ἔνθαπερ τἀνθρώπινον ἄρχεται σχῆμα.  
> (Right where the human form begins.)

- *Οὐκ οἴομαι Μαρέμμαν τοσούτους τρέφειν* makes ‘I do not think’ the explicit governing expression, followed by accusative and infinitive instead of *che … abbia*. *Οἴομαι* is 1st sg. pres. mid. ind., middle in form but active in meaning; *Μαρέμμαν* is the accusative subject of *τρέφειν* (pres. act. infinitive of *τρέφω*). The place thus ‘nourishes’ or ‘rears’ rather than simply ‘has’. Masculine accusative plural *τοσούτους* ‘so many’ anticipates *ὄφεις*; Italian partitive *n’* has no separate equivalent.
- *Ὅσους εἶχ᾽ αὐτὸς ὄφεις* completes the correlation *τοσούτους … ὅσους*, ‘as many … as’. Relative *ὅσους* agrees with the delayed *ὄφεις* (acc. pl. of *ὄφις*), so that the explicit noun appears in the second clause as *bisce* does. *Εἶχ᾽* is elided *εἶχε*, 3rd sg. imperfect act. ind. of *ἔχω*, with the irregular augment *ει-*; nominative *αὐτός* corresponds to expressed *egli*. *Ἐπὶ τῶν νώτων* uses *ἐπί* with the genitive for position ‘upon the back’, with plural *νῶτα* for singular *groppa*.
- *Ἔνθαπερ* is local *ἔνθα* strengthened by enclitic *-περ*, ‘just where’. It identifies a point but does not expressly mark the limit conveyed by *infin*. *Τἀνθρώπινον* is crasis of *τὸ ἀνθρώπινον*; the article and adjective belong with final *σχῆμα*, nominative neuter singular subject of *ἄρχεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *ἄρχω*, ‘begins’). *Τὸ ἀνθρώπινον … σχῆμα* interprets *nostra labbia* as ‘the human form’, replacing the first-person possessive with an adjective of kind.

## Lines 22–54 — Cacus and the Six-Footed Serpent

> 22 Sopra le spalle, dietro dalla coppa,  
> 23 con l'ale aperte gli giacea un draco;  
> 24 e quello affoca qualunque s'intoppa.  

Over his shoulders, behind the head, a dragon lay with outstretched wings; and it sets on fire every one he meets.

> 22 Ἐπὶ τῶν ὤμων ὄπισθεν τοῦ τραχήλου,  
> (Upon his shoulders, behind his neck,)
>
> 23 Πτεροῖς ἀνεῳγμένοις ἵστατο δράκων,  
> (With outspread wings stood a dragon,)
>
> 24 Φλοξὶ βάλλων ἅπαντας, οἷς ἂν ἐντύχοι.  
> (Smiting with flames all whomsoever it might meet.)

- *Ἐπὶ τῶν ὤμων* ‘upon the shoulders’ uses *ἐπί* with the genitive of position; *ὄπισθεν τοῦ τραχήλου* ‘behind the neck’ makes *dietro dalla coppa* a second, more precise indication of place. The articles suffice for the body parts; Greek does not express a possessor. *Πτεροῖς ἀνεῳγμένοις* ‘with wings opened’ is a dative of attendant circumstance; *ἀνεῳγμένοις* is the perfect middle-passive participle of *ἀνοίγω*, dative neuter plural, describing the resulting position of the wings.
- Greek retains the delayed subject *δράκων* but changes the posture: *ἵστατο* (3rd sg. imperfect middle of *ἵστημι*) gives ‘stood’ for *giacea* ‘lay’. There is no separate equivalent of the possessive dative *gli*. Instead of resuming the dragon with *e quello* and a finite present, Musurus continues with *φλοξὶ βάλλων* ‘striking with flames’: *βάλλων* is a present active participle, nominative masculine singular agreeing with *δράκων*, and *φλοξί* the instrumental dative plural of *φλόξ*.
- *ἅπαντας, οἷς ἂν ἐντύχοι* expands the singular *qualunque* into ‘all those whom he might meet’. The antecedent is accusative as object of *βάλλων*, the relative dative because *ἐντυγχάνω* takes the dative of the person encountered. *ἐντύχοι* is 3rd sg. second aorist active optative; with *ἄν* it expresses a possible encounter, rather than simply reproducing the Italian present.

> 25 Lo mio maestro disse: "Quegli è Caco,  
> 26 che sotto il sasso di monte Aventino  
> 27 di sangue fece spesse volte laco.  

My Master said: "That is Cacus, who, beneath the rock of Mount Aventine, full often made a lake of blood.

> 25 Οὑμὸς δ᾽ εἶπε Προστάτης· “Ὅδ᾽ ἐστὶ Κάκος,  
> (And my Master said: “This is Cacus,)
>
> 26 „ Ὃς ὄρους Αὐεντίνου ὑπὸ τὰς πέτρας  
> („ Who beneath the rocks of Mount Aventine)
>
> 27 „ Ἐποίησ᾽ ἐξ αἱμάτων πολλάκις λίμνην.  
> („ Oftentimes made a lake of blood.)

- *Οὑμὸς δ᾽ εἶπε Προστάτης* separates the possessive from its noun by the speech verb. *Οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός* (the rough breathing persists), answering to *lo mio*; *Προστάτης* means ‘one who stands before’, hence ‘protector, leader’, rather than literally *maestro*. *δ᾽* is elided postpositive *δέ*; *εἶπε* is 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative, the aorist used with *λέγω*.
- *Ὅδ᾽ ἐστὶ Κάκος* ‘this one is Cacus’ substitutes the near demonstrative *ὅδε* (with final vowel elided) for *quegli*. *Ὃς* is nominative as subject of *ἐποίησ᾽* (elided *ἐποίησε*, 3rd sg. aorist active indicative of *ποιέω*). *ὄρους Αὐεντίνου*, genitive noun and agreeing adjective, precedes the phrase it qualifies: ‘of Mount Aventine, under the rocks’. *ὑπὸ τὰς πέτρας* uses the plural for *sotto il sasso* and the accusative in a local sense.
- *ἐξ αἱμάτων … λίμνην* ‘out of blood … a lake’ takes *di sangue* as the material from which the lake was made. *αἱμάτων* is genitive plural of *αἷμα*, a plural suited to shed blood; *λίμνην* is the direct object. As in *fece spesse volte laco*, the adverb of repetition *πολλάκις* comes between verb and object; the aorist collects the repeated acts into a whole.

> 28 Non va co' suoi fratei per un cammino,  
> 29 per lo furar frodolente che fece  
> 30 del grande armento, ch'egli ebbe a vicino:  

He goes not with his brethren on one same road, because of the cunning theft he made [of] the great herd that lay near him:

> 28 „ Οὐ σὺν ἀδελφοῖς ὁδῷ μιᾷ βαδίζει·  
> („ He does not walk along one road with his brothers;)
>
> 29 „ Ὑπέκλεψε γὰρ δολίῳ τινὶ τρόπῳ  
> („ For he stole away by a certain crafty way)
>
> 30 „ Ἧς ἔτυχ᾽ ἐγγὺς αὑτοῦ μεγάλης ποίμνης.  
> („ From the great herd that chanced to be near him.)

- *Οὐ σὺν ἀδελφοῖς ὁδῷ μιᾷ βαδίζει* keeps the initial negative and the final idea of going, but gives the two datives different constructions: *σύν* governs *ἀδελφοῖς* for the companions, whereas bare *ὁδῷ μιᾷ* is the route, ‘by one road’. *μιᾷ* is dative feminine singular of *εἷς*. The possessive *suoi* is implicit; *βαδίζει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *βαδίζω*) is specifically ‘walks’.
- *Ὑπέκλεψε γὰρ δολίῳ τινὶ τρόπῳ* replaces *per lo furar frodolente che fece* with an independent explanation introduced by postpositive *γάρ*: ‘for he secretly stole in a certain crafty manner’. *ὑπέκλεψε* is 3rd sg. aorist active indicative of *ὑποκλέπτω*, with internal augment; *δολίῳ τινὶ τρόπῳ* is a dative of manner, with enclitic indefinite *τινί*. Thus the theft becomes the finite verb and the adjective qualifying the theft becomes a qualification of the manner.
- *Ἧς ἔτυχ᾽ ἐγγὺς αὑτοῦ μεγάλης ποίμνης* compresses the source of the theft and the relative clause into one genitival group, with *ἧς* anticipating *μεγάλης ποίμνης*. The sense is partitive, ‘from the great herd’; no accusative naming the animals taken is supplied. *ἔτυχ᾽* is elided *ἔτυχε* (3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *τυγχάνω*); the turn with ‘chance’ replaces *ch'egli ebbe a vicino*. The construction is compressed rather than an ordinary Greek clause with ‘herd’ as nominative subject. *ἐγγύς* governs the genitive *αὑτοῦ*, the contracted reflexive *ἑαυτοῦ*, ‘near himself’.

> 31 onde cessàr le sue opere biece  
> 32 sotto la mazza d'Ercole, che forse  
> 33 gliene diè cento, e non sentì le diece."  

whence his crooked actions ceased beneath the club of Hercules, who gave him perhaps a hundred _blows_ with it; and he felt not the _first ten._"

> 31 „ Ἀλλ᾽ αὐτοῦ τὰ πανοῦργα κατέπαυσ᾽ ἔργα  
> („ Yet his villainous deeds were ended by)
>
> 32 „ Ἡρακλέους ῥόπαλον, ἐπενεγκόντος  
> („ The club of Hercules, who dealt him)
>
> 33 „ Ἑκατὸν πληγάς, ὧν οὐκ ᾔσθετο δέκα.”  
> („ A hundred blows, of which he felt not ten.”)

- *Ἀλλ᾽* (elided *ἀλλά*) marks a contrast rather than the consequence expressed by *onde*. *αὐτοῦ τὰ πανοῦργα … ἔργα* ‘his villainous deeds’ becomes the accusative object, not the subject: *κατέπαυσ᾽* (elided *κατέπαυσε*, 3rd sg. aorist active indicative of *καταπαύω*) is transitive, ‘put a stop to’. The delayed subject is *Ἡρακλέους ῥόπαλον* ‘Hercules’ club’, where Italian has the prepositional *sotto la mazza d'Ercole*.
- *ἐπενεγκόντος* is the genitive masculine singular second aorist active participle of *ἐπιφέρω*, agreeing with *Ἡρακλέους*: ‘Hercules having dealt a hundred blows’. It takes *ἑκατὸν πληγάς* as object; the noun implicit in *cento* is supplied, but neither *forse* nor the pronominal elements of *gliene* receive separate equivalents.
- *ὧν οὐκ ᾔσθετο δέκα* ‘of which he did not feel ten’ subordinates the final assertion with a partitive relative genitive. *ᾔσθετο* is 3rd sg. second aorist middle indicative of *αἰσθάνομαι*, with a change of subject from the participle. *δέκα* is indeclinable; there is neither an article corresponding to *le* nor a word specifying “first”.

> 34 Mentre che sì parlava, ed ei trascorse,  
> 35 e tre spiriti venner sotto noi,  

Whilst he thus spake, the Centaur ran past, and also under us there came three spirits,

> 34 Ταῦτα λέγοντος, διέβη μὲν ἐκεῖνος,  
> (While he was speaking thus, that one passed on,)
>
> 35 Κάτωθεν δ᾽ ἡμῶν ἦλθε πνεύματα τρία·  
> (And below us came three spirits;)

- *Ταῦτα λέγοντος* ‘while [he] was saying these things’ renders *mentre che sì parlava* by a genitive absolute with the subject understood. *λέγοντος* is present active participle, genitive masculine singular of *λέγω*, simultaneous with *διέβη*; *ταῦτα* is its neuter plural object in place of adverbial *sì*. *διέβη* (3rd sg. root aorist active indicative of *διαβαίνω*) gives ‘passed across’ for *trascorse*, without specifying running; *ἐκεῖνος* ‘that one’ answers to *ei*.
- Postpositive *μέν … δέ* pairs the departure and arrival. *Κάτωθεν … ἡμῶν* ‘below us’ takes the genitive after the local adverb. *ἦλθε* (3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι*) is singular in accordance with the usual Attic construction of a neuter plural subject, *πνεύματα τρία*, unlike plural *venner*.

> 36 de' quai nè io nè il duca mio s'accorse,  
> 37 se non quando gridar: "Chi siete voi?"  

whom neither I nor my Guide perceived, until they cried: "Who are ye?"

> 36 Ἀλλ᾽ ἡμῶν οὐδέτερος αὐτοὺς κατεῖδε,  
> (Yet neither of us caught sight of them,)
>
> 37 Πλὴν ὅτ᾽ ἔκραξαν· “Ὑμεῖς ἐστὲ δὴ τίνες;”  
> (Save when they cried out: “Who, pray, are you?”)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ ἡμῶν οὐδέτερος αὐτοὺς κατεῖδε* replaces the Italian relative and paired negatives with an adversative main clause: ‘but neither of us saw them’. *ἡμῶν* is the partitive genitive with singular *οὐδέτερος*; *αὐτούς* is masculine by sense despite neuter *πνεύματα*. *κατεῖδε* is 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *καθοράω*, ‘caught sight of’, rather than reflexive *s'accorse*.
- *Πλὴν ὅτ᾽* ‘except when’ closely follows *se non quando*; *ὅτ᾽* is elided *ὅτε*. *ἔκραξαν* is 3rd pl. aorist active indicative of *κράζω*. In the question Musurus puts emphatic *ὑμεῖς* first and interrogative *τίνες* last, reversing *Chi siete voi?*; *ἐστέ* is 2nd pl. present indicative of *εἰμί*, and *δή* lends insistence, ‘who, then?’

> 38 Per che nostra novella si ristette,  
> 39 ed intendemmo pure ad essi poi.  

Our story therefore paused, and we then gave heed to them alone.

> 38 Εὐθὺς δ᾽ ἐπαυσάμεθα τῆς ὁμιλίας,  
> (And straightway we ceased from our conversation,)
>
> 39 Ἐπὶ τούτους ἀτενῶς τὸν νοῦν προσχόντες.  
> (Fixing our minds intently upon these.)

- *Εὐθὺς δ᾽* ‘and straightway’ substitutes immediate sequence for causal *per che*. *ἐπαυσάμεθα τῆς ὁμιλίας* ‘we ceased from conversation’ makes the speakers the subject instead of *nostra novella*. *ἐπαυσάμεθα* is 1st pl. aorist middle indicative of *παύω*; the middle in the sense ‘cease’ takes the genitive of what one desists from.
- *Ἐπὶ τούτους … τὸν νοῦν προσχόντες* makes the second Italian finite verb a circumstantial participle agreeing with the understood ‘we’: *προσχόντες* is nominative masculine plural, second aorist active of *προσέχω*. ‘Turning the mind’ is the Greek idiom for paying attention; *ἐπί* with accusative *τούτους* marks its direction. *ἀτενῶς* ‘intently’ stresses concentration rather than explicitly restricting it with *pure*; *poi* has no separate equivalent.

> 40 Io non gli conoscea; ma ei seguette,  
> 41 come suol seguitar per alcun caso,  

I knew them not; but it happened, as usually it happens by some chance,

> 40 Οὐκ ἐγνώριζον αὐτούς, ἀλλὰ συνέβη,  
> (I did not recognize them, but it came to pass,)
>
> 41 Ὡς συμβαίνει συχνάκις ἐκ ταὐτομάτου,  
> (As happens often of its own accord,)

- *Οὐκ ἐγνώριζον αὐτούς* preserves the imperfect of *non gli conoscea*: *ἐγνώριζον* is here 1st sg. imperfect active indicative of *γνωρίζω*, with no expressed subject pronoun. Impersonal *συνέβη* (3rd sg. root aorist active indicative of *συμβαίνω*) takes up *ei seguette*; what happened is given by the clause in line 42.
- *Ὡς συμβαίνει* repeats the same Greek verb in the present, reproducing the repetition *seguette … seguitar*. The habitual force of *suol* is conveyed by the present and *συχνάκις* ‘often’. *ἐκ ταὐτομάτου* ‘of its own accord, by chance’ renders *per alcun caso*: *ταὐτομάτου* is crasis of *τοῦ αὐτομάτου*, substantivized neuter genitive after *ἐκ*.

> 42 che l'un nomare un altro convenette,  
> 43 dicendo: "Cianfa dove fia rimaso?"  

that one had to name another, saying: "Where has Cianfa stopt?"

> 42 Ὅτι προσεφθέγξατο τὸν ἄλλον ἄλλος,  
> (That one addressed another,)
>
> 43 Λέγων· “Ποῦ ποτ᾽ ἆρ᾽ ἔμεινεν ὁ Κιάμφας;”  
> (Saying: “Where, I wonder, has Cianfa remained?”)

- *Ὅτι προσεφθέγξατο τὸν ἄλλον ἄλλος* gives the content of *συνέβη* as a finite *ὅτι*-clause, closely following Italian *che*; an infinitive would also be idiomatic with *συνέβη*. *προσεφθέγξατο* is 3rd sg. aorist middle indicative of deponent *προσφθέγγομαι*, ‘addressed’. The accusative *τὸν ἄλλον* precedes nominative *ἄλλος*: ‘one addressed the other’. Musurus thus interprets *nomare* as speaking to someone, rather than mentioning a name, and drops the necessity expressed by *convenette*.
- *Λέγων* (present active participle, nominative masculine singular) attaches *dicendo* to *ἄλλος*. *Ποῦ ποτ᾽ ἆρ᾽* combines interrogative *ποῦ* with elided enclitic *ποτε* and interrogative *ἆρα*: ‘where ever, I wonder’. The particles convey the uncertainty of *dove fia rimaso*; the verb itself is simply *ἔμεινεν*, 3rd sg. aorist active indicative of *μένω*, not a future perfect. The name, moved to the end, has a Greek nominative ending and article, *ὁ Κιάμφας*.

> 44 Per ch'io, acciocchè il duca stesse attento,  
> 45 mi posi il dito su dal mento al naso.  

Whereat I, in order that my Guide might stand attentive, placed my finger upwards from the chin to the nose.

> 44 Ἐγὼ δ᾽ ἵν᾽ ὁ Προστάτης ἀτενὴς εἴη,  
> (And I, so that my Master might be attentive,)
>
> 45 Ἐθέμην τὸν δάκτυλον ἐπὶ τὰ χείλη.  
> (Placed my finger upon my lips.)

- *Ἐγὼ δ᾽* ‘and I’ keeps the explicit *io* but weakens *per ch'*. *ἵν᾽* is elided *ἵνα*, introducing a purpose clause; *εἴη* is 3rd sg. present optative of *εἰμί*, the optative being available after the past main verb. Predicate *ἀτενής* ‘intent’ agrees with *ὁ Προστάτης*: *stesse attento* becomes ‘might be attentive’.
- *Ἐθέμην* is 1st sg. second aorist middle indicative of *τίθημι*. The middle suits an action performed with one’s own finger, corresponding to *mi posi*; *τὸν δάκτυλον* is its object. *ἐπὶ τὰ χείλη* ‘onto the lips’ uses *ἐπί* with the accusative of destination and gives only the point of contact, replacing the more exact direction *su dal mento al naso*.

> 46 Se tu sei or, lettore, a creder lento  
> 47 ciò ch'io dirò, non sarà maraviglia,  
> 48 chè io, che il vidi, appena il mi consento.  

If thou art now, O Reader, slow to credit what I have to tell, it will be no wonder: for I who saw it, scarce allow it to myself.

> 46 Εἰ δὲ πιστεύειν διστάζεις, ἀναγνῶστα,  
> (And if you hesitate to believe, O reader,)
>
> 47 Εἰς ὅσ᾽ ἤδη λέξω σοι, τοῦτ᾽ οὐ θαυμάζω·  
> (What I am now about to tell you, I wonder not at this;)
>
> 48 Κἀγὼ γὰρ αὐτὸς ἰδὼν μόλις πιστεύω.  
> (For I myself, having seen it, scarcely believe.)

- *Εἰ δὲ πιστεύειν διστάζεις, ἀναγνῶστα* ‘and if you hesitate to believe, reader’ uses *εἰ* with the present indicative *διστάζεις* (2nd sg. of *διστάζω*); the complementary present active infinitive *πιστεύειν* precedes it. The adjective in *sei … lento* thus becomes a verb of hesitation. *ἀναγνῶστα* is the vocative singular of *ἀναγνώστης*, set at the end rather than inside the predicate.
- *Εἰς ὅσ᾽ ἤδη λέξω σοι* ‘in all that I shall now tell you’ supplies the object of belief. *ὅσ᾽* is elided *ὅσα*, neuter accusative plural: the relative is object of *λέξω* within its clause, and the whole expression follows *εἰς*. *πιστεύειν εἰς* is less characteristically Attic than *πιστεύειν* with the dative. *λέξω* is 1st sg. future active indicative of *λέγω*, with added recipient *σοι*; *ἤδη* attaches ‘now’ to the telling rather than to the reader’s present hesitation.
- *τοῦτ᾽ οὐ θαυμάζω* ‘I do not wonder at this’ replaces impersonal future *non sarà maraviglia* with a personal present and accusative object (*τοῦτ᾽* for *τοῦτο*). *Κἀγὼ γάρ* ‘for I too’ combines crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ* with explanatory *γάρ*. *αὐτὸς ἰδών* ‘myself having seen’ turns *che il vidi* into a nominative masculine singular second aorist active participle of *ὁράω*, reinforced by intensive *αὐτός*. *μόλις πιστεύω* ‘I scarcely believe’ repeats the verb used of the reader, dispensing with the object and reflexive pronouns of *il mi consento*.

> 49 Com'io tenea levate in lor le ciglia,  
> 50 ed un serpente con sei piè si lancia  
> 51 dinanzi all'uno, e tutto a lui s'appiglia.  

Whilst I kept gazing on them, lo! a serpent with six feet darts up in front of one, and fastens itself all upon him.

> 49 Ἐν ᾧ πρὸς αὐτοὺς εἰστήκειν ἀτενίζων,  
> (While I stood gazing intently at them,)
>
> 50 Αἴφνης ὥρμησ᾽ ἐφ᾽ ἑνὸς ὄφις ἑξάπους,  
> (Suddenly a six-footed serpent darted at one of them,)
>
> 51 Ἐπ᾽ αὐτὸν περιπλεχθεὶς ὅλος ἐφ᾽ ὅλον.  
> (Twining itself around him, whole upon whole.)

- *Ἐν ᾧ* ‘in which [time], while’ introduces the simultaneous action. *εἰστήκειν* is 1st sg. pluperfect active indicative of *ἵστημι* (compare Attic *εἱστήκη*), with the past stative sense ‘I stood’. Present active *ἀτενίζων*, nominative masculine singular, gives the continuing act of gazing, with *πρὸς αὐτούς* for its direction. Musurus renders the sense of *tenea levate in lor le ciglia*, not the image of raised brows.
- *Αἴφνης* ‘suddenly’ conveys the abruptness of Italian *ed* introducing the main event. *ὥρμησ᾽* (elided *ὥρμησε*, 3rd sg. aorist active indicative of *ὁρμάω*) replaces the vivid reflexive present *si lancia* with an intransitive narrative aorist. *ὄφις ἑξάπους* condenses *un serpente con sei piè* into a noun and an agreeing compound adjective, ‘six-footed’. *ἐφ᾽ ἑνός* retains the genitive of ‘one’ in *dinanzi all'uno*; for motion against someone the accusative would be more usual. *ἐφ᾽* represents *ἐπί* elided and aspirated before the rough breathing.
- *Ἐπ᾽ αὐτὸν περιπλεχθείς* ‘having twined itself about him’ replaces the second finite present *s'appiglia* with a nominative masculine singular aorist passive participle of *περιπλέκω*, passive in form but describing the serpent’s own entwinement. Here *ἐπί* does take the accusative. *ὅλος ἐφ᾽ ὅλον* ‘whole upon whole’ develops *tutto a lui*: the nominative refers to the serpent, the accusative to the entire person it grasps.

> 52 Coi piè di mezzo gli avvinse la pancia,  
> 53 e con gli anterior le braccia prese;  
> 54 poi gli addentò e l'una e l'altra guancia.  

With its middle feet it clasped his belly, with the anterior it seized his arms; then fixed its teeth in both his cheeks.

> 52 Τοῖς μέσοις ποσὶ κατέσχε τὴν γαστέρα,  
> (With its middle feet it held fast his belly,)
>
> 53 Τοῖς δ᾽ ἐμπροσθίοις συνέλαβε τὰς χεῖρας·  
> (And with its forefeet it seized his arms;)
>
> 54 Εἶτ᾽ ἔδακ᾽ ὀδὰξ παρειῶν ἑκατέραν.  
> (Then it bit with its teeth each of his cheeks.)

- *Τοῖς μέσοις ποσὶ κατέσχε τὴν γαστέρα* keeps instrument, verb and object in the Italian order. *ποσί* is dative plural of *πούς*, qualified by *μέσοις*; *κατέσχε* is 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *κατέχω*, ‘held fast’ rather than *avvinse* ‘wound about’. *τὴν γαστέρα* is accusative singular of *γαστήρ*; the possessor expressed by Italian *gli* is understood.
- *Τοῖς δ᾽ ἐμπροσθίοις* ‘and with the front ones’ leaves *ποσί* understood just as *con gli anterior* leaves *piè* understood. *συνέλαβε* (3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *συλλαμβάνω*) takes *τὰς χεῖρας*, accusative plural of *χείρ*, a word that can denote the arm as well as the hand.
- *Εἶτ᾽ ἔδακ᾽ ὀδάξ* has successive elisions, of *εἶτα* ‘then’ and *ἔδακε* ‘bit’ (3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *δάκνω*). Poetic *ὀδάξ* ‘with the teeth’ reinforces the verb and makes explicit what is already contained in *addentò*. *παρειῶν ἑκατέραν* ‘each of the two cheeks’ replaces the repeated Italian articles in *e l'una e l'altra guancia* with a partitive genitive plural of *παρειά* and a distributive accusative feminine singular of *ἑκάτερος*. The reference is to a pair, but neither form is grammatically dual.

## Lines 55–87 — The Fusion and the Serpent’s Stroke

> 55 Gli diretani alle cosce distese,  

The hinder *feet* it stretched along his thighs;

> 55 Ἐξέτειν᾿ ἐπὶ μηρῶν τοὺς ὀπισθίους,  
> (It stretched its hind feet upon his thighs,)

- *Ἐξέτειν᾿* is elided *ἐξέτεινε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐκτείνω*, with the augment after the preverb and no *-σ-* in the liquid aorist. Musurus puts the verb first, where Dante puts *distese* last.
- *Ἐπὶ μηρῶν* ‘upon thighs’ gives the place of contact with *ἐπί* + genitive; the possessor expressed by Italian *gli* is understood. *Τοὺς ὀπισθίους* ‘the hind ones’ (acc. pl. masc.) leaves *πόδας* ‘feet’ unexpressed, much as *i diretani* leaves the noun to be supplied.

> 56 e miseli la coda tr’ambe e due,  
> 57 e dietro per le ren su la ritese.  

and put its tail between the two, and bent it upwards on his loins behind.

> 56 Μέσον αὐτῶν τὴν οὐρὰν διαπεράσας,  
> (Passing its tail between them,)
>
> 57 Ἣν ἠνώρθωσεν ἐπὶ νεφροὺς ὀπίσω.  
> (Which it raised upright behind against his loins.)

- The two coordinated verbs *miseli … e … ritese* become a participle and a relative clause. *Μέσον αὐτῶν* ‘between them’ uses *μέσον* adverbially with the genitive; *αὐτῶν* refers back to the thighs without repeating the numeral in *tr’ambe e due*. *Τὴν οὐράν* is the object of *διαπεράσας* ‘having passed through’ (nom. sg. masc., aor. act. participle of *διαπεράω*). The participle refers to the agent of *ἐξέτεινε*, construed as masculine.
- *Ἣν* (acc. sg. fem.) takes up *οὐράν* as the object of *ἠνώρθωσεν* ‘it straightened up’ (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀνορθόω*, with initial temporal augment). Where Dante has *dietro per le ren su*, Greek puts the upward motion into the verb, uses *ἐπὶ νεφρούς* with the accusative for its direction, and postpones *ὀπίσω* ‘behind’.

> 58 Ellera abbarbicata mai non fue  
> 59 ad arbor sì, come l’orribil fiera  
> 60 per l’altrui membra avviticchiò le sue;  

Ivy was never so rooted to a tree, as round the other's limbs the hideous monster entwined its own;

> 58 Οὔποτε κισσὸς δένδρῳ προσεκολλήθη  
> (Never did ivy cling to a tree)
>
> 59 Οὕτως ἰσχυρῶς, ὡς τὸ φρικτὸν θηρίον  
> (So strongly as the dreadful beast)
>
> 60 Τοῖς ἐκείνου συνέπλεξ᾿ αὑτοῦ τὰ μέλη.  
> (Intertwined its own limbs with his.)

- *Οὔποτε κισσὸς δένδρῳ προσεκολλήθη* ‘never did ivy adhere to a tree’: *προσεκολλήθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *προσκολλάω*, augmented after *προσ-*) replaces *abbarbicata … fue*. The Greek speaks of adhesion rather than rooting; *δένδρῳ* is the dative of the thing to which the ivy adheres.
- *Οὕτως ἰσχυρῶς, ὡς* makes the terms of *sì, come* explicit: ‘so firmly as’. *Τὸ φρικτὸν θηρίον* is the neuter singular subject of *συνέπλεξ᾿* (elided *συνέπλεξε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *συμπλέκω*). The construction is ‘interwove its own limbs **with** his’, rather than *per l’altrui membra*.
- *Τοῖς ἐκείνου* ‘with that one’s [limbs]’ is dative plural, with the possessed noun omitted; *αὑτοῦ τὰ μέλη* is the accusative object, with the contracted reflexive genitive *αὑτοῦ* (from *ἑαυτοῦ*, not *αὐτοῦ*) referring to the subject. Thus Greek expresses *membra* in the second phrase, where Italian leaves it understood in *le sue*.

> 61 poi s’appiccar, come di calda cera  
> 62 fossero stati, e mischiâr lor colore;  

then they stuck together, as if they had been of heated wax, and mingled their colours;

> 61 Εἶτα συνετάκησαν, ὡς εἴπερ ἦσαν  
> (Then they melted together, as if they were)
>
> 62 Ἐκ κηροῦ θερμοῦ, συμμίξαντες τὸ χρῶμα,  
> (Of warm wax, mingling their colour,)

- *Εἶτα συνετάκησαν* ‘then they melted together’ interprets *poi s’appiccar* in light of *cera*. *Συνετάκησαν* is the 3rd pl. second aor. pass. ind. of *συντήκω*, intransitive in sense; the two participants take a plural verb, not a dual.
- *Ὡς εἴπερ ἦσαν ἐκ κηροῦ θερμοῦ* ‘as if indeed they were of warm wax’: *εἴπερ* strengthens *εἰ*, and *ἦσαν* is the imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*, not a pluperfect corresponding to the Italian pluperfect subjunctive *fossero stati*. *Ἐκ* + genitive expresses material.
- *Συμμίξαντες* ‘having mingled’ (nom. pl. masc., aor. act. participle of *συμμίγνυμι*) refers back to the pair and turns the coordinate finite verb *mischiâr* into a participial qualification. *Τὸ χρῶμα* retains the singular of *lor colore* without the possessive.

> 63 nè l’un nè l’altro già parea quel ch’era:  

neither the one, nor the other, now seemed what it *was at first*:

> 63 Οἷος ἦν πρῶτον οὐδετέρου δοκοῦντος.  
> (Neither seeming such as he was at first.)

- *Οὐδετέρου δοκοῦντος* ‘neither seeming’ is a genitive absolute: gen. sg. of *οὐδέτερος* with the pres. act. participle of *δοκέω*. It makes the state simultaneous with the preceding fusion, instead of giving it the independent imperfect *parea*.
- The predicative clause is fronted: *οἷος ἦν πρῶτον* ‘such as he was at first’. *Οἷος* is nominative masculine singular with *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. of *εἰμί*). *Πρῶτον* is an adverbial accusative: Greek explicitly places ‘at first’ with ‘was’, whereas *già* qualifies what the two now seemed.

> 64 come procede innanzi dall’ardore  
> 65 per lo papiro suso un color bruno,  
> 66 che non è nero ancora, e il bianco more.  

as up before the flame on paper, goes a brown colour which is not yet black, and the white dies away.

> 64 Οὕτως ἐπὶ παπύρου φλογιζομένου  
> (Just so upon burning papyrus)
>
> 65 Ἐπεκτείνεται χρῶμά τι φαιόν, ὅπερ  
> (Spreads a certain dusky colour, which)
>
> 66 Μέλαν οὐκέτ᾿ ἔσθ᾿, ἡ δὲ λευκότης θνήσκει.  
> (Is no longer black, while the whiteness dies.)

- *Οὕτως* ‘thus’ introduces the illustration in place of comparative *come*. *Ἐπὶ παπύρου φλογιζομένου* ‘upon papyrus catching fire’ makes *ardore* a process predicated of the paper: *φλογιζομένου* is a pres. mid./pass. participle agreeing with the genitive governed by *ἐπί*, not a genitive absolute.
- *Ἐπεκτείνεται* ‘extends, spreads’ (3rd sg. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *ἐπεκτείνω*) conveys *procede* without distinguishing *innanzi* and *suso*. Its postponed subject *χρῶμά τι φαιόν* has the enclitic indefinite *τι* and the adjective *φαιόν* ‘dusky’, all nom. sg. neuter.
- *Ὅπερ* is the strengthened neuter relative pronoun, subject of *ἔσθ᾿*, with predicate *μέλαν*. In *οὐκέτ᾿ ἔσθ᾿* both *οὐκέτι* and *ἔστι* lose their final vowel; the *τ* of the latter becomes *θ* before the rough breathing of *ἡ*. There is a substantive reversal: *οὐκέτι* means ‘no longer’, not ‘not yet’ as in *non … ancora*. *Ἡ δὲ λευκότης θνήσκει* replaces substantival *il bianco* with the abstract noun ‘whiteness’; postpositive *δέ* contrasts the two changes.

> 67 Gli altri due riguardavano, e ciascuno  
> 68 gridava: “O me, Agnèl, come ti muti!  
> 69 vedi che già non sei nè due nè uno.”  

The other two looked on, and each cried: "O me! Agnello, how thou changest! lo, thou art already neither two nor one!"

> 67 Οἱ δ᾿ ἄλλοι δύο πρὸς αὐτὸν προσεώρων  
> (And the other two looked toward him,)
>
> 68 Κράζοντες· “Οἴμ᾿, Ἄγνελε, πῶς μεταλλάσσῃ!  
> (Crying out: “Alas, Agnello, how you are changing!)
>
> 69 „ Ἰδοὺ γὰρ οὐχ εἷς ὑπάρχεις, οὔτε δύο.”  
> („ For behold, you are neither one nor two.”)

- *Οἱ δ᾿ ἄλλοι δύο* has the masculine plural article with *ἄλλοι* and the numeral, not a dual. *Πρὸς αὐτόν* supplies a singular object of attention absent from *riguardavano*. *Προσεώρων* is the contracted 3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *προσοράω* (compare imperfect *ἑώρων*).
- *Κράζοντες* (nom. pl. masc., pres. act. participle of *κράζω*) makes the crying simultaneous with the looking; the plural replaces distributive *ciascuno gridava*. *Οἴμ᾿* is elided *οἴμοι*; *Ἄγνελε* gives the name a Greek vocative. *Πῶς μεταλλάσσῃ* renders *come ti muti* by the 2nd sg. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *μεταλλάσσω*, ‘how you are changing’.
- *Ἰδοὺ γάρ* ‘for behold’ replaces *vedi che* with a deictic exclamation and a direct assertion; *γάρ* links it to the preceding cry. *Ὑπάρχεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ὑπάρχω*) serves as a copula. *Οὐχ εἷς … οὔτε δύο* ‘not one … nor two’ reverses *nè due nè uno*; *οὐχ* is the form before a rough breathing. There is no separate equivalent of *già* here.

> 70 Già eran li due capi un divenuti,  
> 71 quando n’apparver due figure miste  
> 72 in una faccia, ov’eran due perduti.  

The two heads had now become one, when two shapes appeared to us mixed in one face, where both were lost.

> 70 Ἐγένοντ᾿ ἤδη δύο κεφαλαὶ μία,  
> (Already two heads had become one,)
>
> 71 Ὅτ’ ἐφάνησαν εἰς μίαν μορφαὶ δύο  
> (When two shapes appeared in one,)
>
> 72 Μιχθεῖσαι, κἀμφότεραι συγχωνευθεῖσαι.  
> (Mingled together, and both fused into one.)

- *Ἐγένοντ᾿* is elided *ἐγένοντο*, 3rd pl. second aor. mid. ind. of *γίγνομαι*. The aorist with *ἤδη* ‘already’ gives the anterior sense of *già eran … divenuti*. *Δύο κεφαλαί* is a plural subject; predicate *μία* is feminine singular because the understood noun is *κεφαλή*.
- *Ὅτ’* is elided *ὅτε*, introducing a temporal indicative clause. *Ἐφάνησαν* (3rd pl. second aor. pass. ind. of *φαίνω*) is intransitive ‘appeared’; the observer expressed by *n’* in *n’apparver* is not represented. The subject *μορφαὶ δύο* follows the verb.
- *Εἰς μίαν* ‘into one’ gives the result of the mingling, with a feminine noun understood, rather than naming *una faccia*. *Μιχθεῖσαι* and *συγχωνευθεῖσαι* are nom. pl. fem. aor. pass. participles of *μίγνυμι* and *συγχωνεύω*, agreeing with *μορφαί*. The latter interprets ‘lost’ as ‘fused together’ and dispenses with the Italian relative clause of place. *Κἀμφότεραι* is crasis of *καὶ ἀμφότεραι*, ‘and both’.

> 73 Fêrsi le braccia due di quattro liste;  

Two arms were made of the four lists;

> 73 Γίνοντ᾿ ἐκ μελῶν τεσσάρων χεῖρες δύο·  
> (Out of four limbs are made two arms;)

- *Γίνοντ᾿* is elided *γίνονται*, 3rd pl. pres. mid. ind. of *γίνομαι*, the shorter form of *γίγνομαι*: a vivid present for past *fêrsi*. *Ἐκ μελῶν τεσσάρων* ‘out of four limbs’ interprets *di quattro liste* anatomically rather than preserving the image of strips. *Χεῖρες δύο*, the postponed nominative subject, uses *χείρ* in its extended sense ‘arm’.

> 74 le cosce con le gambe, il ventre e il casso  
> 75 divenner membra che non fur mai viste.  

the thighs with the legs, the belly, and the chest, became such members as were never seen.

> 74 Ἐξ ὤμων, ποδῶν, κοιλίας τε καὶ στήθους  
> (Out of shoulders, legs, belly, and breast)
>
> 75 Πέφυκε μέλη μήποτε θεαθέντα·  
> (Grew limbs never before beheld;)

- *Ἐξ ὤμων, ποδῶν, κοιλίας τε καὶ στήθους* continues the preceding construction with *ἐκ* (before a vowel *ἐξ*): Dante’s subjects become genitives of source. *Ὤμων* means ‘shoulders’, not *cosce* ‘thighs’; *ποδῶν*, from *πούς*, is literally ‘feet’. Enclitic *τε* and *καί* pair the last two terms.
- *Πέφυκε* (3rd sg. perf. act. ind. of *φύω*) presents the result, ‘there have grown’, for *divenner*. Its singular number is regular with the neuter plural subject *μέλη*. *Μήποτε θεαθέντα* ‘never beheld’ turns *che non fur mai viste* into an attributive participial phrase: *θεαθέντα* is nom. pl. neut., aor. pass. of *θεάομαι*. *Μήποτε* characterizes the kind of limbs produced rather than independently asserting a past negative.

> 76 Ogni primaio aspetto ivi era casso:  

The former shape was all extinct in them:

> 76 Ἐξηφανίσθη πᾶν τἀρχέτυπον σχῆμα.  
> (Every original shape vanished away.)

- *Ἐξηφανίσθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ἐξαφανίζω*, augmented after *ἐξ-*) ‘was effaced’ makes *era casso* an event. *Πᾶν τἀρχέτυπον σχῆμα* is its neuter singular subject: *τἀρχέτυπον* is crasis of *τὸ ἀρχέτυπον*. The phrase gives ‘the whole original form’ for *ogni primaio aspetto*; *ivi* is not separately expressed.

> 77 due e nessun l’imagine perversa  
> 78 parea, e tal sen gía con lento passo.  

both, and neither, the perverse image seemed; and such it went away with languid step.

> 77 Ἡ τερατώδης δ᾿ εἰκών, δοκοῦσα δύο  
> (And the monstrous image, seeming two)
>
> 78 Καὶ μηδέν, τοιάδ᾿ ἐβάδιζε βραδέως.  
> (And nothing, walked slowly away in such guise.)

- *Ἡ τερατώδης δ᾿ εἰκών* ‘and the monstrous image’ places the subject first; *τερατώδης* is a two-termination adjective, here nom. sg. fem. *Δοκοῦσα* (nom. sg. fem., pres. act. participle of *δοκέω*) subordinates *parea* to the verb of going. Predicate *δύο καὶ μηδέν* gives ‘two and nothing’ for *due e nessun*: *μηδέν* is neuter singular, not a masculine ‘no one’.
- *Τοιάδ᾿* is elided *τοιάδε*, the nom. sg. fem. of *τοιόσδε*, agreeing predicatively with *εἰκών*: ‘being such’. *Ἐβάδιζε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *βαδίζω*) retains the ongoing motion of *gía*, though not the explicit movement away in *sen gía*. *Βραδέως* ‘slowly’ turns *con lento passo* into an adverb.

> 79 Come il ramarro, sotto la gran fersa  
> 80 de’ dì canicular cangiando siepe,  
> 81 folgore par, se la via attraversa:  

As the lizard, beneath the mighty scourge of the canicular days, going from hedge to hedge, appears a flash of lightning, if it cross the way:

> 79 Ὡς δ᾿ ὁ σαῦρος οἰστρηθεὶς ἐν κυνὸς ὥρᾳ  
> (And as the lizard, stung to frenzy in the dog-days)
>
> 80 Ὑπὸ καύσωνος, αἱμασιὰν ἀλλάσσων,  
> (By scorching heat, changing its hedge,)
>
> 81 Ἀστραπηδὸν τὴν ὁδὸν ἐκδιαβαίνει,  
> (Crosses the road like lightning,)

- *Ὡς δ᾿ ὁ σαῦρος* opens the comparison answered by *οὕτω* in 82; *σαῦρος* is the general ‘lizard’ for *ramarro*. *Οἰστρηθείς* (nom. sg. masc., aor. pass. participle of *οἰστρέω*) means ‘goaded to frenzy’: the force of *fersa* is transferred from the noun to the lizard’s response. *Ὑπὸ καύσωνος* gives the cause with *ὑπό* + genitive, ‘by scorching heat’. *Ἐν κυνὸς ὥρᾳ* is literally ‘in the dog’s season’, a temporal dative phrase for *de’ dì canicular*.
- *Αἱμασιὰν ἀλλάσσων* closely follows *cangiando siepe*: *αἱμασιάν*, the accusative object, denotes an enclosure wall or fence; *ἀλλάσσων* is a nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle simultaneous with the crossing.
- *Ἀστραπηδόν* ‘lightning-like’ is an adverb of manner in *-δόν*. Instead of *folgore par* followed by the condition *se … attraversa*, Greek makes the crossing the main assertion: *τὴν ὁδὸν ἐκδιαβαίνει* ‘it crosses the road’, with an accusative of the space traversed and a generic present indicative.

> 82 così parea, venendo verso l’epe  
> 83 degli altri due, un serpentello acceso,  
> 84 livido e nero come gran di pepe.  

so, coming towards the bowels of the other two, appeared a little reptile burning *with rage*, livid and black as pepper corn.

> 82 Οὕτω κατὰ πρόσωπον τῶν ἄλλων δύο  
> (So straight toward the other two)
>
> 83 Ἐλθὸν ὥρμησεν ὀφείδιόν τι καῖον,  
> (Coming, a burning little serpent darted,)
>
> 84 Πελιδνόν, μέλαν, ὡς πεπέρεως κόκκος.  
> (Livid and black as a peppercorn.)

- *Οὕτω κατὰ πρόσωπον τῶν ἄλλων δύο* ‘so, facing the other two’ gives the direction of approach but not the anatomical target *verso l’epe*. *Κατὰ πρόσωπον* uses the accusative after *κατά*, with a dependent genitive specifying those confronted.
- *Ἐλθόν* (nom. sg. neut., second aor. act. participle of *ἔρχομαι*) gives *venendo* a perfective rather than ongoing aspect. *Ὥρμησεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ὁρμάω*) means ‘darted’ rather than *parea* ‘appeared’, matching the shift from appearance to motion in 81. Both forms precede *ὀφείδιόν τι*, the neuter diminutive with enclitic indefinite, ‘a certain little snake’.
- *Καῖον* is nom. sg. neut., pres. **active** participle of *καίω*, used for *acceso* as ‘burning’, although the Italian participle describes what has been kindled. No word for rage is supplied. *Πελιδνόν, μέλαν* are juxtaposed neuter singular adjectives. *Ὡς πεπέρεως κόκκος* keeps ‘as a grain of pepper’, with the genitive of *πέπερι* before *κόκκος*, the nominative term of comparison.

> 85 E quella parte, donde prima è preso  
> 86 nostro alimento, all’un di lor trafisse;  
> 87 poi cadde giuso innanzi lui disteso.  

And it pierced that part, in one of them, at which we first receive our nourishment; then fell down stretched out before him.

> 85 Τὸ μέρος δ᾿, ὅθεν δεχόμεθα τὴν πρώτην  
> (And that part whence we receive our first)
>
> 86 Τροφήν, διετρύπησε θἀτέρου τούτων,  
> (Nourishment, it pierced in one of these two,)
>
> 87 Οὗ πρὸ τῶν ποδῶν ἔπεσε τεταμένον.  
> (Before whose feet it fell stretched out.)

- *Τὸ μέρος δ᾿* fronts the accusative object of *διετρύπησε* as Dante fronts *quella parte*; the definite article suffices in place of the demonstrative. *Ὅθεν* ‘whence’ renders *donde*. *Δεχόμεθα* (1st pl. pres. mid. ind. of *δέχομαι*) turns passive *è preso nostro alimento* into ‘we receive nourishment’: the possessors become the subject. *Τὴν πρώτην τροφήν* makes adverbial *prima* an attributive adjective, ‘the first nourishment’.
- *Διετρύπησε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *διατρυπάω*, with internal augment) renders *trafisse*. *Θἀτέρου* is crasis of *τοῦ ἑτέρου*, with aspiration of the initial stop: ‘of the one [of the two]’. This possessive genitive belongs with *μέρος*, though separated from it by the relative clause and verb; *τούτων* is partitive, ‘of these’. Greek thus replaces *all’un di lor* with a genitive specifying whose part was pierced.
- *Οὗ* is a possessive relative genitive dependent on *ποδῶν*: ‘before whose feet’, expanding *innanzi lui* and linking the action by a relative rather than *poi*. *Πρό* governs *τῶν ποδῶν*. *Ἔπεσε* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *πίπτω*, without a separate word for *giuso*. *Τεταμένον* (nom. sg. neut., perf. mid./pass. participle of *τείνω*) agrees with the serpent, not the man, and conveys the resulting posture of *disteso*.

## Lines 88–120 — The Exchange of Forms

> 88 Lo trafitto il mirò, ma nulla disse;  

The pierced *thief* gazed on it but said nothing;

> 88 Τοῦτ᾿ εἶδεν ὁ πληγωθείς, ἀλλ᾿ οὐδὲν εἶπε·  
> (The wounded one saw this, but said nothing;)

- *Τοῦτ᾿ εἶδεν ὁ πληγωθείς* ‘this the wounded one saw’ puts the object first. Where *Lo trafitto* is the subject and *il* the object of *mirò*, Greek marks the subject by the nominative article and participle *ὁ πληγωθείς* (aor. pass. participle of *πληγόω*). Neither text supplies a noun corresponding to “thief.”
- *Τοῦτ᾿* is elided *τοῦτο*, neuter accusative: the Greek says ‘this’, not ‘him’. *Εἶδεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*) expresses seeing rather than the directed gaze of *mirò*. *Ἀλλ᾿ οὐδὲν εἶπε* closely follows *ma nulla disse*: *οὐδέν* is the neuter accusative object, *εἶπε* the 3rd sg. second aorist used with *λέγω*.

> 89 anzi co' piè fermati sbadigliava,  
> 90 pur come sonno o febbre l' assalisse.  

nay, with his feet motionless, yawned only as if sleep or fever had come upon him.

> 89 Στὰς δ᾿ ἐπὶ ποδῶν στερεῶς ἐχασμᾶτο,  
> (And standing firmly on his feet he yawned,)
>
> 90 Ὥσπερ ὑφ᾿ ὕπνου κατασχεθεὶς ἢ θέρμης.  
> (As though overcome by sleep or fever.)

- *Στὰς δ᾿ ἐπὶ ποδῶν στερεῶς* ‘but having taken his stand firmly on his feet’ transfers the stillness of *co' piè fermati* from the feet to the man. *Στάς* is the nominative masculine singular participle of the intransitive second aorist of *ἵστημι*; *ἐπί* takes the genitive plural *ποδῶν*. Postpositive *δέ*, elided before *ἐπί*, is less corrective than *anzi*.
- *Ἐχασμᾶτο* (3rd sg. imperf. mid. ind. of *χασμάομαι*) preserves the continuing action of *sbadigliava*. *Ὥσπερ* gives the comparison in *pur come*, without a separate restrictive adverb.
- *Ὑφ᾿ ὕπνου κατασχεθεὶς ἢ θέρμης* ‘as if seized by sleep or fever’ replaces *sonno o febbre l'assalisse*, an active clause with imperfect subjunctive, by an aorist passive participle of *κατέχω* agreeing with the man. The coordinated genitives depend on *ὑπό*; its final vowel is elided and its *π* aspirated before the rough breathing of *ὕπνου*.

> 91 Egli il serpente, e quei lui riguardava;  

He eyed the reptile, the reptile him;

> 91 Ὁ μὲν τὸν ὄφιν ἔβλεπ᾿, ἐκεῖνον δ᾿ οὗτος.  
> (The one looked at the serpent, and it at him;)

- *Ὁ μὲν τὸν ὄφιν ἔβλεπ᾿, ἐκεῖνον δ᾿ οὗτος* ‘the one looked at the serpent, and at him this one [looked]’. Greek supplies *ἔβλεπ᾿* (elided *ἔβλεπε*, 3rd sg. imperf. act. of *βλέπω*) in the first member and understands it in the second; Italian withholds *riguardava* until the end.
- *Ὁ μέν* uses the article pronominally. In the answering *δέ* member, the accusative *ἐκεῖνον* points back to the man, the nominative *οὗτος* to the last-named serpent: the cases keep the references clear even though the order is object–subject, the reverse of *quei lui*.

> 92 l' un per la piaga, e l' altro per la bocca  
> 93 fumavan forte, e il fummo si scontrava.  

the one from his wound, the other from its mouth, smoked violently, and their smoke met.

> 92 Τοῦ μὲν ἡ πληγή, τοῦ δ᾿ ἑτέρου τὸ στόμα  
> (The wound of the one, and the mouth of the other)
>
> 93 Ἀφίη καπνὸν πυκνόν, ὃς συνηνοῦτο.  
> (Sent forth thick smoke, which united together.)

- *Τοῦ μὲν ἡ πληγή, τοῦ δ᾿ ἑτέρου τὸ στόμα* ‘the wound of the one, the mouth of the other’ turns the two subjects of *fumavan* into possessive genitives and the two outlets introduced by *per* into nominative subjects. *Ἀφίη* is an imperfect active indicative, 3rd sg., of *ἀφίημι*; its singular suits the distributive phrasing and the nearer subject *τὸ στόμα*.
- The verb now takes an object, *καπνὸν πυκνόν* ‘thick smoke’: the force of adverbial *forte* is transferred to an adjective denoting density. In place of *e il fummo si scontrava*, *ὃς συνηνοῦτο* ‘which was being united’ has a relative whose masculine singular agrees with *καπνόν* but whose nominative case comes from its own clause. *Συνηνοῦτο* is 3rd sg. imperf. mid./pass. of *συνενόω*, with the augment after the preverb; it expresses union rather than collision.

> 94 Taccia Lucano omai, là dove tocca  
> 95 del misero Sabello e di Nassidio,  
> 96 ed attenda ad udir quel ch' or si scocca.  

Let Lucan now be silent, where he tells of poor Sabellus and Nasidius; and wait to hear that which is now sent forth.

> 94 Σῖγα, Λουκανέ, μὴ λέγε τὰ συμβάντα  
> (Be silent, Lucan, tell not what befell)
>
> 95 Τοῦ δυστυχοῦς Σαβέλλου καὶ Νασιδίου·  
> (Unfortunate Sabellus and Nasidius;)
>
> 96 Ἄκουσον δ’ ἅπερ εἰπεῖν ἤδη σοι μέλλω.  
> (And listen to what I am now about to tell you.)

- *Σῖγα, Λουκανέ* replaces the third-person jussive *Taccia Lucano* with direct address: *σῖγα* is the 2nd sg. present active imperative of *σιγάω*, *Λουκανέ* vocative. *Μὴ λέγε* ‘do not tell’ continues the command with *μή* and the present imperative of *λέγω*, instead of the local clause *là dove tocca*.
- *Τὰ συμβάντα* is the substantivized neuter plural second-aorist active participle of *συμβαίνω*, accusative after *λέγε*: ‘the things that happened’. *Τοῦ δυστυχοῦς Σαβέλλου καὶ Νασιδίου* attaches the names as genitives, as in *del misero Sabello e di Nassidio*, rather than as datives of the persons to whom things happened. *Δυστυχοῦς*, gen. sg. of *δυστυχής*, corresponds to *misero* beside the first name.
- *Ἄκουσον δ᾿* ‘but listen’ (2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *ἀκούω*) collapses *attenda ad udir* into one command. *Ἅπερ* is an emphatic neuter plural relative, ‘the very things which’, object of *εἰπεῖν* (second-aorist active infinitive). *Ἤδη σοι μέλλω* ‘I am now about to [tell] you’ supplies speaker and recipient in place of impersonal *si scocca* and drops the image of discharge. *Ἤδη* answers to *or*; there was no separate adverb for the earlier *omai*.

> 97 Taccia di Cadmo e d' Aretusa Ovidio:  
> 98 chè se quello in serpente, e quella in fonte  
> 99 converte poetando, io non l' invidio:  

Of Cadmus and of Arethusa be Ovid silent: for if he, poetizing, converts the one into a serpent and the other into a fount, I envy him not:

> 97 Σῖγ’, Ὀβίδιε, τὰ Κάδμου κ᾽ Ἀρεθούσης·  
> (Be silent, Ovid, concerning Cadmus and Arethusa;)
>
> 98 Εἰ γὰρ ποιητικεύων τὸν μὲν εἰς ὄφιν,  
> (For if in your poetry the one into a serpent,)
>
> 99 Τὴν δ’ εἰς κρήνην ἤλλαξας, οὐδὲν φθονῶ σοι·  
> (And the other into a fountain you changed, I envy you not at all;)

- *Σῖγ᾿, Ὀβίδιε* repeats the imperative, now elided, and the vocative. *Τὰ Κάδμου κ᾽ Ἀρεθούσης* ‘the matters of Cadmus and Arethusa’ gives *di Cadmo e d'Aretusa* an accusative of topic with dependent genitives; *κ᾽* is shortened *καί*.
- *Εἰ γὰρ ποιητικεύων* puts postpositive causal *γάρ* inside the condition introduced by *εἰ*. *Ποιητικεύων* (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *ποιητικεύω*) renders the gerund *poetando*, but agrees with an understood ‘you’, not *quello* or a third-person poet.
- *Τὸν μὲν εἰς ὄφιν, τὴν δ᾿ εἰς κρήνην* balances the masculine and feminine pronominal articles, both accusative, with *μέν … δέ*; each resulting form is expressed by *εἰς* with the accusative. *Ἤλλαξας* (2nd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀλλάσσω*) presents *converte* as a completed act. In the conclusion, *οὐδὲν φθονῶ σοι* ‘I do not envy you at all’, *οὐδέν* is adverbial and *φθονῶ* (1st sg. pres. act. of *φθονέω*) takes a dative of the person, unlike transitive *l'invidio*.

> 100 chè due nature mai a fronte a fronte  
> 101 non trasmutò, sì ch' ambo e due le forme  
> 102 a cambiar lor materia fosser pronte.  

for never did he so transmute two natures front to front, that both forms were ready to exchange their substance.

> 100 Δύο γὰρ φύσεις ἑτέραν εἰς ἑτέραν  
> (For two natures, one into the other,)
>
> 101 Οὐ μετέβαλες, ὅπως τὰς αὑτῶν ὕλας  
> (You did not transform, so that their own substances)
>
> 102 Ἀλλοιώσωσ’ αἱ δύο μορφαὶ συγχρόνως.  
> (The two forms might alter simultaneously.)

- *Δύο γὰρ φύσεις* fronts the accusative object as in *chè due nature*. *Ἑτέραν εἰς ἑτέραν* ‘one into the other’ interprets the relation as reciprocal change instead of reproducing the spatial *a fronte a fronte*. *Οὐ μετέβαλες* is 2nd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *μεταβάλλω*, still addressed to the poet; the simple negative does not separately express *mai*.
- *Ὅπως … ἀλλοιώσωσ᾿* replaces *sì ch' … fosser pronte* with a clause of intended outcome, ‘so that … they might alter’. *Ἀλλοιώσωσ᾿* is elided *ἀλλοιώσωσι*, 3rd pl. aor. act. subjunctive of *ἀλλοιόω*, not an optative despite the past main verb. Readiness and its dependent infinitive *a cambiar* give way to the finite verb of alteration itself.
- *Τὰς αὑτῶν ὕλας* ‘their own substances’ precedes the verb: *αὑτῶν* is the contracted reflexive genitive plural. Greek pluralizes *materia*. The delayed subject *αἱ δύο μορφαί* uses the plural, not the dual; *συγχρόνως* adds ‘simultaneously’ to the pairing expressed by *ambo e due*.

> 103 Insieme si risposero a tai norme,  
> 104 che il serpente la coda in forca fesse,  
> 105 e il feruto ristrinse insieme l' orme.  

They mutually responded in such a way, that the reptile cleft its tail into a fork, and the wounded *spirit* drew his steps together.

> 103 Ἄμφω μετετράπησαν· τοιούτοις τρόποις,  
> (Both were transformed in such wise:)
>
> 104 Ὁ μὲν ὄφις τὴν οὐρὰν εἰς δύο σχίσας,  
> (The serpent cleaving its tail into two,)
>
> 105 Ὁ δὲ πληγωθεὶς τοὺς πόδας συνενώσας.  
> (And the wounded man uniting his feet together.)

- *Ἄμφω μετετράπησαν* ‘both were transformed’ substitutes a passive of change for reciprocal *si risposero*. *Ἄμφω* is the nominative/accusative dual form ‘both’, here subject with a **plural** verb, 3rd pl. second aor. pass. ind. of *μετατρέπω*. *Τοιούτοις τρόποις* is a dative of manner, ‘in such ways’, for *a tai norme*.
- Instead of *che* with two finite verbs, Greek sets out the manner in paired participial phrases. *Ὁ μὲν ὄφις … σχίσας, ὁ δὲ πληγωθεὶς … συνενώσας* distributes the collective subject into two nominative singulars, each with an aorist active participle (*σχίζω*, *συνενόω*); *ὁ πληγωθείς* repeats the designation of line 88.
- *Τὴν οὐρὰν εἰς δύο* ‘the tail into two’ replaces the figure of *in forca* by the number of divisions. *Τοὺς πόδας* ‘the feet’ interprets *l'orme* as the limbs themselves, and *συνενώσας* ‘having joined together’ makes the union more explicit than *ristrinse*.

> 106 Le gambe con le cosce seco stesse  
> 107 s' appiccar sì, che in poco la giuntura  
> 108 non facea segno alcun che si paresse.  

The legs and the thighs along with them so stuck to one another, that soon their juncture left no mark that was discernible.

> 106 Τούτου δ’ ἐκ ταὐτομάτου μηροὶ καὶ κνῆμαι  
> (And of him, of their own accord, thighs and shins)
>
> 107 Ἐκολλήθησαν οὕτως, ὥστ’ ἐν ὀλίγῳ  
> (Were glued together so that in a short while)
>
> 108 Οὐδὲν κατεφαίνετο συναφῆς ἴχνος.  
> (No trace of the joining was visible.)

- *Τούτου δ᾿ … μηροὶ καὶ κνῆμαι* ‘and this one's thighs and shins’ makes the possessor explicit by a demonstrative genitive and reverses *le gambe con le cosce*. *Ἐκ ταὐτομάτου* ‘of their own accord’ is *ἐκ τοῦ αὐτομάτου* with crasis; it gives *seco stesse* the sense of spontaneous action rather than explicitly saying ‘to each other’.
- *Ἐκολλήθησαν* (3rd pl. aor. pass. ind. of *κολλάω*) ‘were glued’ replaces reflexive *s'appiccar*. *Οὕτως, ὥστ᾿* answers closely to *sì, che*; *ὥστ᾿* is elided *ὥστε*, here with the indicative to state the actual result. *Ἐν ὀλίγῳ* ‘in a short time’ uses the substantivized neuter dative.
- *Οὐδὲν κατεφαίνετο συναφῆς ἴχνος* makes ‘no trace of joining’ the subject of a single verb, replacing both *la giuntura non facea segno alcun* and the relative *che si paresse*. *Οὐδέν* agrees with the separated neuter nominative *ἴχνος*; *συναφῆς* is a dependent genitive. *Κατεφαίνετο* (3rd sg. imperf. mid./pass. of *καταφαίνω*) means ‘was apparent’.

> 109 Togliea la coda fessa la figura,  
> 110 che si perdeva là, e la sua pelle  
> 111 si facea molle, e quella di là dura.  

The cloven tail assumed the figure that was lost in the other; and its skin grew soft, the other's hard.

> 109 Ἐλάμβαν’ ἡ δισχιδὴς οὐρὰ τὸ σχῆμα  
> (The cloven tail took on the shape)
>
> 110 Τὸ φθαρὲν ἐκεῖ· γέγονε δὲ τὸ δέρμα  
> (That was lost over there; and the skin became)
>
> 111 Μαλακὸν μὲν ἐν τούτῳ, σκληρὸν δ’ ἐν κείνῳ.  
> (Soft in the one, and hard in the other.)

- *Ἐλάμβαν᾿ ἡ δισχιδὴς οὐρὰ τὸ σχῆμα* retains the verb-first order of *Togliea la coda fessa la figura*. *Ἐλάμβαν᾿* is elided *ἐλάμβανε*, 3rd sg. imperf. act. of *λαμβάνω*; the compound adjective *δισχιδής* ‘cleft in two’ renders the participial *fessa*.
- *Τὸ φθαρὲν ἐκεῖ* ‘the [shape] lost there’ is in the second attributive position after *τὸ σχῆμα*. *Φθαρέν*, neuter acc. sg. second-aorist passive participle of *φθείρω*, presents the loss as accomplished rather than in progress as in *che si perdeva là*.
- *Γέγονε δὲ τὸ δέρμα* ‘and the skin has become’ replaces imperfect *si facea* with the resultative perfect active of *γίγνομαι* (3rd sg.). Greek uses one subject for both predicates: *μαλακὸν μὲν ἐν τούτῳ, σκληρὸν δ᾿ ἐν κείνῳ* ‘soft in this one, hard in that one’. The adjectives are neuter nominative; locative phrases replace possessive *sua* and the substitution of *quella* for the second skin. *Κείνῳ* is the poetic shorter form of *ἐκείνῳ*.

> 112 Io vidi entrar le braccia per l' ascelle,  
> 113 e i duo piè della fiera, ch' eran corti,  
> 114 tanto allungar, quanto accorciavan quelle.  

I saw the arms enter at the armpits, and the two feet of the brute, which were short, lengthen themselves as much as those *arms* were shortened.

> 112 Εἶδον τὰς χεῖρας εἰσδυούσας μασχάλαις,  
> (I saw the arms drawing into the armpits,)
>
> 113 Καὶ τοῦ θηρίου τοὺς δύο βραχεῖς πόδας  
> (And the two short feet of the beast)
>
> 114 Αὔξοντας ὅσον αἱ χεῖρες ἐμειοῦντο.  
> (Lengthening just as much as the arms grew smaller.)

- *Εἶδον* is here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*. Where *vidi* governs *entrar* and *allungar*, the Greek verb of perception governs two accusatives with supplementary present participles, presenting both actions as observed in progress.
- *Τὰς χεῖρας εἰσδυούσας μασχάλαις* ‘the arms slipping into the armpits’: *χεῖρας*, from *χείρ* ‘hand’ or, more broadly, ‘arm’, represents *le braccia*. *Εἰσδυούσας* (acc. pl. fem. pres. act. participle of *εἰσδύω*) agrees with it. The compound verb carries the inward direction; the bare dative plural *μασχάλαις* locates the entry rather than reproducing *per* with a preposition.
- *Τοῦ θηρίου* ‘of the beast’ is placed before *τοὺς δύο βραχεῖς πόδας*; attributive *βραχεῖς* absorbs the relative clause *ch'eran corti*. *Αὔξοντας* (acc. pl. masc. pres. act. participle of *αὔξω*) is used intransitively, ‘growing’. Accusative of extent *ὅσον* expresses the correlation *tanto … quanto* without a separate antecedent. *Αἱ χεῖρες ἐμειοῦντο* names the limbs again in place of *quelle*; *ἐμειοῦντο* is 3rd pl. imperf. mid./pass. of *μειόω*, ‘were diminishing’ or ‘were being reduced’.

> 115 Poscia li piè dirietro, insieme attorti,  
> 116 diventaron lo membro che l' uom cela,  
> 117 e il misero del suo n' avea duo porti.  

Then the *two* hinder feet, twisted together, became the member which man conceals; and the wretch from his had two thrust forth.

> 115 Οἱ δ᾽ ὀπίσθιοι πόδες ὁμοῦ στραφέντες  
> (And the hind feet, twisted together,)
>
> 116 Ἐγένοντ᾽ ἀνδρὸς τὸ κρυπτόμενον μέλος·  
> (Became the member that a man conceals;)
>
> 117 Τὸ τοῦ τάλανος δ᾽ εἰς δύ’ ἐσχίσθη πόδας.  
> (While that of the wretch was split into two feet.)

- *Οἱ δ᾽ ὀπίσθιοι πόδες* makes *dirietro* an attributive adjective; connective *δέ* does not expressly mark time as *Poscia* does. *Ὁμοῦ στραφέντες* ‘having been twisted together’ renders *insieme attorti* with an adverb and the nom. pl. masc. second-aorist passive participle of *στρέφω*.
- *Ἐγένοντ᾿* is elided *ἐγένοντο*, 3rd pl. second aor. mid. ind. of *γίγνομαι*, with the singular predicate nominative *τὸ κρυπτόμενον μέλος*. In *ἀνδρὸς τὸ κρυπτόμενον μέλος* ‘a man's concealed member’, the subject *l'uom* of the active relative *che l'uom cela* becomes a possessive genitive, and *κρυπτόμενον* a present passive participle agreeing with *μέλος*.
- *Τὸ τοῦ τάλανος* ‘the wretch's [member]’ leaves *μέλος* understood, the article marking the whole expression as subject; *τάλανος* is the genitive singular of *τάλας*. Instead of *il misero … n'avea duo porti*, with the man as subject, *εἰς δύ᾿ ἐσχίσθη πόδας* says ‘was split into two feet’. *Ἐσχίσθη* is 3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *σχίζω*, answering the active *σχίσας* of line 104; *δύ᾿* is elided *δύο*, and *πόδας* supplies the noun left implicit after *duo*.

> 118 Mentre che il fummo l' uno e l' altro vela  
> 119 di color nuovo, e genera il pel suso  
> 120 per l' una parte, e dall' altra il dipela,  

Whilst the smoke with a new colour *veils them* both, and generates on one part *hair*, and strips it from another,

> 118 Ἐν ᾧ δ᾽ ὁ καπνὸς ἐκάλυπτ᾽ ἀμφοτέρους  
> (And while the smoke covered both)
>
> 119 Χρώματι νέῳ, τὴν τρίχα τ᾽ ἀνεγέννα  
> (With a new colour, and generated hair anew)
>
> 120 Ἐπὶ τοῦδ᾽, ἥνπερ ἅτερος ἀφῃρεῖτο,  
> (Upon the one, which it stripped away from the other,)

- *Ἐν ᾧ δ᾿* ‘and while’ uses *ἐν* with a dative relative, literally ‘in which [time]’, for *Mentre che*. *Ὁ καπνὸς ἐκάλυπτ᾿ ἀμφοτέρους* replaces the Italian present *vela* with elided *ἐκάλυπτε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *καλύπτω*). Masculine accusative plural *ἀμφοτέρους* is the single object corresponding to *l'uno e l'altro*.
- *Χρώματι νέῳ* is a dative of means answering to *di color nuovo*. *Τὴν τρίχα τ᾿ ἀνεγέννα* ‘and was producing the hair anew’ has collective singular *τρίχα* (acc. of *θρίξ*) for *il pel*. Postpositive *τε*, elided before the verb, joins this predicate to *ἐκάλυπτ᾿*. *Ἀνεγέννα* is 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἀναγεννάω*, with internal augment and contracted ending, again imperfect for Italian present.
- *Ἐπὶ τοῦδ᾿* ‘upon this one’ (elided gen. sg. *τοῦδε*) gathers up *suso per l'una parte*. For the coordinated *e dall'altra il dipela*, Musurus changes subjects and makes a relative clause: *ἥνπερ* ‘the very [hair] which’ agrees with *τρίχα* in gender and number, while *ἅτερος*, crasis of *ὁ ἕτερος*, is **nominative**, ‘the other one’. *Ἀφῃρεῖτο* (3rd sg. imperf. mid./pass. of *ἀφαιρέω*) is best taken as passive with a retained accusative of the thing removed: literally ‘of which the other was being deprived’. Thus the Greek expresses the loss from the standpoint of the one stripped, rather than continuing with smoke as the active subject.

## Lines 121–151 — The Exchange of Forms

> 121 l' un si levò, e l' altro cadde giuso,  
> 122 non torcendo però le lucerne empie,  
> 123 sotto le quai ciascun cambiava muso.  

the one rose upright, and prostrate the other fell, not therefore turning the impious lights, under which they mutually exchanged visages.

> 121 Ὁ μὲν ἀνέστη ποσίν, ὁ δ᾽ ἄλλος κάτω  
> (The one rose up on his feet, and the other downward)
>
> 122 Ἔπεσ᾽· ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἔτρεψαν τὰς σκαιὰς γλήνας,  
> (Fell; yet they turned not aside their baleful eyes,)
>
> 123 Ὑφ᾽ ἃς ἤλλασσεν ἑκάτερος μυκτῆρα,  
> (Beneath which each was changing his snout,)

- *Ὁ μὲν … ὁ δ᾽ ἄλλος* sets *l’ un … l’ altro* in a correlative construction: ‘the one, on the one hand … the other, on the other’. *Ἀνέστη* (3rd sg. intransitive root aor. act. ind. of *ἀνίστημι*) needs no equivalent of reflexive *si* in *si levò*. Musurus supplies *ποσίν* ‘on his feet’, the dat. pl. of *πούς* with movable *ν*. *Κάτω*, corresponding to *giuso*, comes **before** *ἔπεσ᾽* (for *ἔπεσε*, 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *πίπτω*); the verb is carried over into 122.
- *Ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἔτρεψαν* ‘but they did not turn’ makes *non torcendo però* an independent adversative clause. The two subjects are now taken together in the 3rd **plural**, not the dual, of the aor. act. ind. *ἔτρεψαν* (*τρέπω*). *Ἀλλ᾽* is elided *ἀλλά*; *οὐκ* is the form of the negative before a smooth-breathed vowel. *Τὰς σκαιὰς γλήνας* ‘their baleful eyes’ names the eyes or eyeballs rather than calling them lamps (*lucerne*); *σκαιός* suggests something sinister rather than specifically impious (*empie*).
- *Ὑφ᾽ ἃς* ‘beneath which’ closely follows *sotto le quai*: *ὑπό* is elided and its consonant aspirated before the rough breathing of *ἅς* (acc. pl. fem., referring to *γλήνας*). *Ἤλλασσεν* is the imperfect act. ind., 3rd sg., of *ἀλλάσσω*, with temporal augment, matching the ongoing *cambiava*. Its postposed subject *ἑκάτερος* ‘each of the two’ reproduces distributive *ciascun*: reciprocity is inferred, not expressed by a reciprocal pronoun. *Μυκτῆρα* (acc. sg. of *μυκτήρ*, ‘nostril, nose, snout’) narrows *muso* to the projecting part of the face.

> 124 Quel ch' era dritto il trasse ver le tempie,  

He that was erect, drew his towards the temples;

> 124 Ὃν ὁ μὲν ὀρθὸς εἵλκυσ᾽ ἐν τοῖς κροτάφοις·  
> (Which he who stood upright drew toward his temples;)

- *Ὃν* (acc. sg. masc.) takes up *μυκτῆρα*: where Dante has an independent clause with resumptive *il* ‘it’, Musurus continues the preceding sentence with a relative object, ‘which the upright one drew’. *Ὁ μὲν ὀρθός*, article and adjective, dispenses with the verb of *Quel ch’ era dritto*; *μέν* prepares for *ὁ δέ* in 130.
- *Εἵλκυσ᾽* is elided *εἵλκυσε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἕλκω* (aor. *εἵλκυσα*). *Ἐν τοῖς κροτάφοις* is literally ‘at the temples’, *ἐν* with dat. pl. of *κρόταφος*: it locates the result of the drawing rather than expressing the direction as *ver le tempie* does.

> 125 e di troppa materia che in là venne,  
> 126 uscir gli orecchi delle gote scempie;  

and from the too much matter that went thither, ears came out of the smooth cheeks;

> 125 Ἐκ τῆς δ᾽ ἐν αὐτῷ πλεοναζούσης ὕλης  
> (And from the superfluous matter in it)
>
> 126 Ἐπὶ παρειῶν ἰσχνῶν ἐξῆλθεν ὦτα·  
> (Out upon his lean cheeks came forth ears;)

- *Ἐκ τῆς δ᾽ ἐν αὐτῷ πλεοναζούσης ὕλης* makes *di troppa materia che in là venne* a single prepositional phrase. *Πλεοναζούσης* (pres. act. ptcp., gen. sg. fem., of *πλεονάζω*) stands between article and noun: ‘from the matter that was in excess in it’. *Ἐν αὐτῷ* gives a location in place of directional *in là*, and the participle describes an existing excess rather than an arrival (*venne*). Postpositive *δέ*, elided, links the clause to its predecessor.
- *Ἐπὶ παρειῶν ἰσχνῶν* (*ἐπί* with gen. pl.) locates the emergence ‘upon lean cheeks’; *ἰσχνῶν* interprets *scempie* differently from “smooth”. Thus *ἐκ* marks the material source, while the cheeks are a surface, not a second source as in *delle gote*. *Ἐξῆλθεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἐξέρχομαι*) precedes *ὦτα* (nom. pl. of neuter *οὖς*): the singular verb with a neuter plural subject is regular Attic syntax, unlike plural *uscir*.

> 127 ciò che non corse in dietro e si ritenne,  
> 128 di quel soperchio fe' naso alla faccia,  
> 129 e le labbra ingrossò quanto convenne.  

that which went not back, but was retained, of its superfluity formed a nose, and enlarged the lips to a fit size.

> 127 Τὸ δ᾽ ὀπίσω μὴ συρθὲν καὶ μεῖναν μέρος  
> (And the part not drawn back and remaining)
>
> 128 Τὴν ῥῖν᾽ ἐσχημάτισεν ἐν τῷ προσώπῳ,  
> (Shaped the nose upon the face,)
>
> 129 Τὰ χείλη τ᾽ ἐπάχυνε καθ᾽ ὅσον ἔδει.  
> (And thickened the lips to the measure needed.)

- *Τὸ δ᾽ ὀπίσω μὴ συρθὲν καὶ μεῖναν μέρος* supplies a noun for *ciò che*: ‘the part not drawn backward and remaining’. Both attributive participles agree with *μέρος* (nom. sg. neut.): *συρθέν* is aor. pass. of *σύρω*, replacing active *non corse*; *μεῖναν* is aor. act. of *μένω*, replacing *si ritenne*. *Μή* negatives the first participle as a qualification defining the part in question.
- *Τὴν ῥῖν᾽ ἐσχημάτισεν* places the definite object first: *ῥῖν᾽* is elided *ῥῖνα*, acc. sg. of *ῥίς*; *ἐσχημάτισεν* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *σχηματίζω*, ‘gave shape to’, for simple *fe’*. *Ἐν τῷ προσώπῳ* ‘in the face’ replaces *alla faccia*. *Di quel soperchio* is not separately translated: the subject *μέρος* is already a part of the excess just specified.
- *Τὰ χείλη τ᾽ ἐπάχυνε* ‘and it thickened the lips’ has the same subject; *χείλη* is contracted acc. pl. neut. of *χεῖλος*, *τ᾽* elided connective *τε*, and *ἐπάχυνε* 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *παχύνω*. *Καθ᾽ ὅσον ἔδει* ‘to the extent that was necessary’ renders *quanto convenne* by *κατά* (elided and aspirated before *ὅσον*) with an accusative of extent and the impersonal imperfect of *δεῖ*.

> 130 Quel che giacea il muso innanzi caccia,  
> 131 e gli orecchi ritira per la testa,  
> 132 come face le corna la lumaccia;  

He that lay prone, thrusts forward his sharpened visage, and draws back his ears into the head, as the snail does its horns;

> 130 Ὁ δὲ χαμαὶ κείμενος μυκτῆρ᾽ ἐκτείνει,  
> (And he who lay on the ground stretches out his snout,)
>
> 131 Τά τ᾽ ὦτ᾽ ἔνδοθι τῆς κεφαλῆς ἀνέλκει,  
> (And draws his ears back inside his head,)
>
> 132 Ὡς τὰ κέρατα συνεκροφεῖ κοχλίας.  
> (As a snail sucks in its horns.)

- *Ὁ δὲ χαμαὶ κείμενος* ‘but the one lying on the ground’ answers *ὁ μὲν ὀρθός*. *Κείμενος* (pres. mid. ptcp., nom. sg. masc., of *κεῖμαι*) replaces the relative and imperfect *Quel che giacea*; the adverb *χαμαί* specifies where he lay. *Μυκτῆρ᾽* is again elided acc. sg. *μυκτῆρα*; there is no adjective corresponding to English “sharpened”. *Ἐκτείνει* ‘stretches out’ (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἐκτείνω*) conveys *innanzi caccia* through the compound verb and retains the Italian shift to the present.
- *Τά τ᾽ ὦτ᾽* represents *τά τε ὦτα*, with two successive elisions; the ears are the acc. pl. object of *ἀνέλκει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἀνέλκω*), ‘draws back’. *Ἔνδοθι τῆς κεφαλῆς* uses the poetic local adverb *ἔνδοθι* with a dependent genitive, ‘inside the head’; motion into that position is understood from the verb, not expressed by *ἔνδοθι* itself.
- *Ὡς τὰ κέρατα συνεκροφεῖ κοχλίας* keeps the comparison and the snail as final subject, but replaces the pro-verb *face* ‘does’ with a specific action. *Συνεκροφεῖ* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *συνεκροφέω*) suggests sucking in together; *τὰ κέρατα* is the acc. pl. of *κέρας*. *Κοχλίας* is anarthrous: ‘as a snail [does]’, for generic *la lumaccia*.

> 133 e la lingua, che avea unita e presta  
> 134 prima a parlar, si fende, e la forcuta  
> 135 nell' altro si richiude, e il fummo resta.  

and his tongue, which was before united and apt for speech, cleaves itself; and in the other the forked *tongue* recloses; and the smoke *now rests*.

> 133 Ἡ δὲ πρότερον συνηνωμένη γλῶσσα  
> (And the tongue, formerly united)
>
> 134 Καὶ πολύλαλος ἐσχίσθη, τοῦ δ᾽ ἑτέρου  
> (And loquacious, split in two, while the other's)
>
> 135 Ἡ σχιστὴ συνηνώθη· καπνὸς δ᾽ ἐπαύθη.  
> (Forked tongue united; and the smoke ceased.)

- *Ἡ δὲ πρότερον συνηνωμένη γλῶσσα καὶ πολύλαλος* folds *che avea unita e presta prima a parlar* into the description of the subject. *Πρότερον* ‘formerly’ precedes *συνηνωμένη* (perf. mid./pass. ptcp., nom. sg. fem., of *συνενόω*), ‘in a state of being joined’. *Καὶ πολύλαλος* adds a second qualification after the noun: ‘and talkative’, a propensity rather than the capacity expressed by *presta … a parlar*. There is no possessive verb equivalent to *avea*.
- *Ἐσχίσθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *σχίζω*) ‘was split’ and *συνηνώθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *συνενόω*) ‘was joined together’ replace the reflexive presents *si fende* and *si richiude* with completed changes. *Τοῦ δ᾽ ἑτέρου ἡ σχιστή* is ‘the other’s split [tongue]’: the possessive genitive replaces locative *nell’ altro*, and the articular verbal adjective *σχιστή* supplies the sense of *la forcuta* without repeating *γλῶσσα*.
- *Καπνὸς δ᾽ ἐπαύθη* ‘and smoke ceased’ again uses a 3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. (*παύω*), intransitive in sense. It interprets *resta* as cessation rather than continued presence; the Greek has neither the Italian article of *il fummo* nor a separate adverb for English “now”.

> 136 L' anima, ch' era fiera divenuta,  
> 137 si fuggì sufolando per la valle,  

The soul that had become a brute, [fled] hissing along the valley,

> 136 Ἡ ψυχὴ δ᾽, ἥτις ἐγένετο θηρίον,  
> (And the soul that had become a beast)
>
> 137 Ἔφυγε συρίζουσα διὰ τῆς τάφρου.  
> (Fled hissing along the ditch.)

- *Ἡ ψυχὴ δ᾽, ἥτις ἐγένετο θηρίον* retains the relative construction. *Ἥτις* (nom. sg. fem. of *ὅστις*) agrees with *ψυχή*; here the compound relative refers to a particular antecedent much like simple *ἥ*. *Ἐγένετο* (3rd sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *γίγνομαι*) renders the anterior change of *era … divenuta* by a simple aorist. *Θηρίον* ‘beast’ is a predicate nominative, neuter by its own gender.
- *Ἔφυγε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *φεύγω*) needs no reflexive corresponding to *si* in *si fuggì*. *Συρίζουσα* (pres. act. ptcp., nom. sg. fem., of *συρίζω*) agrees with *ψυχή* and expresses the simultaneous manner of flight, like *sufolando*. *Διὰ τῆς τάφρου* ‘through the ditch’ renders *per la valle* by *διά* with the genitive of the space traversed, but changes the noun.

> 138 e l' altro dietro a lui parlando sputa.  

and after it the other talking and sputtering.

> 138 Ὁ δ᾽ ἄλλος ὄπισθ᾽ αὐτοῦ πτύων ἐλάλει·  
> (And the other spoke behind it, spitting;)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ ἄλλος ὄπισθ᾽ αὐτοῦ* follows *e l’ altro dietro a lui*: *ὄπισθ᾽* is elided *ὄπισθε*, a local adverb governing the genitive *αὐτοῦ*. The pronoun can refer grammatically to neuter *θηρίον* rather than feminine *ψυχή*.
- Musurus reverses the grammatical emphasis of *parlando sputa*: *πτύων* ‘spitting’ is the attendant pres. act. ptcp. (nom. sg. masc. of *πτύω*), *ἐλάλει* ‘was speaking’ the finite verb (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *λαλέω*). The imperfect also replaces Dante’s narrative present.

> 139 Poscia gli volse le novelle spalle,  
> 140 e disse all' altro: "Io vo' che Buoso corra,  
> 141 com' ho fatt' io, carpon, per questo calle."  

Then he turned his novel shoulders towards it, and said to the other : “Buoso shall run crawling, as I have done, along this road

> 139 Εἶτα στρέφων πρὸς αὐτὸν τοὺς νέους ὤμους,  
> (Then turning toward it his new shoulders,)
>
> 140 Τῷ τρίτῳ λέγει· “Τρεχέτω δὴ Βουῶσος,  
> (He says to the third: “Let Buoso now run,)
>
> 141 „ Ὡς ἐγὼ ᾿ποίουν, ἕρπων ἐπὶ τῆς τρίβου.”  
> („ As I used to do, crawling along the path.”)

- *Εἶτα στρέφων πρὸς αὐτὸν τοὺς νέους ὤμους* ‘then turning his new shoulders toward him’ subordinates finite *volse* to the following verb of speaking. *Στρέφων* (pres. act. ptcp., nom. sg. masc., of *στρέφω*) presents the turn as accompanying the speech; *πρὸς αὐτόν* makes the direction of *gli* explicit. *Αὐτόν* is now unambiguously masculine. *Τοὺς νέους ὤμους* is the direct object, with the article doing duty for a possessive.
- *Τῷ τρίτῳ λέγει* ‘he says to the third’ interprets *all’ altro* rather than repeating *ἄλλος*; the articular ordinal is a dative of addressee. *Λέγει* is a historical present for past *disse*. *Τρεχέτω δὴ Βουῶσος* eliminates both *Io vo’* and the subordinate *che … corra*: *τρεχέτω* is a direct 3rd sg. pres. act. imperative of *τρέχω*, ‘let Buoso run’. Postpositive *δή* lends insistence; *Βουῶσος* has a Greek nominative ending.
- *Ὡς ἐγὼ ᾿ποίουν* keeps emphatic *io* as *ἐγώ*, but *᾿ποίουν* is prodelided *ἐποίουν* (1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of contracted *ποιέω*): the initial augment is suppressed after the vowel of *ἐγώ*. The imperfect ‘as I used to do’ replaces the perfect *com’ ho fatt’ io*. *Ἕρπων* ‘creeping’ (pres. act. ptcp., nom. sg. masc., of *ἕρπω*) gives adverbial *carpon* a participle agreeing with Buoso. *Ἐπὶ τῆς τρίβου* ‘on the path’ replaces *per questo calle* with *ἐπί* and a local genitive; the demonstrative becomes an article.

> 142 Così vid' io la settima zavorra  
> 143 mutare e trasmutare; e qui mi scusi  
> 144 la novità, se fior la penna abborra.  

!” Thus I beheld the seventh ballast change and rechange ; and here let the novelty excuse me, if my tongue [pen] goes aught astray.

> 142 Εἶδον οὑτωσὶ τὸν ἑβδόματον βόθρον  
> (Thus I saw the seventh pit)
>
> 143 Μεταλλάσσοντα μορφάς· καὶ συγγνωσθείην  
> (Changing shapes; and may I be pardoned)
>
> 144 Τῆς καινότητος, εἰ γλῶσσ᾽ ἐμὴ πλανᾶται.  
> (For the strangeness, if my tongue goes astray.)

- *Εἶδον οὑτωσὶ τὸν ἑβδόματον βόθρον* puts the verb before the adverb of *Così vid’ io*. *Εἶδον* is the 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. used for *ὁράω*; the subject pronoun is not expressed. *Οὑτωσί* is deictically strengthened *οὕτως*, ‘in just this way’. The poetic ordinal *ἑβδόματον* (acc. sg. masc. of *ἑβδόματος*, for usual *ἕβδομος*) agrees with *βόθρον* ‘pit’: Musurus names the place instead of reproducing *zavorra* ‘ballast’.
- *Μεταλλάσσοντα μορφάς* ‘changing shapes’ is a supplementary participial construction after a verb of perception. *Μεταλλάσσοντα* (pres. act. ptcp., acc. sg. masc., of *μεταλλάσσω*) agrees with *βόθρον*, the thing seen in the act of changing; *μορφάς* is its supplied acc. pl. object. The present stem gives an ongoing action, but there is no second verb for *trasmutare*.
- *Καὶ συγγνωσθείην τῆς καινότητος* ‘and may I be pardoned on account of the novelty’ reverses the construction of *mi scusi la novità*. *Συγγνωσθείην* is the 1st sg. aor. pass. optative of *συγγιγνώσκω*: an independent wish, without *ἄν*, where Italian has a 3rd-person jussive subjunctive. The speaker becomes subject and *τῆς καινότητος* a genitive giving the ground for pardon, not an agent. *Qui* is not separately rendered.
- *Εἰ γλῶσσ᾽ ἐμὴ πλανᾶται* ‘if my tongue strays’ has *εἰ* with the pres. mid./pass. ind., 3rd sg., of contracted *πλανάω*, intransitive here. *Γλῶσσ᾽* is elided nom. sg. *γλῶσσα*, qualified by possessive *ἐμή*. Musurus has ‘tongue’, like the unrevised English “tongue”, not *penna* ‘pen’; nothing separately expresses the minimizer *fior* ‘at all’.

> 145 Ed avvegna che gli occhi miei confusi  
> 146 fossero alquanto, e l' animo smagato,  
> 147 non poter quei fuggirsi tanto chiusi,  
> 148 ch' io non scorgessi ben Puccio Sciancato.  

And though my eyes were somewhat perplexed, and my mind dismayed, [those] could not flee so covertly, but that I well distinguished Puccio Sciancato:

> 145 Καίτοι γοῦν τῶν ὀφθαλμῶν συγκεχυμένων  
> (And although indeed the eyes confused)
>
> 146 Ὄντων μοι, καὶ τῆς ψυχῆς ἐκπεπληγμένης,  
> (Were within me, and my soul bewildered,)
>
> 147 Οὐ μὴν ἠδυνήθησαν ἐκφυγεῖν κρύβδην,  
> (Yet they could not escape secretly,)
>
> 148 Πρὶν ἢ γνωρίσω Πούκκιον Σκιαγκάτον,  
> (Before I recognized Puccio Sciancato,)

- *Καίτοι γοῦν* marks concession, with confirmatory *γοῦν* ‘at any rate’ added to *καίτοι* ‘although’. Instead of *avvegna che* with finite subjunctive *fossero*, Musurus uses genitive absolutes. In *τῶν ὀφθαλμῶν συγκεχυμένων ὄντων μοι*, *συγκεχυμένων* (perf. mid./pass. ptcp., gen. pl. masc., of *συγχέω*) is predicative with *ὄντων* (pres. ptcp. of *εἰμί* in agreement): ‘my eyes being in a state of confusion’. *Μοι* is a possessive dative for *miei*; there is no adverb for *alquanto*.
- *Καὶ τῆς ψυχῆς ἐκπεπληγμένης* is the parallel absolute, without another participle of *εἰμί*. *Ἐκπεπληγμένης* (perf. mid./pass. ptcp., gen. sg. fem., of *ἐκπλήσσω*) gives *smagato* as the resulting state of having been struck with dismay; *ψυχή* renders *animo*.
- *Οὐ μὴν ἠδυνήθησαν* ‘nevertheless they could not’ resumes the main assertion after the concession; *μήν* is an adversative particle, not a second negative. *Ἠδυνήθησαν* is 3rd pl. aor. pass. ind., in form, of deponent *δύναμαι*, with the variant augment *ἠ-*; its subject, explicit in *quei*, is understood. Its complement *ἐκφυγεῖν* is the second aor. act. inf. of *ἐκφεύγω*, without a reflexive for *-si*. The manner adverb *κρύβδην* ‘secretly’ stands for adjectival *chiusi*, without the degree word *tanto*.
- *Πρὶν ἢ γνωρίσω* ‘before I recognize’ turns the Italian degree-and-result construction *tanto … ch’ io non scorgessi* into a temporal limit on escape. *Γνωρίσω* is read here as 1st sg. aor. act. subj. of *γνωρίζω* (identical in spelling to the future indicative); after a negative main clause, Attic prose would normally use *πρὶν ἄν* with a prospective subjunctive. There is no subordinate negative and no separate *ben*. *Πούκκιον Σκιαγκάτον* gives both parts of the name Greek masculine accusative endings as the object of recognition.

> 149 ed era quei che sol, de' tre compagni  
> 150 che venner prima, non era mutato;  

and it was he alone, of the three companions that first came, who was not changed

> 149 Ὅστις δὴ μόνος τῶν τριῶν συνεταίρων  
> (Who indeed alone of the three companions)
>
> 150 Τῶν πρότερον ἐλθόντων οὐκ ἠλλοιώθη·  
> (Who had come at first was not transformed;)

- *Ὅστις δὴ μόνος* continues directly from the accusative name with a relative in the **nominative**, its case determined by its role in the new clause. The compound relative *ὅστις*, reinforced by *δή*, replaces *ed era quei che*: ‘who indeed alone …’, without a copula or demonstrative. *Μόνος* agrees with the subject, and *τῶν τριῶν συνεταίρων* is its partitive genitive, as in *sol, de’ tre compagni*.
- *Τῶν πρότερον ἐλθόντων* repeats the article to attach a postpositive attributive participle to *συνεταίρων*: ‘the ones who came earlier’. *Ἐλθόντων* is the second aor. act. ptcp., gen. pl. masc., of *ἔρχομαι*, replacing finite *che venner*; *πρότερον* renders *prima*. *Οὐκ ἠλλοιώθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ἀλλοιόω*, with temporal augment) states that he did not undergo the change, where *non era mutato* describes him as not having changed.

> 151 l' altro era quel che tu, Gaville, piagni.  

; the other was he whom thou, Gaville, lamentest.

> 151 Ὁ δ᾽ ἄλλος ἦν, δι᾽ ὃν σὺ θρηνεῖς, Γαυΐλη.  
> (And the other was he for whom you mourn, Gaville.)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ ἄλλος ἦν* closely follows *l’ altro era*; *ἦν* is 3rd sg. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*. Musurus leaves *quel* unexpressed before *δι᾽ ὃν* (elided *διά* with acc. sg. masc. relative): literally ‘the other was [he] on whose account …’. Italian *che* is the direct object of *piagni*; Greek makes the person the cause of the mourning.
- *Σὺ θρηνεῖς* keeps the explicit, emphatic *tu* alongside *θρηνεῖς* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of contracted *θρηνέω*). *Γαυΐλη*, a feminine name in direct address, is moved from between pronoun and verb to the very end; the diaeresis on *ΐ* marks it as a separate vowel.

## Summary — The Grammar of Exchange

Musurus often keeps Dante’s order of perception while changing the grammar of what is perceived. Where *vidi* governs the infinitives *entrar* and *allungar*, *εἶδον* governs two accusatives with agreeing present participles, *εἰσδυούσας* ‘slipping into’ and *αὔξοντας* ‘growing’. The agreement identifies the limbs involved in each motion, while the present stems keep both changes in view as processes. Elsewhere a participle alters the emphasis: in *parlando sputa* the finite verb denotes spitting, whereas *πτύων ἐλάλει* means ‘he was speaking, spitting’. The Greek makes speech the principal action and replaces Dante’s narrative present with an imperfect.

The particles give the exchanges a particularly clear syntax. Musurus repeatedly uses *μέν … δέ* to set one participant or body part against the other. In the exchange of looks, he puts the verb in the first clause, where Dante reserves it for the second; the cases of *ἐκεῖνον* ‘him’ and *οὗτος* ‘this one’ then mark the reversal without a second verb. The pairing need not entail grammatical duals: *ἄμφω* ‘both’ takes a plural verb, while *ἑκάτερος* ‘each of the two’ takes a singular. Elsewhere the correspondence is close even at the level of clause structure: *οὕτως … ὥστε* with the indicative follows *sì … che* in presenting an observable result. Musurus is freer with connections between sentences: *γάρ* can turn a phrase of cause into an explanatory assertion, and *ἀλλά* can mark contrast where *onde* marks consequence.

Musurus is least literal where he chooses to name directly what Dante conveys by an image or gesture. The path of the finger *dal mento al naso* becomes its point of contact, *ἐπὶ τὰ χείλη* ‘onto the lips’; *zavorra* ‘ballast’ becomes *βόθρον* ‘pit’. Some departures affect the sense more sharply. At Pistoia, transitive *avanzi* ‘you surpass’ becomes intransitive *προβαίνει* ‘advances’, so that *σπέρμα* ‘seed’ is now the subject rather than the object of comparison. In the papyrus simile, *οὐκέτι* ‘no longer’ reverses the temporal relation of *non … ancora* ‘not yet’. Such readings stand out precisely because, in the paired transformations, Musurus otherwise tracks so closely which feature is emerging, which is disappearing, and to whom each belongs.
