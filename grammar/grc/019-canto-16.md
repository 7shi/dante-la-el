# Inferno 16

## Lines 1–21 — Three Shades Call for a Halt

> 1 Già era in loco ove s' udia il rimbombo  
> 2 dell' acqua che cadea nell' altro giro,  
> 3 simile a quel che l' arnie fanno rombo ;  

Already I was in a place where the resounding of the water, that fell into the other circle, was heard like the hum which bee-hives make;

> 1 Ἐν τόπῳ δ᾽ ἦμεν, ἔνθεν ἠκούετ᾽ ἦχος  
> (Now we were in a place whence was heard the sound)
>
> 2 Ὕδατος εἰς τὸν ἄλλον πίπτοντος κύκλον,  
> (Of water falling into the other circle,)
>
> 3 Παρόμοιος τῷ βόμβῳ τῶν μελισσώνων,  
> (Resembling the humming of beehives,)

- *Ἐν τόπῳ δ᾽ ἦμεν* ‘and we were in a place’ puts the locative phrase first. *ἦμεν* is the 1st pl. imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*: Musurus includes the companion where *era* has the narrator alone. Postpositive *δέ*, elided before *ἦμεν*, connects the sentence to what precedes; it does not translate the temporal *Già*, which has no separate equivalent.
- *ἔνθεν* properly means ‘whence’, rather than simply ‘where’ as in *ove*: the place is the point from which the sound could be heard. *ἠκούετ᾽* is *ἠκούετο*, 3rd sg. imperfect middle/passive indicative of *ἀκούω*, with temporal augment and elision of final *-ο*. It is passive here, with the following nominative *ἦχος* ‘sound’ as subject, answering to *s' udia il rimbombo*.
- *ὕδατος … πίπτοντος* ‘of water falling’ depends on *ἦχος*. The present active participle of *πίπτω* is gen. sg. neut., agreeing with *ὕδατος* (from *ὕδωρ*); it replaces the finite relative clause *che cadea*. The participle comes between the adjective and noun of *εἰς τὸν ἄλλον … κύκλον*: *εἰς* with the accusative makes *nell' altro giro* explicitly a destination.
- *παρόμοιος* ‘resembling’ agrees with *ἦχος* and takes the dative *τῷ βόμβῳ* ‘the humming’. Where *quel che l' arnie fanno rombo* has the hives as subject of a verb, *τῶν μελισσώνων* (gen. pl. of *μελισσών*, ‘bee-house, hive’) simply names the source of the hum. *βόμβος* catches the sound of *rombo* more closely than *ἦχος* does that of *rimbombo*.

> 4 quando tre ombre insieme si partiro,  
> 5 correndo, d' una torma che passava  
> 6 sotto la pioggia dell' aspro martiro.  

when three shades together, running, quitted a troop that passed beneath the rain of the sharp torment.

> 4 Ὅτε σκιαὶ τρεῖς ἀπεσπάσθησαν ἅμα  
> (When three shades detached themselves together)
>
> 5 Τροχάδην τινὸς προβαίνοντος ὁμίλου  
> (Running from a troop that was passing by)
>
> 6 Ὑπὸ τὸν ὄμβρον τῆς ἀπηνοῦς βασάνου.  
> (Beneath the rain of the cruel torment.)

- *ὅτε* introduces the event that breaks into the preceding imperfect description, like *quando*. *σκιαὶ τρεῖς* (nom. pl. fem.) reverses *tre ombre*. *ἀπεσπάσθησαν* is the 3rd pl. aorist passive indicative of *ἀποσπάω*, with the augment after the preverb: ‘were drawn away’, or in context ‘broke away’, for reflexive *si partiro*. *ἅμα* ‘together’ comes after the verb rather than before it as *insieme* does.
- *τροχάδην* ‘at a run’ is an adverb of manner for the Italian gerund *correndo*. *τινὸς … ὁμίλου* ‘from a certain throng’ is a genitive of separation with *ἀπεσπάσθησαν*, not a genitive absolute. *τινός*, from indefinite *τις*, answers to *una*; the intervening *προβαίνοντος*, gen. sg. masc. present active participle of *προβαίνω*, renders *che passava* by an agreeing participle, ‘moving onward’.
- *ὑπὸ τὸν ὄμβρον* ‘under the downpour’ uses *ὑπό* with the accusative in describing the troop's course. *τῆς ἀπηνοῦς βασάνου* keeps the dependent genitive of *dell' aspro martiro*: *ἀπηνοῦς* is the contracted gen. sg. fem. of *ἀπηνής*, ‘harsh, pitiless’.

> 7 Venian ver noi, e ciascuna gridava :  

They came towards us, and each cried:

> 7 Ἤρχοντο δὲ πρὸς ἡμᾶς, κράζουσ᾽ ἑκάστη·  
> (And they came toward us, each one crying:)

- *ἤρχοντο δὲ πρὸς ἡμᾶς* ‘and they were coming towards us’ keeps the imperfect of *Venian*: *ἤρχοντο* is the 3rd pl. imperfect middle indicative of *ἔρχομαι*, middle in form but active in sense. *πρός* takes the accusative of the persons approached; postpositive *δέ* connects this movement with the preceding clause.
- Musurus does not coordinate a second finite verb as in *e ciascuna gridava*. *κράζουσ᾽ ἑκάστη* is ‘each crying’: the elided form is *κράζουσα*, nom. sg. fem. present active participle of *κράζω*. Its singular agreement with distributive *ἑκάστη* picks out each of the plural *σκιαί* individually.

> 8 " Sostati tu, che all' abito ne sembri  
> 9 essere alcun di nostra terra prava."  

“Stay thee, thou who by thy dress to us appearest to be some one from our perverse country.”

> 8 “ Στῆθι σὺ νῦν, ὁ δοκῶν ἐκ σῆς ἐσθῆτος  
> (“Wait now, you who seem by your garb)
>
> 9 „ Εἶναί τις τῆς μοχθηρᾶς ἡμῶν πατρίδος.”  
> („ To be someone from our wicked fatherland.”)

- *στῆθι* is the 2nd sg. second aorist active imperative of *ἵστημι*, intransitive ‘come to a stand’. It needs no reflexive pronoun for *Sostati*. *σύ* keeps the emphatic *tu*, and *νῦν* ‘now’ is supplied.
- *ὁ δοκῶν* ‘the one seeming’ stands in apposition to *σύ*: an articular present active participle of *δοκέω* (nom. sg. masc.) replaces *che … sembri*. There is no dative for *ne* ‘to us’. *ἐκ σῆς ἐσθῆτος*, literally ‘from your clothing’, makes the dress the evidence for the inference; *σῆς* (gen. sg. fem. of *σός*) supplies the possessive implicit in *all' abito*.
- *εἶναί τις* completes *δοκῶν*. Since the understood subject of *εἶναι* is the person addressed, its predicate *τις* ‘someone’, for *alcun*, is nominative, not accusative. The enclitic gives *εἶναί* its additional accent. *τῆς μοχθηρᾶς ἡμῶν πατρίδος* is a genitive of belonging or origin without a preposition: *πατρίδος* (from *πατρίς*) makes *terra* specifically ‘fatherland’, and the possessive genitive *ἡμῶν* stands between adjective and noun.

> 10 Ahi me, che piaghe vidi ne' lor membri  
> 11 recenti e vecchie dalle fiamme incese !  

Ah me! what wounds I saw upon their limbs, recent and old, by the flames burnt in.

> 10 Φεῦ! οἵας πληγὰς ἐκ φλογῶν εἰς τὰ μέλη  
> (Ah! what wounds from the flames upon the limbs)
>
> 11 Αὐτῶν ἅμα παλαιὰς καὶ νέας εἶδον!  
> (Of them I saw, both old and new!)

- *φεῦ* is a self-contained exclamation of pain for *Ahi me*. *οἵας πληγάς* ‘what manner of wounds!’ is the accusative object of *εἶδον* (1st sg. second aorist active indicative used for *ὁράω*). The exclamatory adjective renders *che*; unlike *vidi*, the Greek verb is held back to the very end.
- *ἐκ φλογῶν* ‘from flames’ makes the flames the source of the wounds, omitting the participle *incese*. *εἰς τὰ μέλη αὐτῶν* corresponds to *ne' lor membri*. The directional *εἰς* with the accusative is less usual for a static location than *ἐν* with the dative: the wording suggests wounds inflicted on the limbs. Possessive *αὐτῶν* follows *μέλη* across the line break.
- *ἅμα παλαιὰς καὶ νέας* ‘old and new together’ reverses *recenti e vecchie* and adds *ἅμα* to bring the two kinds together. Both adjectives are acc. pl. fem., agreeing back with *πληγάς*, not with neuter *μέλη*.

> 12 Ancor men duol, pur ch' io me ne rimembri.  

It pains me yet, when I but think thereof.

> 12 Ἔτι πάσχω νῦν, ἐνθυμούμενος μόνον.  
> (Even now I suffer, merely remembering it.)

- *ἔτι πάσχω νῦν* ‘I still suffer now’ makes the sufferer the grammatical subject: *πάσχω* is 1st sg. present active indicative. *ἔτι* renders *Ancor*, reinforced by the added *νῦν*; the Italian pronominal reference to what causes the pain is left implicit.
- *ἐνθυμούμενος μόνον* ‘merely calling it to mind’ replaces *pur ch' io me ne rimembri*, a finite clause with the subjunctive, by a circumstantial participle. *ἐνθυμούμενος* is nom. sg. masc., present middle of *ἐνθυμέομαι*, agreeing with the subject of *πάσχω*; adverbial *μόνον* limits the act of remembering, which has no expressed object.

> 13 Alle lor grida il mio dottor s' attese,  

To their cries my Teacher listened;

> 13 Ταῖς φωναῖς αὐτῶν προσέσχ᾽ οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ·  
> (To their cries my Guide gave heed;)

- *ταῖς φωναῖς αὐτῶν* retains the emphatic initial position of *Alle lor grida*. The dative is governed by *προσέσχ᾽*, elided *προσέσχε*, 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *προσέχω*, ‘gave heed to’; Greek needs no reflexive for *s' attese*. The augment is internal to the compound (*προσ-έ-σχ-ε*).
- *οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ* ‘my Guide’ is the postponed subject. *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, retaining the article's rough breathing; the possessive adjective is nom. sg. masc. *ἡγήτωρ* names one who leads rather than one who teaches, the distinction obscured if it is taken as a lexical equivalent of *dottor*.

> 14 volse il viso ver me, e : “ Ora aspetta,”  
> 15 disse, “ a costor si vuole esser cortese ;  

turned his face toward me, and said: “Now wait: to these courtesy is due;

> 14 Καὶ δὴ πρὸς ἐμὲ στραφείς· “Περίμεν᾽ ἤδη,”  
> (And indeed turning toward me: “Wait now,”)
>
> 15 Εἶπεν, “εἰ βούλει προσηνὴς αὐτοῖς εἶναι.  
> (He said, “if you wish to be courteous to them.)

- *καὶ δὴ πρὸς ἐμὲ στραφείς* ‘and then, turning towards me’ subordinates *volse* to *εἶπεν*. *στραφείς* is the nom. sg. masc. second aorist passive participle of *στρέφω*, passive in form but intransitive here; the Guide himself turns, with no separate object for *il viso*. *δή* marks the transition, and *πρός* takes the full accusative pronoun *ἐμέ*.
- *περίμεν᾽ ἤδη* ‘wait now’ reverses *Ora aspetta*. *περίμεν᾽* is elided *περίμενε*, 2nd sg. present active imperative of *περιμένω*. The reporting verb *εἶπεν* (3rd sg. second aorist active indicative, ‘he said’) interrupts the speech after the imperative, as *disse* does in Italian.
- *εἰ βούλει προσηνὴς αὐτοῖς εἶναι* means ‘if you wish to be courteous to them’. This is a consequential change of construction: impersonal *si vuole* expresses what is called for, whereas *εἰ* with indicative *βούλει* (2nd sg. present middle of *βούλομαι*) makes courtesy a matter of the addressee's wish. *εἶναι* depends on *βούλει*; predicate *προσηνής* stays nominative because both verbs have the same subject. *αὐτοῖς* is the dative of the persons towards whom courtesy is shown, masculine with reference to the individuals rather than feminine in agreement with *σκιαί*.

> 16 e se non fosse il foco che saetta  
> 17 la natura del loco, io dicerei  
> 18 che meglio stesse a te, che a lor, la fretta.”  

and were there not the fire, which the nature of the place darts, I should say the haste befitted thee more than them.”

> 16 „ Εἰ μὴ τὸ πῦρ ἦν, ὅπερ κατατοξεύει  
> („ Were it not for the fire, which darts down upon)
>
> 17 „ Τόπον φύσεως τοιᾶσδ᾽, ἔλεγον ἄν σοι,  
> („ A place of such a nature, I would tell you)
>
> 18 „ Ὡς ἥρμοζέ σοι μᾶλλον σπεύδειν ἢ τούτοις.”  
> („ That haste befitted you more than these.”)

- *εἰ μὴ τὸ πῦρ ἦν* ‘if the fire were not there’ opens a present contrary-to-fact condition, without a separate connective for *e*. Greek uses *εἰ μή* and the imperfect indicative *ἦν* (3rd sg. of *εἰμί*) where Italian has the imperfect subjunctive *fosse*.
- *ὅπερ κατατοξεύει τόπον φύσεως τοιᾶσδ᾽* construes the relative differently from the supplied English. *ὅπερ*, the emphatic neut. sg. relative referring to *πῦρ*, could in isolation be nominative or accusative; *τόπον* is unambiguously accusative. The fire is therefore the **subject** of *κατατοξεύει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *κατατοξεύω*, ‘shoots down upon’), the place its target. Instead of taking *la natura del loco* as what darts the fire, Musurus gives *τόπον φύσεως τοιᾶσδε* ‘a place of such a nature’: *φύσεως* is a descriptive genitive, and *τοιᾶσδ᾽* is elided *τοιᾶσδε*, gen. sg. fem. of *τοιόσδε*, agreeing with it.
- *ἔλεγον ἄν σοι* ‘I should tell you’ completes the unreal condition: 1st sg. imperfect active indicative of *λέγω* with *ἄν*, not an optative, for conditional *dicerei*. *σοι* supplies an explicit recipient of the saying.
- *ὡς* introduces what would be said. *ἥρμοζέ σοι* is impersonal, ‘it befitted you’ (3rd sg. imperfect active indicative of *ἁρμόζω*, with temporal augment and an additional accent before enclitic *σοι*). For *che meglio stesse … la fretta*, Greek makes the action *σπεύδειν* ‘to hasten’ (present active infinitive) the subject of the verb of fitness. *μᾶλλον … ἢ τούτοις* ‘more than [it befitted] these men’ compares the two datives *σοι* and *τούτοις*, corresponding to *a te* and *a lor*.

> 19 Ricominciar, come noi ristemmo, ei  
> 20 l' antico verso ; e quando a noi fur giunti,  
> 21 fenno una rota di sè tutti e trei.  

They recommenced, as we stood still, their ancient wail; and when they had reached us, all the three made of themselves a wheel.

> 19 Ἐπεὶ δ᾽ ἡμεῖς ἔστημεν, ἤρξαντο πάλιν  
> (And when we stopped, they began again)
>
> 20 Τὸν πρῶτον γόον· ἐγγὺς δὲ γεγονότες  
> (Their former lamentation; and having come near,)
>
> 21 Ἐσχημάτισαν οἱ τρεῖς ἑαυτοῖς γῦρον.  
> (The three formed themselves into a wheel.)

- *ἐπεὶ δ᾽ ἡμεῖς ἔστημεν* ‘and when we stopped’ puts the temporal clause first rather than inserting *come noi ristemmo* after the main verb. *δέ* is elided; explicit *ἡμεῖς* marks the change of subject. *ἔστημεν* is the 1st pl. intransitive second aorist active indicative of *ἵστημι*, ‘came to a stand’, not an imperfect describing continued immobility.
- *ἤρξαντο πάλιν τὸν πρῶτον γόον* ‘they began their former lament again’ divides the force of *Ricominciar* between *ἤρξαντο* (3rd pl. aorist middle indicative of *ἄρχω*, middle ‘begin’) and *πάλιν*. There is no expressed counterpart to subject *ei*. The accusative *τὸν πρῶτον γόον* identifies what they began: *πρῶτον* looks back to the earlier utterance rather than suggesting antiquity, and *γόον* (from *γόος*, ‘lament’) makes *verso* specifically a cry of grief.
- *ἐγγὺς δὲ γεγονότες* ‘and having come near’ replaces *quando a noi fur giunti* by a circumstantial participle. *γεγονότες* is nom. pl. masc., perfect active of *γίγνομαι*: with adverbial *ἐγγύς* it marks the resulting proximity at the moment of the main action. The point of reference *a noi* is not expressed. The masculine anticipates *οἱ τρεῖς* rather than resuming the grammatical gender of *σκιαί*.
- *ἐσχημάτισαν οἱ τρεῖς ἑαυτοῖς γῦρον* has verb, subject, dative, then object: *ἐσχημάτισαν* is the augmented 3rd pl. aorist active indicative of *σχηματίζω*, ‘gave shape to, formed’. *οἱ τρεῖς* ‘the three’ requires no separate word for *tutti*. Where *di sè* makes the three themselves the material of the *rota*, *ἑαυτοῖς* is dative, not a genitive of material or a reflexive direct object: literally ‘the three formed for themselves a circle’. *γῦρον*, acc. sg. of *γῦρος*, renders the circular shape rather than naming a wheel as an object.

## Lines 22–54 — Wrestlers, Renown, and Sorrow

> 22 Qual soleano i campion far nudi ed unti,  
> 23 avvisando lor presa e lor vantaggio,  
> 24 prima che sien tra lor battuti e punti:  

As champions, naked and anointed, were wont to do, spying their grasp and vantage, ere they came to blows and thrusts at one another:

> 22 Ὡς δ᾽ ἔθος ἔστ᾽ ἀθληταῖς ἀληλιμμένους  
> (And as is the custom for athletes, anointed)
>
> 23 Καὶ γυμνοὺς ζητεῖν λαβὴν καὶ προτερήσεις,  
> (And naked, to seek a grip and advantage,)
>
> 24 Πρὶν ἐν ἀγῶνι συμπλεχθῶσιν ἀλλήλοις,  
> (Before they grapple with one another in the contest,)

- *Ὡς δ᾽ ἔθος ἔστ᾽ ἀθληταῖς* ‘and as it is customary for athletes’ replaces the personal imperfect *soleano i campion* with an impersonal present: *ἔθος* is neuter nominative singular, *ἔστ᾽* is *ἔστι* with its final vowel elided before *ἀθληταῖς*, and the dative plural names those whose custom it is. Postpositive *δ᾽* is elided *δέ*.
- *ἀληλιμμένους καὶ γυμνούς* reverses *nudi ed unti*. The perfect middle-passive participle *ἀληλιμμένους* (from *ἀλείφω*) describes the resulting state, ‘having been anointed’. Both words are masculine accusative plural: rather than agreeing with dative *ἀθληταῖς*, they describe the understood accusative subject of *ζητεῖν*.
- *ζητεῖν* (present active infinitive of *ζητέω*), ‘to seek’, gives the customary action directly, dispensing with *far* and replacing the attendant gerund *avvisando*. Its objects are *λαβήν* ‘a hold’ and plural *προτερήσεις* ‘advantages’; neither instance of *lor* is expressed.
- *πρὶν … συμπλεχθῶσιν ἀλλήλοις* ‘before they grapple with one another’ has *συμπλεχθῶσιν* (3rd plural aorist passive subjunctive of *συμπλέκω*) in a reciprocal sense, with reciprocal dative *ἀλλήλοις* for *tra lor*. Musurus retains the finite subjunctive of *prima che sien*, where classical Greek after an affirmative main clause would more usually have *πρίν* with an infinitive. One verb replaces the two participles *battuti e punti*; *ἐν ἀγῶνι* ‘in the contest’ is supplied.

> 25 così, rotando, ciascuno il visaggio  
> 26 drizzava a me, sì che in contrario il collo  
> 27 faceva a' piè continuo viaggio.  

thus, wheeling, each directed his visage toward me, so that the neck kept travelling in a direction contrary to the feet.

> 25 Οὕτως ἕκαστος στρεφόμενος, τὴν ὄψιν  
> (So each one, wheeling round, his gaze)
>
> 26 Πρὸς ἔμ᾽ ἔτεινε, ποδῶν τε καὶ τραχήλων  
> (Directed toward me, while feet and necks)
>
> 27 Ἀπεναντίως ᾀεὶ συγκινουμένων.  
> (Were ever moving together in opposite directions.)

- *Οὕτως* answers *ὡς* as *così* answers *qual*. Musurus puts the distributive subject *ἕκαστος* before *στρεφόμενος* (nominative masculine singular present middle-passive participle of *στρέφω*); the participle, ‘turning round’, gives the simultaneous action of *rotando*.
- *τὴν ὄψιν πρὸς ἔμ᾽ ἔτεινε* ‘kept directing his face towards me’ uses the article, not a possessive, with the body part. The directional phrase precedes *ἔτεινε* (3rd singular imperfect active of *τείνω*), whereas *a me* follows *drizzava*. *ἔμ᾽* is elided *ἐμέ*, the full accusative form after a preposition.
- For the result clause *sì che … il collo faceva … viaggio*, Greek has the attendant circumstance *ποδῶν τε καὶ τραχήλων … συγκινουμένων*, a genitive absolute. Feet and necks are now coordinate plural subjects (*τε καί* ‘both … and’), not respectively a point of comparison and a singular subject. The present middle-passive participle *συγκινουμένων* (from *συγκινέω*) gives motion together in time, *ἀπεναντίως* opposition in direction, and *ᾀεί* (the long-initial poetic spelling of *ἀεί*) the continuity of *continuo viaggio*.

> 28 “Eh, se miseria d'esto loco sollo  
> 29 rende in dispetto noi e nostri preghi,”  
> 30 cominciò l' uno, “e il tinto aspetto e brollo,  

And one of them began: “If the misery of this loose place, and our [stained] and scorched aspect, bring us and our prayers into contempt,

> 28 “Εἰ τὸ τληπαθὲς ἀμμώδους τόπου τοῦδε,”  
> (“If the wretchedness of this sandy place,”)
>
> 29 Εἷς ἤρξατ᾽ αὐτῶν, “ἡμᾶς καταφρονήτους  
> (One of them began, “makes us despised,)
>
> 30 „ Ποιεῖ, δεήσεις θ᾽ ἡμῶν, οἰκτράν τε θέαν,  
> („ As well as our prayers and our pitiable aspect,)

- *Εἰ τὸ τληπαθὲς ἀμμώδους τόπου τοῦδε* begins the condition before the reporting clause, as in Italian. *τὸ τληπαθές* is the substantivized neuter singular of *τληπαθής* ‘suffering, afflicted’, made to stand for the abstract *miseria*. *ἀμμώδους* ‘sandy’ interprets the physical quality of *sollo* rather than translating it as ‘loose’; it agrees with genitive *τόπου*, specified by the postposed near demonstrative *τοῦδε*. There is no equivalent of *Eh*.
- *Εἷς ἤρξατ᾽ αὐτῶν* ‘one of them began’ brings *cominciò l’uno* forward into the break before the predicate. *εἷς* takes a partitive genitive; *ἤρξατ᾽* is elided *ἤρξατο*, 3rd singular aorist middle of *ἄρχω* in the sense ‘begin’.
- *ἡμᾶς καταφρονήτους ποιεῖ* ‘makes us despicable’ is a double-accusative construction, with the predicate adjective before the verb (*ποιεῖ*, 3rd singular present active indicative of *ποιέω*, in the open condition with *εἰ*). *δεήσεις θ᾽ ἡμῶν* adds ‘and our petitions’; *θ᾽* is *τε* elided and aspirated before rough breathing.
- Crucially, *οἰκτράν τε θέαν* ‘and a pitiable appearance’ is **accusative** too: the appearance is added to what is held in contempt, not made a second subject of *ποιεῖ*, as *il tinto aspetto e brollo* is of *rende*. *καταφρονήτους* agrees directly with *ἡμᾶς*; its sense carries over to the added objects. One adjective of appraisal replaces the two descriptive adjectives.

> 31 la fama nostra il tuo animo pieghi  
> 32 a dirne chi tu se', che i vivi piedi  
> 33 così sicuro per lo inferno freghi.  

let our fame incline thy mind to tell us who thou art, that thus securely movest thy living feet through Hell.

> 31 „ Ἡμῶν ἡ φήμη τὴν σὴν ψυχὴν καμψάτω,  
> („ Let our renown incline your soul)
>
> 32 „ Ὥστ᾽ εἰπεῖν, τίς εἶ, καὶ πῶς τοὺς ζῶντας πόδας  
> („ To tell who you are, and how your living feet)
>
> 33 „ Οὕτως ἀσφαλῶς παρατρίβεις ἐν ᾍδου.  
> („ You thus safely trail through Hell.)

- *Ἡμῶν ἡ φήμη τὴν σὴν ψυχὴν καμψάτω* fronts the possessive genitive of ‘our renown’, but uses an attributive possessive adjective for ‘thy soul’. *καμψάτω* (3rd singular aorist active imperative of *κάμπτω*) answers the condition with a command, where *pieghi* is a jussive present subjunctive; the metaphor ‘bend’ is retained.
- *ὥστ᾽ εἰπεῖν* ‘so as to tell’ recasts *a dirne* as *ὥστε* with the aorist active infinitive of *λέγω*. Its understood subject is the person addressed; the recipient expressed by *-ne* is not repeated. *τίς εἶ* retains the embedded question in the second person (*εἶ*, present indicative of *εἰμί*).
- *καὶ πῶς … παρατρίβεις* coordinates a second question, ‘and how … dost thou rub’, rather than the relative characterization *che … freghi*. *τοὺς ζῶντας πόδας* uses the attributive present active participle of *ζάω* for *vivi*. *οὕτως ἀσφαλῶς* turns predicative *così sicuro* into adverbs modifying *παρατρίβεις* (2nd singular present active of *παρατρίβω*), which preserves the friction in *freghi*. *ἐν ᾍδου* is the elliptical idiom ‘in [the house] of Hades’: *ᾍδου* is possessive genitive, not a genitive governed by *ἐν*.

> 34 Questi, l' orme di cui pestar mi vedi,  
> 35 tutto che nudo e dipelato vada,  
> 36 fu di grado maggior che tu non credi.  

He in whose footsteps thou seest me tread, all naked and peeled though he be, was higher in degree than thou believest.

> 34 „ Οὗτος, ᾧ μ᾽ ἑπόμενον κατ᾽ ἴχνος βλέπεις,  
> („ This one, in whose footsteps you see me following,)
>
> 35 „ Βαίνων γυμνός τε κἀπολελεπισμένος,  
> („ Though he goes naked and flayed of skin,)
>
> 36 „ Εἶχε μείζονα βαθμὸν ἤπερ νομίζεις.  
> („ Was of higher rank than you suppose.)

- *Οὗτος* reproduces the pointing *Questi*. In *ᾧ μ᾽ ἑπόμενον κατ᾽ ἴχνος βλέπεις* ‘whom thou seest me following on his track’, the relative is dative, governed by *ἕπομαι*, not possessive as in *l’orme di cui*. *βλέπεις* (2nd singular present active) takes elided *μ᾽* (*με*) and its supplementary participle *ἑπόμενον* (present middle, accusative masculine singular) in place of *mi vedi* with infinitive *pestar*. *κατ᾽ ἴχνος* has *κατά* with the accusative singular ‘track’.
- *βαίνων γυμνός τε κἀπολελεπισμένος* ‘going both naked and stripped of skin’ gives *tutto che … vada* by a circumstantial participle with concessive force but no explicit ‘although’. *βαίνων* (present active of *βαίνω*), *γυμνός*, and *ἀπολελεπισμένος* (perfect middle-passive of *ἀπολεπίζω*, marking a resultant state) are all nominative masculine singular with *οὗτος*. *κἀπολελεπισμένος* incorporates *καί* by crasis.
- *εἶχε μείζονα βαθμόν* ‘held a greater rank’ replaces *fu di grado maggior* with an imperfect of possession (*εἶχε*, 3rd singular of *ἔχω*) and an accusative object. *ἤπερ νομίζεις* ‘than thou supposest’ introduces the comparison with *ἤ* reinforced by *-περ*; the Italian comparative *non* needs no Greek negative.

> 37 Nepote fu della buona Gualdrada;  
> 38 Guido Guerra ebbe nome, ed in sua vita  
> 39 fece col senno assai e con la spada.  

Grandson of the good Gualdrada, his name was Guido Guerra; and in his lifetime he did much with counsel and with sword.

> 37 „ Υἱωνὸς τῆς ἀγαθῆς ἦν Γουαλδράδας·  
> („ He was the grandson of good Gualdrada;)
>
> 38 „ Γιγδογγέρας δ᾽ ὄνομ᾽ ἦν αὐτῷ, κἀν βίῳ  
> („ And Guido Guerra was his name, and in life)
>
> 39 „ Μεγάλ᾽ ἔπραξεν ἔργα φρεσὶ καὶ σπάθῃ.  
> („ He did great deeds with counsel and with the sword.)

- *Υἱωνὸς τῆς ἀγαθῆς ἦν Γουαλδράδας* keeps *Nepote fu* as a finite predication: *υἱωνός* is specifically ‘grandson’, predicate nominative with *ἦν* (3rd singular imperfect of *εἰμί*). The verb separates *τῆς ἀγαθῆς* from its noun *Γουαλδράδας*, the genitive of the Hellenized feminine name, dependent on *υἱωνός*.
- *Γιγδογγέρας δ᾽ ὄνομ᾽ ἦν αὐτῷ* makes the two-part name a single Greek nominative. Instead of *ebbe nome* there is a possessive dative with ‘be’, literally ‘Guido Guerra was a name to him’; *δ᾽* links the clauses and *ὄνομ᾽* elides the final vowel of *ὄνομα*.
- *κἀν βίῳ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐν βίῳ*, with no separate possessive for *sua*. *μεγάλ᾽ ἔπραξεν ἔργα* ‘he accomplished great deeds’ replaces quantitative *fece … assai* with a verb and an explicit object; elided *μεγάλ᾽* (*μεγάλα*) agrees with *ἔργα* across *ἔπραξεν* (3rd singular aorist active of *πράσσω*, with movable *-ν*). *φρεσὶ καὶ σπάθῃ* gives the instruments without prepositions: poetic plural *φρεσί*, dative of *φρήν*, for *senno*, and singular *σπάθῃ* for *spada*.

> 40 L' altro che appresso me l' arena trita,  
> 41 è Tegghiaio Aldobrandi, la cui voce  
> 42 nel mondo su dovria esser gradita.  

The other, that treads the sand behind me, is Tegghiaio Aldobrandi, whose fame should be grateful up in the world.

> 40 „ Ὁ δ᾽ ἕτερος μετ᾽ ἐμὲ πατῶν τὴν ἄμμον,  
> („ And the other who treads the sand behind me)
>
> 41 „ Ἔστ᾽ Ἀλδοβράνδης Τεγγαῖος· οὗ τὴν φήμην  
> („ Is Tegghiaio Aldobrandi, whose renown)
>
> 42 „ Εἶναι προσφιλῆ προσῆκεν ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ·  
> („ Ought to be cherished in the world;)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ ἕτερος … πατῶν τὴν ἄμμον* replaces the finite relative *che … l’arena trita* with a present active participle of *πατέω*, nominative masculine singular with *ὁ ἕτερος*. *μετ᾽ ἐμέ* (*μετά* with accusative) interprets *appresso me* as ‘after me’ in the sequence. *Ἔστ᾽* is elided *ἔστι*; the two names *Ἀλδοβράνδης Τεγγαῖος* are nominative and in the reverse of the Italian order.
- *οὗ τὴν φήμην* ‘whose renown’ takes *voce* in the sense of reputation, using the same noun as for *fama* in line 31. The relative genitive *οὗ* depends on *φήμην*, which is now the accusative subject of infinitive *εἶναι*. Predicate *προσφιλῆ* ‘dear’ is correspondingly accusative feminine singular (of *προσφιλής*).
- *προσῆκεν* is the impersonal imperfect active of *προσήκω*, ‘it was fitting’, with the infinitive: the past indicative of propriety conveys the modal *dovria*, not a Greek optative. *ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ* renders *nel mondo* but omits the directional *su*.

> 43 Ed io, che posto son con loro in croce,  
> 44 Jacopo Rusticucci fui; e certo  
> 45 la fiera moglie più ch' altro mi nuoce.”  

And I, who am placed with them in torment, was Jacopo Rusticucci; and certainly, more than aught else, my savage wife injures me.”

> 43 „ Ἐγὼ δ᾽ ὁ ταχθεὶς σταυρηδὸν μετὰ τούτων  
> („ And I, who am placed crosswise with these,)
>
> 44 „ Ῥουστικούκης εἴμ᾽ Ἰακώβ· καὶ βεβαίως  
> („ Am Jacopo Rusticucci; and surely)
>
> 45 „ Ἡ σκληρὰ γυνὴ πρὸ πάντων ὤλεσέ με.”  
> („ My harsh wife above all else ruined me.”)

- *Ἐγὼ δ᾽ ὁ ταχθεὶς … μετὰ τούτων* ‘and I, the one stationed … with these’ replaces the relative *che posto son* with an articular aorist passive participle (*ταχθείς*, nominative masculine singular of *τάσσω*). *μετά* now takes a genitive of association. The adverb *σταυρηδόν* ‘crosswise’ gives *in croce* a literal sense of disposition, unlike the English “in torment”.
- *Ῥουστικούκης εἴμ᾽ Ἰακώβ* again reverses the names. The first receives a Greek nominative ending, the second is indeclinable; the copula between them is elided *εἰμί* ‘I am’, present for *fui*.
- *καὶ βεβαίως* closely follows *e certo*. *ἡ σκληρὰ γυνή* ‘the harsh wife’ leaves the possessive *mia* to be inferred from the article and context. *πρὸ πάντων* ‘before all things’ uses *πρό* with genitive in place of the comparison *più ch’altro*. *ὤλεσέ με* ‘ruined me’ is stronger and more final than present *mi nuoce*: transitive 3rd singular aorist active of *ὄλλυμι* with accusative object. The additional acute on *ὤλεσέ* is due to enclitic *με*.

> 46 S' io fussi stato dal foco coperto,  
> 47 gittato mi sarei tra lor di sotto,  
> 48 e credo che il dottor l' avria sofferto.  

Had I been sheltered from the fire, I should have thrown myself amid them below, and I believe my Teacher would have permitted it.

> 46 Εἴ γ᾽ ἐν σκέπῃ τοῦ πυρὸς ἤμην, ἐν μέσῳ  
> (Had I indeed been sheltered from the fire, among)
>
> 47 Αὐτῶν προσέπεσον ἄν· νομίζω δ᾽ ὅτι  
> (Them I would have thrown myself; and I think that)
>
> 48 Οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ ἐπέτρεψ᾽ ἄν μοι τοῦτο.  
> (My Guide would have permitted me this.)

- *Εἴ γ᾽ ἐν σκέπῃ τοῦ πυρὸς ἤμην* ‘if indeed I were under shelter from the fire’ gives *s’io fussi stato … coperto* by a prepositional predicate and past indicative rather than a pluperfect subjunctive. *γ᾽* is elided restrictive or emphatic *γε*; *τοῦ πυρός* identifies that against which there is shelter. *ἤμην* is the later 1st singular imperfect of *εἰμί* (classical Attic *ἦ*).
- *ἐν μέσῳ αὐτῶν προσέπεσον ἄν* ‘I should have fallen into their midst’ is the counterfactual consequence: *προσέπεσον* is 1st singular second aorist active indicative of *προσπίπτω*, with *ἄν*, not an optative. An intransitive verb replaces reflexive *gittato mi sarei*; *ἐν μέσῳ* with partitive *αὐτῶν* describes the position reached, without a separate equivalent for *di sotto*.
- *νομίζω δ᾽ ὅτι* retains the finite complement of *credo che*. *Οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ* ‘my Guide’ has crasis of *ὁ ἐμός* (the rough breathing is retained) and substitutes a title of guidance for *dottor*. *ἐπέτρεψ᾽ ἄν μοι τοῦτο* repeats the counterfactual aorist indicative with *ἄν*: *ἐπέτρεψ᾽* is elided *ἐπέτρεψε*, 3rd singular of *ἐπιτρέπω*. The person permitted is explicit in dative *μοι*, the act in accusative *τοῦτο* for Italian *l’*.

> 49 Ma perch' io mi sarei bruciato e cotto,  
> 50 vinse paura la mia buona voglia,  
> 51 che di loro abbracciar mi facea ghiotto.  

But as I should have burnt and baked myself, fear overcame the good will which made me greedy to embrace them.

> 49 Ἀλλ᾽ ὡς ἔμελλον πυρίκαυστος γενέσθαι,  
> (But since I would have been burnt by the fire,)
>
> 50 Ἐνίκησεν ὁ φόβος τὴν προθυμίαν,  
> (Fear conquered my eager desire,)
>
> 51 Τὴν ἀσπάσασθαί μ᾽ αὐτοὺς ποθεῖν ποιοῦσαν.  
> (Which made me long to embrace them.)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ ὡς ἔμελλον … γενέσθαι* ‘but since I was going to become …’ takes *perch’* causally. The prospect expressed by *mi sarei bruciato e cotto* becomes the imperfect *ἔμελλον* (1st singular active of *μέλλω*) with *γενέσθαι* (second aorist middle infinitive of *γίγνομαι*). One compound predicate adjective, *πυρίκαυστος* ‘burnt by fire’, replaces both participles; it is nominative because the subject of the infinitive is the subject of *ἔμελλον*.
- *ἐνίκησεν ὁ φόβος τὴν προθυμίαν* keeps the verb-first order of *vinse paura la mia buona voglia*. *ἐνίκησεν* is 3rd singular aorist active of *νικάω*; the articles distinguish nominative subject from accusative object. *προθυμία* expresses positive eagerness without a separate adjective for *buona* or possessive for *mia*.
- *τὴν … ποιοῦσαν* resumes *τὴν προθυμίαν* in the second attributive position: the present active participle of *ποιέω*, accusative feminine singular, replaces relative *che … facea*. The nested infinitives unpack *di loro abbracciar mi facea ghiotto*: *μ᾽* (*με*) is the person made to *ποθεῖν* ‘long’ (present active of *ποθέω*), and what he longs to do is *ἀσπάσασθαι … αὐτούς* ‘embrace them’ (aorist middle infinitive of *ἀσπάζομαι*). This last action stands first in the Greek phrase, as *abbracciar* precedes *facea*; the second accent of *ἀσπάσασθαί* is induced by the following enclitic.

> 52 Poi cominciai : “Non dispetto, ma doglia  
> 53 la vostra condizion dentro mi fisse  
> 54 tanto che tardi tutta si dispoglia,  

Then I began: “Not contempt, but sorrow, your condition fixed within me, so deeply that it will not leave me soon,

> 52 Εἶτ᾽ ἠρξάμην· “Οὐχ ὕβριν, ἀλλ᾽ ἄχθος μέγα  
> (Then I began: “Not disdain, but great grief)
>
> 53 „Ἐχάραξε βαθέως ὑμῶν τὰ πάθη  
> („Your sufferings engraved deeply)
>
> 54 „ᾈεί τ’ ἀνεξαλείπτως ἐν τῇ ψυχῇ μου,  
> („And ever indelibly upon my soul,)

- *Εἶτ᾽ ἠρξάμην* has elided *εἶτα* for *poi* and the 1st singular aorist middle of *ἄρχω* for *cominciai*. *οὐχ ὕβριν, ἀλλ᾽ ἄχθος μέγα* keeps the contrasted objects at the front: *οὐχ* is the form of *οὐ* before a rough-breathed vowel, *ἀλλ᾽* elides *ἀλλά*. *ὕβρις* suggests affront or insolence rather than simply *dispetto*; *ἄχθος μέγα* makes *doglia* a ‘great burden’ of grief.
- *ἐχάραξε βαθέως ὑμῶν τὰ πάθη* ‘your sufferings engraved deeply’ gives *fisse* a more specifically graphic verb (*ἐχάραξε*, 3rd singular aorist active of *χαράσσω*). The subject, placed after the verb, is plural *τὰ πάθη* for singular *la vostra condizion*; a neuter plural regularly takes a singular verb. Possessive *ὑμῶν* precedes the article and noun.
- *ᾈεί τ’ ἀνεξαλείπτως ἐν τῇ ψυχῇ μου* ‘and for ever indelibly in my soul’ carries on the image of engraving. *τ’* is elided postpositive *τε*; *ᾀεί* and *ἀνεξαλείπτως* modify the engraving alongside *βαθέως*, and *ἐν τῇ ψυχῇ μου* makes *dentro mi* explicit. For the degree and result *tanto che tardi tutta si dispoglia*, Musurus gives adverbs, no result clause and no reflexive verb: slow disappearance becomes the stronger claim of an indelible impression.

## Lines 55–87 — Florentine Courtesy and the Request for Remembrance

> 55 tosto che questo mio signor mi disse  
> 56 parole, per le quali io mi pensai,  
> 57 che qual voi siete, tal gente venisse.  

when this my Lord spake words to me, by which I felt that such men as you are might be coming.

> 55 „Εὐθὺς ὡς οὑμὸς προεῖπέ μοι Προστάτης  
> („As soon as my Protector spoke to me)
>
> 56 „Τοὺς λόγους, ἐξ ὧν ἐνόησα τοιούτους,  
> („Those words, from which I understood that such)
>
> 57 „Οἷοί περ ὑμεῖς, ἄνδρας ἐλευσομένους.  
> („Men as you are were about to come.)

- *Εὐθὺς ὡς* ‘as soon as’ renders *tosto che*. *οὑμὸς … Προστάτης* ‘my protector’ brackets the verb and its dative: *οὑμός* is the crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, so that the possessive is retained but *questo* has no demonstrative equivalent. *Προστάτης* denotes one who stands before another as protector, rather than a *signor* by title.
- *προεῖπέ μοι … τοὺς λόγους* ‘told me the words beforehand’ adds the anticipatory force of *προ-* to *disse*. *προεῖπε* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *προλέγω*, with the suppletive aorist of *λέγω*; the additional acute is due to enclitic *μοι*. The accusative object, unlike bare *parole*, has the article.
- *ἐξ ὧν ἐνόησα* ‘from which I understood’ makes the words the basis of an inference: *ἐξ* governs the gen. pl. relative, and *ἐνόησα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *νοέω*) needs no reflexive corresponding to *mi*.
- For *che qual voi siete, tal gente venisse*, Greek begins with the demonstrative *τοιούτους*, inserts its correlative *οἷοί περ ὑμεῖς* ‘just such as you [are]’, and only then supplies *ἄνδρας*. *τοιούτους* is acc. masc. pl. with *ἄνδρας*; *οἷοι* is nominative with *ὑμεῖς* and an understood copula; *περ* emphasizes the correspondence. In place of the Italian finite subjunctive, *ἄνδρας ἐλευσομένους* is an accusative with a predicative participle after the verb of understanding. *ἐλευσομένους* (fut. mid. ptc. of *ἔρχομαι*, active in sense) marks the coming as subsequent to the understanding, not as merely possible.

> 58 Di vostra terra sono; e sempre mai  
> 59 l' opre di voi e gli onorati nomi  
> 60 con affezion ritrassi ed ascoltai.  

Of your city am I, and always with affection have I rehearsed and heard your deeds and honoured names.

> 58 „Τῆς ὑμετέρας εἰμὶ κἀγὼ πατρίδος,  
> („Of your fatherland am I also,)
>
> 59 „Τὰ τ’ ἔνδοξ᾽ ὑμῶν ὀνόματα καὶ πράξεις  
> („And your glorious names and deeds)
>
> 60 „ᾈεὶ προσπαθῶς ἤκουσα καὶ κηρύττω.  
> („I have ever heard with affection and proclaim.)

- *Τῆς ὑμετέρας εἰμὶ κἀγὼ πατρίδος* ‘of your native land am I too’ keeps the initial genitive of belonging but separates its adjective from its noun. *ὑμετέρας* is the gen. fem. sg. of the second-person plural possessive; *κἀγώ*, crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*, adds ‘I too’ to *sono*. *πατρίς* brings out native affiliation rather than simply rendering *terra* as land.
- *Τὰ τ’ ἔνδοξ᾽ ὑμῶν ὀνόματα καὶ πράξεις* puts the names before the deeds, reversing *l’opre … e gli onorati nomi*. *τ’* is elided *τε*, answered by *καί*; *ἔνδοξ᾽* is elided *ἔνδοξα*, acc. neut. pl. agreeing with *ὀνόματα*, not with fem. *πράξεις*. The adjective means ‘held in repute’ rather than literally ‘honoured’; possessive *ὑμῶν* is tucked between adjective and noun.
- *ᾈεὶ προσπαθῶς* renders *sempre mai … con affezion* by two adverbs, ‘always, with attachment’. *ἤκουσα καὶ κηρύττω* reverses *ritrassi ed ascoltai* and differentiates the tenses: *ἤκουσα* is 1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀκούω*, with temporal augment; *κηρύττω* is 1st sg. pres. act. ind., with Attic *-ττ-* for *-σσ-*. Thus ‘I heard and I proclaim’, not two coordinated past acts.

> 61 Lascio lo fele, e vo per dolci pomi  
> 62 promessi a me per lo verace duca;  
> 63 ma fino al centro pria convien ch' io tomi.”  

I leave the gall, and go for *the sweet* apples promised me by my veracious Guide; but to the centre it behoves me first to fall."

> 61 „Τὴν χολὴν ἀφεὶς πρὸς τοὺς καρποὺς ὑπάγω,  
> („Leaving behind the gall, I go toward the fruits)
>
> 62 „Οὓς ἀψευδὴς Ἡγήτωρ ὑπέσχετό μοι·  
> („Which a truthful Guide promised me;)
>
> 63 „Ἀλλ᾽ ἄχρι κέντρου δεῖ με πρὶν καταβῆναι.”  
> („Yet down to the centre must I first descend.”)

- *Τὴν χολὴν ἀφείς* ‘having left the gall’ subordinates *lascio lo fele* to *ὑπάγω* ‘I go’. *ἀφείς* is the irregular nom. masc. sg. aor. act. ptc. of *ἀφίημι*, agreeing with the unexpressed first-person subject. *πρὸς τοὺς καρπούς* gives a direction, ‘toward the fruits’; *καρπούς* is less specific than “apples,” and there is no adjective for *dolci*.
- *Οὓς ἀψευδὴς Ἡγήτωρ ὑπέσχετό μοι* turns passive *promessi … per lo verace duca* into an active-sense relative clause. *οὕς* refers to *καρπούς* and is the accusative object of *ὑπέσχετο* (3rd sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *ὑπισχνέομαι*); *μοι* is the recipient. *ἀψευδής* ‘not speaking falsely’ and *Ἡγήτωρ* ‘leader’ are both nominative; the Italian definite article is not reproduced.
- *Ἀλλ᾽ ἄχρι κέντρου* ‘but as far as the centre’ has elided *ἀλλά* and *ἄχρι* with the genitive. Impersonal *δεῖ* takes accusative subject *με* and *καταβῆναι* (root-aor. act. inf. of *καταβαίνω*), in place of *convien ch’ io tomi*. *πρίν* is adverbial ‘first, beforehand’, corresponding to *pria*; the infinitive means ‘descend’, without specifying a fall.

> 64 “Se lungamente l' anima conduca  
> 65 le membra tue,” rispose quegli allora,  
> 66 “e se la fama tua dopo te luca,  

"So may the soul long animate thy members," he then replied, "and so thy fame shine after thee;

> 64 — “Εἴθ᾽ ἐπὶ μακρὸν ἡ σὴ ψυχὴ διέποι  
> (—“May your soul long govern)
>
> 65 „Τὰ σὰ μέλη!” τότ᾽ ἀπεκρίνατ᾽ ἐκεῖνος·  
> („Your limbs!” then answered that shade;)
>
> 66 “Εἴθ᾽ ἡ σὴ φήμη μετὰ σὲ διαλάμψαι!  
> (“And may your renown shine forth after you!)

- Musurus turns the two *se* clauses into independent wishes, repeating *εἴθ᾽* (elided *εἴθε*) in place of *se … e se*. *ἐπὶ μακρόν*, with substantival neut. acc. sg. of *μακρός*, expresses duration. *διέποι* is 3rd sg. pres. act. opt. of *διέπω*: the wish is that the soul should continue to ‘direct’ the limbs, rather than literally ‘lead’ them.
- *ἡ σὴ ψυχή … τὰ σὰ μέλη* gives possessive adjectives to both soul and limbs, where Italian has *l’anima … le membra tue*. *τότ᾽ ἀπεκρίνατ᾽ ἐκεῖνος* puts ‘then’ first and the demonstrative subject last. Both *τότε* and *ἀπεκρίνατο* are elided; the latter is 3rd sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, ‘answered’. *ἐκεῖνος* alone answers to *quegli*: there is no Greek noun for “shade.”
- *ἡ σὴ φήμη μετὰ σὲ διαλάμψαι* closely follows *la fama tua dopo te luca*. *μετά* with the accusative is temporal ‘after’; *διαλάμψαι*, despite its identity in spelling with the infinitive, is here the short 3rd sg. aor. act. opt. of *διαλάμπω*, with nominative subject *φήμη* and wish-particle *εἴθε*. In contrast to present *διέποι*, the aorist presents the shining forth as a whole.

> 67 cortesia e valor di' se dimora  
> 68 nella nostra città sì come suole,  
> 69 o se del tutto se n'è gita fuora?  

tell, if courtesy and valour abide within our city as they were wont, or have gone quite out of it?

> 67 „Λέγε, φιλοφροσύνη καὶ γενναιότης  
> („Tell me, do courtesy and valour)
>
> 68 „Τὴν ἡμετέραν οἰκοῦσ᾽, ὡς πάλαι, πόλιν ;  
> („Dwell in our city, as of old,)
>
> 69 „Ἢ ᾽κεῖθεν γεγόνασιν ἔξοροι πάντως ;  
> („Or have they been utterly banished thence?)

- *Λέγε* is 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *λέγω*, corresponding to *di’*. Rather than continuing with an indirect question introduced by *se*, Greek gives the command and then asks the question directly. *φιλοφροσύνη καὶ γενναιότης* pairs ‘kindliness, courtesy’ with ‘nobility of spirit’, both nominative subjects.
- *Τὴν ἡμετέραν οἰκοῦσ᾽, ὡς πάλαι, πόλιν* ‘do they inhabit our city, as formerly?’ interposes verb and comparison between possessive and noun. *οἰκοῦσ᾽* is elided *οἰκοῦσι*, 3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of contracted *οἰκέω*, here transitive with acc. *πόλιν*. Greek thus has plural agreement and a direct object where *dimora nella nostra città* has a singular verb and a locative phrase. *ὡς πάλαι* leaves the verb understood rather than translating *suole* by a verb of custom.
- *Ἢ ᾽κεῖθεν γεγόνασιν ἔξοροι πάντως* ‘or have they become altogether exiles from there?’ has alternative *ἤ* and prodelision of initial *ἐ-* in *ἐκεῖθεν*; the ending *-θεν* already expresses ‘from’. *γεγόνασιν* is 3rd pl. perf. act. ind. of *γίγνομαι*, with reduplication and movable *-ν*. Predicate *ἔξοροι* (nom. fem. pl. of two-termination *ἔξορος*) describes a resulting state of exile: Italian *se n’è gita fuora* says that they have gone out, not that they have become exiles. Final *πάντως* answers to *del tutto*.

> 70 chè Guglielmo Borsiere, il qual si duole  
> 71 con noi per poco, e va là coi compagni,  
> 72 assai ne cruccia con le sue parole.”  

For Guglielmo Borsiere—who has been short time in pain with us, and yonder goes with our companions—greatly torments us with his words."

> 70 „Γουλιέλμος γὰρ Βορσεῖρος, ὁ συμπάσχων  
> („For Guglielmo Borsiere, who shares in suffering)
>
> 71 „Ἡμῖν ἄρτι, νῦν σὺν ὀπαδοῖς ὢν ἄλλοις,  
> („With us of late, and now is with other companions,)
>
> 72 „Ἡμᾶς λυπεῖ μεγάλως ἐξ ὅσων λέγει.”—  
> („Grieves us sorely by what he tells.”—)

- Postpositive *γάρ* renders initial causal *chè*. *Γουλιέλμος … Βορσεῖρος* gives both names Greek nominative endings. Articular *ὁ συμπάσχων* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. ptc. of *συμπάσχω*) replaces *il qual si duole*: the compound itself means ‘suffer together with’, and takes associative dat. *ἡμῖν*. *ἄρτι* ‘just now, recently’ interprets *per poco* as recency rather than measuring duration.
- *νῦν σὺν ὀπαδοῖς ὢν ἄλλοις* ‘now being with other companions’ changes finite *va* to *ὤν*, nom. masc. sg. pres. ptc. of *εἰμί*, still referring to Borsiere. *νῦν* is temporal, not spatial like *là*; *σύν* governs dat. pl. *ὀπαδοῖς … ἄλλοις*. The adjective ‘other’ is supplied, and there is no possessive corresponding to English “our.”
- *Ἡμᾶς λυπεῖ μεγάλως* ‘he grieves us greatly’ uses transitive *λυπεῖ* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *λυπέω*) with the personal object emphatically first. *ἐξ ὅσων λέγει* replaces instrumental *con le sue parole* by ‘in consequence of all that he says’: *ὅσων* is gen. neut. pl. under *ἐξ*, by attraction for the accusative expected as the object of *λέγει*. The possessive becomes the understood subject of the verb.

> 73 “La gente nuova e i subiti guadagni  
> 74 orgoglio e dismisura han generata,  
> 75 Fiorenza, in te, sì che tu già ten piagni.”  

“The upstart people and the sudden gains, O Florence, have engendered in thee pride and excess, so that thou already weepest thereat.”

> 73 — “Ἡ νέα φυλὴ κέρδη τ᾽ ἐξαπιναῖα  
> (—“The new folk and sudden gains)
>
> 74 „ Ἀλαζονείαν ἐν σοὶ κἀμέτρους τάσεις  
> („ Arrogance in you and immoderate ambitions)
>
> 75 „ Ἔτεκον, Φλωρεντία, ἐφ᾽ οἷς καὶ κλαίεις.” —  
> („ Have bred, O Florence, whereat you already weep.”—)

- *Ἡ νέα φυλή* translates *la gente nuova* as ‘the new stock’, keeping a collective singular and putting the adjective in attributive position. *κέρδη τ᾽ ἐξαπιναῖα* ‘and sudden gains’ has enclitic *τε*, elided before the adjective; *κέρδη* (contracted nom. neut. pl. of *κέρδος*) and poetic *ἐξαπιναῖα* agree. Unlike *i subiti guadagni*, this second subject has no article.
- *Ἀλαζονείαν ἐν σοὶ κἀμέτρους τάσεις* places both accusative objects before the verb, but moves *in te* between them. *ἀλαζονεία* makes *orgoglio* specifically ‘arrogance, pretension’; *κἀμέτρους* is crasis of *καὶ ἀμέτρους*, acc. fem. pl. of two-termination *ἄμετρος*. ‘Immoderate strivings’ converts the single abstract *dismisura* into a qualified plural noun.
- *Ἔτεκον* (3rd pl. second aor. act. ind. of *τίκτω*) retains the metaphor ‘gave birth to’ but replaces compound perfect *han generata* by a simple aorist, plural for the combined subjects. *Φλωρεντία* is vocative. *ἐφ᾽ οἷς* ‘on account of which things’ uses *ἐπί* with a causal dative, the final consonant aspirated on elision before the rough breathing. The neuter plural relative sums up what precedes rather than agreeing with either feminine object. This relative construction replaces consecutive *sì che* and resumes the cause expressed by *ne*; *κλαίεις* is 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *κλαίω*, and *καί* gives emphasis rather than an explicit temporal equivalent of *già*.

> 76 Così gridai con la faccia levata;  

Thus I cried with face uplifted;

> 76 Οὕτως ἔκραξα τὰς ὄψεις ἀνυψώσας.  
> (Thus I cried out with my face uplifted.)

- *Οὕτως ἔκραξα* follows *Così gridai*: *ἔκραξα* is 1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *κράζω*, with syllabic augment. *τὰς ὄψεις ἀνυψώσας* ‘having raised my eyes/countenance’ replaces *con la faccia levata* by an active participial construction. *ἀνυψώσας* is nom. masc. sg. aor. act. ptc. of *ἀνυψόω*, agreeing with the speaker; plural *ὄψεις* is its accusative object, and the article suffices for the possessor.

> 77 e i tre, che ciò inteser per risposta,  
> 78 guatar l'un l'altro, come al ver si guata.  

and the three, who understood this as an answer, looked at one another as men look when truth is told.

> 77 Ταῦθ᾽ οἱ τρεῖς ἀκούσαντες ἐκπλήκτως εἶδον  
> (Hearing these words, the three looked in amazement)
>
> 78 Ἀλλήλους, καθὼς ὁ τἀληθὲς ἀκούων.  
> (At one another, like one who hears the truth.)

- *Ταῦθ᾽ οἱ τρεῖς ἀκούσαντες* ‘the three, having heard these things’ fronts the acc. neut. pl. object. *ταῦθ᾽* is *ταῦτα* elided before rough-breathed *οἱ*, with *τ* becoming *θ*. *ἀκούσαντες* is nom. masc. pl. aor. act. ptc. of *ἀκούω*, agreeing with substantival *οἱ τρεῖς*. It renders *inteser* in the sense ‘heard’, without *per risposta* or an explicit verb of understanding.
- *ἐκπλήκτως εἶδον ἀλλήλους* adds ‘in amazement’ to *guatar l’un l’altro*. *εἶδον*, here 3rd pl. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*, takes the reciprocal acc. masc. pl. *ἀλλήλους*. *καθὼς ὁ τἀληθὲς ἀκούων* shifts from sight to hearing: ‘as one hearing the truth [looks]’. The generic singular articular pres. act. ptc. replaces impersonal *si guata*; its object *τἀληθές* is crasis of *τὸ ἀληθές*, the substantivized neut. sg. of *ἀληθής*.

> 79 “Se l' altre volte sì poco ti costa,”  
> 80 risposer tutti, “il satisfare altrui,  
> 81 felice te, se sì parli a tua posta!  

“If otherwhile it costs thee so little to satisfy others,” they all replied, “happy thou, [if] thus [thou] speakest at thy will!

> 79 “Ὡς ἀνέκαθεν οὐ πάνυ τι διστάζεις,”  
> (“Since from of old you do not much hesitate,”)
>
> 80 Ἀπεκρίναντο πάντες, “ἄλλοις ἀρέσκειν,  
> (They all replied, “to please others,)
>
> 81 „ Εὐτυχὴς εἶ σύ, λέγων σαφῶς σὴν γνώμην.  
> („ Happy are you, speaking your mind plainly.)

- *Ὡς ἀνέκαθεν* ‘since from the beginning’ makes conditional *se* causal and reads *l’altre volte* as a settled habit, not ‘on other occasions’. *οὐ πάνυ τι διστάζεις* ‘you do not hesitate very much’ personalizes *sì poco ti costa*. *διστάζεις* is 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *διστάζω*; enclitic neut. *τι* is adverbial in *πάνυ τι*, an expression of degree.
- *Ἀπεκρίναντο πάντες* (3rd pl. aor. mid. ind. of *ἀποκρίνομαι*) interrupts the sentence in the same place as *risposer tutti*. *ἄλλοις ἀρέσκειν* then completes *διστάζεις*: *ἀρέσκειν*, pres. act. inf. of *ἀρέσκω*, means ‘to please’, and the persons pleased stand in the dative. Italian nominalizes the infinitive in *il satisfare*; Greek leaves it an anarthrous complement.
- *Εὐτυχὴς εἶ σύ* makes explicit the copula omitted in *felice te*, with predicate nom. sg. *εὐτυχής* and emphatic subject *σύ*. *λέγων σαφῶς σὴν γνώμην* ‘speaking your opinion plainly’ is a circumstantial nom. masc. sg. pres. act. ptc., not a second explicit condition for *se sì parli*. *σαφῶς* specifies the manner as clarity, while the object *σὴν γνώμην* interprets *a tua posta* as freedom to say what one thinks.

> 82 Però, se campi d' esti lochi bui  
> 83 e torni a riveder le belle stelle,  
> 84 quando ti gioverà dicere: 'Io fui,'  
> 85 fa che di noi alla gente favelle.”  

Therefore, if thou escape out of these gloomy regions, and return to see again the beauteous stars; when thou shalt rejoice to say, ‘I was,’ see that thou speak of us to men.”

> 82 „ Ἀλλ᾽ ἂν τῶν ζόφου χωρῶν τούτων ἐξέλθῃς,  
> („ Yet if you come forth from these gloomy regions,)
>
> 83 „ Ἐπανίδῃς τε πάλιν λαμπροὺς ἀστέρας,  
> („ And look once more upon the shining stars,)
>
> 84 „ Ὅτε χάρμα μέγ᾽ ἔσται σοι λέγειν—‘ ἤμην,’  
> („ When it will be a great joy to you to say—‘I was,’)
>
> 85 „ Δὸς ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν ἄνω ποιεῖσθαι λόγους.”  
> („ Grant that above men may speak of us.”)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ ἂν … ἐξέλθῃς* opens with adversative *ἀλλά*, elided, rather than inferential *però*. Here *ἄν* has the conditional use of *ἐάν* ‘if’, with the subjunctive. *ἐξέλθῃς* is 2nd sg. second aor. act. subj. of *ἐξέρχομαι*, ‘come out’, not specifically ‘survive’. *τῶν ζόφου χωρῶν τούτων* is a genitive of separation; embedded *ζόφου* ‘of gloom’ replaces adjectival *bui*, and the demonstrative follows the articular noun.
- *Ἐπανίδῃς τε πάλιν λαμπροὺς ἀστέρας* coordinates a second 2nd sg. aor. act. subj. under the same condition by postpositive *τε*. *ἐπανίδῃς*, from the *ἰδ-* aorist of the compound verb of seeing, means ‘see again’; *πάλιν* reinforces the prefix where Italian has both *torni* and *riveder*. *λαμπρούς*, acc. masc. pl. with *ἀστέρας*, gives brightness rather than the beauty of *belle*.
- *Ὅτε χάρμα μέγ᾽ ἔσται σοι λέγειν* ‘when it will be a great joy to you to say’ retains *quando* with a future indicative. *ἔσται* is 3rd sg. fut. of *εἰμί*, middle in form; *λέγειν* is the infinitival subject, *χάρμα μέγ᾽* the predicate, with neut. sg. *μέγα* elided. The experiencer is dative as in *ti gioverà*. *ἤμην* renders *Io fui* without an independent pronoun: it is the later middle-form 1st sg. imperfect of *εἰμί*, rather than the usual Attic *ἦ*.
- *Δὸς ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν ἄνω ποιεῖσθαι λόγους* substitutes ‘grant … to make mention of us above’ for *fa che … favelle*. *δός* is 2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *δίδωμι*; it governs a pres. mid. infinitive, *ποιεῖσθαι*, with acc. pl. *λόγους* in the idiom ‘make remarks, speak’. *ὑπέρ* with gen. *ἡμῶν* gives the subject or interest of the remarks, *ἄνω* their location. Greek names neither an audience corresponding to *alla gente* nor a separate accusative subject of the infinitive: “men” in the line-by-line gloss is supplied.

> 86 Indi rupper la rota, ed a fuggirsi  
> 87 ale sembiar le gambe loro snelle.  

Then they broke their wheel; and, as they fled, their nimble legs seemed wings.

> 86 Εἶτ᾽ ἔλυσαν τὸν γῦρον, καὐτῶν φευγόντων  
> (Then they broke up their wheel, and as they fled)
>
> 87 Πτέρυξιν ἐῴκεισαν οἱ ψιλοὶ πόδες.  
> (Their bare feet seemed like wings.)

- *Εἶτ᾽ ἔλυσαν τὸν γῦρον* ‘then they dissolved the circle’ has elided *εἶτα* and *ἔλυσαν*, 3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *λύω*. Greek takes *rota* as a circular formation, *γῦρος*, rather than translating the object ‘wheel’ literally.
- *καὐτῶν φευγόντων* ‘and while they were fleeing’ recasts *ed a fuggirsi* as a genitive absolute. *καὐτῶν* is crasis of *καὶ αὐτῶν*; *φευγόντων* is gen. masc. pl. pres. act. ptc. of *φεύγω*, marking simultaneity. The change of construction distinguishes the fleeing persons from the grammatical subject of the next verb, their feet.
- *Πτέρυξιν ἐῴκεισαν οἱ ψιλοὶ πόδες* preserves the Italian order of comparison, verb, and delayed subject. *πτέρυξιν* is dat. pl. of *πτέρυξ*, with movable *-ν*: where Italian has predicative *ale* after *sembiar*, Greek construes resemblance with the dative. *ἐῴκεισαν* is 3rd pl. pluperfect act. ind. of the stative perfect *ἔοικα* ‘be like’, hence ‘were like’, not ‘had seemed’. *οἱ ψιλοὶ πόδες* means ‘the bare feet’: the article leaves possession implicit, *πόδες* replaces *gambe*, and *ψιλοί* does not express the nimbleness of *snelle*.

## Lines 88–120 — The Waterfall and the Signal

> 88 Un ammen non saria potuto dirsi  
> 89 tosto così, com' ei furo spariti:  

An "Amen" could not have been said so quickly as they vanished:

> 88 Ἀμὴν οὐκ ἂν εἴποι τις τάχιον, ὅσον  
> (Not sooner could one say Amen than)
>
> 89 Ἀφανεῖς αὐτοὶ γεγόνασιν εὐθέως.  
> (They straightway vanished out of sight.)

- *Ἀμὴν οὐκ ἂν εἴποι τις τάχιον* turns the passive *un ammen non saria potuto dirsi* into ‘one could not say Amen faster’. *Ἀμήν* is indeclinable, here the object of *εἴποι*; enclitic *τις* supplies an indefinite subject. *ἄν* with *εἴποι* (3rd sg. second aor. act. opt. of *λέγω*) expresses potentiality, without a separate verb for *potuto* or an explicit past counterfactual.
- *τάχιον* is the adverbial neuter comparative of *ταχύς*. Musurus combines it with *ὅσον* ‘in the measure that’, retaining something of the Italian equative *tosto così, com’* rather than using the ordinary comparative conjunction *ἤ*. *Ἀφανεῖς αὐτοὶ γεγόνασιν* renders *ei furo spariti* by a predicate adjective (nom. pl. of *ἀφανής*) and the perfect active indicative of *γίγνομαι*, 3rd pl.: ‘they themselves have become invisible’. *εὐθέως* adds ‘straightaway’.

> 90 per che al maestro parve di partirsi.  

wherefore it pleased my Master to depart.

> 90 Ἐπεὶ δ᾽ ἀπέρχεσθ᾽ οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ ἔγνω,  
> (And since my Guide decided to depart,)

- *Ἐπεὶ δ᾽ … ἔγνω* ‘and since … he decided’ reverses the connective relation: *per che* makes the decision a consequence of the disappearance; Greek subordinates the decision to *Εἱπόμην* in 91. *δ᾽* is elided *δέ*. *ἔγνω* (3rd sg. root aor. act. ind. of *γιγνώσκω*) with a complementary infinitive gives ‘resolved to’, in place of impersonal *parve* with dative *al maestro*.
- *ἀπέρχεσθ᾽* is *ἀπέρχεσθαι* (pres. mid. inf. of *ἀπέρχομαι*), with elision of final *-αι* and aspiration of *τ* before the rough breathing of *οὑμός*. *οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ* ‘my Guide’ is the nominative subject; *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*.

> 91 Io lo seguiva, e poco eravam iti,  
> 92 che il suon dell' acqua n' era sì vicino  
> 93 che, per parlar, saremmo appena uditi.  

I followed him; and we had gone but little, when the sound of the water was so near us, that in speaking we should scarce have heard each other.

> 91 Εἱπόμην αὐτῷ, καὶ μικρόν τι προβάντες  
> (I followed him, and having gone a little way)
>
> 92 Ἦμεν ἔγγιστα τοῦ τῶν ὑδάτων ἤχου,  
> (We came close to the roaring of the waters,)
>
> 93 Ὥστ᾽ ὁμιλοῦντες ἠκουόμεθα μόλις.  
> (So that in speaking we could scarcely hear each other.)

- *Εἱπόμην αὐτῷ* (1st sg. imperf. mid. ind. of *ἕπομαι*, with augmented stem *εἱπ-*) matches the imperfect *seguiva*, but the person followed is dative, not accusative as in *lo*. *μικρόν τι* is an accusative of extent, ‘a little way’; *προβάντες* (nom. masc. pl. second aor. act. participle of *προβαίνω*) makes *poco eravam iti* an action anterior to the plural main verb.
- *Ἦμεν ἔγγιστα τοῦ τῶν ὑδάτων ἤχου* ‘we were very close to the sound of the waters’ reverses the Italian subject and reference point: *il suon* was near *us*. *ἦμεν* is 1st pl. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*; superlative adverb *ἔγγιστα* takes the genitive *τοῦ … ἤχου*, enclosing the dependent genitive plural *τῶν ὑδάτων* where Italian has singular *dell’ acqua*.
- *Ὥστ᾽* (elided *ὥστε*) introduces a finite result clause. *ὁμιλοῦντες* (nom. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *ὁμιλέω*) gives the circumstances, ‘while conversing’. *ἠκουόμεθα μόλις* (1st pl. imperf. pass. ind. of *ἀκούω*) states ‘we were scarcely heard’ as fact rather than retaining conditional *saremmo … uditi*; reciprocity is implicit, not expressed by a pronoun.

> 94 Come quel fiume, che ha proprio cammino  
> 95 prima da Monte Veso in ver levante  
> 96 dalla sinistra costa d' Apennino,  

As that river—which first has a path of its own from Monte Veso toward the east, on the left skirt of the Apennine;

> 94 Καθάπερ ὁ ποταμὸς ὁ διαῤῥέων  
> (Even as that river which flows)
>
> 95 Πρῶτον ἐν ὄρει Βησούλῳ πρὸς ἑῴαν  
> (First on Mount Vesulus toward the east)
>
> 96 Ἐπ᾽ ἀριστερὰ τῆς πλευρᾶς Ἀπεννίνου  
> (Upon the left side of the Apennine slope)

- *Καθάπερ ὁ ποταμὸς ὁ διαῤῥέων* opens a comparison answered by *Οὕτως* in 103. The article alone stands for *quel*; its repetition introduces an attributive participle (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. of *διαρρέω*), ‘the river that flows through’. This condenses *che ha proprio cammino*: neither a distinct path nor its being the river’s own is explicit. The spelling *-ῤῥ-* marks doubled *ρ* in the compound.
- *Πρῶτον* ‘first’ attaches to the participle. *ἐν ὄρει Βησούλῳ* gives location rather than the point of departure in *da Monte Veso*: *ὄρει* is dat. sg. of *ὄρος*, with the mountain name likewise dative. *πρὸς ἑῴαν* uses *πρός* with the accusative for direction, ‘toward the east’.
- *Ἐπ᾽ ἀριστερὰ* (elided *ἐπί* with adverbial neut. pl. *ἀριστερά*) means ‘on the left’; ‘left’ no longer modifies *costa* directly. *τῆς πλευρᾶς Ἀπεννίνου* specifies ‘of the flank of the Apennine’, the proper name in the genitive.

> 97 che si chiama Acquacheta suso, avante  
> 98 che si divalli giù nel basso letto,  
> 99 ed a Forlì di quel nome è vacante,  

which is called Acquacheta above, ere it descends to its low bed, and is vacant of that name at Forli—

> 97 Ὑπ᾽ ὄνομ᾽ Ἀκβακέτας πρὸς τἄνω, κλῆσιν  
> (Under the name Acquacheta in its upper course, a name)
>
> 98 Ἣν ἀποβάλλει καταβὰς εἰς τὴν κάτω  
> (Which it loses when it has descended to its lower)
>
> 99 Κοίτην ἐν Φόρῳ Λιβίου, καταπίπτει  
> (Bed at Forum Livii, plunges down)

- *Ὑπ᾽ ὄνομ᾽ Ἀκβακέτας* ‘under the name of Acquacheta’ continues the description of the river’s course instead of supplying a finite passive for *si chiama*. Both *ὑπό* and accusative *ὄνομα* are elided; *Ἀκβακέτας* is genitive. *πρὸς τἄνω* ‘toward the upper parts’ has crasis of *τὰ ἄνω* and renders *suso*.
- *κλῆσιν Ἣν ἀποβάλλει* resumes *ὄνομα* with an appositional accusative, ‘a designation which it sheds’. *ἥν* (acc. fem. sg.) takes its gender from *κλῆσιν* and is the object of *ἀποβάλλει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἀποβάλλω*). Active loss replaces the state *di quel nome è vacante*.
- *καταβὰς εἰς τὴν κάτω Κοίτην* ‘having descended into the lower bed’ makes the descent prior to the loss of the name, rather than marking the upper reach by *avante che si divalli*. *καταβάς* is nom. masc. sg. second aor. act. participle of *καταβαίνω*, agreeing with the river; indeclinable *κάτω* is attributive between article and noun. *ἐν Φόρῳ Λιβίου* renders *a Forlì* by the ancient name, with *Φόρῳ* dative after *ἐν* and *Λιβίου* genitive. The line ends with *καταπίπτει* ‘falls down’, already the main verb of the comparison.

> 100 rimbomba là sopra San Benedetto  
> 101 dell' alpe, per cadere ad una scesa,  
> 102 ove dovea per mille esser ricetto:  

resounds from the mountain, there above San Benedetto, in falling at a descent, where for a thousand there should be refuge:

> 100 Ἀντιβομβῶν ἐπάνω τοῦ Μαρουΐου  
> (Booming back above Maruvium)
>
> 101 Ἐξ ὕψους εἰς φάραγγας, ἔνθα περ ἔδει  
> (From on high into chasms, where there ought)
>
> 102 Χιλιάσιν ἂν καταφύγιον εἶναι·  
> (To be a refuge for thousands:)

- *Ἀντιβομβῶν* ‘booming in answer’ (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *ἀντιβομβέω*) accompanies *καταπίπτει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *καταπίπτω*). Musurus thus exchanges the ranks of *rimbomba* and *per cadere*: falling is the finite action and reverberation its accompaniment.
- *ἐπάνω τοῦ Μαρουΐου* takes the genitive after ‘above’ and substitutes a different place-name for *San Benedetto dell’ alpe*. *Ἐξ ὕψους εἰς φάραγγας* supplies explicit endpoints, ‘from a height into ravines’ (*ὕψους*, gen. sg. of *ὕψος*; *φάραγγας*, acc. pl. of *φάραγξ*), rather than a single *scesa*.
- *ἔνθα περ ἔδει … καταφύγιον εἶναι* ‘just where there ought to be a refuge’ uses relative adverb *ἔνθα* strengthened by *περ*. Impersonal *ἔδει* (imperf. ind. of *δεῖ*) governs the infinitive *εἶναι*, with neuter *καταφύγιον* its accusative subject. *ἄν* gives the construction a qualified or unreal force beyond simple *ἔδει*. *Χιλιάσιν* (dat. pl. of *χιλιάς*) is a dative of advantage, ‘for thousands’, not the single thousand of *per mille*.

> 103 così, giù d' una ripa discoscesa,  
> 104 trovammo risonar quell' acqua tinta,  
> 105 sì che in poc' ora avria l' orecchie offesa.  

thus down from a steep bank we found that tainted water re-echoing, so that in little time it would have stunned the ear.

> 103 Οὕτως ἐκ πέτρας ἀποῤῥωγάδος κάτω  
> (So from a sheer precipitous rock downward)
>
> 104 Εὕρομεν περιηχοῦν τὸ βαπτὸν ὕδωρ,  
> (We found the dyed water resounding all around,)
>
> 105 Ὥστ᾽ ἀκοὰς ἐν βραχεῖ κωφὰς γενέσθαι.  
> (So that in a short while our ears would be deafened.)

- *Οὕτως ἐκ πέτρας ἀποῤῥωγάδος κάτω* answers *Καθάπερ*. *κάτω* ‘down’ comes after the phrase of origin, not before it as *giù* does. *ἐκ* governs *πέτρας*, ‘rock’ rather than ‘bank’; *ἀποῤῥωγάδος* (gen. fem. sg. of *ἀπορρωγάς*) agrees with it, ‘broken sheer’, again with the spelling *-ῤῥ-*.
- *Εὕρομεν περιηχοῦν τὸ βαπτὸν ὕδωρ* retains the order of *trovammo risonar quell’ acqua tinta*. *εὕρομεν* (1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *εὑρίσκω*, without the Attic lengthened augment *ηὑ-*) takes an object and supplementary participle rather than an infinitive: *περιηχοῦν* (pres. act. of *περιηχέω*) agrees with *ὕδωρ* in the accusative neuter singular. *βαπτόν* is a verbal adjective, ‘dyed’; the article replaces *quell’*.
- *Ὥστ᾽ ἀκοὰς ἐν βραχεῖ κωφὰς γενέσθαι* gives the natural consequence by *ὥστε* with infinitive, rather than a finite conditional as in *avria … offesa*. *ἀκοάς* ‘ears’ is the accusative subject of *γενέσθαι* (second aor. mid. inf. of *γίγνομαι*); *κωφάς* is its predicate, ‘to become deaf’, rather than an injury inflicted by the water. *ἐν βραχεῖ* has substantival dative neuter of *βραχύς*, ‘in a short while’.

> 106 Io aveva una corda intorno cinta,  
> 107 e con essa pensai alcuna volta  
> 108 prender la lonza alla pelle dipinta.  

I had a cord girt round me; and with it I thought some time to catch the Leopard of the painted skin.

> 106 Ἐγὼ περὶ τὴν ζώνην εἶχον σχοινίον,  
> (I had about my waist a cord,)
>
> 107 ᾯπερ ἑλεῖν ἄλλοτε διενοούμην  
> (With which I had once thought to capture)
>
> 108 Τὴν ποικιλοδέρματον πάρδαλιν κείνην.  
> (That leopard with the dappled hide.)

- *Ἐγὼ … εἶχον σχοινίον* retains *Io aveva* with expressed subject and imperfect (1st sg. act. ind. of *ἔχω*). *περὶ τὴν ζώνην* ‘about the waist’ specifies what the cord encircled, and dispenses with the participle *cinta*. *σχοινίον* is a neuter diminutive used for ‘cord’.
- *ᾯπερ* ‘with which very thing’ replaces *e con essa* by a relative link: instrumental dat. neut. sg. of *ὅς* with intensive *περ*. *ἑλεῖν* (second aor. act. inf. of *αἱρέω*) is placed before *ἄλλοτε διενοούμην*, ‘I once had it in mind’. *διενοούμην* (1st sg. imperf. mid. ind. of *διανοέομαι*, with internal augment) presents as an intention in progress what *pensai* presents as a past act.
- *Τὴν ποικιλοδέρματον πάρδαλιν κείνην* is the object of *ἑλεῖν*. The compound adjective *ποικιλοδέρματον* ‘variegated-hided’ agrees with *πάρδαλιν* (acc. fem. sg. of *πάρδαλις*), compressing *alla pelle dipinta*. Postposed *κείνην*, the poetic shorter form of *ἐκείνην*, adds ‘that’ to the Italian definite article; the demonstrative stands outside the article–noun group.

> 109 Poscia che l'ebbi tutta da me sciolta,  
> 110 sì come il duca m' avea comandato,  
> 111 porsila a lui aggroppata e ravvolta.  

After I had quite unloosed it from me, as my Guide commanded me, I held it out to him coiled and wound up.

> 109 Τοῦτο γοῦν ἀπ᾽ ἐμαυτοῦ συνάπαν λύσας,  
> (Having then wholly loosed this from myself,)
>
> 110 Καθά μοι διέταξεν οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ,  
> (Even as my Guide commanded me,)
>
> 111 Παρέσχον αὐτῷ κυκληδὸν εἰλιγμένον.  
> (I handed it to him coiled up in a ring.)

- *Τοῦτο γοῦν ἀπ᾽ ἐμαυτοῦ συνάπαν λύσας* makes *Poscia che l’ebbi … sciolta* a circumstantial participle: *λύσας* (nom. masc. sg. aor. act. of *λύω*) agrees with the subject of *Παρέσχον* and denotes prior action. Accusative neuter *τοῦτο* resumes *σχοινίον*; *συνάπαν* agrees with it, ‘the whole of it’. *γοῦν* ‘at any rate, then’ adds a connective; *ἀπ᾽ ἐμαυτοῦ* has elided *ἀπό* with the reflexive genitive of separation.
- *Καθά μοι διέταξεν οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ* ‘just as my Guide directed me’ retains the comparison *sì come*. *μοι* is enclitic dative of the person instructed; *διέταξεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *διατάσσω*, augmented after the prefix) uses a simple aorist for *m’ avea comandato*.
- *Παρέσχον αὐτῷ* ‘I offered [it] to him’ (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *παρέχω*) takes a dative recipient, with the cord understood as object. *κυκληδὸν εἰλιγμένον* renders the two participles *aggroppata e ravvolta* by a manner adverb ‘in a circle’ and a single perfect mid.-pass. participle of *ἑλίσσω*, acc. neut. sg., describing the resulting coiled state.

> 112 Ond' ei si volse in ver lo destro lato,  
> 113 e alquanto di lungi dalla sponda  
> 114 la gittò giuso in quell' alto burrato.  

Then he bent himself toward the right side, and threw it, some distance from the edge, down into that steep abyss.

> 112 Οὗτος δ᾽ ἐστράφη πρὸς τὸ δεξιὸν μέρος  
> (And he turned toward the right side)
>
> 113 Μικρόν τι μακρὰν τῆς προκειμένης ἄκρας,  
> (A little way back from the jutting brink,)
>
> 114 Αὐτό τ’ εἰς βυθὸν ἔῤῥιψε τοῦ βαράθρου.  
> (And flung it down into the depth of the abyss.)

- *Οὗτος δ᾽ ἐστράφη* ‘and he turned’ uses the demonstrative to resume the Guide, with connective *δέ* rather than the fuller transition *Ond’ ei*. *ἐστράφη* is 3rd sg. second aor. pass. ind. of *στρέφω*, intransitive here for reflexive *si volse*. *πρὸς τὸ δεξιὸν μέρος* expresses direction by *πρός* with the accusative.
- *Μικρόν τι μακρὰν* combines an accusative of degree, ‘a little’, with adverbial *μακράν* ‘far’, closely echoing *alquanto di lungi*. *τῆς προκειμένης ἄκρας* is a genitive of separation; *προκειμένης* (gen. fem. sg. pres. mid. participle of *πρόκειμαι*) qualifies the brink as ‘lying out in front’, more explicit than *sponda*.
- *Αὐτό τ’ … ἔῤῥιψε* ‘and he threw it’ postpones the connective (*τε*, elided before *εἰς*) until after the object, neuter for the cord rather than feminine *la*. *ἔῤῥιψε* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ῥίπτω*, with initial rho doubled after the augment. *εἰς βυθὸν … τοῦ βαράθρου* ‘into the depth of the chasm’ interprets *giuso in quell’ alto burrato* through a noun of depth with dependent genitive, without reproducing the demonstrative.

> 115 “E pur convien che novità risponda,”  
> 116 dicea fra me medesmo, “al nuovo cenno  
> 117 che il maestro con l' occhio sì seconda.”  

"Surely," said I within myself, "something new must answer this new signal, which my Master thus follows with his eye."

> 115 “Ἀναμφιβόλως νέον τι προμηνύει,”  
> (“Undoubtedly it portends something new,”)
>
> 116 Ἔλεγον κατ’ ἐμαυτόν, “ἡ νέα τάσις  
> (I said to myself, “this new intent)
>
> 117 „Ἣν ἐμφαίνει τὸ βλέμμα τοὐμοῦ Προστάτου.”  
> („Which the gaze of my Protector reveals.”)

- *Ἀναμφιβόλως νέον τι προμηνύει* ‘undoubtedly it portends something new’ substantially changes the syntax of *E pur convien che novità risponda*. The adverb absorbs the insistence of *pur convien*; there is no impersonal necessity or subordinate subjunctive. *νέον τι* (acc. neut. sg., adjective with enclitic indefinite) is the object, not the subject as *novità* was. *προμηνύει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *προμηνύω*) expresses prediction rather than response.
- *Ἔλεγον κατ’ ἐμαυτόν* (1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *λέγω*; elided *κατά* with reflexive accusative) matches *dicea fra me medesmo*. The insertion separates *προμηνύει* from its nominative subject *ἡ νέα τάσις*, ‘the new intent’, Musurus’ interpretation of *al nuovo cenno*. The Italian repetition survives as *νέον … νέα*, but the signal to which something must respond is now the intent which foretells it.
- *Ἣν ἐμφαίνει τὸ βλέμμα τοὐμοῦ Προστάτου* ‘which my Protector’s gaze reveals’ makes *ἥν* (acc. fem. sg., referring to *τάσις*) the object of *ἐμφαίνει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἐμφαίνω*). *τὸ βλέμμα* is the subject: the master no longer follows a signal *con l’ occhio*, but his gaze discloses his intent. *τοὐμοῦ* is crasis of genitive *τοῦ ἐμοῦ*, agreeing with *Προστάτου*; there is no separate *sì*.

> 118 Ahi quanto cauti gli uomini esser denno  
> 119 presso a color, che non veggon pur l' opra,  
> 120 ma per entro i pensier miran col senno!  

Ah! how cautious ought men to be with those who see not only the deed, but with their sense look through into the thoughts!

> 118 Πόσον φρονίμους δεῖ τοὺς ἀνθρώπους εἶναι  
> (How cautious men ought to be)
>
> 119 Παρὰ τοῖς μὴ βλέπουσι μόνον εἰς τοὖργον,  
> (Around those who do not look merely at the outward deed,)
>
> 120 Τὰ δ’ ἐνδόμυχ’ ὁρῶσι τῇ διανοίᾳ!  
> (But behold the innermost thoughts with their mind!)

- *Πόσον φρονίμους δεῖ τοὺς ἀνθρώπους εἶναι* uses exclamatory adverbial accusative *πόσον* ‘how very’, without an interjection for *Ahi*. Impersonal *δεῖ* replaces personal *denno*: *τοὺς ἀνθρώπους* is the accusative subject of present infinitive *εἶναι*, and *φρονίμους* its predicate, ‘prudent’ for *cauti*.
- *Παρὰ τοῖς μὴ βλέπουσι μόνον εἰς τοὖργον* ‘in the presence of those not looking only at the deed’ uses *παρά* with the dative for *presso a*. An articular participle replaces *color, che*: *βλέπουσι* is dat. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *βλέπω*, not a finite 3rd plural. *μή* characterizes the class of persons; *μόνον* is adverbial. *εἰς τοὖργον* makes the deed the direction of their looking rather than the direct object of *veggon*; *τοὖργον* is crasis of *τὸ ἔργον*.
- *Τὰ δ’ ἐνδόμυχ’ ὁρῶσι τῇ διανοίᾳ* balances *μὴ … μόνον* with adversative *δέ*. *ὁρῶσι* (dat. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *ὁράω*) is parallel to *βλέπουσι* under the same article. Its direct object *τὰ ἐνδόμυχα* ‘the things hidden within’ is a substantivized neuter plural for *per entro i pensier*, without a separate noun for ‘thoughts’. Both *δέ* and *ἐνδόμυχα* are elided. *τῇ διανοίᾳ* is the instrumental dative, ‘with the understanding’, corresponding to *col senno*.

## Lines 121–136 — The Figure Swimming Upwards

> 121 Ei disse a me: “Tosto verrà di sopra  
> 122 ciò ch'io attendo, e che il tuo pensier sogna  
> 123 tosto convien ch' al tuo viso si scopra.”  

He said to me: “What I expect will soon come up; and what thy thought dreams of, soon must be discovered to thy view.”

> 121 Εἶπέ μοι δ’ αὐτός· “Αὐτίκ’ ἄνωθεν ὄψει  
> (And he said to me: “Straightway from above you shall see)
>
> 122 „Ὅ, τι προσμένω, καὐτίκ’ ἐνώπιόν σου  
> („What I await, and straightway before you)
>
> 123 „Ὅ, τι διαμαντεύῃ φανήσεταί σοι.”  
> („What your thought divines shall appear to you.”)

- *Εἶπέ μοι δ’ αὐτός* ‘and he himself said to me’ puts the verb first, where Italian begins with the pronoun *Ei*. *Εἶπε* is the 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *λέγω*; its extra acute in *εἶπέ μοι* is due to the enclitic dative *μοι*, which renders *a me*. Postpositive *δέ*, elided before *αὐτός*, adds a connective; nominative *αὐτός* gives the subject more emphasis than a mere personal pronoun.
- *Αὐτίκ’ ἄνωθεν ὄψει ὅ, τι προσμένω* ‘straightway from above you will see what I await’ changes both the subject and the direction of *Tosto verrà di sopra ciò ch’io attendo*. What is awaited is the subject of Italian *verrà* but the object of Greek *ὄψει* (2nd sg. future middle indicative of *ὁράω*, active in sense). *Ἄνωθεν* properly means ‘from above’: the Greek gives the direction of viewing rather than saying that the thing will come up.
- *Ὅ, τι* is the neuter singular of the indefinite relative *ὅστις*, distinguished in writing from the conjunction *ὅτι*. It is accusative as object of *προσμένω* (1st sg. present active indicative, ‘I await’); the whole relative clause, without a separate antecedent corresponding to *ciò*, serves as object of *ὄψει*.
- *Καὐτίκ’* combines crasis of *καί* with *αὐτίκα* and elision of the final vowel before *ἐνώπιον*. Musurus thus repeats *αὐτίκα* for the repeated *tosto*, but puts it before the second relative clause rather than after it. *Ὅ, τι διαμαντεύῃ* means ‘what you divine’: *διαμαντεύῃ* is 2nd sg. present middle indicative of *διαμαντεύομαι*. The person addressed replaces *il tuo pensier* as subject, and divining replaces dreaming.
- *Ἐνώπιόν σου … φανήσεταί σοι* ‘before you … will appear to you’ distributes *al tuo viso si scopra* between *ἐνώπιον* with genitive *σου* and *φαίνομαι* with dative *σοι*. The additional acutes in *ἐνώπιόν* and *φανήσεταί* mark the following enclitics. The second relative clause is the subject of *φανήσεται*, 3rd sg. future passive indicative of *φαίνω*, intransitive in sense. The simple prediction replaces impersonal *convien* and its subjunctive clause *ch’ … si scopra*: the Greek does not separately express necessity.

> 124 Sempre a quel ver ch' ha faccia di menzogna  
> 125 de' l' uom chiuder le labbra finch' ei puote,  
> 126 però che senza colpa fa vergogna;  

Always to that truth which has an air of falsehood, a man should close his lips, [so far as he is able], for, though blameless, he incurs reproach;

> 124 Προσήκει πρὸς τἀληθὲς τὸ ψεύδους φέρον  
> (It is fitting, before a truth that bears falsehood's)
>
> 125 Σχῆμ’, εἰ δυνατόν, κλείειν ἡμᾶς τὰ χείλη,  
> (Semblance, if possible, for us to close our lips,)
>
> 126 Ἵνα μὴ χωρὶς σφάλματος αἰσχυνθῶμεν.  
> (Lest without fault we be put to shame.)

- Impersonal *προσήκει* ‘it is fitting’ (3rd sg. present active indicative of *προσήκω*) brings the equivalent of *de’* to the front. *Sempre* has no separate equivalent. *Πρὸς τἀληθές* ‘in the face of the truth’ renders *a quel ver*: *πρός* takes the accusative, and *τἀληθές* is crasis of *τὸ ἀληθές*, the substantivized neuter singular of *ἀληθής*. There is no demonstrative corresponding to *quel*.
- *Τὸ ψεύδους φέρον σχῆμα* ‘bearing the semblance of falsehood’ recasts *ch’ ha faccia di menzogna*. The repeated article *τό* introduces an attributive participial phrase qualifying *τἀληθές*. *Φέρον* (present active participle of *φέρω*, acc. neut. sg.) agrees with the truth, not with the falsehood; its object is *σχῆμα*, with dependent genitive *ψεύδους* (from *ψεῦδος*). The words *ψεύδους* and *σχῆμα* are separated by the participle and the line break; *σχῆμ’* loses its final vowel before *εἰ*.
- *Κλείειν ἡμᾶς τὰ χείλη* ‘for us to close our lips’ is the infinitival construction dependent on *προσήκει*. *Κλείειν* is the present active infinitive of *κλείω*; accusative *ἡμᾶς* is its subject and *τὰ χείλη* (acc. neut. pl. of *χεῖλος*) its object. The inclusive ‘we’ replaces generic singular *l’uom*. Parenthetic *εἰ δυνατόν* ‘if possible’, with *ἐστί* understood, reads *finch’ ei puote* as a qualification of practicability rather than duration.
- *Ἵνα μὴ χωρὶς σφάλματος αἰσχυνθῶμεν* ‘lest we be put to shame without fault’ turns the Italian reason, *però che*, into a negative purpose clause. *Χωρίς* governs genitive singular *σφάλματος* (from *σφάλμα*); the first-person plural of *αἰσχυνθῶμεν* (aorist passive subjunctive of *αἰσχύνω*) continues *ἡμᾶς*. Where *fa vergogna* states what happens, the subjunctive after *ἵνα μή* states what one seeks to avoid.

> 127 ma qui tacer nol posso; e per le note  
> 128 di questa Commedìa, lettor, ti giuro,  
> 129 s' elle non sien di lunga grazia vote,  

but here keep silent I cannot; and, Reader, I swear to thee, by the notes of this my Comedy—so may they not be void of lasting favour—

> 127 Ἀλλ’ ὧδ’ οὐκ ἔχω σιγᾷν, καὶ νὴ τοὺς στίχους  
> (Yet here I cannot keep silent; and by the verses)
>
> 128 Τῆς κωμῳδίας ταύτης (εἴθ’ εὐμενείας  
> (Of this Comedy (may favour)
>
> 129 Μακρᾶς τύχοιεν), ἀναγνῶστ’, ὄμνυμί σοι,  
> (Long-enduring be theirs!), reader, I swear to you,)

- *Ἀλλ’ ὧδ’ οὐκ ἔχω σιγᾷν* closely follows *ma qui tacer nol posso*. Both *ἀλλά* and *ὧδε* are elided; *οὐκ* is the form of the negative before a smooth-breathed vowel. *Ἔχω* with an infinitive means ‘I am able to’; the infinitive of *σιγάω*, printed *σιγᾷν* (normally *σιγᾶν*), is intransitive here. Greek does not reproduce the object pronoun in *nol*.
- *Καὶ νὴ τοὺς στίχους τῆς κωμῳδίας ταύτης* ‘and by the verses of this Comedy’ uses the affirmative oath particle *νή* with the accusative. *Στίχους* makes *note* specifically verses; *τῆς κωμῳδίας* is their dependent genitive, and demonstrative *ταύτης* stands outside the article–noun group, the usual Greek position. The words sworn by precede the verb of swearing, as in Italian.
- *Εἴθ’ εὐμενείας μακρᾶς τύχοιεν* ‘may they obtain lasting favour’ changes the negative *s’elle non sien … vote* into a positive wish. *Εἴθ’* is elided *εἴθε*, introducing the optative without *ἄν*. *Τύχοιεν* is 3rd pl. second aorist active optative of *τυγχάνω*; as a non-indicative form it has no augment. Its understood subject is the verses, and the thing obtained stands in the genitive, *εὐμενείας μακρᾶς*.
- *Ἀναγνῶστ’, ὄμνυμί σοι* ‘reader, I swear to you’ postpones both the address and the verb until after the wish. *Ἀναγνῶστ’* is the elided vocative singular *ἀναγνῶστα* of *ἀναγνώστης*; *ὄμνυμι* is the 1st sg. present active indicative, with an extra acute before enclitic *σοι*. The dative corresponds to *ti*; the Italian subject *io* is expressed by the verb ending alone.

> 130 ch' io vidi per quell' aer grosso e scuro  
> 131 venir nuotando una figura in suso,  
> 132 meravigliosa ad ogni cor sicuro,  

that I saw, through that air gross and dark, come swimming upwards, a figure marvellous to every steadfast heart;

> 130 Ὡς διὰ τοῦ ζοφώδους πυκνοῦ τ’ ἀέρος  
> (That through the murky and thick air)
>
> 131 Εἶδον ἀνερχομένην καὶ νηχομένην  
> (I saw ascending and swimming)
>
> 132 Μορφὴν θαμβοῦσαν πᾶσαν ἔμπεδον φρένα,  
> (A figure able to amaze every steadfast heart,)

- *Ὡς* introduces the declaration dependent on *ὄμνυμι*, like *ch’* after *ti giuro*. *Διὰ τοῦ ζοφώδους πυκνοῦ τ’ ἀέρος* ‘through the murky and dense air’ makes the path explicit by *διά* with the genitive. *Ζοφώδους* (from *ζοφώδης*) and *πυκνοῦ* (from *πυκνός*) agree with *ἀέρος* (from *ἀήρ*); their order reverses *grosso e scuro*. Postpositive *τε*, elided after the second adjective before *ἀέρος*, couples the two. The article replaces *quell’*.
- *Εἶδον ἀνερχομένην καὶ νηχομένην μορφήν* ‘I saw a figure ascending and swimming’ postpones the perceived object, as Italian postpones *una figura*. *Εἶδον* is here 1st sg. second aorist active indicative of *ὁράω*. A verb of perception admits an accusative with supplementary participle: *ἀνερχομένην* and *νηχομένην* are present middle participles of *ἀνέρχομαι* and *νήχομαι*, both acc. fem. sg. agreeing with *μορφήν*. They present two simultaneous actions on an equal footing, replacing the infinitive and gerund of *venir nuotando*; the prefix *ἀν-* incorporates *in suso*.
- *Θαμβοῦσαν πᾶσαν ἔμπεδον φρένα* ‘amazing every steadfast mind’ turns the adjective *meravigliosa* into a transitive present active participle, again acc. fem. sg. with *μορφήν*. Hence the experiencer is a direct object, not the equivalent of Italian *ad* with its complement. *Φρένα* (acc. sg. of *φρήν*) renders *cor* as the seat of feeling or thought; *πᾶσαν* and the two-termination adjective *ἔμπεδον* are both feminine singular in agreement with it.

> 133 sì come torna colui che va giuso  
> 134 talora a solver ancora, ch' aggrappa  
> 135 o scoglio od altro che nel mare è chiuso,  
> 136 che in su si stende, e da piè si rattrappa.  

like as he returns, who on a time goes down to loose the anchor, which grapples a rock or other thing that in the sea is hid, who spreads the arms and gathers up the feet.

> 133 Οἷος ἀνέρχετ᾽ ἄνθρωπος, ὃς κατέβη  
> (Even as a man ascends who has gone down)
>
> 134 Ἄγκυράν ποτε λύσων ἐπισχεθεῖσαν  
> (To loose an anchor caught at some time)
>
> 135 Εἰς σκόπελον, εἴτ᾽ ἄλλως ἐν τῇ θαλάσσῃ,  
> (Upon a reef, or elsewhere in the sea,)
>
> 136 Χεῖρας ἐκτείνων καὶ πόδας ἐπανέλκων.  
> (Stretching out his arms and drawing up his feet.)

- *Οἷος ἀνέρχετ’ ἄνθρωπος* ‘as a man comes up’ renders *sì come torna colui*. Masculine nominative singular *οἷος* agrees with the supplied noun *ἄνθρωπος* and introduces the comparison. *Ἀνέρχετ’* is poetic elision of final *-αι* in *ἀνέρχεται* (3rd sg. present middle indicative of *ἀνέρχομαι*); it repeats the verb of *ἀνερχομένην*, specifying ascent where *torna* specifies return.
- *Ὃς κατέβη ἄγκυράν ποτε λύσων* ‘who went down once to loose an anchor’ keeps the relative clause but replaces present *va giuso* with *κατέβη*, 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *καταβαίνω*, with the augment after the prefix. Enclitic *ποτε* renders *talora* and accounts for the additional acute on *ἄγκυράν*. *Λύσων* (future active participle of *λύω*, nom. masc. sg.) agrees with *ὅς*: the future participle after a verb of motion expresses the purpose conveyed by *a solver*. Its object *ἄγκυραν* stands before it.
- *Ἐπισχεθεῖσαν εἰς σκόπελον* ‘caught on a reef’ reverses the construction of *ch’ aggrappa … scoglio*: the anchor no longer actively grips something but is itself held fast. *Ἐπισχεθεῖσαν* is an aorist passive participle of *ἐπέχω*, acc. fem. sg. agreeing with *ἄγκυραν*. *Εἰς* with accusative *σκόπελον* expresses the point against which it has caught, not a direct object of gripping.
- *Εἴτ’ ἄλλως ἐν τῇ θαλάσσῃ* ‘or otherwise in the sea’ gives the alternative adverbially. *Εἴτ’* is elided *εἴτε*; *ἄλλως* expresses no second object corresponding to *altro*. Static *ἐν* with dative singular *τῇ θαλάσσῃ* retains the location, but there is no relative clause or verb corresponding to *che … è chiuso*.
- *Χεῖρας ἐκτείνων καὶ πόδας ἐπανέλκων* ‘extending his arms and drawing his feet back up’ replaces the final Italian relative clause with two circumstantial present active participles. *Ἐκτείνων* (from *ἐκτείνω*) and *ἐπανέλκων* (from *ἐπανέλκω*) are nom. masc. sg., returning to *ἄνθρωπος* and describing what he does as he comes up. Each has its accusative plural object first: *χεῖρας* (from *χείρ*) supplies the limbs not named in *in su si stende*; *πόδας* (from *πούς*) turns *da piè* into the thing drawn up. Both Italian reflexives thus become transitive active expressions.

## Summary — Recasting the Clause

Musurus often keeps Dante’s order of emphasis while changing the grammar that sustains it. At 52–54 the contrast *οὐχ ὕβριν, ἀλλ᾽ ἄχθος μέγα* (‘not affront, but great grief’) comes first, as *non dispetto, ma doglia* does; the subject is again delayed beyond the verb. Yet *ἐχάραξε* (‘engraved’) gives *fisse* a more specific image, which *ἀνεξαλείπτως* (‘indelibly’) carries further: where Dante’s result clause allows the impression to fade slowly, the Greek adverb denies that it can be effaced. Elsewhere the correspondence is closer. The repeated *αὐτίκα* answers the repeated *tosto* at 121–123, and at 127–129 the oath with *νή* and the accusative places the verses sworn by before the verb of swearing, just as the Italian does.

The participle is Musurus’ characteristic means of recasting a clause. An articular participle can replace a relative, as *ὁ δοκῶν* (‘the one who seems’) does for *che … sembri*; a circumstantial aorist participle such as *λύσας* (‘having untied’) can carry the priority expressed by *poscia che* and a finite verb. At 25–27 a genitive absolute turns the result introduced by *sì che* into simultaneous motion: *συγκινουμένων* gives feet and necks a common verb while *ἀπεναντίως* (‘in opposite directions’) distinguishes their courses. At 130–132, by contrast, the two agreeing participles after *εἶδον* (‘I saw’) put ascent and swimming on an equal footing where *venir nuotando* makes swimming the manner of ascent.

Particles and moods often preserve the force of an Italian construction without reproducing its form. Postpositive *δέ* carries a transition, while *τε* can link a second term without disturbing the prominence of its first word. In the unreal conditions, a past indicative with *ἄν* regularly answers an Italian conditional; the repeated *εἴθε* with the optative at 64–66 makes the wishes implicit in *se … e se* independent and explicit. But a change of construction can also change the claim. At 15, *εἰ βούλει* (‘if you wish’) makes courtesy depend on the addressee’s inclination rather than presenting it, as *si vuole* does, as what the occasion requires.

Some of the sharpest departures turn on case and verbal government rather than vocabulary. At 28–30 the accusative *οἰκτράν τε θέαν* (‘and a pitiable appearance’) puts appearance among the things held in contempt, not among the causes of contempt. At 115–117 the echo of *novità* and *nuovo* survives in *νέον* and *νέα*, but the relation is reversed: *νέον τι* (‘something new’) is what the intent portends, not what must answer the signal. Musurus can thus preserve a conspicuous verbal echo even where his syntax gives the passage a different reading.
