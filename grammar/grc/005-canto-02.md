# Inferno 2

## Lines 1–24 — The Journey and the Poet’s Doubt

> 1 Lo giorno se n’ andava, e l’ aer bruno  
> 2 toglieva gli animai, che sono in terra,  
> 3 dalle fatiche loro; ed io sol uno  

The day was departing, and the brown air taking the animals, that are on earth, from their toils; and I, one alone,

> 1 Ἡ μὲν ἡμέρα παρήρχετο, καὶ πάντα  
> (The day was passing away, and all)
>
> 2 Ὁ μελανώδης ἀὴρ τἀπὶ γῆς ζῶντα  
> (The living things upon the earth the dusky air)
>
> 3 Τῶν πόνων ἀπήλαυνεν· ἐγὼ δὲ μόνος  
> (Was withdrawing from their toils; while I alone)

- *Ἡ μὲν ἡμέρα παρήρχετο* ‘the day, for its part, was passing’ renders *Lo giorno se n’ andava*. *Παρήρχετο* is the augmented 3rd sg. imperfect middle indicative of *παρέρχομαι*: the imperfect preserves the continuing action, while the compound verb needs no equivalent of the Italian pronominal *se n’*. Postpositive *μέν* anticipates *δέ* in *ἐγὼ δὲ μόνος* ‘but I alone’; the Greek thus marks a contrast where the Italian twice has *e*.
- *Πάντα … τἀπὶ γῆς ζῶντα* ‘all the things living on earth’ is the accusative object, interrupted by the subject *ὁ μελανώδης ἀήρ* ‘the dark-hued air’. Musurus adds *πάντα* and replaces the noun and relative clause *gli animai, che sono in terra* with the substantivized present active participle *ζῶντα* (acc. neut. pl. of *ζάω*). *Τἀπὶ* represents *τὰ ἐπί* in crasis: the locative phrase *ἐπὶ γῆς* stands between article and participle.
- *Τῶν πόνων ἀπήλαυνεν* ‘was driving [them] away from toils’ renders *toglieva … dalle fatiche loro*. *Ἀπήλαυνεν* (3rd sg. imperfect active indicative of *ἀπελαύνω*, with the augment after the preverb) is stronger than *toglieva*; the bare genitive marks separation, and *loro* is left implicit. In *ἐγὼ δὲ μόνος*, *μόνος* is predicative, nominative masculine singular with the emphatic subject pronoun; it does the work of both *sol* and *uno*.

> 4 m’ apparecchiava a sostener la guerra  
> 5 sì del cammino, e sì della pietate,  
> 6 che ritrarrà la mente, che non erra.  

was preparing myself to bear the war both of the journey and the pity, which memory, that errs not, shall relate.

> 4 Ἡτοιμαζόμην ὑποστῆναι τὸ βάρος  
> (Was preparing to endure the burden)
>
> 5 Τῆς τε πορείας καὶ τοῦ ψυχικοῦ πάθους,  
> (Both of the journey and of the soul's suffering,)
>
> 6 Ὃ νῦν ἡ πιστὴ μνήμη μου περιγράψει.  
> (Which now my faithful memory shall describe.)

- *Ἡτοιμαζόμην ὑποστῆναι τὸ βάρος* ‘I was preparing myself to endure the burden’ retains the construction of *m’ apparecchiava a sostener* but changes the image of *la guerra*. *Ἡτοιμαζόμην* is the 1st sg. imperfect middle of *ἑτοιμάζω*, with temporal augment and reflexive force. Its complementary infinitive *ὑποστῆναι* is the root-aorist active of *ὑφίστημι*: here the sense is ‘stand up to’, with accusative *τὸ βάρος*, not the causative ‘set beneath’.
- *Τῆς τε πορείας καὶ τοῦ ψυχικοῦ πάθους* gives two genitives dependent on *βάρος*. Enclitic *τε* after the first article and *καί* before the second reproduce the correlation *sì … e sì*. *Ψυχικοῦ πάθους* ‘of inward suffering’ interprets *della pietate* as something suffered in the soul rather than naming pity directly.
- *Ὃ νῦν ἡ πιστὴ μνήμη μου περιγράψει* ‘which my faithful memory will now describe’ makes the relative *ὅ* accusative neuter singular, naturally resuming *βάρος* as object of *περιγράψει* (3rd sg. future active indicative of *περιγράφω*). *Μνήμη* specifies the commemorative faculty of *mente*; attributive *πιστή* ‘trustworthy’ replaces the relative negation *che non erra*. Both *νῦν* and possessive *μου* are supplied.

> 7 O Muse, o alto ingegno, or m’ aiutate!  

O Muses, o high Genius, now help me!

> 7 Ὦ Μοῦσαι, πνεῦμα θεῖον, νῦν βοηθεῖτε·  
> (O Muses, O divine spirit, now give aid;)

- *Ὦ Μοῦσαι, πνεῦμα θεῖον* has two vocatives but only one *ὦ*, against the repeated *O*. The neuter singular *πνεῦμα* (vocative like the nominative) with *θεῖον* gives ‘divine spirit’ for *alto ingegno*, changing both noun and epithet. *Νῦν βοηθεῖτε* preserves *or* and the plural appeal: *βοηθεῖτε* is the contracted 2nd pl. present active imperative of *βοηθέω*. The person helped, explicit in *m’*, is understood rather than expressed by a dative.

> 8 O mente, che scrivesti ciò ch’ io vidi,  
> 9 qui si parrà la tua nobilitate.  

O Memory, that hast inscribed what I saw, here will be shewn thy nobleness.

> 8 Ὦ μνήμη τ᾽, ἐν ᾗ γέγραπται πάνθ᾽ ὅσ᾽ εἶδον,  
> (And O memory, in which is written all that I saw,)
>
> 9 Νῦν ἡ σὴ φανήσεται σεμνηγορία.  
> (Now shall your noble eloquence be shown.)

- *Ὦ μνήμη τ᾽* ‘and O memory’ links this vocative to the preceding ones by postpositive *τε*, elided before *ἐν*. *Ἐν ᾗ γέγραπται* ‘in which there stands written’ turns the active address *che scrivesti* into a locative relative clause: *ᾗ* is dat. fem. sg. with *ἐν*, and *γέγραπται* is the reduplicated 3rd sg. perfect middle-passive indicative of *γράφω*, passive in sense. Memory is the place of inscription, not the writer; the perfect stresses the resulting record.
- *Πάνθ᾽ ὅσ᾽ εἶδον* ‘all that I saw’ expands *ciò ch’ io vidi*. *Πάντα* is the neuter plural subject of singular *γέγραπται*; *ὅσα* is the neuter plural accusative object of *εἶδον* (here 1st sg., the suppletive second aorist of *ὁράω*). Both finals are elided; in *πάνθ᾽* the remaining *τ* is aspirated before the rough breathing of *ὅσα*.
- *Νῦν ἡ σὴ φανήσεται σεμνηγορία* substitutes temporal *νῦν* for local *qui*. *Φανήσεται*, 3rd sg. future passive indicative of *φαίνω*, has the intransitive sense ‘will become manifest’. It separates attributive *ἡ σή* from its noun *σεμνηγορία*: what will appear is specifically ‘dignity of utterance’, a narrower, more verbal quality than *nobilitate*.

> 10 Io cominciai: “Poeta che mi guidi,  

I began: "Poet, who guidest me,

> 10 „Ὁδηγέ μου ποιητά,“ λέγειν ἠρξάμην,  
> („Poet, my guide,“ I began to say,)

- *Ὁδηγέ μου ποιητά* ‘my guide, poet’ puts the address before the reporting clause and turns *che mi guidi* into a second noun in apposition. *Ὁδηγέ* and *ποιητά* are vocative singulars of *ὁδηγός* and *ποιητής*; enclitic possessive *μου* replaces the object *mi*.
- *Λέγειν ἠρξάμην* ‘I began to speak’ supplies a present active infinitive where *Io cominciai* leads straight into the speech. *Ἠρξάμην* is the 1st sg. aorist middle indicative of *ἄρχω*, with temporal augment; the middle *ἄρχομαι* means ‘begin’, and the personal ending suffices for *Io*.

> 11 guarda la mia virtù, s’ ella è possente,  
> 12 prima che all’ alto passo tu mi fidi.  

look if there be worth in me sufficient, before thou trust me to the arduous passage.

> 11 „Ἴδε μου τὴν ἀξίαν εἴποτ᾽ ἀντέχει,  
> („See whether my worth is strong enough,)
>
> 12 „Πρὶν εἰς τὴν δεινὴν προχωρήσω πορείαν.  
> („Before I proceed upon the dread journey.)

- *Ἴδε μου τὴν ἀξίαν* ‘look at my worth’ follows the imperative and direct object of *guarda la mia virtù*. *Ἴδε* is the 2nd sg. second-aorist active imperative corresponding to *εἶδον*; enclitic *μου* belongs with accusative *τὴν ἀξίαν*. *Ἀξία* emphasizes worth or fitness rather than strength.
- *Εἴποτ᾽ ἀντέχει* ‘whether it can hold out’ retains *s’ ella* as an indirect question but omits the subject pronoun. *Εἴποτ᾽* is *εἴ ποτε*, with the final vowel elided; *ποτε* adds the nuance ‘at all’. *Ἀντέχει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *ἀντέχω*) replaces the copula and adjective *è possente* with a verb of endurance.
- *Πρὶν εἰς τὴν δεινὴν προχωρήσω πορείαν* ‘before I advance upon the fearful journey’ changes the subject: instead of the guide entrusting *me*, the speaker himself advances. *Εἰς* governs accusative *πορείαν*, separated from *τὴν δεινήν* by the verb. *Προχωρήσω* (*προχωρέω*) is formally either 1st sg. future indicative or aorist active subjunctive; the prospective *πρίν* clause suggests the subjunctive. The finite construction follows Italian *prima che* more closely than the infinitive usual with *πρίν* after an affirmative main clause.

> 13 Tu dici, che di Silvio lo parente,  
> 14 corruttibile ancora, ad immortale  
> 15 secolo andò, e fu sensibilmente.  

Thou sayest that the father of Sylvius, while subject to corruption, went to the immortal world, and was there in body.

> 13 „Σὺ λέγεις ἐλθεῖν τὸν τοῦ Σιλβίου πάππον,  
> („You say that the grandfather of Sylvius went,)
>
> 14 „Ἔτ᾽ ὄντα θνητόν, εἰς τὴν τῶν ἀθανάτων  
> („While still mortal, into the immortals')
>
> 15 „ Χώραν ἔχοντα ζῶντος ἀνδρὸς αἰσθήσεις.  
> („ Realm, having the senses of a living man.)

- *Σὺ λέγεις ἐλθεῖν* keeps explicit *Tu* but turns *dici, che … andò* into accusative and infinitive after a verb of saying. *Ἐλθεῖν* is the second-aorist active infinitive of *ἔρχομαι*, anterior in this indirect statement. Its subject *τὸν τοῦ Σιλβίου πάππον* encloses the dependent genitive between article and noun. *Πάππον* means ‘grandfather’, not the “father” of the English rendering of *parente*.
- *Ἔτ᾽ ὄντα θνητόν* ‘being still mortal’ makes *corruttibile ancora* a circumstantial participial phrase. *Ἔτ᾽* is elided *ἔτι*; *ὄντα* (present active participle of *εἰμί*) and its predicate *θνητόν* agree in the masculine accusative singular with *πάππον*. Mortality replaces susceptibility to corruption.
- *Εἰς τὴν τῶν ἀθανάτων χώραν* ‘into the land of the immortals’ recasts *ad immortale secolo*: the adjective becomes a substantivized genitive plural, and the destination a *χώρα*. *Ἔχοντα ζῶντος ἀνδρὸς αἰσθήσεις* ‘having a living man’s senses’ replaces the coordinated *e fu sensibilmente* with another concomitant present participle, *ἔχοντα* (acc. masc. sg. of *ἔχω*). Its object is *αἰσθήσεις* (acc. pl. of *αἴσθησις*); *ζῶντος* (gen. masc. sg. of *ζάω*) modifies possessive *ἀνδρός*. Musurus interprets the adverb through the exercise of the senses, not simply bodily presence.

> 16 Però se l’ avversario d’ ogni male  
> 17 cortese i fu, pensando l’ alto effetto,  
> 18 che uscir dovea di lui, ed il chi, ed il quale,  

But if the Adversary of all evil was propitious [to him], considering the high effect, and who and what should come from him,

> 16 „ Ἀλλ᾽ εἴπερ ὁ κακίας ἐχθρὸς ἁπάσης  
> („ Yet if the adversary of all wickedness)
>
> 17 „ Εὔνους ἦν αὐτῷ, φρονῶν καλὰ μεγάλα  
> („ Was gracious to him, having in mind the great benefits)
>
> 18 „ Ἐκβησόμεν᾽ ἐξ αὐτοῦ, ποίου καὶ ποῖα,  
> („ To issue from him, both who and what they were,)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ εἴπερ* ‘but if indeed’ renders *Però se* adversatively and reinforces the condition with *-περ*. *Ἀλλ᾽* is elided *ἀλλά*. In *ὁ κακίας ἐχθρὸς ἁπάσης*, substantival *ἐχθρός* ‘enemy’ governs genitive *κακίας*; agreeing *ἁπάσης* is postponed across *ἐχθρός*. The abstract singular ‘all wickedness’ renders *ogni male*.
- *Εὔνους ἦν αὐτῷ* ‘was well-disposed towards him’ preserves the predicative construction of *cortese i fu*. *Εὔνους* is the contracted nominative masculine singular adjective; *αὐτῷ* is the dative of the person favoured, corresponding to *i*. *Ἦν* (3rd sg. imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*) presents a state in the protasis, not a hypothetical optative.
- *Φρονῶν καλὰ μεγάλα* ‘having great good things in mind’ keeps the attendant action of *pensando* in *φρονῶν* (nom. masc. sg. present active participle of *φρονέω*, agreeing with *ἐχθρός*). The singular *l’ alto effetto* becomes a neuter plural object, qualified as good as well as great.
- *Ἐκβησόμεν᾽ ἐξ αὐτοῦ* ‘to issue from him’ replaces *che uscir dovea di lui* with the future middle participle of *ἐκβαίνω*, active in meaning (*ἐκβησόμενα*, acc. neut. pl., elided). It agrees with *καλὰ μεγάλα* and marks prospect without a separate verb of necessity. The compressed *ποίου καὶ ποῖα* echoes *ed il chi, ed il quale* but does not reproduce two Italian substantives: *ποίου* is gen. sg., suggesting ‘from one of what sort’, while *ποῖα* is neut. pl., looking back to the things to issue. The Greek asks about quality in both forms, leaving their syntactic connections elliptical.

> 19 non pare indegno ad uomo d’ intelletto:  

it seems not unfitting to an understanding mind:

> 19 „ Οὐ δοκεῖ τοῦτ᾽ ἀπρεπὲς ἀνδρὶ φρονίμῳ.  
> („ This does not seem unseemly to a prudent man.)

- *Οὐ δοκεῖ τοῦτ᾽ ἀπρεπὲς ἀνδρὶ φρονίμῳ* supplies the conclusion to *εἴπερ … ἦν*. *Τοῦτ᾽* (elided *τοῦτο*, nom. neut. sg.) makes explicit the subject left implicit in *non pare indegno*; *ἀπρεπές* is its predicate with *δοκεῖ* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *δοκέω*). *Οὐ* negates the assertion, preserving the Italian understatement. *Ἀνδρὶ φρονίμῳ* ‘to a discerning man’ is a dative of judgement; the agreeing adjective replaces *d’ intelletto*.

> 20 ch’ ei fu dell’ alma Roma e di suo impero  
> 21 nell’ empireo ciel per padre eletto;  

for in the empyreal heaven, he was chosen to be the father of generous Rome, and of her Empire;

> 20 „ Αὐτὸς γὰρ Ῥώμης τῆς σεμνῆς καὶ τοῦ Κράτους  
> („ For he of august Rome and of the Empire)
>
> 21 „ Ἡρέθη πατὴρ ἐν Οὐρανῶν αἰθέρι·  
> („ Was chosen father in the ether of the Heavens;)

- *Αὐτὸς γάρ* ‘for he himself’ makes *ch’ ei* explicitly explanatory with postpositive *γάρ*. *Ῥώμης τῆς σεμνῆς καὶ τοῦ Κράτους* are genitives dependent on the delayed *πατήρ*. The articular epithet after the proper name gives ‘august Rome’ for *alma Roma*; *τοῦ Κράτους* ‘of the dominion’ leaves *suo* unexpressed.
- *Ἡρέθη πατήρ* ‘was chosen father’ renders *fu … per padre eletto* with a predicate nominative, without preposition or infinitive. *Ἡρέθη* is the 3rd sg. aorist passive indicative of *αἱρέω* (usually printed *ᾑρέθη* in Attic). *Ἐν Οὐρανῶν αἰθέρι* ‘in the ether of the heavens’ follows the verb instead of preceding it: *ἐν* governs *αἰθέρι* (dat. sg. of *αἰθήρ*), and *Οὐρανῶν* is a dependent genitive plural. Thus *empireo* is not translated by an adjective.

> 22 la quale, e il quale, a voler dir lo vero,  
> 23 fur stabiliti per lo loco santo,  
> 24 u’ siede il successor del maggior Piero.  

both these, to say the truth, were established for the holy place, where the Successor of [the greatest] Peter sits.

> 22 „ Ῥώμη δὲ καὶ τὸ Κράτος, ἵν᾽ ὀρθῶς εἴπω,  
> („ And Rome and the Empire, to speak aright,)
>
> 23 „ Προωρίσθησαν ὡς ἱερός τις τόπος,  
> („ Were foreordained as a holy place,)
>
> 24 „ Ἔνθ᾽ ὁ τοῦ Πέτρου διάδοχος ἑδρεύει.  
> („ Where the successor of Peter has his seat.)

- *Ῥώμη δὲ καὶ τὸ Κράτος* repeats the two nouns as nominative subjects rather than continuing with the gendered relatives *la quale, e il quale*. Postpositive *δέ* marks the transition. Parenthetic *ἵν᾽ ὀρθῶς εἴπω* ‘that I may speak correctly’ turns *a voler dir lo vero* into a purpose clause: elided *ἵνα* governs *εἴπω* (1st sg. second-aorist active subjunctive corresponding to *λέγω*). The adverb *ὀρθῶς* replaces the object *lo vero*, with no separate equivalent of *voler*.
- *Προωρίσθησαν* (3rd pl. aorist passive indicative of *προορίζω*) agrees with the coordinated subjects; the preverb adds the notion of prior determination to *fur stabiliti*. *Ὡς ἱερός τις τόπος* is predicative, ‘as a certain holy place’: singular *τόπος* characterizes the two together, and enclitic indefinite *τις* replaces the definiteness of *lo*. In particular, *ὡς* makes the place what they were designated as, rather than what they were established for.
- *Ἔνθ᾽* is local relative *ἔνθα* ‘where’, corresponding to *u’*, elided before *ὁ*. *Ὁ τοῦ Πέτρου διάδοχος* puts the genitive inside the article–noun group and leaves *maggior* untranslated. *Ἑδρεύει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *ἑδρεύω*) gives ‘has his seat’ for *siede*; unlike the Italian verb, it follows its full subject.

## Lines 25–57 — Doubt, Rebuke, and a Lady’s Summons

> 25 Per questa andata, onde gli dai tu vanto,  
> 26 intese cose che furon cagione  
> 27 di sua vittoria e del papale ammanto.  

By this journey, for which thou honourest him, he learned things that were the causes of his victory, and of the Papal Mantle.

> 25 „ Ἐκ τῆς πορείας, ἣν ἐξύμνησας, ταύτης  
> („ From this journey, which you celebrated in song,)
>
> 26 „ Προκατέμαθεν ὅσ᾽ ὑπῆρξεν αἰτία  
> („ He learned beforehand all that was the cause)
>
> 27 „ Τῆς νίκης αὐτοῦ καὶ τῆς Παπικῆς χλαίνης.  
> („ Of his victory and of the Papal mantle.)

- *Ἐκ τῆς πορείας … ταύτης* ‘from this journey’ makes *Per questa andata* the source of the knowledge. *Ταύτης* (gen. sg. fem. of *οὗτος*) stands outside the article–noun group, as is usual for a demonstrative, but is separated from it by the whole relative clause.
- *ἣν ἐξύμνησας* ‘which you celebrated in song’ changes the object of the praise: *ἣν* (acc. sg. fem.) refers to the journey itself, whereas in *onde gli dai tu vanto* the journey is the occasion for honouring *him*. *Ἐξύμνησας* (2nd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐξυμνέω*, with the augment after the prefix) also exchanges the Italian present for a completed act.
- *Προκατέμαθεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *προκαταμανθάνω*) renders *intese* with the added sense ‘learned beforehand’. *Ὅσ᾽*, elided *ὅσα*, incorporates the antecedent of *cose che*: ‘all the things that’. The relative is neuter plural, subject of the singular *ὑπῆρξεν* (aor. act. ind. of *ὑπάρχω*), regular Greek agreement. *Αἰτία* is a singular predicate nominative, as *cagione* is singular.
- *Τῆς νίκης αὐτοῦ καὶ τῆς Παπικῆς χλαίνης* retains the two dependent genitives. The possessive *αὐτοῦ* follows *νίκης*, whereas the adjective *Παπικῆς* stands between article and noun; *χλαίνη* is a cloak or mantle.

> 28 Andovvi poi lo Vas d' elezione,  
> 29 per recarne conforto a quella fede,  
> 30 ch'è principio alla via di salvazione.  

Afterwards, the Chosen Vessel went thither, to bring confirmation of that Faith which is the entrance to the way of salvation.

> 28 „ Ἦλθεν εἶτ᾽ ἐκεῖ τῆς Ἐκλογῆς τὸ σκεῦος,  
> („ Thither came afterwards the vessel of Election,)
>
> 29 „ Ὅπως ἐνδυναμώσῃ τὴν πίστιν κείνην,  
> („ To strengthen that faith,)
>
> 30 „ Ἥτις ἔστ᾽ ἀρχὴ τῆς σωτηρίου τρίβου.  
> („ Which is the beginning of the saving path.)

- *Ἦλθεν εἶτ᾽ ἐκεῖ* ‘came then there’ unpacks *Andovvi poi*: *ἦλθεν* is the 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*, *εἶτ᾽* is elided *εἶτα*, and the adverb *ἐκεῖ* supplies the Italian locative clitic. The postponed subject *τῆς Ἐκλογῆς τὸ σκεῦος* keeps the noun and dependent genitive of *lo Vas d’elezione*, reversing their order: ‘of Election the vessel’.
- *Ὅπως ἐνδυναμώσῃ* ‘in order that he might strengthen’ replaces *per recarne conforto* with a finite purpose clause. *Ἐνδυναμώσῃ* is 3rd sg. aor. act. subj. of *ἐνδυναμόω*; Greek retains the subjunctive after a past main verb rather than shifting to an optative. *Τὴν πίστιν κείνην* is now the direct object, not the recipient of *conforto*; there is no separate equivalent of *ne*. *Κείνην* is the short, poetic form of *ἐκείνην*, in demonstrative position after the articular noun.
- *Ἥτις* (nom. sg. fem. of *ὅστις*) resumes *πίστιν* in a characterizing relative clause. *Ἔστ᾽* is elided *ἐστί*; *ἀρχή* ‘beginning’ is its predicate. *Τῆς σωτηρίου τρίβου* ‘of the saving path’ depends directly on *ἀρχή*: the adjective *σωτηρίου* (gen. sg. fem. of the two-termination *σωτήριος*) replaces *di salvazione*.

> 31 Ma io, perchè venirvi? o chi 'l concede?  

But I, why go? or who permits it?

> 31 „ Ἀλλ᾽ ἐγὼ πῶς ὑπάγω; τίς τ᾽ ἐπιτρέπει;  
> („ But I, how do I go? and who permits it?)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ ἐγὼ πῶς ὑπάγω;* ‘but I, how do I go?’ keeps the emphatic pronoun after elided *ἀλλά*, but substitutes *πῶς* ‘how?’ for *perchè* and a finite present (1st sg. act. ind. of *ὑπάγω*) for the deliberative infinitive *venirvi*. The direction expressed by *vi* is left implicit.
- *Τίς τ᾽ ἐπιτρέπει;* ‘and who permits?’ uses elided enclitic *τε*, connective rather than disjunctive like *o*. The present *ἐπιτρέπει* has no expressed object corresponding to *’l*.

> 32 Io non Enea, io non Paolo sono;  
> 33 me degno a ciò nè io nè altri 'l crede.  

I am not Æneas, am not Paul; neither myself nor others deem me worthy of it.

> 32 „ Οὔκ εἰμι γὰρ Αἰνείας, οὐδέ γε Παῦλος·  
> („ For I am not Æneas, nor indeed am I Paul;)
>
> 33 „ Οὔτ᾽ ἄλλος ἔμ᾽ ἄξιον, οὔτ᾽ ἐγὼ κρίνω.  
> („ Neither another, nor I myself, judges me worthy.)

- *Οὔκ εἰμι γὰρ Αἰνείας, οὐδέ γε Παῦλος* supplies the explanatory postpositive *γάρ*. One initial copula serves both predicate nominatives, instead of the repeated *io* and final *sono*. *Οὔκ* has the form used before a smooth-breathed vowel and bears an accent before enclitic *εἰμι*; *γε* reinforces the second denial, ‘nor indeed Paul’.
- *Οὔτ᾽ ἄλλος ἔμ᾽ ἄξιον, οὔτ᾽ ἐγὼ κρίνω* reverses *nè io nè altri*: ‘neither does another [judge] me worthy, nor do I’. Both occurrences of *οὔτε* and the oblique pronoun *ἐμέ* are elided. *Ἔμ᾽* is the object and *ἄξιον* its predicate accusative; *κρίνω* ‘judge’ is 1st sg. with the nearer *ἐγώ*, leaving the corresponding third-person verb understood with *ἄλλος*. The qualification *a ciò* is not expressed.

> 34 Per che se del venire io m' abbandono,  
> 35 temo che la venuta non sia folle;  

Wherefore, if I resign myself to go, I fear my going may prove foolish;

> 34 „ Ἐὰν οὖν ἐλθεῖν ἐγὼ συγκατανεύσω,  
> („ If therefore I consent to come,)
>
> 35 „Φοβοῦμαι μήπως μωρὸν ἔργον τολμήσω.  
> („I fear lest I dare a foolish deed.)

- *Ἐὰν οὖν … συγκατανεύσω* ‘if, then, I consent’ renders inferential *Per che* by postpositive *οὖν* and *se … io m’abbandono* by a prospective condition. After *ἐάν*, *συγκατανεύσω* is 1st sg. aor. act. subj. of *συγκατανεύω*, not future indicative despite the identical form. The prefixed verb means ‘assent’ and needs no reflexive object.
- *Ἐλθεῖν* (second aor. act. inf. of *ἔρχομαι*) is fronted as the complement of ‘consent’. In the main clause *φοβοῦμαι μήπως … τολμήσω* ‘I fear lest I venture’ uses present middle *φοβοῦμαι* and *μήπως* with the aorist subjunctive of *τολμάω*. Italian *non* after *temo* is not an independent negation here. *Μωρὸν ἔργον* ‘a foolish deed’ becomes the accusative object of ‘venture’: the speaker, not *la venuta*, is the subject of the feared action.

> 36 se' savio, intendi me' ch' io non ragiono.”  

thou art wise, and understandest better than I speak.”

> 36 „Σοφὸς εἶ καὶ διορᾷς, εἰ καλῶς λέγω.”  
> („You are wise and discern whether I speak well.”)

- *Σοφὸς εἶ* places the predicate adjective first; *εἶ* is 2nd sg. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*. *Καὶ διορᾷς* explicitly coordinates the second assertion; *διορᾷς* (2nd sg. contracted pres. act. ind. of *διοράω*) suggests discerning clearly.
- *Εἰ καλῶς λέγω* ‘whether I speak well’ is an indirect yes-or-no question dependent on *διορᾷς*. This is a change of sense, not simply a Greek comparative idiom: *me’ ch’ io non ragiono* compares the hearer’s understanding with the speaker’s expression; *καλῶς* qualifies the speaking alone.

> 37 E quale è quei, che disvuol ciò che volle,  
> 38 e per novi pensier cangia proposta,  
> 39 sì che dal cominciar tutto si tolle,  

And as one who unwills what he willed, and with new thoughts changes his purpose, so that he wholly quits the thing commenced,

> 37 Ὡς δ’ εἴ τις ὅπερ ἤθελ’ οὐκέτι θέλει,  
> (And as one who no longer wills what he willed,)
>
> 38 Καὶ διὰ νέους σκοποὺς ἀλλάσσων φρένα  
> (And changing his mind through new purposes)
>
> 39 Τὴν πρᾶξιν, ἧς ἤρξατο, καταλιμπάνει,  
> (Abandons the undertaking he had begun,)

- *Ὡς δ’ εἴ τις* ‘and as if someone’ introduces a hypothetical comparison in place of *quale è quei, che*. *Δ’* is elided postpositive *δέ*; enclitic *τις* supplies the indefinite subject. *Ὅπερ* (neut. sg. acc., relative *ὅ* strengthened by *-περ*) is the object of *ἤθελ’*, elided *ἤθελε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἐθέλω*); the whole relative is the object of *θέλει*. *Οὐκέτι θέλει* ‘no longer wills’ answers *disvuol* without a corresponding prefixed verb.
- *Διὰ νέους σκοπούς* uses *διά* with the accusative of cause: *novi pensier* become ‘new aims’. *Ἀλλάσσων φρένα* ‘changing his mind’ is a pres. act. participle of *ἀλλάσσω*, nom. sg. masc. agreeing with *τις*, with poetic *φρένα* (acc. sg. of *φρήν*) as object. The Italian finite *cangia* thus becomes attendant circumstance.
- *Τὴν πρᾶξιν … καταλιμπάνει* ‘he abandons the undertaking’ becomes the finite culmination, without a separate result connective for *sì che* or intensifier for *tutto*. *Καταλιμπάνει* is 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *καταλιμπάνω*, an expanded present beside *καταλείπω*. In *ἧς ἤρξατο* ‘which he began’, the feminine relative is genitive because middle *ἄρχομαι* takes a genitive; *ἤρξατο* is 3rd sg. aor. mid. ind.

> 40 tal mi fec' io in quella oscura costa:  

such I made myself on that dim coast:

> 40 Τοιοῦτος ἤμην ἐν τῇ ζοφώδει ῥάχει·  
> (Such was I upon that gloomy ridge;)

- *Τοιοῦτος* ‘such’ answers *ὡς … τις* as *tal* answers *quale*. *Ἤμην* ‘I was’ (1st sg. imperf. of *εἰμί*, a later form in place of Attic *ἦ*) turns reflexive *mi fec’ io* into a description of state. *Ἐν τῇ ζοφώδει ῥάχει* is static *ἐν* with the dative, ‘on the gloomy ridge’; *ζοφώδει* is dat. sg. of *ζοφώδης*. The article replaces *quella* without its demonstrative force.

> 41 perchè pensando consumai la impresa,  
> 42 che fu nel cominciar cotanto tosta.  

for with thinking I wasted the enterprise, that had been so quick in its commencement.

> 41 Σκεφθεὶς γὰρ ἀπετράπην οὗπερ ἠρξάμην  
> (For on reflecting I turned away from that which I had begun,)
>
> 42 Ἐγχειρήματος τοσαύτῃ προθυμίᾳ.  
> (The enterprise, with such eagerness.)

- *Σκεφθεὶς γάρ* ‘for, having reflected’ makes *pensando* anterior rather than ongoing. *Σκεφθείς* is nom. sg. masc. of the aorist passive-form participle of *σκέπτομαι*, active in sense. *Ἀπετράπην* (1st sg. second aor. pass. ind. of *ἀποτρέπω*) means ‘I turned aside’: instead of *consumai* acting on the enterprise, the speaker turns away from it, hence the genitive *ἐγχειρήματος*.
- *Οὗπερ ἠρξάμην ἐγχειρήματος* interlaces the relative and its postponed noun. *Οὗπερ* is the emphatic gen. sg. neut. relative; as in line 39, middle ‘begin’ governs its genitive, here with *ἠρξάμην* in the first person. *Τοσαύτῃ προθυμίᾳ* is a dative of manner, ‘with such eagerness’: *che fu … cotanto tosta* becomes a statement about how the speaker began, not a predication about the enterprise.

> 43 “Se io ho ben la tua parola intesa,”  
> 44 rispose del magnanimo quell' ombra,  
> 45 “l' anima tua è da viltade offesa,  

“If I have rightly understood thy words,” replied that shade of the Magnanimous, “thy soul is smit with coward fear,

> 43 “Εἰ καλῶς ἔγνων τὰ παρὰ σοῦ ῥηθέντα,”  
> (“If I have rightly understood what you have spoken,”)
>
> 44 Ἀπήντησεν ἡ σκιὰ τοῦ μεγαθύμου,  
> (Replied the shade of the great-souled one,)
>
> 45 “Τὴν σὴν ἀπηχρείωσε ψυχὴν ὁ φόβος,  
> (“Fear has rendered your soul worthless,)

- *Εἰ καλῶς ἔγνων* is *εἰ* with the indicative, presenting the understanding as a condition to be tested. *Ἔγνων* is the 1st sg. root aor. act. ind. of *γιγνώσκω*, replacing the Italian compound perfect. *Τὰ παρὰ σοῦ ῥηθέντα* ‘the things said by you’ expands singular *la tua parola*: the article substantivizes the acc. neut. pl. aor. pass. participle of *λέγω* (passive stem *ῥη-*); *παρά* with the genitive marks the speaker as the source.
- *Ἀπήντησεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀπαντάω*) has the sense ‘answered’. *Ἡ σκιὰ τοῦ μεγαθύμου* restores noun-before-genitive order after *del magnanimo quell’ ombra*; an article replaces *quell’*, and *μεγαθύμου* is a substantivized masculine adjective, ‘of the great-souled one’.
- *Τὴν σὴν ἀπηχρείωσε ψυχὴν ὁ φόβος* makes ‘fear’ the postponed nominative agent, where Italian has passive *è … offesa* with *da viltade*. The verb splits possessive *τὴν σήν* from *ψυχήν*. *Ἀπηχρείωσε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀπαχρειόω*) is a causative, ‘rendered useless’; *φόβος* names fear rather than explicitly qualifying it as cowardice.

> 46 la qual molte fiate l' uomo ingombra,  
> 47 sì che d' onrata impresa lo rivolve,  
> 48 come falso veder bestia quand' ombra.  

which oftentimes encumbers men, so that it turns them back from honoured enterprise; as false seeing does a startled beast.

> 46 „Ὃς τὸν ἄνθρωπον συχνῶς οὕτω πιέζει,  
> („Which so often presses hard upon a man)
>
> 47 „Ὥστ’ ἀποτρέπει παντὸς εὐγενοῦς ἔργου,  
> („That it turns him away from every noble deed,)
>
> 48 „Ὡς ἵππον θέα ψευδὴς ἀφηνιῶντα.  
> („Like a false sight making a horse shy.)

- *Ὃς* is masc. nom. sg., agreeing with the new antecedent *φόβος* rather than the gender of *la qual*. *Τὸν ἄνθρωπον* is generic singular, following *l’uomo*. *Συχνῶς … πιέζει* ‘often presses’ replaces *molte fiate … ingombra*; the added *οὕτω* ‘so’ anticipates the result.
- *Ὥστ’ ἀποτρέπει* uses elided *ὥστε* with a finite indicative to state an actual result. The object ‘him’ carries over from *τὸν ἄνθρωπον*; *παντὸς εὐγενοῦς ἔργου* is a genitive of separation, ‘from every noble deed’, adding *παντός*. Compare the passive of the same verb in line 41.
- *Ὡς ἵππον θέα ψευδὴς ἀφηνιῶντα* leaves ‘turns aside’ to be supplied: *θέα ψευδής* ‘a deceptive sight’ is nominative subject, *ἵππον* its accusative object. *Ἀφηνιῶντα* (acc. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *ἀφηνιάω*, ‘chafe at the reins, become restive’) agrees with *ἵππον*. Musurus specifies a horse for *bestia* and compresses temporal *quand’ ombra* into a participle describing the animal.

> 49 Da questa tema acciocchè tu ti solve,  
> 50 dirotti, perch' io venni, e quel che intesi,  
> 51 nel primo punto che di te mi dolve.  

To free thee from this dread, I will tell thee why I came, and what I heard in the first moment when I took pity of thee.

> 49 „Πρὸς ἀπαλλαγὴν τοῦ τοσούτου σου φόβου  
> („To rid you of this so great fear of yours,)
>
> 50 „Ἐρῶ σοι, πόθεν καὶ τί μαθὼν ὧδ’ ἦλθον,  
> („I will tell you whence and on learning what I came hither,)
>
> 51 „Ὅτ’ ἐπὶ τοῖς σοῖς πρῶτον ἠχθέσθην πόνοις.  
> („When first I was grieved for your troubles.)

- *Πρὸς ἀπαλλαγήν* ‘for release’ uses *πρός* with an accusative of purpose instead of the personal clause *acciocchè tu ti solve*. *Τοῦ τοσούτου σου φόβου* depends on the noun of release: ‘from so great a fear of yours’. *Σου* is possessive; *τοσούτου* expresses degree rather than merely pointing like *questa*.
- *Ἐρῶ σοι* ‘I shall tell you’ separates the future (1st sg. act., future of *λέγω*) and its dative recipient, combined in *dirotti*. *Πόθεν* asks ‘from where?’ rather than *perch’* ‘why?’; *ὧδ’*, elided *ὧδε*, adds ‘hither’. *Τί μαθών* ‘having learned what?’ converts the second finite *quel che intesi* into a question within the account of coming: *μαθών* (nom. sg. masc. second aor. act. participle of *μανθάνω*) is anterior to *ἦλθον*, here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*.
- *Ὅτ’ … πρῶτον* ‘when first’ reduces *nel primo punto che* to elided *ὅτε* and an adverb. *Ἠχθέσθην* (1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ἄχθομαι*, ‘be distressed’) takes *ἐπί* with the dative for the occasion of distress, *τοῖς σοῖς … πόνοις* ‘at your troubles’, expanding personal *di te*.

> 52 Io era tra color, che son sospesi,  
> 53 e donna mi chiamò beata e bella,  
> 54 tal che di comandare io la richiesi.  

I was amongst them who are in suspense; and a Lady, so fair and blessed that I prayed her to command, called me.

> 52 „Ὄντα μεταξὺ τῶν ὧδε μετεώρων,  
> („While I was among those who are in suspense here,)
>
> 53 „Καλεῖ με γυνὴ καλὴ καὶ μακαρία,  
> („A fair and blessed lady called me,)
>
> 54 „Ἧς τὰς προσταγὰς προθύμως ᾐτησάμην.  
> („Whose commands I eagerly asked for.)

- *Ὄντα* (acc. sg. masc. pres. participle of *εἰμί*) anticipates *με*, object of *καλεῖ*: literally ‘me, being among …, a woman calls’. Thus independent *Io era* becomes a circumstantial participle agreeing with the person called, not the caller. *Μεταξύ* governs *τῶν ὧδε μετεώρων* ‘of those suspended here’; the articular adjective replaces *color, che son sospesi*, with local *ὧδε* added.
- *Καλεῖ με γυνὴ καλὴ καὶ μακαρία* uses a historical present (3rd sg. act. ind. of *καλέω*) for *mi chiamò*. Subject and adjectives follow the verb; the adjectives reverse *beata e bella*.
- *Ἧς τὰς προσταγὰς προθύμως ᾐτησάμην* ‘whose commands I eagerly requested’ replaces consecutive *tal che* with a possessive relative, gen. sg. fem. *ἧς* depending on *προσταγάς*. Italian *la* is no longer the object of the request: *di comandare* becomes the object noun ‘commands’. *ᾐτησάμην* is 1st sg. aor. mid. ind. of *αἰτέω*, with temporal augment of initial *αι-* to *ῃ-*; the middle presents the request as made on the speaker’s own behalf. *Προθύμως* supplies the eagerness explicitly.

> 55 Lucevan gli occhi suoi più che la stella;  
> 56 e cominciommi a dir soave e piana  
> 57 con angelica voce, in sua favella:  

Her eyes shone brighter than the star[s]; and she began soft and gentle to tell me with angelic voice, in her language:

> 55 „Ὄμμασιν ἄστρων πλέον ἀκτινοβόλοις  
> („With eyes more radiant than the stars,)
>
> 56 „Ἤρξατο λέγειν προσηνὴς καὶ γλυκεῖα,  
> („She began to speak, gentle and sweet,)
>
> 57 „Ἀγγελικῇ τῇ φωνῇ προσφθεγγομένη.”—  
> („Addressing me with angelic voice.”—)

- *Ὄμμασιν … ἀκτινοβόλοις* ‘with radiant eyes’ turns the independent imperfect *Lucevan gli occhi suoi* into an attendant description in the dative plural; the possessor is inferred from the subject of the next verb. *Ἄστρων* is gen. pl. of comparison with adverbial *πλέον* ‘more’, which qualifies *ἀκτινοβόλοις* ‘ray-emitting’. Thus Greek expressly compares with stars in the plural, not the Italian singular *la stella*.
- *Ἤρξατο λέγειν* (3rd sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἄρχομαι* with pres. act. inf. of *λέγω*) follows *cominciommi a dir* in making speaking the activity begun. *Προσηνὴς καὶ γλυκεῖα* are predicate adjectives in the nom. sg. fem., ‘gentle and sweet’, describing the speaker as she speaks rather than functioning as Greek adverbs.
- *Ἀγγελικῇ τῇ φωνῇ* is a dative of manner or means, with *ἀγγελικῇ* outside the article–noun group in predicate position, ‘with her voice angelic’. *Προσφθεγγομένη* (nom. sg. fem. pres. mid. participle of *προσφθέγγομαι*) agrees with the speaker and adds the simultaneous action ‘addressing’. The verb implies an addressee, but there is no expressed pronoun for Italian *-mi* and no distinct equivalent for *in sua favella*.

## Lines 58–90 — Beatrice’s Request and Her Answer on Fear

> 58 ‘O anima cortese Mantovana,  
> 59 di cui la fama ancor nel mondo dura,  
> 60 e durerà quanto il moto lontana!  

‘O courteous Mantuan Spirit, whose fame still lasts in the world, and will last as long as Time!

> 58 —“Ὦ κεχαριτωμένη ψυχὴ Μαντύας,  
> (—“O gracious soul of Mantua,)
>
> 59 „Ἧς ἔτ’ ἐν τῇ γῇ διατελεῖ τὸ κλέος,  
> („Whose renown still endures upon the earth,)
>
> 60 „Διαμενεῖ τ’ ἐφ’ ὅσον τὸ πᾶν κινεῖται·  
> („And shall endure as long as the universe moves:)

- *Ὦ κεχαριτωμένη ψυχὴ Μαντύας* keeps the direct address and places the epithet before *ψυχή*, as in *anima cortese* with the order reversed. *κεχαριτωμένη* (voc. fem. sg., perfect passive participle of *χαριτόω*) means ‘endowed with grace’: an acquired quality takes the place of the Italian adjective of courtesy. *Μαντύας* is the genitive of the place-name *Μαντύα*, ‘of Mantua’, rather than an adjective agreeing with ‘soul’.
- *ἧς* (gen. fem. sg. of the relative *ὅς*) refers to *ψυχή* but depends on *τὸ κλέος*, ‘whose renown’. Musurus delays the subject until after *διατελεῖ* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *διατελέω*, here absolute, ‘continues’). *ἔτ’* is *ἔτι* elided before *ἐν*; *ἐν τῇ γῇ*, ‘on earth’, narrows *nel mondo*.
- *διαμενεῖ τ’* ‘and will endure’ varies the verb where *dura, e durerà* repeats it: *διαμενεῖ* is the contracted future active, 3rd sg., of *διαμένω*, and *τ’* is connective *τε* elided. *ἐφ’ ὅσον* is *ἐπί* with an accusative of extent, ‘for as long as’, with aspiration of the final consonant before the rough breathing. Rather than name Time, Musurus interprets *quanto il moto lontana* by a finite clause: *τὸ πᾶν* ‘the whole, the universe’ is the subject of *κινεῖται* (3rd sg. present middle/passive of *κινέω*), ‘is in motion’.

> 61 l’ amico mio, e non della ventura,  
> 62 nella diserta piaggia è impedito  
> 63 sì nel cammin, che volto è per paura;  

my friend, and not the friend of fortune, is so impeded in his way upon the desert shore, that he has turned back for terror;

> 61 „Φίλος τις ἐμός, ἀλλ’ οὐ φίλος τῆς τύχης,  
> („A certain friend of mine, though not a friend of fortune,)
>
> 62 „Οὕτω κωλύετ’ ἐν τοῖς ἐρήμοις τόποις  
> („Is so hindered in the desert places)
>
> 63 „Καθ’ ὁδόν, ὥστ’ ἐκ φόβου τρέπετ’ ὀπίσω·  
> („Along his way, that out of fear he turns back;)

- *φίλος τις ἐμός* ‘a certain friend of mine’ adds the enclitic indefinite *τις* to *l’ amico mio*. *ἀλλ’ οὐ φίλος τῆς τύχης* makes the contrast of *e non della ventura* explicit with *ἀλλά* elided, and supplies a second *φίλος*, on which the genitive depends.
- *οὕτω κωλύετ’* ‘is so hindered’ brings *sì* forward to announce the result clause. *κωλύετ’* and *τρέπετ’* are elisions of *κωλύεται* and *τρέπεται*, both 3rd sg. present indicatives: the former is passive, the latter middle, ‘turns himself back’. The Greek thus presents *volto è* as an action in progress rather than a resulting state.
- *ἐν τοῖς ἐρήμοις τόποις* generalizes the singular *nella diserta piaggia* to ‘in the deserted places’; *καθ’ ὁδόν* ‘along the way’ renders *nel cammin* with *κατά* and the accusative (elision and aspiration before *ὁδόν*). *ὥστ’* is elided *ὥστε*, here with the indicative of actual result. *ἐκ φόβου* gives the cause with *ἐκ* and the genitive; the adverb *ὀπίσω* supplies the direction.

> 64 e temo che non sia già sì smarrito,  
> 65 ch’ io mi sia tardi al soccorso levata,  
> 66 per quel ch’ io ho di lui nel Cielo udito.  

and I fear he may already be so far astray, that I have risen too late for his relief, from what I heard of him in Heaven.

> 64 „Καὶ δέος μήπως ἤδη παρεπλανήθη,  
> („And there is fear lest he has already gone astray,)
>
> 65 „Κἀγὼ πρὸς ἐπαρωγὴν ἦλθον βραδέως,  
> („And I have come too late to his succour,)
>
> 66 „Ὡς ἐν οὐρανοῖς ἤκουσα περὶ τούτου.  
> („From what I heard in the heavens concerning him.)

- *καὶ δέος μήπως* ‘and [there is] fear lest’ replaces first-person *e temo che non* with a noun and an understood *ἐστί*. *μήπως* introduces the feared possibility, not a denial. *ἤδη παρεπλανήθη* ‘he has already strayed’ uses the 3rd sg. aorist passive indicative of *παραπλανάω*, with internal augment. The indicative after an expression of fear presents something that may already have happened; it is not a Greek optative corresponding to Italian *sia*.
- *κἀγώ* (crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*) coordinates a second first-person clause instead of reproducing *sì … che* as degree and consequence. *πρὸς ἐπαρωγήν* ‘for assistance’ is *πρός* with an accusative of purpose; *ἦλθον* is the 1st sg. second aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι*, ‘I came’, not ‘I rose’. *βραδέως* literally says ‘tardily’; ‘too’ is inferred.
- *ὡς … ἤκουσα* ‘as I heard’ recasts *per quel ch’ … ho … udito* without a substantive antecedent to the relative. *ἤκουσα* is the augmented 1st sg. aorist active of *ἀκούω*. *ἐν οὐρανοῖς* has the anarthrous plural ‘in the heavens’ for singular *nel Cielo*; *περὶ τούτου* gives the topic with the genitive, ‘concerning him’ (formally also ‘concerning this’).

> 67 Or muovi, e con la tua parola ornata,  
> 68 e con ciò, ch’ è mestieri al suo campare,  
> 69 l’ aiuta sì, ch’ io ne sia consolata.  

Now go, and with thy ornate speech, and with what is necessary for his escape, help him so, that I may be consoled thereby.

> 67 „Ἄγε δὴ κινήθητι, σαῖς τ’ εὐεπείαις  
> („Come now, bestir yourself, and with your fair speech,)
>
> 68 „Καὶ πᾶσι τρόποις, οἷς δύναται σωθῆναι,  
> („And with all the means whereby he may be saved,)
>
> 69 „Αὐτῷ βοήθει, κἀμοὶ συνεργὸς γίνου.  
> („Assist him, and become a helper to me.)

- *ἄγε δή* uses the singular imperative of *ἄγω* as an exhortation, ‘come now’, reinforced by *δή*. The actual command answering to *muovi* is *κινήθητι* (2nd sg. aorist passive imperative of *κινέω*), intransitive in sense, ‘set yourself in motion’.
- *σαῖς τ’ εὐεπείαις καὶ πᾶσι τρόποις* coordinates two instrumental datives by *τε … καί*. *σαῖς* agrees with the plural *εὐεπείαις* ‘felicities of speech’: the quality expressed by *ornata* is built into the noun. *πᾶσι τρόποις* ‘by all means’ broadens *con ciò, ch’ è mestieri*; relative *οἷς* is likewise instrumental, ‘by which’. *δύναται* (3rd sg. present of deponent *δύναμαι*) governs *σωθῆναι*, the aorist passive infinitive of *σῴζω*: the test is what can save him, not what is necessary.
- *αὐτῷ βοήθει* has the personal dative required by *βοηθέω* and its contracted 2nd sg. present active imperative. Instead of *sì, ch’ io ne sia consolata*, Musurus gives a parallel command: *κἀμοὶ συνεργὸς γίνου* ‘and become a helper to me’. *κἀμοί* is crasis of *καὶ ἐμοί*; *συνεργός* is predicate nominative with *γίνου*, the 2nd sg. present middle imperative of *γίγνομαι* in its shorter form *γίνομαι*.

> 70 Io son Beatrice, che ti faccio andare;  

I am Beatrice who send thee;

> 70 „Εἰμὶ Βεάτριξ, ἐγώ τε σ’ ἀποστέλλω·  
> („I am Beatrice, and I send you forth;)

- *εἰμὶ Βεάτριξ* leaves the first *Io* in the person of the verb; *ἐγώ τε σ’ ἀποστέλλω* then introduces an emphatic independent subject, ‘and it is I who send you’. Connective *τε* replaces the relative link *che*, and *ἀποστέλλω* (1st sg. present active indicative) replaces causative *ti faccio andare* with one transitive verb. *σ’* is elided accusative *σε*.

> 71 vegno di loco, ove tornar disio;  

I come from a place where I desire to return;

> 71 „Ἔρχομ’ ἔνθεν μοι ποθητὸν ἐπιστρέψαι.  
> („I come from a place whither I long to return.)

- *ἔρχομ’* is elided *ἔρχομαι*, 1st sg. present middle in form. The single relative adverb *ἔνθεν* ‘whence’ compresses *di loco, ove*: its form marks the starting point of *ἔρχομαι*, while the same place is understood as the goal of returning.
- *μοι ποθητὸν ἐπιστρέψαι* recasts personal *tornar disio* as ‘to return [is] desirable to me’. *μοι* is dative of the person concerned; neuter singular *ποθητόν* is predicated of *ἐπιστρέψαι*, the aorist active infinitive of *ἐπιστρέφω*, with the copula omitted.

> 72 amor mi mosse, che mi fa parlare.  

love moved me, that makes me speak.

> 72 „Ἔρως ἐκίνησέ με, λόγους τ’ ἐμπνέει.  
> („Love moved me, and inspires my words.)

- *ἔρως ἐκίνησέ με* closely follows *amor mi mosse*, though the enclitic object follows the verb (hence the extra accent on *ἐκίνησέ*). The augmented 3rd sg. aorist active of *κινέω* echoes the command *κινήθητι* in line 67.
- *λόγους τ’ ἐμπνέει* ‘and inspires words’ keeps the change to the present (*ἐμπνέει*, uncontracted 3rd sg. present active of *ἐμπνέω*) but turns *che* into connective *τε* and replaces *mi fa parlare* with a verb taking *λόγους* as its direct object. The recipient of the inspiration is understood.

> 73 Quando sarò dinanzi al Signor mio,  
> 74 di te mi loderò sovente a lui.’  

When I shall be before my Lord, I oft will praise thee to him.’

> 73 „Ὅτ’ ἔσομ’ ἐνώπιον τοὐμοῦ Κυρίου,  
> („When I shall stand before my Lord,)
>
> 74 „Αὐτῷ συχνάκις περὶ σοῦ καλῶς λέξω.”—  
> („I will often speak well of you to him.”—)

- *ὅτ’ ἔσομ’ ἐνώπιον* elides both *ὅτε* and *ἔσομαι* (1st sg. future indicative of *εἰμί*, middle in form). The future indicative follows *quando sarò* closely; for an anticipated occasion an Attic alternative would be *ὅταν* with the subjunctive. Prepositional *ἐνώπιον* governs the genitive *τοὐμοῦ Κυρίου*, with crasis of *τοῦ ἐμοῦ*, ‘of my Lord’.
- *αὐτῷ … περὶ σοῦ καλῶς λέξω* distinguishes the addressee (dative) from the topic (*περί* with genitive): ‘to him I shall speak well about you’. This analytical phrase renders *di te mi loderò … a lui* without a reflexive. *λέξω* is 1st sg. future active of *λέγω*; *συχνάκις* ‘frequently’ renders *sovente*.

> 75 Tacette allora, e poi cominciai io:  

She was silent then, and I began:

> 75 —“Ἡ μὲν ἐσιώπησεν, ἐγὼ δ᾽ ἠρξάμην.”  
> (—“She fell silent, and I began.”)

- *ἡ μὲν ἐσιώπησεν, ἐγὼ δ’ ἠρξάμην* sets the two speakers in explicit contrast by *μέν … δέ* (the latter elided). *ἡ* is the article used pronominally in this construction, ‘she’; there is no separate equivalent of *allora* or *poi*.
- *ἐσιώπησεν* is 3rd sg. aorist active of *σιωπάω*, ‘fell silent’; *ἠρξάμην* is 1st sg. aorist middle of *ἄρχω*, the regular middle for ‘begin’, with ‘to speak’ left implicit as in *cominciai io*.

> 76 ‘O donna di virtù, sola per cui  
> 77 l’ umana spezie eccede ogni contento  
> 78 da quel ciel, che ha minori li cerchi sui!  

‘O Lady [of virtue], through whom [alone] mankind excels all that is contained within the heaven which has the smallest circles!

> 76 “Ὦ δέσποινα, κάτοχε τοῦ μόνου δώρου,  
> (“O lady, possessor of the sole gift,)
>
> 77 „Δι᾽ οὗπερ τἀνθρώπινον ἐξέχει γένος  
> („Through which the human race surpasses)
>
> 78 „Πάντων τῶν ἐν οὐρανῷ τῷ μειοτρόχῳ·  
> („All things in the lesser-wheeled heaven:)

- *ὦ δέσποινα, κάτοχε τοῦ μόνου δώρου* ‘O mistress, possessor of the sole gift’ redistributes *donna di virtù, sola per cui*. *κάτοχε* is vocative singular of the two-termination *κάτοχος*, used substantivally with a dependent genitive. *μόνου* agrees with supplied *δώρου*: uniqueness belongs to the gift rather than, as with feminine *sola*, to the lady.
- *δι’ οὗπερ* ‘through which indeed’ accordingly refers to the gift, not the woman: *οὗ* is genitive neuter singular after elided *διά*, with the emphasizing suffix *-περ*. *τἀνθρώπινον … γένος* (crasis of *τὸ ἀνθρώπινον*) encloses *ἐξέχει* between adjective and noun. The neuter singular subject renders *l’ umana spezie* collectively.
- *ἐξέχει* (3rd sg. present active of *ἐξέχω*) takes the comparative genitive *πάντων τῶν ἐν οὐρανῷ* ‘above all the things in heaven’, rather than a direct object as in *eccede ogni contento*. The article makes the prepositional phrase substantival; no participle for ‘contained’ is needed. *τῷ μειοτρόχῳ*, a following articular epithet agreeing with *οὐρανῷ*, compresses *che ha minori li cerchi sui* into the compound ‘of the lesser circuit’.

> 79 tanto m’ aggrada il tuo comandamento,  
> 80 che l’ ubbidir, se già fosse, m’ è tardi;  

so grateful to me is thy command, that my obeying, were it done already, seems tardy;

> 79 „Οὕτως εὐάρεστόν μοι τὸ πρόσταγμά σου,  
> („So pleasing to me is your command,)
>
> 80 „Ὥστ᾽ ἀμέσως ὑπείκων δοκῶ βραδύνειν.  
> („That even in obeying at once I seem to linger.)

- *οὕτως εὐάρεστόν μοι τὸ πρόσταγμά σου* is a verbless predicate, ‘so pleasing to me [is] your command’. *εὐάρεστον* agrees with neuter *πρόσταγμα*; enclitic *μοι* marks the experiencer and *σου* the possessor.
- *ὥστ’ ἀμέσως ὑπείκων δοκῶ βραδύνειν* ‘that, yielding immediately, I seem to delay’ has elided *ὥστε* with finite *δοκῶ* (1st sg. present active of *δοκέω*), followed by the present active infinitive *βραδύνειν*. *ὑπείκων* (nom. masc. sg. present active participle of *ὑπείκω*) agrees with the speaker and is concessive in context. The paradox survives, but actual simultaneity and *ἀμέσως* ‘immediately’ replace the hypothetical priority of *se già fosse*.

> 81 più non t’ è uopo aprirmi il tuo talento.  

it needs not that thou more explain to me thy wish.

> 81 „Οὐ χρήζεις πέρα φράζειν τὰς σὰς ἐφέσεις·  
> („You need not express your wishes further;)

- *οὐ χρήζεις … φράζειν* ‘you have no need to explain’ makes the person in *non t’ è uopo* the grammatical subject: *χρήζεις* is 2nd sg. present active of *χρήζω*, with complementary present active infinitive *φράζειν*. Adverbial *πέρα* means ‘further’; the recipient in *aprirmi* is not expressed.
- *τὰς σὰς ἐφέσεις* is the accusative object, ‘your desires’: the possessive adjective stands in attributive position and the plural of *ἔφεσις* replaces singular *talento*.

> 82 Ma dimmi la cagion, che non ti guardi  
> 83 dello scender quaggiuso in questo centro  
> 84 dall’ ampio loco, ove tornar tu ardi.’  

But tell me the cause, why thou forbearest not to descend into this centre here below from the spacious place, to which thou burnest to return.’

> 82 „Ἀλλὰ λέγε μοι, πῶς κατέβης ἐνταῦθα  
> („But tell me, how did you descend hither)
>
> 83 „Ἐν τῷ χαμηλῷ τούτῳ κέντρῳ, λιποῦσα  
> („Into this lowly centre, leaving)
>
> 84 „Τὸν εὐρὺν χῶρον, ἔνθ᾽ ἐπανελθεῖν θέλεις.” —  
> („The spacious realm, whither you wish to return.”—)

- *ἀλλὰ λέγε μοι, πῶς κατέβης ἐνταῦθα* ‘but tell me how you came down here’ moves directly from the 2nd sg. present imperative *λέγε* to an interrogative. *πῶς* asks how rather than explicitly why; neither *la cagion* nor the negative *non ti guardi* is translated separately. *κατέβης* (2nd sg. second aorist active indicative of *καταβαίνω*, augmented after the preverb) treats the descent as accomplished.
- *ἐν τῷ χαμηλῷ τούτῳ κέντρῳ* ‘in this low centre’ specifies the location of *ἐνταῦθα*. The demonstrative stands outside the article–adjective group. Where *in questo centro* indicates the goal of *scender*, Greek uses local *ἐν* with the dative, not directional *εἰς* with the accusative.
- *λιποῦσα τὸν εὐρὺν χῶρον* ‘having left the spacious place’ turns the Italian phrase of origin *dall’ ampio loco* into a transitive construction. *λιποῦσα* is the nom. fem. sg. second aorist active participle of *λείπω*, agreeing with the unexpressed ‘you’; *εὐρύν* is the acc. masc. sg. of *εὐρύς*. *ἔνθ’* is *ἔνθα* elided, ‘where’, understood directionally with *ἐπανελθεῖν* (second aorist active infinitive of *ἐπανέρχομαι*). Plain *θέλεις* ‘you wish’ drops the intensity of *ardi*.

> 85 ‘Da che tu vuoi saper cotanto addentro,  

‘Since thou desirest to know thus far,

> 85 —“Ἐπεὶ τὰ πάντ᾽ εἰς βάθος εἰδέναι βούλει,  
> (—“Since you wish to know all things deeply,)

- *ἐπεί* is causal, as is *da che*. *τὰ πάντ’* (elided *τὰ πάντα*, neut. acc. pl.) supplies an object, ‘everything’, while *εἰς βάθος* ‘into depth’ retains the spatial figure of *addentro*. Together they make a more sweeping claim than *cotanto addentro*.
- *εἰδέναι βούλει* places the infinitive before its governing verb: *εἰδέναι* is the perfect active infinitive of *οἶδα*, present in meaning, ‘to know’; *βούλει* is the Attic 2nd sg. present indicative of middle deponent *βούλομαι*.

> 86 dirotti brevemente,’ mi rispose,  

I will tell thee briefly,’ she replied,

> 86 „Ἐρῶ σοι βραχυεπῶς,” εἶπεν ἐκείνη,  
> („I will tell you briefly,” she said,)

- *ἐρῶ σοι βραχυεπῶς* closely unpacks *dirotti brevemente*: *ἐρῶ* is the contracted 1st sg. future of the verb ‘say’, *σοι* the dative addressee, and *βραχυεπῶς* a compound adverb, ‘in few words’.
- *εἶπεν ἐκείνη* ‘said she’ uses the 3rd sg. second aorist active *εἶπεν* with a postposed feminine demonstrative as subject. It supplies the speaker explicitly but drops both the sense ‘answered’ in *rispose* and its dative *mi*.

> 87 ‘perch’ io non temo di venir qua entro.  

‘why I fear not to come within this place.

> 87 “Ὡς οὐκ ἐνθάδε δειλιῶ καταβῆναι.  
> (“Why I do not fear to descend hither.)

- *ὡς οὐκ ἐνθάδε δειλιῶ καταβῆναι* resumes the complement of *ἐρῶ* after the reporting clause. *ὡς* more literally introduces ‘how’ or ‘that’ than a direct equivalent of interrogative *perché*. *οὐκ* is the indicative negation in its form before a smooth-breathing vowel.
- *δειλιῶ* is the contracted 1st sg. present active of *δειλιάω*, ‘I shrink in fear’, with complementary *καταβῆναι*, the second aorist active infinitive of *καταβαίνω*. *ἐνθάδε* ‘hither’ and the verb of descent replace *venir qua entro*; the Greek repeats the verb of line 82 rather than the Italian verb for coming.

> 88 Temer si dee di sole quelle cose  
> 89 ch’ hanno potenza di fare altrui male;  

Those things alone are to be feared that have the power of hurting;

> 88 „Ἐκεῖνα μόνα πᾶς ὀφείλει φοβεῖσθαι,  
> („Those things alone ought every man to fear,)
>
> 89 „Ἅπερ δύναται προξενεῖν αὐτῷ βλάβην·  
> („Which have power to bring him harm;)

- *ἐκεῖνα μόνα* ‘those things alone’ is the fronted neuter plural object of *φοβεῖσθαι*, the present middle/passive infinitive of *φοβέω*, here ‘to fear’, not passive ‘to be feared’. Impersonal *temer si dee* becomes personal *πᾶς ὀφείλει* ‘everyone ought’; *πᾶς* is substantival nom. masc. sg., and *ὀφείλει* governs the infinitive.
- *ἅπερ* (nom. neut. pl. relative with emphatic *-περ*) takes up *ἐκεῖνα* as subject. Singular *δύναται* with this neuter plural subject is regular Greek, in contrast to plural *hanno*. The auxiliary plus infinitive replaces *hanno potenza di*: *προξενεῖν* is the contracted present active infinitive of *προξενέω*, ‘to cause, procure’.
- *αὐτῷ βλάβην* supplies the recipient and object of the harm, ‘injury to him’. Where *altrui* is indefinite, singular *αὐτῷ* points back to the generic person *πᾶς*.

> 90 dell’ altre no, che non son paurose.  

the others not, which are not fearful.

> 90 „Οὐχὶ δὲ τἆλλα· ταῦτ᾽ οὐ γὰρ φοβητέα.  
> („But not the others; for these are not to be feared.)

- *οὐχὶ δὲ τἆλλα* ‘but emphatically not the rest’ leaves the verb of fearing to be supplied. *οὐχί* is the strengthened negative, *δέ* marks the contrast, and *τἆλλα* is crasis of *τὰ ἄλλα*, neut. acc. pl.
- *ταῦτ’ οὐ γὰρ φοβητέα* turns the Italian relative *che non son paurose* into an independent explanation with postpositive *γάρ*: ‘for these are not to be feared’. Elided *ταῦτα* is now nominative, and *φοβητέα* is its neuter plural predicate with the copula omitted. The verbal adjective in *-τέος* expresses what ought to be feared rather than simply what has a frightening quality, taking up the obligation expressed by *ὀφείλει* in line 88.

## Lines 91–123 — Beatrice’s Appeal and Virgil’s Rebuke

> 91 Io son fatta da Dio, sua mercè, tale,  
> 92 che la vostra miseria non mi tange,  
> 93 nè fiamma d’esto incendio non m’ assale.  

I am made such by God, in his grace, that your misery does not touch me; nor the flame of this burning assail me.

> 91 „Τοιάδ᾽ ἐγὼ χάριτι κατέστην θείᾳ,  
> („Such have I been made by divine grace,)
>
> 92 „Ὥστ᾽ ἡ τληπάθει᾽ ὑμῶν οὐδέν με βλάπτει,  
> („That your wretchedness harms me not at all,)
>
> 93 „Οὔτε φλὸξ τούτου τοῦ πυρὸς ἅπτεταί μου.  
> („Nor does any flame of this fire touch me.)

- *Τοιάδ᾽ ἐγὼ χάριτι κατέστην θείᾳ* ‘such I became by divine grace’ brings *tale* to the front. *Τοιάδ᾽* is elided *τοιάδε*, feminine nominative singular of *τοιόσδε*, predicative to the expressed *ἐγώ*. *Κατέστην* is the intransitive second aorist active indicative, first singular, of *καθίστημι*: Greek says ‘I came to be’, not ‘I was made’. The agent *da Dio* and the qualification *sua mercè* become one instrumental dative, *χάριτι … θείᾳ*, with the adjective separated from its noun by the verb.
- *Ὥστ᾽* is elided *ὥστε*, answering *τοιάδ᾽* as *che* answers *tale*. It introduces an actual result with the indicative. *Ἡ τληπάθει᾽ ὑμῶν* ‘the wretchedness of you’ has elision of the final *-α* of *τληπάθεια* and a possessive genitive plural for *vostra*. *Οὐδέν* is an adverbial accusative, ‘not at all’; *βλάπτει* (present active indicative of *βλάπτω*) takes *με* as direct object. Thus *non mi tange* becomes ‘does not harm me at all’.
- Conversely, Musurus uses the verb of touching in the second clause: *ἅπτεταί μου* ‘touches me’, present middle indicative, third singular, of *ἅπτω*, with the genitive usual after middle *ἅπτομαι*. The extra written accent on the verb is due to enclitic *μου*. *Οὔτε* continues the negation without reproducing both *nè* and *non*. *Φλόξ* is the subject; *τούτου τοῦ πυρός* ‘of this fire’ puts the demonstrative outside the article in the regular Greek construction.

> 94 Donna è gentil nel Ciel, che si compiange  
> 95 di questo impedimento, ov’ io ti mando,  
> 96 sì che duro giudizio lassù frange.  

There is a noble Lady in Heaven who has such pity of this hindrance, for which I send thee, that she breaks the sharp judgment there on high.

> 94 „Ἐν Οὐρανοῖς εὐμενής ἐστι Κυρία,  
> („In Heaven there is a gracious Lady,)
>
> 95 „ Οἰκτείρουσα τὸν ἄνδρα, πρὸς ὅν σε στέλλω,  
> („ Who pities the man to whom I send you,)
>
> 96 „ Ὡς τὰς στυγερὰς ἄνω κάμπτουσα κρίσεις.  
> („ So as to bend the stern judgments on high.)

- *Ἐν Οὐρανοῖς* ‘in the heavens’ changes singular *nel Ciel* to a locative dative plural and moves it to the front. In *εὐμενής ἐστι Κυρία*, *εὐμενής* ‘kindly disposed’ renders *gentil* by disposition rather than rank; it is nominative feminine singular, the same form as the masculine.
- *Οἰκτείρουσα* (present active participle, nominative feminine singular, of *οἰκτείρω*) replaces the finite relative *che si compiange*. Its accusative object *τὸν ἄνδρα* changes the object of pity from *questo impedimento* to the man himself. Accordingly, *ov’ io ti mando* becomes *πρὸς ὅν σε στέλλω* ‘to whom I send you’: a personal antecedent and *πρός* with the accusative of direction, rather than a relative adverb.
- *Ὡς τὰς στυγερὰς ἄνω κάμπτουσα κρίσεις* ‘so as to bend the hateful judgments on high’ retains the connection of consequence in *sì che*, but uses a second present active participle agreeing with *Κυρία*, not a finite verb. This *ὡς* with a participle is a less explicit result construction than *ὥστε* with the indicative in 92. *Κάμπτω* ‘bend’ softens *frange* ‘breaks’; *τὰς στυγερὰς … κρίσεις* makes *duro giudizio* plural, and the adverb *ἄνω* intervenes between adjective and noun.

> 97 Questa chiese Lucia in suo dimando,  
> 98 e disse: “Or ha bisogno il tuo fedele  
> 99 di te, ed io a te lo raccomando.”  

She called Lucia, in her request, and said: “Now thy faithful one has need of thee; and I commend him to thee.”

> 97 „ Αὕτη Λουκίαν μετακαλεσαμένη,  
> („ She, having summoned Lucia,)
>
> 98 „ Εἶπε· ‘“Νῦν ὁ σὸς πιστὸς σοῦ χρείαν ἔχει·  
> („ Said: ‘“Now your faithful one has need of you;)
>
> 99 „ Αὐτὸν ἐνθέρμως κἀγὼ συνίστημί σοι.”’  
> („ And I also warmly commend him to you.”’)

- *Αὕτη* ‘this lady’ resumes *Questa*. *Λουκίαν μετακαλεσαμένη* ‘having summoned Lucia’ makes the first Italian finite action subordinate to *εἶπε* ‘said’. *Μετακαλεσαμένη* is the first aorist middle participle, nominative feminine singular, of *μετακαλέω*; the middle gives the sense ‘call to oneself’. There is no separate equivalent for *in suo dimando*.
- *Νῦν ὁ σὸς πιστὸς σοῦ χρείαν ἔχει* closely follows the vocabulary of *Or ha bisogno il tuo fedele di te*, but puts the subject before the verb. *Πιστός* is substantival, ‘faithful one’, with the possessive adjective *σός* inside the article; the distinct, accented pronoun *σοῦ* is the genitive complement of *χρείαν ἔχει* ‘has need’.
- *Αὐτὸν ἐνθέρμως κἀγὼ συνίστημί σοι* fronts the person commended rather than the recipient as in *a te lo raccomando*. *Ἐνθέρμως* ‘warmly’ is supplied. *Κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*, ‘I too’; *συνίστημι* is first singular present active indicative, here ‘recommend’, with accusative of the person recommended and dative of the recipient. Enclitic *σοι* accounts for the additional accent in *συνίστημί*.

> 100 Lucia, nimica di ciascun crudele,  
> 101 si mosse, e venne al loco dov’ io era,  
> 102 che mi sedea con l’antica Rachele.  

Lucia, enemy of all cruelty, arose and came to the place where I was sitting with the ancient Rachel.

> 100 “Λουκία πάντων ἐχθρὰ σκληροκαρδίων  
> (“Lucia, foe of all the hard-hearted,)
>
> 101 „ Εὐθὺς ἀνέστη, κ᾿ εἰς τόπον ἦλθεν ὅπου  
> („ Immediately arose, and came to the place where)
>
> 102 „ Μετὰ τῆς Ῥαχὴλ καθήμην τῆς ἀρχαίας·  
> („ I was sitting with ancient Rachel;)

- *Λουκία πάντων ἐχθρὰ σκληροκαρδίων* makes the object of enmity personal, as in *di ciascun crudele*: ‘Lucia, foe of all the hard-hearted’. *Ἐχθρά* is feminine nominative singular in apposition to *Λουκία*; the dependent genitive *πάντων … σκληροκαρδίων* encloses it. Italian distributive *ciascun* with a singular becomes Greek *πάντων* with a plural.
- *Εὐθὺς ἀνέστη* adds ‘at once’ and makes *si mosse* specifically ‘rose’. *Ἀνέστη* (from *ἀνίστημι*) is an intransitive second aorist active indicative, third singular; *ἦλθεν* is the corresponding second aorist of *ἔρχομαι*. *Κ᾿ εἰς τόπον* has shortened *καί* before a vowel and *εἰς* with the accusative for motion to a place; *τόπον*, unlike *al loco*, has no article.
- *Ὅπου … καθήμην* ‘where I was sitting’ compresses *dov’ io era, che mi sedea* into one local relative clause. *Καθήμην* is first singular imperfect of *κάθημαι*. *Μετά* takes the genitive of company; in *τῆς Ῥαχὴλ … τῆς ἀρχαίας* the articles mark the case of the indeclinable name and place its postponed adjective in the second attributive position.

> 103 Disse: “Beatrice, loda di Dio vera,  
> 104 che non soccorri quei che t’ amò tanto,  
> 105 che uscìo per te della volgare schiera?  

She said: “Beatrice, true praise of God; why helpest thou not him who loved thee so, that for thee he left the vulgar crowd?

> 103 „ Εἶπε· ‘“Βεάτριξ, ἀληθὴς θεῖος αἶνος,  
> („ She said: ‘“Beatrice, true praise of God,)
>
> 104 „ Πῶς οὐ βοηθεῖς τῷ σε πεφιληκότι,  
> („ Why do you not help him who loved you,)
>
> 105 „ Διὰ σέ τ᾿ ἐξελθόντι τοῦ χύδην ὄχλου;  
> („ And for your sake came out from the vulgar crowd?)

- *Εἶπε* is third singular second aorist active indicative of the verb ‘say’. *Βεάτριξ* is the name used in address; *ἀληθὴς θεῖος αἶνος* ‘true divine praise’ stands in apposition, nominative in form rather than the ordinary vocative *αἶνε*. The two adjectives agree with masculine *αἶνος*, not with the person addressed; *θεῖος* turns *di Dio* into an adjective.
- *Πῶς οὐ βοηθεῖς* ‘how is it that you do not help?’ interprets interrogative *che non*. *Βοηθεῖς* is the contracted second singular present active indicative of *βοηθέω*, which takes the dative of the person helped. Hence *τῷ σε πεφιληκότι* ‘the one who has loved you’: the article substantivizes the perfect active participle of *φιλέω* (dative masculine singular), with *σε* its accusative object. The Greek perfect replaces *amò*.
- *Διὰ σέ τ᾿ ἐξελθόντι* ‘and who went out because of you’ coordinates another dative participle under the same article. *Τ᾿* is elided postpositive *τε*; *ἐξελθόντι* is the second aorist active participle of *ἐξέρχομαι*. The Italian degree and consequence, *tanto, che*, disappear: loving and leaving are coordinate descriptions. *Τοῦ χύδην ὄχλου* is a genitive of separation; *χύδην*, an adverb meaning ‘indiscriminately, in a mass’, occupies the attributive position for *volgare*.

> 106 Non odi tu la pieta del suo pianto?  

Hearest not thou the misery of his plaint?

> 106 „ Οὐκ ἀκούεις νῦν αὐτοῦ τοὺς οἰκτροὺς θρήνους;  
> („ Do you not hear now his piteous laments?)

- *Οὐκ ἀκούεις* is a negative question with second singular present active indicative, closely matching *Non odi tu*. *Νῦν* ‘now’ is added. *Αὐτοῦ τοὺς οἰκτροὺς θρήνους* ‘his piteous laments’ replaces the head noun *pieta* by an attributive adjective and makes singular *pianto* the plural direct object of hearing. The possessive genitive *αὐτοῦ* precedes the article.

> 107 Non vedi tu la morte che il combatte  
> 108 su la fiumana, ove il mar non ha vanto?”  

Seest thou not the death which combats him upon the river [over] which the sea has no boast?”

> 107 „ Οὐχ ὁρᾷς αὐτὸν μαχόμενον θανάτῳ  
> („ Do you not see him battling with death)
>
> 108 „ Ἐπὶ ποταμοῦ θαλάσσης δεινοτέρου;”’  
> („ Upon a river more terrible than the sea?”’)

- *Οὐχ ὁρᾷς* has *οὐχ* before a rough breathing and the contracted second singular present active indicative of *ὁράω*. *Αὐτὸν μαχόμενον* illustrates the accusative with a supplementary participle after a verb of perception: ‘see him fighting’. *Μαχόμενον* is present middle participle, accusative masculine singular, of *μάχομαι*; *θανάτῳ* is the dative of the opponent. This reverses the grammar of *la morte che il combatte*: the man, not death, is the grammatical fighter.
- *Ἐπὶ ποταμοῦ* uses *ἐπί* with a local genitive. *Θαλάσσης δεινοτέρου* ‘more terrible than the sea’ interprets rather than copies *ove il mar non ha vanto*. *Δεινοτέρου* is genitive masculine singular agreeing with *ποταμοῦ*; *θαλάσσης*, though also genitive, is governed by the comparison. Neither the relative clause nor the image of boasting remains.

> 109 Al mondo non fur mai persone ratte  
> 110 a far lor pro, nè a fuggir lor danno,  
> 111 com’ io, dopo cotai parole fatte,  

None on earth were ever swift to seek their good, or flee their hurt, as I, after these words were uttered,

> 109 “Οὐδείς ποτ᾿ ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ θηρεύων κέρδος,  
> (“No one ever in the world, hunting for gain,)
>
> 110 „ Ἢ βλάβην ἀποφεύγων, ἔδραμε τόσον,  
> („ Or fleeing from harm, ran so swiftly)
>
> 111 „ Ὁπόσον ὅτ᾿ ἤκουσα τοιούτους λόγους.  
> („ As I did when I heard such words.)

- *Οὐδείς ποτ᾿ ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ* ‘no one ever in the world’ substitutes a singular subject for *non fur mai persone*. *Ποτ᾿* is elided enclitic *ποτε*; under the negative it means ‘ever’.
- *Θηρεύων κέρδος, ἢ βλάβην ἀποφεύγων* ‘hunting gain or avoiding harm’ replaces the two infinitives *a far … a fuggir* with circumstantial present active participles, nominative masculine singular, agreeing with *οὐδείς*. Each governs an accusative; their order is reversed in the second phrase. *Θηρεύω* supplies the image of hunting; neither *lor* is expressed.
- *Ἔδραμε τόσον, ὁπόσον …* ‘ran so much as …’ turns *fur … ratte* into a verb of motion, the third singular second aorist active indicative of *τρέχω*. The correlative neuter accusatives mark degree, here understood of speed. The comparison leaves ‘I ran’ implicit. *Ὅτ᾿ ἤκουσα τοιούτους λόγους* ‘when I heard such words’ has elided *ὅτε* and first singular aorist active indicative of *ἀκούω*: Musurus gives the hearer’s action rather than the completed utterance of *dopo cotai parole fatte*.

> 112 venni quaggiù dal mio beato scanno,  

to come from my blessed seat;

> 112 „ Ὧδε κατέβην ἐκ τῆς μακάρων ἕδρας,  
> („ Hither I came down from the seat of the blessed,)

- *Ὧδε κατέβην* ‘I came down hither’ starts a fresh finite clause after the comparison, whereas Italian *venni* completes it. *Κατέβην* is first singular second aorist active indicative of *καταβαίνω*, with augment after the preverb. *Ὧδε* gives the direction toward the speaker’s present place; the verb itself supplies the downward motion of *quaggiù*.
- *Ἐκ τῆς μακάρων ἕδρας* ‘from the seat of the blessed’ uses *ἐκ* with the genitive of source. *Μακάρων* is the substantival genitive plural of *μάκαρ*, dependent on *ἕδρας*: *beato* no longer qualifies the seat directly, and *mio* is omitted.

> 113 fidandomi del tuo parlare onesto,  
> 114 che onora te, e quei che udito l’ hanno.’  

confiding in thy noble speech, which honours thee, and them who have heard it.’

> 113 „ Ἔχουσα πίστιν εἰς τὸ σὸν καλῶς λέγειν,  
> („ Putting my trust in your noble speech,)
>
> 114 „ Ὅπερ τιμᾷ σε καὶ τοὺς ἀκούσαντάς σου.” —  
> („ Which honours both you and those who have heard you.”—)

- *Ἔχουσα πίστιν* ‘having trust’ is a present active participle, nominative feminine singular, agreeing with the subject of *κατέβην*; it preserves the circumstantial function of *fidandomi* without its reflexive form. *Εἰς τὸ σὸν καλῶς λέγειν* ‘in your speaking well’ retains the Italian possessive with a substantivized infinitive. *Εἰς* governs the whole articular phrase in the accusative; *καλῶς*, an adverb modifying present infinitive *λέγειν*, replaces adjectival *onesto*. The phrase ‘have trust in’ with *εἰς* is less characteristically Attic than a construction with *πιστεύω* and a dative.
- *Ὅπερ* is the strengthened neuter singular relative, nominative subject of *τιμᾷ* (contracted present active indicative of *τιμάω*); it refers to the preceding articular infinitive. *Σε καὶ τοὺς ἀκούσαντάς σου* supplies two accusative objects of ‘honours’. The articular aorist active participle *τοὺς ἀκούσαντας* replaces *quei che udito l’ hanno*; genitive *σου* after the participle means that they heard you, not ‘it’. The additional acute on the participle precedes the enclitic.

> 115 Poscia che m’ ebbe ragionato questo,  
> 116 gli occhi lucenti lagrimando volse:  
> 117 per che mi fece del venir più presto;  

After saying this to me, she turned away her bright eyes weeping; by which she made me hasten more to come;

> 115 — “Εἰποῦσα ταῦτα, τοὺς ὀφθαλμοὺς δακρύοις  
> (—“Having said these things, her eyes with tears)
>
> 116 „ Πρός με λάμποντας προσέτρεψε, καὶ τοῦτο  
> („ Shining she turned toward me, and this)
>
> 117 „ Ἐπετάχυνεν ἐμὴν ἄφιξιν ὧδε.  
> („ Hastened my coming hither.)

- *Εἰποῦσα ταῦτα* ‘having said these things’ replaces *Poscia che m’ ebbe ragionato questo* with a second aorist active participle, nominative feminine singular, marking action prior to *προσέτρεψε*. *Ταῦτα* is accusative neuter plural for singular *questo*. There is no personal complement to the verb of saying.
- *Τοὺς ὀφθαλμοὺς δακρύοις … λάμποντας* ‘her eyes shining with tears’ redistributes *gli occhi lucenti lagrimando*: *λάμποντας*, present active participle accusative masculine plural, agrees with the eyes, while *δακρύοις* is a dative of means or cause, not a participle describing the woman. *Πρός με … προσέτρεψε* makes the direction explicit, ‘she turned [them] toward me’, unlike “turned away” in the English. *Προσέτρεψε* is third singular aorist active indicative of *προστρέπω*.
- *Καὶ τοῦτο ἐπετάχυνεν ἐμὴν ἄφιξιν ὧδε* ‘and this hastened my arrival here’ replaces connective *per che* with a coordinate clause whose subject is demonstrative *τοῦτο*. *Ἐπετάχυνεν* is third singular aorist active indicative of *ἐπιταχύνω*. Instead of *mi fece del venir più presto*, Greek makes the arrival itself the direct object and the traveller its possessor, *ἐμὴν ἄφιξιν*; there is no separate comparative.

> 118 e venni a te così, com’ ella volse;  

and thus I came to thee, as she desired;

> 118 „ Παρὰ σὲ δ’ ἐλθών, ὡς προὔτρεψεν ἐκείνη,  
> („ And coming to you, as she urged,)

- *Παρὰ σὲ δ’ ἐλθών* ‘and having come to you’ has *παρά* with the accusative of motion to a person, postpositive *δέ* elided before a vowel, and the nominative masculine singular second aorist active participle of *ἔρχομαι*. The shift from feminine *εἰποῦσα* to masculine *ἐλθών* identifies the change of agent grammatically. Finite *venni* is subordinated to the rescue verb in 119.
- *Ὡς προὔτρεψεν ἐκείνη* ‘as she urged’ retains comparative *com’* but gives *volse* the force of an exhortation rather than a wish. *Προὔτρεψεν* is third singular aorist active indicative of *προτρέπω*, with contraction of *προ-* and the augment in *προέτρεψεν*. Postponed *ἐκείνη* is an explicit feminine subject for *ella*; *così* has no separate counterpart.

> 119 dinanzi a quella fiera ti levai,  
> 120 che del bel monte il corto andar ti tolse.  

took thee from before that savage beast, which bereft thee of the short way to the beautiful mountain.

> 119 „ Ἔσωσά σε τῆς θηρός, ἥτις τὴν ὄρους  
> („ I saved you from the beast, which, to the mountain)
>
> 120 „ Τοῦ καλοῦ προσέλευσιν ἐκώλυέ σοι.  
> („ That is fair, hindered your approach.)

- *Ἔσωσά σε τῆς θηρός* ‘I saved you from the beast’ substitutes a verb of rescue for *dinanzi a … ti levai*. *Ἔσωσα* is first singular aorist active indicative of *σῴζω*, with personal accusative and genitive of separation; its second accent is due to enclitic *σε*. The article replaces *quella*. *Θήρ* is here treated as feminine, as both *τῆς* and the following relative show.
- *Ἥτις* is the feminine nominative singular of the compound relative *ὅστις*, here continuing the description of a definite antecedent. In *τὴν ὄρους τοῦ καλοῦ προσέλευσιν*, the article *τὴν* belongs to *προσέλευσιν* ‘approach’; the intervening genitive *ὄρους τοῦ καλοῦ* identifies the mountain approached, with its adjective postponed. *Il corto andar* becomes simply ‘the approach’, with *corto* omitted.
- *Ἐκώλυέ σοι* ‘was hindering for you’ is imperfect active indicative, third singular, of *κωλύω*, rather than the completed deprivation of *tolse*. The accusative object is the approach; *σοι* is a dative of disadvantage echoing *ti*, rather than the ordinary personal accusative in ‘prevent someone from doing something’. The second accent on the verb is due to *σοι*.

> 121 Dunque che è? perchè, perchè ristai?  

What is it then? why, why haltest thou?

> 121 „ Τί τὸ λοιπόν; διὰ τί, διὰ τί μένεις;  
> („ What then? why, why do you tarry?)

- *Τί τὸ λοιπόν;* ‘what then?’ is verbless, unlike *che è?*. *Τὸ λοιπόν*, the articular neuter singular of *λοιπός*, has transitional force. *Διὰ τί, διὰ τί* ‘for what reason, for what reason’ reproduces the repeated *perchè* with *διά* and the interrogative accusative. *Μένεις*, second singular present active indicative of *μένω*, renders *ristai* by ‘remain, tarry’.

> 122 perchè tanta viltà nel cuore allette?  

why lodgest in thy heart such coward fear?

> 122 „ Πῶς εἶ τοσοῦτον ἄνανδρος τὴν καρδίαν;  
> („ Why are you so faint-hearted?)

- *Πῶς εἶ τοσοῦτον ἄνανδρος τὴν καρδίαν;* ‘how are you so cowardly in heart?’ replaces *viltà*, the thing lodged, with *ἄνανδρος*, a predicate adjective describing the person. *Εἶ* is second singular present indicative of *εἰμί*; neuter accusative *τοσοῦτον* is adverbial, giving the degree of the adjective rather than agreeing with a noun as *tanta* does. *Τὴν καρδίαν* is accusative of respect, not a prepositional expression of place like *nel cuore*.

> 123 perchè ardire e franchezza non hai,  

why art thou not bold and free,

> 123 „ Πῶς οὔτε τόλμην οὔτε θρασύτητ’ ἔχεις,  
> („ Why have you neither daring nor boldness,)

- *Πῶς οὔτε τόλμην οὔτε θρασύτητ’ ἔχεις* keeps the Italian construction with *hai* more closely than the English does: ‘how have you neither daring nor boldness’. *Ἔχεις* is second singular present active indicative; the paired *οὔτε … οὔτε* distributes the single Italian *non* over its two accusative objects instead of joining them with *e*.
- *Τόλμην* renders substantival *ardire* by ‘daring’; *θρασύτητ’*, elided *θρασύτητα* (accusative singular of *θρασύτης*), interprets *franchezza* as boldness or even audacity, not literally freedom.

## Lines 124–142 — Courage and Purpose Renewed

> 124 poscia che tai tre Donne benedette  
> 125 curan di te nella corte del cielo,  
> 126 e il mio parlar tanto ben t’ impromette ?”  

when three such blessed Ladies care for thee in the court of Heaven, and my words promise thee so much good?”

> 124 „ Ἐν ᾧ τοιαῦται τρεῖς μάκαιραι Κυρίαι  
> („ While three such blessed Ladies)
>
> 125 „ Ἐν τοῖς οὐρανοῖς ὑπὲρ σοῦ μεριμνῶσι,  
> („ Care for you in the heavens,)
>
> 126 „ Καλά σοι δ’ οὑμοὶ προσυπισχνοῦνται λόγοι; ”  
> („ And my words promise you such good things?”)

- *Ἐν ᾧ* ‘while’, literally ‘in which [time]’, takes the indicative. Where *poscia che* can mean ‘seeing that’, the Greek presents the circumstances as holding at the very time in question.
- *τοιαῦται τρεῖς μάκαιραι Κυρίαι* keeps the order of *tai tre Donne* but puts the epithet before the noun. *τοιαῦται* (nom. fem. pl. of *τοιοῦτος*) gives the qualitative force of *tai*, ‘such’; *μάκαιραι* is the poetic feminine plural of *μάκαρ*, ‘blessed’. All four words are nominative, the subject of *μεριμνῶσι*.
- *ἐν τοῖς οὐρανοῖς* ‘in the heavens’ comes before the verb and replaces the singular *cielo* and its *corte* with a plural place-name alone. *ὑπὲρ σοῦ* ‘on your behalf’ gives *di te* a specifically benefactive sense. *μεριμνῶσι* is the contracted 3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *μεριμνάω*, with movable *-ν* absent.
- *Καλά σοι δ’ οὑμοὶ προσυπισχνοῦνται λόγοι* fronts *καλά* (acc. neut. pl. of *καλός*), ‘good things’, making *tanto ben* the object without separately expressing *tanto*. Enclitic *σοι* is the dative of the person promised; continuative *δ’* is elided *δέ*. *οὑμοί* is crasis for *οἱ ἐμοί*, ‘my’; the possessive is separated from its noun *λόγοι* by the verb. The plural ‘my words’ thus encloses *προσυπισχνοῦνται* (3rd pl. pres. mid. ind. of *προσυπισχνέομαι*, ‘promise in addition’), replacing the singular verbal noun and verb of *il mio parlar … t’impromette*.

> 127 Quali i fioretti dal notturno gelo  
> 128 chinati e chiusi, poi che il sol gl’ imbianca,  
> 129 si drizzan tutti aperti in loro stelo:  

As flowerets, by the nightly chillness bended down and closed, erect themselves all open on their stems when the sun whitens them

> 127 Ὥσπερ τἄνθος ἐκ πάγους νυκτίου κύπτει,  
> (Just as the flower droops from the nightly frost,)
>
> 128 Καὶ μένει κλειστόν· ἡλίου δ’ ἀνιόντος,  
> (And remains closed; then when the sun rises,)
>
> 129 Ἐπὶ τοῦ καυλοῦ διανοιχθὲν ὀρθοῦται·  
> (Opening upon its stem it stands upright;)

- *Ὥσπερ* ‘just as’ introduces a clause, in anticipation of *Οὕτως* ‘so’ in 130. *τἄνθος* is crasis for *τὸ ἄνθος*, a generic neuter singular, ‘the flower’, in place of the diminutive plural *i fioretti*. In *ἐκ πάγους νυκτίου*, *ἐκ* with the genitive marks the cause, as does *dal*; *νυκτίου* (gen. sg. of *νύκτιος*) agrees with *πάγους*, from the neuter *πάγος*, ‘frost’.
- The participial pair *chinati e chiusi* becomes two predicates in the generic present: *κύπτει* ‘it bends down’ (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *κύπτω*) and *μένει κλειστόν* ‘it stays closed’. *μένει*, from *μένω*, supplies the notion of persistence; *κλειστόν* is a predicate adjective agreeing with *ἄνθος*.
- *ἡλίου δ’ ἀνιόντος* ‘but as the sun comes up’ is a genitive absolute: *ἀνιόντος* is the gen. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *ἄνειμι*, ‘go up’. The present marks the accompanying process; *δ’* turns from the first condition to the second. The Greek takes the whitening in *poi che il sol gl’imbianca* as sunrise: the sun no longer acts on an expressed object.
- *ἐπὶ τοῦ καυλοῦ* ‘upon the stem’ uses *ἐπί* with the genitive of position; the article suffices for *loro*. *διανοιχθέν* (nom. neut. sg. aor. pass. participle of *διανοίγω*) means ‘having opened’, treating *aperti* as a change already achieved. *ὀρθοῦται* (3rd sg. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *ὀρθόω*) is intransitive in sense, ‘stands upright’, for reflexive *si drizzan*. The singular construction leaves no distributive *tutti*.

> 130 tal mi fec’ io, di mia virtute stanca;  

: thus I did, with my fainting courage;

> 130 Οὕτως ἐμαυτὸν ἀναψυχθέντ’ ᾐσθόμην·  
> (So I felt myself refreshed;)

- *Οὕτως ἐμαυτὸν ἀναψυχθέντ’ ᾐσθόμην* is literally ‘so I felt myself revived’. Adverbial *οὕτως* answers *ὥσπερ*; *ἐμαυτόν*, the acc. masc. sg. first-person reflexive, is the object of *ᾐσθόμην* (1st sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *αἰσθάνομαι*, with temporal augment). The Italian reflexive *tal mi fec’ io* thus becomes a verb of perception with an object and a supplementary participle.
- *ἀναψυχθέντ’* is elided *ἀναψυχθέντα* (acc. masc. sg. aor. pass. participle of *ἀναψύχω*), agreeing with *ἐμαυτόν*. Rather than name the failing *virtute* and describe it as *stanca*, Musurus describes the speaker by the completed reversal of that condition.

> 131 e tanto buono ardire al cuor mi corse,  
> 132 ch’ io cominciai come persona franca:  

and so much good daring ran into my heart, that I began as one set free:

> 131 Καὶ τοσοῦτον ἐν ἐμοὶ θάρσος ἐπῆλθεν,  
> (And so much courage came upon me,)
>
> 132 Ὥστ’ ἀπεκρίθην ὡς ἄνθρωπος γενναῖος.  
> (That I answered like a brave man.)

- *Καὶ τοσοῦτον ἐν ἐμοὶ θάρσος ἐπῆλθεν* retains *e tanto … ardire* in *καὶ τοσοῦτον … θάρσος*; the demonstrative adjective and its noun are nom. neut. sg., subject of *ἐπῆλθεν*. *θάρσος* is the poetic/Ionic counterpart of Attic *θάρρος*, ‘courage’; *buono* has no separate equivalent. Interposed *ἐν ἐμοί* ‘in me’ replaces *al cuor*: a location rather than a goal. *ἐπῆλθεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἐπέρχομαι*, with augment after the preverb) means ‘came on’, without the image of running in *corse*.
- *τοσοῦτον … ὥστ’* renders consecutive *tanto … ch’*. Elided *ὥστε* takes a finite indicative for an actual result: *ἀπεκρίθην* (1st sg. aor. pass. in form of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, active in sense), ‘I answered’. It specifies the speech act left implicit by *cominciai*. *ὡς ἄνθρωπος γενναῖος* ‘like a brave man’ has a nominative referring back to the speaker; the adjective interprets *franca* as boldness rather than freedom.

> 133 “O pietosa colei che mi soccorse,  
> 134 e tu cortese, ch’ ubbidisti tosto  
> 135 alle vere parole che ti porse!  

“O compassionate she, who succoured me! and courteous thou, who quickly didst obey the true words that she gave thee!

> 133 “ Ὦ ψυχὴ φιλέλεος, προστάτριά μου,  
> (“O compassionate soul, my protectress,)
>
> 134 „ Καὶ σὺ φιλόφρων ὁ τάχος ὑπακούσας  
> („ And you, kindly one, who swiftly obeyed)
>
> 135 „Οἷς αὐτὴ λόγοις πιστοῖς παρώτρυνέ σε!  
> („The faithful words with which she urged you on!)

- *Ὦ ψυχὴ φιλέλεος, προστάτριά μου* turns *O pietosa colei che mi soccorse* into a direct address throughout: ‘O compassionate soul, my protectress’. *ψυχή* supplies a noun for the demonstrative *colei*; *φιλέλεος* is a two-termination adjective, feminine here. *προστάτρια* is a feminine agent noun in vocative apposition, with enclitic possessive genitive *μου* (hence the additional accent in *προστάτριά μου*). A relation expressed by a noun replaces the past action *mi soccorse*.
- *καὶ σὺ φιλόφρων* closely follows *e tu cortese*: emphatic *σύ* and *φιλόφρων* ‘kindly disposed’ address the second person. *ὁ τάχος ὑπακούσας* ‘you who swiftly obeyed’ uses the article and a nom. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *ὑπακούω* in apposition to *σύ*, in place of finite *ch’ ubbidisti*. *τάχος* is an adverbial accusative, ‘with speed’, for *tosto*.
- *οἷς αὐτὴ λόγοις πιστοῖς παρώτρυνέ σε* combines the two Italian constructions *alle vere parole* and *che ti porse*. The antecedent *λόγοις πιστοῖς* stands inside the relative clause: ‘[the] trustworthy words with which she herself urged you’. The dative suits both *ὑπακούσας* (‘obeying words’) and instrumental *οἷς* (‘by which’). *πιστοῖς* interprets *vere* as ‘worthy of trust’. Intensive *αὐτή* is nominative; *παρώτρυνέ* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *παροτρύνω*, with internal temporal augment and an extra accent before enclitic *σε*) makes the addressee the direct object of urging, not the indirect object of giving.

> 136 Tu m’ hai con desiderio il cuor disposto  
> 137 sì al venir, con le parole tue,  
> 138 ch’ io son tornato nel primo proposto.  

Thou hast disposed my heart with such desire to go, by what thou sayest, that I have returned to my first purpose.

> 136 „ Οὕτω ταῖς σαῖς προτροπαῖς ἐν τῇ καρδίᾳ  
> („ So with your exhortations into the heart)
>
> 137 „ Ἐνέσταξάς μοι πόθον τοῦ προχωρῆσαι,  
> („ You have instilled in me longing to go forward,)
>
> 138 „ Ὥστ᾽ εἰς τὴν πρώτην πρόθεσιν ἐπανῆλθον.  
> („ That I have returned to my first purpose.)

- *Οὕτω ταῖς σαῖς προτροπαῖς* ‘in such a way, by your exhortations’ brings *sì* and *con le parole tue* forward together. *οὕτω* is the form of *οὕτως* without final sigma before a consonant; it anticipates *ὥστ’*. *ταῖς σαῖς προτροπαῖς* is instrumental dative plural, with the possessive adjective inside the article–noun group; ‘exhortations’ specifies the function of the *parole*.
- *ἐν τῇ καρδίᾳ ἐνέσταξάς μοι πόθον* ‘in the heart you instilled longing in me’ reverses the roles of *il cuor* and *con desiderio*: the heart is the location, *πόθον* the accusative object. *ἐνέσταξάς* is the 2nd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐνστάζω*, ‘drip in’, with augment after *ἐν-* and a second accent before enclitic dative *μοι*. The simple aorist replaces the compound past *m’hai … disposto*.
- *τοῦ προχωρῆσαι* is an articular infinitive in the genitive dependent on *πόθον*: ‘longing to go forward’. *προχωρῆσαι* is aor. act. inf. of *προχωρέω*, presenting the action as a whole; ‘advance’ replaces the directional *venir*. *ὥστ’ εἰς τὴν πρώτην πρόθεσιν ἐπανῆλθον* gives the actual result with *ὥστε* and the indicative. *εἰς* plus accusative treats *nel primo proposto* as a destination; *ἐπανῆλθον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἐπανέρχομαι*) renders *son tornato* with another simple past.

> 139 Or va, chè un sol volere è d’ ambo due;  

Now go, for both have one will;

> 139 „ Ἄγε δή, μία θέλησις ἡμῖν ἔστω·  
> („ Come now, let one will be ours;)

- *Ἄγε δή* ‘come now’ uses the 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *ἄγω* as an exhortatory formula, rather than a literal command to go; postpositive *δή* lends immediacy to *or*. No causal conjunction answers to *chè*.
- *μία θέλησις ἡμῖν ἔστω* ‘let there be one will for us’ changes Italian indicative *è* to *ἔστω*, 3rd sg. pres. imperative of *εἰμί*: shared resolve is enjoined rather than asserted. *μία* (fem. nom. sg. of *εἷς*) agrees with *θέλησις*; the possessive dative *ἡμῖν* is plural, not dual. Neither *sol* nor *ambo due* receives a separate equivalent for its emphasis.

> 140 tu duca, tu signore, e tu maestro.”  

thou guide, thou lord and master.”

> 140 „ Σύ μοι ποδηγός, σὺ κύριος, σύ τ᾽ ἄρχων.“  
> („ You my guide, you my lord, and you my master.“)

- *Σύ μοι ποδηγός, σὺ κύριος, σύ τ’ ἄρχων* keeps all three occurrences of *tu*, each with a predicate nominative and no expressed copula. Enclitic dative *μοι*, supplied in the first member, marks the speaker as the person to whom these titles apply. *ποδηγός* ‘guide’ renders *duca* in its sense of leading the way; *κύριος* gives *signore*.
- In *σύ τ’ ἄρχων*, *τ’* is postpositive *τε* elided before the vowel, corresponding to *e*. *ἄρχων*, originally the pres. act. participle of *ἄρχω* (nom. masc. sg.), is substantive, ‘ruler’: it takes *maestro* in the sense of authority rather than teaching.

> 141 Così gli dissi; e poichè mosso fue,  
> 142 entrai per lo cammino alto e silvestro.  

Thus I spake to him; and he moving, I entered on the arduous and savage way.

> 141 Αὐτῷ ταῦτ᾽ εἶπον· ὁ δ᾽ ἔπειτα προέβη,  
> (These things I said to him; and then he went forward,)
>
> 142 Ἐγὼ δ᾽ εἰς τὴν ἀγρίαν ὁδὸν εἰσῆλθον.  
> (And I entered upon the savage way.)

- *Αὐτῷ ταῦτ’ εἶπον* ‘to him I said these things’ puts the dative recipient *αὐτῷ* first for *gli* and replaces adverbial *così* with a direct object, *ταῦτ’*, elided *ταῦτα* (acc. neut. pl. of *οὗτος*). *εἶπον* is the 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. used as the past of *λέγω*.
- *ὁ δ’ ἔπειτα προέβη* ‘and he then went forward’ makes *poichè mosso fue* an independent clause. *ὁ δ’* is the pronominal use of the article with elided *δέ* to mark a change of subject; *ἔπειτα* preserves the time sequence without subordination. *προέβη* (3rd sg. root aor. act. ind. of *προβαίνω*, with augment after *προ-*) specifies forward motion.
- *Ἐγὼ δ’* switches subjects again, supplying the nominative pronoun where *entrai* alone marks the person. *εἰς τὴν ἀγρίαν ὁδὸν εἰσῆλθον* ‘I went into the wild road’ replaces *per lo cammino* with *εἰς* plus accusative of goal, repeating the preposition in the compound verb *εἰσῆλθον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *εἰσέρχομαι*). Feminine accusative *ἀγρίαν* agrees with feminine *ὁδόν* and gives *silvestro* as ‘wild’; *alto* has no distinct equivalent.

## Summary — Close Phrasing and Grammatical Recasting

Musurus often preserves the relation between Dante’s clauses while changing the forms that express it. The imperfect *παρήρχετο* ‘was passing’ follows the unfolding action of *se n’ andava*, but *μέν … δέ* marks a contrast that the Italian leaves to the repeated *e*. Elsewhere *τε … καί* closely matches the pairing of *sì … e sì*, and *οὕτω … ὥστε* with the indicative gives *sì … che* its force of actual consequence. Yet the same Italian construction need not produce the same Greek: at lines 104–105, *tanto, che* makes one action the consequence of another, whereas *πεφιληκότι* ‘having loved’ and *ἐξελθόντι* ‘having gone out’ are coordinated participles describing the same person.

The participle is Musurus’s most versatile means of recasting a clause. At lines 52–53, independent *Io era* becomes accusative *ὄντα* ‘being’, agreeing with *με* ‘me’ in the next line: the subject of the Italian clause becomes the object of the Greek verb of calling. At line 128, the genitive absolute *ἡλίου … ἀνιόντος* ‘as the sun rises’ does more than condense *poi che il sol gl’ imbianca*: it replaces what the sun does to the flowers with the circumstance of its rising. Elsewhere the syntax stays notably close. *Σὺ λέγεις* ‘you say’ retains the explicit pronoun of *Tu dici*, though the following assertion becomes an accusative and infinitive; near the close, all three occurrences of *tu* survive as *σύ*, each followed by a title without a copula.

Small choices of word or construction can also determine the reading. In the invocation, *ἐν ᾗ γέγραπται* ‘in which there stands written’ makes memory the place where the record survives, not its writer as in *che scrivesti*. The kinship term *πάππον* ‘grandfather’ differs outright from Carlyle’s ‘father’ for *parente*. A less conspicuous but equally consequential shift is from *per lo loco santo* to *ὡς ἱερός τις τόπος* ‘as a certain holy place’: the place becomes what Rome and the Empire were designated as, not what they were established for. Conversely, Musurus can retain a turn that the English prose smooths away: *ἔχεις* ‘you have’, governing two abstract nouns, follows *ardire e franchezza non hai* more closely than ‘art thou not bold and free’.
