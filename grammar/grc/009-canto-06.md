# Inferno 6

## Lines 1–21 — The Rain of the Third Circle and Cerberus

> 1 Al tornar della mente, che si chiuse  
> 2 dinanzi alla pietà de’ duo cognati,  
> 3 che di tristizia tutto mi confuse,  

On sense returning, which closed itself before the misery of the two kinsfolk that stunned me all with sadness,

> 1 Ὡς δ’ ἀνέλαβον τὰς ταραχθείσας φρένας  
> (And when I recovered my troubled mind)
>
> 2 Ἐκ συμπαθείας πρὸς τοὺς δύο συγγόνους,  
> (From compassion for the two kinsmen,)
>
> 3 Τῆς ὅλον μ’ ἐμπλησάσης βαθείας λύπης,  
> (Which had filled me wholly with deep grief,)

- *Ὡς δ’ ἀνέλαβον* ‘and when I recovered’ turns the temporal *Al tornar della mente* into a finite clause. *ὡς* is temporal, *δ’* is connective *δέ* elided before a vowel, and *ἀνέλαβον* is the 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἀναλαμβάνω*, with the augment after the preverb. In Italian the mind returns; in Greek the speaker recovers his faculties, *τὰς … φρένας* (acc. pl. of *φρήν*, poetic plural).
- *τὰς ταραχθείσας φρένας* ‘my troubled faculties’ replaces the relative *che si chiuse*. *ταραχθείσας* (acc. fem. pl. aor. pass. participle of *ταράσσω*) is attributive: the faculties ‘had been disturbed’, not ‘had closed themselves’. *ἐκ συμπαθείας* gives the cause of that disturbance. Musurus takes *pietà* as the speaker’s compassion, rather than the “misery” of the English; *πρὸς τοὺς δύο συγγόνους* marks those towards whom he feels it. The article and noun are accusative plural after *πρός*, not dual despite *δύο*.
- *τῆς ὅλον μ’ ἐμπλησάσης βαθείας λύπης* ‘which filled the whole of me with deep grief’ resumes *συμπαθείας* with an articular participle in the gen. fem. sg. *ἐμπλησάσης* (aor. act. of *ἐμπίπλημι*) takes the person filled in the accusative, *μ’* (elided *με*), and the thing with which he is filled in the genitive, *βαθείας λύπης*. *ὅλον* agrees with *με*, closely following *tutto mi*; ‘deep’ is added, and filling replaces *confuse*.

> 4 nuovi tormenti e nuovi tormentati  
> 5 mi veggio intorno, come ch’ io mi muova,  
> 6 e ch’ io mi volga, e come ch’ io mi guati.  

I discern new torments, and new tormented souls, whithersoever I move, and turn, and gaze.

> 4 Νέας στρεβλώσεις καὶ νέους στρεβλουμένους  
> (New torments and new tormented ones)
>
> 5 Ὁρῶ μου πέριξ, καθ’ ὅσον ἐκινούμην,  
> (I see around me, whichever way I moved,)
>
> 6 Εἴτ’ ἀτενίζων, εἴτε τρεπόμενός που.  
> (Whether gazing intently, or turning anywhere.)

- *νέας στρεβλώσεις καὶ νέους στρεβλουμένους* reproduces both the repetition and the change from suffering to sufferers in *nuovi tormenti e nuovi tormentati*. Both objects precede *ὁρῶ* as in Italian. *στρεβλώσεις* (acc. pl. of *στρέβλωσις*) denotes acts of twisting or torture; *στρεβλουμένους* (acc. masc. pl. pres. pass. participle of *στρεβλόω*) denotes people undergoing it, without a noun corresponding to “souls”.
- *ὁρῶ μου πέριξ* ‘I see round about me’ uses the contracted 1st sg. pres. act. ind. of *ὁράω*. The enclitic genitive *μου* belongs to the postposed *πέριξ*; there is no reflexive attached to the verb to match *mi veggio*.
- *καθ’ ὅσον ἐκινούμην* is literally ‘insofar as I was moving’: *κατά* loses its final vowel and its *τ* is aspirated before the rough breathing of *ὅσον*. The imperfect middle/passive indicative *ἐκινούμην* (1st sg. of *κινέω*, here intransitive) shifts away from both the present *ὁρῶ* and Dante’s present subjunctive *mi muova*. The phrase does not explicitly express direction as “whithersoever” does.
- *εἴτ’ ἀτενίζων, εἴτε τρεπόμενός που* substitutes alternatives, ‘whether gazing intently or turning somewhere’, for the coordinated *e ch’ io mi volga, e come ch’ io mi guati*, and reverses their order. The participles are nom. masc. sg., agreeing with the speaker: present active of *ἀτενίζω*, present middle of *τρέπω*. The first *εἴτε* is elided; indefinite enclitic *που* accounts for the additional accent on *τρεπόμενός*.

> 7 Io sono al terzo cerchio della piova  
> 8 eterna, maledetta, fredda e greve;  
> 9 regola e qualità mai non l’è nova.  

I am in the Third Circle, that of the eternal, accursed, cold, and heavy rain; its [law] and quality is never new.

> 7 Εἰς τὸν τρίτον νῦν εἰμι κύκλον τοῦ ὄμβρου  
> (Now I am in the third circle, of the rain)
>
> 8 Ἐναγοῦς, αἰωνίου, ψυχροῦ, ῥαγδαίου,  
> (Accursed, eternal, cold, torrential,)
>
> 9 ᾈεὶ τὸν αὐτὸν καταῤῥέοντος τρόπον.  
> (Always pouring down in the same manner.)

- *εἰς τὸν τρίτον νῦν εἰμι κύκλον* ‘I am now in the third circle’ inserts *νῦν εἰμι* between adjective and noun and adds ‘now’. Static *εἰμι* (1st sg. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*) with *εἰς* and the accusative corresponds to *sono al*; ordinary Attic would distinguish location, *ἐν* with the dative, from motion into a place.
- *τοῦ ὄμβρου* ‘of the downpour’ depends on *κύκλον* as *della piova* does on *cerchio*. The four gen. masc. sg. adjectives agree with *ὄμβρου*: *ἐναγοῦς* (from *ἐναγής*, ‘accursed’) precedes *αἰωνίου* ‘eternal’, reversing Dante’s first pair; *ψυχροῦ* keeps *fredda*, whereas *ῥαγδαίου* ‘torrential’ interprets *greve* as intensity of fall rather than weight.
- *ᾈεὶ τὸν αὐτὸν καταῤῥέοντος τρόπον* ‘always streaming down in the same manner’ turns the independent negative statement *regola e qualità mai non l’è nova* into an affirmative description of *ὄμβρου*. *καταῤῥέοντος* (gen. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *καταρρέω*) agrees with the rain; *τὸν αὐτὸν … τρόπον* is an adverbial accusative, with attributive *αὐτόν* meaning ‘same’. The single ‘manner’ does duty for both Italian nouns. *ᾈεὶ* is a spelling of *ἀεί* with iota subscript; *ῤῥ* is the older typography for doubled rho in the compound.

> 10 Grandine grossa, e acqua tinta, e neve  
> 11 per l’ aer tenebroso si riversa;  

Large hail, and turbid water, and snow, [pour down] through the darksome air;

> 10 Χάλαζα πυκνή, χιών, μελάγχρουν ὕδωρ  
> (Dense hail, snow, dark-coloured water)
>
> 11 Ἄρδην ἐκχεῖται δι’ ἀέρος ζοφώδους·  
> (Pour forth utterly through the murky air;)

- *χάλαζα πυκνή, χιών, μελάγχρουν ὕδωρ* gives three nominatives without Dante’s repeated *e*, moving the snow ahead of the water. *πυκνή* ‘dense’ characterizes the fall rather than the size expressed by *grossa*; *μελάγχρουν* (contracted nom. neut. sg., agreeing with *ὕδωρ*) makes *tinta* specifically dark in colour.
- *ἄρδην ἐκχεῖται* ‘is poured out wholesale’ adds the adverb *ἄρδην* and retains the singular of *si riversa*: *ἐκχεῖται* is 3rd sg. pres. pass. ind. of *ἐκχέω*, agreeing most immediately with *ὕδωρ* though applying to the whole series. *δι’ ἀέρος ζοφώδους* follows the verb, unlike *per l’ aer tenebroso*; elided *διά* takes the genitive of the medium traversed, and *ζοφώδους* (from *ζοφώδης*) agrees with *ἀέρος* (from *ἀήρ*).

> 12 pute la terra che questo riceve.  

the ground, on which it falls, emits a putrid smell.

> 12 Ἡ δὲ βρεχομένη γῆ βρῶμον ἐξόζει.  
> (And the drenched earth sends forth a stench.)

- *ἡ δὲ βρεχομένη γῆ* ‘and the ground being drenched’ puts the subject first, adds connective *δέ*, and replaces the active relative *che questo riceve* with an attributive pres. pass. participle of *βρέχω* (nom. fem. sg.). The Italian object *questo* disappears with the change of voice.
- *βρῶμον ἐξόζει* ‘gives off a stench’ expands *pute* into a verb and an accusative of the odour emitted: *βρῶμον* is from *βρῶμος*, *ἐξόζει* the 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἐξόζω*.

> 13 Cerbero, fiera crudele e diversa,  
> 14 con tre gole caninamente latra  
> 15 sovra la gente che quivi è sommersa.  

Cerberus, a monster fierce and strange, with three throats, barks dog-like over those that are immersed in it.

> 13 Κέρβερος, ἀλλόκοτος θὴρ ἀπηνής τε,  
> (Cerberus, a monstrous and ruthless beast,)
>
> 14 Τρισὶν ὑλακτεῖ χάσμασιν, ὡσεὶ κύων,  
> (Barks with three mouths, like a dog,)
>
> 15 Πρὸς τὴν πληθὺν τὴν ἐκεῖ βεβυθισμένην.  
> (At the multitude submerged there.)

- *Κέρβερος, ἀλλόκοτος θὴρ ἀπηνής τε* keeps the nominative apposition of *Cerbero, fiera* but puts ‘strange’ (*ἀλλόκοτος*, for *diversa*) first and ‘ruthless’ (*ἀπηνής*, for *crudele*) last. Enclitic *τε* after the second adjective coordinates it with the first.
- *τρισὶν ὑλακτεῖ χάσμασιν* ‘barks with three gaping mouths’ splits the instrumental dative around the 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. *ὑλακτεῖ* (contracted from *ὑλακτέω*). *τρισίν* is the dative of *τρεῖς*, *χάσμασιν* the dat. pl. of *χάσμα*, an opening or gape rather than specifically a throat. *ὡσεὶ κύων* ‘as if a dog’ makes the Italian adverb *caninamente* a comparison, with *κύων* nominative like the subject.
- *πρὸς τὴν πληθὺν* ‘at the multitude’ renders *sovra la gente* as the direction of the barking, not the position of the beast. *τὴν ἐκεῖ βεβυθισμένην* is in the second attributive position, with the article repeated: *ἐκεῖ* retains *quivi*, and *βεβυθισμένην* (acc. fem. sg. perf. pass. participle of *βυθίζω*, with reduplication) expresses the submerged state of *πληθύν* in place of *che … è sommersa*.

> 16 Gli occhi ha vermigli, e la barba unta ed atra,  
> 17 e il ventre largo, e unghiate le mani;  

His eyes are red, his beard [greasy] and black, his belly wide, and clawed his hands;

> 16 Ὄμματ’ ἐρυθρά, πώγων’ ἔχει γλοιώδη,  
> (He has red eyes, a slimy beard,)
>
> 17 Γαστέρ’ εὐρεῖαν καὶ γαμψώνυχας χεῖρας·  
> (A broad belly, and talon-clawed hands;)

- *ὄμματ’ ἐρυθρά, πώγων’ ἔχει γλοιώδη* places *ἔχει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἔχω*) in the second pair, not the first as in *Gli occhi ha vermigli*. It governs the whole list. *ὄμματ’* is elided *ὄμματα* (acc. neut. pl. of *ὄμμα*), *πώγων’* elided *πώγωνα* (acc. masc. sg. of *πώγων*). *γλοιώδη* (acc. masc. sg. of *γλοιώδης*) gives *unta* as ‘slimy’; *atra* has no counterpart.
- *γαστέρ’ εὐρεῖαν καὶ γαμψώνυχας χεῖρας* retains the Italian noun–adjective order for the belly and adjective–noun order for the hands, but keeps only the last conjunction. *γαστέρ’* is elided *γαστέρα*, acc. sg. of *γαστήρ*; *εὐρεῖαν* is the fem. acc. sg. of *εὐρύς*. *γαμψώνυχας* (acc. fem. pl. of *γαμψῶνυξ*, agreeing with *χεῖρας*, from *χείρ*) specifies ‘with hooked claws’ for *unghiate*.

> 18 graffia gli spirti, gli scuoia, ed isquatra.  

he clutches the spirits, flays, and piecemeal rends them.

> 18 Σχίζει τὰ πνεύματ’, ἐκδέρει καὶ μελίζει,  
> (He rends the spirits, flays and quarters them,)

- *σχίζει τὰ πνεύματ’, ἐκδέρει καὶ μελίζει* follows the order of verb, object, and two further verbs. All three verbs are 3rd sg. pres. act. indicatives: *σχίζω* ‘split, rend’ is stronger than *graffia* ‘scratches’; *ἐκδέρω* corresponds closely to *scuoia*; *μελίζω* ‘cut limb from limb’ gives the sense of *isquatra* without specifying quarters. *τὰ πνεύματ’* (elided *πνεύματα*, acc. neut. pl. of *πνεῦμα*) serves all three verbs without a repeated object pronoun for *gli*.

> 19 Urlar gli fa la pioggia come cani;  

The rain makes them howl like dogs;

> 19 Ποιεῖ δ’ αὔτ’ ὄμβρος ὠρύειν, ὥσπερ κύνες·  
> (And the rain makes them howl like dogs;)

- *ποιεῖ δ’ αὔτ’ ὄμβρος ὠρύειν* retains the causative construction of *Urlar gli fa la pioggia*, but moves the infinitive from first to last. *ποιεῖ* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ποιέω*) takes an accusative and infinitive: *αὔτ’* is elided *αὐτά*, neuter plural referring back to *τὰ πνεύματα*, and is the subject of *ὠρύειν* (pres. act. inf. of *ὠρύω*). *ὄμβρος*, without an article, is the subject of *ποιεῖ*; elided *δ’* adds a connective.
- *ὥσπερ κύνες* ‘just as dogs do’ keeps *come cani*. *κύνες* (nom. pl. of *κύων*) is the subject of an understood finite verb of howling, not an accusative in agreement with *αὔτ’*.

> 20 dell’ un de’ lati fanno all’ altro schermo;  

with one side they screen the other;

> 20 Ἀμύνονται θἀτέρου πλευροῦ θἀτέρῳ·  
> (They shield one side with the other;)

- *ἀμύνονται* (3rd pl. pres. mid. ind. of *ἀμύνω*) recasts *fanno … schermo* ‘make a screen’ as ‘defend themselves’. *θἀτέρου πλευροῦ θἀτέρῳ* juxtaposes the side protected and the side used for protection: the former is genitive, the latter an instrumental dative with *πλευρῷ* understood. The genitive is compressed: where Italian has *all’ altro* for the beneficiary, Greek gives the side in respect of which they defend themselves, not a straightforward accusative object of ‘shield’.
- *θἀτέρου* and *θἀτέρῳ* are the gen. and dat. sg. of the crasis form *θάτερος* (article plus *ἕτερος*), here masculine with *πλευροῦ* (from *πλευρόν* or masculine *πλευρός*; the oblique forms do not distinguish gender). The repeated ‘other’ distributes the two sides as ‘one … the other’.

> 21 volgonsi spesso i miseri profani.  

they often turn themselves, the impious wretches.

> 21 Οἱ δυστυχεῖς βέβηλοι στρέφοντ’ ἀπαύστως.  
> (The wretched profane ones turn unceasingly.)

- *οἱ δυστυχεῖς βέβηλοι* brings the subject *i miseri profani* forward. The article and both adjectives are nom. masc. pl.; *δυστυχεῖς* (from *δυστυχής*) qualifies *βέβηλοι* (from *βέβηλος*, used substantivally), ‘the unfortunate profane ones’.
- *στρέφοντ’ ἀπαύστως* ‘turn themselves unceasingly’ uses the present middle for reflexive *volgonsi*. *στρέφοντ’* represents *στρέφονται* (3rd pl. pres. mid. ind. of *στρέφω*), with poetic elision of final *-αι* before a vowel. *ἀπαύστως* intensifies *spesso*: the Italian says ‘often’, not ‘without cease’.

## Lines 22–54 — Cerberus Silenced and Ciacco Named

> 22 Quando ci scorse Cerbero, il gran vermo,  
> 23 le bocche aperse, e mostrocci le sanne;  

When Cerberus, the great Worm, perceived us, he opened his mouths and shewed his tusks:

> 22 Ὡς δ’ ἡμᾶς εἶδε Κέρβερος βρωτὴρ μέγας,  
> (And when Cerberus, the great devourer, saw us,)
>
> 23 Χάσματ’ ἤνοιξε, χαυλιόδοντας φαίνων·  
> (He opened his gaping jaws, showing his tusks;)

- *Ὡς δ’ ἡμᾶς εἶδε Κέρβερος* keeps the order of *Quando ci scorse Cerbero*: temporal *ὡς* ‘when’, the object *ἡμᾶς*, the verb, then the subject. Postpositive *δέ*, elided before the pronoun, links the sentence to what precedes. *Εἶδε* is the 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*, ‘saw’.
- *Βρωτὴρ μέγας* ‘a great devourer’ stands in nominative apposition to *Κέρβερος*. For *il gran vermo* Musurus substitutes an agent noun, *βρωτήρ*, characterizing Cerberus by what he does rather than by the creature Dante calls him.
- *Χάσματ’ ἤνοιξε* retains the fronted object of *le bocche aperse*: *χάσματ’* is elided *χάσματα*, acc. pl. of *χάσμα*, ‘gaping openings’; *ἤνοιξε* is the 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀνοίγω*. The second finite verb of *e mostrocci le sanne* becomes *χαυλιόδοντας φαίνων*, ‘displaying tusks’: *φαίνων* is a simultaneous pres. act. participle, nom. sg. masc., with the acc. pl. of *χαυλιόδους*. The second *ci*, ‘to us’, is not repeated.

> 24 non avea membro che tenesse fermo.  

no limb of him kept still.

> 24 Αὐτοῦ δ’ ἄτρομον οὐδὲν ἔμεινε μέλος.  
> (And no limb of him remained unquivering.)

- *Αὐτοῦ δ’ ἄτρομον οὐδὲν ἔμεινε μέλος* ‘and of him not one limb remained untrembling’ replaces possessive *non avea membro* and its relative clause *che tenesse fermo* with a single indicative clause. Fronted *αὐτοῦ* is possessive genitive; *οὐδέν* agrees with the delayed neut. sg. subject *μέλος*. *Ἄτρομον* is predicative, not a second modifier meaning ‘no untrembling limb’: it states the condition in which no limb remained. *Ἔμεινε* is the 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *μένω*, against the Italian imperfect and imperfect subjunctive.

> 25 E il duca mio distese le sue spanne,  
> 26 prese la terra, e con piene le pugna  
> 27 la gittò dentro alle bramose canne.  

My Guide, spreading his palms, took up earth; and, with full fists, cast it into his ravening gullets.

> 25 Ὁ δ’ ἡγήτωρ μου χερσὶν ἀνεῳγμέναις  
> (And my guide, with outspread hands,)
>
> 26 Λαβὼν ἐκ τῆς γῆς χῶμα δραξὶν ἀφθόνοις,  
> (Taking earth from the ground in ample handfuls,)
>
> 27 Ἔῤῥιψεν ἐντὸς τῶν παμβόρων φαρύγγων.  
> (Cast it into the all-devouring gullets.)

- *Ὁ δ’ ἡγήτωρ μου* ‘and my guide’ renders *E il duca mio*, with elided postpositive *δέ* and enclitic possessive *μου*. The finite *distese le sue spanne* becomes *χερσὶν ἀνεῳγμέναις*, ‘with hands spread open’. *Χερσίν* is dat. pl. of *χείρ*, with movable *ν*; *ἀνεῳγμέναις*, dat. pl. fem. perfect middle/passive participle of *ἀνοίγω*, describes the resulting position of the hands rather than the act of spreading them. The possessive is understood.
- *Λαβών* (nom. sg. masc. second aor. act. participle of *λαμβάνω*) subordinates *prese* to the eventual main verb: ‘having taken’. Greek distinguishes the source, *ἐκ τῆς γῆς* ‘from the ground’, from the material taken, acc. *χῶμα* ‘earth’. *Δραξὶν ἀφθόνοις* ‘in unstinted handfuls’ is a dative of manner or measure (*δράξ*, gen. *δρακός*); attached to the taking, it anticipates the quantity conveyed by *con piene le pugna*. The hands and handfuls are plural, not dual.
- *Ἔῤῥιψεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ῥίπτω*) alone carries the main assertion. The spelling shows the doubled *ρ* after the augment, traditionally written with smooth and rough breathings. Its object *χῶμα* is understood, where Italian resumes *la terra* with *la*. *Ἐντός* governs the genitive *τῶν παμβόρων φαρύγγων* (*φάρυγξ*, gen. *φάρυγγος*); with a verb of throwing, ‘inside’ gives the destination. *Παμβόρων* ‘all-devouring’ replaces the desire expressed by *bramose* with a capacity for eating.

> 28 Qual è quel cane che abbaiando agugna,  
> 29 e si racqueta poi che il pasto morde,  
> 30 chè solo a divorarlo intende e pugna:  

As the dog, that barking craves, and grows quiet when he bites his food, for he strains and battles only to devour it:

> 28 Ὡς κύων πειναλέος ὑλακτῶν χάσκει,  
> (As a hungry dog gapes while barking,)
>
> 29 Πραΰνεται δ’, ὅτ’ ἄγραν ὀδοῦσι δάκνει,  
> (And grows calm when he bites his prey with his teeth,)
>
> 30 Ἀγωνιῶν ἕως ἂν αὐτὴν βροχθίσῃ,  
> (Striving until he gulps it down,)

- *Ὡς κύων … χάσκει* ‘as a dog … gapes’ makes *κύων* the subject of a finite comparison, without the demonstrative and relative of *Qual è quel cane che*. *Πειναλέος* ‘hungry’ supplies the appetite of *agugna*, while *χάσκει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *χάσκω*) gives it a visible action. *Ὑλακτῶν*, nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *ὑλακτέω*, closely matches the simultaneous gerund *abbaiando*.
- *Πραΰνεται δ’* ‘but grows calm’ uses the 3rd sg. pres. middle/passive of *πραΰνω* for reflexive *si racqueta*. Temporal *ὅτ’* is elided *ὅτε*; the present indicative *δάκνει* (*δάκνω*) describes the typical occasion, as does *morde*. *Ἄγραν* ‘prey’ replaces *il pasto*, and instrumental *ὀδοῦσι* (dat. pl. of *ὀδούς*) adds ‘with its teeth’.
- *Ἀγωνιῶν* (contracted nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *ἀγωνιάω*) gathers *intende e pugna* into ‘striving’, without an explicit causal conjunction for *chè*. *Ἕως ἂν … βροχθίσῃ* means ‘until it gulps it down’: *ἄν* with the 3rd sg. aor. act. subjunctive of *βροχθίζω* marks the prospective limit of the striving. This is a temporal limit, not a literal rendering of *solo a* plus the infinitive of purpose. Feminine *αὐτήν* refers to *ἄγραν*, where *-lo* refers to masculine *pasto*.

> 31 cotai se fecer quelle facce lorde  
> 32 dello demonio Cerbero che introna  
> 33 l'anime sì, ch'esser vorrebber sorde.  

so did those squalid visages of Cerberus the Demon, who thunders on the spirits so, that they would fain be deaf.

> 31 Οὕτως ἔπραξαν αἱ κακόσχημοι γνάθοι  
> (So did the foul jaws)
>
> 32 Τοῦ Κερβέρου δαίμονος, ὠτοκοποῦσαι  
> (Of the demon Cerberus, which deafen)
>
> 33 Τὰς ψυχάς, ὥστε ποθεῖν κωφὰς γενέσθαι.  
> (The souls, so that they long to become deaf.)

- *Οὕτως ἔπραξαν* ‘thus acted’ answers *ὡς*: adverb plus 3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *πράττω* replaces predicative *cotai* and reflexive *se fecer*, ‘such did they become’. *Αἱ κακόσχημοι γνάθοι* ‘the ill-favoured jaws’ narrows *quelle facce lorde* to the jaws and conveys ugliness rather than specifically dirt. *Κακόσχημοι* is the feminine plural of a two-termination adjective; the article replaces the demonstrative.
- *Τοῦ Κερβέρου δαίμονος* puts the name first, with *δαίμονος* in genitive apposition. Where *che introna* has a singular verb referring to Cerberus, *ὠτοκοποῦσαι* ‘battering the ears’ (nom. pl. fem. pres. act. participle of *ὠτοκοπέω*) agrees with the jaws. *Τὰς ψυχάς* is its direct object.
- *Ὥστε ποθεῖν κωφὰς γενέσθαι* expresses the result by *ὥστε* plus infinitive, without a separate degree word for *sì*. The souls are the understood subject of *ποθεῖν* (pres. act. inf. of *ποθέω*) and of its complement *γενέσθαι* (second aor. mid. inf. of *γίγνομαι*). Predicate *κωφάς* is accordingly acc. pl. fem. Greek gives ‘long to **become** deaf’ rather than the conditional wish *esser vorrebber sorde*.

> 34 Noi passavam su per l'ombre, che adona  
> 35 la greve pioggia, e ponevam le piante  
> 36 sopra lor vanità, che par persona.  

We passed over the shadows whom the heavy rain subdues; and placed our soles upon their emptiness, which seems a body.

> 34 Ἐβαίνομεν ἐπ᾿ αὐτῶν, πιεζομένων  
> (We walked over them, weighed down)
>
> 35 Ὑπ᾿ ὄμβρου δεινοῦ, πατοῦντες ὑπὸ πόδας  
> (By the dreadful rain, treading under foot)
>
> 36 Τὰ φάσματ᾿ αὐτῶν, σχῆμ᾿ ἔχοντα σωμάτων.  
> (Their phantoms, having the semblance of bodies.)

- *Ἐβαίνομεν ἐπ᾿ αὐτῶν* ‘we were walking over them’ keeps the imperfect of *passavam* (1st pl. imperf. act. ind. of *βαίνω*), but uses a pronoun referring back to the souls rather than naming *l’ombre*. Elided *ἐπί* with the genitive indicates the surface traversed.
- *Πιεζομένων ὑπ᾿ ὄμβρου δεινοῦ* ‘being pressed down by dreadful rain’ reverses the active *che adona la greve pioggia*. *Πιεζομένων* is the pres. passive participle of *πιέζω*, gen. pl. in agreement with *αὐτῶν*, not a genitive absolute. *Ὑπό* with the genitive marks the force acting on them; *δεινοῦ* gives dreadfulness rather than weight.
- *Πατοῦντες* (nom. pl. masc. pres. act. participle of *πατέω*) makes *e ponevam* a simultaneous action of the walkers. *Ὑπὸ πόδας* ‘underfoot’ has the accusative after *ὑπό*; the feet are no longer the object as *le piante* was. The object of treading is *τὰ φάσματ᾿ αὐτῶν* ‘their apparitions’ (*φάσματ᾿* for *φάσματα*), a concrete plural for abstract singular *lor vanità*.
- *Σχῆμ᾿ ἔχοντα σωμάτων* ‘having the form of bodies’ replaces finite *che par persona*. *Ἔχοντα* is acc. pl. neut. pres. act. participle agreeing with *φάσματα*, not a singular referring to the walker; its object is elided *σχῆμα*, qualified by the gen. pl. *σωμάτων*.

> 37 Elle giacean per terra tutte quante,  
> 38 fuor d'una che a seder si levò, ratto  
> 39 ch'ella ci vide passarsi davante.  

They all were lying on the ground save one, who sat up forthwith when he saw us pass before him.

> 37 Μίγδην κατὰ γῆς ἔκειντ᾽, ἐξαιρουμένης  
> (All together on the ground they lay, except)
>
> 38 Μιᾶς, ἥτις ἀναστᾶσ᾽ ἐκάθισ᾽, ἅμα  
> (One, which rose and sat up, as soon)
>
> 39 Ὡς εἶδεν ἡμᾶς διαβαίνοντας πρόσθεν.  
> (As it saw us passing before it.)

- *Μίγδην κατὰ γῆς ἔκειντ᾽* ‘in a mingled mass on the ground they lay’ substitutes the adverb *μίγδην* for the explicit totality of *tutte quante*. *Κατὰ γῆς* is local; *ἔκειντ᾽* is elided *ἔκειντο*, 3rd pl. imperfect of *κεῖμαι*, a middle-form verb meaning ‘lie’. No subject pronoun corresponds to *Elle*.
- *Ἐξαιρουμένης μιᾶς* ‘one being excepted’ renders *fuor d’una* by a genitive absolute. The gen. sg. fem. pres. passive participle of *ἐξαιρέω* agrees with *μιᾶς* (from *εἷς, μία, ἕν*), feminine as of a *ψυχή*. *Ἥτις*, nom. sg. fem. of the compound relative *ὅστις*, resumes this one as subject.
- *Ἀναστᾶσ᾽ ἐκάθισ᾽* divides *a seder si levò* into ‘having risen, sat down’: elided *ἀναστᾶσα* is the feminine intransitive second aor. act. participle of *ἀνίστημι*; *ἐκάθισ᾽* is elided *ἐκάθισε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *καθίζω*. *Ἅμα ὡς* ‘as soon as’ carries *ratto ch’* across the line break.
- *Εἶδεν ἡμᾶς διαβαίνοντας πρόσθεν* uses the Greek perception construction, accusative object plus supplementary participle: ‘saw us passing in front’. *Διαβαίνοντας* is acc. pl. masc. pres. act. of *διαβαίνω*, against Italian *passarsi* in the infinitive; the person before whom they pass is implicit in adverbial *πρόσθεν*.

> 40 “O tu, che se' per questo inferno tratto,”  
> 41 mi disse, “riconoscimi, se sai;  
> 42 tu fosti, prima ch'io disfatto, fatto.”  

“O thou, who through this Hell art led,” he said to me, “recognise me if thou mayest; thou wast made before I was unmade.”

> 40 “Ὦ σύ,” λέγει μοι, “διαβαίνων δι᾽ ᾍδου,  
> (“O you,” it says to me, “who pass through Hell,)
>
> 41 „Ἀναγνώρισόν μ᾽, εἰ τοῦτο δυνατόν σοι·  
> („Recognize me, if this is possible for you;)
>
> 42 „Ὑπῆρξας δὴ σὺ πρὸ τοῦ παῦσαί μ᾽ ὑπάρχειν.”  
> („For indeed you came into being before I ceased to be.”)

- *Ὦ σύ* retains the direct address; *λέγει μοι* moves *mi disse* into it and changes the aoristic past to a historical present (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *λέγω*, with dative recipient). *Διαβαίνων δι᾽ ᾍδου* replaces the passive relative *che se’ per questo inferno tratto* with an active pres. participle agreeing with *σύ*: ‘passing through Hades’. *Διά* is elided before the genitive of the name; there is no demonstrative for *questo*.
- *Ἀναγνώρισόν μ᾽* is the 2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *ἀναγνωρίζω* with elided enclitic *με*; the additional acute on the imperative accompanies the enclitic. *Εἰ τοῦτο δυνατόν σοι* ‘if this is possible for you’ interprets *se sai* as ability rather than knowledge. *Δυνατόν* is predicate to neuter *τοῦτο*, with *ἐστί* understood and *σοι* dative of the person concerned.
- *Ὑπῆρξας δὴ σύ* ‘you indeed came into being’ is the 2nd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ὑπάρχω*, with emphatic *δή* and expressed subject *σύ*. *Πρὸ τοῦ παῦσαί μ᾽ ὑπάρχειν* translates *prima ch’io disfatto* with *πρό* and a genitive articular infinitive: *τοῦ* marks the case of the whole infinitival expression, *μ᾽* its accusative subject. *Παῦσαι* is aor. **active** inf. of *παύω*, used here for ‘cease’, where ordinary Attic would favour middle *παύεσθαι* with a participle. *Ὑπάρχειν* is pres. act. inf., ‘to exist’. Musurus thus answers *disfatto, fatto* with two forms of *ὑπάρχω*, but makes the contrast one of beginning and ceasing, not of passive making and unmaking.

> 43 Ed io a lei: “L'angoscia che tu hai  
> 44 forse ti tira fuor della mia mente,  
> 45 sì che non par ch'io ti vedessi mai.  

And I to him: “The anguish which thou hast, perhaps withdraws thee from my memory, so that it seems not as if I ever saw thee.

> 43 Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν· “Τἄχθος, ὃ νῦν αἰσθάνῃ,  
> (And I to him: “The anguish which you now feel)
>
> 44 „Ἴσως ἐξαλείφει σε τῆς ἐμῆς μνήμης,  
> („Perhaps blots you out from my memory,)
>
> 45 „Ὥστε σε μηδεπώποτ᾽ ἰδεῖν δοκεῖ μοι.  
> („So that it seems to me I have never seen you.)

- *Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν* ‘and I to him’ keeps the ellipsis of a verb of speaking in *Ed io a lei*. *Κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*; the masculine object of *πρός* anticipates the personal gender of the speaker, where *lei* still refers to the shade as feminine.
- *Τἄχθος* is crasis of *τὸ ἄχθος*, ‘the burden, distress’. In *ὃ νῦν αἰσθάνῃ*, the neut. sg. relative is accusative object of *αἰσθάνῃ* (2nd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *αἰσθάνομαι*, a middle-form verb of perception). Thus *che tu hai* becomes ‘which you now feel’, with the time adverb added.
- *Ἴσως ἐξαλείφει σε τῆς ἐμῆς μνήμης* keeps the qualification *forse* but turns *ti tira fuor* into ‘effaces you’. *Ἐξαλείφει* is 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἐξαλείφω*; the bare genitive expresses separation, helped by the prefix *ἐξ-*, where Italian has *fuor della*. *Ἐμῆς* is an attributive possessive adjective.
- *Ὥστε … δοκεῖ μοι* gives the result as a finite assertion, ‘so that it seems to me’. Within it, *σε μηδεπώποτ᾽ ἰδεῖν* means ‘never yet to have seen you’: *σε* is object of *ἰδεῖν*, second aor. act. inf. of *ὁράω*, whose understood subject is the person indicated by *μοι*. Elided *μηδεπώποτε* negates the infinitive. Italian instead negates *par* and leaves *ch’io ti vedessi mai* dependent on it.

> 46 Ma dimmi chi tu se', che in sì dolente  
> 47 luogo se' messa, ed a sì fatta pena,  
> 48 che s'altra è maggio, nulla è sì spiacente.”  

But tell me who art thou, that art put in such a doleful place, and in such punishment; that, though other may be greater, none is so displeasing.”

> 46 „Ἀλλά μοι σύ, τίς εἶ, λέγ᾽, ὃς ἐβυθίσθης  
> („But tell me, who are you, who have been plunged)
>
> 47 „Ἐν τόπῳ τόσον δυστήνῳ καὶ βασάνοις,  
> („In a place so wretched and into torments)
>
> 48 „Ὧν, εἴγ᾽ ἦν μείζων, οὐδεμί᾽ ἐστι χείρων.”  
> („Than which, even if there were a greater, none is more loathsome.”)

- *Ἀλλά μοι σύ, τίς εἶ, λέγ᾽* distributes *Ma dimmi chi tu se’* around the direct question *τίς εἶ* ‘who are you?’: recipient *μοι* comes first, then emphatic *σύ*, with the imperative postponed. *Λέγ᾽* is elided *λέγε*, 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative; *εἶ* is the 2nd sg. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*.
- *Ὃς ἐβυθίσθης* ‘who were plunged’ has a masculine nominative relative referring to *σύ* and hence a second-person verb. *Ἐβυθίσθης* is 2nd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *βυθίζω*: an event rather than the state *se’ messa*, and a more specific image than ‘put’. *Ἐν* governs both *τόπῳ* and *βασάνοις*, making the place and the torments parallel datives, against the change from *in* to *ad* in Italian. *Τόσον* functions adverbially, ‘so’, with *δυστήνῳ*, dat. sg. masc. agreeing with *τόπῳ*; Italian singular *pena* becomes plural *βασάνοις*.
- *Ὧν* is a gen. pl. relative referring to the torments and giving the standard of comparison: ‘than which’. *Εἴγ᾽ ἦν μείζων* ‘if indeed there was a greater one’ uses *εἰ* strengthened by elided *γε*, with imperfect indicative *ἦν* of *εἰμί*, not an optative. Feminine *βάσανος* is understood with comparative *μείζων* and with *οὐδεμί᾽* (elided *οὐδεμία*). *Οὐδεμί᾽ ἐστι χείρων* says ‘none is worse’: the comparative of quality replaces *nulla è sì spiacente*, which distinguishes displeasingness from magnitude more explicitly.

> 49 Ed egli a me: “La tua città, ch'è piena  
> 50 d'invidia sì, che già trabocca il sacco,  
> 51 seco mi tenne in la vita serena.  

And he to me: “Thy city, which is so full of envy that the sack already overflows, contained me in the clear life.

> 49 Ὁ δέ φησιν· “Ἐν τῇ σῇ πόλει τῇ πλήρει  
> (And he says: “In your city, which is full)
>
> 50 „Ὑπερπλεονάζοντος ἀφθόνου φθόνου  
> („Of superabundant, unsparing envy,)
>
> 51 „ᾬκουν ἐν ταῖς γαληναῖς ζωῆς ἡμέραις·  
> („I dwelt in the serene days of life;)

- *Ὁ δέ φησιν* ‘and he says’ uses the article pronominally with continuative *δέ*; *φησιν* is 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *φημί*. Greek supplies a historical present for the elliptical *Ed egli a me*, but does not express the recipient.
- *Ἐν τῇ σῇ πόλει τῇ πλήρει* turns *La tua città*, the subject of *seco mi tenne*, into a fronted locative: ‘in your city, the one full …’. *Σῇ* is the attributive possessive; the repeated article puts *πλήρει* (dat. sg. fem. of *πλήρης*) in second attributive position, replacing *ch’è piena*.
- *Ὑπερπλεονάζοντος ἀφθόνου φθόνου* is the genitive complement of *πλήρει*. *Ὑπερπλεονάζοντος* is gen. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *ὑπερπλεονάζω*, ‘being present to excess’; *ἀφθόνου* ‘unstinted, abundant’ plays on the sound of *φθόνου* ‘envy’. Excess is thus predicated of the envy itself: the result clause *sì, che già trabocca il sacco*, including the sack and *già*, disappears.
- *ᾬκουν* ‘I was dwelling’ is here 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of contracted *οἰκέω*, with temporal augment of initial *οι-* to *ῳ-*. The Italian object *mi* has become the subject, and the city its place of residence. *Ἐν ταῖς γαληναῖς ζωῆς ἡμέραις* expands *in la vita serena* to ‘in the serene days of life’: *γαληναῖς* agrees with *ἡμέραις*, while the intervening *ζωῆς* is a dependent genitive.

> 52 Voi, cittadini, mi chiamaste Ciacco:  

You, citizens, called me Ciacco:

> 52 „Πολῖται δ᾽ ὑμεῖς Κίακόν μ᾽ ἐκαλεῖτε.  
> („And you citizens called me Ciacco.)

- *Πολῖται δ᾽ ὑμεῖς* reverses *Voi, cittadini*, placing ‘citizens’ first and adding connective *δέ*. *Πολῖται* can be read as a vocative address (identical in form to the nominative plural); *ὑμεῖς* is the explicit nominative subject.
- *Κίακόν μ᾽ ἐκαλεῖτε* has two accusatives, object *μ᾽* (elided *με*) and predicative name *Κίακον*, placed first. The second acute on *Κίακόν* is occasioned by the enclitic. *Ἐκαλεῖτε* is 2nd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *καλέω*: ‘you used to call me’, making customary naming explicit where *chiamaste* is a simple past.

> 53 per la dannosa colpa della gola,  
> 54 come tu vedi, alla pioggia mi fiacco;  

for the baneful crime of gluttony, as thou seest, I languish in the rain;

> 53 „ Διὰ τὸ κακὸν πάθος τῆς λαιμαργίας  
> („ On account of the evil vice of gluttony)
>
> 54 „ Κατακόπτομ᾽, ὡς ὁρᾷς, ἐνθάδ᾽ ὑπ᾽ ὄμβρου.  
> („ I am battered, as you see, here by the rain.)

- *Διὰ τὸ κακὸν πάθος τῆς λαιμαργίας* renders causal *per* by *διά* with the accusative. *Πάθος* ‘affection, passion’, qualified by *κακόν* ‘bad’, interprets *dannosa colpa* as a vicious appetite rather than explicitly as guilt or harm done. The gen. sg. *τῆς λαιμαργίας* specifies what the passion consists in, following *della gola* but naming gluttony directly.
- *Κατακόπτομ᾽* is elided *κατακόπτομαι*, 1st sg. pres. middle/passive ind. of *κατακόπτω*, passive in context: ‘I am battered’. This stands first in the clause, where Italian delays *mi fiacco* to the end. Parenthetic *ὡς ὁρᾷς* ‘as you see’ retains *come tu vedi*, with the subject expressed by the ending of the contracted 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ὁράω*.
- *Ἐνθάδ᾽ ὑπ᾽ ὄμβρου* (elided *ἐνθάδε*, *ὑπό*) adds local ‘here’ but makes the rain the force doing the battering, by *ὑπό* with the genitive. Thus *alla pioggia* is not simply reproduced as the location of the suffering.

## Lines 55–87 — Ciacco on Discord and the Worthy

> 55 ed io anima trista non son sola,  
> 56 chè tutte queste a simil pena stanno  
> 57 per simil colpa”; e più non fe’ parola.  

and I, wretched spirit, am not alone, since all these for like crime are in like punishment”; and more he said not.

> 55 „ Ἐγὼ δὲ ψυχὴ τάλαιν᾽ οὔκ εἰμι μόνη·  
> („ Yet I am not the only wretched soul;)
>
> 56 „ Τὴν αὐτὴν ποινὴν τίνουσιν αὗται πᾶσαι  
> („ All these pay the same penalty)
>
> 57 „ Δι᾽ ἁμάρτημα ταὐτόν·” κ᾽ οὐδὲν πλεῖν εἶπεν.  
> („ For the very same sin.” And nothing more he said.)

- *Ἐγὼ δὲ ψυχὴ τάλαιν᾽ οὔκ εἰμι μόνη* follows *ed io anima trista non son sola* closely. The explicit subject *ἐγώ* is set against the others by postpositive *δέ*; *ψυχή* is in nominative apposition, *τάλαιν᾽* is *τάλαινα* (fem. nom. sg. of *τάλας*) with final vowel elided, and *μόνη* is feminine in agreement with *ψυχή*. The accented negative in *οὔκ εἰμι* precedes the enclitic copula.
- Musurus leaves causal *chè* to be inferred by juxtaposition. In place of static *a simil pena stanno*, he fronts the object of the idiom *ποινὴν τίνειν*, ‘pay a penalty’: *τὴν αὐτὴν ποινὴν τίνουσιν* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *τίνω*). *Αὗται πᾶσαι*, ‘all these’, is a feminine plural subject, picking up *ψυχή*. Both instances of *simil* become identity rather than likeness: *τὴν αὐτήν*, ‘the same’, and *δι᾽ ἁμάρτημα ταὐτόν*, ‘on account of the same offence’ (*διά* with accusative; *ταὐτόν* by crasis from *τὸ αὐτό*).
- *Κ᾽ οὐδὲν πλεῖν εἶπεν*, ‘and he said nothing more’, replaces *non fe’ parola* with a verb of saying. *Κ᾽* is a poetic shortening of *καί* before a vowel; *πλεῖν* is the comparative ‘more’ (a form of *πλέον*), not an infinitive here. *Εἶπεν* is 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *λέγω*.

> 58 Io gli risposi: “Ciacco, il tuo affanno  
> 59 mi pesa sì, ch’ a lagrimar m’ invita;  

I answered him : “ Ciacco, thy sore distress weighs upon me so, that it bids me weep;

> 58 Ἀπεκρίθην· “ Κίακε, τὸ σὸν μὲν ἄλγος  
> (I answered: “Ciacco, your grief indeed)
>
> 59 „ Λυπεῖ με λίαν κ᾽ εἰς δάκρυα κινεῖ με.  
> („ Pains me exceedingly and moves me to tears.)

- *Ἀπεκρίθην* alone renders *Io gli risposi*: the person is in the ending, and no dative for *gli* is expressed. It is 1st sg. aorist passive in form, active in sense, of *ἀποκρίνομαι*. *Κίακε* is the vocative of the Hellenized name.
- *Τὸ σὸν μὲν ἄλγος*, ‘your pain’, is the neuter singular subject of two present active indicatives, *λυπεῖ* (*λυπέω*) and *κινεῖ* (*κινέω*), each with its own accusative *με*. *Λυπεῖ με λίαν*, ‘grieves me exceedingly’, drops the weight metaphor of *mi pesa sì*; *κ᾽ εἰς δάκρυα κινεῖ με*, ‘and moves me to tears’, replaces the result clause *ch’ a lagrimar m’ invita* with coordination and a noun of result. *Μέν* prepares the turn to *δέ* in the next request.

> 60 ma dimmi, se tu sai, a che verranno  
> 61 li cittadin della città partita?  

but tell me, if thou canst, what the citizens of the divided city shall come to?

> 60 „ Εἰπέ μοι δ᾽, εἰς τί καταντήσουσι τέλος  
> („ But tell me, to what end will come)
>
> 61 „ Τῆς διμεροῦς πόλεως οἱ νῦν πολῖται·  
> („ The present citizens of the divided city;)

- *Εἰπέ μοι δ᾽* gives ‘but tell me’ (*εἰπέ*, 2nd sg. second aor. act. imperative of *λέγω*; *μοι*, dative of recipient; *δ᾽* for *δέ*). There is no Greek clause for *se tu sai*. Instead *εἰς τί καταντήσουσι τέλος* asks ‘to what will they come at last?’: *καταντήσουσι* is 3rd pl. fut. act. ind. of *καταντάω*, with *εἰς τί* expressing the outcome and *τέλος* readable as an adverbial accusative, ‘in the end’.
- As in Italian, the subject comes after the verb; within it Musurus reverses the order, *τῆς διμεροῦς πόλεως οἱ νῦν πολῖται*, ‘of the bipartite city the present citizens’. *Διμεροῦς* is gen. sg. fem. of *διμερής*; attributive *νῦν* between article and noun adds a qualification absent from *li cittadin*.

> 62 s’ alcun v’ è giusto; e dimmi la cagione,  
> 63 perchè l’ ha tanta discordia assalita.”  

if any one in it be just; and tell me the reason why such discord has assailed it.”

> 62 „ Εἴ τις ἐκεῖ δίκαιός ἐστι, καὶ πόθεν  
> („ Whether any one there is just, and whence)
>
> 63 „ Διχόνοι᾽ αὐτὴν κατέλαβε τοσαύτη.”  
> („ So great a discord has seized upon it.”)

- *Εἴ τις ἐκεῖ δίκαιός ἐστι* is an indirect yes-or-no question still dependent on *εἰπέ*: *εἰ* means ‘whether’, *τις* is indefinite ‘anyone’, *ἐκεῖ* renders locative *v’*, and *δίκαιος* is predicate nominative with the present indicative *ἐστι*. The Greek does not use an optative.
- *Καὶ πόθεν* coordinates a further indirect question, ‘and whence’, compressing *e dimmi la cagione, perchè* without repeating the imperative. *Διχόνοι᾽* is elided *διχόνοια*, subject of *κατέλαβε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *καταλαμβάνω*, with augment inside the compound), ‘seized’, for the Italian perfect *ha ... assalita*. *Αὐτήν* is the city as accusative object; *τοσαύτη*, ‘so great’, agrees with the subject but is placed emphatically at the end, unlike *tanta discordia*.

> 64 Ed egli a me: “Dopo lunga tenzone  
> 65 verranno al sangue, e la parte selvaggia  
> 66 caccerà l’ altra con molta offensione.  

And he to me : “ After long contention, they shall come to blood, and the party of the woods shall expel the other with much offence.

> 64 Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ· “ Μετὰ μακροὺς ἀγῶνας  
> (And he to me: “After long struggles)
>
> 65 „ Εἰς αἵματ᾽ ἐλεύσονται· τἄγριον μέρος  
> („ They will come to bloodshed; the savage party)
>
> 66 „ Ἐκδιώξει θἄτερον σὺν πολλῇ βλάβῃ.  
> („ Will drive out the other with much injury.)

- *Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ*, ‘and he to me’, uses pronominal *ὁ* with *δέ* and, like *Ed egli a me*, leaves the verb of speaking unexpressed. *Μετὰ μακροὺς ἀγῶνας* (*μετά* with accusative of time) makes singular *lunga tenzone* plural, ‘after long struggles’.
- *Εἰς αἵματ᾽ ἐλεύσονται* preserves the directional figure of *verranno al sangue*, but uses elided accusative plural *αἵματα* for bloodshed. *Ἐλεύσονται* is the suppletive future of *ἔρχομαι*, 3rd pl. middle in form. The next clause follows without a conjunction for *e*.
- *Τἄγριον μέρος* (crasis of *τὸ ἄγριον*) interprets *la parte selvaggia* adjectivally as ‘the wild party’, rather than explicitly as “the party of the woods.” *Ἐκδιώξει* (3rd sg. fut. act. ind. of *ἐκδιώκω*) takes *θἄτερον*, ‘the other [party]’, neuter accusative by crasis of *τὸ ἕτερον*, with aspiration of the stop. *Σὺν πολλῇ βλάβῃ* uses the dative of accompaniment or attendant circumstance: *offensione* is injury inflicted.

> 67 Poi appresso convien che questa caggia  
> 68 infra tre soli, e che l’ altra sormonti  
> 69 con la forza di tal, che testè piaggia.  

Then it behoves this to fall within three suns, and the other to prevail through the force of one who now keeps tacking.

> 67 „ Εἶτ᾽ ἀντιπαθεῖν τοῦτο πάλιν δεήσει  
> („ Then this one in turn must suffer the same)
>
> 68 „ Μεθ᾽ ἡλίους τρεῖς πρὸς θρίαμβον θἀτέρου  
> („ After three suns, for the triumph of the other)
>
> 69 „ Τῇ δυνάμει τινός, ὃς τὰ νῦν θωπεύει.  
> („ Through the power of one who now flatters.)

- *Εἶτ᾽* is elided *εἶτα*, ‘then’. Impersonal *δεήσει* (3rd sg. fut. act. ind. of *δέω* in the sense of *δεῖ*, ‘it is necessary’) replaces present *convien* and its subjunctive *caggia* with a future governing an infinitive. *Τοῦτο* is the neuter accusative subject of *ἀντιπαθεῖν* (second aor. act. inf. of *ἀντιπάσχω*), ‘suffer in return’; with *πάλιν* this makes the fall a reversal of the earlier injury.
- *Μεθ᾽ ἡλίους τρεῖς* means ‘after three suns’, not *infra tre soli*, “within three suns”; *μετά* loses its vowel and its stop is aspirated before rough-breathed *ἡλίους*. *Πρὸς θρίαμβον θἀτέρου*, ‘to the triumph of the other’, converts the coordinated *e che l’ altra sormonti* into a prepositional phrase of outcome. *Θἀτέρου* is the corresponding genitive by crasis, ‘of the other [party]’.
- Instrumental *τῇ δυνάμει* renders *con la forza*; possessive *τινός*, ‘of someone’, is the gen. sg. of indefinite *τις*. The relative *ὅς* is nominative as subject of *θωπεύει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *θωπεύω*). Musurus takes *piaggia* as ‘flatters’, not “keeps tacking”; adverbial *τὰ νῦν*, ‘at present’, renders *testè*.

> 70 Alto terrà lungo tempo le fronti,  
> 71 tenendo l’ altra sotto gravi pesi,  
> 72 come che di ciò pianga, e che ne adonti.  

It shall carry its front high for a long time, keeping the other under heavy burdens, however it may weep thereat and be ashamed.

> 70 „ Οἵδ᾽ ἐπὶ μακρὸν ἕξουσ᾽ ἄνω τὸ κέρας,  
> („ These for a long time shall hold their horn aloft,)
>
> 71 „ Κρατοῦντες ὑπὸ δεινὰ τοὺς ἄλλους ἄχθη,  
> („ Holding the others under grievous burdens,)
>
> 72 „ Καίπερ θρηνοῦντας καὶ καταισχυνομένους.  
> („ Although they lament and are put to shame.)

- *Οἵδ᾽* is elided *οἵδε*, nom. pl. masc. of *ὅδε*, ‘these men’: the members replace the singular party of *terrà*. Correspondingly *ἕξουσ᾽* is elided *ἕξουσι*, 3rd pl. fut. act. ind. of *ἔχω*. *Ἐπὶ μακρόν*, ‘for long’, supplies the duration; *ἄνω τὸ κέρας*, ‘the horn aloft’, substitutes a singular emblem for plural *le fronti*, retaining the gesture rather than the noun.
- *Κρατοῦντες* (nom. pl. masc. pres. act. participle of *κρατέω*) agrees with *οἵδε* and, like *tenendo*, expresses continuing action. The accusative object *τοὺς ἄλλους*, ‘the others’, likewise makes *l’ altra* personal and plural. The placement inside *ὑπὸ δεινὰ ... ἄχθη* separates adjective from noun; the accusative plural of *ἄχθος* after *ὑπό* closely tracks *sotto gravi pesi*, where a static Greek expression could use the dative.
- *Καίπερ* with *θρηνοῦντας καὶ καταισχυνομένους* replaces *come che ... pianga, e che ne adonti* with a concessive participial construction. Both participles are present accusative masculine plural, agreeing with the **object** of *κρατοῦντες*: active *θρηνέω*, ‘lament’, and middle/passive *καταισχύνω*, ‘be put to shame’. The references back in *di ciò* and *ne* are not separately expressed.

> 73 Giusti son duo, ma non vi sono intesi;  

Two are just; but are not listened to there;

> 73 „ Δίκαιοι δ’ εἰσὶ δύο, πλὴν οὐδεὶς τούτων  
> („ And there are two just men, yet no one to them)

- *Δίκαιοι δ’ εἰσὶ δύο* gives predicate adjective first as in *Giusti son duo*. *Δύο* takes plural *δίκαιοι* and 3rd pl. pres. ind. *εἰσί*: the Greek does not use dual agreement. *Πλὴν οὐδεὶς τούτων* begins the reversal of passive *ma non vi sono intesi* into ‘yet no one listens to them’. *Οὐδείς* is the nominative singular subject; *τούτων* is the genitive plural of the persons heard, governed by *ἀκούει* at the beginning of 74, not a rendering of locative *vi*, which drops out.

> 74 superbia, invidia ed avarizia sono  
> 75 le tre faville ch’ hanno i cuori accesi.”  

Pride, Envy, and Avarice are the three sparks which have set the hearts of all on fire.”

> 74 „ Ἀκούει. Τῦφος, φθόνος, φιλαργυρία,  
> („ Listens. Pride, envy, avarice)
>
> 75 „ Εἴσ’ αἱ καύσασαι τὰς καρδίας τρεῖς δᾷδες.”  
> („ Are the three torches that have inflamed their hearts.”)

- After *ἀκούει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἀκούω*) completes the preceding clause, *τῦφος, φθόνος, φιλαργυρία* gives three nominatives without the final conjunction of *superbia, invidia ed avarizia*. *Τῦφος* suggests conceit or inflated self-regard; *φιλαργυρία* specifies love of money.
- *Εἴσ’* is elided *εἰσί*, now placed at the start of the next line rather than in the position of *sono*. In *αἱ καύσασαι τὰς καρδίας τρεῖς δᾷδες*, ‘the three torches which burned the hearts’, *δᾷδες* (nom. pl. of *δᾴς*) enlarges *faville* from sparks to torches. Attributive *καύσασαι* (nom. pl. fem. first aor. act. participle of *καίω*) and its accusative object stand between article and noun, replacing the finite relative perfect *ch’ hanno i cuori accesi*. There is no Greek equivalent of “of all” beyond the definite *τὰς καρδίας*, just as Italian has *i cuori*.

> 76 Qui pose fine al lacrimabil suono.  

Here he ended the lamentable sound.

> 76 Ὧδ’ ἐπέρανε τὸν ὀδυρτικὸν λόγον.  
> (Thus he ended his lamentable speech.)

- *Ὧδ’* (elided *ὧδε*) admits the sense ‘thus’ as well as local ‘here’ for *Qui*. *Ἐπέρανε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *περαίνω*) takes the direct object *τὸν ὀδυρτικὸν λόγον*, replacing *pose fine al* with a single transitive verb. *Λόγον* makes *suono* articulate speech, characterized by *ὀδυρτικόν*, ‘plaintive’.

> 77 Ed io a lui: “Ancor vo’ che m’ insegni,  
> 78 e che di più parlar mi facci dono.  

And I to him : “ Still I wish thee to instruct me, and to bestow a little farther speech on me.

> 77 Κἀγώ· “Διδάξαι μ’ ἔτι νῦν δέομαί σου  
> (And I: “I pray you now to instruct me further)
>
> 78 „ Καὶ παρασχεῖν μοι χάριν ὀλίγων λόγων.  
> („ And grant me the favour of a few words.)

- *Κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*; neither *a lui* nor a verb of saying is expressed. *Διδάξαι μ’* (first aor. act. inf. of *διδάσκω* with elided accusative *με*) fronts the requested act. *Ἔτι νῦν* conveys *Ancor*; *δέομαί σου* (1st sg. pres. mid. ind. of *δέομαι* with genitive of the person petitioned) changes *vo’* from willing to entreating. The addressee is the understood subject of both infinitives, which replace the two Italian *che* clauses.
- *Παρασχεῖν* is the second aor. act. inf. of *παρέχω*, ‘grant’; it takes dative recipient *μοι* and accusative object *χάριν*. *Ὀλίγων λόγων*, ‘of a few words’, defines the favour: *di più parlar* becomes a genitive noun phrase, and the Italian comparative idea of more speaking is recast in terms of the smallness of the request.

> 79 Farinata e il Tegghiaio, che fur sì degni,  
> 80 Jacopo Rusticucci, Arrigo e il Mosca,  
> 81 e gli altri, che a ben far poser gl’ ingegni,  

Farinata and Tegghiaio, who were so worthy ; Jacopo Rusticucci, Arrigo and Mosca, and the rest who set their minds on doing good

> 79 „ Φαρινάτης, Τεγγαῖος, οἱ παμμεγέθεις,  
> („ Farinata, Tegghiaio, who were so great,)
>
> 80 „ Ἰακὼβ Ῥουστικούκης, Ἀριγός, Μόσκας,  
> („ Jacopo Rusticucci, Arrigo, Mosca,)
>
> 81 „ Λοιποί τ’ ἐν ἔργοις ἀγαθοῖς ἐγνωσμένοι,  
> („ And the others known for good deeds,)

- *Φαρινάτης, Τεγγαῖος, οἱ παμμεγέθεις* gives two juxtaposed nominatives, without *e*, followed by an articular adjective in apposition, ‘the exceedingly great ones’ (nom. pl. masc. of *παμμεγέθης*). This removes the relative verb and past time of *che fur sì degni*, and renders worthiness as greatness.
- The list continues in the nominative: *Ἰακώβ* is indeclinable, while *Ῥουστικούκης, Ἀριγός, Μόσκας* have Greek masculine endings. Again no conjunction corresponds to *e il Mosca*. These names are put forward as the topic and will be taken up by the plural verb and pronoun in 82.
- *Λοιποί τ’* has postpositive, elided *τε* for *e gli altri*. *Ἐν ἔργοις ἀγαθοῖς ἐγνωσμένοι* replaces *che a ben far poser gl’ ingegni* with ‘known in respect of good works’. The dative phrase defines the sphere; *ἐγνωσμένοι* is nom. pl. masc. perfect middle/passive participle of *γιγνώσκω*, passive in sense and expressing an established reputation. The active exertion of the Italian relative is no longer stated.

> 82 dimmi ove sono, e fa ch’ io li conosca:  

; tell me where they are, and give me to know them:

> 82 „ Εἰπέ μοι, ποῦ νῦν εἰσιν· αὐτούς μοι δεῖξον·  
> („ Tell me where they now are; point them out to me;)

- *Εἰπέ μοι, ποῦ νῦν εἰσιν* retains imperative plus indirect question; *ποῦ* is interrogative ‘where’, and *νῦν* adds ‘now’ to the 3rd pl. present *εἰσιν*. *Αὐτούς μοι δεῖξον*, ‘show them to me’, is a second, unconnected imperative (*δεῖξον*, 2nd sg. first aor. act. imperative of *δείκνυμι*). It puts *αὐτούς* first as the object to be shown, changing *fa ch’ io li conosca* from a causative with a first-person subordinate subject to a direct request with dative recipient.

> 83 chè gran desio mi stringe di sapere,  
> 84 se il ciel gli addolcia o l’ inferno gli attosca.”  

for great desire urges me to learn whether Heaven soothes or Hell empoisons them.”

> 83 „ Ποθῶ γὰρ μαθεῖν, εἴπερ αὐτοὺς εὐφραίνει  
> („ For I long to learn whether they are gladdened)
>
> 84 „ Οὐρανός, ἢ κολάζει πικρίαις ᾍδης.”  
> („ By Heaven, or Hell punishes them with bitterness.”)

- Postpositive *γάρ* gives the reason expressed by *chè*. *Ποθῶ* (1st sg. pres. act. ind. of *ποθέω*), ‘I long’, makes the speaker the subject, suppressing the separate *gran desio* and its action *mi stringe*. Complementary *μαθεῖν* is the second aor. act. inf. of *μανθάνω*, ‘to learn’, rather than a stative ‘to know’.
- *Εἴπερ ... ἤ* introduces the alternatives dependent on *μαθεῖν*, ‘whether indeed ... or’; *-περ* lends emphasis to *εἰ*. *Αὐτούς* is the expressed accusative object in the first alternative and understood in the second. Both subjects are delayed: *εὐφραίνει Οὐρανός*, ‘Heaven gladdens’, and *κολάζει πικρίαις ᾍδης*, ‘Hades punishes with bitter things’. The verbs are 3rd sg. pres. act. indicative of *εὐφραίνω* and *κολάζω*. The opposed verbal images *addolcia ... attosca* are thus distributed unevenly: sweetness becomes an effect on feeling, poison an instrumental dative plural of *πικρία* qualifying a verb of punishment.

> 85 E quegli: “Ei son tra le anime più nere;  
> 86 diversa colpa giù gli aggrava al fondo:  
> 87 se tanto scendi, gli potrai vedere.  

And he to me : “ They are amongst the blackest spirits; a different crime weighs them downwards to the bottom; shouldst thou descend so far, thou mayest see them.

> 85 Ὁ δ’ εἶπεν· “Οὗτοι μεταξὺ μελαντάτων  
> (And he said: “They among the blackest)
>
> 86 „ Ψυχῶν τυγχάνουσ’, ὡς ἁμαρτήματ’ ἄλλα  
> („ Souls are, since other sins)
>
> 87 „ Ἐβύθισ’ αὐτοὺς κάτω, καθάπερ ὄψει.  
> („ Have sunk them lower down, as you shall see.)

- *Ὁ δ’ εἶπεν*, ‘and he said’, supplies a finite speaking verb for *E quegli* but no explicit addressee. *Οὗτοι* is more demonstrative than *Ei*. *Μεταξὺ μελαντάτων ψυχῶν* renders *tra le anime più nere* with *μεταξύ* governing the genitive and the superlative *μελαντάτων* (gen. pl. fem., from *μέλας*). *Τυγχάνουσ’* is elided *τυγχάνουσι*, 3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *τυγχάνω*: with the expression of place it has the force ‘are situated’, without a separate participle of being.
- Causal *ὡς* makes the relation to the preceding statement explicit. *Ἁμαρτήματ’ ἄλλα* is elided *ἁμαρτήματα* with its adjective, a neuter plural subject for singular *diversa colpa*. It accordingly takes a **singular** verb in Greek, *ἐβύθισ’* (elided *ἐβύθισε*, 3rd sg. first aor. act. ind. of *βυθίζω*). ‘Other sins sank them’ replaces the continuing weight of *gli aggrava* with a completed act; *κάτω* gives the downward direction, but no separate noun renders *al fondo*.
- *Καθάπερ ὄψει*, ‘just as you will see’, replaces both the condition *se tanto scendi* and the possibility *gli potrai vedere* with an assured comparison. *Ὄψει* is 2nd sg. fut. mid. ind. of *ὁράω*, active in meaning; neither a verb of descent nor an explicit object pronoun accompanies it.

## Lines 88–115 — Remembrance, Judgment, and Descent

> 88 Ma quando tu sarai nel dolce mondo,  
> 89 pregoti che alla mente altrui mi rechi ;  

But [when thou shalt be in] the sweet world, I pray thee recall me to the memory of men;

> 88 „ Ἀλλ’ ὅταν ἐπανίδῃς τὸν γλυκὺν κόσμον,  
> („ But when you see again the sweet world,)
>
> 89 „ Παρακαλῶ σε τοῖς ἐμοῖς μ’ ἀναμνῆσαι.  
> („ I beseech you to recall me to my own folk.)

- *Ἀλλ’ ὅταν ἐπανίδῃς* ‘but when you see again’ renders *Ma quando tu sarai*. *Ἀλλ’* is *ἀλλά* elided; *ὅταν* (*ὅτε* + *ἄν*) takes the subjunctive for an occasion still to come. Instead of the Italian future of being, *ἐπανίδῃς* is 2nd sg. second aor. act. subj. of *ἐπανιδεῖν* (the compound of the ‘see’ aorist *εἶδον*). There is no separate pronoun for *tu*.
- *Τὸν γλυκὺν κόσμον* preserves the adjective of *nel dolce mondo*, but changes the place where one will be into the accusative object of seeing.
- *Παρακαλῶ σε … μ’ ἀναμνῆσαι* ‘I ask you to recall me’ replaces *pregoti che … mi rechi*: the contracted present *παρακαλῶ* (1st sg. of *παρακαλέω*) takes the person asked, *σε*, as the understood subject of the aor. act. infinitive *ἀναμνῆσαι* (of *ἀναμιμνῄσκω*). *Μ’* is elided *με*, the person to be recalled.
- *Τοῖς ἐμοῖς* ‘to my own people’ is a substantivized possessive, dat. pl. It narrows *altrui* ‘of others’ and supplies recipients in place of the destination *alla mente*. The resulting ‘recall me to them’ is closer to the Italian image than to the usual classical construction ‘remind someone [acc.] of someone [gen.]’.

> 90 più non ti dico, e più non ti rispondo.”  

more I tell thee not, and more I answer not.”

> 90 „ Οὐ λέγω σοί τι πλέον, οὔτ’ ἀπαντῶ σοι.”  
> („ I say no more to you, nor answer you further.”)

- *Οὐ λέγω σοί τι πλέον* ‘I tell you nothing further’ puts the negative first rather than fronting *più*. *Σοί* is the dative recipient; *τι πλέον* combines the indefinite pronoun with the neut. sg. comparative of *πολύς*, ‘anything more’.
- *Οὔτ’ ἀπαντῶ σοι* ‘nor do I answer you’ joins a second negative without repeating *πλέον* for the second *più*. *Οὔτ’* is elided *οὔτε*; *ἀπαντῶ* is 1st sg. pres. act. ind. of contracted *ἀπαντάω*, with a dative. Its sense ‘answer’ is later than its ordinary classical sense ‘meet’.

> 91 Gli diritti occhi torse allora in biechi ;  

Therewith he writhed his straight eyes asquint;

> 91 Τἀτενῆ βλέμματ’ ἔτρεψε λοξῶς τότε·  
> (His fixed gaze he then turned askance;)

- *Τἀτενῆ βλέμματ’* ‘the fixed looks’ is the fronted accusative object, like *Gli diritti occhi*. Musurus substitutes looks for eyes and steadiness for straightness. *Τἀτενῆ* is crasis of *τὰ ἀτενῆ*; *ἀτενῆ* is acc. neut. pl. of *ἀτενής*, agreeing with elided *βλέμματ[α]*. The forms are plural, not dual.
- *Ἔτρεψε λοξῶς τότε* ‘he turned [them] obliquely then’: *ἔτρεψε* is 3rd sg. augmented aor. act. ind. of *τρέπω*. The result expressed by *in biechi* becomes the adverb of manner *λοξῶς*; *τότε*, for *allora*, moves to the end.

> 92 guardommi un poco ; e poi chinò la testa ;  
> 93 cadde con essa a par degli altri ciechi.  

looked at me a little; then bent his head, and fell down with it like his blind companions.

> 92 Μικρὸν ἔτ’ εἶδέ μ’, εἶτα κεφαλὴν κλίνας  
> (A little longer he looked at me, then bowed his head)
>
> 93 Ἔπεσε πάλιν ἐν μέσῳ τυφλῶν ἄλλων.  
> (And fell back in the midst of the other blind ones.)

- *Μικρὸν ἔτ’ εἶδέ μ’* ‘a little longer he looked at me’ moves *un poco* to the front as an accusative of duration and adds *ἔτ’* (elided *ἔτι*) ‘still’. *Εἶδε* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*, with elided *με* as object; the extra accent in *εἶδέ* is due to the enclitic.
- *Εἶτα κεφαλὴν κλίνας / ἔπεσε* ‘then, having bowed his head, he fell’ subordinates *chinò la testa* to *cadde*. *Κλίνας* is the nom. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *κλίνω*, agreeing with the subject of *ἔπεσε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *πίπτω*); the anarthrous *κεφαλήν* needs no possessive here.
- *Πάλιν ἐν μέσῳ τυφλῶν ἄλλων* ‘again in the midst of other blind ones’ adds *πάλιν* and gives the position of the fall. *Ἐν μέσῳ* takes a dependent genitive plural. There is no equivalent of *con essa*, and proximity replaces the comparison *a par degli altri ciechi*.

> 94 E il duca disse a me : “ Più non si desta  
> 95 di qua dal suon dell' angelica tromba ;  

And my Guide said to me: “He wakes no more until the angel’s trumpet sounds;

> 94 Πρός με δ’ Ἡγήτωρ. “Οὐκ ἐγεροῦντ’ ἐντεῦθεν  
> (And my Guide to me: “Not hence shall rise)
>
> 95 „ Οὗτοι, πρὶν ἀγγελικὴ σάλπιγξ ἠχήσῃ,  
> („ These, until the angelic trumpet sounds,)

- *Πρός με δ’ Ἡγήτωρ* ‘and to me the Guide’: elided postpositive *δέ* connects the speeches. *Ἡγήτωρ* is a poetic agent noun, nom. masc. sg.; *πρός* takes the accusative of the addressee. The order foregrounds *a me*, and *disse* has no expressed verb to match it.
- *Οὐκ ἐγεροῦντ’ ἐντεῦθεν / οὗτοι* ‘these shall not rise from here’ changes singular *si desta* to a future plural with a delayed demonstrative subject. *Ἐγεροῦντ’* is elided *ἐγεροῦνται*, 3rd pl. contracted fut. mid. ind. of *ἐγείρω*, intransitive ‘rise’. *Ἐντεῦθεν* makes *di qua* spatial; *πιὸ* (*più*) has no separate equivalent.
- *Πρὶν ἀγγελικὴ σάλπιγξ ἠχήσῃ* turns *di qua dal suon dell’ angelica tromba* into a temporal clause after a negative, ‘not … until an angelic trumpet sounds’. The trumpet is nominative subject, its sound a verb, *ἠχήσῃ* (3rd sg. aor. act. subj. of *ἠχέω*). Classical Attic would normally have *πρὶν ἄν* with this prospective subjunctive.

> 96 quando verrà la nimica podesta,  

when the adverse Power shall come,

> 96 „ Καὐτοῖς τρομερὰ τότ’ ἐπισκήψει δίκη·  
> („ And dreadful justice then shall fall upon them;)

- *Καὐτοῖς τρομερὰ τότ’ ἐπισκήψει δίκη* ‘and on them dreadful judgment will then descend’ is coordinated, not subordinate as *quando verrà* is. *Καὐτοῖς* is crasis of *καὶ αὐτοῖς*, dative of those affected; *τότ’* is elided *τότε*.
- *Ἐπισκήψει* is 3rd sg. fut. act. ind. of *ἐπισκήπτω*, ‘fall upon’, stronger than *verrà*. Its subject *δίκη* ‘justice, judgment’ is postponed, separated from its adjective *τρομερά* ‘terrible’: Musurus interprets *la nimica podesta* by what it brings upon them rather than naming an adverse power.

> 97 ciascun ritroverà la trista tomba,  

each shall revisit his sad grave;

> 97 „ Ἕκαστος ἀνευρήσει τὸν λυπρὸν τάφον,  
> („ Each one shall find again his dismal tomb,)

- *Ἕκαστος ἀνευρήσει τὸν λυπρὸν τάφον* closely follows *ciascun ritroverà la trista tomba*. *Ἕκαστος* is distributive nom. masc. sg.; *ἀνευρήσει* is 3rd sg. fut. act. ind. of *ἀνευρίσκω*, ‘find out, recover’. The article with the accusative object suffices without an explicit ‘his’.

> 98 ripiglierà sua carne e sua figura,  

shall resume his flesh and form;

> 98 „ Πάλιν ἕξει τὴν αὑτοῦ μορφὴν καὶ σάρκα,  
> („ Shall take again his own shape and flesh,)

- *Πάλιν ἕξει* ‘will again have’ distributes the sense of *ripiglierà* between an adverb and *ἕξει*, 3rd sg. fut. act. ind. of *ἔχω*. *Τὴν αὑτοῦ μορφὴν καὶ σάρκα* reverses *sua carne e sua figura*: the possessive genitive *αὑτοῦ* (contracted *ἑαυτοῦ*, with rough breathing) refers back to *ἕκαστος*. One possessive serves both accusatives rather than repeating *sua*.

> 99 udirà quel che in eterno rimbomba.”  

shall hear that which resounds to all eternity.”

> 99 „ Κρίσεώς τ’ αἰωνίου φωνὴν ἀκούσει.”  
> („ And shall hear the voice of eternal judgement.”)

- *Κρίσεώς τ’ αἰωνίου φωνὴν ἀκούσει* ‘and he will hear the voice of eternal judgment’ makes *quel che in eterno rimbomba* a noun phrase. *Φωνήν* is the accusative object of *ἀκούσει* (3rd sg. fut. act. ind. of *ἀκούω*); the preposed gen. sg. *κρίσεως* depends on *φωνήν*. *Αἰωνίου* qualifies the judgment rather than the duration of the sound.
- Enclitic *τ’* (elided *τε*) links this prediction to the preceding one and accounts for the extra accent of *κρίσεώς*. There is no relative clause and no distinct verb for *rimbomba*.

> 100 Sì trapassammo per sozza mistura  
> 101 dell' ombre e della pioggia, a passi lenti,  
> 102 toccando un poco la vita futura.  

Thus passed we through the filthy mixture of the shadows and the rain, with paces slow, touching a little on the future life.

> 100 Ἐν τούτοις διέβημεν διὰ φρικώδους  
> (Meanwhile we passed through a dreadful)
>
> 101 Ἐκ σκιῶν κὤμβρου μικτῆς διόδου βάδην,  
> (Passage mingled of shades and rain, at a slow pace,)
>
> 102 Συλλαλοῦντές τι περὶ ζωῆς μελλούσης.  
> (Conversing somewhat of the life to come.)

- *Ἐν τούτοις* ‘meanwhile’ gives temporal coincidence rather than the manner of *Sì*. *Διέβημεν* is 1st pl. root aor. act. ind. of *διαβαίνω*, with the augment inside the compound. The following *διά* + genitive makes the route explicit.
- *Διὰ φρικώδους … μικτῆς διόδου* ‘through a dreadful … mingled passage’ gives *sozza* the sense ‘horrible’ rather than ‘filthy’. *Διόδου* (gen. sg. of feminine *δίοδος*) supplies a concrete head noun; *μικτῆς*, agreeing with it like *φρικώδους*, expresses adjectivally the *mistura* of the Italian. Intervening *ἐκ σκιῶν κὤμβρου* specifies the constituents: *κὤμβρου* is poetic crasis of *καὶ ὄμβρου*, with singular ‘rain’ beside plural ‘shades’.
- *Βάδην* ‘at a walk’ replaces *a passi lenti* with an adverb, leaving slowness implicit. *Συλλαλοῦντές τι* ‘conversing somewhat’ interprets *toccando un poco* as speech. The pres. act. participle of *συλλαλέω* is nom. masc. pl., agreeing with the speakers and simultaneous with their passing; enclitic *τι* is adverbial. *Περὶ ζωῆς μελλούσης* changes the direct object into a topic with *περί* + genitive; *μελλούσης* is the attributive gen. fem. sg. pres. act. participle of *μέλλω*, ‘to come’.

> 103 Per ch' io dissi : “ Maestro, esti tormenti  
> 104 cresceranno ei dopo la gran sentenza,  
> 105 o fien minori, o saran sì cocenti ? ”  

Wherefore I said: “Master, shall these torments increase after the great Sentence, or grow less, or remain as burning?”

> 103 “Ἄρχων,” τότ’ εἶπον, “ἆρα τὰ δεινὰ ταῦτα  
> (“Master,” I then said, “shall these torments)
>
> 104 „ Αὐξηθήσεται μετὰ κρίσιν ἐσχάτην,  
> („ Be increased after the last judgement,)
>
> 105 „ Ἢ μείον᾽ ἔσετ’, ἢ καθὼς νῦν δριμέα ;”  
> („ Or be less, or just as sharp as now?”)

- *Ἄρχων, τότ’ εἶπον* ‘Master, then I said’ puts the address before the reporting clause. *Ἄρχων* is the vocative singular (identical to the nominative) of the title ‘ruler’ rather than a word for ‘teacher’. *Τότ’* gives sequence instead of the inference *Per ch’*; *εἶπον* is 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*.
- Interrogative *ἆρα* opens the question; *τὰ δεινὰ ταῦτα* ‘these dreadful things’ renders *esti tormenti* by a substantivized neuter plural adjective with demonstrative. *Αὐξηθήσεται*, 3rd sg. fut. pass. ind. of *αὐξάνω*, means ‘will be increased’ rather than active *cresceranno*. The singular verb with a neuter plural subject is regular Attic agreement; the Italian resumptive *ei* is not reproduced.
- *Μετὰ κρίσιν ἐσχάτην* ‘after a final judgment’ interprets *gran* as ‘last’; temporal *μετά* takes the accusative. *Ἢ μείον’ ἔσετ’, ἢ καθὼς νῦν δριμέα* gives the alternatives with repeated *ἤ*. *Μείον’* is elided *μείονα*, comparative neut. pl.; *ἔσετ’* is elided *ἔσεται*, 3rd sg. fut. of *εἰμί*, understood again in the last alternative. The predicates, unlike the verb, are plural. *Δριμέα* (uncontracted neut. pl. of *δριμύς*) gives ‘sharp’ for *cocenti*; *καθὼς νῦν* makes the comparison with present intensity explicit.

> 106 Ed egli a me : “ Ritorna a tua scienza,  
> 107 che vuol, quanto la cosa è più perfetta,  
> 108 più senta il bene, e così la doglienza.  

And he to me: “Return to thy science, which has it, that the more a thing is perfect, the more it feels pleasure and likewise pain.

> 106 Αὐτός δ’· “Εἰς τὴν σὴν ἐπάνελθε σοφίαν,  
> (And he: “Return to your philosophy,)
>
> 107 „ Καθ’ ἣν ὅσον τις ἐντελέστερος εἴη,  
> („ According to which, the more perfect a thing may be,)
>
> 108 „ Τοσοῦτον αἰσθάνεται χαρᾶς ἢ λύπης.  
> („ The more it feels of joy or of pain.)

- *Αὐτός δ’* ‘and he’ marks the change of speaker with the emphatic nominative and elided postpositive *δέ*, omitting *a me* and leaving ‘said’ understood. *Εἰς τὴν σὴν ἐπάνελθε σοφίαν* ‘return to your wisdom’ places the verb between possessive and noun. *Ἐπάνελθε* is 2nd sg. second aor. act. imperative of *ἐπανέρχομαι*, without augment; *εἰς* + accusative retains the direction of *a tua scienza*.
- *Καθ’ ἣν* ‘according to which’ replaces *che vuol* with a relative prepositional phrase. *Ἥν* is acc. fem. sg., referring to *σοφίαν*; *καθ’* is *κατά* elided with aspiration before the rough breathing. *Ὅσον … τοσοῦτον* are correlative accusatives of degree, ‘insofar as … to that extent’.
- *Τις ἐντελέστερος εἴη* ‘someone might be more complete’ personalizes *la cosa*: indefinite *τις* has a masculine comparative predicate. *Εἴη* is 3rd sg. pres. opt. of *εἰμί*. It presents a supposed case, though this bare optative is not the ordinary Attic form for a general fact or for a potential assertion (the latter normally takes *ἄν*).
- *Αἰσθάνεται* is 3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of deponent *αἰσθάνομαι*: a direct assertion rather than the dependent subjunctive *senta*. It governs the genitives *χαρᾶς ἢ λύπης* ‘of joy or pain’. *Χαρά* narrows *il bene* to a feeling; disjunctive *ἤ* replaces additive *e così*.

> 109 Tuttochè questa gente maledetta  
> 110 in vera perfezion giammai non vada,  
> 111 di là, più che di qua, essere aspetta.”  

Though these accursed people never attain to true perfection, yet they [look to] be nearer it after than before.”

> 109 „ Ἀλλ’ εἰ καὶ γένος τὸ κατάρατον τόδε  
> („ And although this accursed race)
>
> 110 „ Μήποτ’ ἀφίξετ’ εἰς ἐντέλειαν. ἔσται  
> („ Shall never attain to perfection, it shall be)
>
> 111 „ Ἐντελέστερον ἢ πρὶν μετὰ τὴν κρίσιν.”  
> („ More perfect after the judgement than before.”)

- *Ἀλλ’ εἰ καί* ‘but even if, although’ renders *Tuttochè*, introducing a finite concessive clause. *Γένος τὸ κατάρατον τόδε* ‘this accursed race’ makes *questa gente maledetta* a neut. sg. collective. The article with the postposed adjective marks it as attributive; the demonstrative *τόδε* stands outside that group.
- *Μήποτ’ ἀφίξετ’ εἰς ἐντέλειαν* ‘should it never arrive at completeness’: *μήποτ’* is elided *μήποτε*, the negative suited to a supposition with *εἰ*; *ἀφίξετ’* is elided *ἀφίξεται*, 3rd sg. fut. mid. ind. of deponent *ἀφικνέομαι*. The Italian subjunctive *vada* thus becomes a Greek future of arrival with *εἰς* + accusative. *Ἐντέλειαν* has no qualifier for *vera*.
- *Ἔσται ἐντελέστερον* ‘it will be more complete’ resumes *γένος* with a singular future of *εἰμί* and a neut. sg. comparative predicate. Musurus resolves *più … essere aspetta* into a prediction of greater completeness, without a verb of expectation. *Ἢ πρὶν μετὰ τὴν κρίσιν* places ‘than before’ immediately after the comparative, then adds ‘after the judgment’: temporal expressions make explicit the contrast *di là … di qua*.

> 112 Noi aggirammo a tondo quella strada,  
> 113 parlando più assai ch' io non ridico ;  

We went round along that road, speaking much more than I repeat;

> 112 Διήλθομεν τὸν κύκλον συνομιλοῦντες  
> (We traversed the circle conversing together)
>
> 113 Περὶ παντοίων, ἅπερ οὐ χρὴ νῦν λέγειν.  
> (Of sundry things, which it is not fitting now to tell.)

- *Διήλθομεν τὸν κύκλον* ‘we traversed the circle’ compresses *aggirammo a tondo quella strada*: the shape of the route becomes the accusative object, and the demonstrative disappears. *Διήλθομεν* is 1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *διέρχομαι*, with internal augment. The ending alone renders *Noi*.
- *Συνομιλοῦντες περὶ παντοίων* ‘conversing about all sorts of things’ retains the simultaneous action of *parlando* in a nom. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *συνομιλέω*. *Περί* takes a substantivized gen. neut. pl. of *παντοῖος*: variety replaces the comparison of quantity *più assai*.
- *Ἅπερ οὐ χρὴ νῦν λέγειν* ‘which things one ought not now to tell’ replaces *ch’ io non ridico*. The emphatic relative *ἅπερ* (neut. pl., with attached *-περ*) is accusative as the object of *λέγειν*, despite the genitive antecedent. Impersonal *χρή* with the pres. act. infinitive substitutes a judgment of fitness for the first-person statement; *νῦν* is added and the sense ‘again’ of *ridico* is lost.

> 114 venimmo al punto dove si digrada ;  

we reached the point where the descent begins;

> 114 Ἀφικόμεθα δ’ ἔνθ’ ἔδει καταβῆναι,  
> (And we came to where it was necessary to descend,)

- *Ἀφικόμεθα δ’* ‘and we arrived’ is 1st pl. second aor. mid. ind. of deponent *ἀφικνέομαι*, followed by elided connective *δέ*. *Ἔνθ’* is *ἔνθα* ‘where’, elided before *ἔδει*. The relative adverb needs no expressed antecedent corresponding to *al punto*.
- *Ἔδει καταβῆναι* ‘it was necessary to go down’ interprets impersonal *si digrada* in terms of what the travelers had to do. *Ἔδει* is impf. ind. of impersonal *δεῖ*; *καταβῆναι* is the root aor. act. infinitive of *καταβαίνω*, with the persons who must descend left implicit.

> 115 quivi trovammo Pluto il gran nemico.  

here found we Plutus, the great enemy.

> 115 Κἀκεῖ Πλούτων’ εὕρομεν ἐχθρὸν τὸν μέγαν.  
> (And there we found Plutus, the great enemy.)

- *Κἀκεῖ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐκεῖ*, ‘and there’, adding a connective to *quivi*. *Πλούτων’* is acc. sg. *Πλούτωνα* elided before *εὕρομεν*. The form is from *Πλούτων* ‘Plouton, Pluto’, not *Πλοῦτος* ‘Plutus’: the choice of Greek name is more specific than Italian *Pluto*.
- *Εὕρομεν* is 1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *εὑρίσκω*, with *ευ-* rather than the augmented variant *ηυ-*. The object precedes the verb, its apposition follows: *ἐχθρὸν τὸν μέγαν* ‘the enemy, the great one’. Both words are acc. masc. sg.; the article marks the postposed adjective as attributive, reversing the order of *il gran nemico*.

## Summary — Close Echoes and Changes of Construction

Musurus often preserves the order of Dante’s words even when he changes their grammatical relation. The paired objects *νέας στρεβλώσεις* and *νέους στρεβλουμένους* echo the repetition of *nuovi tormenti* and *nuovi tormentati*: the noun names the torment, the substantivized present passive participle those undergoing it. Both precede the verb of seeing, as in Italian. Participles also let Musurus distinguish actions that Dante coordinates. Of the three finite verbs *distese*, *prese*, and *gittò*, only the last remains finite in Greek. The perfect participle *ἀνεῳγμέναις* describes the hands as already open, and the aorist *λαβών* (‘having taken’) marks the act preceding the throw.

Relative clauses likewise give way to participles. For *che quivi è sommersa*, the repeated article introduces *τὴν ἐκεῖ βεβυθισμένην* (‘the one submerged there’): the perfect passive expresses a continuing state without a second finite verb. Elsewhere Musurus changes the subject rather than the verb form alone. The city that is subject of *mi tenne* becomes a place expressed by *ἐν* with the dative, while Ciacco becomes the subject of *ᾤκουν* (‘I dwelt’). Postpositive *δέ* regularly links speeches and actions; *μέν* can prepare a turn to *δέ*, as when an expression of sympathy gives way to a question. Elision and crasis are frequent, especially in recurrent forms such as *κἀγώ* (‘and I’) and *θἄτερον* (‘the other’).

Some of the sharpest differences appear in short phrases that otherwise look close to the Italian. *Μεθ᾽ ἡλίους τρεῖς* retains Dante’s ‘three suns’ but places the event **after** them, not within them as in *infra tre soli*. *Θωπεύει* (‘flatters’) commits to one reading of *piaggia*, while *καθάπερ ὄψει* (‘just as you will see’) turns the conditional prospect of *se tanto scendi* into an assurance. Musurus can also preserve an effect by different means: where Dante figures excess through an overflowing sack, *ἀφθόνου φθόνου* (‘of unstinted envy’) makes the adjective of abundance sound almost identical to the vice it qualifies.
