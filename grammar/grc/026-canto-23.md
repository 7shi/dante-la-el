# Inferno 23

## Lines 1–21 — One Thought Doubles the Fear

> 1 Taciti, soli e senza compagnia  
> 2 n'andavam l' un dinanzi e l' altro dopo,  
> 3 come frati minor vanno per via.  

Silent, apart, and without escort we went on, the one before and the other after; as Minor Friars go their way.

> 1 Σιγηλοί, μόνοι καὶ χωρὶς συνοδίας  
> (Silent, alone, and without company,)
>
> 2 Ἐπορευόμεθ᾽, εἷς πρόσθεν, εἷς ὀπίσω,  
> (We went on, one in front, one behind,)
>
> 3 Ὡς βαδίζουσ᾽ ἐν ὁδοῖς Φρᾶτρες Μινῶρες.  
> (As Friars Minor walk along the roads.)

- *Σιγηλοί, μόνοι καὶ χωρὶς συνοδίας* keeps the order and construction of *Taciti, soli e senza compagnia*: the two masculine nominative plural adjectives describe the unexpressed subject of ‘we went’, while the preposition *χωρίς* governs the genitive singular *συνοδίας*, ‘company, escort’.
- *Ἐπορευόμεθ᾽* is *ἐπορευόμεθα*, first person plural imperfect middle indicative of *πορεύομαι*, with syllabic augment. It gives the continuing movement of *n'andavam* without a separate equivalent for the pronominal particle. Final *-α* is elided before the rough breathing of *εἷς*, so that *τ* becomes *θ*.
- *εἷς πρόσθεν, εἷς ὀπίσω*, ‘one in front, one behind’, uses the masculine nominative singular numeral twice, distributively, in apposition to the plural subject. The repeated *εἷς* and the juxtaposed adverbs replace *l' un … e l' altro* without either an article or a conjunction.
- In *Ὡς βαδίζουσ᾽ ἐν ὁδοῖς Φρᾶτρες Μινῶρες*, comparative *ὡς* introduces a clause with its verb first. *βαδίζουσ᾽* is elided *βαδίζουσι*, third person plural present active indicative of *βαδίζω*, ‘walk’; *ἐν ὁδοῖς* gives a plural dative of place, ‘on roads’, for singular *per via*. The postponed subject *Φρᾶτρες Μινῶρες* transcribes the Latin title in Greek letters rather than translating *frati minor* into Greek common nouns.

> 4 Volto era in su la favola d' Isopo  
> 5 lo mio pensier per la presente rissa,  
> 6 dov' ei parlò della rana e del topo:  

My thought was turned, by the present strife, to Æsop's fable where he [spoke] of the frog and mouse:

> 4 Ἐπὶ τὸν μῦθον ἀνήγαγον Αἰσώπου  
> (To the fable of Æsop I turned back)
>
> 5 Τὴν διάνοιαν ἐν τῇ παρούσῃ πάλῃ,  
> (My thought amid the present strife,)
>
> 6 Ἔνθα περὶ βατράχου καὶ μυὸς λόγος.  
> (Where the tale is of the frog and the mouse.)

- *Ἐπὶ τὸν μῦθον ἀνήγαγον Αἰσώπου / Τὴν διάνοιαν*, ‘to the fable of Aesop I led my thought back’, changes *Volto era … lo mio pensier* from a description of the thought's state to an action by the thinker. *ἀνήγαγον* is first person singular second aorist active indicative of *ἀνάγω*, with the augmented aorist stem of *ἄγω* after the prefix. *τὴν διάνοιαν* is its direct object; the first person makes *mio* understood.
- *ἐπί* with the accusative marks the direction of thought, rendering *in su*. Musurus fronts this phrase but separates the possessive genitive *Αἰσώπου* from *τὸν μῦθον* by the verb. *ἐν τῇ παρούσῃ πάλῃ*, ‘in the present struggle’, gives the circumstance rather than the cause expressed by *per la presente rissa*. *παρούσῃ* is the feminine dative singular present participle of *πάρειμι*, used attributively with *πάλῃ*.
- *Ἔνθα* retains the relative ‘where’ of *dov'*, but *περὶ βατράχου καὶ μυὸς λόγος* is a nominal clause, ‘there is an account of a frog and a mouse’, with *ἐστί* understood. Nominative singular *λόγος* thus replaces both *ei* and the personal verb *parlò*. *περί* governs the two genitives of subject matter; *μυός* is the genitive singular of *μῦς*.

> 7 chè più non si pareggia mo ed issa,  
> 8 che l' un con l' altro fa se ben s' accoppia  
> 9 principio e fine con la mente fissa.  

for Ay and Yea pair not better, than does the one *case* with the other, if with attentive mind the beginning and end of *each* be well accoupled.

> 7 Ἤδη γὰρ καὶ Νῦν οὐ μᾶλλον ὁμοιοῦνται  
> (For Already and Now are not more alike)
>
> 8 Τῶν δύο παλῶν, ἐάν περ ἀκριβῶς τις  
> (Than those two struggles, if indeed one closely)
>
> 9 Ἀρχὴν καὶ τέλος τῷ νῷ συμπαραλάβῃ.  
> (Takes in together with the mind their beginning and end.)

- *Ἤδη γὰρ καὶ Νῦν* keeps the comparison between two quoted words. Unlike the English “Ay and Yea”, Musurus chooses temporal adverbs for *mo ed issa*: *ἤδη*, ‘already, by now’, and *νῦν*, ‘now’. The capitalized adverbs function as a compound subject, with explanatory *γάρ* in its usual second position for *chè*.
- *οὐ μᾶλλον ὁμοιοῦνται*, ‘are not more alike’, renders *più non si pareggia* with the comparative adverb *μᾶλλον* and the contracted third person plural present middle-passive indicative of *ὁμοιόω*. *Τῶν δύο παλῶν* is the genitive of comparison after *μᾶλλον*: Greek supplies the noun ‘struggles’ and leaves ‘are alike’ understood of them. This replaces the Italian's reciprocal *l' un con l' altro* and its substitute verb *fa*. *δύο* is indeclinable here; *παλῶν* is plural, not dual.
- *ἐάν περ … συμπαραλάβῃ* makes *se* a prospective condition with the subjunctive; *περ* emphasizes the proviso, ‘if indeed’. Instead of impersonal *s' accoppia*, Greek supplies the indefinite subject *τις*, ‘someone’, and an active verb: *συμπαραλάβῃ* is third person singular second aorist active subjunctive of *συμπαραλαμβάνω*, ‘take in together’. The aorist views the comparison as a whole; it does not denote past time here.
- *Ἀρχὴν καὶ τέλος* are the accusative objects, without an expressed ‘of each’, as in *principio e fine*. *τῷ νῷ* is the instrumental dative singular of contracted *νοῦς*, ‘with the mind’. The attention expressed by *con la mente fissa* and the force of *ben* are conveyed by *ἀκριβῶς*, ‘closely, accurately’, modifying the act rather than the mind.

> 10 E come l' un pensier dell' altro scoppia,  
> 11 così nacque di quello un altro poi,  
> 12 che la prima paura mi fe' doppia.  

And as one thought from the other springs, so arose from that another then, which made my first fear double.

> 10 Ὡς δ᾽ ἐκ λογισμοῦ λογισμὸς ἀναβρύει,  
> (And as from one thought another thought wells up,)
>
> 11 Οὕτως ἐγεννήθη τις ἄλλος ἐκ τούτου,  
> (So was another born out of this one,)
>
> 12 Ὅστις τὸν πρῶτον ἐπηύξησέ μοι φόβον.  
> (Which increased my first fear.)

- *Ὡς δ᾽ … Οὕτως* reproduces the correlation *E come … così*. Connective *δέ*, elided before *ἐκ*, follows *ὡς* rather than opening the clause. *ἐκ λογισμοῦ λογισμός* gives ‘from thought, thought’: the same noun occurs first in the genitive of source, then in the nominative as subject, without articles or words for *l' un* and *l' altro*. *ἀναβρύει*, third person singular present active indicative of *ἀναβρύω*, substitutes ‘wells up’ for *scoppia*.
- *ἐγεννήθη τις ἄλλος ἐκ τούτου*, ‘another was born from this one’, renders intransitive *nacque* by the third person singular aorist passive indicative of *γεννάω*. Indefinite *τις* and masculine *ἄλλος* imply *λογισμός*; so does the demonstrative genitive *τούτου* after *ἐκ*. The source follows the subject here, reversing *di quello un altro*; *poi* has no separate equivalent.
- *Ὅστις* is the masculine nominative singular compound relative, agreeing with the implied *λογισμός* and serving here as a continuative ‘which’. *τὸν πρῶτον … φόβον* is the accusative object of *ἐπηύξησέ*, third person singular aorist active indicative of *ἐπαυξάνω*, with augment after the prefix. Enclitic *μοι* accounts for the additional acute on the verb's last syllable and marks the person affected, as does *mi*. Where *mi fe' doppia* specifies doubling, the single Greek verb says only ‘increased’.

> 13 Io pensava così: “Questi per noi  
> 14 sono scherniti, e con danno e con beffa  
> 15 sì fatta, ch' assai credo che lor noi.  

I thus bethought me: "These through us are put to scorn, and with damage and mockery of such sort, as I believe must greatly vex them.

> 13 Οὕτω πως ἐσκεπτόμην· “Δι᾽ ἡμᾶς οὗτοι  
> (Thus somewhat I reasoned: “On account of us these)
>
> 14 „ Ἐνεπαίχθησαν μετ᾽ αἰσχύνης καὶ βλάβης  
> („ Were mocked with shame and hurt)
>
> 15 „Τοιαύτης, ὥστ᾽ ἄχθεσθαι πάνυ νομίζω.  
> („So great, that I think it vexes them sorely.)

- *Οὕτω πως ἐσκεπτόμην* gives ‘I was reasoning somewhat thus’. *ἐσκεπτόμην* is the augmented first person singular imperfect middle indicative of *σκέπτομαι*; it keeps the duration of *pensava*, with no separate emphatic *Io*. Enclitic indefinite *πως*, not interrogative *πῶς*, qualifies *οὕτω* more tentatively than *così* alone.
- *Δι᾽ ἡμᾶς οὗτοι / Ἐνεπαίχθησαν* puts the cause first: *δι᾽* is *διά* elided before the accusative *ἡμᾶς*, hence ‘on account of us’, not a construction naming the agent of a passive. *οὗτοι* is the masculine nominative plural subject; *ἐνεπαίχθησαν*, third person plural aorist passive indicative of *ἐμπαίζω*, presents the mockery as an accomplished act rather than reproducing the present state of *sono scherniti*.
- *μετ᾽ αἰσχύνης καὶ βλάβης* uses elided *μετά* with genitives of attendant circumstance for *con danno e con beffa*. *βλάβη* corresponds to *danno* but comes second; *αἰσχύνη*, ‘shame, disgrace’, gives the effect of *beffa* rather than another word for mockery. *Τοιαύτης*, feminine genitive singular, attaches most directly to the adjacent *βλάβης*, ‘hurt of such a kind’.
- *ὥστ᾽ ἄχθεσθαι πάνυ νομίζω* replaces *ch' assai credo che lor noi* with *ὥστε* (elided) introducing the result, and an infinitive of reported belief with *νομίζω*, ‘I think’. *ἄχθεσθαι* is the present middle infinitive of *ἄχθομαι*, ‘be vexed’; its unexpressed subject is the people designated by *οὗτοι*. Thus the Italian dative experiencer *lor* becomes the understood subject of the feeling, and *πάνυ* indicates its degree.

> 16 Se l' ira sopra il mal voler s' aggueffa,  
> 17 ei ne verranno dietro più crudeli  
> 18 che il cane a quella lepre ch' egli acceffa.”  

If rage be added to their malice, they will pursue us, fiercer than [the] dog that leveret which he snaps."

> 16 „Τῆς δ᾽ ὀργῆς προστεθείσης τῇ δυσμενείᾳ,  
> („And if wrath is added to their malice,)
>
> 17 „Ὁρμήσουσι πρὸς ἡμᾶς ἀγριωτέρως  
> („They will rush upon us more fiercely)
>
> 18 „Ἢ κύων ὁρμᾷ λαγὼν ἐπιδιώκων.”  
> („Than a hound rushes pursuing a hare.”)

- *Τῆς δ᾽ ὀργῆς προστεθείσης τῇ δυσμενείᾳ* turns the finite condition *Se l' ira … s' aggueffa* into a genitive absolute with conditional force, ‘if wrath is added to ill will’. *προστεθείσης* is the feminine genitive singular aorist passive participle of *προστίθημι*, agreeing with its own subject *τῆς ὀργῆς*. *τῇ δυσμενείᾳ* is the dative of that to which the addition is made, replacing *sopra il mal voler*. Connective *δ᾽* is elided *δέ*.
- *Ὁρμήσουσι*, third person plural future active indicative of *ὁρμάω*, preserves the future of *verranno* but changes its direction and manner: *πρὸς ἡμᾶς* with the accusative is ‘towards us’, not *dietro*. The verb supplies the rush; comparative adverb *ἀγριωτέρως*, ‘more fiercely’, modifies that verb instead of qualifying its subject as *più crudeli* does.
- *Ἢ κύων ὁρμᾷ* explicitly repeats the action after comparative *ἤ*, ‘than’: *κύων* is nominative singular, and contracted *ὁρμᾷ* is third person singular present active indicative, generic rather than future. *λαγὼν ἐπιδιώκων* means ‘pursuing a hare’: *λαγών* is the accusative singular of Attic *λαγώς*, object of the masculine nominative singular present active participle of *ἐπιδιώκω*, which agrees with *κύων*. The participle replaces *a quella lepre ch' egli acceffa* without the demonstrative, the relative clause, or the specific act of snapping.

> 19 Già mi sentia tutti arricciar li peli  
> 20 della paura, e stava indietro intento,  
> 21 quando io dissi: “Maestro, se non celi  

Already I felt my hair all rise with fear; and was looking back intently, as I said: "Master, if thou do not hide

> 19 Ἤδη τὰς τρίχας ᾐσθόμην ὀρθουμένας  
> (Already I felt my hairs standing on end)
>
> 20 Ὑπὸ τοῦ φόβου, καὶ θεωρῶν ὀπίσω,  
> (From fear, and looking behind,)
>
> 21 “Ἢν μὴ σεαυτόν,” εἶπον, “κἀμέ, Προστάτα,  
> (“Unless yourself,” I said, “and me, Protector,)

- *Ἤδη* directly renders *Già*. *τὰς τρίχας ᾐσθόμην ὀρθουμένας* uses the Greek construction of perception with an accusative and supplementary participle: ‘I felt the hairs standing upright’. *ᾐσθόμην* is first person singular second aorist middle indicative of *αἰσθάνομαι*, with temporal augment, in place of imperfect *sentia*. *ὀρθουμένας*, feminine accusative plural present middle-passive participle of *ὀρθόω*, agrees with *τὰς τρίχας* and represents *arricciar* as simultaneous with the perception. The article suffices for one's own hairs; *tutti* has no equivalent.
- *Ὑπὸ τοῦ φόβου* uses *ὑπό* with the genitive for the producing cause, *della paura*. *καὶ θεωρῶν ὀπίσω*, ‘and looking behind’, compresses *e stava indietro intento*: *θεωρῶν* is the contracted masculine nominative singular present active participle of *θεωρέω*, agreeing with the speaker. There is no separate adjective for *intento*. The looking becomes attendant on the main verb *εἶπον*, whereas Italian makes *stava* finite and subordinates the speaking with *quando*.
- *Ἢν μὴ σεαυτόν … κἀμέ* begins the condition before the form of address. *ἤν* is a contracted form of conditional *ἐάν*, normally introducing a subjunctive; *μή* negates the condition. No verb corresponding to *celi* has yet appeared in the Greek. Instead, Musurus has already supplied its two accusative objects, the second person singular reflexive *σεαυτόν*, ‘yourself’, and *κἀμέ*, ‘and me’, formed by crasis of *καὶ ἐμέ*.
- *εἶπον*, here first person singular second aorist active indicative of *λέγω*, interrupts the direct speech rather than preceding it like *io dissi*. *Προστάτα* is the vocative singular of masculine *προστάτης*, ‘protector’; Musurus changes the title *Maestro* and moves the address to the end of the line.

## Lines 22–54 — A Mother’s Haste in Flight

> 22 te e me tostamente, i' ho pavento  
> 23 di Malebranche: noi gli avem già dietro;  
> 24 io gl' imagino sì che già gli sento."  

thyself and me speedily, I dread the Malebranche: they are already after us; I so imagine them that I hear them now.”

> 22 „Κρύψῃς ταχέως, ἐμβάλλουσί μοι τρόμον  
> („You hide swiftly, they cast terror into me,)
>
> 23 „Οἱ Κακώνυχες· νῦν εἰσι παρὰ πόδας·  
> („The Evil-claws: now they are close at our heels;)
>
> 24 „Πέπεισμαι δ᾽ οὕτως, ὥστ᾽ αὐτοὺς δοκῶ βλέπειν.”  
> („And I am so convinced of it, that I seem to see them.”)

- *Κρύψῃς ταχέως* ‘that you hide swiftly’ continues a construction begun before line 22. *Κρύψῃς* is 2nd sg. aor. act. subj. of *κρύπτω*, not a present indicative or an imperative; *ταχέως* renders *tostamente*. The Greek does not repeat the objects *te e me* in this line.
- *Ἐμβάλλουσί μοι τρόμον οἱ Κακώνυχες* reverses *i’ ho pavento di Malebranche*: ‘the Evil-claws strike terror into me’. *Ἐμβάλλουσι* is 3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *ἐμβάλλω*, with acc. *τρόμον* for what is instilled and dat. *μοι* for the person affected; the extra accent in *ἐμβάλλουσί μοι* is due to the enclitic. The delayed articular subject *οἱ Κακώνυχες* is a Greek compound name, with *ὄνυξ* ‘claw’ for *branca*.
- *Νῦν εἰσι παρὰ πόδας* ‘now they are at [our] heels’ replaces *noi gli avem già dietro*: the pursuers become the subject of *εἰσι* (3rd pl. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*); *παρά* with acc. pl. *πόδας* gives their proximity without a possessive. *Νῦν* is here rather than in the following result clause.
- *Πέπεισμαι δ᾽ οὕτως* ‘and I am so convinced’ substitutes a settled conviction for *io gl’ imagino*: *πέπεισμαι* is 1st sg. perf. mid./pass. ind. of *πείθω*, with present-result sense. In *ὥστ᾽ αὐτοὺς δοκῶ βλέπειν*, *ὥστε* introduces a finite result clause, ‘so that I seem to see them’; the pres. act. inf. *βλέπειν* depends on *δοκῶ*, and *αὐτούς* is its object. Sight replaces the hearing of *gli sento*. Both *δέ* and *ὥστε* lose their final vowel before a vowel.

> 25 E quei: "S' io fossi d' impiombato vetro,  
> 26 l' imagine di fuor tua non trarrei  
> 27 più tosto a me, che quella d' entro impetro.  

And he: “If I were of leaded glass, I should not draw thy outward image more quickly to me, than I impress that from within.

> 25 Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπεν· “Εἰ κάτοπτρον ἤμην ὑέλου,  
> (And he said: “If I were a mirror of glass,)
>
> 26 „Οὐκ ἂν τάχιον τὴν σὴν ἐκτὸς εἰκόνα  
> („Not sooner would I your outward image)
>
> 27 „Ἀντανέκλων, ἢ τὴν ἔνδον σου νῦν βλέπω.  
> („Reflect back, than I now see your inward one.)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπεν* expands *E quei* with an explicit verb: *ὁ* has pronominal force, ‘he in turn’, and *εἶπεν* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*. *Εἰ κάτοπτρον ἤμην ὑέλου* makes the implied mirror explicit: *κάτοπτρον* is predicate nom. neut. sg., *ὑέλου* a genitive of material from *ὕελος* ‘glass’. The qualification *impiombato* disappears.
- *Εἰ … ἤμην, οὐκ ἂν … ἀντανέκλων* is a present contrary-to-fact condition: imperfect indicative in the protasis, imperfect indicative with *ἄν* in the apodosis, not an optative. *Ἤμην* is the later 1st sg. imperfect of *εἰμί* beside Attic *ἦ*; *ἀντανέκλων* is the internally augmented, contracted 1st sg. imperfect active of *ἀντανακλάω* ‘reflect back’. Reflection replaces drawing the image *a me*.
- *Τάχιον* is the comparative adverb ‘more quickly’. *Τὴν σὴν ἐκτὸς εἰκόνα* groups the possessive and the adverb *ἐκτός* within the article–noun phrase; *ἢ τὴν ἔνδον σου* ‘than the inward [image] of you’ suppresses the repeated noun but retains its fem. acc. sg. article. *Νῦν βλέπω* ‘I now see’ is factual pres. act. ind., not governed by *ἄν*: Musurus interprets *impetro* as immediate inward perception.

> 28 Pur mo venian li tuoi pensier tra i miei  
> 29 con simile atto e con simile faccia,  
> 30 sì che d' intrambi un sol consiglio fei.  

Even now thy thoughts [were] enter[ing] among mine, with similar act and similar face; so that of both I have made one resolve.

> 28 „Ἤδη ταῖς ἐμαῖς αἱ σαὶ συνῆλθον σκέψεις  
> („Already with mine your thoughts have met)
>
> 29 „Τὸν αὐτὸν τρόπον, ὑπὸ μορφὴν ὁμοίαν,  
> („In the same manner, under a like form,)
>
> 30 „Ὥστ᾽ ἀμφοῖν μίαν βουλὴν ἐποιησάμην.  
> („So that of both I have made a single resolve.)

- *Ἤδη ταῖς ἐμαῖς αἱ σαὶ συνῆλθον σκέψεις* ‘already your thoughts met with mine’ puts *ταῖς ἐμαῖς* first, with *σκέψεσι* understood. It is a dative of association with *συνῆλθον* (here 3rd pl. second aor. act. ind. of *συνέρχομαι*, augmented in the verbal element). The separated *αἱ σαὶ … σκέψεις* is the nom. pl. subject. A completed meeting replaces the motion in progress of *venian … tra i miei*.
- *Τὸν αὐτὸν τρόπον* is an adverbial accusative, ‘in the same manner’: *αὐτός* after the article means ‘same’, stronger than *simile*. *Ὑπὸ μορφὴν ὁμοίαν* ‘under a like form’ renders *con simile faccia* by a prepositional phrase; this abstract use of *ὑπό* with the accusative is less characteristically Attic than the preceding accusative of manner.
- *Ὥστ᾽ ἀμφοῖν μίαν βουλὴν ἐποιησάμην* closely follows *sì che d’intrambi un sol consiglio fei*. Elided *ὥστε* introduces an actual result with the indicative. *Ἀμφοῖν* is the common gen./dat. dual form of *ἄμφω*, here genitive, ‘of the two’; *μίαν* (fem. acc. sg. of *εἷς*) sets one resolve against the two contributions. *Ἐποιησάμην* is 1st sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ποιέω*, ‘I formed for myself’, rather than a simple active like *fei*.

> 31 S' egli è che sì la destra costa giaccia,  
> 32 che noi possiam nell' altra bolgia scendere,  
> 33 noi fuggirem l' imaginata caccia."  

In case the right coast so slopes, that we may descend into the other chasm, we shall escape the imagined chase.”

> 31 „Ἢν ἡ δεξιὰ πλευρὰ προσφόρως κλίνῃ  
> („If the right slope inclines suitably)
>
> 32 „Πρὸς κατάβασιν εἰς τὸν ἕτερον βόθρον,  
> („For a descent into the next chasm,)
>
> 33 „Φευξούμεθα δίωξιν, ἣν ὑποπτεύεις.”  
> („We shall escape the pursuit which you suspect.”)

- *Ἢν … κλίνῃ* gives a prospective condition: *ἤν* is a contracted equivalent of *ἐάν*, and *κλίνῃ* is here 3rd sg. pres. act. subj. of *κλίνω*, used intransitively of *ἡ δεξιὰ πλευρά* ‘the right side’. *Προσφόρως* ‘suitably’ expresses the fitness implicit in *sì … che*.
- *Πρὸς κατάβασιν εἰς τὸν ἕτερον βόθρον* turns *che noi possiam … scendere* into ‘for a descent into the other pit’. *Πρός* + acc. marks suitability for a purpose; *εἰς* + acc. marks destination. The verbal noun *κατάβασις* dispenses with both the personal subject and the verb of possibility.
- *Φευξούμεθα* is the contracted future middle indicative, 1st pl., of *φεύγω*, with active sense and a direct object *δίωξιν* ‘pursuit’. The relative clause *ἣν ὑποπτεύεις* ‘which you suspect’ replaces the participial adjective *imaginata*: *ἥν* takes its feminine singular from *δίωξιν*, its accusative from *ὑποπτεύεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind.).

> 34 Già non compiè di tal consiglio rendere,  
> 35 ch' io gli vidi venir con l' ali tese,  
> 36 non molto lungi, per volerne prendere.  

He had not ended giving this resolve, when I saw them come with wings extended, not far off, in will to seize us.

> 34 Οὐκ ἔφθη γνώμην φράσας τοιάνδ᾽, ὅτ᾽ εἶδον  
> (Scarcely had he finished telling such a resolve, when I saw)
>
> 35 Αὐτοὺς ἥκοντας πτέρυξι τεταμέναις,  
> (Them coming with outstretched wings,)
>
> 36 Οὐ μακρὰν ἡμῶν, κατασχεῖν βουλομένους.  
> (Not far from us, wishing to seize us.)

- *Οὐκ ἔφθη γνώμην φράσας τοιάνδ᾽* uses *φθάνω* with a supplementary participle, ‘he had not got so far as to utter such a resolve’. *Ἔφθη* is 3rd sg. intransitive root aor. active indicative, not a passive; *φράσας* is aor. act. participle of *φράζω*, nom. masc. sg. with its subject. *Τοιάνδ᾽* is elided *τοιάνδε*, fem. acc. sg. of *τοιόσδε*, agreeing across the participle with *γνώμην*.
- *Ὅτ᾽ εἶδον αὐτοὺς ἥκοντας* ‘when I saw them approaching’ renders *ch’ io gli vidi venir*. After elided *ὅτε*, *εἶδον* is 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*; Greek uses the accusative with a supplementary pres. act. participle of *ἥκω* after a verb of perception, rather than the Italian infinitive.
- *Πτέρυξι τεταμέναις* is a dative of attendant circumstance, ‘with wings outstretched’; *πτέρυξι* is dat. pl. of *πτέρυξ*, and the agreeing perf. mid./pass. participle of *τείνω* expresses the resulting posture. *Οὐ μακρὰν ἡμῶν* supplies a genitive of distance from a point, ‘not far from us’, for *non molto lungi*.
- *Κατασχεῖν βουλομένους* renders *per volerne prendere* by a second participle agreeing with *αὐτούς*: *βουλομένους* is pres. mid. acc. masc. pl. of *βούλομαι* ‘wish’, with complementary second aor. act. inf. *κατασχεῖν* of *κατέχω* ‘seize, hold fast’. Its object, expressed by Italian *-ne*, is understood; *ἡμῶν* belongs with *μακράν*, not to the infinitive.

> 37 Lo duca mio di subito mi prese,  

My Guide suddenly took me,

> 37 Οὑμὸς δ᾽ Ἡγήτωρ εὐθὺς ἐλάβετό μου,  
> (And my Leader straightway took hold of me,)

- *Οὑμὸς δ᾽ Ἡγήτωρ* ‘and my leader’ has crasis of *ὁ ἐμός* in *οὑμός*, an Attic poetic compression of article and possessive; the rough breathing is retained. *Εὐθύς* ‘at once’ renders *di subito*. *Ἐλάβετό μου* is 3rd sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *λαμβάνω*; the middle in the sense ‘take hold of’ governs gen. *μου*, unlike the direct object *mi* of *prese*. The enclitic accounts for the added final accent on *ἐλάβετό*.

> 38 come la madre ch' al romore è desta,  
> 39 e vede presso a sè le fiamme accese,  
> 40 che prende il figlio e fugge e non s' arresta,  

as a mother—that is awakened by the noise, and near her sees the kindled flames— who takes her child and flies,

> 38 Οἷά τις μήτηρ ἐγερθεῖσ᾽ ἐκ θορύβου,  
> (Just as a mother awakened by an uproar,)
>
> 39 Κἀγγὺς αὑτῆς βλέπουσα φλόγας ἡμμένας,  
> (And seeing near herself kindled flames,)
>
> 40 Τοῦ παιδὸς δραξαμένη φεύγει κοὐ μένει,  
> (Seizing her child flees and does not linger,)

- *Οἷά τις μήτηρ* ‘just as a mother’ uses comparative adverbial *οἷα* (in origin neut. pl.), not a fem. adjective agreeing with *μήτηρ*; enclitic *τις* makes the comparison indefinite and gives *οἷά* its extra accent. *Ἐγερθεῖσ᾽ ἐκ θορύβου* replaces *ch’ al romore è desta* with the elided fem. nom. sg. aor. pass. participle *ἐγερθεῖσα* of *ἐγείρω*; *ἐκ* with the genitive indicates the cause of waking.
- *Κἀγγὺς αὑτῆς βλέπουσα φλόγας ἡμμένας* continues with a simultaneous pres. act. participle, *βλέπουσα*, instead of finite *vede*. *Κἀγγύς* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγγύς*; *ἐγγύς* governs *αὑτῆς*, the contracted fem. gen. sg. reflexive *ἑαυτῆς*. *Ἡμμένας* is fem. acc. pl. perf. mid./pass. participle of *ἅπτω* ‘kindle’, agreeing with *φλόγας*: the flames are already alight.
- *Τοῦ παιδὸς δραξαμένη* ‘having grasped the child’ turns *che prende il figlio* into an antecedent action: *δραξαμένη* is fem. nom. sg. aor. mid. participle of *δράσσομαι*, with the genitive of what is grasped. *Φεύγει κοὐ μένει* finally gives finite verbs, both 3rd sg. pres. act. ind.: ‘she flees and does not wait’. *Κοὐ* is crasis of *καὶ οὐ*. The second verb corresponds to *e non s’arresta* in line 40, though the English defers “pauses not” until after the phrase of caring.

> 41 avendo più di lui che di sè cura,  
> 42 tanto che solo una camicia vesta;  

and caring more for him than for herself, pauses not so long as even to cast a shift about her;

> 41 Ἧττον φροντίζουσ᾽ ἑαυτῆς ἢ τοῦ τέκνου,  
> (Caring less for herself than for her child,)
>
> 42 Οὕτως, ὥσθ᾽ ἕνα μόνον φορεῖν χιτῶνα.  
> (So much so, as to wear only a single shift.)

- *Ἧττον φροντίζουσ᾽ ἑαυτῆς ἢ τοῦ τέκνου* reverses the terms of *più di lui che di sè*: ‘caring less for herself than for the child’. Comparative adverb *ἧττον* correlates with *ἤ*; both genitives depend on *φροντίζουσα*, the elided fem. nom. sg. pres. act. participle of *φροντίζω*. The participle corresponds to *avendo … cura*, and *τέκνου* replaces the pronoun *lui*.
- *Οὕτως, ὥσθ᾽ ἕνα μόνον φορεῖν χιτῶνα* takes *tanto che solo una camicia vesta* as ‘so as to wear only one shift’, not as a failure even to put one on. Here *ὥστε* takes an infinitive of result; elision before the rough breathing of *ἕνα* changes the final *τ* to *θ*. *Φορεῖν*, contracted pres. act. inf. of *φορέω*, denotes wearing, not putting on; its subject remains the mother. *Ἕνα μόνον* is masc. acc. sg. with *χιτῶνα*.

> 43 e giù dal collo della ripa dura  
> 44 supin si diede alla pendente roccia,  
> 45 che l' un dei lati all' altra bolgia tura.  

and down from the ridge of the hard bank, supine he gave himself to the pendent rock, which dams up one side of the other chasm.

> 43 Ἐκ δὲ τῆς ἄκρας τῆς σκληρᾶς ὄχθης κάτω  
> (And from the edge of the hard bank down)
>
> 44 Παρὰ τὸν κρημνὸν ὠλίσθησεν ὑπτίως,  
> (Along the cliff he slid supine,)
>
> 45 Τὸν ἐντεῦθεν φράττοντα τὸν ἄλλον βόθρον.  
> (Which on this side encloses the next chasm.)

- *Ἐκ δὲ τῆς ἄκρας τῆς σκληρᾶς ὄχθης κάτω* brings the point of departure first and postpones directional *κάτω* ‘down’ to the end of the line. *Ἐκ* governs *τῆς ἄκρας* ‘from the crest’; *τῆς σκληρᾶς ὄχθης* is the dependent genitive ‘of the hard bank’. *Ἄκρα* gives the topographical sense rather than the bodily figure of *collo*.
- *Παρὰ τὸν κρημνὸν ὠλίσθησεν ὑπτίως* replaces *supin si diede alla pendente roccia* by ‘he slid along the cliff on his back’. *Παρά* with the accusative traces the course; the sheer slope is conveyed by *κρημνός*, without a separate equivalent for *pendente*. *Ὠλίσθησεν* is 3rd sg. aor. active indicative of *ὀλισθαίνω*, with temporal augment and the aorist stem *ὀλισθησ-*; it is intransitive, not reflexive. Adverbial *ὑπτίως* replaces the predicative adjective *supin*.
- *Τὸν ἐντεῦθεν φράττοντα τὸν ἄλλον βόθρον* is a second-attributive-position participial phrase following *τὸν κρημνόν*: ‘the [cliff] enclosing the other pit on this side’. *Φράττοντα*, pres. act. participle of Attic *φράττω*, agrees in masc. acc. sg. with *κρημνόν* and takes *τὸν ἄλλον βόθρον* as object. The Italian relative clause has *l’un dei lati* as object; Greek expresses the side adverbially by *ἐντεῦθεν*.

> 46 Non corse mai sì tosto acqua per doccia  
> 47 a volger rota di molin terragno,  
> 48 quand' ella più verso le pale approccia,  

Never did water run so fast through spout to turn a land-mill’s wheel, when it approaches nearest to the ladles,

> 46 Οὐ δι᾽ ὀχετοῦ ῥέει ταχέως ὕδωρ  
> (Not so swiftly through a conduit does water flow)
>
> 47 Στρέφον τὸν τροχὸν μυλῶνος ἐπιγείου,  
> (Turning the wheel of a landward mill,)
>
> 48 Ὅτε κύκλωθεν τῶν πτερῶν ἐπιπίπτει,  
> (When it falls down around the paddles,)

- *Οὐ δι᾽ ὀχετοῦ ῥέει ταχέως ὕδωρ* substitutes a general present, ‘water does not flow swiftly through a conduit’, for *Non corse mai sì tosto acqua*. There is no separate *mai* or *sì*: the degree comparison is completed by *ὡς* in line 49. *Δι᾽* is elided *διά* with gen. of passage; *ῥέει* is the uncontracted 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ῥέω* (Attic *ῥεῖ*). Its postponed subject *ὕδωρ* is neut. nom. sg.
- *Στρέφον τὸν τροχὸν μυλῶνος ἐπιγείου* ‘turning the wheel of a mill on land’ gives an accompanying action rather than the purpose infinitive *a volger*. *Στρέφον* is the neut. nom. sg. pres. act. participle of *στρέφω*, agreeing with *ὕδωρ*; *μυλῶνος* (gen. sg. of *μυλών*) depends on *τροχόν*, and *ἐπιγείου* qualifies the mill, as *terragno* qualifies *molin*.
- *Ὅτε κύκλωθεν τῶν πτερῶν ἐπιπίπτει* ‘when it falls all around the paddles’ retains the temporal clause, with *ἐπιπίπτει* in the pres. act. ind., 3rd sg. The gen. pl. *τῶν πτερῶν* (from *πτερόν*, literally ‘wing’) belongs with adverbial *κύκλωθεν*. Falling around the paddles replaces *più verso le pale approccia*: neither the approach nor the comparative of proximity is retained.

> 49 come il maestro mio per quel vivagno,  
> 50 portandosene me sopra il suo petto,  
> 51 come suo figlio, non come compagno.  

as my Master down that bank, carrying me away upon his breast, as his son and not as his companion.

> 49 Ὡς εἷρπ᾽ ἐπὶ τὸν κρημνὸν οὑμὸς Προστάτης,  
> (As my Protector glided down upon the cliff,)
>
> 50 Περιπλέγδην με φέρων ἐπὶ τοῦ στήθους,  
> (Carrying me clasped upon his breast,)
>
> 51 Ὡς υἱόν, κοὐχὶ συνοπαδὸν ἑταῖρον.  
> (As his son, and not as a fellow-travelling companion.)

- *Ὡς εἷρπ᾽ ἐπὶ τὸν κρημνὸν οὑμὸς Προστάτης* supplies a finite verb where *come il maestro mio per quel vivagno* leaves the motion to be understood. *Εἷρπ᾽* is elided *εἷρπε*, 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἕρπω*, with poetic augmented spelling: the verb presents the movement as continuous, literally ‘crept’ or ‘glided’. *Ἐπί* with the accusative gives direction; *κρημνόν* repeats the cliff of line 44 rather than introducing a distinct word for *vivagno*. *Οὑμός* is again *ὁ ἐμός* in crasis; *Προστάτης* ‘protector’ interprets *maestro* by function.
- *Περιπλέγδην με φέρων ἐπὶ τοῦ στήθους* renders the manner of *portandosene me* by the adverb *περιπλέγδην* ‘clasped round’ and a simple active participle. *Φέρων* is pres. act. nom. masc. sg. of *φέρω*, agreeing with *Προστάτης* and governing acc. *με*; there is no separate particle for motion away. *Ἐπί* with gen. *τοῦ στήθους* (from *στῆθος*) gives the place of support; the article suffices for *suo*.
- *Ὡς υἱόν, κοὐχὶ συνοπαδὸν ἑταῖρον* has two predicate accusatives referring to *με*, ‘as a son, and not [as] a companion attending him’. Greek supplies neither a possessive with *υἱόν* nor a second *ὡς* for the repeated *come*. *Κοὐχί* combines *καὶ οὐχί* by crasis, with emphatic negation; *συνοπαδόν* qualifies *ἑταῖρον*, expanding the single *compagno*.

> 52 Appena fur li piè suoi giunti al letto  
> 53 del fondo giù, ch' ei furono in sul colle  
> 54 sopresso noi; ma non gli era sospetto:  

Scarcely had his feet reached the bed of the depth below, when they [were on] the height above us; but no fear it gave him:

> 52 Μόλις δ᾽ οἱ πόδες ἔψαυσαν τῆς γῆς κάτω,  
> (And scarcely had his feet touched the ground below,)
>
> 53 Καὶ τοὺς δαίμονας εἶδον ἐπὶ τῆς ἄκρας  
> (When I saw the demons upon the ridge)
>
> 54 Ἄνωθεν ἡμῶν· ἀλλ᾿ οὐκέτ᾿ ἐφοβούμην.  
> (Above us; yet no longer was I afraid.)

- *Μόλις δ᾽ οἱ πόδες ἔψαυσαν τῆς γῆς κάτω* renders *Appena fur li piè suoi giunti* by an active aorist, ‘scarcely did the feet touch’. *Ἔψαυσαν* is 3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *ψαύω*, governing gen. *τῆς γῆς*; the ground touched replaces the destination *al letto del fondo*. *Κάτω* retains *giù*, and the possessive *suoi* is left implicit.
- *Καὶ τοὺς δαίμονας εἶδον* answers *μόλις* by coordination, ‘and [then] I saw the demons’, rather than by a subordinate clause corresponding to *ch’*. Musurus supplies a perceiving subject, with 1st sg. aor. *εἶδον* as in line 34, and names the demons in the accusative where *ei furono* has a pronominal subject. *Ἐπὶ τῆς ἄκρας* has the genitive for stationary location, ‘on the crest’; *ἄνωθεν ἡμῶν* ‘above us’ takes another genitive and renders *sopresso noi*.
- *Ἀλλ᾿ οὐκέτ᾿ ἐφοβούμην* ‘but I was no longer afraid’ has elision of both *ἀλλά* and *οὐκέτι*. *Ἐφοβούμην* is 1st sg. contracted imperf. mid./pass. ind. of *φοβέομαι* ‘fear’. Thus *non gli era sospetto*, construed in the English as “but no fear it gave him:”, becomes a personal verb whose subject is the speaker, not the guide; *οὐκέτι* also adds the temporal qualification ‘no longer’ to *non*.

## Lines 55–87 — The Souls in Gilded Cloaks

> 55 chè l'alta Provvidenza, che lor volle  
> 56 porre ministri della fossa quinta,  
> 57 poder di partirs' indi a tutti tolle.  

for the high Providence, that willed to place them ministers of the fifth ditch, takes the power of leaving it from all.

> 55 Ἡ γὰρ θεία πρόνοια φύλακας τούτους  
> (For divine Providence, these as guards)
>
> 56 Εἶναι διατάξασα τοῦ πέμπτου βόθρου,  
> (Having appointed to be of the fifth chasm,)
>
> 57 Ἀπηγόρευσεν ἐκεῖθεν ἀποστῆναι.  
> (Forbade them to depart from thence.)

- In *ἡ γὰρ θεία πρόνοια*, postpositive *γάρ* carries the causal force of initial *chè*, and *θεία* ‘divine’ interprets *alta* rather than translating the image of height. *Διατάξασα* (nom. fem. sg., aor. act. participle of *διατάσσω*) agrees with *πρόνοια*: the relative clause *che lor volle porre* becomes a prior act, ‘having ordered’, without a separate verb of willing.
- *Φύλακας τούτους εἶναι* means ‘that these should be guards’: *τούτους* is the accusative subject of the present infinitive *εἶναι*, and the fronted *φύλακας* its predicate accusative. *Ministri* thus becomes the more specific *φύλακας*, with *τοῦ πέμπτου βόθρου* dependent on it across the participle.
- *Ἀπηγόρευσεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. indic. of *ἀπαγορεύω*, with internal augment) replaces present *poder … tolle* ‘takes away the power’ with ‘forbade’. Its complement *ἐκεῖθεν ἀποστῆναι* renders *partirs' indi*: the adverb already means ‘from there’, and *ἀποστῆναι* is the intransitive second aorist active infinitive of *ἀφίστημι*, not a reflexive. The persons forbidden are understood from *τούτους*; *a tutti* has no separate equivalent.

> 58 Laggiù trovammo una gente dipinta,  
> 59 che giva intorno assai con lenti passi  
> 60 piangendo, e nel sembiante stanca e vinta.  

There beneath we found a painted people, who were going round with steps exceeding slow, weeping, and in their look tired and overcome.

> 58 Ἐκεῖ κάτω δ᾿ εὕρομεν ψυχὰς εὐχρόους,  
> (And down there we found fair-hued souls,)
>
> 59 Βαδιζούσας βραδέως πέριξ τοῦ βόθρου,  
> (Walking slowly round about the chasm,)
>
> 60 Θρηνούσας, φλαύρας, τετρυμένας τὴν θέαν.  
> (Weeping, wretched, and worn in countenance.)

- *Ἐκεῖ κάτω* gives the two spatial elements of *laggiù* separately. *Δ᾿* is connective *δέ* elided before *εὕρομεν* (1st pl. second aor. act. indic. of *εὑρίσκω*, with *ευ-* rather than the augmented *ηυ-* also found in Attic). The collective singular *una gente dipinta* becomes *ψυχὰς εὐχρόους* ‘well-coloured souls’; the adjective denotes an appearance rather than an act of painting.
- The relative *che giva* becomes *βαδιζούσας*, a present active participle, acc. fem. pl. agreeing with *ψυχάς*. *Βραδέως* ‘slowly’ replaces *assai con lenti passi* without a noun for ‘steps’ or an intensifier; *πέριξ τοῦ βόθρου* makes explicit what they walk around, with adverbial *πέριξ* governing a genitive.
- *Θρηνούσας* (pres. act. participle of *θρηνέω*) continues the same agreement for *piangendo*. *Φλαύρας* ‘in poor condition’ and *τετρυμένας* ‘worn out’ redistribute *stanca e vinta*. The latter is a reduplicated perfect middle-passive participle of *τρύω*, expressing a resulting state. Final *τὴν θέαν* ‘as to their appearance’ is an accusative of respect for *nel sembiante*.

> 61 Egli avean cappe con cappucci bassi  
> 62 dinanzi agli occhi, fatte della taglia  
> 63 che per li monaci in Cologna fassi.  

They had cloaks on, with deep hoods before their eyes, made in the shape that they make for the monks in Cologne.

> 61 Ἔφερον δὲ μανδύας σὺν κατωτίσιν  
> (And they wore cloaks with hoods)
>
> 62 Ὀφθαλμοὺς καλυπτούσαις, κατὰ τὸν τρόπον  
> (Covering their eyes, after the fashion)
>
> 63 Τῆς στολῆς τῶν μοναχῶν τῆς Κολωνίας,  
> (Of the habit of the monks of Cologne,)

- *Ἔφερον δὲ μανδύας* ‘and they wore cloaks’ puts the garment in the accusative after *ἔφερον* (here 3rd pl. imperf. act. indic. of *φέρω*); the subject corresponding to *egli* is unexpressed. *Σὺν κατωτίσιν* renders *con cappucci* by *σύν* with the dative plural of *κατωτίς*, a covering hanging down.
- *Ὀφθαλμοὺς καλυπτούσαις* ‘covering eyes’ replaces *bassi dinanzi agli occhi*: the present active participle of *καλύπτω* is dat. fem. pl. agreeing with *κατωτίσιν*, not with the cloaks. Greek expresses what the position of the hoods does, without a separate adjective for *bassi*.
- *Κατὰ τὸν τρόπον τῆς στολῆς* ‘after the fashion of the dress’ dispenses with both *fatte* and the relative *che … fassi*. The succession of genitives makes *τῶν μοναχῶν* possessive rather than benefactive (*per li monaci*), and *τῆς Κολωνίας* an affiliation of the monks rather than a phrase with *in*.

> 64 Di fuor dorate son sì ch' egli abbaglia,  
> 65 ma dentro tutte piombo, e gravi tanto,  
> 66 che Federico le mettea di paglia.  

Outward they are gilded, so that it dazzles; but within all lead, and so heavy, that Frederick's compared to them were straw.

> 64 Ἐκτὸς μὲν χρυσοῦς, περιλαμπεῖς, ἀλλ᾿ ἔνδον  
> (Outside indeed golden, dazzling bright, yet inside)
>
> 65 Ἐκ μολύβδου, καὶ βαρεῖς τοσοῦτον, ὥστε  
> (Of lead, and so heavy, that)
>
> 66 Οἱ Φρεδερίκου πρὸς αὐτοὺς ἦσαν ἄχναι.  
> (Those of Frederick compared to them were chaff.)

- Rather than begin a new finite clause for *dorate son*, Greek continues the description of *μανδύας* in the accusative masculine plural: *χρυσοῦς* (contracted from *χρυσέους*) ‘golden’, *περιλαμπεῖς* ‘brilliant’, then *βαρεῖς* ‘heavy’. The second adjective compresses the whole result clause *sì ch' egli abbaglia* into a quality of the cloaks.
- *Ἐκτὸς μὲν … ἀλλ᾿ ἔνδον* ‘outside indeed … but inside’ marks the contrast with anticipatory *μέν* and elided *ἀλλά*. *Ἐκ μολύβδου* uses *ἐκ* with the genitive of material where Italian has simply *piombo*; there is no equivalent of *tutte*.
- *Τοσοῦτον* is an adverbial accusative of degree, answering to *tanto* and introducing *ὥστε* with the indicative *ἦσαν* (3rd pl. imperf. of *εἰμί*) for an asserted result. *Οἱ Φρεδερίκου* ‘Frederick’s ones’ supplies ‘cloaks’ from the masculine article; Frederick, the subject of *mettea*, becomes a possessive genitive. *Πρὸς αὐτούς* ‘in comparison with them’ makes the comparison explicit, and predicate *ἄχναι* ‘chaff’ replaces *di paglia*.

> 67 O in eterno faticoso manto!  

O weary mantle for eternity!

> 67 Ὦ χλαίνης αἰωνίως συγκαταθλώσης !  
> (O cloak eternally crushing them down!)

- *Ὦ χλαίνης* uses the exclamatory genitive ‘O for the cloak!’, not a vocative. *Αἰωνίως* turns *in eterno* into an adverb modifying *συγκαταθλώσης* (gen. fem. sg. pres. act. participle of *συγκαταθλάω*). For the adjective *faticoso* Greek thus gives the garment an ongoing action, ‘crushing down’; the object is understood.

> 68 Noi ci volgemmo ancor pure a man manca  
> 69 con loro insieme, intenti al tristo pianto;  

We turned again to the left hand, along with them, intent upon their dreary weeping;

> 68 Ἡμεῖς δ᾿ ἐπ᾿ ἀριστερὰ στραφέντες πάλιν  
> (And we, turning to the left again)
>
> 69 Μετ᾿ αὐτῶν, προσείχομεν τοῖς πικροῖς θρήνοις.  
> (Along with them, gave heed to their bitter lamentations.)

- *Ἡμεῖς δ᾿* retains emphatic *noi*, but reverses the grammatical weighting of the two actions. *Στραφέντες* (nom. masc. pl., second aor. pass. participle of *στρέφω*, intransitive in sense) renders finite reflexive *ci volgemmo* as ‘having turned’. *Ἐπ᾿ ἀριστερά* is elided *ἐπί* with a directional neuter plural accusative, ‘to the left’; *πάλιν* gives ‘again’, without separately reproducing *pure*.
- *Μετ᾿ αὐτῶν* (*μετά* with the genitive) suffices for *con loro insieme*. *Προσείχομεν* (1st pl. imperf. act. indic. of *προσέχω*) makes *intenti* the finite action, ‘we were attending’; it governs the dative *τοῖς πικροῖς θρήνοις* ‘to the bitter laments’, plural for singular *al tristo pianto*.

> 70 ma per lo peso quella gente stanca  
> 71 venia sì pian, che noi eravam nuovi  
> 72 di compagnia ad ogni muover d' anca.  

but that people, tired by their burden, came so slowly that our company was new at every movement of the hip.

> 70 Ἀλλ᾿ ἐκ τοῦ βάρους αἱ ψυχαὶ κεκμηκυῖαι  
> (But the souls, wearied by the weight,)
>
> 71 Οὕτως ἐβραδυπόρουν, ὥστ᾿ ἀπηντῶμεν  
> (Walked so slowly, that we met)
>
> 72 Νέαις συμβαδιζούσαις κατὰ πᾶν βῆμα.  
> (New ones walking alongside at every step.)

- *Ἀλλ᾿ ἐκ τοῦ βάρους* gives *ma per lo peso* a causal *ἐκ* with the genitive. *Αἱ ψυχαὶ κεκμηκυῖαι* resumes the plural ‘souls’: *κεκμηκυῖαι* (nom. fem. pl., perfect active participle of *κάμνω*, from *κέκμηκα*) describes their continuing exhaustion.
- *Οὕτως ἐβραδυπόρουν* incorporates *venia sì pian* into a verb of slow travel (3rd pl. imperf. act. indic. of *βραδυπορέω*) modified by ‘so’. Elided *ὥστ᾿* introduces the indicative *ἀπηντῶμεν* (1st pl. imperf. act. of *ἀπαντάω*), ‘so that we kept meeting’: the Italian predicate *eravam nuovi di compagnia* becomes a repeated encounter.
- *Νέαις συμβαδιζούσαις* is the dative complement of ‘meet’, ‘new ones walking alongside’, with *ψυχαῖς* understood and a present active participle of *συμβαδίζω*. Newness now belongs to the souls encountered, not to *noi*. Distributive *κατὰ πᾶν βῆμα* ‘at each step’ replaces the bodily detail of *ad ogni muover d' anca*.

> 73 Per ch' io al duca mio: "Fa che tu trovi  
> 74 alcun ch' al fatto o al nome si conosca,  
> 75 e gli occhi sì andando intorno movi."  

Wherefore I to my Guide: "See that thou find some one who may by deed or name be known; and move thy eyes around as we go on."

> 73 Τῷ ᾿μῷ δ᾿ εἶπον Προστάτῃ· “ Μεταξὺ τούτων  
> (And to my Protector I said: “Among these)
>
> 74 „ Τοὔνομ᾽ ἔστι τις γνώριμος ἢ τὰς πράξεις ;  
> („ Is there anyone known by name or by his deeds?)
>
> 75 „ Κύκλῳ στρέψον ὀφθαλμούς· τάχ᾽ ἄν τιν᾽ εὕροις.”  
> („ Turn your eyes around; perchance you may find someone.”)

- *Τῷ ᾿μῷ … Προστάτῃ* ‘to my protector’ frames *δ᾿ εἶπον* ‘and I said’. *᾿Μῷ* shows prodelision of the initial vowel of *ἐμῷ* after the article; article, possessive and noun are all dat. masc. sg. *Εἶπον* (1st sg. second aor. act. indic., used as the aorist of *λέγω*) supplies the speech verb missing from *io al duca mio*. Connective *δέ* does not express the full causal force of *per ch'*.
- *Μεταξὺ τούτων* ‘among these’ supplies a field of search, with *μεταξύ* governing the genitive. Instead of *fa che tu trovi alcun ch' … si conosca*, Musurus asks a direct question: *ἔστι τις γνώριμος* ‘is anyone recognizable?’ Enclitic indefinite *τις* is the subject and *γνώριμος* the predicate adjective. *Τοὔνομ᾽* (crasis of *τὸ ὄνομα*, with final elision before *ἔστι*) and *τὰς πράξεις* are accusatives of respect, ‘as to name or deeds’, reversing *al fatto o al nome* and pluralizing ‘deeds’.
- *Κύκλῳ* is an adverbial dative ‘round about’. *Στρέψον* (2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *στρέφω*) takes bare *ὀφθαλμούς* as object, replacing *movi* with ‘turn’ and dropping *sì andando*. The request to find someone reappears as a possibility: *τάχ᾽ ἄν τιν᾽ εὕροις* ‘perhaps you might find someone’, with elided *τάχα* and *τινά* and the potential *ἄν* with the second aorist active optative (2nd sg. of *εὑρίσκω*).

> 76 Ed un che intese la parola Tosca,  
> 77 di retro a noi gridò: "Tenete i piedi,  
> 78 voi che correte sì per l' aura fosca;  
> 79 forse ch' avrai da me quel che tu chiedi."  

And one, who understood the Tuscan speech, cried after us: "Stay your feet, ye who run so fast through the brown air; perhaps thou shalt obtain from me that which thou askest."

> 76 Εἷς δ᾽ ἐξ αὐτῶν Ἐτρούσκων φωνὴν ἀκούσας  
> (And one of them, hearing the Tuscan speech,)
>
> 77 Ὄπισθ᾽ ἡμῶν ἔκραξεν· “Ἴσχετε πόδας  
> (Behind us cried out: “Stay your feet,)
>
> 78 „ Ὑμεῖς, οἳ δι᾽ ὀρφνώδους τρέχετ᾽ ἀέρος.  
> („ You who run through the gloomy air.)
>
> 79 „ Ἴσως ἄν, ὃ σὺ ζητεῖς, παρ᾽ ἐμοῦ μάθοις.”  
> („ Perhaps what you seek you may learn from me.”)

- *Εἷς δ᾽ ἐξ αὐτῶν* makes *un* explicitly partitive, ‘one of them’. *Ἐτρούσκων φωνὴν ἀκούσας* replaces the relative *che intese la parola Tosca* by a nom. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *ἀκούω* agreeing with *εἷς*. ‘Having heard’ need not itself mean ‘having understood’; the ethnic genitive plural ‘of the Etruscans’ modifies ‘speech’ in place of the Italian adjective.
- *Ὄπισθ᾽ ἡμῶν* is *ὄπισθε* with elision and aspiration before the rough breathing, governing a genitive as does ‘behind’ in *di retro a noi*. *Ἔκραξεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. indic. of *κράζω*) gives *gridò*. *Ἴσχετε πόδας* closely follows *tenete i piedi*: *ἴσχετε* is a 2nd pl. present active imperative of *ἴσχω* ‘hold, check’, with the possessors of the feet understood.
- *Ὑμεῖς, οἳ … τρέχετ᾽* preserves the direct address *voi che correte*: the relative verb is second person after the personal antecedent (*τρέχετε*, 2nd pl. pres. act. indic.). *Δι᾽ ὀρφνώδους … ἀέρος* uses *διά* with the genitive for movement through something; poetic *ὀρφνώδης* means ‘dark, murky’. The verb intervenes between adjective and noun, and *sì* has no separate rendering.
- *Ἴσως ἄν … μάθοις* changes *forse ch' avrai* from a qualified future of possession to the potential optative ‘perhaps you might learn’ (*μάθοις*, 2nd sg. second aor. act. opt. of *μανθάνω*). *Παρ᾽ ἐμοῦ* marks the personal source with *παρά* and the genitive. The intervening relative *ὃ σὺ ζητεῖς* ‘what you seek’ needs no expressed *quel*; emphatic singular *σύ* retains the Italian shift from plural address to *tu*.

> 80 Onde il duca si volse, e disse: "Aspetta,  
> 81 e poi secondo il suo passo procedi."  

Whereat my Guide turned round and said: "Wait, and then at his pace proceed."

> 80 Οὑμὸς δ᾽ Ἡγήτωρ στραφεὶς εἶπέ μοι· “Μένε,  
> (And my Leader, turning, said to me: “Wait,)
>
> 81 „ Προχώρει δ᾽ εἶτα κατὰ τὸ βῆμα τούτου.”  
> („ And then go forward according to this one's pace.”)

- *Οὑμὸς … Ἡγήτωρ* ‘my leader’ has crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*; *ἡγήτωρ* is a poetic agent noun. Connective *δέ* stands for consequential *onde*. *Στραφείς* (nom. masc. sg., second aor. pass. participle of *στρέφω*) subordinates *si volse* to *εἶπέ μοι* ‘said to me’; the Greek supplies the recipient, and the enclitic *μοι* accounts for the additional accent on *εἶπέ*.
- *Μένε* and *προχώρει* are 2nd sg. present active imperatives of *μένω* and *προχωρέω*: ‘stay’ and ‘go forward’. *Δ᾽ εἶτα* renders *e poi*, but follows the second imperative. *Κατὰ τὸ βῆμα τούτου* uses *κατά* with an accusative of standard, ‘according to this one’s step’; the demonstrative genitive identifies the possessor more pointedly than *suo*.

> 82 Ristetti, e vidi due mostrar gran fretta  
> 83 dell' animo, col viso, d' esser meco;  
> 84 ma tardavagli il carco e la via stretta.  

I stood still, and saw two, showing by their look great haste of mind to be with me; but the load and the narrow way retarded them.

> 82 Στὰς δ᾽ εἶδον δύο πολὺν δεικνύντας πόθον  
> (And halting I saw two showing great eagerness)
>
> 83 Συντυχεῖν ἡμῖν, ὡς ἐκ τῆς αὐτῶν θέας,  
> (To meet with us, to judge from their look,)
>
> 84 Ἀλλ᾽ οὓς ἐβράδυν᾽ ἄχθος καὶ στενὴ τρίβος.  
> (Yet whom their burden and the narrow path delayed.)

- *Στὰς δ᾽ εἶδον* subordinates *ristetti* to *vidi*: *στάς* is the nom. masc. sg. intransitive second aor. act. participle of *ἵστημι*, ‘having stopped’, and *εἶδον* is the 1st sg. second aor. act. indic. used for *ὁράω*. After the perception verb, *δύο … δεικνύντας* gives ‘two in the act of showing’: Greek uses the present active participle of *δείκνυμι* in the accusative masculine plural, not the dual, where Italian has infinitival *mostrar*.
- *Πολὺν … πόθον* ‘great longing’ concentrates *gran fretta dell' animo* in an emotional noun. Its complement *συντυχεῖν* (second aor. act. infinitive of *συντυγχάνω*) means ‘to meet’ rather than *esser*; the dative *ἡμῖν* is ‘us’, not *meco*. *Ὡς ἐκ τῆς αὐτῶν θέας* ‘to judge from their appearance’ turns instrumental *col viso* into a qualification of the speaker’s inference.
- *Ἀλλ᾽ οὓς ἐβράδυν᾽* introduces a relative, ‘but whom … delayed’, for the independent *ma tardavagli*. *Οὕς* is the masculine plural accusative object; *ἐβράδυν᾽* is elided *ἐβράδυνε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. indic. of *βραδύνω*). As in Italian the singular verb precedes two subjects, *ἄχθος καὶ στενὴ τρίβος* ‘burden and narrow track’, both without articles.

> 85 Quando fur giunti, assai con l'occhio bieco  
> 86 mi rimiraron senza far parola;  

When they came up, long with eye askance they viewed me, without uttering a word;

> 85 Ἐγγὺς δ᾽ ἐλθόντες διὰ λοξῶν ὀμμάτων  
> (And coming near, with sidelong eyes)
>
> 86 Ἔβλεπον πρός με, οὐ προφέροντες λέξιν.  
> (They looked at me, uttering not a word.)

- *Ἐγγὺς δ᾽ ἐλθόντες* ‘and having come near’ makes *quando fur giunti* a temporal participle (nom. masc. pl., second aor. act. of *ἔρχομαι*). *Διὰ λοξῶν ὀμμάτων* ‘through sidelong eyes’ follows the physical phrasing of *con l'occhio bieco*, but uses *διά* with the genitive and the poetic plural *ὀμμάτων* (from *ὄμμα*) for the singular eye.
- *Ἔβλεπον πρός με* ‘they were looking towards me’ replaces *mi rimiraron* with the imperfect active indicative (3rd pl. of *βλέπω*) and a directional prepositional phrase rather than a direct object. Duration is conveyed by the imperfect, not by a distinct word for *assai*. *Οὐ προφέροντες λέξιν* ‘not uttering a word’ renders *senza far parola* by a negated simultaneous participle (nom. masc. pl., pres. act. of *προφέρω*).

> 87 poi si volsero in sè, e dicean seco:  

then they turned to one another, and said between them:

> 87 Εἶτα στραφέντες ἔλεγον πρὸς ἀλλήλους·  
> (Then turning to one another they said:)

- *Εἶτα στραφέντες ἔλεγον πρὸς ἀλλήλους* reduces *si volsero in sè, e dicean seco* to one participle and one finite verb. *Στραφέντες* again has passive form but intransitive sense, ‘having turned’; *ἔλεγον* (3rd pl. imperf. act. indic. of *λέγω*) preserves the imperfect of *dicean*.
- Rather than render *in sè* and *seco* by two reflexive expressions, Greek gives the direction of the speech once, after the verb: *πρὸς ἀλλήλους* ‘to one another’, with the reciprocal pronoun in the accusative masculine plural.

## Lines 88–120 — The Hypocrites’ Cloaks and the Crucified Man

> 88 “Costui par vivo all’ atto della gola;  

“This one seems alive by the action of his throat;

> 88 “Οὗτος δοκεῖ ζῶν τῇ κινήσει τοῦ βρόγχου·  
> (“This man seems alive by the movement of his throat;)

- *Οὗτος δοκεῖ ζῶν* keeps the order of *Costui par vivo*. Where *vivo* is an adjective, *ζῶν* is the contracted present active participle of *ζάω*, nominative masculine singular, predicated of *οὗτος*. The participle sits directly beside *δοκεῖ* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *δοκέω*), without the infinitive one might expect in *δοκεῖ ζῆν*, ‘he seems to live’.
- *τῇ κινήσει τοῦ βρόγχου* makes *all’ atto della gola* specifically ‘by the movement of the throat’: the dative of *κίνησις* gives the sign by which the judgment is made; the genitive singular *τοῦ βρόγχου* identifies what moves.

> 89 e s’ei son morti, per qual privilegio  
> 90 vanno scoperti della grave stola?”  

and if they are dead, by what privilege go they divested of the heavy stole?”

> 89 „ Εἰ δ᾽ εἰσὶ νεκροί, ποίῳ δὴ προνομίῳ  
> („ And if they are dead, by what privilege indeed)
>
> 90 „ Βαδίζουσ᾽ ἄνευ στολῆς βαρείας τῆσδε ; ”  
> („ Do they walk without this heavy robe?”)

- *Εἰ δ᾽ εἰσὶ νεκροί* renders *e s’ei son morti* by *εἰ* with the present indicative, ‘and if they are dead’. Greek omits the subject pronoun; *νεκροί* is a predicate nominative plural. Postpositive *δέ*, elided before *εἰσί*, marks the turn from the singular observation to the plural question.
- *ποίῳ δὴ προνομίῳ* ‘by what privilege, then?’ renders *per qual privilegio* with an interrogative adjective agreeing with *προνομίῳ* in the dative of means or warrant. *δή* presses the question; it has no separate Italian equivalent.
- *Βαδίζουσ᾽ ἄνευ στολῆς βαρείας τῆσδε* retains *vanno* as *βαδίζουσ᾽* (elided *βαδίζουσι*, 3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *βαδίζω*), but replaces predicative *scoperti* with *ἄνευ* governing the genitive, ‘without’. *βαρείας* and the added deictic *τῆσδε* (gen. fem. sg. of *ὅδε*) both agree with *στολῆς*: ‘without this heavy robe’.

> 91 Poi disser me: “O Tosco, ch’al collegio  
> 92 degl’ ipocriti tristi se’ venuto,  
> 93 dir chi tu sei non avere in dispregio.”  

Then they said to me: “O Tuscan, that art come to the college of the sad hypocrites! to tell us who thou art disdain not.”

> 91 Ἐμοὶ δ᾽ εἶτ᾽ εἶπον· “Ὁ τὸν σύλλογον, Τοῦσκε,  
> (And then to me they said: “O Tuscan, you who the company)
>
> 92 „ Ὧδ᾽ ἐπισκοπῶν ὑποκριτῶν ἀθλίων,  
> („ Of wretched hypocrites thus look upon,)
>
> 93 „ Εἰπεῖν ἡμῖν, τίς ποτ’ εἶ, μὴ δὴ παρίδῃς.”  
> („ Do not indeed disdain to tell us who exactly you are.”)

- *Ἐμοὶ δ᾽ εἶτ᾽ εἶπον* fronts the dative recipient *ἐμοί*, followed by connective *δέ* and temporal *εἶτα*, both elided. *εἶπον* is here 3rd plural second aorist active indicative, the suppletive past of *λέγω*, corresponding to *disser*.
- *Ὁ τὸν σύλλογον, Τοῦσκε, ὧδ᾽ ἐπισκοπῶν* substitutes ‘you who are looking upon the company here’ for *ch’al collegio … se’ venuto*. The Italian completed motion to a destination becomes a present activity with a direct object, *τὸν σύλλογον*. The article *ὁ* belongs with *ἐπισκοπῶν* (contracted pres. act. ptcp., nom. masc. sg., of *ἐπισκοπέω*); the articular nominative addresses the hearer alongside the actual vocative *Τοῦσκε*. *ὧδ᾽* is elided *ὧδε*, ‘here’ or ‘thus’.
- *ὑποκριτῶν ἀθλίων*, genitive plural dependent on *σύλλογον*, preserves the noun–adjective order of *ipocriti tristi*, interpreting *tristi* as ‘wretched’. *Εἰπεῖν ἡμῖν, τίς ποτ’ εἶ* supplies the recipients ‘to us’ with the second aorist active infinitive *εἰπεῖν*. The embedded question retains second-person *εἶ*; elided enclitic *ποτε* lends emphasis, ‘who you actually are’.
- *μὴ δὴ παρίδῃς* ‘do not, then, disregard’ replaces *non avere in dispregio*. *παρίδῃς* is 2nd sg. second aor. act. subj. of *παροράω*, with prohibitive *μή*; it takes the preceding infinitive phrase as what the addressee is not to neglect.

> 94 Ed io a loro: “Io fui nato e cresciuto  
> 95 sopra il bel fiume d’ Arno alla gran villa;  

And I to them: “On Arno’s beauteous river, in the great city I was born and grew;

> 94 Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτούς· “Ἐγεννήθην καὶ ᾽τράφην  
> (And I to them: “I was born and reared)
>
> 95 „ Παρὰ ποταμὸν Ἄρνον κλεινῇ τ’ ἐν πόλει·  
> („ Beside the river Arno and in the illustrious city;)

- *Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτούς* reproduces the verbless *Ed io a loro*: *κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*, and *πρός* with the accusative marks those addressed. Unlike the English prose, Greek keeps the Italian order, verbs first and places second.
- *Ἐγεννήθην καὶ ᾽τράφην* uses two 1st sg. aorist passive indicatives, of *γεννάω* and *τρέφω*: ‘I was born and reared’. The latter is a second passive aorist, giving *cresciuto* a passive rather than intransitive sense. The apostrophe at the beginning of *᾽τράφην* marks prodelision of the syllabic augment of *ἐτράφην* after *καί*.
- *Παρὰ ποταμὸν Ἄρνον* ‘alongside the river Arno’ has *παρά* with the accusative and the river-name in apposition, without an equivalent of *bel*. *κλεινῇ τ’ ἐν πόλει* coordinates a second place phrase by elided *τε*: *κλεινῇ*, separated from its noun by *τ’ ἐν*, agrees with the dative singular *πόλει*. *gran villa* becomes ‘illustrious city’, changing size or eminence into renown.

> 96 e son col corpo ch’i’ ho sempre avuto.  

and I am with the body that I have always had.

> 96 „ Φέρω δ’ ὅπερ ἀπ’ ἀρχῆς ἐφόρουν σῶμα.  
> („ And I wear the body that I have worn from the beginning.)

- *Φέρω δ’ … σῶμα* changes *son col corpo* to ‘I bear a body’, with *σῶμα* the postponed accusative object of *φέρω*. *ὅπερ* is the strengthened relative *ὅ* + *περ*, accusative neuter singular referring forward to *σῶμα*: ‘the very body which’.
- *ἀπ’ ἀρχῆς ἐφόρουν* replaces *ho sempre avuto* by ‘I used to wear from the beginning’. *ἀπό* elides before the genitive; *ἐφόρουν* is the augmented, contracted 1st sg. imperfect active indicative of *φορέω*. Habitual wearing in the past is set against present *φέρω*, rather than expressed by an Italian compound perfect of possession.

> 97 Ma voi chi siete, a cui tanto distilla,  
> 98 quant’ io veggio, dolor giù per le guance,  

But you, who are ye from whom distils such sorrow as I see, down your cheeks?

> 97 „ Ἀλλ’ ὑμεῖς ἐστὲ τίνες, ἐξ ὧν σταλάζει  
> („ But who are you, from whom distils)
>
> 98 „ Τῶν παρειῶν, ὡς βλέπω, τοσαύτη λύπη;  
> („ Along your cheeks, as I see, so much sorrow?)

- *Ἀλλ’ ὑμεῖς ἐστὲ τίνες* follows the emphasis on *Ma voi*, retaining the otherwise unnecessary nominative pronoun. *τίνες* is the interrogative predicate nominative plural with *ἐστέ*, ‘but you are who?’
- *ἐξ ὧν σταλάζει τῶν παρειῶν … τοσαύτη λύπη* retains *a cui tanto distilla … dolor* as a relative clause with a delayed subject. *ἐξ ὧν* is ‘from whom’, genitive plural after *ἐκ*; *σταλάζει* is 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *σταλάζω*. The further genitive *τῶν παρειῶν* specifies the bodily source, ‘from the cheeks’, rather than marking a downward course as *giù per le guance* does. The doubling of the source is closer to the successive Italian phrases than to a simple Greek ‘from whose cheeks’.
- *ὡς βλέπω* ‘as I see’ is parenthetic, not a second expression of quantity answering to *tanto … quant’*. The amount is conveyed by *τοσαύτη*, nominative feminine singular agreeing with *λύπη*, ‘so much sorrow’.

> 99 e che pena è in voi che sì sfavilla?”  

and what punishment is on ye that glitters so?”

> 99 „ Ποινὴ δ’ ὑμῖν τίς ἐκ τῆς λαμπροφορίας;”  
> („ And what punishment is yours from your shining attire?”)

- *Ποινὴ δ’ ὑμῖν τίς* is a verbless question: *τίς* agrees with nominative feminine *ποινή*, and *ὑμῖν* is the dative of the persons affected, ‘what punishment is there for you?’
- *ἐκ τῆς λαμπροφορίας* ‘from the wearing of splendid clothes’ replaces *che sì sfavilla*. Rather than make the punishment the subject of ‘glitters’, Greek asks what punishment arises from the attire. The compound noun absorbs the idea of brilliance; *sì* is not separately expressed.

> 100 E l’un rispose a me: “Le cappe rance  
> 101 son di piombo, sì grosse che li pesi  
> 102 fan così cigolar le lor bilance.  

And one of them replied to me: “Our orange mantles are of lead so thick, that the weights thus cause their scales to creak.

> 100 Ἀπεκρίθη δ’ εἷς αὐτῶν· “Αἱ χρυσαῖ χλαῖναι  
> (And one of them answered: “The golden cloaks)
>
> 101 „ Ἐντός εἰσ’ ἐκ μολύβδου, βάρους δὲ τόσου,  
> („ Are of lead within, and of so great a weight,)
>
> 102 „ Ὥσθ’ οὕτω τὰς πλάστιγγας τρίζειν ποιοῦσι.  
> („ That they make the balance-pans creak thus.)

- *Ἀπεκρίθη δ’ εἷς αὐτῶν* puts the verb first and omits *a me*. *ἀπεκρίθη* is passive in form (3rd sg. aor. ind. of *ἀποκρίνομαι*) but means ‘answered’; *αὐτῶν* is partitive genitive after nominative masculine *εἷς*, ‘one of them’.
- *Αἱ χρυσαῖ χλαῖναι* ‘the golden cloaks’ interprets the colour of *cappe rance* through their golden appearance. *χρυσαῖ* is the contracted nominative feminine plural of *χρύσεος*. *Ἐντός εἰσ’ ἐκ μολύβδου* supplies ‘within’: *ἐντός* is adverbial, *εἰσ’* is elided *εἰσί*, and *ἐκ* with genitive *μολύβδου* specifies material.
- *βάρους δὲ τόσου* ‘and of so great a weight’ is a genitive of quality predicated of the cloaks. It transfers the idea of *li pesi* to the description of the cloaks themselves and measures weight rather than thickness (*sì grosse*).
- *Ὥσθ’ οὕτω τὰς πλάστιγγας τρίζειν ποιοῦσι* makes the cloaks the understood subject of *ποιοῦσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *ποιέω*), not the weights as in Italian. *ὥσθ’* is *ὥστε*, elided with aspiration before rough-breathed *οὕτω*; it introduces a finite result clause after *τόσου*. Within it *τὰς πλάστιγγας* (acc. pl. of *πλάστιγξ*, ‘scale-pan’) is the subject of *τρίζειν*, present active infinitive, after causative *ποιοῦσι*: ‘they make the pans creak thus’. There is no possessive for *lor*.

> 103 Frati Godenti fummo, e Bolognesi:  

We were Jovial Friars, and Bolognese:

> 103 „ Φρᾶτρες Γαυδέντες ἦμεν ἐκ Βονωνίας·  
> („ Jovial Friars were we from Bologna;)

- *Φρᾶτρες Γαυδέντες* takes over the title in Latinizing form rather than translating ‘jovial’ by a Greek adjective. The two plurals serve as predicate nominative with *ἦμεν*, 1st pl. imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*, for Italian preterite *fummo*.
- *ἐκ Βονωνίας* ‘from Bologna’ substitutes a genitive of origin after *ἐκ* for the coordinated adjective *e Bolognesi*. It qualifies the men’s origin without a second conjunction.

> 104 io Catalano e questi Loderingo  
> 105 nomati, e da tua terra insieme presi,  
> 106 come suole esser tolto un uom solingo  
> 107 per conservar sua pace; e fummo tali,  

I named Catalano, and Loderingo he; and by thy city chosen together, as usually one solitary man is chosen, to maintain its peace;

> 104 „ Καταλάνος μὲν ἐγώ, Λοδρίγκος δ’ οὗτος  
> („ Catalano I, and Loderingo this one)
>
> 105 „ Ἐκαλούμεθ’, οὓς ἐξέλεξ’ ἡ σὴ πόλις  
> („ Were we called, whom your city chose)
>
> 106 „ Σύναμ’ ἀνθ’ ἑνός, ὡς ἔθος, ἀνδρὸς μόνου  
> („ Together instead of one man alone, as is the custom,)
>
> 107 „ Εἰρηναρχεῖν ἐν αὐτῇ. Τοιοῦτοι δ’ ἦμεν,  
> („ To keep the peace within it; and such we were,)

- *Καταλάνος μὲν ἐγώ, Λοδρίγκος δ’ οὗτος ἐκαλούμεθ’* balances the two names with postpositive *μέν … δέ*, putting each name before its pronoun. *οὗτος*, ‘this man’, preserves the deixis of *questi*. The shared verb *ἐκαλούμεθ’* is contracted 1st pl. imperfect middle/passive indicative of *καλέω*, passive in sense, ‘we were called’; final *-α* elides and *τ* is aspirated before rough-breathed *οὕς*. It replaces the participle *nomati*.
- *οὓς ἐξέλεξ’ ἡ σὴ πόλις* turns *da tua terra insieme presi* from passive to active: accusative masculine plural *οὕς* refers to the two men, and ‘your city’ is nominative subject. *ἐξέλεξ’* is elided *ἐξέλεξε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐκλέγω*, with internal augment after the prefix.
- *Σύναμ’ ἀνθ’ ἑνός … ἀνδρὸς μόνου* ‘together in place of one man alone’ combines elided *σύναμα* for *insieme* with *ἀντί* and the genitive. *ἀνθ’* shows aspiration before *ἑνός*, the genitive masculine of *εἷς*; *ἀνδρός* and its adjective *μόνου* complete the phrase after the parenthesis. *ὡς ἔθος* ‘as is customary’ leaves ‘is’ understood and dispenses with the Italian passive infinitive *esser tolto*.
- *Εἰρηναρχεῖν ἐν αὐτῇ* is a present active infinitive of purpose after ‘chose’, ‘to keep the peace in it’. The compound *εἰρηναρχέω* does the work of *conservar … pace*; the city that possesses the peace in *sua* becomes its location, *ἐν* with the dative feminine pronoun.

> 108 ch’ancor si pare intorno dal Gardingo.”  

and we were such, that it yet appears round the Gardingo.”

> 108 „ Ὡς ἔστιν ἰδεῖν ἔτι πέριξ Γαρδίγκου.”  
> („ As may still be seen round about Gardingo.”)

- The transition already falls at the end of Greek 107: *Τοιοῦτοι δ’ ἦμεν* ‘and such were we’ renders *e fummo tali*, with a predicate nominative masculine plural and the imperfect again. *Ὡς ἔστιν ἰδεῖν* completes *τοιοῦτοι* with a clause of result, ‘as it is possible to see’. Impersonal *ἔστιν* governs *ἰδεῖν*, second aorist active infinitive of *ὁράω*; what is visible is left unexpressed. Italian *si pare* thus becomes a statement about what one can see.
- *ἔτι πέριξ Γαρδίγκου* closely follows *ancor … intorno dal Gardingo*: temporal *ἔτι* ‘still’, then *πέριξ* used prepositionally with the genitive of the place-name.

> 109 Io cominciai: “O frati, i vostri mali . . .”  

I began: “O Friars, your evil”—

> 109 Ἐγὼ δ’ ἔκραξα· “Φρᾶτρες, ὑμῶν τὰ πάθη. . .”  
> (And I cried out: “Friars, your sufferings...”)

- *Ἐγὼ δ’ ἔκραξα* retains the explicit subject of *Io cominciai* but gives the manner rather than the inception of speech: ‘I cried out’. *ἔκραξα* is 1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *κράζω*.
- *Φρᾶτρες, ὑμῶν τὰ πάθη* drops the introductory *O* but retains direct address. *ὑμῶν* is a fronted possessive genitive plural, and *πάθη* the contracted neuter plural of *πάθος*, interpreting *mali* as ‘sufferings’. The interruption leaves its nominative or accusative function undetermined.

> 110 ma più non dissi, chè all’ occhio mi corse  
> 111 un, crocifisso in terra con tre pali.  

but said no more, for to my eyes came one, cross-fixed in the ground with three stakes.

> 110 Ἀλλ’ οὐδὲν εἶπον πλέον· αἴφνης γὰρ εἶδον  
> (But nothing more did I say; for suddenly I saw)
>
> 111 Χαμαί τιν’ ἥλοις τρισὶν ἐσταυρωμένον.  
> (One crucified upon the ground with three stakes.)

- *Ἀλλ’ οὐδὲν εἶπον πλέον* ‘but I said nothing further’ follows *ma più non dissi* with neuter accusative *οὐδέν* as object and comparative *πλέον* ‘further’. *εἶπον* is here 1st singular, in contrast to the formally identical plural in 91.
- *αἴφνης γὰρ εἶδον* replaces *chè all’ occhio mi corse* by ‘for suddenly I saw’. Postpositive *γάρ* gives the reason, *αἴφνης* conveys the suddenness of *corse*, and the observer becomes the subject of *εἶδον*, 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*.
- *Χαμαί τιν’ ἥλοις τρισὶν ἐσταυρωμένον* places the locative adverb *χαμαί* ‘on the ground’ first. Elided *τιν’* (*τινά*) is the accusative masculine singular object of ‘saw’; *ἐσταυρωμένον*, perfect middle/passive participle of *σταυρόω* agreeing with it, gives the resulting state, ‘crucified’. *ἥλοις τρισίν* is an instrumental dative plural, literally ‘with three nails’, more specific than *con tre pali*.

> 112 Quando mi vide, tutto si distorse,  
> 113 soffiando nella barba co’ sospiri;  

When he saw me, he writhed all over, blowing into his beard with sighs;

> 112 Ἰδών με δ’ οὗτος, ὅλος διελυγίσθη  
> (And seeing me, this one writhed all over)
>
> 113 Ἐν τῷ πώγωνι πνευστιῶν καὶ στενάζων.  
> (Panting and groaning in his beard.)

- *Ἰδών με δ’ οὗτος* replaces finite temporal *Quando mi vide* with a circumstantial second aorist active participle of *ὁράω*, nominative masculine singular agreeing with the supplied subject *οὗτος*. Its action precedes the main verb: ‘this man, on seeing me’.
- *ὅλος διελυγίσθη* ‘he was twisted all over’ renders *tutto si distorse*. *ὅλος* is predicative, agreeing with the man, not an adverb; *διελυγίσθη* is the internally augmented 3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *διαλυγίζω*, used for the Italian reflexive change of posture.
- *Ἐν τῷ πώγωνι* uses a static dative singular of *πώγων* for *nella barba*. *πνευστιῶν καὶ στενάζων* are coordinated present active participles, nominative masculine singular, expressing simultaneous action: ‘panting and groaning’. *πνευστιῶν* is contracted from *πνευστιάω*; *στενάζων* turns the accompanying noun phrase *co’ sospiri* into a second verbal action.

> 114 e il frate Catalan, ch’ a ciò s’ accorse,  
> 115 mi disse: “Quel confitto, che tu miri,  

and Friar Catalano, who perceived this, said to me: “That confixed one, on whom thou gazest,

> 114 Ὁ δὲ Φρα—Καταλάνος νοήσας τοῦτο  
> (And Fra Catalano, perceiving this,)
>
> 115 Εἶπεν· “Ὁ καθηλωτὸς οὗτος, ὃν βλέπεις,  
> (Said: “This nailed one whom you see,)

- *Ὁ δὲ Φρα—Καταλάνος νοήσας τοῦτο εἶπεν* places the title and name under one article, leaving *Φρα* as a borrowed title. For relative *ch’ a ciò s’ accorse* it uses *νοήσας*, nominative masculine singular aorist active participle of *νοέω*, with direct object *τοῦτο*: ‘having noticed this’. *εἶπεν* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind.; the dative corresponding to *mi* in *mi disse* is omitted.
- *Ὁ καθηλωτὸς οὗτος, ὃν βλέπεις* renders *Quel confitto, che tu miri*. *καθηλωτός* is a verbal adjective from *καθηλόω*, ‘nailed down’, made substantival by the article; *οὗτος* has the regular demonstrative position outside the article–adjective group. It is proximal ‘this’, rather than distal *quel*. *ὅν* is the accusative masculine singular object of *βλέπεις*, 2nd sg. pres. act. ind.; the verb alone expresses Italian *tu*.

> 116 consigliò i Farisei, che convenia  
> 117 porre un uom per lo popolo a’ martiri.  

counselled the Pharisees that it was expedient to put one man to tortures for the people.

> 116 „ Ἐστὶν ὁ συμβουλεύσας τοῖς Φαρισαίοις  
> („ Is he who counselled the Pharisees)
>
> 117 „ Ὑπὲρ τοῦ λαοῦ συμφέρειν θανεῖν ἕνα.  
> („ That it was expedient for one man to die for the people.)

- *Ἐστὶν ὁ συμβουλεύσας* changes finite *consigliò* into identification: ‘is the one who advised’. The article substantivizes the nominative masculine singular aorist active participle of *συμβουλεύω*. *τοῖς Φαρισαίοις* is the dative of those advised, where Italian has a direct object.
- *Ὑπὲρ τοῦ λαοῦ συμφέρειν θανεῖν ἕνα* puts ‘on behalf of the people’ first (*ὑπέρ* + genitive). In place of *che convenia* with a finite verb, *συμφέρειν* is a present active infinitive in indirect statement after ‘advised’, used impersonally, ‘that it is expedient’. Within that statement *θανεῖν ἕνα* is what is expedient: *θανεῖν* is the second aorist active infinitive of *θνῄσκω*, with accusative masculine *ἕνα* as its subject. Thus *un uom*, the object of *porre … a’ martiri*, becomes ‘one [man]’ who dies; the Greek expresses the outcome rather than the infliction of suffering.

> 118 Attraversato e nudo è nella via,  
> 119 come tu vedi, ed è mestier ch’ ei senta  
> 120 qualunque passa com’ ei pesa pria;  

Traverse and naked he is upon the road, as thou seest; and has to feel the weight of every one that passes;

> 118 „ Διὰ μέσης τῆς ὁδοῦ πάγγυμνος κεῖται,  
> („ Across the middle of the path he lies all naked,)
>
> 119 „ Καθάπερ ὁρᾷς· αἰσθάνεσθαι δ᾽ ὀφείλει  
> („ Just as you see; and he must feel)
>
> 120 „ Πόσον βαρύνει πᾶς ἐπ᾽ αὐτὸν βαδίζων.  
> („ How heavily weighs everyone who treads upon him.)

- *Διὰ μέσης τῆς ὁδοῦ* ‘across the middle of the road’ absorbs predicative *Attraversato* into a phrase of position. *διά* takes the genitive; *μέσης* in predicate position specifies the middle part of *τῆς ὁδοῦ*. *πάγγυμνος κεῖται* ‘he lies completely naked’ strengthens *nudo* with the compound adjective and substitutes the postural verb *κεῖται* (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *κεῖμαι*) for *è*.
- *Καθάπερ ὁρᾷς* closely matches *come tu vedi*: emphatic comparative *καθάπερ* ‘just as’ and contracted 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ὁράω*. *αἰσθάνεσθαι δ᾽ ὀφείλει* changes impersonal *è mestier ch’ ei senta* with subjunctive into personal *ὀφείλει* ‘he is bound’ with the present middle infinitive of *αἰσθάνομαι*, ‘to feel’.
- *Πόσον βαρύνει πᾶς ἐπ᾽ αὐτὸν βαδίζων* makes the object of ‘feel’ an indirect question: ‘how heavily each person who steps on him weighs’. Neuter accusative *πόσον* measures the extent of *βαρύνει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *βαρύνω*), used here absolutely like *pesa*, rather than transitively, ‘weighs down [something]’. *πᾶς* with the anarthrous nominative masculine singular present participle *βαδίζων* renders *qualunque passa*. *ἐπ᾽ αὐτόν* makes the contact explicit by *ἐπί* with the accusative; *pria* has no distinct rendering.

## Lines 121–148 — The Broken Bridge and the Friar’s Retort

> 121 ed a tal modo il suocero si stenta  
> 122 in questa fossa, e gli altri del concilio  
> 123 che fu per li Giudei mala sementa.”  

and after the like fashion his father-in-law is racked in this ditch, and the others of that Council, which was a seed of evil for the Jews.”

> 121 „ Τὴν αὐτὴν ὁ πενθερὸς ποινὴν ἐκτίνει  
> („ The same penalty his father-in-law pays)
>
> 122 „ Ἐν βόθρῳ τῷδε, κἄλλοι τοῦ συμβουλίου,  
> („ In this chasm, and the others of the council)
>
> 123 „ Οἳ κακὴ σπορὰ τοῖς Ἑβραίοις ὑπῆρξαν.”  
> („ Who were an evil seed for the Jews.”)

- *Τὴν αὐτὴν ὁ πενθερὸς ποινὴν ἐκτίνει* turns *ed a tal modo il suocero si stenta* into ‘the father-in-law pays the same penalty’. There is no opening conjunction. Likeness of manner becomes identity of penalty: *τὴν αὐτήν* ‘the same’ belongs to *ποινήν* (acc. sg.), though the subject *ὁ πενθερός* intervenes. The possessive implied in *il suocero* remains implicit. Instead of the Italian *si* construction, Greek has active *ἐκτίνει* (3rd sg. pres. ind. of *ἐκτίνω*) with a direct object.
- *Ἐν βόθρῳ τῷδε* renders *in questa fossa* with *ἐν* and the locative dative; *τῷδε* is the masc. dat. sg. of *ὅδε*, ‘this one here’. *κἄλλοι* is crasis of *καὶ ἄλλοι*: the nominative adds a second subject after the singular verb, with the predicate understood again for the plural. *τοῦ συμβουλίου* is a partitive genitive, ‘others of the council’; there is no separate demonstrative for *quel* in *del concilio* as rendered by “of that Council”.
- *Οἳ … ὑπῆρξαν* changes the antecedent of *che*: the Italian singular *fu* refers to *concilio*, but the Greek masc. nom. pl. relative *οἵ* refers to *ἄλλοι*. *ὑπῆρξαν* (3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *ὑπάρχω*) serves as ‘were’; *κακὴ σπορά* is a singular predicate nominative even with this plural subject. *τοῖς Ἑβραίοις* ‘for the Hebrews’ is a dative of disadvantage for *per li Giudei*.

> 124 Allor vid’ io maravigliar Virgilio  
> 125 sopra colui ch’ era disteso in croce  
> 126 tanto vilmente nell’ eterno esilio.  

Then I saw Virgil wonder over him that was distended on the cross so ignominiously in the eternal exile.

> 124 Τὸν Βιργίλιον τότε θαυμάζοντ᾽ εἶδον  
> (Then I saw Virgil marvelling)
>
> 125 Ἐπὶ τῷ τεταμένῳ σταυρηδὸν οὕτω  
> (Over him who was stretched out crosswise thus)
>
> 126 Δι᾽ αἰωνίας αἰσχίστως ἐξορίας.  
> (Most shamefully in eternal exile.)

- *Τὸν Βιργίλιον τότε θαυμάζοντ᾽ εἶδον* puts the person seen first and the verb of seeing last. For *vid’ io maravigliar Virgilio*, Greek uses the accusative with a supplementary participle after a verb of perception: *θαυμάζοντ᾽* is elided *θαυμάζοντα* (pres. act. ptcp., masc. acc. sg., of *θαυμάζω*), agreeing with *Βιργίλιον*. *εἶδον*, here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*, needs no separate *io*.
- *Ἐπὶ τῷ τεταμένῳ* makes *sopra colui ch’ era disteso* a prepositional complement of *θαυμάζω*: *ἐπί* with the dative marks the occasion of wonder. The articular *τεταμένῳ* (masc. dat. sg. perfect middle/passive ptcp. of *τείνω*, passive in sense) denotes the person in the resulting state, without a relative clause. *σταυρηδόν* ‘crosswise’ is an adverb for *in croce*.
- *οὕτω … αἰσχίστως* renders *tanto vilmente* with ‘thus, most shamefully’; *αἰσχίστως* is a superlative adverb. *Δι᾽ αἰωνίας … ἐξορίας* replaces the location *nell’ eterno esilio* with *διά* (elided before a vowel) and the genitive, ‘throughout eternal exile’; the adverb intervenes between adjective and noun.

> 127 Poscia drizzò al frate cotal voce :  

Afterwards he to the Friar addressed these words:

> 127 Εἶτα τῷ Φρατρὶ τούσδ᾽ ἀπηύθυνε λόγους·  
> (Then to the Friar he addressed these words:)

- *Εἶτα* corresponds to *Poscia*. *τῷ Φρατρί* is the dative recipient, from Musurus’ Hellenized *Φράτηρ* for *frate* (cf. nom. in 142), not the ordinary Greek *ἀδελφός*. *τούσδ᾽ … λόγους* (masc. acc. pl., *τούσδε* elided) turns singular *cotal voce* into ‘these words’, pointing forward. *ἀπηύθυνε* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀπευθύνω*, with the augment after the prefix: Greek retains the directional sense of *drizzò*.

> 128 “Non vi dispiaccia, se vi lece, dirci  

“Let it not displease you, so it be lawful for you, to tell us

> 128 “ Δέομαί σου νῦν εἰπεῖν ἡμῖν, εἰ θέμις·  
> (“I pray you now to tell us, if it be lawful:)

- *Δέομαί σου νῦν εἰπεῖν ἡμῖν* replaces the negative courtesy formula *Non vi dispiaccia … dirci* with ‘I ask you now to tell us’. *δέομαι* (1st sg. pres. middle ind.) takes the genitive of the person asked, enclitic *σου*, hence the additional accent in *δέομαί*. Greek chooses a singular addressee for polite *vi*. *νῦν* is supplied; *εἰπεῖν*, second aor. act. infinitive of the verb ‘say’, gives the requested act, and *ἡμῖν* the recipients.
- *εἰ θέμις* moves *se vi lece* to the end: ‘if [it is] permitted’, with predicate noun *θέμις*, omitted copula, and no expressed equivalent of the second *vi*.

> 129 se alla man destra giace alcuna foce,  
> 130 onde noi ambedue possiamo uscirci  
> 131 senza costringer degli angeli neri,  
> 132 che vegnan d’ esto fondo a dipartirci.”  

if on the right hand lies any gap by which we both may go out hence, without constraining any of the Black Angels to come and extricate us from this bottom.”

> 129 „ Ἔστιν ἐπὶ δεξιᾷ διέξοδός τις,  
> („ Is there on the right hand any passage out,)
>
> 130 „ Ὁπόθεν δυναίμεθ᾽ ἂν ἐξελθεῖν ἄμφω,  
> („ Whereby we both might go forth,)
>
> 131 „ Οὐ βιάζοντες τοὺς μέλανας ἀγγέλους,  
> („ Without compelling the black angels)
>
> 132 „ Ἵν᾽ ἔλθωσ᾽ ἡμᾶς τοῦδ᾽ ἀπάξοντες βόθρου;”  
> („ To come and lead us out of this chasm?”)

- After the request to speak, *Ἔστιν … διέξοδός τις … ;* is a direct question, ‘is there some way out?’, rather than an indirect question introduced by *se*. Existential *ἔστιν* replaces *giace*; *ἐπὶ δεξιᾷ* retains the dative adjective with ‘hand’ understood. *διέξοδος* denotes a passage out rather than the opening itself; enclitic *τις* (fem. nom. sg.) renders *alcuna* and accounts for the additional accent on *διέξοδός*.
- *Ὁπόθεν* is a relative adverb, ‘from where’, for *onde*. *δυναίμεθ᾽ ἂν ἐξελθεῖν* replaces *possiamo uscirci* with a potential optative, ‘we might be able to go out’: *δυναίμεθα* is 1st pl. pres. middle opt. of *δύναμαι*, with final vowel elided and *τ* of the ending *-μεθα* already aspirated in *θ*. *ἐξελθεῖν* is the second aor. act. infinitive of *ἐξέρχομαι*. *ἄμφω* ‘both’, the nominative dual, makes *ambedue* explicit alongside the first-person plural verb; *noi* and locative *-ci* are not separately translated.
- *Οὐ βιάζοντες* ‘not compelling’ turns *senza costringer* into a circumstantial participle (pres. act., masc. nom. pl., of *βιάζω*) agreeing with the speakers. *τοὺς μέλανας ἀγγέλους* is a definite accusative object, in place of partitive *degli angeli neri*.
- *Ἵν᾽ ἔλθωσ᾽* gives the intended outcome with *ἵνα* and *ἔλθωσι*, both elided (3rd pl. second aor. act. subj. of *ἔρχομαι*). *ἀπάξοντες* (fut. act. ptcp., masc. nom. pl., of *ἀπάγω*) expresses purpose after the verb of coming, like Italian *a dipartirci*. Its object is *ἡμᾶς*; *τοῦδ᾽ … βόθρου* is a genitive of separation, ‘away from this pit’, for *d’ esto fondo*.

> 133 Rispose adunque: “Più che tu non speri  
> 134 s’ appressa un sasso, che dalla gran cerchia  
> 135 si muove, e varca tutti i vallon feri,  

So he answered: “Nearer than thou dost hope, there is a stone that moves from the great circular wall, and bridges all the cruel valleys,

> 133 Ὁ δ’ ἀπεκρίθη· “Πλέον ἤπερ ἐλπίζεις  
> (And he answered: “More than you hope,)
>
> 134 „Ἐγγύς ἐστι σπιλάς τις ἐκ τοῦ μεγάλου  
> („Near is a crag from the great)

> 135 „Τείχους διερχομένη κοιλάδας πάσας·  
> („Wall crossing over all the valleys;)

- *Ὁ δ’ ἀπεκρίθη* ‘and he answered’ uses pronominal *ὁ* with postpositive, elided *δέ* to mark the change of speaker rather than drawing a conclusion with *adunque*. *ἀπεκρίθη* is 3rd sg. aor. passive in form, active in sense, of *ἀποκρίνομαι*.
- *Πλέον ἤπερ ἐλπίζεις / Ἐγγύς ἐστι* divides the comparison across the lines: ‘more than you hope, near is …’. *πλέον* is adverbial, the neuter comparative of *πολύς*; *ἤπερ* introduces the standard of comparison, with emphatic *-περ*. *ἐλπίζεις* is 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. Instead of the movement of *s’ appressa*, *ἐγγύς ἐστι* gives a position. *σπιλάς τις* ‘a certain crag’ is the postponed nominative subject.
- *ἐκ τοῦ μεγάλου Τείχους* supplies the starting point but not the circular shape of *dalla gran cerchia*. The relative verbs *si muove, e varca* are reduced to *διερχομένη* (pres. middle ptcp., fem. nom. sg., of *διέρχομαι*) agreeing with *σπιλάς*. ‘Traversing’ takes *κοιλάδας πάσας* ‘all the valleys’ as its accusative of extent traversed; *feri* has no equivalent.

> 136 salvo ch’ a questo è rotto e nol coperchia :  

save that in this ’tis broken and covers it not:

> 136 „Ἀλλ’ ὧδε κατεῤῥάγη κοὐδαμόσ᾽ ἄγει.  
> („Save that here it is broken down and leads nowhere.)

- *Ἀλλ’ ὧδε* ‘but here’ replaces *salvo ch’ a questo* with a conjunction and a locative adverb. *κατεῤῥάγη* is 3rd sg. second aor. pass. ind. of *καταρρήγνυμι*, ‘broke apart’, an event rather than the state *è rotto*. The augment is internal; *ῤῥ* is the spelling with breathings on doubled rho.
- *κοὐδαμόσ᾽ ἄγει* has both crasis (*καὶ οὐδαμόσε*) and elision of the final *-ε*. The directional adverb means ‘to nowhere’: *ἄγει* ‘leads’ (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἄγω*) interprets *nol coperchia* by its consequence, without a pronoun for *lo*.

> 137 montar potrete su per la ruina,  
> 138 che giace in costa e nel fondo soperchia.”  

you [will be able to] mount up by its ruins, which slope down the side, and on the bottom make a heap.”

> 137 „Διὰ τοῦ ῥηγμοῦ δύνασθ’ ἀνελθεῖν ἄνω,  
> („Through the ruin you can climb up above,)
>
> 138 „Ὃς τῇδε κλίνων, ἐκεῖθεν ἀνυψοῦται.”  
> („Which sloping on this side, rises up on the other.”)

- *Διὰ τοῦ ῥηγμοῦ* ‘through the breach’ fronts the route expressed by *su per la ruina*. *δύνασθ’* is elided *δύνασθε* (2nd pl. pres. middle ind. of *δύναμαι*): present ability replaces future *potrete*, and the plural now addresses both, unlike singular *ἐλπίζεις*. *ἀνελθεῖν* (second aor. act. infinitive of *ἀνέρχομαι*) and *ἄνω* express the upward motion of *montar … su*.
- *Ὃς* is masc. nom. sg., agreeing in gender and number with *ῥηγμοῦ* but nominative as subject of *ἀνυψοῦται* (3rd sg. pres. middle/passive ind. of *ἀνυψόω*), ‘rises up’. *κλίνων* (pres. act. ptcp., masc. nom. sg., of *κλίνω*) is used intransitively, ‘sloping’. The two finite predicates *giace … e … soperchia* thus become participle and verb; *τῇδε* ‘on this side’ and *ἐκεῖθεν* ‘from there’ reinterpret their spatial relation without naming either *costa* or *fondo*.

> 139 Lo duca stette un poco a testa china,  

The Guide stood still a while with head bent down,

> 139 Οὑμὸς δ’ Ἡγήτωρ ἔστη μικρόν τι κύψας,  
> (And my Leader stood for a little with bowed head,)

- *Οὑμὸς … Ἡγήτωρ* ‘my leader’ adds a possessive to *Lo duca*: *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*. Elided postpositive *δ’* marks the transition. *ἔστη* is the intransitive root aorist (3rd sg. act. ind. of *ἵστημι*), ‘came to a stand’.
- *μικρόν τι* is an adverbial neuter accusative, ‘a little’, for *un poco*. *κύψας* (aor. act. ptcp., masc. nom. sg., of *κύπτω*), ‘having bent forward’, attributes an action to the subject rather than describing the position of the head with *a testa china*.

> 140 poi disse : “Mal contava la bisogna  
> 141 colui che i peccator di là uncina.”  

then said: “Falsely did he tell the way, who hooks the sinners yonder.”

> 140 Εἶτ’ εἶπε· “Κακῶς διηγεῖτο τὸ πρᾶγμα  
> (Then said: “Falsely did he recount the matter)
>
> 141 „Ὁ πέραν τοὺς ἐνόχους κάμαξι πλήττων.”  
> („Who yonder strikes the culprits with hooks.”)

- *Εἶτ’ εἶπε* is elided *εἶτα* followed by the 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. ‘said’. *Κακῶς διηγεῖτο τὸ πρᾶγμα* follows the Italian order and imperfect: *κακῶς* ‘badly, wrongly’ renders *Mal*, and *διηγεῖτο* is 3rd sg. imperf. middle ind. of *διηγέομαι*, ‘was relating’. *τὸ πρᾶγμα* takes *la bisogna* as ‘the matter’, not explicitly “the way”.
- *Ὁ … πλήττων* ‘the one striking’ (articular pres. act. ptcp., masc. nom. sg., of *πλήττω*, Attic *-ττ-*) is the postponed subject, replacing *colui che … uncina*. *πέραν* ‘yonder’ retains *di là*; substantival *τοὺς ἐνόχους* calls *i peccator* ‘the guilty’. The implement implicit in *uncina* becomes instrumental dative *κάμαξι* (pl. of *κάμαξ*, ‘pole, shaft’); the verb itself denotes striking, not hooking.

> 142 E il frate: “Io udi’ già dire a Bologna  
> 143 del diavol vizii assai, tra i quali udi’  
> 144 ch’ egli è bugiardo e padre di menzogna.”  

And the Friar: “I heard once at Bologna many of the Devil’s vices told; amongst which, I heard that he is a liar and the father of lies.”

> 142 Ὁ δὲ Φράτηρ· “Ἤκουσ’ ὢν ἐν Βονωνίᾳ  
> (And the Friar: “I heard while in Bologna)
>
> 143 „Τοῦ Διαβόλου τὰς πολλὰς πονηρίας,  
> („Of the Devil's many wickednesses,)
>
> 144 „Πρὸς δ’ ὅτι ψεύστης ἐστὶ καὶ πατὴρ ψεύδους.”  
> („And besides, that he is a liar and the father of lies.”)

- *Ὁ δὲ Φράτηρ* retains the Italian omission of a verb of speaking. *Ἤκουσ’* is elided *ἤκουσα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀκούω*), with temporal augment. *ὢν ἐν Βονωνίᾳ* ‘while being in Bologna’ adds a circumstantial pres. ptcp. of *εἰμί* (masc. nom. sg., agreeing with the implicit speaker); *Βονωνίᾳ* is locative dative after *ἐν*. There is no separate *già* or infinitive for *dire*.
- *Τοῦ Διαβόλου* is a possessive genitive placed before *τὰς πολλὰς πονηρίας*, the accusative object of hearing. *πολλάς* turns *assai* into an attributive adjective; *πονηρίας* denotes ‘wickednesses’ for *vizii*.
- *Πρὸς δ’ ὅτι* ‘and in addition, that …’ uses *πρός* adverbially and subordinates the new item to the original *ἤκουσα*, instead of repeating *udi’* with *tra i quali*. *ψεύστης* and *πατήρ* are predicate nominatives of *ἐστί*; *egli* is understood. *ψεύδους* is the gen. sg. of neuter *ψεῦδος*, ‘of falsehood’, singular like *di menzogna*.

> 145 Appresso il duca a gran passi sen gì,  
> 146 turbato un poco d’ ira nel sembiante;  

Then with large steps my Guide went on, somewhat disturbed with anger in his look;

> 145 Τότε δ’ Ἡγήτωρ οὑμὸς ἀπῆλθ’ ὠκέως,  
> (And then my Leader departed swiftly,)
>
> 146 Παρωργισμένος μικρὸν δοκῶν τὴν ὄψιν·  
> (Seeming somewhat angered in his countenance;)

- *Τότε δ’* ‘and then’ gives temporal *Appresso*. *Ἡγήτωρ οὑμός* again adds ‘my’ to *il duca*, now with the contracted article and possessive following the noun. *ἀπῆλθ’* is elided *ἀπῆλθε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἀπέρχομαι*), ‘went away’, for *sen gì*. *ὠκέως*, the uncontracted poetic adverb from *ὠκύς*, gives the speed rather than the length of *a gran passi*.
- *Παρωργισμένος* (perfect middle/passive ptcp., masc. nom. sg., of *παροργίζω*) expresses the resulting state ‘angered’, combining *turbato* and *d’ ira*. *μικρόν* is an adverbial accusative of degree for *un poco*. *δοκῶν* (contracted pres. act. ptcp., masc. nom. sg., of *δοκέω*) makes ‘seeming’ explicit, with *παρωργισμένος* predicative in sense; *τὴν ὄψιν* is an accusative of respect, ‘as to his appearance’, for *nel sembiante*.

> 147 ond’ io dagl’ incarcati mi parti’  
> 148 dietro alle poste delle care piante.  

whereat I from the laden *spirits* parted, following the prints of his beloved feet.

> 147 Ἐγὼ δὲ κατέλιπον τοὺς ἀχθενδύτας,  
> (And I left behind the burden-clad ones,)
>
> 148 Ἑπόμενος ἴχνεσι ποδῶν φιλτάτων.  
> (Following the footprints of those dearest feet.)

- *Ἐγὼ δέ* retains emphatic *io* but replaces connective *ond’* with simple transition or contrast. *κατέλιπον* (here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *καταλείπω*, with internal augment) is transitive: *dagl’ incarcati mi parti’* ‘I parted from the laden ones’ becomes ‘I left the laden ones behind’. *τοὺς ἀχθενδύτας* is the masc. acc. pl. of the compound *ἀχθενδύτης*, ‘one clad in a burden’; like the Italian, it needs no noun meaning ‘spirits’.
- *Ἑπόμενος* (pres. middle ptcp., masc. nom. sg., of *ἕπομαι*) turns *dietro alle poste* into an action simultaneous with *κατέλιπον*. The verb takes a dative, here poetic *ἴχνεσι* (dat. pl. of *ἴχνος*, Attic contracted *ἴχνεσι* also occurring), ‘following the tracks’. *ποδῶν φιλτάτων* is a dependent genitive plural: *ποδῶν* gives ‘feet’ for *piante* ‘soles’, and superlative *φιλτάτων* ‘dearest’ intensifies *care*. No possessive is expressed.

## Summary — Close Parallels and Shifts of Emphasis

Musurus often keeps Dante’s order and balance while changing the grammar that sustains them. The opening adjectives *Σιγηλοί, μόνοι* follow *Taciti, soli* in sequence and function; *ὡς … οὕτως* likewise preserves the correlation of *come … così*. Even the comparison of *mo* and *issa* remains a comparison of temporal adverbs, though *ἤδη* ‘already, by now’ and *νῦν* ‘now’ are not exact synonyms. Greek particles give these parallels their own articulation: postpositive *γάρ* carries the force of initial *chè*, while *δέ* can mark a transition without preserving the inference expressed by *onde*.

The most persistent recasting concerns which action gets the finite verb. Forms of *στρέφω* such as *στραφείς* ‘having turned’ repeatedly subordinate a turn that Italian coordinates with the next action. At *ci volgemmo … intenti*, the shift works in both directions: *στραφέντες* makes the movement participial, while *προσείχομεν* ‘we were attending’ makes attention the continuing action. After verbs of perception, an accusative with a supplementary participle regularly replaces an Italian infinitive: *θαυμάζοντα* ‘marvelling’, for instance, predicates of Virgil what Dante expresses by *maravigliar*. Musurus is equally alert to distinctions of mood. The mirror comparison preserves a present counterfactual through *εἰ* with the imperfect and an imperfect with *ἄν*, whereas *forse ch’avrai* becomes *ἴσως ἄν … μάθοις* ‘perhaps you might learn’: a qualified future of possession becomes a potential optative of learning.

Small changes of wording can alter the precision or perspective of an image. *Ἐπηύξησε* ‘increased’ leaves unspecified the doubling in *fe’ doppia*; *δοκῶ βλέπειν* ‘I seem to see’ makes the imagined pursuers visible rather than audible as in Carlyle’s reading of *gli sento*. Most pointedly, *οὐκέτ᾽ ἐφοβούμην* ‘I was no longer afraid’ assigns the feeling to the narrator where Carlyle takes *gli* in *non gli era sospetto* to refer to the guide. Yet a departure from literal wording can also recover a detail rather than lose one: the closing compound *ἀχθενδύτας* ‘burden-clad ones’ renders *incarcati* without supplying a noun for the sufferers, and makes the burden itself their garment.
