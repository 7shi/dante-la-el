# Inferno 28

## Lines 1–21 — Wounds Beyond Words

> 1 Chi poria mai pur con parole sciolte  
> 2 dicer del sangue e delle piaghe appieno,  
> 3 ch’i’ ora vidi, per narrar più volte?  

Who, even with words set free, could ever fully tell, by oft relating, the blood and the wounds that I now saw?

> 1 Τίς ποτ᾽ ἂν εἴποι καὶ δι᾽ ἀῤῥύθμων λόγων,  
> (Who could ever tell, even in unrhythmed words,)
>
> 2 Ἃς νῦν ῥοὰς αἱμάτων καὶ πληγὰς εἶδον,  
> (What streams of blood and wounds I now beheld,)
>
> 3 Αὐτάς τ᾽ ἀφηγήσαιτ᾽ ἂν ἀλλεπαλλήλως ;  
> (And recount them one after another?)

- *Τίς ποτ᾽ ἂν εἴποι* ‘who could ever say?’ answers closely to *Chi poria mai … dicer*. Instead of a conditional of ability governing an infinitive, Greek has the potential optative: *εἴποι* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. opt. of *λέγω*, with *ἄν*. The interrogative *τίς* is nominative, its subject; *ποτ᾽* is elided *ποτε*, ‘ever’.
- *καὶ δι᾽ ἀῤῥύθμων λόγων* ‘even by means of unrhythmed words’ interprets *pur con parole sciolte*: *καί* is intensive, and *διά*, elided before the vowel, takes the genitive of means. *ἀῤῥύθμων* (gen. pl. of *ἄρρυθμος*, agreeing with *λόγων*) makes the sense of *sciolte* explicit rather than translating ‘loosed’. The breathings on *ῤῥ* are an older spelling convention for doubled rho.
- *Ἃς νῦν ῥοὰς αἱμάτων καὶ πληγὰς εἶδον* draws the nouns inside the relative clause: ‘what streams of blood and wounds I now saw’. *ἅς* is fem. acc. pl., agreeing with *ῥοάς* and applicable also to *πληγάς*; both are objects of *εἶδον* (here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*). Where Italian has simply *del sangue*, Greek supplies *ῥοάς* ‘streams’ and makes *αἱμάτων* (gen. pl. of *αἷμα*) dependent on it. *νῦν* keeps *ora* beside a past verb; the ending of *εἶδον* suffices for *i’*.
- *Αὐτάς τ᾽ ἀφηγήσαιτ᾽ ἂν ἀλλεπαλλήλως* ‘and could recount them in succession’ resumes the two feminine nouns with acc. pl. *αὐτάς*. Italian *per narrar più volte* is subordinate to *dicer*; Greek coordinates a second potential verb by postpositive *τε* and repeats *ἄν*. *ἀφηγήσαιτ᾽* is elided *ἀφηγήσαιτο*, 3rd sg. aor. mid. opt. of the deponent *ἀφηγέομαι*; *τ᾽* likewise loses the vowel of *τε*. The adverb brings out succession rather than repeated tellings, and *appieno* has no separate equivalent.

> 4 Ogni lingua per certo verria meno  
> 5 per lo nostro sermone e per la mente,  
> 6 c’ hanno a tanto comprender poco seno.  

Every tongue assuredly would fail, because of our speech and our memory that have small capacity to comprehend so much.

> 4 Βεβαίως πᾶσαν γλῶσσαν εἰκὸς ἡττᾶσθαι,  
> (Assuredly it is likely every tongue must fail,)
>
> 5 Τῆς δυνάμεως οὐδόλως ἐπαρκούσης  
> (Since power in no wise suffices)
>
> 6 Ἡμῖν τοῦ λέγειν καὶ τῷ νῷ συλλαμβάνειν.  
> (For us to speak and to comprehend with the mind.)

- *Βεβαίως πᾶσαν γλῶσσαν εἰκὸς ἡττᾶσθαι* ‘assuredly every tongue is likely to be worsted’ keeps *per certo* as an adverb but changes the construction of *verria meno*. Impersonal *εἰκός* ‘likely’ (neut. sg. perfect participle of *ἔοικα*, with *ἐστί* understood) introduces the infinitive *ἡττᾶσθαι* (pres. mid./pass. of *ἡττάω*, passive in sense); *πᾶσαν γλῶσσαν* is its accusative subject, not the object of ‘defeat’.
- *Τῆς δυνάμεως οὐδόλως ἐπαρκούσης ἡμῖν* ‘since capacity in no way suffices for us’ replaces *per lo nostro sermone e per la mente, c’ hanno … poco seno* with a causal genitive absolute. *ἐπαρκούσης* (gen. sg. fem. pres. act. participle of *ἐπαρκέω*) agrees with *δυνάμεως*; *ἡμῖν* is the dative of the persons for whom it does not suffice. *οὐδόλως* ‘not at all’ makes the Italian *poco* more absolute.
- *τοῦ λέγειν καὶ τῷ νῷ συλλαμβάνειν* specifies the capacity in question: ‘of speaking and of grasping with the mind’. The genitive article *τοῦ* brings the two coordinated present active infinitives under *δυνάμεως*. *sermone* becomes the activity *λέγειν*; *mente* becomes instrumental *τῷ νῷ* (contracted dat. sg. of *νοῦς*) modifying *συλλαμβάνειν*. There is no expressed object corresponding to *tanto*.

> 7 S’ ei s’ adunasse ancor tutta la gente,  
> 8 che già in su la fortunata terra  
> 9 di Puglia fu del suo sangue dolente  

If all the people too were gathered, who of old upon Apulia’s fateful land wailed for their blood,

> 7 Εἴπερ συνήγοντ᾽ εἰς ἓν ἅπαντες, ὅσων  
> (Even if all were gathered into one place, of whom)
>
> 8 Ἐπὶ τῆς δυστήνου γῆς τῆς Ἀπουλίας  
> (Upon the wretched land of Apulia)
>
> 9 Πάλαι ποτὲ τἀλγεινὸν αἷμ᾽ ἐξεχύθη  
> (Long ago the grievous blood was poured forth)

- *Εἴπερ συνήγοντ᾽ εἰς ἓν ἅπαντες* ‘if indeed all were being gathered into one’ opens the long condition. *εἴπερ* strengthens *εἰ*; *συνήγοντ᾽* is elided *συνήγοντο*, 3rd pl. imperf. mid./pass. ind. of *συνάγω*, passive in sense, with the augment inside the compound. Greek thus uses a past indicative for the imagined gathering where *s’ … s’adunasse* has an imperfect subjunctive. *εἰς ἕν* (acc. neut. sg. of *εἷς*) spells out the coming together; substantival masc. pl. *ἅπαντες* replaces collective singular *tutta la gente*. There is no separate adverb for *ancor*.
- *ὅσων … τἀλγεινὸν αἷμ᾽ ἐξεχύθη* changes who does what in *che … fu del suo sangue dolente*. Greek *ὅσων* ‘of all those whose’ (gen. pl. of *ὅσος*) is possessive with *αἷμα*: the blood, not the people, is subject. *ἐξεχύθη* is 3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ἐκχέω*, ‘was poured out’; *dolente* is transferred to the blood as *τἀλγεινόν* ‘the grievous’ (crasis of *τὸ ἀλγεινόν*). *αἷμ᾽* is elided *αἷμα*, nominative neuter singular.
- *ἐπὶ τῆς δυστήνου γῆς τῆς Ἀπουλίας* uses local *ἐπί* with the genitive for *in su*; *δυστήνου* (fem. gen. sg. of the two-ending adjective *δύστηνος*) interprets *fortunata* in the adverse sense ‘ill-fated’. *πάλαι ποτέ* ‘once long ago’ expands *già* and, unlike it, follows the phrase of place.

> 10 per li Troiani, e per la lunga guerra  
> 11 che dell’ anella fe’ sì alte spoglie,  
> 12 come Livio scrive che non erra,  

by [reason of the Trojans], and [of] that long war which made so vast a spoil of rings, as Livy writes, who errs not;

> 10 Ὑπὸ Ῥωμαίων, κἀν τῷ μακρῷ πολέμῳ,  
> (By the Romans, and in the long war,)
>
> 11 Ἔνθ᾽ ἦν λάφυρα παμπλείστων δακτυλίων  
> (Where there were spoils of countless rings)
>
> 12 (Καθὰ Λίβιος ὁ φιλαλήθης γράφει),  
> ((As Livy the truth-loving writes),)

- *Ὑπὸ Ῥωμαίων* ‘by Romans’ gives the personal agent of *ἐξεχύθη*, with *ὑπό* and the genitive. Musurus names Romans where the fixed Italian has *per li Troiani*. Nor does he render the two occurrences of *per* alike: *κἀν τῷ μακρῷ πολέμῳ* ‘and in the long war’ gives the circumstances, with *κἀν* by crasis for *καὶ ἐν* and *πολέμῳ* in the dative.
- *Ἔνθ᾽ ἦν λάφυρα παμπλείστων δακτυλίων* ‘where there were spoils of very many rings’ replaces the relative subject *che* and active *fe’* with a relative adverb and an existential imperfect. *ἔνθ᾽* is *ἔνθα* elided before *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*); singular *ἦν* with neuter plural subject *λάφυρα* is regular Greek. Italian measures the spoil, *sì alte spoglie*; Greek specifies the number of rings, with superlative *παμπλείστων* agreeing with gen. pl. *δακτυλίων*.
- Parenthetic *Καθὰ Λίβιος ὁ φιλαλήθης γράφει* keeps *come … scrive* as *καθά* ‘just as’ and *γράφει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind.). The relative clause *che non erra* becomes the articular epithet *ὁ φιλαλήθης* ‘the truth-loving’, nominative singular in apposition to *Λίβιος*: a positive disposition rather than a negated act.

> 13 con quella che sentì di colpi doglie,  
> 14 per contrastare a Roberto Guiscardo,  

with those who, by withstanding Robert Guiscard, felt the pains of blows;

> 13 Κἀκεῖνοι πάντες οἱ δεινῶς πληγωθέντες  
> (Together with all those who were grievously wounded)
>
> 14 Ἀνθιστάμενοι πρὸς Ῥουβέρτον Γισκάρδον  
> (While resisting Robert Guiscard)

- *Κἀκεῖνοι πάντες οἱ δεινῶς πληγωθέντες* ‘and all those who were grievously wounded’ adds another nominative group to *ἅπαντες*, rather than reproducing *con quella* as a prepositional phrase. *κἀκεῖνοι* is crasis for *καὶ ἐκεῖνοι*. The article introduces *πληγωθέντες* (nom. pl. masc. aor. pass. participle of *πληγόω*), characterizing *ἐκεῖνοι*. The passive absorbs *sentì di colpi doglie* into the act of being wounded, with *δεινῶς* marking its severity.
- *Ἀνθιστάμενοι πρὸς Ῥουβέρτον Γισκάρδον* ‘while resisting Robert Guiscard’ replaces *per contrastare* with a circumstantial participle. *ἀνθιστάμενοι* (nom. pl. masc. pres. mid. participle of *ἀνθίστημι*) agrees with the wounded men; its present stem represents resistance in progress when they were wounded. *πρός* with the two accusatives marks the opponent, in place of Italian *a*.

> 15 e l’ altra, il cui ossame ancor s’ accoglie  
> 16 a Ceperan, là dove fu bugiardo  
> 17 ciascun Pugliese, e là da Tagliacozzo,  
> 18 ove senz’ arme vinse il vecchio Alardo;  

and the rest whose bones are gathered still at Ceperano, where each Apulian proved false; and there at Tagliacozzo, where old Alardo conquered without weapons;

> 15 Σὺν ἄλλοις, ὧν ἔτ᾿ ὀστᾶ σεσωρευμένα  
> (With the others, whose bones, still heaped up,)
>
> 16 Ἔστ᾿ ἐν Φρεγέλλαις, ὅπουπερ πᾶς ἦν ψεύστης  
> (Lie in Fregellae, where a liar was every)
>
> 17 Ἀπουλιανός, ἔν τε Τιλακοσσίοις,  
> (Apulian, and at Tagliacozzo,)
>
> 18 Ἔνθ᾿ ἐνίκησ’ ἀόπλως γέρων Ἀλάρδος,  
> (Where aged Alardo conquered without arms,)

- *Σὺν ἄλλοις* ‘with others’ returns to a construction of accompaniment, *σύν* with the dative plural, for *e l’altra*. *ὧν* ‘whose’ is a possessive genitive plural, and *ὀστᾶ* (contracted nom. pl. neut. of *ὀστέον*) replaces collective singular *ossame*.
- *ἔτ᾿ ὀστᾶ σεσωρευμένα ἔστ᾿ ἐν Φρεγέλλαις* ‘bones are still heaped up in Fregellae’ gives a resulting state for *ancor s’accoglie*. Distinguish *ἔτ᾿* (elided *ἔτι*, ‘still’) from *ἔστ᾿* (elided *ἔστι*, ‘are’): the latter is singular with neuter plural *ὀστᾶ*. *σεσωρευμένα* (nom. pl. neut. perf. pass. participle of *σωρεύω*) agrees with the bones and forms the predicate with *ἔστι*. Musurus substitutes *Φρεγέλλαις*, a dative plural after *ἐν*, for *a Ceperan*.
- *ὅπουπερ πᾶς ἦν ψεύστης Ἀπουλιανός* ‘where indeed every Apulian was a liar’ strengthens relative *ὅπου* with *-περ*. *πᾶς* belongs with the delayed nominative singular *Ἀπουλιανός*, rendering distributive *ciascun Pugliese*; *ψεύστης* is a predicate noun with imperfect *ἦν* in place of *fu bugiardo*. *ἔν τε Τιλακοσσίοις* coordinates another local *ἐν* with a plural place-name in the dative; postpositive *τε* supplies *e*, with no separate equivalent for *là*.
- *Ἔνθ᾿ ἐνίκησ’ ἀόπλως γέρων Ἀλάρδος* again uses elided *ἔνθα* for *ove*. *ἐνίκησ’* is elided *ἐνίκησε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *νικάω*. *ἀόπλως* ‘unarmed’ turns *senz’ arme* into an adverb and follows rather than precedes the verb. *γέρων* ‘old man’ stands in the nominative with the Hellenized name *Ἀλάρδος*.

> 19 e qual forato suo membro, e qual mozzo  
> 20 mostrasse : da equar sarebbe nulla  
> 21 al modo della nona bolgia sozzo.  

and one should shew his limbs transpierced, and another his cut off: it were nought to equal the hideous mode of the ninth chasm.

> 19 Πάντων πεπηρωμένα καὶ τρητὰ μέλη  
> (If all their mutilated and pierced limbs)
>
> 20 Δεικνύντων,—οὐδέν ποτ᾿ ἂν ὁμοιωθείη  
> (They showed,—nothing could ever be likened)
>
> 21 Ταῖς εἰδεχθείαις ἐνάτου περιβόλου.  
> (To the horrors of the ninth enclosure.)

- *Πάντων … μέλη δεικνύντων* ‘with all showing limbs’ recasts *e qual … e qual … mostrasse*. Instead of distributing the action between individuals by repeating *qual* and continuing the condition with a finite subjunctive, Musurus gathers the individuals into gen. pl. *πάντων*, subject of a genitive absolute. *δεικνύντων* (gen. pl. masc. pres. act. participle of *δείκνυμι*) takes *μέλη* (acc. pl. neut. of *μέλος*) as its object; there is no separate possessive for *suo*.
- *πεπηρωμένα καὶ τρητὰ μέλη* ‘maimed and pierced limbs’ reverses *forato … mozzo* and attaches both descriptions to one plural noun. *πεπηρωμένα* (acc. pl. neut. perf. pass. participle of *πηρόω*) denotes the resulting mutilation without specifying severance; *τρητά* (acc. pl. neut. of the verbal adjective *τρητός*) gives the sense of *forato*.
- *οὐδέν ποτ᾿ ἂν ὁμοιωθείη* ‘nothing could ever be likened’ makes *οὐδέν* (nom. sg. neut. of *οὐδείς*) the subject of a passive verb in place of *da equar sarebbe nulla*. *ὁμοιωθείη* is 3rd sg. aor. pass. opt. of *ὁμοιόω*; with *ἄν* it forms a potential conclusion, echoing the opening question rather than matching the past indicative of *εἴπερ συνήγοντο*. Elided *ποτ᾿* adds ‘ever’.
- *Ταῖς εἰδεχθείαις ἐνάτου περιβόλου* gives the standard of comparison in the dative after *ὁμοιωθείη*. Instead of a singular *modo* qualified by the delayed adjective *sozzo*, Greek has an abstract noun in the plural, ‘the hideous aspects’. *ἐνάτου περιβόλου* is a dependent genitive singular, ‘of the ninth enclosure’: *ἐνάτου* is masculine in agreement with *περιβόλου*, the noun substituted for *bolgia*.

## Lines 22–54 — The Sowers of Schism Cleft Apart

> 22 Già veggia, per mezzul perdere o lulla,  
> 23 com' io vidi un, così non si pertugia,  
> 24 rotto dal mento infin dove si trulla:  

Even a cask, through loss of middle-piece or cant, yawns not so wide as one I saw, ripped from the chin down to the part that utters vilest sound

> 22 Οὐ πίθος πλευρὰν ὀλέσας ἢ πυθμένα  
> (No cask that has lost a stave or bottom-piece)
>
> 23 Τοιάνδ᾿ ὑπέστη τρῆσιν, οἵαν ἕν᾿ εἶδον  
> (Suffered such a breach as one I saw)
>
> 24 Σχισθέντ᾿ ἐκ τοῦ πώγωνος ἄχρι τῆς ἕδρας,  
> (Cleft from the chin down to the seat,)

- *Οὐ πίθος … τοιάνδ᾿ ὑπέστη τρῆσιν* ‘no cask sustained such a breach’ distributes the Italian negative *non* and verb *si pertugia* between the opening *οὐ* and the later *ὑπέστη τρῆσιν*. *Ὑπέστη* is the intransitive second aorist active indicative, 3rd singular, of *ὑφίστημι*, here with an accusative of what is undergone. The generic Italian present becomes an aorist; *già* has no separate equivalent.
- *Πλευρὰν ὀλέσας ἢ πυθμένα* ‘having lost a side or a bottom’ replaces *per mezzul perdere o lulla*. *Ὀλέσας* (nominative masculine singular, first aorist active participle of *ὄλλυμι*) agrees with *πίθος* and makes the loss anterior to the breach. Its two accusative objects are less specialized than the Italian names for pieces of a cask head.
- *Τοιάνδ᾿ … τρῆσιν, οἵαν* gives a correlative comparison, ‘such a breach as’: *τοιάνδ᾿* is elided *τοιάνδε* (accusative feminine singular of *τοιόσδε*), and *οἵαν* agrees with the understood *τρῆσιν*. The construction is compressed: where one might expect ‘as a man sustained’, Musurus, following *com’ io vidi un*, writes *ἕν᾿ εἶδον* ‘I saw one’, with elided *ἕνα* as the masculine accusative object of *εἶδον* (1st singular, second aorist active indicative of *ὁράω*).
- *Σχισθέντ᾿* is elided *σχισθέντα* (accusative masculine singular, aorist passive participle of *σχίζω*), agreeing with *ἕνα* as *rotto* qualifies *un*. *Ἐκ τοῦ πώγωνος ἄχρι τῆς ἕδρας* marks both endpoints by genitives, ‘from the chin as far as the seat’; the anatomical noun *ἕδρα* replaces the circumlocution *dove si trulla*.

> 25 tra le gambe pendevan le minugia;  
> 26 la corata pareva, e il tristo sacco  
> 27 che merda fa di quel che si trangugia.  

: between his legs the entrails hung; the pluck appeared, and the wretched sack that makes excrement of what it swallowed.

> 25 Ἔχοντ᾿ ἔντερα κρεμάμεν᾿ ἐπὶ σκέλη,  
> (Having his entrails hanging upon his legs,)
>
> 26 Τὰ σπλάγχνα γυμνά, καὶ τὸν βδελυκτὸν σάκκον  
> (His inward parts laid bare, and the loathsome sack)
>
> 27 Τὸ καταβροχθὲν μεταβάλλοντ᾿ εἰς κόπρον.  
> (Which turns what is swallowed into dung.)

- Instead of starting a new finite clause with *pendevan le minugia*, Greek continues the description of *ἕνα* with *ἔχοντ᾿* (elided *ἔχοντα*, accusative masculine singular, present active participle of *ἔχω*). *Ἔντερα κρεμάμεν᾿* ‘entrails hanging’ is its object with a predicative participle: *κρεμάμενα* is accusative neuter plural, present middle of *κρέμαμαι* ‘hang’. *Ἐπὶ σκέλη* (accusative neuter plural of *σκέλος*) places them upon the legs, not *tra le gambe*.
- *Τὰ σπλάγχνα γυμνά* ‘the inward parts bare’ likewise depends on *ἔχοντα*: the predicative adjective *γυμνά* does the work of *pareva*, and a neuter plural replaces the collective singular *la corata*. *Τὸν βδελυκτὸν σάκκον*, a further accusative object, substitutes ‘loathsome’ for *tristo*.
- *Τὸ καταβροχθέν* is a substantivized aorist passive participle (accusative neuter singular, from *καταβροχθίζω*), ‘what has been swallowed’, for the impersonal *quel che si trangugia*. It is the object of *μεταβάλλοντ᾿* (elided *μεταβάλλοντα*, present active participle, accusative masculine singular, of *μεταβάλλω*), which agrees with *σάκκον* and replaces the finite relative *che … fa*. Greek puts the material first and the result last, *εἰς κόπρον* ‘into dung’, reversing *merda fa di quel che …*.

> 28 Mentre che tutto in lui veder m' attacco,  
> 29 guardommi e con le man s' aperse il petto,  
> 30 dicendo: "Or vedi come io mi dilacco;  

Whilst I stood all occupied in seeing him, he looked at me, and with his hands opened his breast, saying : " Now see how I dilacerate myself!

> 28 Ἐν ᾧ δ᾿ εἰς αὐτὸν ἀπέρειδον τὰς ὄψεις,  
> (And while I was fixing my eyes upon him,)
>
> 29 Εἶδέ με, καὶ τὸ στῆθος χερσὶν ἀνοίξας  
> (He saw me, and opening his breast with his hands)
>
> 30 Εἶπεν· “Ἰδὲ νῦν ὡς σχιζόμενος πάσχω·  
> (Said: "Behold now how I suffer being rent apart;)

- *Ἐν ᾧ δ᾿* ‘and while’ uses a relative dative with *ἐν* as a temporal connective; *δ᾿* is elided *δέ*. *Εἰς αὐτὸν ἀπέρειδον τὰς ὄψεις* ‘I was fixing my eyes upon him’ makes *τὰς ὄψεις* the direct object and *εἰς αὐτόν* the direction of the gaze. The imperfect active indicative *ἀπέρειδον* (1st singular of *ἀπερείδω*, with augment after the preverb) gives duration to *m’ attacco*; the absorption expressed by *tutto* is conveyed by the verb rather than a quantifier.
- *Εἶδέ με* ‘he saw me’ (3rd singular of the aorist *εἶδον*, with an additional accent before enclitic *με*) renders *guardommi*. Musurus reverses the Italian hierarchy of verbs: *s’ aperse … dicendo* becomes *τὸ στῆθος χερσὶν ἀνοίξας εἶπεν* ‘having opened his breast with his hands, he said’. *Ἀνοίξας* is a nominative masculine singular aorist active participle of *ἀνοίγω*; *εἶπεν* is the finite second aorist of *λέγω*. The article with *στῆθος* suffices without a reflexive possessive; *χερσίν* is instrumental dative plural of *χείρ*, not dual.
- *Ἰδὲ νῦν* reverses *Or vedi*: *ἰδέ* is the 2nd singular second aorist active imperative corresponding to *εἶδον*. *Ὡς σχιζόμενος πάσχω* ‘how I suffer being split’ replaces reflexive *mi dilacco* with a present passive participle of *σχίζω* (nominative masculine singular) and an explicit verb of suffering, *πάσχω* (1st singular present active indicative).

> 31 vedi come storpiato è Maometto.  

see how Mahomet is mangled!

> 31 „ Ὡς κατάπηρός ἐστιν Ἄρειος βλέπε·  
> (See how maimed Arius is;)

- *Ὡς κατάπηρός ἐστιν Ἄρειος βλέπε* puts the clause introduced by *ὡς* ‘how’ before the command, whereas *vedi* comes first. *Βλέπε* (2nd singular present active imperative of *βλέπω*) varies the preceding *ἰδέ*. *Κατάπηρος* ‘maimed’ is a predicate adjective with *ἐστιν*, acquiring a second accent before the enclitic. The subject *Ἄρειος* is Arius, a substitution of a different name for *Maometto*, not a Greek form of that name.

> 32 Dinanzi a me sen va piangendo Alì  
> 33 fesso nel volto dal mento al ciuffetto;  

Before me Ali weeping goes, cleft in the face from chin to forelock;

> 32 „ Ἄλλος ἔμπροσθεν ἐμοῦ βαδίζει κλαίων,  
> (Another walks weeping before me,)
>
> 33 „ Σχιστὸς πρόσωπον ἐκ πώγωνος εἰς κάρα·  
> (Cleft in the face from chin to crown;)

- *Ἄλλος* ‘another’ occupies the subject position instead of naming *Alì*. *Ἔμπροσθεν ἐμοῦ* ‘before me’ governs the genitive; *βαδίζει κλαίων* ‘walks weeping’ renders *sen va piangendo* by a simple present active indicative and a simultaneous present active participle (nominative masculine singular of *κλαίω*), without a separate equivalent for *sen*.
- *Σχιστὸς πρόσωπον* ‘cleft as to the face’ combines a nominative verbal adjective from *σχίζω* with an accusative of respect, rather than a prepositional phrase like *nel volto*. *Ἐκ πώγωνος εἰς κάρα* opposes a genitive of origin to an accusative of goal; poetic *κάρα* ‘head’ replaces the more precise *ciuffetto*.

> 34 e tutti gli altri, che tu vedi qui,  
> 35 seminator di scandalo e di scisma  
> 36 fur vivi, e però son fessi così.  

and all the others, whom thou seest here, were in their lifetime sowers of scandal and of schism; and therefore are they thus cleft.

> 34 „ Καὶ λοιποὶ πάντες, οὓς σὺ βλέπεις ἐνταῦθα,  
> (And all the rest whom you see here,)
>
> 35 „ Σπορεῖς ζῶντες σκανδάλων σχισμάτων τ᾽ ἦσαν·  
> (Were, while living, sowers of scandals and of schisms;)
>
> 36 „ Καὶ δὴ κατεσχισμένοι πάσχουσιν ἤδη.  
> (And therefore they now suffer cleft in pieces.)

- *Καὶ λοιποὶ πάντες* ‘and all the rest’ reverses the order of *tutti gli altri*. In *οὓς σὺ βλέπεις ἐνταῦθα*, the relative *οὕς* is masculine plural like its antecedent but accusative as object of *βλέπεις* (2nd singular present active indicative); the explicit subject *σύ* corresponds to explicit *tu*.
- *Σπορεῖς ζῶντες σκανδάλων σχισμάτων τ᾽ ἦσαν* interposes ‘while living’ between ‘sowers’ and its two dependent genitives. *Σπορεῖς* (nominative plural of *σπορεύς*) is predicative with *ἦσαν* (3rd plural imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*); *ζῶντες* is a circumstantial present active participle of *ζάω*, not a second predicate coordinated with ‘sowers’. Greek pluralizes *scandalo* and *scisma*; postpositive *τ᾽* is *τε* elided before *ἦσαν*.
- *Καὶ δὴ κατεσχισμένοι πάσχουσιν ἤδη* gives *e però* a consequential ‘and so indeed’. *Κατεσχισμένοι* (nominative masculine plural, perfect middle-passive participle of *κατασχίζω*, passive in sense) expresses the resultant condition; *πάσχουσιν* makes suffering explicit, as in line 30. *Ἤδη* ‘now’ contrasts with *ζῶντες* rather than translating the manner adverb *così*.

> 37 Un diavolo è qua dietro che n' accisma  
> 38 sì crudelmente, al taglio della spada  
> 39 rimettendo ciascun di questa risma,  
> 40 quando avem volta la dolente strada;  

A Devil is here behind, who splits us thus cruelly, reapplying each of this class to his sword's edge, when we have wandered round the doleful road;

> 37 „ Ἔστ᾽ ὄπισθ᾽ ἐκεῖ δαίμων ἡμᾶς πατάσσων  
> (There is behind us yonder a demon who smites us)
>
> 38 „ Οὕτως ἀπηνῶς, καὶ στόματι μαχαίρας  
> (So cruelly, and with the edge of the sword)
>
> 39 „ Πλήττων ἕκαστον τῆς συμμορίας ταύτης,  
> (Striking each one of this company,)
>
> 40 „ Ὅτ᾽ ἀλγεινὴν τήνδ᾽ ὁδὸν ἀνακυκλοῦμεν·  
> (When we complete the circuit of this doleful road;)

- *Ἔστ᾽ ὄπισθ᾽ ἐκεῖ δαίμων* ‘there is a demon behind, yonder’ uses existential *ἔστι* and the adverbs *ὄπισθεν* (here in the shortened form *ὄπισθ᾿*) and *ἐκεῖ*; the latter is distal rather than the proximal *qua*. *Ἡμᾶς πατάσσων* ‘smiting us’ replaces the finite relative *che n’ accisma*: *πατάσσων* is nominative masculine singular, present active participle of *πατάσσω*, agreeing with *δαίμων*.
- *Οὕτως ἀπηνῶς* keeps *sì crudelmente* as two adverbs. *Καὶ στόματι μαχαίρας πλήττων* ‘and striking with the edge of a sword’ coordinates a second present active participle (*πλήττων*, Attic form of *πλήσσω*) with *πατάσσων*. The Italian *al taglio della spada* is the destination to which each is brought back; Greek *στόματι* (dative singular of *στόμα*, literally ‘mouth’) is the instrument of the blow, with possessive genitive *μαχαίρας*. The repeated application in *rimettendo* is left to the temporal clause.
- *Ἕκαστον τῆς συμμορίας ταύτης* ‘each of this company’ preserves the distributive singular with a partitive genitive for *ciascun di questa risma*. *Ὅτ᾽* is elided *ὅτε*; *ἀλγεινὴν τήνδ᾽ ὁδόν* ‘this grievous road’ is the object of *ἀνακυκλοῦμεν* (1st plural contracted present active indicative of *ἀνακυκλόω*). Greek presents the recurring circuit in the present rather than marking its completion with the compound tense *avem volta*; *τήνδ᾽* is elided accusative feminine *τήνδε*.

> 41 però che le ferite son richiuse  
> 42 prima ch' altri dinanzi gli rivada.  

for the wounds heal up ere any goes again before him.

> 41 „ Τὰ τραύματα γὰρ ἡμῶν κλείεται πάλιν,  
> (For our wounds close up again,)
>
> 42 „ Πρὶν ἔμπροσθ᾽ αὐτοῦ διαβῶμεν ἐκ νέου.  
> (Before we pass in front of him anew.)

- Postpositive *γάρ* renders causal *però che*. *Τὰ τραύματα … ἡμῶν* adds the possessive genitive ‘our’. *Κλείεται* (3rd singular present middle-passive indicative of *κλείω*) takes the neuter plural subject *τὰ τραύματα* in the regular Attic construction. *Πάλιν* expresses separately the renewed closure of *son richiuse*.
- *Πρὶν … διαβῶμεν* means ‘before we pass’: *διαβῶμεν* is 1st plural second aorist active subjunctive of *διαβαίνω*, viewing the next passage as a single event. Musurus uses a finite subjunctive after affirmative *κλείεται*, where ordinary Attic would favor *πρίν* with an infinitive; the construction is close to *prima ch’ … rivada*. The person shifts from indefinite singular *altri* to ‘we’. *Ἔμπροσθ᾽ αὐτοῦ* has the genitive after ‘in front of’; *ἐκ νέου* ‘anew’ conveys the prefix of *rivada*.

> 43 Ma tu chi se' che in su lo scoglio muse,  
> 44 forse per indugiar d' ire alla pena,  
> 45 ch' è giudicata in su le tue accuse?"  

But who art thou, that musest on the cliff, perhaps in order to delay thy going to the punishment, adjudged upon thy accusations?

> 43 „ Ἀλλὰ σὺ τίς εἶς, ἑστὼς ἐπὶ γεφύρας,  
> (But who are you, standing upon the bridge,)
>
> 44 „ Βραδύνων τ᾽ ἴσως πρὸς τὴν ποινὴν προβῆναι  
> (And lingering perhaps to go forward to the punishment)
>
> 45 „ Τὴν ἐπιβληθεῖσάν σοι διὰ σὰς πράξεις ;”  
> (Imposed upon you for your deeds?")

- *Ἀλλὰ σὺ τίς εἶς* follows *Ma tu chi se’* closely, including the expressed pronoun *σύ*. *Εἶς* is a variant of Attic *εἶ* (2nd singular present indicative of *εἰμί*). *Ἑστώς* is the nominative masculine singular intransitive perfect active participle of *ἵστημι*, ‘standing’: it replaces the finite relative *che … muse* and does not express musing. *Ἐπὶ γεφύρας* (genitive of position) interprets *in su lo scoglio* as ‘on a bridge’.
- *Βραδύνων τ᾽ ἴσως* ‘and perhaps delaying’ coordinates a present active participle of *βραδύνω* with *ἑστώς* by elided *τε*. This removes the explicit purposive relation of *per indugiar*. *Προβῆναι* (second aorist active infinitive of *προβαίνω*) completes *βραδύνων*, as *d’ ire* completes *indugiar*; *πρὸς τὴν ποινήν* gives the goal.
- *Τὴν ἐπιβληθεῖσάν σοι* repeats the article after *τὴν ποινήν* to introduce an attributive participle, ‘the [punishment] imposed on you’. *Ἐπιβληθεῖσαν* is accusative feminine singular, aorist passive of *ἐπιβάλλω*, with an additional accent before enclitic *σοι*, the dative of the person affected. *Διὰ σὰς πράξεις* (causal *διά* with accusative) interprets *in su le tue accuse* as ‘on account of your deeds’, replacing accusations with acts.

> 46 "Nè morte il giunse ancor, nè colpa il mena,"  
> 47 rispose il mio maestro, "a tormentarlo;  

" " Not yet has death come to him; nor does guilt lead him," replied my Master, " to torment him;

> 46 — “Οὔπω τέθνηκεν, οὔτε πταῖσμ᾽ αὐτὸν φέρει  
> (—"He is not yet dead, nor does guilt bring him)
>
> 47 „ Πρὸς τιμωρίαν,” εἶπεν οὑμὸς Προστάτης·  
> (To torment," said my Protector;)

- *Οὔπω τέθνηκεν* ‘he is not yet dead’ makes the person the subject in place of Italian *morte*. *Τέθνηκεν* (3rd singular perfect active indicative of *θνῄσκω*) denotes the present state that would result from dying. *Οὔπω … οὔτε* links this negation with *πταῖσμ᾽ αὐτὸν φέρει*: *πταῖσμ᾽* is elided *πταῖσμα*, nominative neuter singular, ‘a fault’, subject of *φέρει* (3rd singular present active indicative of *φέρω*).
- *Πρὸς τιμωρίαν* ‘to punishment’ replaces *a tormentarlo* with a preposition and an action noun, leaving its object to be inferred. Musurus completes this phrase before the reporting clause, whereas Italian inserts *rispose il mio maestro* between *mena* and its infinitive. *Εἶπεν* means ‘said’, not specifically ‘replied’; *οὑμὸς Προστάτης* ‘my protector’ has crasis of *ὁ ἐμός* to *οὑμός* and substitutes a noun of protection for *maestro*.

> 48 ma per dar lui esperienza piena,  
> 49 a me, che morto son, convien menarlo  
> 50 per lo inferno quaggiù di giro in giro;  
> 51 e questo è ver così com' io ti parlo."  

but to give him full experience, it behooves me, who am dead, to lead him through the Hell down here, from round to round; and this is true as that I speak to thee."

> 48 „ Ἀλλ᾽ ἵνα λάβῃ πάντων ἐντελῆ πεῖραν,  
> ("But that he may gain full experience of all,)
>
> 49 „ Νεκρὸς ὢν ἐγὼ νομίζω πρέπον ἄγειν  
> (I, who am dead, deem it fitting to lead)
>
> 50 „ Αὐτὸν εἰς πάντας τοὺς ἐντὸς ᾍδου κύκλους·  
> (Him into all the circles within Hell;)
>
> 51 „ Τόδ᾽ ἔστ᾽ ἀληθὲς οὕτως, ὡς ὁμιλῶ σοι.” —  
> (This is as true as that I speak to you."—)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ ἵνα λάβῃ* turns *ma per dar lui* into a finite purpose clause: *λάβῃ* is 3rd singular second aorist active subjunctive of *λαμβάνω*, and the recipient becomes the subject, ‘that he may gain’. *Ἐντελῆ πεῖραν* is its accusative object (*ἐντελῆ*, contracted accusative feminine singular of *ἐντελής*); the supplied genitive *πάντων* specifies ‘experience of all things’.
- *Νεκρὸς ὢν ἐγώ* ‘I, being dead’ replaces dative *a me* and relative *che morto son* with an emphatic nominative subject and a participial predication (*ὤν*, present participle of *εἰμί*). *Νομίζω πρέπον ἄγειν* ‘I deem it fitting to lead’ personalizes impersonal *convien*: *πρέπον* (neuter singular present active participle of *πρέπω*, used predicatively) gives the speaker’s judgment on the action expressed by the present active infinitive *ἄγειν*.
- *Αὐτόν*, object of *ἄγειν*, recovers the pronominal element of *menarlo*. *Εἰς πάντας τοὺς ἐντὸς ᾍδου κύκλους* ‘into all the circles within Hades’ substitutes one goal phrase for *per lo inferno quaggiù di giro in giro*. *Ἐντός* governs genitive *ᾍδου*; the whole phrase stands attributively between article and noun. *Πάντας* expresses totality rather than the Italian progression, and there is no separate ‘down here’.
- *Τόδ᾽ ἔστ᾽ ἀληθές* ‘this is true’ has elision of *τόδε* and *ἔστι*, with neuter predicate adjective *ἀληθές* agreeing with the demonstrative. There is no connective for initial *e*. *Οὕτως, ὡς ὁμιλῶ σοι* preserves the correlation *così com’*: *ὁμιλῶ* (1st singular contracted present active indicative of *ὁμιλέω*) takes a dative of the interlocutor.

> 52 Più fur di cento che, quando l' udiro,  
> 53 s' arrestaron nel fosso a riguardarmi,  
> 54 per maraviglia obbliando il martiro.  

More than a hundred, when they heard him, stopped in the foss to look at me, through wonder forgetting their torment.

> 52 Ἑκατὸν καὶ πρὸς τότ᾽ ἀκούσαντες ταῦτα,  
> (More than a hundred then, hearing these words,)
>
> 53 Ἔστησαν ἐν τῇ τάφρῳ προσβλέποντές με,  
> (Stood still in the ditch gazing at me,)
>
> 54 Ἐκ τοῦ θαυμασμοῦ λαθόμενοι βασάνων.  
> (Through amazement forgetting their torments.)

- *Ἑκατὸν καὶ πρός* ‘a hundred and more’ uses indeclinable *ἑκατόν* and adverbial *πρός* ‘besides’. Instead of *Più fur di cento che … s’ arrestaron*, the numeral phrase is directly the subject of *ἔστησαν*. *Τότ᾽ ἀκούσαντες ταῦτα* ‘then, having heard these things’ substitutes a temporal aorist active participle (nominative masculine plural of *ἀκούω*) for *quando l’ udiro*; *τότ᾽* is elided *τότε*, and neuter plural *ταῦτα* makes what was said, not the speaker, the object.
- *Ἔστησαν* (3rd plural intransitive second aorist active indicative of *ἵστημι*) renders reflexive *s’ arrestaron* without a middle form. *Ἐν τῇ τάφρῳ* keeps the static location, with the feminine dative singular of *τάφρος*. *Προσβλέποντές με* ‘gazing at me’ uses a simultaneous present active participle of *προσβλέπω*, rather than marking the purpose or direction of *a riguardarmi*; the extra accent precedes enclitic *με*.
- *Ἐκ τοῦ θαυμασμοῦ* gives *per maraviglia* as a cause conceived as a source. *Λαθόμενοι* is a poetic second aorist middle participle (nominative masculine plural, from *λανθάνω*, middle *λήθομαι* ‘forget’). It expresses the forgetting as an event, in contrast to present *προσβλέποντες* and Italian *obbliando*. Its object *βασάνων* is genitive plural, as Greek verbs of forgetting can govern the genitive, instead of the accusative singular *il martiro*.

## Lines 55–87 — A Warning to Dolcino and Pier’s Prophecy

> 55 "Or di' a Fra Dolcin dunque che s' armi,  
> 56 tu che forse vedrai lo sole in breve,  

“Well, then, thou who perhaps shalt see the Sun ere long, tell Fra Dolcino,

> 55 — “Εἰπὲ τῷ Φρα-Δουλκίνῳ τροφὰς πορίζειν  
> (—"Tell Fra Dolcino to lay in provisions)
>
> 56 „ Ἑαυτῷ σύ γ᾽, ὃς ἥλιον αὐτίκ᾽ ὄψει,  
> (For himself—you indeed who shall soon see the sun—)

- *Εἰπὲ τῷ Φρα-Δουλκίνῳ* keeps the Italian order *di' a Fra Dolcin*: *εἰπέ* is the second-person singular imperative of the second aorist *εἶπον* (used as the aorist of *λέγω*), with the person addressed in the dative. Neither *Or* nor *dunque* has a separate equivalent.
- For *che s' armi*, *τροφὰς πορίζειν ἑαυτῷ* gives an infinitive of indirect command, ‘to procure provisions for himself’. The understood subject of the present active *πορίζειν* is Dolcino, to whom the reflexive dative *ἑαυτῷ* refers. Musurus drops the metaphor of arming and brings *di vivanda* forward from line 58 as the accusative object *τροφάς*.
- The delayed *σύ γ᾽* (*σύ γε*) singles out the subject of the imperative, ‘you at least’, not the subject of the infinitive. *ὃς ἥλιον αὐτίκ᾽ ὄψει* follows *che … vedrai lo sole in breve*: *ὄψει* is second-person singular future middle indicative of *ὁράω*, with active sense, agreeing in person with the antecedent *σύ*. *αὐτίκ᾽* is elided *αὐτίκα*, ‘presently’; the qualification *forse* disappears.

> 57 s' egli non vuol qui tosto seguitarmi,  

if he wish not speedily to follow me *down here*,

> 57 „ Εἰ μὴ τάχος ὧδ᾽ ἀκολουθεῖν μοι θέλει,  
> (If he does not wish speedily to follow me hither,)

- *Εἰ μὴ … θέλει* retains *s' egli non vuol* as an open condition with the present indicative; Greek needs no expressed subject. Within it, *τάχος* is an adverbial accusative, ‘speedily’, and elided *ὧδ᾽* (*ὧδε*) is directional, ‘hither’.
- *ἀκολουθεῖν μοι* renders *seguitarmi* with a present active infinitive dependent on *θέλει*. Greek *ἀκολουθέω* takes the person followed in the dative, hence enclitic *μοι*, not an accusative.

> 58 sì di vivanda, che stretta di neve  
> 59 non rechi la vittoria al Noarese,  

so to arm himself with victuals, that stress of snow may not bring victory to the Novarese,

> 58 „ Ἵν᾽ ἡ στέρησις, χιόνος ἐπελθούσης,  
> (So that scarcity, when the snow comes on,)
>
> 59 „ Μὴ παράσχῃ τὴν νίκην τῷ Ναυαῤῥαίῳ·  
> (May not grant the victory to the Navarrese;)

- With the provisioning already expressed, *ἵνα … μὴ παράσχῃ* recasts the Italian correlation *sì … che … non rechi* as negative purpose, ‘in order that … may not grant’. *Ἵν᾽* is elided *ἵνα*; *παράσχῃ* is third-person singular second-aorist active subjunctive of *παρέχω*, without augment.
- *ἡ στέρησις* ‘the deprivation’ is the subject. Instead of attaching ‘of snow’ to this noun as in *stretta di neve*, Musurus makes *χιόνος ἐπελθούσης* a temporal genitive absolute, ‘when snow has come on’: the feminine singular participle is second-aorist active of *ἐπέρχομαι*.
- *τὴν νίκην τῷ Ναυαῤῥαίῳ* preserves the thing granted in the accusative and the recipient in the dative. The singular ethnic, with the older breathing notation *ῤῥ* for doubled rho, denotes a Navarrese rather than reproducing *Noarese*.

> 60 ch' altrimenti acquistar non saria lieve."  

which otherwise would not be easy to attain.”

> 60 „ Οὐ γὰρ ἔστ᾽ ἄλλως αὐτῷ διασωθῆναι.” —  
> (For otherwise he cannot be saved."—)

- Italian *ch' altrimenti acquistar non saria lieve* qualifies the victory: it would otherwise be hard to win. *Οὐ γὰρ ἔστ᾽ ἄλλως αὐτῷ διασωθῆναι* instead gives an independent explanation, ‘for otherwise it is not possible for him to be saved’. Postpositive *γάρ* replaces the relative link; there is no counterpart to ‘acquire’ or ‘easy’.
- *ἔστ᾽* is elided impersonal *ἔστι*, with the dative of the person for whom something is possible and the aorist passive infinitive *διασωθῆναι* (*διασῴζω*). The present assertion replaces the Italian conditional *saria*.

> 61 Poi che l' un piè per girsene sospese,  
> 62 Maometto mi disse esta parola,  
> 63 indi a partirsi in terra lo distese.  

After lifting up one foot to go away, Mahomet said *this to me*; then *on the ground he* *stretched it to depart*.

> 61 Ἄρας τὸν πόδα τοῦ πορευθῆναι πρόσω  
> (Having lifted his foot to walk onward,)
>
> 62 Ἄρειος προεῖπέ μοι τοιούτους λόγους·  
> (Arius spoke these words to me;)
>
> 63 Εἶτ᾽ ἐξέτεινε τὸν πόδ᾽ ἀναχωρήσας.  
> (Then he stretched out his foot and departed.)

- *Ἄρας* (nominative masculine singular, aorist active participle of *αἴρω*) subordinates the completed lifting to the verb of speaking, replacing finite *Poi che … sospese*. *τὸν πόδα* gives the ordinary article with a body part, without *un*; *τοῦ πορευθῆναι πρόσω* is a genitive articular infinitive of purpose, ‘in order to go onward’. *πορευθῆναι* is passive in aorist form but intransitive in sense.
- *Ἄρειος* substitutes Arius for *Maometto*. *προεῖπέ μοι τοιούτους λόγους* has a third-person singular second-aorist active of *προλέγω*; the additional acute before enclitic *μοι* is accentual, not a different ending. Plural ‘words of this sort’ replaces singular *esta parola*.
- *Εἶτ᾽* is elided *εἶτα*, ‘then’; *ἐξέτεινε* is the imperfect or, in this sequence, naturally the aorist active of *ἐκτείνω* (the forms coincide here). *τὸν πόδ᾽* repeats ‘the foot’ for Italian *lo*. *ἀναχωρήσας*, aorist active participle of *ἀναχωρέω* agreeing with the speaker, presents withdrawal as an act, not as the purpose *a partirsi*; *in terra* is omitted.

> 64 Un altro, che forata avea la gola  
> 65 e tronco il naso infin sotto le ciglia,  
> 66 e non avea ma che un' orecchia sola,  

Another, who had his throat pierced through, and nose cut off up to the eyebrows, and had but one single ear,

> 64 Ἄλλος τις ἔχων τὸν λαιμὸν τετρημένον,  
> (Another, having his throat pierced through,)
>
> 65 Τὴν ῥῖνα τετμημένην ἄχρις ὀφρύων,  
> (His nose cut off up to the eyebrows,)
>
> 66 Οὖς τε μόνον ἕν, ἔστη τεθαυμασμένως  
> (And only one ear, stood in wonder)

- *Ἄλλος τις ἔχων* changes *Un altro, che … avea* into ‘a certain other man having’, with enclitic indefinite *τις* and a present active participle agreeing with *ἄλλος*. One *ἔχων* governs three accusatives, where the Italian repeats *avea* for the ear.
- *τὸν λαιμὸν τετρημένον* and *τὴν ῥῖνα τετμημένην* use predicative perfect middle-passive participles, respectively of *τιτράω* ‘pierce’ and *τέμνω* ‘cut’. Their different genders follow their objects; the perfects describe the resulting condition. *ἄχρις ὀφρύων* takes the genitive plural, ‘as far as the eyebrows’, without the more precise *sotto*.
- *οὖς τε μόνον ἕν* adds the last object by postpositive *τε*: *οὖς* is neuter accusative singular, as are *μόνον* and the numeral *ἕν*. This positive ‘one single ear’ replaces restrictive *non … ma che*. Already at the end of 66 the Greek moves on to *ἔστη τεθαυμασμένως*, ‘he stood in amazement’.

> 67 restato a riguardar per maraviglia  

standing to gaze in wonder with the rest,

> 67 Ἀτενίζων πρὸς ἡμᾶς μετὰ τῶν ἄλλων,  
> (Gazing intently at us with the others,)

- Where *restato* is participial, *ἔστη* is finite (third-person singular intransitive second-aorist active of *ἵστημι*, ‘took his stand’). The perfect-participial adverb *τεθαυμασμένως*, from *θαυμάζω*, gives the manner of standing for *per maraviglia*.
- *Ἀτενίζων* (present active participle of *ἀτενίζω*) makes the gazing simultaneous, not a purpose expressed by *a riguardar*. *πρὸς ἡμᾶς* supplies its direction, ‘toward us’; *μετὰ τῶν ἄλλων* renders *con gli altri* by *μετά* with the genitive of company.

> 68 con gli altri, innanzi agli altri aprì la canna  
> 69 ch' era di fuor d' ogni parte vermiglia;  

before the rest opened his weasand, *which outwardly* *was red on every part,*

> 68 Ὧν ἀπέναντι λάρυγγ᾽ ἤνοιξεν, οὗπερ  
> (In front of whom he opened his windpipe, which)
>
> 69 Ἐρυθρὰ τἀκτὸς ἅπαντ᾽ ἦν ἐξ αἱμάτων,  
> (Was all red on the outside with blood,)

- *Ὧν ἀπέναντι* ‘opposite whom’ takes up *τῶν ἄλλων* by a relative genitive governed by the following *ἀπέναντι*. Thus *innanzi agli altri* becomes spatial rather than marking precedence in speaking. *λάρυγγ᾽* is elided accusative *λάρυγγα*, object of *ἤνοιξεν* (third-person singular aorist active of *ἀνοίγω*).
- *οὗπερ* is a possessive relative genitive referring to the windpipe, with strengthening *-περ*. Instead of ‘which was red’, Musurus writes ‘all the outer parts of which were red’: *τἀκτός* is crasis for *τὰ ἐκτός*, *ἅπαντ᾽* is elided *ἅπαντα*, and predicative *ἐρυθρά* is likewise neuter plural. Singular *ἦν* is regular with a neuter plural subject. *ἐξ αἱμάτων*, literally ‘from blood’, adds the cause of the redness, with poetic plural *αἱμάτων*.

> 70 e disse: "Tu, cui colpa non condanna,  
> 71 e cui io vidi su in terra Latina,  
> 72 se troppa simiglianza non m' inganna,  

and said: “Thou ! whom guilt condemns not, and whom I have seen above on Latian ground, *unless too much resemblance deceive me*;

> 70 Λέγων· “Ὦ σύ, πταισμάτων ὑπάρχων ἄνευ,  
> (Saying: "O you, who are without guilt,)
>
> 71 „ Ὃν ἐπὶ Λατινίδος γῆς εἶδον ἤδη,  
> (Whom upon the Latian land I saw before,)
>
> 72 „ Εἰ μὴ λανθάνει μ᾽ ὁμοιότης μεγίστη,  
> (Unless a very close likeness deceives me,)

- *Λέγων*, present active participle of *λέγω*, attaches the speech to the opening of the throat instead of coordinating a second finite verb, *e disse*. *Ὦ σύ, πταισμάτων ὑπάρχων ἄνευ* substitutes ‘O you, being without offences’ for the relative clause in which *colpa* is the condemning subject. Postposed *ἄνευ* governs genitive plural *πταισμάτων*; *ὑπάρχων* agrees with *σύ*.
- *Ὃν … εἶδον ἤδη* retains the second relative as an accusative object; *εἶδον* is first-person singular second-aorist active of *ὁράω*. *ἐπὶ Λατινίδος γῆς* uses *ἐπί* with a local genitive, ‘on Latian soil’; *ἤδη* supplies ‘before now’, not the spatial *su*.
- *Εἰ μὴ λανθάνει μ᾽ ὁμοιότης μεγίστη* has the same negative condition and delayed subject as *se troppa simiglianza non m' inganna*. *μεγίστη* is an intensive superlative, ‘very great’, not strictly ‘excessive’. *λανθάνει* (present active indicative of *λανθάνω*) with accusative *μ᾽* (*με*) normally means ‘escapes my notice’; its use for ‘deceives me’ presses the Greek toward the Italian.

> 73 rimembriti di Pier da Medicina,  
> 74 se mai torni a veder lo dolce piano,  
> 75 che da Vercelli a Marcabò dichina.  

remember Pier da Medicina, if ever thou return *to see the gentle plain that from Vercelli* *slopes to Marcabò.*

> 73 „ Ἀναμνήσθητι Πέτρου Μεδικιναίου,  
> (Remember Pier da Medicina,)
>
> 74 „ Ἢν τὴν ἡδεῖαν πεδιάδ᾽ ἐπανίδῃς,  
> (If ever you behold again the sweet plain,)
>
> 75 „ Τὴν ἐκ Βερκελλῶν κλίνουσαν εἰς Μαρκᾶβον.  
> (Which slopes from Vercelli down to Marcabò.)

- *Ἀναμνήσθητι* is the second-person singular aorist passive imperative of *ἀναμιμνῄσκω*, with the sense ‘call to mind’. It takes the genitive *Πέτρου Μεδικιναίου*: Greek turns *da Medicina* into an agreeing ethnic adjective.
- *Ἢν … ἐπανίδῃς* is a prospective condition, with contracted *ἤν* (= *ἐάν*) and the second-person singular second-aorist active subjunctive of *ἐπανεῖδον*. The compound means ‘see again’, compressing *torni a veder*; *mai* has no separate equivalent. *τὴν ἡδεῖαν πεδιάδ᾽* has the feminine accusative of *ἡδύς* and elided *πεδιάδα*, ‘the sweet plain’.
- Repeated *τὴν* introduces an attributive participial phrase in place of *che … dichina*. *κλίνουσαν* is present active, feminine accusative singular, used intransitively, ‘sloping’; *ἐκ Βερκελλῶν* marks the starting point by the genitive plural, *εἰς Μαρκᾶβον* the endpoint by the accusative.

> 76 E fa saper ai due miglior di Fano,  
> 77 a messer Guido ed anco ad Angiolello,  

And make known to the worthiest two of Fano, to Messer Guido and to Angiolello likewise,

> 76 „ Δήλωσον δὲ τοῖς δυσὶ βελτίστοις Φάνου,  
> (And make known to the two worthiest men of Fano,)
>
> 77 „ Τῷ κυρῷ Γουΐδῳ τε κ’ Ἀγγιολέλλῳ,  
> (To Messer Guido and to Angiolello,)

- *Δήλωσον δέ* gives a single second-person singular aorist active imperative of *δηλόω* for causative *fa saper*; connective *δέ* is postpositive. *τοῖς δυσὶ βελτίστοις Φάνου* has dative *δυσί* of *δύο*, with plural article and substantival superlative, not a dual construction. *βελτίστοις* ‘best’ interprets *miglior* as a superlative; *Φάνου* retains the genitive of place.
- *Τῷ κυρῷ Γουΐδῳ τε κ’ Ἀγγιολέλλῳ* supplies the names in dative apposition. The honorific *κυρῷ* accompanies the first name only; *τε κ’* couples the names, with *κ’* representing shortened *καί*, rather than separately emphasizing *anco*.

> 78 che, se l' antiveder qui non è vano,  
> 79 gittati saran fuor di lor vasello,  
> 80 e mazzerati presso alla Cattolica,  
> 81 per tradimento d' un tiranno fello.  

*that, unless our foresight here be vain,* *they shall be cast out of their ship*, and drowned near the Cattolica, *by a fell tyrant's* *treachery.*

> 78 „ Ὡς, εἰ μὴ ψεύδοντ’ ἐνταῦθ’ αἱ προοράσεις,  
> (That, unless foresight deceives here,)
>
> 79 „ Κατακρημνισθήσονται τῆς νηὸς ἔξω,  
> (They shall be cast headlong out of their ship,)
>
> 80 „ Καὶ πνιγήσονται Καθολικῆς πλησίον  
> (And drowned near Cattolica)
>
> 81 „ Τῇ προδοσίᾳ παρασπόνδου τυράννου  
> (By the treachery of a faithless tyrant)

- *Ὡς* introduces the content of *δήλωσον*. In the intervening condition, *εἰ μὴ ψεύδοντ’ ἐνταῦθ’ αἱ προοράσεις*, the substantival infinitive *l' antiveder* becomes a plural noun, ‘the forevisions’. *ψεύδοντ’* is elided *ψεύδονται*, third-person plural present middle of *ψεύδω*: ‘unless the forevisions prove false’, rather than ‘unless foreseeing is vain’. *ἐνταῦθ’* is *ἐνταῦθα* elided before the article.
- *Κατακρημνισθήσονται* and *πνιγήσονται* are third-person plural future passives of *κατακρημνίζω* and *πνίγω*, the first with *-θησ-*, the second with *-ησ-*. They expand the single auxiliary of *gittati saran … e mazzerati* into two finite predictions. The first specifies a headlong fall, the second the drowning rather than its particular manner.
- *τῆς νηὸς ἔξω* puts *ἔξω* after its genitive; *νηός* is the poetic/Ionic genitive of *ναῦς* (Attic *νεώς*). The article suffices without *lor*. Similarly, *Καθολικῆς πλησίον* puts the place-name in the genitive before ‘near’.
- *Τῇ προδοσίᾳ* is a dative of means or cause for *per tradimento*. The dependent genitive *παρασπόνδου τυράννου* makes *fello* specifically ‘faith-breaking’. Unlike the Italian and English, the supplied Greek does not close its sentence here.

> 82 Tra l' isola di Cipro e di Maiolica  
> 83 non vide mai sì gran fallo Nettuno,  
> 84 non da pirati, non da gente Argolica.  

Between the isles of Cyprus and Majorca, Neptune never saw so great a crime—not even with Pirates, not even with Argives.

> 82 „ Μεταξὺ νήσων Κύπρου καὶ Μαιορίκης.  
> (Between the islands of Cyprus and Majorca.)
>
> 83 „ Ποσειδῶν τοιόνδ’ οὔποτ’ ἀσέβημ’ εἶδεν,  
> (Neptune never saw so great an impiety,)
>
> 84 „ Οὔτ’ ἐκ πειρατῶν οὔτ’ ἀνδρῶν Ἀργολίδος.  
> (Either from pirates or from men of Argolis.)

- *Μεταξὺ νήσων Κύπρου καὶ Μαιορίκης* has *μεταξύ* governing the genitive, with the two names identifying the islands. The period **after** *Μαιορίκης* attaches the geographical phrase to the preceding prediction; in Italian *Tra l' isola di Cipro e di Maiolica* sets the limits for *non vide mai*.
- *Ποσειδῶν* replaces *Nettuno* by the Greek name and moves the subject to the front. *τοιόνδ’ … ἀσέβημ’* (elided *τοιόνδε*, *ἀσέβημα*) is the accusative object, ‘such an impious deed’, shifting *sì gran fallo* from magnitude to kind. *οὔποτ’* is elided *οὔποτε*, and *εἶδεν* is third-person singular second-aorist active of *ὁράω*.
- *Οὔτ’ … οὔτ’* repeats elided *οὔτε* to coordinate two sources under the negation. *ἐκ* governs both *πειρατῶν* and *ἀνδρῶν*; *Ἀργολίδος* is a further genitive, ‘men of Argolis’, for the collective *gente Argolica*.

> 85 Quel traditor, che vede pur con l' uno,  
> 86 e tien la terra, che tal è qui meco,  
> 87 vorrebbe di veder esser digiuno,  

That traitor who sees with but one eye, and holds the land which one *who is here with* *me would wish that he had never seen,*

> 85 „ Προδότης οὗτος ὁ μονοφθάλμως βλέπων,  
> (That traitor who sees with a single eye,)
>
> 86 „ Ὁ τῆς γῆς ἄρχων, ἣν ἐκ τῶν σὺν ἐμοί τις  
> (The ruler of the land which one of those with me)
>
> 87 „ Ἐβούλετο μή ποτ’ ἂν πρότερον γνῶναι,  
> (Would wish never to have known before,)

- *Προδότης οὗτος ὁ μονοφθάλμως βλέπων* follows *Quel traditor* with noun then demonstrative, but *οὗτος* is ‘this one’. The articular present active participle *ὁ … βλέπων* replaces the finite relative *che vede*; the single adverb *μονοφθάλμως*, ‘one-eyedly’, absorbs *pur con l' uno*.
- *Ὁ τῆς γῆς ἄρχων* adds an appositive articular expression, ‘the ruler of the land’, in place of coordinated finite *e tien la terra*. *ἄρχων*, formally the present active participle of *ἄρχω* and also used as a noun, takes the genitive of what is ruled.
- *ἣν ἐκ τῶν σὺν ἐμοί τις* begins with the feminine accusative relative referring to *γῆς* but governed by the infinitive in the next line. Indefinite *τις* is the subject of *ἐβούλετο*; partitive *ἐκ τῶν σὺν ἐμοί* is ‘from among those with me’, the article turning *σύν* with its dative into a designation of persons. There is no separate *qui*.
- *Ἐβούλετο* is third-person singular imperfect middle of *βούλομαι*. With *ἄν* the imperfect can express an unreal wish, answering to conditional *vorrebbe*; the particle is placed well after the finite verb, within *μή ποτ’ ἂν πρότερον γνῶναι*. *γνῶναι* is the second-aorist active infinitive of *γιγνώσκω*, with the same understood subject as *ἐβούλετο*. Negative *μή* with *ποτε* (elided *ποτ’*) gives ‘never’, and *πρότερον* makes the anteriority explicit. Thus ‘never to have known [it] before’ replaces the figurative *di veder esser digiuno*, literally ‘to be fasting from seeing’.

## Lines 88–120 — Curio, Mosca, and the Headless Trunk

> 88 farà venirli a parlamento seco;  

will make them come to parley with him;

> 88 „ Αὐτοὺς εἰς βουλὴν παρ’ ἑαυτῷ καλέσει,  
> (Will summon them to a parley with himself,)

- *Αὐτοὺς εἰς βουλὴν παρ’ ἑαυτῷ καλέσει* ‘them he will summon to a council at his own place’: *καλέσει* (3rd sg. fut. act. ind. of *καλέω*) does the work of both verbs in *farà venirli*. The object, enclitic in Italian, stands first as the independent accusative *αὐτούς*.
- *εἰς βουλήν* expresses the purpose of the summons; *παρ’* is *παρά* elided before *ἑαυτῷ*, the dative singular reflexive referring to the summoner. Where *seco* means ‘with himself’, the Greek dative after *παρά* locates the meeting ‘with him, at his place’, rather than expressing motion to his side.

> 89 poi farà sì che al vento di Focara  
> 90 non farà lor mestier voto nè preco.”  

then act so, that they shall need no vow nor prayer for Focara's wind."

> 89 „ Ποιήσει δ’ ὅπως κατ’ ἀνέμων Φωκάρου  
> (And will bring it to pass that against the winds of Focara)
>
> 90 „ Οὐ χρείαν ἕξουσ’ εὐχῶν οὐδ’ ἱκετείας.” —  
> (They will have no need of vows nor of prayer."—)

- *Ποιήσει δ’ ὅπως … οὐ χρείαν ἕξουσ’* ‘and he will see to it that they will have no need’: *ποιήσει* (3rd sg. fut. act. ind. of *ποιέω*) takes a clause of intended effect with *ὅπως* and the future indicative. Postpositive *δ’* (elided *δέ*) connects the actions without explicitly translating *poi*; there is no separate equivalent of *sì*.
- *ἕξουσ’* is elided *ἕξουσι*, 3rd pl. fut. act. ind. of *ἔχω*. The persons represented by *lor* become its subject: instead of ‘vow or prayer will not be necessary to them’, Greek has *χρείαν ἔχω* ‘have need’, with genitives *εὐχῶν* (plural of *εὐχή*, ‘vow, prayer’) and *ἱκετείας* (singular, ‘supplication’). *οὐ … οὐδ’* (*οὐδέ*) gives ‘not … nor’.
- *κατ’ ἀνέμων Φωκάρου* makes *al vento di Focara* adversative: *κατά* elided before the genitive means ‘against’, *ἀνέμων* is plural for the Italian singular, and *Φωκάρου* is a dependent genitive of place-name.

> 91 Ed io a lui: “Dimostrami e dichiara,  
> 92 se vuoi ch’io porti su di te novella,  
> 93 chi è colui dalla veduta amara.”  

And I to him : "Shew me and explain, so thou wouldst have me carry tidings up of thee, who he is that rues that sight."

> 91 Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν· “Εἰπὲ καὶ δήλωσόν μοι,  
> (And I to him: "Tell me and make known to me,)
>
> 92 „ Εἴ μ’ ἄνω θέλεις ἀγγέλματά σου φέρειν,  
> (If you wish me to carry tidings of you above,)
>
> 93 „ Τίς ἐσθ’ ὁ πικρὰν μνήμην τῆς χώρας ἔχων.”  
> (Who is he that has bitter memory of that region.")

- *Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν* follows *Ed io a lui* even in omitting the verb of saying. *κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*; *πρός* with the accusative marks the person addressed. *Εἰπὲ καὶ δήλωσόν μοι* gives two 2nd sg. aor. act. imperatives, *εἰπέ* (from the aorist *εἶπον*, ‘tell’) and *δήλωσον* (from *δηλόω*, ‘make clear’); the enclitic dative *μοι* is placed after the pair.
- *Εἴ μ’ ἄνω θέλεις ἀγγέλματά σου φέρειν* retains the open condition, *εἰ* with 2nd sg. pres. ind. *θέλεις*. For *vuoi ch’io porti* with a finite subjunctive, Greek uses an accusative subject *μ’* (elided *με*) and pres. act. infinitive *φέρειν*. *ἄνω* renders *su*; *ἀγγέλματά σου* takes singular *novella* as plural ‘tidings’, with *σου* understood objectively, ‘about you’.
- *Τίς ἐσθ’ ὁ … ἔχων* is the dependent question ‘who is the one who has …?’; *ἐσθ’* is *ἐστί* elided with aspiration before *ὁ*. The articular pres. act. participle *ἔχων* (nom. sg. masc.) replaces *colui dalla veduta amara*. Musurus interprets the compact *veduta* phrase as *πικρὰν μνήμην τῆς χώρας*, ‘a bitter memory of the region’: bitterness qualifies the recollection, and the place is made its explicit genitive object.

> 94 Allor pose la mano alla mascella  
> 95 d’un suo compagno, e la bocca gli aperse  
> 96 gridando: “Questi è desso, e non favella;  

Then he laid his hand upon the jaw of one of his companions; and opened the mouth of him, saying : "This is he, and he speaks not;

> 94 Τότε τὴν χεῖρα θέμενος ἐπὶ γνάθου  
> (Then laying his hand upon the jaw)
>
> 95 Ὀπαδοῦ τινος, ἤνοιξ’ αὐτοῦ τὸ στόμα  
> (Of one of his companions, he opened his mouth)
>
> 96 Κράζων· “Ὅδ’ αὐτὸς οὗτος· φωνὴν δ’ οὐκ ἔχει.  
> (Crying: "This is the very man; and he has no speech.)

- *Τότε τὴν χεῖρα θέμενος ἐπὶ γνάθου* ‘then, having laid his hand on a jaw’: finite *pose* becomes *θέμενος* (nom. sg. masc., 2nd aor. mid. participle of *τίθημι*), an action preliminary to the finite verb. The middle suits the use of his own hand; *ἐπί* with genitive *γνάθου* marks the point of contact. *Ὀπαδοῦ τινος* ‘of a certain companion’ depends on *γνάθου*; *suo* is not expressed.
- *ἤνοιξ’ αὐτοῦ τὸ στόμα* ‘he opened his mouth’ has elided *ἤνοιξε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀνοίγω*. The possessive genitive *αὐτοῦ* replaces the Italian dative *gli*. *Κράζων* (nom. sg. masc., pres. act. participle of *κράζω*) gives the simultaneous manner, ‘crying out’, close to *gridando*.
- *Ὅδ’ αὐτὸς οὗτος* ‘this one, this very man’ reproduces the emphasis of *Questi è desso* by juxtaposing demonstratives (*ὅδ’* = *ὅδε*) and intensive *αὐτός*, without a copula. *φωνὴν δ’ οὐκ ἔχει* ‘but he has no voice’ turns *non favella* from an action into a lack; *δέ* is contrastive where Italian has *e*.

> 97 questi scacciato il dubitar sommerse  
> 98 in Cesare, affermando che il fornito  
> 99 sempre con danno l’attender sofferse.”  

this outcast quenched the doubt in Cæsar, affirming that to men prepared delay is always hurtful."

> 97 „ Ἐξόριστος ὢν διέλυσ’ ἐνδοιάσεις  
> (Being an exile, he dispelled the doubts)
>
> 98 „ Καίσαρος δογματίσας· ‘“Ὅστις ὑπάρχει  
> (Of Caesar, declaring: 'Whoever is)
>
> 99 ‚ „ Ἕτοιμος, ᾀεὶ ζημιοῦται προσμένων.”’  
> (Prepared always suffers loss by waiting.'")

- *Ἐξόριστος ὢν* ‘being an exile’ renders *scacciato* by a predicate adjective with *ὤν*, pres. participle of *εἰμί* (nom. sg. masc.); the repeated *questi* is understood. *διέλυσ’ ἐνδοιάσεις Καίσαρος* ‘he dispelled Caesar’s doubts’ replaces the metaphor *il dubitar sommerse in Cesare*: *διέλυσ’* is elided *διέλυσε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *διαλύω*, with internal augment. Plural accusative *ἐνδοιάσεις* replaces the substantival infinitive, and genitive *Καίσαρος* identifies the doubter.
- *δογματίσας* ‘having pronounced’ is a nom. sg. masc. aor. act. participle of *δογματίζω*: it gives the pronouncement by which the doubt was dispelled. Instead of *affermando che* with indirect statement, the Greek introduces the pronouncement directly.
- *Ὅστις ὑπάρχει ἕτοιμος* ‘whoever is ready’ expands substantival *il fornito* into a general relative subject, with predicate *ἕτοιμος* after copular *ὑπάρχει*. *ᾀεὶ ζημιοῦται προσμένων* ‘always incurs loss by waiting’ makes the maxim present rather than using the gnomic past *sofferse*. *ζημιοῦται* is 3rd sg. pres. pass. ind. of *ζημιόω*; *προσμένων*, nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *προσμένω*, makes *l’attender* an attendant action of the person who suffers. *ᾀεί* is the spelling here of the adverb ‘always’ (usually *ἀεί*).

> 100 O quanto mi pareva sbigottito  
> 101 con la lingua tagliata nella strozza,  
> 102 Curio, ch’a dire fu così ardito!  

Oh, how dejected, with tongue slit in his gorge, seemed Curio to me, who was so daring in his speech!

> 100 Ὦ, πόσον νῦν μοι κατάπληκτος ἐφάνη,  
> (Oh, how dismayed he now appeared to me,)
>
> 101 Ἔχων τὴν γλῶσσαν ἐν λαιμῷ τετμημένην,  
> (Having his tongue cut off in his throat,)
>
> 102 Κουρίων, ὁ τολμήσας εἰπεῖν τοιαῦτα!  
> (Curio, who dared to speak such words!)

- *Ὦ, πόσον νῦν μοι κατάπληκτος ἐφάνη* ‘oh, how dismayed he now appeared to me’: *πόσον* is an adverbial accusative of degree; *κατάπληκτος* is predicate nominative. *ἐφάνη* (3rd sg., 2nd aor. pass. ind. of *φαίνω*, in the intransitive sense ‘appeared’) replaces imperfect *pareva*, and *νῦν* is supplied. As in Italian, the subject’s name is held back.
- *Ἔχων τὴν γλῶσσαν ἐν λαιμῷ τετμημένην* turns *con la lingua tagliata* into ‘having the tongue in the throat in a severed state’. *ἔχων* agrees with *Κουρίων*; *τετμημένην* (acc. sg. fem., reduplicated perfect pass. participle of *τέμνω*) agrees with *γλῶσσαν* and expresses the resulting condition.
- *Κουρίων, ὁ τολμήσας εἰπεῖν τοιαῦτα* ‘Curio, the one who dared to say such things’: the Italian relative *ch’a dire fu così ardito* becomes an articular aor. act. participle of *τολμάω* in apposition to the nominative name. *εἰπεῖν* is its complementary 2nd aor. act. infinitive; the degree of boldness in *così ardito* is recast as the character of the words, neut. acc. pl. *τοιαῦτα*.

> 103 Ed un, ch’avea l’una e l’altra man mozza,  
> 104 levando i moncherin per l’aura fosca,  
> 105 sì che il sangue facea la faccia sozza,  
> 106 gridò: “Ricordera’ ti anche del Mosca,  

And one who had both hands cut off, raising the stumps through the dim air so that their blood defiled his face, said : "Thou wilt recollect the Mosca, too, ah me!

> 103 Ἄλλος τις ὢν κολοβὸς ἄμφω τὰς χεῖρας,  
> (Another, maimed of both his hands,)
>
> 104 Ἄρας τὰς τομὰς ἐν τῷ στυγερῷ ζόφῳ,  
> (Raising the stumps in the gloomy darkness,)
>
> 105 Αἵματί τ’ ἔχων τὴν ὄψιν πεφυρμένην,  
> (And having his face defiled with blood,)
>
> 106 Ἔκραξε· “Μέμνησο δὴ σὺ καὶ τοῦ Μόσκου,  
> (Cried out: "You, then, remember also Mosca,)

- *Ἄλλος τις ὢν κολοβὸς ἄμφω τὰς χεῖρας* ‘another, mutilated as to both hands’: *ἄλλος τις* specifies *un* as another individual. Instead of the relative *ch’avea … man mozza*, *ὤν* joins the predicate *κολοβός* to the subject; *τὰς χεῖρας* is accusative of respect. *ἄμφω* is the dual numeral ‘both’ (here accusative), used with a plural article and noun for *l’una e l’altra man*.
- *Ἄρας τὰς τομάς* ‘having raised the cut ends’ has the nom. sg. masc. aor. act. participle of *αἴρω* (unaugmented, as participles are), presenting the raising as accomplished, unlike *levando*. *ἐν τῷ στυγερῷ ζόφῳ* ‘in the hateful gloom’ makes *per l’aura fosca* a setting rather than a space traversed, and replaces the air itself with darkness.
- *Αἵματί τ’ ἔχων τὴν ὄψιν πεφυρμένην* ‘and having his face befouled with blood’: *τ’* is elided connective *τε*. The result clause *sì che il sangue facea la faccia sozza* becomes a coordinated participial description: blood is instrumental dative, not subject, and *πεφυρμένην*, acc. sg. fem. perfect pass. participle of *φύρω*, predicates the resultant state of *τὴν ὄψιν*.
- *Ἔκραξε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *κράζω*) finally supplies the finite verb, as does *gridò*. *Μέμνησο δὴ σὺ καὶ τοῦ Μόσκου* replaces future *Ricordera’ ti* with a direct injunction: *μέμνησο* is the 2nd sg. perfect mid. imperative of *μιμνῄσκω*, ‘keep in mind’, governing the genitive. *δή* urges attention, explicit *σύ* stresses the addressee, and additive *καί* immediately precedes the name. The English places “ah me!” here; Italian *lasso* and Greek *φεῦ* fall in the next line.

> 107 che dissi, lasso! ‘Capo ha cosa fatta,’  
> 108 che fu il mal seme della gente tosca.”  

who said, 'A thing done has an end!' which was the seed of evil to the Tuscan people."

> 107 „ Ὅστις, φεῦ! εἶπε,” ‘“Πᾶν πραχθὲν ἐτελέσθη,”’  
> (Who, alas! said, 'What is done has an end,')
>
> 108 „ Ὃ πηγὴ κακῶν ἐγένετο τοῖς Τούσκοις.”  
> (Which became a fountain of evils to the Tuscans.")

- *Ὅστις, φεῦ! εἶπε* ‘who, alas, said’: the masculine relative is nominative as subject although its antecedent *τοῦ Μόσκου* is genitive. Italian *che dissi* refers back to the speaker in the first person; Greek uses 3rd sg. *εἶπε* (2nd aor. act. ind.) in agreement with the name. Interjection *φεῦ* replaces adjectival *lasso*.
- *Πᾶν πραχθὲν ἐτελέσθη* ‘everything done was brought to completion’: *πᾶν* generalizes *cosa*, with *πραχθέν* (nom. sg. neut., aor. pass. participle of *πράσσω*) for *fatta*. Musurus takes *capo* as ‘end’, but replaces present possession (*ha*) with completed action, *ἐτελέσθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *τελέω*).
- *Ὃ πηγὴ κακῶν ἐγένετο τοῖς Τούσκοις* ‘which became a source of evils for the Tuscans’: neuter relative *ὅ* takes up the saying. *πηγή* ‘spring’ replaces *seme*, with genitive plural *κακῶν* in place of adjective *mal*. *ἐγένετο* (3rd sg. 2nd aor. mid. ind. of *γίγνομαι*) means ‘became’ rather than *fu*; dative plural *τοῖς Τούσκοις* makes *della gente tosca* the people adversely affected.

> 109 Ed io gli aggiunsi: “E morte di tua schiatta”;  

"And death to thy kindred!" I added [thereto];

> 109 Ἐγὼ δ’ ἐπεῖπον· “Καὶ θανάτου φυλῇ σου.”  
> (And I added: "And of death to your kindred.")

- *Ἐγὼ δ’ ἐπεῖπον* ‘and I added’ precedes the quotation as in Italian; emphatic *ἐγώ* changes the speaker. *ἐπεῖπον* is 1st sg. 2nd aor. act. ind. of the compound *ἐπιλέγω*, ‘say in addition’; its prefix conveys *aggiunsi*, without a separate dative for *gli*.
- *Καὶ θανάτου φυλῇ σου* ‘and of death for your clan’ continues the preceding Greek construction with remarkable economy: *θανάτου* is genitive like *κακῶν* after understood *πηγή*, and *φυλῇ σου* is dative like *τοῖς Τούσκοις*. Thus *di tua schiatta* is not translated by a genitive dependent on ‘death’; *σου* alone is possessive, dependent on *φυλῇ*.

> 110 per ch’egli accumulando duol con duolo,  
> 111 sen gìo come persona trista e matta.  

wherefore he, accumulating pain on pain, went away as one distressed and mad.

> 110 Ὁ δ’ ἐπὶ λύπης ἐπισωρεύων λύπην,  
> (And he, heaping grief upon grief,)
>
> 111 Ἀπῆλθ’ ὡς ἀνὴρ μαινόμενος τῷ πάθει.  
> (Departed like a man maddened by suffering.)

- *Ὁ δ’* ‘and he’ uses the article pronominally in the common construction with *δέ*, without explicitly expressing the consequence in *per ch’egli*. *ἐπὶ λύπης ἐπισωρεύων λύπην* puts ‘upon grief’ first (*ἐπί* + genitive), then repeats the noun in the accusative as object of *ἐπισωρεύων*, nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *ἐπισωρεύω*, ‘heap upon’. The participle follows the continuing action of *accumulando*.
- *Ἀπῆλθ’* is elided *ἀπῆλθε*, 3rd sg. 2nd aor. act. ind. of *ἀπέρχομαι*; the prefix supplies the direction in *sen gìo*. *ὡς ἀνὴρ μαινόμενος τῷ πάθει* ‘like a man raving from suffering’ replaces the coordinate adjectives *trista e matta* by *μαινόμενος* (nom. sg. masc. pres. mid. participle of *μαίνομαι*) and a causal dative: the distress is made the cause of the madness.

> 112 Ma io rimasi a riguardar lo stuolo,  
> 113 e vidi cosa ch’io avrei paura,  
> 114 senza più prova, di contarla solo;  

But I remained to view the troop, and saw a thing which I should be afraid even to relate, without more proof;

> 112 Ἐγὼ δὲ μείνας κἀτενίζων τὸ πλῆθος,  
> (And I, remaining and gazing intently at the throng,)
>
> 113 Πρᾶγμ’ αἴφνης εἶδον, ὅπερ οὐκ ἂν ἐτόλμων  
> (Suddenly saw a thing which I would not dare)
>
> 114 Ἀφηγήσασθαι μόνος ἀναποδείκτως,  
> (To recount alone without proof,)

- *Ἐγὼ δὲ μείνας κἀτενίζων τὸ πλῆθος* ‘but I, having stayed and looking intently at the crowd’: *μείνας* (nom. sg. masc. aor. act. participle of *μένω*) subordinates finite *rimasi* to ‘saw’. *κἀτενίζων* is crasis of *καὶ ἀτενίζων*; the pres. act. participle of *ἀτενίζω* makes *a riguardar* a simultaneous action rather than the purpose of remaining. *τὸ πλῆθος* is its direct object.
- *Πρᾶγμ’ αἴφνης εἶδον* fronts the accusative *πρᾶγμα* (elided) for *cosa*, and adds *αἴφνης* ‘suddenly’. *εἶδον* is the 1st sg. 2nd aor. act. ind. used with *ὁράω*. Neuter relative *ὅπερ* refers to *πρᾶγμα* and serves as object of *ἀφηγήσασθαι*; Greek needs no resumptive pronoun corresponding to *-la* in *contarla*.
- *οὐκ ἂν ἐτόλμων ἀφηγήσασθαι* ‘I would not dare to recount’: *ἄν* with 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. *ἐτόλμων* (from *τολμάω*) gives the unreal consequence, not a potential optative. The negative verb of daring replaces positive *avrei paura*. *ἀφηγήσασθαι* is the aor. mid. infinitive of *ἀφηγέομαι*. *μόνος*, agreeing with the speaker, takes *solo* as ‘alone, on my own’, not as ‘merely’ qualifying the telling; *ἀναποδείκτως* ‘without demonstration’ compresses *senza più prova* and omits the comparative *più*.

> 115 se non che coscienza mi assicura,  
> 116 la buona compagnia che l’uom francheggia  
> 117 sotto l’osbergo del sentirsi pura.  

but that conscience reassures me, that good companion which fortifies a man beneath the hauberk of his self-felt purity.

> 115 Εἰ μή μ’ ἐνεθάῤῥυνε τὸ συνειδός μου,  
> (Did not my conscience embolden me,)
>
> 116 Ὃ πιστός ἐστ’ ὀπαδὸς σώζων ἀνθρώπους  
> (Which is a faithful companion preserving men)
>
> 117 Ὑπὸ θώρακα καθαρῶν αἰσθημάτων.  
> (Beneath the breastplate of pure feelings.)

- *Εἰ μή μ’ ἐνεθάῤῥυνε τὸ συνειδός μου* ‘if my conscience did not encourage me’ makes *se non che* the negative condition answering the preceding *ἄν*. *ἐνεθάῤῥυνε* is 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἐνθαρρύνω*, with internal augment and the older typography *ῤῥ* for double rho; the past indicative belongs to the unreal construction, whereas *assicura* is present. Elided *μ’* is the object; the subject *τὸ συνειδός* is the substantivized neut. sg. perfect act. participle of *σύνοιδα*, ‘conscience’, with possessive *μου* supplied.
- *Ὃ πιστός ἐστ’ ὀπαδὸς σώζων ἀνθρώπους* ‘which is a faithful companion preserving people’: relative *ὅ* agrees with neuter *συνειδός*, and *ἐστ’* is elided *ἐστί*. Italian puts *la buona compagnia* in apposition and follows it with a relative; Greek puts the equation inside the relative clause. *πιστός* ‘faithful’ replaces *buona*; masculine *σώζων* (pres. act. participle of *σώζω*) agrees with predicate *ὀπαδός*, not with the neuter subject. *ἀνθρώπους* generalizes in the plural where *l’uom* is generic singular; ‘preserving’ shifts the sense of *francheggia* away from emboldening.
- *Ὑπὸ θώρακα καθαρῶν αἰσθημάτων* ‘under a breastplate of pure feelings’: *ὑπό* takes the accusative, retaining the image of shelter, and *θώρακα* is acc. sg. of *θώραξ*. The genitive plural makes the *αἰσθήματα* themselves pure; *del sentirsi pura*, by contrast, expresses a reflexive awareness of being pure.

> 118 Io vidi certo, ed ancor par ch’io ’l veggia,  
> 119 un busto senza capo andar, sì come  
> 120 andavan gli altri della trista greggia.  

Certainly I saw, and still seem to see [it,] a trunk going without a head, as the others of that dismal herd were going.

> 118 Εἶδον βεβαίως, οἴομαι δ’ ἔτι βλέπειν,  
> (I saw assuredly, and I seem to see it still,)
>
> 119 Σῶμα βαδίζον ἄνευ κεφαλῆς, ὥσπερ  
> (A body walking without a head, just as)
>
> 120 Βαίνουσ’ οἱ λοιποὶ τῆς λυπηρᾶς ἀγέλης.  
> (The rest of the doleful herd walk.)

- *Εἶδον βεβαίως, οἴομαι δ’ ἔτι βλέπειν* ‘I saw assuredly, and I think I still see’: *βεβαίως* renders adverbial *certo*; *οἴομαι* (1st sg. pres. mid. ind.) personalizes impersonal *par ch’io*. Its same-subject complement is the pres. act. infinitive *βλέπειν*, not a finite subjunctive; *ἔτι* gives *ancor*. The object is deferred rather than anticipated by a pronoun like *’l*.
- *Σῶμα βαδίζον ἄνευ κεφαλῆς* ‘a body walking without a head’: *σῶμα* is less specific than *busto*. Acc. sg. neut. *βαδίζον*, pres. act. participle of *βαδίζω*, agrees with the object in the Greek construction for perceiving something in action, in place of the Italian infinitive *andar*. *ἄνευ* governs genitive *κεφαλῆς*.
- *ὥσπερ βαίνουσ’ οἱ λοιποὶ τῆς λυπηρᾶς ἀγέλης* ‘just as the rest of the doleful herd walk’: *ὥσπερ* alone gives the emphatic comparison *sì come*. *βαίνουσ’* is elided *βαίνουσι*, 3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *βαίνω*, present against imperfect *andavan*. Greek varies the verb where Italian repeats *andar*; substantival *οἱ λοιποί* takes the partitive genitive *τῆς λυπηρᾶς ἀγέλης*, closely following *gli altri della trista greggia*.

## Lines 121–142 — The Severed Head Speaks

> 121 E il capo tronco tenea per le chiome,  
> 122 pesol con mano a guisa di lanterna,  

And it was holding by the hair the severed head, swinging in his hand like a lantern;

> 121 Τμητὴν δ’ ἐκράτει τὴν κεφαλὴν ἐκ κόμης  
> (And by the hair it held the severed head)
>
> 122 Τῇ χειρὶ δονουμένην ἐν εἴδει λύχνου.  
> (Swinging in its hand after the fashion of a lantern.)

- *Τμητὴν δ’ ἐκράτει τὴν κεφαλήν* puts ‘severed’ first, separating it from ‘the head’ by the verb; Dante gives first the whole object, *il capo tronco*, then *tenea*. *Τμητήν* (acc. sg. fem. of the verbal adjective *τμητός*, from *τέμνω*) agrees with *κεφαλήν* but stands outside the article: ‘the head, cut off’. Postpositive *δ’* is *δέ* elided before the augmented *ἐκράτει* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *κρατέω*); the imperfect matches the continuing hold of *tenea*.
- *ἐκ κόμης* ‘by the hair’ uses *ἐκ* with a genitive denoting the point from which the head hangs; singular collective *κόμης* answers to plural *per le chiome*. *Τῇ χειρί* (dat. sg. of *χείρ*) gives the hand as instrument, without a possessive, for *con mano*.
- *δονουμένην* (pres. mid./pass. participle of *δονέω*, acc. sg. fem., passive in sense) goes with *κεφαλήν*: what is held *pesol*, ‘suspended’, is represented in motion, ‘being swung’. *ἐν εἴδει λύχνου* ‘in the likeness of a lamp’ closely follows the noun construction *a guisa di lanterna*: *εἴδει* is the dat. sg. of *εἶδος*, *λύχνου* a dependent genitive.

> 123 e quel mirava noi, e dicea: “O me!”  

and that looked at us and said: "O me!"

> 123 Αὕτη δὲ βλέπουσ’ ἡμᾶς ἔλεγεν· “Οἴμοι!”  
> (And that head, looking at us, said: "Woe is me!")

- *Αὕτη δέ* ‘and this one’ picks up feminine *κεφαλή*, where masculine *quel* picks up *capo*. Of the two coordinated imperfects *mirava … e dicea*, only the second remains finite: *βλέπουσ’ ἡμᾶς* ‘looking at us’ (elided *βλέπουσα*, pres. act. participle, nom. sg. fem., with acc. object) accompanies *ἔλεγεν* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *λέγω*, with augment and movable *ν*).
- *Οἴμοι* is the idiomatic cry corresponding to *O me!*: *οἴ* with dative *μοι*, not an accusative as in Italian.

> 124 Di sè faceva a sè stesso lucerna,  

Of itself it made for itself a lamp,

> 124 Ἐξ αὑτοῦ δ’ ἐποίησεν ἑαυτῷ δᾷδα,  
> (And of itself it made a torch for itself,)

- *Ἐξ αὑτοῦ … ἑαυτῷ* keeps both the order and the distinct functions of *di sè … a sè stesso*: *ἐξ* before a vowel with the genitive marks the material or source, the bare dative the beneficiary. Rough-breathed *αὑτοῦ* is the contracted reflexive *ἑαυτοῦ*, not *αὐτοῦ* ‘of him’; the reflexives now refer to the holder, not to feminine *κεφαλή*.
- *δ’ ἐποίησεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ποιέω*) adds a connective and presents the making as a whole, against imperfect *faceva*. *δᾷδα* (acc. sg. of *δᾴς*) is its object: ‘torch’ for *lucerna*, rather than a repetition of *λύχνος*.

> 125 ed eran due in uno; ed uno in due;  

and they were two in one, and one in two;

> 125 Ἐν ἑνὶ δύο τ’ ἦσαν, πρὸς δ’ ἓν εἰς δύο.  
> (And they were two in one, and moreover one in two.)

- *Ἐν ἑνὶ δύο τ’ ἦσαν* fronts ‘in one’, which in *eran due in uno* comes last. *ἑνί* is the dat. sg. of *εἷς* after *ἐν*; *δύο* is predicative. Despite the count of two, *ἦσαν* is the 3rd **plural** imperfect of *εἰμί*, not a dual. Enclitic *τ’* is *τε* elided before *ἦσαν*.
- *πρὸς δ’* uses *πρός* adverbially, ‘and besides’; the copula is understood in *ἓν εἰς δύο*. *ἕν* is neuter singular, ‘one thing’. Where Dante repeats static *in* in reverse order, Musurus changes from *ἐν* with the dative to directional *εἰς* with the accusative, ‘one into two’.

> 126 com’ esser può, quei sa che sì governa.  

how this can be, He knows who so ordains.

> 126 Πῶς τοῦτο; μόνος ὁ διατάξας οἶδεν.  
> (How this may be, only He who ordained it knows.)

- *Πῶς τοῦτο;* makes *com’ esser può* a separate, verbless question, ‘how this?’; the neuter demonstrative sums up the preceding clause, with no expressed counterpart to *può*. *μόνος* ‘alone’ is added to the answer.
- *ὁ διατάξας* ‘the one who ordained’ is an articular aor. act. participle (nom. sg. masc. of *διατάσσω*) in place of demonstrative and relative *quei … che sì governa*. It presents an ordering as accomplished rather than a governing as continuing; *sì* has no separate equivalent. *οἶδεν* (3rd sg. of *οἶδα*, perfect in form, present in sense) moves *sa* to the end.

> 127 Quando diritto al piè del ponte fue,  
> 128 levò il braccio alto con tutta la testa  
> 129 per appressarne le parole sue,  

When it was just at the foot of our bridge, it raised its arm high up, with all the head, to bring near to us its words,

> 127 Εὐθὺ δ’ εἰς πόδας προῆλθε τῆς γεφύρας,  
> (And straight to the foot of the bridge it came,)
>
> 128 Ἄρας σὺν τῇ κεφαλῇ τὴν χεῖρ’ ἐπάνω,  
> (Raising its hand on high together with the head,)
>
> 129 Ἵν’ ἀκούσωμεν ἐγγὺς αὐτοῦ τοὺς λόγους,  
> (So that we might hear its words close by,)

- *Εὐθὺ δ’ εἰς πόδας προῆλθε τῆς γεφύρας* replaces temporal *Quando … fue* with a main clause of motion, ‘and it came straight to the feet of the bridge’. Adverbial *εὐθύ* takes up *diritto*; *εἰς πόδας* (acc. pl. of *πούς*) pluralizes *al piè*, with the dependent genitive *τῆς γεφύρας* delayed past the verb. *προῆλθε* is the 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *προέρχομαι*, with the augment after the preverb.
- Conversely, the Italian main verb *levò* becomes circumstantial *ἄρας* ‘raising’ (aor. act. participle of *αἴρω*, nom. sg. masc., agreeing with the unexpressed subject of *προῆλθε*). *τὴν χεῖρ’* is elided *τὴν χεῖρα*, acc. sg., ‘the hand’ for *il braccio*; adverbial *ἐπάνω* gives the force of *alto*. *σὺν τῇ κεφαλῇ* keeps *con … la testa* as accompaniment with the dative, without separately rendering *tutta*.
- *Ἵν’ ἀκούσωμεν* (elided *ἵνα* with 1st pl. aor. act. subj. of *ἀκούω*) replaces the purpose infinitive *per appressarne*: instead of his bringing the words near us, ‘that we may hear’ them. The subjunctive is retained after a past main verb rather than shifted to an optative. *ἐγγύς* supplies the notion of nearness; *αὐτοῦ τοὺς λόγους* puts the possessive genitive before the article and the accusative object of hearing.

> 130 che furo: “Or vedi la pena molesta  
> 131 tu che, spirando, vai veggendo i morti;  

which were: "Now see the grievous penalty, thou, who breathing goest to view the dead;

> 130 Λέγοντος· “Τὴν εἰδεχθῆ νῦν ποινὴν βλέπε  
> (As it said: "Behold now the hideous punishment,)
>
> 131 „ Ὁ ζῶν ἔτι σὺ κ’ εἰς τοὺς νεκροὺς φοιτάζων.  
> (You who are still living and visit the dead.)

- *Λέγοντος* ‘as he spoke’ (pres. act. participle of *λέγω*, gen. sg. masc.) drops the Italian relative construction *che furo*, whose subject is the words. Its case allows it to pick up the speaker in the preceding *αὐτοῦ*: the transition is through the person speaking rather than through a verb predicated of *λόγους*.
- *Τὴν εἰδεχθῆ νῦν ποινὴν βλέπε* moves the object of *Or vedi* to the front and the imperative *βλέπε* (2nd sg. pres. act. of *βλέπω*) to the end. *νῦν* intervenes between adjective and noun; *εἰδεχθῆ* (acc. sg. fem. of *εἰδεχθής*) makes *molesta* ‘repulsive to behold’ rather than simply ‘grievous’.
- *Ὁ ζῶν ἔτι σὺ κ’ εἰς τοὺς νεκροὺς φοιτάζων* uses a nominative of address with explicit *σύ*, not a relative clause after *tu*. *ζῶν* (contracted pres. act. participle of *ζάω*) interprets *spirando* as ‘still living’; *φοιτάζων* (pres. act. participle of *φοιτάζω*), likewise nom. sg. masc., changes *vai veggendo* into ‘visiting’, with *εἰς* and the substantivized adjective *τοὺς νεκρούς* as goal. *κ’* is the poetic shortened *καί* before a vowel.

> 132 vedi se alcuna è grande come questa.  

see if any be as great as this!

> 132 „ Βλέπ’, εἰ τῆς ποινῆς ταύτης μείζων ἔστ’ ἄλλη.  
> (See whether any other punishment is greater than this.)

- *Βλέπ’, εἰ* repeats the imperative, now elided, and introduces an indirect yes-or-no question with *εἰ* and indicative *ἔστ’* (elided *ἔστι*). *ἄλλη* ‘another’ (nom. sg. fem., with *ποινή* understood) gives the subject corresponding to *alcuna*.
- *τῆς ποινῆς ταύτης μείζων* changes the comparison: not *grande come questa* but ‘greater than this punishment’. *μείζων* (nom. sg. fem., comparative of *μέγας*) is predicative; its genitive of comparison repeats the noun and puts demonstrative *ταύτης* outside the article.

> 133 E perchè tu di me novella porti,  
> 134 sappi ch’ io son Bertram dal Bornio, quelli  
> 135 che diedi al re giovane mai comforti.  

And that thou mayest carry tidings of me, know, that I am Bertran de Born, he who to the Young King gave evil counsels.

> 133 „ Ἀλλ’ ὡς ἂν ἀγγελίας ἐμὰς κομίσῃς,  
> (But that you may carry tidings of me,)
>
> 134 „ Υἱόν μ᾽ ὄντ᾽ ἴσθι Βερτράμου τοῦ Βρονίου,  
> (Know that I am the son of Bertran de Born,)
>
> 135 „ Κακὴν συμβουλὴν δόντα Βασιλεῖ Νέῳ.  
> (Who gave evil counsel to the Young King.)

- *Ἀλλ’ ὡς ἂν … κομίσῃς* gives *E perchè … porti* a stronger transition, ‘but in order that you may convey’. *ἀλλ’* is elided *ἀλλά*; final *ὡς* with the modal particle *ἄν* takes *κομίσῃς* (2nd sg. aor. act. subj. of *κομίζω*), not an optative. *ἀγγελίας ἐμάς* ‘tidings of mine’ substitutes a possessive adjective agreeing with a plural object for singular *di me novella*.
- *Υἱόν μ᾽ ὄντ᾽ ἴσθι* is literally ‘know me to be a son’. *ἴσθι* is the 2nd sg. imperative of *οἶδα* ‘know’ (not the identically spelled imperative ‘be’); it takes elided accusative *μ᾽* (*με*) with supplementary participle *ὄντ᾽* (*ὄντα*, pres. of *εἰμί*, acc. sg. masc.) instead of *sappi ch’ io son*. The fronted predicate *υἱόν* has no equivalent in Dante: *Βερτράμου τοῦ Βρονίου* now depends on ‘son’ in the genitive. Thus the name identifies the father rather than the speaker.
- *δόντα* (acc. sg. masc., second aor. act. participle of *δίδωμι*) agrees with *με*, not with genitive *Βερτράμου*: ‘me … who gave’. It replaces *quelli che diedi*, dropping both demonstrative and finite first person. *κακὴν συμβουλήν* is a singular direct object for plural *mai comforti*; *Βασιλεῖ Νέῳ* preserves the recipient *al re giovane* in the dative singular, adjective agreeing with title.

> 136 Io feci il padre e il figlio in sè ribelli;  

I made the father and the son rebels to each other;

> 136 „ Πατέρ᾽ ἐποίησ᾽ υἱόν τ᾽ ἐχθροὺς ἀλλήλοις·  
> (I made father and son enemies to one another;)

- *Πατέρ᾽ ἐποίησ᾽ υἱόν τ᾽* interlaces the two accusative objects with the verb: *πατέρα*, *ἐποίησα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ποιέω*), and enclitic *τε* are all elided. Postpositive *τε* coordinates *υἱόν* with *πατέρα*; *Io* is expressed only by the verb ending.
- *ἐχθροὺς ἀλλήλοις* ‘enemies to one another’ gives *ribelli* as a predicate accusative plural applying to both objects; the reciprocal dative *ἀλλήλοις* makes the sense of *in sè* explicit.

> 137 Achitofel non fe’ più d’ Ansalone  
> 138 e di David co’ malvagi pungelli.  

Ahithophel did not do more with Absalom and David by his malicious instigations.

> 137 „ Πρὸς Ἀβεσσαλὼμ καὶ Δαυῒδ Ἀχιτόφελ  
> (Toward Absalom and David, Ahithophel)
>
> 138 „ Οὐ βουλαῖς ἐπιβούλοις ἔπραξε χείρω.  
> (Did no worse with treacherous counsels.)

- *Πρὸς Ἀβεσσαλὼμ καὶ Δαυίδ* fronts the two names governed by *πρός* ‘toward, in relation to’ with the accusative. It thus takes *d’ Ansalone e di David* as specifying the persons concerned, not the standard of comparison. The foreign names show no case ending; *Ἀχιτόφελ*, likewise indeclinable, is the subject.
- *Οὐ … ἔπραξε χείρω* gives ‘did not do worse’ for *non fe’ più*. *ἔπραξε* is the 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *πράσσω*; *χείρω* is the contracted neut. acc. pl. of comparative *χείρων* (for *χείρονα*), ‘worse things’. Instrumental *βουλαῖς ἐπιβούλοις* (dat. pl. fem.) turns *co’ malvagi pungelli* into ‘by treacherous counsels’, with a pointed repetition of the sound of *βουλ-*.

> 139 Perch’ io partii così giunte persone,  
> 140 partito porto il mio cerebro, lasso!  
> 141 dal suo principio ch’ è in questo troncone.  

Because I parted persons thus united, I carry my brain, ah me! parted from its source which is in this trunk.

> 139 „ Ὡς χωρίσας δὲ δύο συνηνωμένους,  
> (And because I parted two so closely joined,)
>
> 140 „ Τὸν ἐγκέφαλον, φεῦ ! χωρισθέντα φέρω  
> (Alas! I carry my brain parted)
>
> 141 „ Ἐκ τῆς βάσεως οὔσης ἐν κορμῷ τῷδε·  
> (From its source which is in this trunk;)

- *Ὡς χωρίσας δέ* replaces causal *Perch’ io partii* with *ὡς* and a circumstantial participle, giving the reason as the speaker presents it. *χωρίσας* is nom. sg. masc., aor. act. of *χωρίζω*, with the subject of *φέρω*. *δύο συνηνωμένους* ‘two who had been joined’ replaces *così giunte persone*: the number is explicit, the noun and the adverb of degree disappear. *συνηνωμένους* (perf. mid./pass. participle of *συνενόω*, acc. pl. masc., passive in sense) denotes the resulting state.
- *Τὸν ἐγκέφαλον … χωρισθέντα φέρω* puts ‘the brain’ first, without a possessive, and agrees *χωρισθέντα* (acc. sg. masc., aor. pass. participle of *χωρίζω*) with it: ‘I carry it separated’. The active/passive pair *χωρίσας … χωρισθέντα* reproduces *partii … partito*, though the Italian puts *partito* before both verb and object. *φέρω* is 1st sg. pres. act. ind.; interjected *φεῦ* renders adjectival *lasso!* without an agreement form.
- *Ἐκ τῆς βάσεως* uses *ἐκ* and the genitive of *βάσις* ‘base’ for *dal suo principio*, again leaving possession implicit. *οὔσης* (pres. participle of *εἰμί*, gen. sg. fem.) qualifies *βάσεως* in place of relative *ch’ è*; it is not a genitive absolute. *ἐν κορμῷ τῷδε* has locative *ἐν* with the dative; postposed *τῷδε* (dat. sg. masc. of *ὅδε*) renders *questo*.

> 142 Così s’ osserva in me lo contrapasso.”  

Thus the law of retribution is observed in me.”

> 142 „ Οὕτω δ᾽ ὑπέχω τὴν ἀντιποίνου δίκην.”  
> (And thus I endure the law of retribution.")

- *Οὕτω δ᾽* ‘and thus’ preserves initial *Così* and adds connective *δέ*, elided before *ὑπέχω*. In place of *s’ osserva in me*, with *lo contrapasso* as subject, Musurus makes the speaker the grammatical subject: *ὑπέχω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind. of *ὑπέχω*) with *δίκην* is the idiom ‘I suffer the penalty’.
- *τὴν ἀντιποίνου δίκην* distributes *lo contrapasso* over two nouns: governing *δίκην* (acc. sg. of *δίκη*) and dependent *ἀντιποίνου* (gen. sg. of *ἀντίποινον*, ‘requital’), placed between its article and noun. The Greek thus ends on the penalty undergone rather than on the observance of a law.

## Summary — Recasting the Clause, Preserving the Echo

Musurus often keeps Dante’s order of perception while changing which action carries the finite verb. Thus *s’aperse … dicendo* becomes *ἀνοίξας εἶπεν*, ‘having opened, he said’: the gesture is preliminary, the speech is the main act. Present participles more often mark an action in progress, while perfect passives describe what remains visible after an action, as in *τετμημένην*, ‘in a severed state’. An article and participle can take the place of a whole Italian relative clause: *che vede pur con l’uno* becomes *ὁ μονοφθάλμως βλέπων*, ‘the one who sees with a single eye’. Yet Musurus can also follow Italian syntax closely. *Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν*, like *Ed io a lui*, dispenses with a verb of saying; *πρὶν … διαβῶμεν*, ‘before we pass’, follows *prima ch’ … rivada* in using a finite subjunctive where Attic prose would more naturally use an infinitive.

Particles help hold together clauses that have been recast. The opening *ἄν* with the optative turns *poria … dicer* into *ἂν εἴποι*, ‘could say’; another potential optative, *ἂν ὁμοιωθείη*, ‘could be likened’, closes the long comparison. Within that comparison, *εἴπερ*, ‘if indeed’, introduces an imperfect indicative rather than matching Dante’s imperfect subjunctive, and the genitive absolute *πάντων … δεικνύντων*, ‘with all showing’, gathers into one group the individuals distinguished by *qual … qual*. Elsewhere postpositive *τε* links words or participles, *γάρ* makes a reason explicit, and *δέ* often carries the narrative forward without preserving a more specific Italian link such as *poi*, ‘then’.

The departures are sharpest where a gloss or a different name takes the place of Dante’s wording. *Ἄρρυθμοι λόγοι*, ‘unrhythmed words’, interprets *parole sciolte* rather than reproducing the image of words set free. *Ῥωμαίων*, ‘of Romans’, answers to *Troiani*; *Ἄρειος*, ‘Arius’, replaces *Maometto*, while *ἄλλος*, ‘another’, leaves *Alì* unnamed. The added *υἱόν*, ‘son’, makes Bertran the speaker’s father rather than the speaker himself. By contrast, the closing *χωρίσας … χωρισθέντα*, ‘having parted … parted’, preserves the verbal echo of *partii … partito* with particular precision. Even there fidelity is local rather than uniform: in the last sentence *ὑπέχω … δίκην*, ‘I suffer the penalty’, makes the speaker the subject where Dante makes *lo contrapasso* itself the subject of *s’osserva*.
