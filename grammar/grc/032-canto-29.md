# Inferno 29

## Lines 1–21 — A Gaze Fixed on Kindred

> 1 La molta gente e le diverse piaghe  
> 2 avean le luci mie sì inebriate,  
> 3 che dello stare a piangere eran vaghe;  

The many people and the diverse wounds had made my eyes so drunken that they longed to stay and weep;

> 1 Πληγῶν παντοίων καὶ πλήθους πολλοῦ θέα  
> (The sight of all manner of wounds and of the great throng)
>
> 2 Οὕτω κατεμέθυσε τὰς ἐμὰς ὄψεις,  
> (Had so intoxicated my eyes,)
>
> 3 Ὥστ’ ἐπιθυμεῖν παραμεῖναι καὶ κλαίειν.  
> (That I longed to remain and to weep.)

- Musurus makes *θέα* ‘sight’, delayed to the end of line 1, the singular subject. Dante’s two subjects become genitives dependent on it, in reverse order: *πληγῶν παντοίων* ‘of wounds of every kind’ (gen. pl.) and *πλήθους πολλοῦ* ‘of a great throng’ (gen. sg. neut. of *πλῆθος* and *πολύς*). It is thus the sight of the two, not the two themselves, that acts on the eyes.
- *Οὕτω κατεμέθυσε τὰς ἐμὰς ὄψεις* ‘so intoxicated my eyes’ turns *avean le luci mie sì inebriate* into a single transitive verb, *κατεμέθυσε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *καταμεθύω*, augmented after the prefix), rather than a pluperfect. *ὄψεις* (acc. pl. of *ὄψις*) retains the visual sense of *luci* without its image of lights; *ἐμάς* is the agreeing possessive adjective between article and noun.
- *Οὕτω … ὥστ’ ἐπιθυμεῖν* ‘so … as to desire’ renders *sì … che … eran vaghe*: *ὥστ’* is *ὥστε* elided, and the result is expressed by the present active infinitive of *ἐπιθυμέω*, not by a finite clause with a plural adjective. No subject of the infinitive is separately expressed: the desire can still be understood of the eyes, as in Dante, though the supplied English gloss takes it of the speaker. *παραμεῖναι* (aor. act. inf. of *παραμένω*) and *κλαίειν* (pres. act. inf. of *κλαίω*) are coordinated complements, ‘to stay and to weep’, where *stare a piangere* binds the activities together.

> 4 ma Virgilio mi disse: “Che pur guate?  

but Virgil said to me: “Why art thou gazing still?

> 4 Πλὴν Βιργίλιος εἶπέ μοι· “ Τί δὴ βλέπεις;  
> (But Virgil said to me: “What then are you gazing at?)

- *Πλήν* stands independently as the adversative ‘but’. *Βιργίλιος εἶπέ μοι* closely follows *Virgilio mi disse*: *εἶπε* is the 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. used for *λέγω*; its additional accent in *εἶπέ μοι* is due to the following enclitic dative *μοι*.
- *Τί δὴ βλέπεις;* allows ‘what, then, are you looking at?’ as well as an adverbial *τί*, ‘why?’ *βλέπεις* is 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *βλέπω*. The postpositive *δή* presses the question; it does not explicitly say ‘still’ for *pur*, as *ἔτι* will in the next question.

> 5 perchè la vista tua pur si soffolge  
> 6 laggiù tra l' ombre triste smozzicate?  

wherefore does thy sight still rest, down there, among the dismal mutilated shadows?

> 5 „ Τί δὲ δὴ τοὺς ὀφθαλμοὺς ἔτ’ ἐπερείδεις  
> („ And why then do you still fix your eyes)
>
> 6 „ Εἰς τὰς οἰκτρὰς καὶ πηρὰς ψυχὰς ἐκείνας;  
> („ Upon those pitiable and maimed souls?)

- *Τί δὲ δὴ … ἔτ’ ἐπερείδεις* ‘and why, then, do you still fix …?’ links the questions by continuative *δέ* and repeats the emphatic *δή*. *ἔτ’* is elided *ἔτι*, answering to the second *pur*. *ἐπερείδεις* is 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἐπερείδω*: where *la vista tua … si soffolge* makes sight the subject of a reflexive verb, Greek makes the observer the subject and *τοὺς ὀφθαλμούς* the direct object. The article suffices for ‘your’ with the body part.
- *Εἰς τὰς οἰκτρὰς καὶ πηρὰς ψυχὰς ἐκείνας* gives the direction of the gaze, ‘upon those pitiable and maimed souls’, instead of locating it *laggiù tra l’ ombre*. *οἰκτράς* interprets *triste* as exciting pity; *πηράς* describes impairment rather than the particular manner of mutilation in *smozzicate*. Both adjectives are acc. pl. fem. with *ψυχάς*, explicit ‘souls’ for ‘shadows’. The distal demonstrative *ἐκείνας*, outside the article–noun group as usual, conveys distance without a separate ‘down there’.

> 7 Tu non hai fatto sì all' altre bolge;  

Thou hast not done so at the other chasms;

> 7 „ Οὐ μὴν ἔπραξας οὕτως ἐν ἄλλαις τάφροις.  
> („ Indeed you did not do so in the other ditches.)

- *Οὐ μὴν ἔπραξας οὕτως* ‘yet you did not act thus’: *μήν* reinforces the contrast after *οὐ*; this is not the emphatic negative construction *οὐ μή*. *ἔπραξας* (2nd sg. aor. act. ind. of *πράσσω*, Attic *πράττω*) presents *hai fatto* as a past act, and *οὕτως* renders *sì*. *ἐν ἄλλαις τάφροις* gives *all’ altre bolge* by *ἐν* with dat. pl. fem., ‘in other ditches’, without the Italian definite article.

> 8 pensa, se tu annoverar le credi,  
> 9 che miglia ventidue la valle volge,  

consider, if thou thinkest to number them, that the valley goes round two-and-twenty miles;

> 8 „ Εἰ θέλεις ἐπαριθμεῖν, ἴσθι τὴν τάφρον  
> („ If you wish to count them, know that the ditch)
>
> 9 „ Γῦρον ἔχειν σταδίων διακοσίων.  
> („ Has a circuit of two hundred stadia.)

- *Εἰ θέλεις ἐπαριθμεῖν* puts the condition first: *θέλεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *θέλω*) makes *se … le credi* a matter of wishing rather than thinking. *ἐπαριθμεῖν* is the contracted pres. act. inf. of *ἐπαριθμέω*, ‘to count up’; the object represented by *le* is left understood.
- *ἴσθι* ‘know’ (2nd sg. imperative of *οἶδα*, perfect with present sense) takes the place of *pensa*. Where Dante has *che* and a finite verb, Greek has an accusative and infinitive: *τὴν τάφρον* ‘the ditch’, for *la valle*, is the subject of *ἔχειν* (pres. act. inf. of *ἔχω*), and *γῦρον* ‘circuit’ its object.
- *σταδίων διακοσίων* ‘of two hundred stades’ is a genitive of measure specifying *γῦρον*, with the numeral agreeing in the gen. pl. neut. Musurus substitutes a Greek unit and a round figure for *miglia ventidue*, rather than retaining the Italian numeral.

> 10 e già la luna è sotto i nostri piedi;  

and the Moon already is beneath our feet;

> 10 „ Ὑπὸ πόδας νῦν ἡμῶν ἔσθ’ ἡ σελήνη·  
> („ Beneath our feet now is the moon:)

- *Ὑπὸ πόδας νῦν ἡμῶν ἔσθ’ ἡ σελήνη* fronts the position and postpones the subject *ἡ σελήνη*. *ὑπὸ πόδας* (acc. pl. of *πούς*) is ‘underfoot’, here with a verb of rest; *ἡμῶν* is the possessive genitive belonging to *πόδας*, separated from it by *νῦν*. The latter says ‘now’ rather than *già*, ‘already’; initial *e* has no Greek counterpart.
- *ἔσθ’* is *ἔστι* (3rd sg. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*) with final vowel elided and *τ* aspirated before the rough breathing of *ἡ*.

> 11 lo tempo è poco omai che n' è concesso,  

the time is now short, that is conceded to us;

> 11 „ Βραχὺς δὲ καιρὸς ἡμῖν εἰσέτι μένει,  
> („ And a short time still remains to us,)

- *Βραχὺς δὲ καιρὸς ἡμῖν εἰσέτι μένει* gives one active clause, ‘and a short time still remains to us’. *βραχύς* qualifies *καιρός* (nom. sg. masc.), the subject of *μένει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *μένω*); *καιρός* can denote the time available for something. Postpositive *δέ* connects the clause to the preceding one.
- *ἡμῖν* is the dative of the persons to whom time remains. Thus *che n’ è concesso* is absorbed into the main verb and its dative: no act of conceding is expressed. *εἰσέτι* ‘still, as yet’ recasts *omai* from the standpoint of what remains.

> 12 ed altro è da veder che tu non vedi.”  

and other *things* are to be seen than thou dost see.”

> 12 „ Ἄλλα τ’ ἔχομεν ἰδεῖν πλὴν ὅσων βλέπεις.”  
> („ And we have other things to see besides those which you see.”)

- *Ἄλλα τ’ ἔχομεν ἰδεῖν* ‘and we have other things to see’ supplies a first-person plural subject for the impersonal *altro è da veder*. *τ’* is the postpositive connective *τε* elided before *ἔχομεν* (1st pl. pres. act. ind. of *ἔχω*). Substantival *ἄλλα* is acc. pl. neut.; *ἰδεῖν* is the second aor. act. inf. used for *ὁράω*.
- *πλὴν ὅσων βλέπεις* ‘besides what you see’ replaces the negative relative *che tu non vedi* with a positive clause after an expression of exclusion. *ὅσων* (gen. pl. neut. of *ὅσος*) takes the case required by *πλήν*, by attraction to its understood antecedent: as object of *βλέπεις* alone one would expect *ὅσα*.

> 13 “Se tu avessi,” rispos' io appresso,  
> 14 “atteso alla cagion per ch' io guardava,  
> 15 forse m' avresti ancor lo star dimesso.”  

“Hadst thou,” I thereupon replied, “attended to the cause for which I looked, perhaps thou mightest have vouchsafed me yet to stay.”

> 13 — “ Εἴ γ’ ἐνόησας,” εὐθὺς ἀπεκρινάμην,  
> (—“If you had perceived,” straightway I answered,)
>
> 14 “ Τὸν λόγον, δι’ ὃν ἐνητένιζον οὕτως,  
> (“The reason for which I was gazing so intently,)
>
> 15 „ Ἴσως ἂν πλέον ἐπέτρεψάς μοι μένειν.”—  
> („ Perhaps you would have allowed me to remain longer.”—)

- *Εἴ γ’ ἐνόησας* ‘if indeed you had perceived’ begins a past contrary-to-fact condition: *γ’* is elided *γε*, focusing the supposition, and *ἐνόησας* is 2nd sg. aor. act. ind. of *νοέω*. Greek uses a past indicative, not the pluperfect subjunctive of *se tu avessi … atteso*. The interruption now falls between verb and object rather than between auxiliary and participle.
- *εὐθὺς ἀπεκρινάμην* ‘I immediately answered’ renders *rispos’ io appresso*: the adverb sharpens the temporal connection, and the pronoun is contained in *ἀπεκρινάμην* (1st sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, with internal augment after the prefix).
- *Τὸν λόγον* ‘the reason’ is the delayed accusative object of *ἐνόησας*. *δι’ ὃν* ‘on account of which’ is elided *διά* with the acc. sg. masc. relative agreeing in gender and number with *λόγον*. *ἐνητένιζον* (1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἐνατενίζω*, with the vowel augmented after *ἐν-*) retains the continuing action of *guardava*; the added *οὕτως* ‘thus’ qualifies the intent gaze.
- *Ἴσως ἂν πλέον ἐπέτρεψάς μοι μένειν* gives the corresponding unreal conclusion: *ἴσως* is *forse*, while *ἄν* with *ἐπέτρεψας* (2nd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐπιτρέπω*) answers to *m’ avresti … dimesso*. It is the past indicative with *ἄν*, not a potential optative. The enclitic *μοι* accounts for the added accent in *ἐπέτρεψάς*; the person permitted is dative and the act permitted is the pres. act. inf. *μένειν*. Adverbial *πλέον* ‘longer’ interprets *ancor* as additional duration.

> 16 Parte sen gia, ed io retro gli andava,  
> 17 lo duca, già facendo la risposta,  

Meantime the Guide was going on; and I went behind him, now making my reply,

> 16 Ἀνεχώρησεν ἤδη, κἀγὼ κατόπιν,  
> (Already he moved onward, and I behind him,)
>
> 17 Οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ, ὡς ὁμιλεῖν ἠρξάμην.  
> (My Guide, as I began to speak.)

- *Ἀνεχώρησεν ἤδη* ‘he had already moved off’ replaces the continuing movement of *Parte sen gia* with *ἀνεχώρησεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀναχωρέω*, augmented after the prefix). *ἤδη* marks the movement as already under way rather than directly translating the simultaneity of *Parte*.
- *κἀγὼ κατόπιν* ‘and I behind’ has crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ* and an adverb, *κατόπιν*, with neither a verb of motion nor an explicit equivalent of *gli*. As in Dante, the subject of the first movement is named only after this intervening phrase: *Οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ* ‘my guide’. *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός* (nom. sg. masc.); it supplies a possessive absent from *lo duca*. *ἡγήτωρ* is a poetic agent noun, ‘leader’.
- *ὡς ὁμιλεῖν ἠρξάμην* ‘as I began to speak’ converts *già facendo la risposta* from a gerund phrase into a temporal clause. *ἠρξάμην* (1st sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἄρχομαι*) takes the contracted pres. act. inf. *ὁμιλεῖν* from *ὁμιλέω*. The person speaking is explicit in the verb ending; the Greek specifies the onset of speech but not that it is a reply.

> 18 e soggiungendo: “Dentro a quella cava,  
> 19 dov' io teneva or gli occhi sì a posta,  
> 20 credo che un spirto del mio sangue pianga  
> 21 la colpa che laggiù cotanto costa.”  

and adding: “Within that cavern where I kept my eyes so fixed, I believe that a spirit of my own blood laments the guilt which costs so much down there.”

> 18 Ἀλλ’ εἶτ’ ἐπεῖπον· “Ἐντὸς τῆς τάφρου ’κείνης  
> (And then I added: “Within that ditch)
>
> 19 „ Ἔνθ’ εἶχον πεπηγμένας τὰς ἐμὰς ὄψεις,  
> („ Where I had fixed my eyes,)
>
> 20 „ Νομίζω, πνεῦμ’ ἔκλαιεν ἐξ αἵματός μου  
> („ I think a spirit of my own blood was weeping for)
>
> 21 „ Τὸ πταῖσμ’, ὃ σκληρῶς τιμωρεῖται ’κεῖ κάτω.”  
> („ The fault that is harshly punished down there.”)

- *Ἀλλ’ εἶτ’ ἐπεῖπον* ‘but then I added’ makes *e soggiungendo* a new finite clause. *ἀλλ’* and *εἶτ’* are elided *ἀλλά* and *εἶτα*; *ἐπεῖπον* is 1st sg. second aor. act. ind., the compound of *εἶπον* with *ἐπί* conveying speech added to what precedes.
- *Ἐντὸς τῆς τάφρου ’κείνης* ‘inside that ditch’ uses *ἐντός* with the genitive and repeats *τάφρος* for *cava*. *’κείνης* is *ἐκείνης* with prodelision of initial *ἐ-* after *τάφρου*; the demonstrative stands outside the articular group. Relative *ἔνθα* ‘where’ resumes the place just named, like *dov’*.
- *εἶχον πεπηγμένας τὰς ἐμὰς ὄψεις* ‘I held my eyes fixed’ retains the imperfect of *teneva*: *εἶχον* is here 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἔχω*. *πεπηγμένας* (perf. mid.-pass. participle of *πήγνυμι*, acc. pl. fem.) is predicative, agreeing with *ὄψεις* but outside its article. It expresses the resulting fixed position, interpreting *sì a posta* without a separate degree adverb; *ἐμάς* makes the possessor explicit.
- *Νομίζω, πνεῦμ’ ἔκλαιεν* is most naturally read ‘I think, a spirit was weeping’. *νομίζω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.) stands parenthetically beside a finite assertion, rather than introducing an accusative and infinitive or a clause with *ὅτι*. *πνεῦμ’* is elided *πνεῦμα*, nom. sg. neut., subject of *ἔκλαιεν* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *κλαίω*). Dante’s *credo che … pianga* thus loses the subordinating conjunction and changes from present subjunctive to imperfect indicative, locating the weeping at the time of the gaze.
- *ἐξ αἵματός μου* ‘from my blood’ renders *del mio sangue* as origin, *ἐξ* with the gen. sg. of *αἷμα*. The phrase belongs with *πνεῦμα* despite the intervening verb; enclitic possessive *μου* accounts for the additional accent on *αἵματός*.
- *Τὸ πταῖσμ’* is elided *τὸ πταῖσμα* ‘the fault’, acc. sg. neut., the direct object of *ἔκλαιεν*: transitive *κλαίω* ‘bewail’ follows *pianga la colpa*. In *ὃ σκληρῶς τιμωρεῖται*, the relative *ὅ* is nominative, subject of *τιμωρεῖται* (3rd sg. pres. mid.-pass. ind. of *τιμωρέω*, passive in sense). ‘Is harshly punished’ interprets the cost in *cotanto costa* as severity of punishment rather than reproducing the idiom. *’κεῖ κάτω* ‘there below’ renders *laggiù* with two adverbs, the first prodelided from *ἐκεῖ* after the preceding vowel.

## Lines 22–54 — Geri’s Threat and the Laments Below

> 22 Allor disse il maestro: “Non si franga  
> 23 lo tuo pensier da qui innanzi sopr’ ello;  

Then the Master said: "Let not thy thought henceforth distract itself on him;

> 22 Τότ’ εἶπέ μοι Προστάτης· “Μὴ δὴ τὴν φρένα  
> (Then my Protector said to me: “Let not your mind)
>
> 23 „ Ἐπανάφερε τοῦ λοιποῦ πρὸς ἐκεῖνον·  
> („ Turn back henceforth toward him;)

- *Τότ’ εἶπέ μοι Προστάτης* renders *Allor disse il maestro*: *τότ’* is *τότε* elided before a vowel; *εἶπε* is the 3rd sg. second aor. act. indic. of *λέγω*, with an additional accent before enclitic *μοι*, the dative of the person addressed, supplied in Greek. The anarthrous subject *Προστάτης* means ‘protector’ rather than ‘master’.
- *Μὴ δὴ τὴν φρένα Ἐπανάφερε* ‘do not, then, keep turning your mind back’ makes the addressee the subject of an active prohibition. Italian has *lo tuo pensier* as subject of *non si franga*; Greek has *τὴν φρένα* (acc. sg. of *φρήν*) as object of *ἐπανάφερε* (2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *ἐπαναφέρω*). The article suffices for the possessive; *δή* lends emphasis.
- *τοῦ λοιποῦ* is an adverbial genitive of time, ‘henceforth’, for *da qui innanzi*. With the verb of turning, *πρὸς ἐκεῖνον* (acc. sg.) gives a direction, ‘towards that man’, for *sopr’ ello*.

> 24 attendi ad altro, ed ei là si rimanga:  

attend to somewhat else, and let him stay there:

> 24 „ Πρὸς τἆλλα πρόσχες· οὗτος δ’ ἐκεῖ μενέτω.  
> („ Attend to the other things; and let him remain there.)

- *Πρὸς τἆλλα πρόσχες* puts the new object of attention first: *τἆλλα* is crasis for *τὰ ἄλλα*, neut. acc. pl., ‘the other things’, for singular *altro*. *πρόσχες* is the 2nd sg. second aor. act. imperative of *προσέχω*, here ‘turn your attention’, with *πρός* + accusative.
- *οὗτος δ’ ἐκεῖ μενέτω* closely matches *ed ei là si rimanga*: nominative *οὗτος* ‘this man’ sets him in contrast to *τἆλλα*, with postpositive *δέ* elided before *ἐκεῖ*. *μενέτω* is a 3rd sg. pres. act. imperative of *μένω*, where Italian uses a jussive subjunctive with *si*.

> 25 ch’ io vidi lui a piè del ponticello  
> 26 mostrarti, e minacciar forte col dito,  
> 27 ed udi ’l nominar Geri del Bello.  

for I saw him, at the foot of the little bridge, point to thee, and vehemently threaten with his finger; and heard *them* call him Geri del Bello.

> 25 „ Εἶδον γὰρ αὐτὸν εἰς πόδας τῆς γεφύρας  
> („ For I saw him at the foot of the bridge)
>
> 26 „ Δεικνύοντά σε κἀπειλοῦντα δακτύλῳ,  
> („ Pointing at you and threatening with his finger,)
>
> 27 „ Ἤκουσά τε λέγοντος· ‘“Γέρης ὁ Βέλλου.”’  
> („ And I heard one saying: ‘“Geri del Bello.”’)

- *Εἶδον γὰρ αὐτόν* gives explanatory *ché* by postpositive *γάρ*. After *εἶδον* (1st sg. second aor. act. indic. of *ὁράω*), Greek uses the accusative and supplementary participle of direct perception: *δεικνύοντά σε κἀπειλοῦντα* (pres. act. participles, masc. acc. sg., of *δεικνύω* and *ἀπειλέω*) agree with *αὐτόν*. Italian instead has *vidi lui … mostrarti, e minacciar*: Greek makes *σε* the direct object, ‘pointing you out’.
- *εἰς πόδας τῆς γεφύρας* ‘at the feet of the bridge’ keeps the image of *a piè del ponticello* but not the diminutive. Static *εἰς* + accusative here is less characteristically Attic than a locative expression. *κἀπειλοῦντα* has crasis of *καί* with *ἀπειλοῦντα*; instrumental *δακτύλῳ* renders *col dito*, but *forte* has no separate equivalent.
- *Ἤκουσά τε λέγοντος* ‘and I heard someone saying’ has *ἤκουσα* (1st sg. aor. act. indic. of *ἀκούω*) and a genitive of the speaker, expressed only by the masc. gen. sg. pres. act. participle of *λέγω*. Connective *τε* is enclitic. Where *udi ’l nominar* presents him as the one named, Greek quotes the name directly in the nominative: *Γέρης ὁ Βέλλου*, ‘Geri, the son of Bello’, with the article and a patronymic genitive.

> 28 Tu eri allor sì del tutto impedito  
> 29 sopra colui che già tenne Altaforte,  
> 30 che non guardasti in là, sì fu partito.”  

Thou wast then so totally entangled upon him who once held Altaforte, that thou didst not look that way; so he departed."

> 28 „ Ἀλλὰ σὺ τότ’ ἦς οὕτως ἠσχολημένος  
> („ But you were then so occupied)
>
> 29 „ Περὶ τὸν ποτὲ κύριον Ἀλταφόρτης,  
> („ About him who was once lord of Altaforte,)
>
> 30 „ Ὥστε, σοῦ μὴ προσχόντος, αὐτὸς ἀπῆλθεν.”  
> („ That, while you paid no heed, he departed.”)

- *Ἀλλὰ σὺ τότ’ ἦς οὕτως ἠσχολημένος* adds adversative *ἀλλά* before the emphatic pronoun. *ἦς* (2nd sg. imperf. indic. of *εἰμί*) with *ἠσχολημένος* (perf. mid.-pass. participle, masc. nom. sg., of *ἀσχολέω*, with initial vowel lengthened) describes a past state, ‘you were occupied’. *οὕτως* answers to *sì* and anticipates *ὥστε*; *del tutto* is not separately expressed.
- *Περὶ τὸν ποτὲ κύριον Ἀλταφόρτης* ‘with the former lord of Altaforte’ replaces *sopra colui che già tenne Altaforte* by *περί* + accusative. The relative clause becomes a noun phrase: adverb *ποτέ* qualifies *κύριον* within the article group, and the place name becomes a dependent genitive.
- *Ὥστε, σοῦ μὴ προσχόντος, αὐτὸς ἀπῆλθεν* makes the departure itself the actual result (*ὥστε* with indicative). *che non guardasti in là* is subordinated as a genitive absolute: *σοῦ* with *μὴ προσχόντος* (masc. gen. sg. second aor. act. participle of *προσέχω*), ‘you not having paid heed’; *in là* disappears. Nominative *αὐτός* marks the change of subject before *ἀπῆλθεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act. indic. of *ἀπέρχομαι*).

> 31 “O duca mio, la violenta morte  
> 32 che non gli è vendicata ancor,” diss’ io,  
> 33 “per alcun che dell’ onta sia consorte,  

"O my Guide! his violent death, which is not yet avenged for him," said I, "by any that is a partner of his shame,

> 31 — “Ἡγήτωρ ἐμός! ὁ βίαιος,” τότ’ εἶπον,  
> (—“My Guide! that violent,” then I said,)
>
> 32 “Θάνατος, οὗπερ δίκην οὐκ ἔσχεν ἔτι  
> (“Death, for which he has not yet had vengeance)
>
> 33 „ Πρός τινος τῶν αἰσχύνης συγκοινωνούντων,  
> („ From any of those who share in the shame,)

- *Ἡγήτωρ ἐμός* renders *O duca mio* with a poetic agent noun and an agreeing possessive. The nominative-shaped address (notably *ἐμός* rather than vocative *ἐμέ*) has no *ὦ*. *ὁ βίαιος … Θάνατος* is the nominative subject whose verb comes in line 34; *τότ’ εἶπον* ‘then I said’ (1st sg. second aor. act. indic.) interrupts adjective and noun, earlier than *diss’ io* interrupts the Italian.
- *οὗπερ δίκην οὐκ ἔσχεν ἔτι* ‘for which he has not yet had redress’ replaces the passive *che non gli è vendicata ancor* by an active clause with the injured person as understood subject. *οὗπερ* is the masc. gen. sg. relative referring to *Θάνατος*, strengthened by *-περ*; the genitive specifies what the *δίκη* is for. *δίκην* is the object of *ἔσχεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act. indic. of *ἔχω*); the Greek uses an aorist for the Italian’s still-unfulfilled present state.
- *Πρός τινος* ‘from someone’ uses *πρός* + genitive of the person from whom redress might come. After indefinite *τινος*, *τῶν … συγκοινωνούντων* is partitive, ‘of those sharing’ (articular pres. act. participle of *συγκοινωνέω*, masc. gen. pl.); the verb takes genitive *αἰσχύνης* of what is shared. Thus *che … sia consorte* becomes a participle, without a corresponding subjunctive.

> 34 fece lui disdegnoso: ond’ ei sen gío  
> 35 senza parlarmi, sì com’ io estimo;  

made him indignant: therefore, as I suppose, he went away without speaking to me;

> 34 „ Τάχ’ αὐτὸν παρώργισε· διὸ κἀπῆλθε  
> („ Perhaps angered him; wherefore he also departed)
>
> 35 „ Μὴ προσφθεγξάμενός μοι, καθὰ νομίζω·  
> („ Without addressing a word to me, as I suppose;)

- *Τάχ’ αὐτὸν παρώργισε* ‘perhaps it angered him’ replaces causative *fece lui disdegnoso* by *παρώργισε* (3rd sg. aor. act. indic. of *παροργίζω*, augment after the preverb). Elided *τάχα* qualifies the explanation already here; Italian withholds its explicit reservation until *sì com’ io estimo*.
- *διὸ κἀπῆλθε* ‘wherefore he also went away’ gives consequential *διό* for *ond’* and contracts *καὶ ἀπῆλθε* by crasis. *Μὴ προσφθεγξάμενός μοι* renders *senza parlarmi* by a negated circumstantial participle agreeing with the departing subject: *προσφθεγξάμενος* is aor. mid., masc. nom. sg., of deponent *προσφθέγγομαι*, which takes the dative of the person addressed. *καθὰ νομίζω* ‘as I suppose’ keeps the Italian parenthesis, with 1st sg. pres. act. indicative but no expressed *io*.

> 36 ed in ciò m’ ha e’ fatto a sè più pio.”  

and in that has made me pity him the more."

> 36 „ Ἀνθ’ ὧνπερ αὐτὸν ἔτι μᾶλλον οἰκτείρω.”  
> („ On which account I pity him even more.”)

- *Ἀνθ’ ὧνπερ* ‘on account of which things’ turns *ed in ciò* into a connective relative phrase. *ἀντί* governs the neut. gen. pl. relative *ὧν* with strengthening *-περ*; elision before the rough breathing changes *τ* to *θ*.
- *αὐτὸν ἔτι μᾶλλον οἰκτείρω* ‘I pity him still more’ reverses the syntax of *m’ ha e’ fatto a sè più pio*: the person made compassionate becomes the subject of *οἰκτείρω* (1st sg. pres. act. indic.), and the Italian subject becomes its accusative object. *ἔτι μᾶλλον* modifies the verb rather than an adjective.

> 37 Così parlammo infino al loco primo  
> 38 che dello scoglio l’ altra valle mostra,  
> 39 se più lume vi fosse, tutto ad imo.  

Thus we spake, up to the first place of the cliff, which shows the other valley, if more light were there, quite to the bottom.

> 37 Ταῦτ’ ἐλέγομεν μέχρι τοῦ πρώτου τόπου  
> (Thus we spoke as far as the first place)
>
> 38 Σπιλάδος, ὅθεν εἴδομεν ἂν τῆς ἄλλης  
> (Of the crag, whence we would have seen the next)
>
> 39 Κοιλάδος βάθος, εἰ πλεῖον φῶς ὑπῆρχεν.  
> (Valley's bottom, if more light had been there.)

- *Ταῦτ’ ἐλέγομεν* ‘these things we were saying’ replaces manner *Così* with an object, elided neut. acc. pl. *ταῦτα*, and perfective *parlammo* with the imperfect active indicative, 1st pl. of *λέγω*. *μέχρι* governs *τοῦ πρώτου τόπου*, with *Σπιλάδος* (gen. sg. of *σπιλάς*) dependent on *τόπου*, for *infino al loco primo … dello scoglio*.
- *ὅθεν εἴδομεν ἂν … βάθος* ‘whence we would have seen the depth’ makes the place a point of observation rather than the subject of *mostra*. *βάθος* is neut. acc. sg., with dependent *τῆς ἄλλης Κοιλάδος*; thus *l’altra valle … tutto ad imo* becomes ‘the depth of the other valley’, without a separate *tutto*.
- The printed *εἴδομεν* is the 1st pl. second aor. act. indicative corresponding to *εἶδον* (usual Attic plural *εἴδομεν*, with recessive accent). With *ἄν* it forms the unreal conclusion; *εἰ πλεῖον φῶς ὑπῆρχεν* supplies the condition, with *ὑπῆρχεν* (3rd sg. imperf. act. indic. of *ὑπάρχω*) rather than a subjunctive matching *fosse*. Comparative *πλεῖον* agrees with neut. nom. sg. *φῶς*.

> 40 Quando noi fummo in su l’ ultima chiostra  
> 41 di Malebolge, sì che i suoi conversi  
> 42 potean parere alla veduta nostra,  

When we were above the last cloister of Malebolge, so that its lay-brethren could appear to our view,

> 40 Ὅτε δ’ ἀφίγμεθ’ ἐπὶ τῆς Κακοβόθρου  
> (And when we had arrived above Evil-pit's)
>
> 41 Ἐσχάτης μονῆς, ἔνθεν οἱ καθειργμένοι  
> (Last enclosure, whence those confined therein)
>
> 42 Θεατοὶ πρὸς τὰς ἡμῶν ὁράσεις ἦσαν,  
> (Were visible to our sight,)

- *Ὅτε δ’ ἀφίγμεθ’* renders *Quando noi fummo* as ‘and when we had arrived’. *ἀφίγμεθ’* represents *ἀφίγμεθα*, a shortened poetic 1st pl. perfect middle of deponent *ἀφικνέομαι* (beside *ἀφίγμεσθα*), with elision; the printed aspirated ending is unexpected before the smooth breathing of *ἐπί*. *δέ* too is elided.
- *ἐπὶ τῆς Κακοβόθρου Ἐσχάτης μονῆς* uses local *ἐπί* + genitive for *in su*. *Ἐσχάτης μονῆς* renders *ultima chiostra* by ‘last abode/enclosure’; the genitive *Κακοβόθρου*, a Greek compound for *Malebolge*, precedes it rather than following as *di Malebolge* does.
- *ἔνθεν οἱ καθειργμένοι Θεατοὶ … ἦσαν* ‘whence the confined were visible’ substitutes a local relative for consecutive *sì che*. The substantivized perf. mid.-pass. participle *καθειργμένοι* (masc. nom. pl. of *καθείργω*) stresses confinement rather than the monastic sense of *conversi*. Predicate adjective *θεατοί* and *ἦσαν* (3rd pl. imperf. of *εἰμί*) replace *potean parere*: possibility is contained in ‘visible’. *πρὸς τὰς ἡμῶν ὁράσεις* follows *alla veduta nostra*, but pluralizes ‘sight’ and sets possessive genitive *ἡμῶν* between article and noun.

> 43 lamenti saettaron me diversi,  
> 44 che di pietà ferrati avean gli strali:  
> 45 ond’ io gli orecchi con le man copersi.  

lamentations pierced me, manifold, which had their arrows barbed with pity: whereat I covered my ears with my hands.

> 43 Οἰμωγαὶ παντομιγεῖς ἠκόντισάν μοι  
> (All-mingled wailings shot at me)
>
> 44 Ἐν τῇ καρδίᾳ σιδηροφόρα βέλη,  
> (Iron-tipped arrows into my heart,)
>
> 45 Ὥστ’ ἐκάλυψα χερσὶ τὰς ἀκοάς μου.  
> (So that I covered my ears with my hands.)

- *Οἰμωγαὶ παντομιγεῖς* ‘wailings all mingled’ puts the adjective beside its noun, unlike separated *lamenti … diversi*. *παντομιγεῖς* is fem. nom. pl. of the compound *παντομιγής*: variety becomes intermixture. *ἠκόντισάν μοι* has the augmented 3rd pl. aor. act. indic. of *ἀκοντίζω*, accented again before enclitic *μοι*. Where *saettaron me* makes the person the direct object, Greek has dative *μοι* and a direct object in the next line.
- *Ἐν τῇ καρδίᾳ σιδηροφόρα βέλη* ‘iron-bearing missiles in the heart’ supplies the heart as the place struck. *σιδηροφόρα* agrees with *βέλη* (neut. acc. pl. of *βέλος*); *che … avean gli strali* disappears as a relative clause, its missiles becoming the object of ‘shot’. The iron of *ferrati* remains, but *di pietà* has no lexical counterpart.
- *Ὥστ’ ἐκάλυψα* uses elided *ὥστε* with an indicative of actual result for *ond’ io*: *ἐκάλυψα* is 1st sg. aor. act. indic. of *καλύπτω*. *χερσί* is the instrumental dat. pl. of *χείρ* for *con le man*; *τὰς ἀκοάς μου* is literally ‘my organs of hearing’, acc. pl. with an explicit possessive absent from *gli orecchi*.

> 46 Qual dolor fora, se degli spedali  
> 47 di Valdichiana tra il luglio e il settembre,  
> 48 e di Maremma e di Sardigna i mali  
> 49 fossero in una fossa tutti insembre:  

Such [pain] as there would be, if the diseases in the hospitals of Valdichiana, between July and September, and of Maremma and Sardinia, were all together in one ditch:

> 46 Ἄλγος ἦν, οἷον εἰ τῶν νοσοκομείων  
> (Such pain there was, as if from the hospitals)
>
> 47 (Ἰουλίου μεταξὺ καὶ Σεπτεμβρίου)  
> ((Between July and September))
>
> 48 Κλάνεως, Σαρδοῦς, Μαρέμμας πάθη πάντα  
> (Of Clanis, Sardinia, and Maremma all the maladies)
>
> 49 Ἐν μιᾷ τάφρῳ παρῆν συνηθροισμένα.  
> (Were present gathered together in a single ditch.)

- *Ἄλγος ἦν, οἷον εἰ …* ‘there was pain such as if …’ starts with the actual pain: it brings forward the assertion of *tal era quivi* in line 50 instead of opening with hypothetical *Qual dolor fora*. Neut. sg. *οἷον* refers back to *ἄλγος* and introduces the comparison; the corresponding hypothetical verb ‘there would be’ is understood.
- *τῶν νοσοκομείων … πάθη πάντα* separates dependent genitive ‘of the hospitals’ from neut. pl. ‘all the afflictions’. The intervening parenthesis has *μεταξύ* between the two genitives it governs, *Ἰουλίου … καὶ Σεπτεμβρίου*. *Κλάνεως, Σαρδοῦς, Μαρέμμας* are geographical genitives attached to the hospitals: the first substitutes the river name for *Valdichiana*, and the last two reverse Dante’s order, without repeated *e*.
- *Ἐν μιᾷ τάφρῳ* is locative, with the feminine dative of *εἷς* agreeing with *τάφρῳ*. *παρῆν* (3rd sg. imperf. indic. of *πάρειμι*, ‘be present’) is singular with the neuter plural subject *πάθη πάντα*, a regular Attic construction. *συνηθροισμένα* (perf. mid.-pass. participle of *συναθροίζω*, neut. nom. pl.) agrees with that subject: *tutti insembre* becomes ‘all … gathered together’. *εἰ* with past indicative renders the hypothetical *se … fossero* without an optative.

> 50 tal era quivi, e tal puzzo n’ usciva,  
> 51 qual suole uscir dalle marcite membre.  

such was there here; and such stench issued thence, as is wont to issue from putrid limbs.

> 50 Τοιάδ’ ἐκεῖθεν ἀπέπνει δυσωδία,  
> (Such a foul stench breathed forth from thence,)
>
> 51 Οἵαν ἐκπέμπει τὰ σεσηπότα μέλη.  
> (As decaying limbs send forth.)

- Having already expressed *tal era quivi* by *Ἄλγος ἦν*, Musurus proceeds directly to *Τοιάδ’ ἐκεῖθεν ἀπέπνει δυσωδία*. *Τοιάδ’* is elided feminine nom. sg. *τοιάδε*, agreeing across the verb with *δυσωδία*; *ἐκεῖθεν* expresses the source implicit in *n’ usciva*. *ἀπέπνει* (3rd sg. imperf. act. indic. of *ἀποπνέω*) replaces neutral *usciva* by ‘breathed forth’.
- Correlative *Οἵαν* (fem. acc. sg. of *οἷος*) stands for the kind of stench emitted, as object of *ἐκπέμπει* (3rd sg. pres. act. indic. of *ἐκπέμπω*). *τὰ σεσηπότα μέλη*, ‘the limbs that have rotted’, is its neuter plural subject with singular verb. *σεσηπότα* is the reduplicated second perfect active participle of *σήπω*, neut. nom. pl., intransitive and resultative. Italian *dalle marcite membre* gives the source instead of the subject; the generic present does the work of *suole* without a separate verb.

> 52 Noi discendemmo in su l’ ultima riva  
> 53 del lungo scoglio, pur da man sinistra,  

We descended on the last bank of the long cliff, again to the left hand;

> 52 Κατέβημεν δὲ μέχρις ἐσχάτης ὄχθης  
> (And we descended as far as the last bank)
>
> 53 Τοῦ μακροῦ κρημνοῦ πρὸς ἀριστερὰν χεῖρα,  
> (Of the long crag toward the left hand,)

- *Κατέβημεν δὲ μέχρις ἐσχάτης ὄχθης* introduces connective *δέ* and uses *κατέβημεν* (1st pl. root aor. act. indic. of *καταβαίνω*, with internal augment). *μέχρις* + genitive makes *in su l’ultima riva* a limit, ‘as far as the last bank’, rather than a surface reached.
- *Τοῦ μακροῦ κρημνοῦ* depends on *ὄχθης* and keeps the genitive relation of *del lungo scoglio*, now using *κρημνός* ‘precipice’ rather than the earlier *σπιλάς*. *πρὸς ἀριστερὰν χεῖρα* ‘towards the left hand’ follows *da man sinistra* with directional *πρός* + accusative; there is no separate rendering of *pur*.

> 54 ed allor fu la mia vista più viva  

and then my sight was more vivid,

> 54 Καὶ τότ’ ὄψεις ἔτεινα ζωηροτέρας  
> (And then I strained my eyes more keenly)

- *Καὶ τότ’* closely matches *ed allor*, but *ὄψεις ἔτεινα ζωηροτέρας* changes both subject and predicate: ‘I strained my eyes more keenly’ for *fu la mia vista più viva*. *ἔτεινα* is 1st sg. aor. act. indic. of *τείνω*; its personal ending replaces the Italian possessive as the indication of whose sight is meant.
- *ὄψεις* (acc. pl. of *ὄψις*) replaces singular *vista*. *ζωηροτέρας* is the agreeing fem. acc. pl. comparative of *ζωηρός*, separated from its noun by the verb. Thus the sense may be glossed adverbially, but the Greek retains an adjective, transferring *più viva* from the predicate of ‘was’ to the accusative phrase.

## Lines 55–87 — The Falsifiers’ Sickness and the Fury of the Itch

> 55 giù ver lo fondo, dove la ministra  
> 56 dell' alto Sire, infallibil giustizia,  
> 57 punisce i falsator che qui registra.  

down towards the depth in which the ministress of the Great Sire, infallible Justice, punishes the falsifiers that she here registers.

> 55 Ἐπὶ τὸν βυθόν, ἔνθαπερ ἡ Κυρίου  
> (Toward the depth, where the Lord's)
>
> 56 Διάκονος, ἀσφαλὴς Δικαιοσύνη,  
> (Handmaid, unerring Justice,)
>
> 57 Τιμωρεῖ γνωστοὺς κιβδηλείας ἐργάτας.  
> (Punishes known workers of falsification.)

- *Ἐπὶ τὸν βυθόν* ‘towards the depth’ uses *ἐπί* with the accusative of the goal for *ver lo fondo*; there is no separate adverb for *giù*. *ἔνθαπερ* ‘just where’ renders *dove* by a local relative adverb strengthened by *-περ*.
- In *ἡ Κυρίου Διάκονος* ‘the Lord’s servant’, the possessive genitive comes between article and noun, reversing the order of *la ministra dell’ alto Sire*. *διάκονος* can be masculine or feminine; here *ἡ* marks it as feminine. *Κυρίου* (gen. sg. of *Κύριος*) renders the title without a separate adjective for *alto*. *ἀσφαλὴς Δικαιοσύνη* ‘unfailing Justice’ is in nominative apposition, as *infallibil giustizia* is to *la ministra*.
- *Τιμωρεῖ* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *τιμωρέω*) takes the persons punished as its direct object; for this sense classical Attic commonly uses the middle *τιμωροῦμαι*. *κιβδηλείας ἐργάτας* ‘workers of falsification’ expands *falsator* into a genitive of the activity and an agent noun (acc. pl. of *ἐργάτης*). *γνωστούς* ‘known’, agreeing with *ἐργάτας*, gives the result implied by *che qui registra* rather than translating the act of registering or the local *qui*.

> 58 Non credo che a veder maggior tristizia  
> 59 fosse in Egina il popol tutto infermo,  
> 60 quando fu l' aer sì pien di malizia,  

I do not think it was a greater sorrow to see the people in Ægina all infirm; when the air was so malignant,

> 58 Οὐκ ἂν ἴδοι τις θέαν λυπηροτέραν,  
> (One could not see a more grievous sight,)
>
> 59 Ὅτ’ ἐν Αἰγίνῃ λαὸς ἅπας ἐνόσει  
> (When in Aegina the whole people sickened)
>
> 60 Ἐξ οὕτω κακῆς μολύνσεως ἀέρος,  
> (From so evil a corruption of the air,)

- *Οὐκ ἂν ἴδοι τις* ‘one could not see’ replaces both the personal *Non credo* and the dependent subjunctive *fosse* with an independent potential optative: *ἴδοι* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. opt. of *ὁράω*, with modal *ἄν* and indefinite *τις* as subject. *θέαν λυπηροτέραν* ‘a more grievous sight’ is its accusative object: the sorrow in *maggior tristizia* becomes a quality of the thing seen.
- *Ὅτ’* is *ὅτε* elided before *ἐν*. The temporal conjunction introduces the illness of the people themselves, whereas Italian first makes *il popol tutto infermo* the object of *veder* and then uses *quando* to introduce the condition of the air. *ἐν Αἰγίνῃ* has the locative dative; *λαὸς ἅπας* ‘the entire people’ is a collective singular subject with the singular *ἐνόσει* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *νοσέω*), a verb of continuing illness for adjectival *infermo*.
- *Ἐξ οὕτω κακῆς μολύνσεως ἀέρος* ‘from so bad a contamination of the air’ makes *l’ aer sì pien di malizia* a cause governed by *ἐξ*, not a finite clause with the air as subject. *μολύνσεως* is gen. sg. of *μόλυνσις*; *ἀέρος*, gen. sg. of *ἀήρ*, depends on it. *οὕτω* qualifies *κακῆς* and prepares the following result clause.

> 61 che gli animali infino al picciol vermo  
> 62 cascaron tutti, e poi le genti antiche,  
> 63 secondo che i poeti hanno per fermo,  
> 64 si ristorar di seme di formiche:  

that every animal, even to the little worm, dropt down; and afterwards, as Poets hold for sure, the ancient peoples were restored from seed of ants:

> 61 Ὥστε τὰ ζῶα μέχρι μικρῶν σκωλήκων  
> (That the living creatures, down to the little worms,)
>
> 62 Ἅπαντ’ ἔπεσε, καὶ δὴ τἀρχαῖον γένος,  
> (All perished, and indeed the ancient race,)
>
> 63 Καθὼς παλαιοὶ ποιηταὶ βεβαιοῦσιν,  
> (As the ancient poets affirm,)
>
> 64 Ἐκ τῆς σπορᾶς μυρμήκων ἀντικατέστη,  
> (Was restored from the seed of ants,)

- *Ὥστε … ἔπεσε* expresses actual result with the indicative, corresponding to *sì … che … cascaron*. *μέχρι μικρῶν σκωλήκων* ‘as far as small worms’ takes the genitive plural (of *σκώληξ*) in place of generic singular *infino al picciol vermo*. *Ἅπαντ’* is elided *ἅπαντα*, neut. nom. pl., picking up *τὰ ζῶα* after the intervening phrase as *tutti* picks up *gli animali*. The neuter plural subject takes a singular verb in classical fashion: *ἔπεσε*, 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *πίπτω*.
- *καὶ δή* ‘and indeed’ marks the next assertion without the explicit temporal adverb *poi*. *τἀρχαῖον* is crasis of *τὸ ἀρχαῖον*; the subject *τἀρχαῖον γένος* ‘the ancient race’ is collective singular for *le genti antiche*.
- *Καθὼς … βεβαιοῦσιν* ‘as … affirm’ retains the parenthesis but substitutes an active verb of assurance (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *βεβαιόω*) for *hanno per fermo*. *παλαιοί* ‘of old’ is supplied with *ποιηταί*, distinct from *ἀρχαῖον* qualifying the race.
- *Ἐκ τῆς σπορᾶς μυρμήκων* ‘from the seed of ants’ gives the source with *ἐκ* and the genitive; *μυρμήκων* is gen. pl. of *μύρμηξ*. *ἀντικατέστη* is the intransitive second aor. act. ind., 3rd sg., of *ἀντικαθίστημι*, with the augment inside the compound. Its sense is ‘came to stand in place again’; the active form, not a morphological passive or reflexive, does duty for *si ristorar*.

> 65 ch' era a veder per quella oscura valle  
> 66 languir gli spirti per diverse biche.  

than it was to see, through that dim valley, the spirits languishing in diverse heaps.

> 65 Ἤπερ ἦν ἰδεῖν ἐν τῷ ζοφώδει βόθρῳ  
> (Than it was to see in that gloomy pit)
>
> 66 Συντηκόμενα τὰ πνεύματ’ ἐν σωρείαις.  
> (The spirits wasting away in heaps.)

- *Ἤπερ* ‘than indeed’ resumes the comparison with *λυπηροτέραν* after the long interruption, making the comparative function of *ch’* explicit. *ἦν ἰδεῖν* ‘it was possible to see’ closely follows *era a veder*: *ἦν* is the imperfect of *εἰμί*, used impersonally with *ἰδεῖν*, second aor. act. inf. of *ὁράω*.
- *ἐν τῷ ζοφώδει βόθρῳ* ‘in the gloomy pit’ substitutes position for *per* and an articular noun for demonstrative *quella … valle*; *ζοφώδει* is dat. sg. of *ζοφώδης*. *Συντηκόμενα τὰ πνεύματ’* ‘the spirits wasting away’ replaces *languir gli spirti* with the participial construction after a verb of perception. *συντηκόμενα* (pres. mid./pass. participle of *συντήκω*, neut. acc. pl.) agrees with the object *τὰ πνεύματα*, elided before *ἐν*. *ἐν σωρείαις* ‘in heaps’ gives the plural disposition without separately rendering *diverse*.

> 67 Qual sopra il ventre, e qual sopra le spalle  
> 68 l' un dell' altro giacea, e qual carpone  
> 69 si trasmutava per lo tristo calle.  

This upon the belly, and that upon the shoulders of the other lay; and some were crawling on along the dismal path.

> 67 Ὁ μὲν ἐπὶ κοιλίας, ὁ δ’ ἐπὶ νώτων  
> (One upon the belly, another upon the back)
>
> 68 Ἔκειντ’ ἀλλήλων, ἄλλος δέ τις προσέρπων  
> (Of one another lay, while another creeping along)
>
> 69 Ἔσυρ’ ἑαυτὸν ἐπὶ τῆς οἰκτρᾶς τρίβου.  
> (Dragged himself upon the piteous path.)

- *Ὁ μὲν … ὁ δ’* (elided *δέ*) uses the article pronominally, with the distributive pair *μέν … δέ*, for *Qual … e qual*. *ἐπὶ κοιλίας … ἐπὶ νώτων* uses *ἐπί* with the genitive of the supporting surface; *νώτων* ‘back’ (gen. pl. of *νῶτον*) shifts *spalle* away from ‘shoulders’.
- *Ἔκειντ’ ἀλλήλων* ‘they lay upon one another’ gathers the distributed singulars into a plural: *ἔκειντ’* is elided *ἔκειντο*, 3rd pl. imperf. of *κεῖμαι*, middle in form. Reciprocal gen. pl. *ἀλλήλων* goes with the body parts, compressing *l’ un dell’ altro*; the Italian verb *giacea* is singular.
- *ἄλλος δέ τις* ‘and another one’ continues the distribution with an indefinite singular. *προσέρπων* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *προσέρπω*) gives *carpone* a verbal form, ‘creeping along’, while *Ἔσυρ’ ἑαυτόν* ‘dragged himself’ retains the reflexive construction of *si trasmutava* but specifies the movement. *ἔσυρ’* is elided *ἔσυρε*, 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *σύρω*. *ἐπὶ τῆς οἰκτρᾶς τρίβου* ‘upon the pitiable path’ again uses *ἐπί* with the genitive rather than a preposition of passage through.

> 70 Passo passo andavam senza sermone,  
> 71 guardando ed ascoltando gli ammalati,  
> 72 che non potean levar le lor persone.  

Step by step we went, without speech, looking at and listening to the sick who could not raise their bodies.

> 70 Ἐπορευόμεθ’ οὐδὲν λέγοντες βάδην,  
> (We walked on step by step, saying nothing,)
>
> 71 Βλέποντες κἀκούοντες τοὺς ἀσθενοῦντας,  
> (Watching and listening to the sick ones,)
>
> 72 Τὸ σῶμ’ ἑαυτῶν οὐ δυναμένους ἆραι.  
> (Who were unable to raise their own bodies.)

- *Ἐπορευόμεθ’* is elided *ἐπορευόμεθα*, 1st pl. imperf. mid. ind. of deponent *πορεύομαι*. The imperfect follows *andavam*; the manner adverb *βάδην* ‘at a walk, at a footpace’ comes last instead of repeating a noun as in *Passo passo*. *οὐδὲν λέγοντες* ‘saying nothing’ converts *senza sermone* into a circumstantial present active participle with neuter accusative *οὐδέν* as object.
- *Βλέποντες κἀκούοντες* are further pres. act. participles, nom. masc. pl., agreeing with the unexpressed ‘we’, as the Italian gerunds do in sense. *κἀκούοντες* is crasis of *καὶ ἀκούοντες*. Both verbs share the accusative *τοὺς ἀσθενοῦντας* ‘those who are ill’ (articular pres. act. participle of *ἀσθενέω*); Greek thus follows the coordination in *guardando ed ascoltando gli ammalati*, though *ἀκούω* commonly takes the genitive of the person heard.
- *οὐ δυναμένους ἆραι* ‘not being able to raise’ continues with an acc. masc. pl. pres. mid. participle of *δύναμαι* agreeing with the sick, rather than a relative clause like *che non potean*. *ἆραι* is the complementary aor. act. inf. of *αἴρω*. Its fronted object *τὸ σῶμ’ ἑαυτῶν* (elided *σῶμα*) has a distributive singular ‘body’ and a reflexive genitive plural ‘of themselves’ for *le lor persone*.

> 73 Io vidi due sedere a sè poggiati,  
> 74 come a scaldar si poggia tegghia a tegghia,  
> 75 dal capo al piè di schianze maculati;  

I saw two sit leaning on each other, as pan is leant on pan to warm, from head to foot spotted with scabs;

> 73 Εἶδον συγκαθημένους σύνεγγυς δύο,  
> (I saw two sitting close together,)
>
> 74 Ὡς πρὸς θέρμανσιν κλίβανος πρὸς κλιβάνῳ,  
> (As for heating, oven against oven,)
>
> 75 Πλήρεις λεπίδων ἐκ κεφαλῆς εἰς πόδας.  
> (Full of scabs from head to foot.)

- *Εἶδον* ‘I saw’ is the 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*. *συγκαθημένους σύνεγγυς δύο* ‘two sitting together close by’ again uses a supplementary participle of perception: *συγκαθημένους*, pres. mid. participle of *συγκάθημαι*, is acc. masc. plural, not dual, with *δύο*. The compound verb and *σύνεγγυς* emphasize proximity without explicitly rendering the mutual leaning in *a sè poggiati*.
- *Ὡς πρὸς θέρμανσιν κλίβανος πρὸς κλιβάνῳ* ‘as, for heating, an oven beside an oven’ follows the repetition *tegghia a tegghia* but changes the utensil and leaves *si poggia* without a finite equivalent. The first *πρός* takes an accusative of purpose, the second a dative of proximity; nominative *κλίβανος* is the subject of the understood comparison.
- *Πλήρεις λεπίδων* ‘full of scales’ is predicative of the two: *πλήρεις* is acc. masc. pl. of *πλήρης* with a genitive of contents. It replaces participial *di schianze maculati*, dropping the idea of spotting. *ἐκ κεφαλῆς εἰς πόδας* marks the endpoints by *ἐκ* + genitive and *εἰς* + accusative; the Greek has plural ‘feet’ for singular *piè*.

> 76 e non vidi giammai menare stregghia  
> 77 da ragazzo aspettato dal signorso,  
> 78 nè da colui che mal volentier vegghia,  

and never did I see currycomb plied by stable-boy for whom his master waits, nor by one who stays unwillingly awake,

> 76 Οὔποτ’ εἶδον κινοῦντα τὴν ξυστρίδ᾽ οὕτως  
> (Never saw I plying the curry-comb so fast)
>
> 77 Οἰκέτην, ὅνπερ ὁ δεσπότης προσμένει,  
> (A servant for whom his master is waiting,)
>
> 78 Οὔτε τιν᾽ ἄλλον ἀγρυπνοῦντ᾽ ἀκουσίως,  
> (Nor anyone else keeping awake against his will,)

- *Οὔποτ’ εἶδον* (elided *οὔποτε*) combines *non … giammai* in one negative adverb and omits the initial connective. Greek makes the person, not the implement, the object seen: *κινοῦντα τὴν ξυστρίδ᾽* ‘moving the scraper’, with acc. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *κινέω* agreeing with the delayed *Οἰκέτην*, and elided *ξυστρίδα* (acc. sg. of *ξυστρίς*) as its object. *οὕτως* ‘so’ anticipates *ὡς* in line 79.
- *Οἰκέτην* ‘household servant’ is less specific than a boy doing stable work. *ὅνπερ ὁ δεσπότης προσμένει* ‘whom the master awaits’ turns passive *aspettato dal signorso* into an active relative clause: *ὅνπερ* is the acc. masc. sg. relative strengthened by *-περ*, *ὁ δεσπότης* the subject, and *προσμένει* 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. The relationship is inferred rather than marked by a possessive.
- *Οὔτε τιν᾽ ἄλλον* ‘nor any other’ (elided *τινά*) supplies a second accusative parallel to *οἰκέτην*, in place of *nè da colui*. *ἀγρυπνοῦντ᾽* is elided *ἀγρυπνοῦντα*, acc. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *ἀγρυπνέω*, for the relative *che … vegghia*; *ἀκουσίως* ‘involuntarily’ compresses *mal volentier*.

> 79 come ciascun menava spesso il morso  
> 80 dell' unghie sopra sè per la gran rabbia  
> 81 del pizzicor, che non ha più soccorso.  

as each of these plied thick the clawing of his nails upon himself, for the great fury of their itch which has no other succour.

> 79 Ὡς αὐτῶν ἑκάτερος ἔξυεν ἄκροις  
> (As each of them was scratching with the tips)
>
> 80 Τῶν ὀνύχων ἑαυτόν, ἵνα πραΰνῃ  
> (Of his nails at himself, in order to soothe)
>
> 81 Τὴν ὅλως ἀνίατον τοῦ κνησμοῦ λύσσαν.  
> (The wholly incurable madness of the itch.)

- *Ὡς αὐτῶν ἑκάτερος* answers *οὕτως* with ‘as each of the two’: *ἑκάτερος* is specifically distributive of a pair, with partitive gen. pl. *αὐτῶν*. *ἔξυεν* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ξύω*, with movable *-ν*) simply means ‘was scratching’, replacing *menava spesso il morso*; the imperfect conveys continuing action, but neither *spesso* nor the metaphor *morso* has a separate equivalent.
- *ἄκροις τῶν ὀνύχων* ‘with the tips of the nails’ uses an instrumental dative with a dependent genitive (gen. pl. of *ὄνυξ*). *ἑαυτόν* is the direct reflexive object, where Italian has *sopra sè*.
- *ἵνα πραΰνῃ* ‘in order that he might soothe’ converts *per la gran rabbia*, the cause of scratching, into its purpose. *πραΰνῃ* is 3rd sg. aor. act. subj. of *πραΰνω*; the purpose subjunctive is retained after a past tense rather than replaced by an optative. Its object *τὴν … τοῦ κνησμοῦ λύσσαν* ‘the frenzy of the itch’ retains *rabbia del pizzicor*. Attributive *ὅλως ἀνίατον* ‘wholly incurable’ qualifies *λύσσαν*, condensing *che non ha più soccorso* and replacing the lack of further relief with absolute incurability.

> 82 E sì traevan giù l' unghie la scabbia,  
> 83 come coltel di scardova le scaglie,  
> 84 o d' altro pesce che più larghe l' abbia.  

And so the nails drew down the scurf, as does a knife the scales from bream or other fish that has them larger.

> 82 Οἱ δ᾽ ὄνυχες ἀπέσπων τὰς ἐφελκίδας,  
> (And their nails tore off the scabs,)
>
> 83 Ὡς ἡ μαχαιρὶς τὰς λεπίδας τοῦ σκάρου,  
> (As the knife does the scales of the parrot-fish,)
>
> 84 Ἢ μείζονας ἔχοντος ἰχθύος ἄλλου.  
> (Or of any other fish that has larger ones.)

- *Οἱ δ᾽ ὄνυχες* (elided *δέ*) makes the nails the explicit nominative subject, as *l’ unghie* is the subject of *traevan*. *ἀπέσπων* is 3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *ἀποσπάω*, with internal augment and contraction: ‘were tearing off’ gives separation rather than the downward direction of *giù*. Acc. pl. *τὰς ἐφελκίδας* ‘the scabs’ (from *ἐφελκίς*) replaces collective singular *la scabbia*. There is no separate equivalent of *sì* before the comparison.
- *Ὡς ἡ μαχαιρὶς τὰς λεπίδας τοῦ σκάρου* ‘as the knife [does] the scales of the parrot-fish’ retains the Italian ellipsis of the verb. *μαχαιρίς* is nominative, *λεπίδας* acc. pl. of *λεπίς*, and *τοῦ σκάρου* a genitive dependent on ‘scales’; Greek postpones the fish name, where *di scardova* precedes *le scaglie*, and chooses *σκάρος* rather than the fish named in the English as “bream”.
- *Ἢ … ἰχθύος ἄλλου* ‘or of another fish’ coordinates a second genitive with *τοῦ σκάρου*. *ἔχοντος*, gen. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *ἔχω*, agrees with *ἰχθύος* rather than forming a genitive absolute; it substitutes for relative *che … l’ abbia*. Its object *μείζονας* is the acc. fem. pl. comparative of *μέγας*, with *λεπίδας* understood: ‘larger ones’, not specifically ‘broader ones’ as in *più larghe*.

> 85 "O tu che con le dita ti dismaglie,"  
> 86 cominciò il duca mio all' un di loro,  
> 87 "e che fai d' esse talvolta tanaglie,  

“ O thou! ” began my Guide to one of them, “ who with thy fingers dismailest thyself, and sometimes makest pincers of them;

> 85 “Ὦ σύ, δακτύλοις σεαυτὸν ἀποδέρων,”  
> (“O you, who with your fingers flay yourself,”)
>
> 86 Οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ εἶπε πρὸς ἕνα τούτων,  
> (My Guide said to one of these,)
>
> 87 “ Καὶ ποιῶν διηνεκῶς αὐτοὺς σχενδύλας,  
> (“And continually make pincers of them,)

- *Ὦ σύ* ‘O you’ retains the direct address. *δακτύλοις σεαυτὸν ἀποδέρων* ‘flaying yourself with fingers’ uses an instrumental dat. pl., the 2nd-person reflexive accusative, and the pres. act. participle of *ἀποδέρω* referring to *σύ*. Where *che … ti dismaglie* is a finite relative clause with an image of stripping off mail, Greek gives a participial description and the literal action of stripping skin.
- *Οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ* ‘my guide’ has crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, article and possessive adjective, for postposed *mio*. *εἶπε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*) means ‘said’ rather than *cominciò* ‘began’. *πρὸς ἕνα τούτων* renders *all’ un di loro* by *πρός* with the accusative of the addressee (*ἕνα*, masc. acc. sg. of *εἷς*) and a partitive gen. pl. of *οὗτος*, ‘one of these’.
- *Καὶ ποιῶν* resumes *ἀποδέρων* after the speech tag with a second pres. act. participle (of *ποιέω*), not a finite verb like *fai*. *αὐτοὺς σχενδύλας* uses a double accusative, object ‘them’ (masculine, referring to *δακτύλοις*) and predicate ‘pincers’, in place of *d’ esse … tanaglie*. *διηνεκῶς* ‘continually’ changes the frequency expressed by *talvolta* ‘sometimes’.

## Lines 88–120 — Two Latians and the Aretine’s Condemnation

> 88 dinne s' alcun Latino è tra costoro  
> 89 che son quinc' entro, se l' unghia ti basti  
> 90 eternalmente a cotesto lavoro.”  

tell [us] if there be any Latian among these who are here within; so may thy nails eternally suffice thee for that work.”

> 88 „ Εἰπέ μοι, Λατίνων τις ἔστ᾽ ἆρ᾽ ἐν μέσῳ  
> („ Tell me, is there any Latian in the midst)
>
> 89 „ Τῶν ἐντὸς τούτων; εἴθ᾽ ἐπαρκέσειάν σοι  
> („ Of these within here? so may there suffice for you)
>
> 90 „ Ὄνυχες οἱ σοὶ πρὸς τοὖργον αἰωνίως!”  
> („ Those nails of yours for the task eternally!”)

- *Εἰπέ μοι* ‘tell me’ has the 2nd sg. second-aorist active imperative of *λέγω* and the enclitic dative *μοι*, which accounts for the accent on the final syllable of *εἰπέ*. The recipient is singular, not the plural of *dinne* and “[us].”
- *Λατίνων τις ἔστ᾽ ἆρ᾽ ἐν μέσῳ τῶν ἐντὸς τούτων;* makes *s’ alcun Latino è* a direct question. *Λατίνων* is a partitive genitive before indefinite *τις*: ‘is there one of the Latians?’ Both *ἔστ᾽* (from *ἐστί*) and interrogative *ἆρ᾽* (from *ἆρα*) are elided. *Ἐν μέσῳ* ‘in the midst’ takes the genitive; *τῶν ἐντὸς τούτων* ‘of these here inside’ incorporates the adverb *ἐντός* into the noun phrase instead of using a relative clause like *che son quinc’ entro*.
- *Εἴθ᾽ ἐπαρκέσειάν σοι* expresses the wish implicit in *se … ti basti*. *Εἴθ᾽* is *εἴθε* elided, with aspiration before the rough breathing conventionally absent here: the *θ* already belongs to *εἴθε*. *Ἐπαρκέσειαν* is 3rd pl. first-aorist active optative of *ἐπαρκέω*, with the alternative plural ending *-σειαν*; it agrees with the delayed plural *ὄνυχες*, where Italian has singular *l’ unghia*. The optative of wish needs no *ἄν*.
- *Ὄνυχες οἱ σοί* ‘those nails of yours’ puts the articular possessive after the noun; *σοι* in the preceding line is the dative of the person for whom they are to suffice. *Πρὸς τοὖργον* gives the purpose, *πρός* with accusative *τοὖργον* (crasis of *τὸ ἔργον*); the article replaces demonstrative *cotesto*. *Αἰωνίως* moves *eternalmente* to the end.

> 91 “Latin sem noi, che tu vedi sì guasti  
> 92 qui ambo e due,” rispose l' un piangendo;  

“Latians are we, whom thou seest so disfigured here, both of us,” replied the one weeping;

> 91 — “Ἐσμὲν Λατῖνοι, δυσμόρφους οὓς σὺ βλέπεις  
> (—“We are Latians, whom you see disfigured)
>
> 92 „ Ὧδ᾽, ἀμφότεροι,” κλαίων εἷς ἀπεκρίθη·  
> („ Here, both of us,” weeping one answered;)

- *Ἐσμὲν Λατῖνοι* puts the copula first, whereas *Latin sem noi* puts the predicate first and expresses the subject pronoun. *Ἐσμέν* is 1st pl. present indicative of *εἰμί*; *Λατῖνοι* is predicate nominative. In *δυσμόρφους οὓς σὺ βλέπεις*, the predicate accusative *δυσμόρφους* ‘misshapen’ comes even before the relative *οὕς*, the object of *βλέπεις*. It renders *sì guasti* by one adjective, without a separate word of degree.
- *Ὧδ᾽, ἀμφότεροι* has elided *ὧδε* ‘here’ and nominative plural *ἀμφότεροι* ‘both’, resuming the subject of *ἐσμέν*, not agreeing with accusative *οὕς*. *Κλαίων εἷς ἀπεκρίθη* reverses the order of *rispose l’ un piangendo*: the simultaneous present active participle *κλαίων* agrees with nominative *εἷς* ‘one’; *ἀπεκρίθη* is 3rd sg. aorist passive in form, with the active sense ‘answered’, from *ἀποκρίνομαι*.

> 93 “ma tu chi se', che di noi domandasti?”  

“but who art thou that hast inquired of us?”

> 93 “Ἀλλὰ σὺ τίς εἶ; πρὸς τί δ᾽ ἡμῶν πυνθάνῃ;”—  
> (“But who are you? and for what reason do you inquire of us?”—)

- *Ἀλλὰ σὺ τίς εἶ;* retains the explicit *tu* as *σύ* and uses interrogative *τίς*, unlike indefinite *τις* in line 88. *Εἶ* is 2nd sg. present of *εἰμί*. Greek breaks off the question here and substitutes a second, *πρὸς τί δ᾽ …;* ‘and to what end …?’, for the relative *che … domandasti*.
- *Ἡμῶν πυνθάνῃ* has the 2nd sg. present middle indicative of deponent *πυνθάνομαι*, rather than a past tense. The genitive with this verb readily denotes the people from whom one seeks information; in context it also echoes Italian *di noi*, ‘about us’. *Δ᾽* is elided postpositive *δέ*.

> 94 E il duca disse: “Io son un che discendo  
> 95 con questo vivo giù di balzo in balzo,  
> 96 e di mostrar l' inferno a lui intendo.”  

And the Guide said : “I am one, who with this *living man* descend from steep to steep, and mean to show him Hell.”

> 94 Εἶπε δ᾽ Ἡγήτωρ· “Ἐγὼ μὲν καταβαίνω  
> (And the Guide said: “I descend)
>
> 95 „ Ἀπὸ βάθους εἰς βάθος σὺν ζῶντι τούτῳ,  
> („ From depth to depth with this living man,)
>
> 96 „ Ὅπως ἂν αὐτῷ δείξω τὸν ᾍδην ὅλον.”  
> („ In order that I may show him the whole of Hell.”)

- *Εἶπε δ᾽ Ἡγήτωρ* puts the aorist verb before the subject; *Ἡγήτωρ* ‘leader’ is a poetic agent noun, used as a title without the article. *Ἐγὼ μὲν καταβαίνω* ‘I for my part descend’ replaces *Io son un che discendo* with a simple 1st sg. present. *Μέν* marks off the speaker’s own answer without a corresponding *δέ* in the speech.
- *Ἀπὸ βάθους εἰς βάθος* ‘from depth to depth’ repeats the same neuter noun in genitive and accusative, rendering *giù di balzo in balzo* in terms of depth rather than a succession of ledges. *Σὺν ζῶντι τούτῳ* follows the directional phrase, unlike *con questo vivo*. *Ζῶντι* is the dative sg. masculine of the contracted present active participle of *ζάω*, used substantivally with the demonstrative.
- *Ὅπως ἂν αὐτῷ δείξω* ‘in order that I may show him’ turns the coordinate *e di mostrar … intendo* into a purpose clause: *ὅπως ἄν* with 1st sg. aorist active subjunctive of *δείκνυμι*. *Αὐτῷ* is the recipient and *τὸν ᾍδην* the object. Predicate-position *ὅλον* adds ‘in its entirety’, absent from *l’ inferno*.

> 97 Allor si ruppe lo comun rincalzo;  
> 98 e tremando ciascuno a me si volse  
> 99 con altri che l' udiron di rimbalzo.  

Then the mutual propping broke, and each turned trembling towards me, with others that by echo heard him.

> 97 Τότε παύσαντες συνερείδειν ἀλλήλους  
> (Then ceasing to prop one another up,)
>
> 98 Ἐτράπησαν πρὸς ἐμὲ τρέμοντες ἄμφω  
> (Both turned toward me, trembling,)
>
> 99 Μετὰ τῶν ἄλλων τὰ ῥηθέντ᾽ ἀκουσάντων.  
> (Together with the others who had heard what was spoken.)

- *Τότε παύσαντες συνερείδειν ἀλλήλους* changes *si ruppe lo comun rincalzo* from an independent clause with an abstract subject into a participial expression with personal agents. *Παύσαντες* is nominative masc. pl. aorist active of *παύω*. The intended sense is ‘having ceased to prop one another up’; ordinary Attic for ceasing oneself would more naturally use middle *παύομαι* with a supplementary participle. Here the present active infinitive *συνερείδειν* names the action discontinued, and reciprocal accusative *ἀλλήλους* renders its mutuality.
- *Ἐτράπησαν* is 3rd pl. second-aorist passive of *τρέπω*, intransitive ‘turned’, for reflexive singular *si volse*. *Τρέμοντες* is the simultaneous present active participle, agreeing with the plural subject. Instead of distributive *ciascuno*, Musurus has *ἄμφω* ‘both’, a dual form construed here with a plural verb and participles.
- *Μετὰ τῶν ἄλλων … ἀκουσάντων* uses *μετά* with the genitive of accompaniment and an agreeing aorist active participle of *ἀκούω* instead of *con altri che l’ udiron*. Its object is *τὰ ῥηθέντ᾽*, elided *τὰ ῥηθέντα* ‘the things said’, a substantivized neuter pl. aorist passive participle of *λέγω*. There is no equivalent of *di rimbalzo*.

> 100 Lo buon maestro a me tutto s' accolse,  
> 101 dicendo: “Di' a lor ciò che tu vuoli.”  

The kind Master to me directed himself wholly, saying: “Tell them what thou wishest.”

> 100 Ὁ δ᾽ ἀγαθὸς Προστάτης προσήγγισέ μοι  
> (And the good Protector drew near to me,)
>
> 101 Λέγων· “Εἰπὲ δὴ τούτοις νῦν, εἴ τι βούλει.”  
> (Saying: “Speak then to these now, if you wish anything.”)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ ἀγαθὸς Προστάτης* gives *lo buon maestro* the title ‘protector’ rather than ‘teacher’. *Προσήγγισέ μοι* ‘drew near to me’ interprets *a me tutto s’ accolse* as a movement of approach, omitting *tutto* and the reflexive. The verb is 3rd sg. aorist active indicative of *προσεγγίζω*, with augment after the preverb and an additional accent before enclitic *μοι*.
- *Λέγων* is the nominative sg. present active participle corresponding to *dicendo*. *Εἰπὲ δὴ τούτοις νῦν* adds the urging particle *δή* and temporal *νῦν* to the imperative; *τούτοις* ‘to these’ makes *a lor* demonstrative. *Εἴ τι βούλει* ‘if you wish anything’ changes the definite relative object *ciò che tu vuoli* into a conditional clause with indefinite *τι*. *Βούλει* is the Attic 2nd sg. present middle indicative of *βούλομαι*.

> 102 Ed io incominciai, poscia ch' ei volse:  

And I began, as he desired:

> 102 Ἐγὼ δ᾽ ἠρξάμην, ἀδείας μοι δοθείσης·  
> (And I began, permission having been granted me:)

- *Ἐγὼ δ᾽ ἠρξάμην* retains the explicit pronoun and uses the 1st sg. aorist middle of *ἄρχομαι*, ‘I began’, with temporal augment *ἠ-* and the aorist stem in *-ξ-*. *Ἀδείας μοι δοθείσης* ‘permission having been given to me’ interprets *poscia ch’ ei volse* as authorization. It is a genitive absolute: *δοθείσης*, gen. fem. sg. aorist passive participle of *δίδωμι*, agrees with *ἀδείας*, while *μοι* is the recipient. The Italian subject *ei* is left unexpressed as agent.

> 103 “Se la vostra memoria non s' imboli  
> 104 nel primo mondo dall' umane menti,  
> 105 ma s' ella viva sotto molti soli,  

“So may your memory not fade away from human minds in the first world, but may it live under many suns,

> 103 “Εἴθ᾽ ὑμῶν μήποτ᾽ ἐξίτηλος ἡ μνήμη  
> (“May the memory of you never fade away)
>
> 104 „ Γένοιτο παρ᾽ ἀνθρώποις τοῦ πρώτου κόσμου,  
> („ Among men of the first world,)
>
> 105 „ Ζῴη δὲ διὰ πολλῶν ἡλίου κύκλων!  
> („ But may it live through many circuits of the sun!)

- *Εἴθ᾽ ὑμῶν μήποτ᾽ ἐξίτηλος ἡ μνήμη γένοιτο* again renders optative *se* by elided *εἴθε*. *Μήποτ᾽* is elided *μήποτε* ‘never’, with the negative appropriate to a wish. The possessive genitive *ὑμῶν* is separated from *ἡ μνήμη*; *ἐξίτηλος* ‘effaced’ is a two-termination adjective in the feminine nominative, predicative with *γένοιτο*, 3rd sg. second-aorist middle optative of *γίγνομαι*. Thus *non s’ imboli* becomes ‘may it never become effaced’.
- *Παρ᾽ ἀνθρώποις τοῦ πρώτου κόσμου* means ‘among people of the first world’: *παρά* with dative replaces *dall’ umane menti*, and *nel primo mondo* becomes a dependent genitive. *Ζῴη δέ* ‘but may it live’ continues the wish without repeating *εἴθε* or the subject. *Ζῴη* is 3rd sg. present active optative of *ζάω*, presenting continued life rather than a change of state.
- *Διὰ πολλῶν ἡλίου κύκλων* has temporal *διά* with the genitive ‘through many circuits’; singular *ἡλίου* ‘of the sun’ depends on *κύκλων*. The Greek thus interprets *sotto molti soli* as duration, not position, and closes the wish as a sentence before the request.

> 106 ditemi chi voi siete e di che genti;  

tell me who ye are, and of what people;

> 106 „ Εἴπατε, τίνες ἐστὲ καὶ ποίου γένους·  
> („ Tell me who you are and of what race;)

- *Εἴπατε* is the 2nd pl. aorist active imperative of *λέγω*, with the *-ατε* ending of the alternative aorist formation beside *εἴπετε*. There is no expressed dative for the *-mi* of *ditemi*. *Τίνες ἐστὲ καὶ ποίου γένους* keeps the two interrogatives: plural *τίνες* asks ‘who?’, while genitive *ποίου γένους* is predicative with *ἐστέ* understood again, ‘of what stock?’ Singular neuter *γένος* replaces plural *genti*.

> 107 la vostra sconcia e fastidiosa pena  
> 108 di palesarvi a me non vi spaventi.”  

let not your ugly and disgusting punishment frighten you from revealing yourselves to me.”

> 107 „ Ἡ δεινὴ κ᾽ αἰσχρὰ βάσανος ὑμῶν αὕτη  
> („ This dreadful and shameful torment of yours)
>
> 108 „ Ὑμᾶς μὴ κωλυέτω φράσαι μοι τοῦτο.”  
> („ Let it not prevent you from declaring this to me.”)

- *Ἡ δεινὴ κ᾽ αἰσχρὰ βάσανος ὑμῶν αὕτη* ‘this dreadful and shameful torment of yours’ keeps the two attributive adjectives but changes their emphasis from *sconcia e fastidiosa*. *Κ᾽* represents shortened *καί*. *Βάσανος* is feminine despite its ending; the two adjectives and the added external demonstrative *αὕτη* agree with it.
- *Ὑμᾶς μὴ κωλυέτω* substitutes prevention for fright. *Κωλυέτω* is 3rd sg. present active imperative of *κωλύω*, with *μή*; its personal object *ὑμᾶς* is also the understood subject of *φράσαι*, aorist active infinitive of *φράζω*. *Μοι τοῦτο* ‘this to me’ gives the recipient and thing declared: *di palesarvi* becomes an infinitive with a demonstrative object instead of a reflexive.

> 109 “Io fui d' Arezzo, ed Albero da Siena,”  
> 110 rispose l' un, “mi fe' mettere al foco;  
> 111 ma quel per ch' io mori' qui non mi mena.  

“I was of Arezzo,” replied the one, “and Albero of Siena had me burned; but what I died for does not bring me here.

> 109 — “Εἴμ᾽ Ἀῤῥητινός· ὁ δ᾽ Ἄλβερος ὁ Σήνης,”  
> (—“I am an Aretine; and Albero of Siena,”)
>
> 110 Εἶπεν ἅτερος, “εἰς τὸ πῦρ ἔῤῥιψέ με·  
> (The one said, “cast me into the fire;)
>
> 111 „ Ἀλλ᾽ ὅ μ᾽ ἐθανάτωσεν οὔ μ᾽ ἤγαγ᾽ ὧδε.  
> („ But that which put me to death did not bring me hither.)

- *Εἴμ᾽ Ἀῤῥητινός* ‘I am an Aretine’ changes *fui* to the present *εἰμί*, elided, and *d’ Arezzo* to a predicate ethnic adjective. The spelling *ῤῥ* marks the two rhos with smooth and rough breathings respectively. *Ὁ δ᾽ Ἄλβερος ὁ Σήνης* gives the personal name an article and adds the identifying genitive ‘of Siena’ after a repeated article.
- *Εἶπεν ἅτερος* ‘said the one’ uses the second aorist of *λέγω* and *ἅτερος*, crasis of *ὁ ἕτερος*, ‘the one of the two’. The speech resumes with *εἰς τὸ πῦρ ἔῤῥιψέ με* ‘threw me into the fire’. *Ἔῤῥιψε* is 3rd sg. aorist active of *ῥίπτω*, with rho doubled after the augment. This makes Albero the direct grammatical agent rather than retaining causative *mi fe’ mettere*.
- *Ἀλλ᾽ ὅ μ᾽ ἐθανάτωσεν* ‘but what put me to death’ has a free relative with neuter nominative *ὅ* as subject of transitive *ἐθανάτωσεν*, 3rd sg. aorist active of *θανατόω*. Italian makes the speaker the subject of *mori’* and gives the relative a causal preposition, *quel per ch’*. *Οὔ μ᾽ ἤγαγ᾽ ὧδε* ‘did not bring me here’ changes present *non mi mena* to the past: *ἤγαγ᾽* is elided *ἤγαγε*, 3rd sg. of the reduplicated second aorist of *ἄγω*. Both instances of *μ᾽* stand for accusative *με*.

> 112 Ver è ch' io dissi a lui, parlando a gioco,  
> 113 ‘Io mi saprei levar per l' aere a volo';  

’Tis true, I said to him, speaking in jest : ‘I could raise myself through the air in flight’;

> 112 „ Ἀληθῶς ποτε πρὸς αὐτὸν εἶπον παίζων,  
> („ Truly once in jest I said to him,)
>
> 113 „ Ὅτι δυναίμην ἵπτασθαι δι᾽ ἀέρος·  
> („ That I could fly through the air;)

- *Ἀληθῶς ποτε* ‘truly, once’ replaces *Ver è ch’* with an adverb and adds indefinite temporal *ποτε*. *Πρὸς αὐτὸν εἶπον παίζων* gives the addressee with *πρός* and accusative; *παίζων*, nom. masc. sg. present active participle of *παίζω*, conveys *parlando a gioco* without a separate verb for speaking.
- *Ὅτι δυναίμην* turns the direct quotation into reported speech after past *εἶπον*. *Δυναίμην* is 1st sg. present middle optative of *δύναμαι*, an oblique optative after a historical tense, not a potential optative with *ἄν*. ‘I could’ thus renders *mi saprei* by capacity rather than knowledge. *Ἵπτασθαι*, present middle infinitive of *ἵπταμαι*, compresses *levar per l’ aere a volo* to ‘fly’; *δι᾽ ἀέρος* is *διά* with the genitive of the medium traversed.

> 114 e quei, che avea vaghezza e senno poco,  
> 115 volle ch' io gli mostrassi l' arte; e solo  
> 116 perch' io nol feci Dedalo, mi fece  
> 117 ardere a tal, che l' avea per figliuolo.  

and he, who had a fond desire and little wit, willed that I should shew him the art; and only because I made him not a Dædalus, he made me be burned by one who had him for a son.

> 114 „ Αὐτὸς δ᾽ ὢν περίεργος καὶ μωρὸς ἅμα,  
> („ And he, being curious and foolish at once,)
>
> 115 „ ᾜτησέ με τὴν τέχνην δεῖξαι, καὶ μόνον  
> („ Asked me to show the art, and merely)
>
> 116 „ Ὡς μὴ Δαίδαλον φανέντ᾽ ἐποίησέ με  
> („ Because I did not prove a Daedalus, he had me)
>
> 117 „ Καυθῆν᾽ ὑπὸ φιλοῦντος αὐτὸν ὡς τέκνον.  
> („ Burned by one who loved him as a son.)

- *Αὐτὸς δ᾽ ὢν περίεργος καὶ μωρὸς ἅμα* ‘but he himself, being at once curious and foolish’ replaces *e quei, che avea* with an emphatic pronoun and a circumstantial participle. *Ὤν*, nom. masc. sg. present participle of *εἰμί*, takes two predicate adjectives in place of the objects *vaghezza e senno poco*; *ἅμα* explicitly couples the qualities.
- *ᾜτησέ με τὴν τέχνην δεῖξαι* ‘asked me to show the art’ turns *volle ch’ io gli mostrassi* into a request with accusative and infinitive. *ᾜτησε* is 3rd sg. aorist active of *αἰτέω*, with temporal augment of *αι-* to *ῃ-*. *Με* is the person asked and the subject understood with *δεῖξαι*, aorist active infinitive of *δείκνυμι*; *τὴν τέχνην* is its object. Italian *gli* has no separate equivalent.
- *Καὶ μόνον ὡς μὴ Δαίδαλον φανέντα* changes who was to be a Daedalus. In *io nol feci Dedalo*, *io* is the maker and *lo* the person not made a Daedalus. In Greek *φανέντ᾽* is elided *φανέντα*, accusative masc. sg. second-aorist passive participle of *φαίνω*, intransitive ‘having proved to be’. It agrees with the speaker, *με*, and *Δαίδαλον* is its predicate accusative. *Ὡς* with the participle presents the alleged ground for acting, with *μή* negating it: ‘merely on the ground that I had not proved a Daedalus’.
- *Ἐποίησέ με καυθῆν᾽* closely follows causative *mi fece ardere*: *με* is the accusative subject of *καυθῆν᾽*, elided *καυθῆναι*, aorist passive infinitive of *καίω*. *Ὑπὸ φιλοῦντος* expresses the agent by *ὑπό* and a genitive sg. present active participle of *φιλέω*, used without a stated noun. *Αὐτὸν ὡς τέκνον* ‘him as a child’ construes *l’ avea per figliuolo* as affection, not literally ‘had him for a son’; *τέκνον* is predicated of accusative *αὐτόν*.

> 118 Ma nell' ultima bolgia delle diece  
> 119 me per alchimia, che nel mondo usai,  
> 120 dannò Minos, a cui fallir non lece.”  

But to the last budget of the ten, for the alchemy that I practised in the world, Minos, who may not err, condemned me.”

> 118 „ Ἀλλά μ᾽ εἰς τὸν ἔσχατον τῶν δέκα βόθρων  
> („ But I to the last of the ten pits)
>
> 119 „ Δι᾽ ἀλχυμίαν, ἣν ἔπραττον ἐν κόσμῳ,  
> („ Because of the alchemy that I practised in the world,)
>
> 120 „ Ἔπεμψε Μίνως, ὃς οὔποτ᾽ ἀπατᾶται.” —  
> („ Was sent by Minos, who is never deceived.”—)

- *Ἀλλά μ᾽ εἰς τὸν ἔσχατον τῶν δέκα βόθρων* brings *με* forward from Italian line 119 but, like the Italian, delays the governing verb. After directional *εἰς*, *τὸν ἔσχατον* ‘the last’ implies masculine *βόθρον*, ‘pit’; *τῶν δέκα βόθρων* is the partitive genitive. Musurus thus supplies the noun with ‘the ten’ rather than with ‘the last’.
- *Δι᾽ ἀλχυμίαν* has causal *διά* with accusative, unlike *δι᾽ ἀέρος* with genitive in line 113. *Ἣν ἔπραττον* gives the feminine relative the accusative required by *πράττω*. *Ἔπραττον* is here 1st sg. imperfect active indicative: it represents *usai* as a continuing practice. *Ἐν κόσμῳ* is locative, without the article of *nel mondo*.
- *Ἔπεμψε Μίνως* ‘Minos sent [me]’ substitutes a verb of dispatch, 3rd sg. aorist active of *πέμπω*, for judicial *dannò*. *Ὃς οὔποτ᾽ ἀπατᾶται* replaces *a cui fallir non lece* with a personal relative clause. *Ὅς* is nominative subject, *οὔποτ᾽* is elided *οὔποτε* ‘never’, and *ἀπατᾶται* is contracted 3rd sg. present passive indicative of *ἀπατάω*: ‘who is never deceived’, rather than ‘to whom it is not permitted to err’.

## Lines 121–139 — Sienese Vanity and Capocchio’s Self-Portrait

> 121 Ed io dissi al poeta: “Or fu giammai  
> 122 gente sì vana come la sanese?  

And I said to the Poet: "Now was there ever people so vain as the Sienese?

> 121 Κἀγὼ πρὸς τὸν Ποιητήν· “Οὔποτ᾽ ἦν ὄντως  
> (And I said to the Poet: “Never truly was there)
>
> 122 „ Γένος οὕτω κενόφρον, ὡς τὸ Σηναῖον·  
> („ A people so vain-minded as the Sienese;)

- *Κἀγὼ πρὸς τὸν Ποιητήν* ‘and I to the Poet’ leaves *dissi* to be understood. *Κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*; the expressed nominative marks the change of speaker, and *πρός* with the accusative marks the person addressed.
- Musurus turns the rhetorical question *Or fu giammai* into its implied negative answer, *Οὔποτ᾽ ἦν ὄντως* ‘never truly was there’. *Οὔποτ᾽* is *οὔποτε* elided before *ἦν*, the 3rd sg. imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*, here existential. The emphatic adverb *ὄντως* ‘really’ is not a temporal rendering of *Or*.
- The subject *γένος* ‘race, people’ follows the verb, like *gente*; *κενόφρον* ‘empty-minded’ (nom. neut. sg. of *κενόφρων*) is its predicate. *Οὕτω … ὡς* answers to *sì … come*. In *τὸ Σηναῖον* ‘the Sienese one’, the article and ethnic adjective are neuter singular because *γένος* is understood.

> 123 certo non la francesca sì d’ assai.”  

certainly the French not so by far."

> 123 „ Βεβαίως οὐ τοσοῦτον τὸ γένος Γάλλων.”  
> („ Assuredly not so much the race of the French.”)

- *Βεβαίως* renders *certo*. *Οὐ τοσοῦτον* ‘not to such a degree’ negates the degree of the preceding predicate, with both verb and adjective left understood; there is no separate intensifier for *d’ assai*.
- Where *la francesca* leaves *gente* understood, Musurus now supplies *τὸ γένος* and changes the adjective to the possessive genitive plural *Γάλλων*: ‘the race of the French’.

> 124 Onde l’ altro lebbroso che m’ intese  
> 125 rispose al detto mio: “Trammene Stricca,  
> 126 che seppe far le temperate spese;  

Whereat the other leper, who heard me, responded to my words: "Except Stricca who contrived to spend so moderately;

> 124 Ὁ δ᾽ ἕτερος λεπρώδης ἀκούσας ταῦτα  
> (And the other leprous one, having heard these words,)
>
> 125 Ἐπεῖπεν ἀποκριθείς· “Ἐκτὸς τοῦ Στρίκκα,  
> (Spoke in reply: “Except Stricca,)
>
> 126 „ Φειδωλοῦ τόσον ἐν ταῖς αὐτοῦ δαπάναις,  
> („ Who was so frugal in his expenses,)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ ἕτερος λεπρώδης* ‘and the other leprous one’ uses the connective *δέ*, elided before *ἕτερος*, rather than an explicitly consequential equivalent of *Onde*. *Λεπρώδης* is adjectival in form, nominative masculine singular; *ἕτερος* picks out the other of two.
- *Ἀκούσας ταῦτα* ‘having heard these things’ replaces the finite relative *che m’ intese*. *Ἀκούσας* (nom. masc. sg., aor. act. participle of *ἀκούω*) agrees with the speaker and presents the hearing as prior to his reply. Its neuter plural object *ταῦτα* refers to what was said: Musurus thus takes up *al detto mio* in the hearing rather than after the verb of answering.
- *Ἐπεῖπεν ἀποκριθείς* ‘he spoke in turn, replying’ expands *rispose*. *Ἐπεῖπεν* is 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative; *ἀποκριθείς* is the nom. masc. sg. aorist participle of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, passive in form but active in sense, specifying the character of the utterance.
- *Ἐκτὸς τοῦ Στρίκκα* ‘except for Stricca’ replaces the imperative and pronouns of *Trammene* with *ἐκτός* governing the genitive. *Φειδωλοῦ* ‘sparing’ is gen. masc. sg. of *φειδωλός*, in apposition to the name: the relative clause *che seppe far* disappears in favour of a description. Adverbial *τόσον* ‘so much’ qualifies the adjective; *ἐν ταῖς αὐτοῦ δαπάναις* ‘in his expenditures’ specifies in what respect he was sparing, adding the possessive genitive *αὐτοῦ*.

> 127 e Niccolò, che la costuma ricca  
> 128 del garofano prima discoperse  
> 129 nell’ orto dove tal seme s’ appicca;  

and Niccolò, who first discovered the costly usage of the clove, in the garden where such seed takes root;

> 127 „ Καὶ τοῦ Νικολοῦ, τοῦ τὴν καρυοφύλλου  
> („ And Niccolò, who the clove's)
>
> 128 „ Πλουσίαν χρῆσιν εὑρόντος ἐν τῷ κήπῳ,  
> („ Lavish use invented in the garden,)
>
> 129 „ Ἔνθ᾽ εὐτυχῶς ἐμβιοῖ τοιοῦτο σπέρμα,  
> („ Where such seed thrives prosperously,)

- *Καὶ τοῦ Νικολοῦ* continues the genitives after *ἐκτός*. The repeated article *τοῦ* introduces an attributive participle in place of *che … discoperse*: *τοῦ … εὑρόντος* ‘the one who discovered’. *Εὑρόντος* is gen. masc. sg. of the second aorist active participle of *εὑρίσκω*; *prima* has no separate equivalent.
- As in Italian, the object precedes the verb, but Musurus brings the dependent genitive *καρυοφύλλου* ‘of the clove’ inside the object phrase, between its article and adjective: *τὴν καρυοφύλλου πλουσίαν χρῆσιν* ‘the lavish use of the clove’. *Πλουσίαν* agrees with *χρῆσιν* (acc. fem. sg. of *χρῆσις*); *χρῆσις* makes *costuma* specifically a ‘use’ rather than a ‘custom’.
- *Ἐν τῷ κήπῳ* follows the participle and renders *nell’ orto* by *ἐν* with the dative of place. The poetic locative adverb *ἔνθ᾽* (*ἔνθα*, elided) takes up *κήπῳ* as *dove* takes up *orto*. *Εὐτυχῶς ἐμβιοῖ* ‘lives successfully therein’ recasts *s’ appicca*: Greek adds the adverb and uses an intransitive active verb (3rd sg. pres. ind. of *ἐμβιόω*, contracted from *-όει*) rather than the Italian reflexive. Its postposed subject *τοιοῦτο σπέρμα* retains the demonstrative force of *tal seme*.

> 130 e tranne la brigata, in che disperse  
> 131 Caccia d’ Ascian la vigna e la gran fronda,  
> 132 e l’ Abbagliato il suo senno proferse.  

and except the company in which Caccia of Asciano squandered his vineyard and his great forest, and the Abbagliato shewed his wit.

> 130 „ Καὶ τῆς φρατρίας, μεθ᾽ ἧς κατεσπατάλα  
> („ And the company with which squandered)
>
> 131 „ Κακκίας Ἀσκιανὸς δρυμοὺς κἀμπέλους,  
> („ Caccia of Asciano woods and vineyards,)
>
> 132 „ Καὶ τὸν αὐτοῦ νοῦν ἔδειξ᾽ Ἀββαλιάτος.  
> („ And Abbagliato displayed his wit.)

- *Καὶ τῆς φρατρίας* ‘and the brotherhood’ adds a third genitive under the original *ἐκτός*, without repeating *tranne*. *Μεθ᾽ ἧς* means ‘with which’, not ‘in which’: Musurus interprets *in che* as association. *Μετά* takes the genitive of the feminine relative pronoun, with elision and aspiration of the final consonant before its rough breathing.
- *Κατεσπατάλα* ‘was squandering’ (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *κατασπαταλάω*, with internal augment and contracted ending) presents as ongoing what *disperse* gives as a completed act. The verb precedes its subject in both languages; *Κακκίας Ἀσκιανός* replaces *Caccia d’ Ascian* with a name and a nominative adjective of origin.
- *Δρυμοὺς κἀμπέλους* ‘woods and vines’ reverses *la vigna e la gran fronda*. Both objects are accusative plural: Musurus interprets the collective *fronda* as woodland, without separately translating *gran*. *Κἀμπέλους* is crasis of *καὶ ἀμπέλους*.
- In *καὶ τὸν αὐτοῦ νοῦν ἔδειξ᾽ Ἀββαλιάτος* ‘and Abbagliato displayed his understanding’, Italian subject–object–verb becomes object–verb–subject. *Αὐτοῦ* is the possessive genitive within the articular object phrase; *νοῦν* is the contracted acc. sg. of *νοῦς*. *Ἔδειξ᾽* is elided *ἔδειξε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *δείκνυμι*: Musurus takes *proferse* in the sense ‘displayed’.

> 133 Ma perchè sappi chi sì ti seconda  
> 134 contra i Sanesi, aguzza ver me l’ occhio  
> 135 sì che la faccia mia ben ti risponda;  

But that thou mayest know who thus seconds thee against the Sienese, sharpen thine eye towards me, that my face may give thee right response;

> 133 „ Ἵνα δὲ μάθῃς, τίς ἔσθ᾽ ὁ συμφρονῶν σοι  
> („ But that you may learn who it is that agrees with you)
>
> 134 „ Κατὰ Σηναίων, τρέψον πρός με τὸ βλέμμα,  
> („ Against the Sienese, turn your gaze toward me,)
>
> 135 „ Ὥστε τὴν ἐμὴν ἔχειν ἄντικρυς ὄψιν.  
> („ So that you may have my face right before you.)

- *Ἵνα δὲ μάθῃς* corresponds closely to *Ma perchè sappi*: postpositive *δέ* marks the turn, and purpose is expressed by *ἵνα* with *μάθῃς* (2nd sg. second aor. act. subj. of *μανθάνω*), ‘that you may learn’ rather than ‘that you may know’.
- *Τίς ἔσθ᾽ ὁ συμφρονῶν σοι* ‘who is the one agreeing with you?’ is the indirect question dependent on *μάθῃς*. *Ἔσθ᾽* is *ἐστί* elided and aspirated before *ὁ*. The articular present active participle *ὁ συμφρονῶν* (nom. masc. sg. of *συμφρονέω*) replaces finite *seconda*, and takes the dative of the person agreed with, *σοι*, rather than a direct object. *Sì* is not separately represented. *Κατὰ Σηναίων* retains *contra i Sanesi*, with the genitive plural after adversative *κατά*.
- *Τρέψον πρός με τὸ βλέμμα* ‘turn your gaze towards me’ preserves the order of *aguzza ver me l’ occhio*, but changes sharpening to turning and the eye to the gaze. *Τρέψον* is the 2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *τρέπω*; *πρός με* gives the direction and *τὸ βλέμμα* the accusative object.
- *Ὥστε τὴν ἐμὴν ἔχειν ἄντικρυς ὄψιν* ‘so as to have my face directly before you’ replaces the finite *sì che … ti risponda* with a result infinitive. The understood subject of *ἔχειν* (pres. act. inf. of *ἔχω*) is the person addressed. The face, subject of *risponda*, becomes the object *τὴν ἐμὴν … ὄψιν*, its noun delayed past the infinitive and the adverb *ἄντικρυς* ‘directly opposite’. The Greek expresses a visual relation instead of making the face answer.

> 136 sì vedrai ch’ io son l’ ombra di Capocchio,  
> 137 che falsai li metalli con alchimia;  

so shalt thou see I am the shadow of Capocchio, who falsified the metals by alchemy;

> 136 „ Ὄψει δή μ᾿ ὄντα τὴν σκιὰν Καποκίου,  
> („ You shall see indeed that I am the shade of Capocchio,)
>
> 137 „ Ὃς μέταλλ᾿ ἐδόλωσα δι᾿ ἀλχυμίας.  
> („ Who falsified metals through alchemy.)

- *Ὄψει δή* ‘you will see, then’ renders *sì vedrai*. *Ὄψει* is the 2nd sg. future middle indicative belonging to *ὁράω*, middle in form with active meaning; postpositive *δή* emphasizes the promised recognition.
- In place of *ch’ io son*, Greek uses the participial construction of perception: *μ᾿ ὄντα* ‘me being’, with elided *με* as object and *ὄντα* (acc. masc. sg., pres. participle of *εἰμί*) agreeing with it. *Τὴν σκιὰν Καποκίου* is the predicate accusative with a dependent genitive, preserving *l’ ombra di Capocchio*.
- *Ὃς μέταλλ᾿ ἐδόλωσα* keeps the first person of *che falsai*: the relative *ὅς* is nominative masculine singular, referring to the male speaker, not agreeing with feminine *σκιάν*. *Ἐδόλωσα* is 1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *δολόω* ‘adulterate’, with syllabic augment; *μέταλλ᾿* is elided *μέταλλα*, the anarthrous neuter plural object. Instrumental *con alchimia* becomes *δι᾿ ἀλχυμίας*, elided *διά* with the genitive, ‘by means of alchemy’.

- *Εἰ δὲ μὴ σφάλλω* ‘and if I am not mistaken’ brings the condition to the front. *Δέ* connects it with the preceding assertion; *εἰ μή* with the indicative makes a negative condition. *Σφάλλω* is 1st sg. pres. act. ind.: the intended sense is intransitive, where classical Greek would more naturally use middle-passive *σφάλλομαι*. Musurus gives the implication of *se ben t’ adocchio*, dropping both the act of looking and its object.
- *Μεμνῆσθαί σε νομίζω* ‘I think you remember’ changes *ti dei ricordar* from an expectation expressed as necessity to a judgment by the speaker. *Νομίζω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.) governs the preceding infinitive with *σε* as its accusative subject. *Μεμνῆσθαι* is the reduplicated perfect middle infinitive of *μιμνῄσκω*, denoting the present state of remembering; the extra accent on its final syllable is due to enclitic *σε*.
- *Ὅτι* introduces the content remembered as a statement of fact, ‘that’, rather than reproducing *com’* as ‘how’. *Καλὸς ὑπῆρξα φύσεως πίθηξ* splits the predicate *καλὸς … πίθηξ* ‘a good ape’ around the verb and genitive. *Ὑπῆρξα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ὑπάρχω*, with internal augment) is copular here, answering to *fui*. The anarthrous genitive *φύσεως* (gen. sg. of *φύσις*) depends on *πίθηξ*: Musurus takes *di natura* as specifying what the ape imitates, not as an adverbial ‘by nature’.

## Summary — Recasting the Grammar of Perception

Musurus often keeps Dante’s order of perception while changing who or what acts. In the opening, *θέα* ‘sight’ becomes the subject of *κατεμέθυσε* ‘intoxicated’: Dante’s two subjects, the crowd and the wounds, become genitives dependent on the act of seeing. A few lines later the change runs the other way: where *la vista tua* itself rests, Greek makes the observer fix *τοὺς ὀφθαλμούς* ‘the eyes’. A recurring construction is the accusative with a supplementary participle after a verb of perception. It serves both for an action witnessed, as in *δεικνύοντά σε* ‘pointing you out’, and for an identity recognized, where *μ᾿ ὄντα* ‘me being’ takes the place of *ch’ io son*. Yet the Greek can follow the Italian’s arrangement closely: *Οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ* ‘my guide’, like *lo duca*, names the moving subject only after the intervening mention of the follower.

Particles and moods make distinctions for which Dante often uses the same connective. Musurus pairs *οὕτω* ‘so’ with *ὥστε* and an infinitive for the opening impulse to weep, but uses *ὥστε* with the indicative for a realized consequence. Italian *se* likewise receives different treatments: *εἰ* with a past indicative leads to an unreal conclusion marked by *ἄν*, while *εἴθε* with the optative brings out a wish. Participles also redistribute the weight of clauses. *σοῦ μὴ προσχόντος* ‘you not having paid heed’ makes a genitive absolute of Dante’s finite statement, leaving the departure as the main result. By contrast, the Italian gerund *soggiungendo* becomes the independent *ἐπεῖπον* ‘I added’. Postpositive *δέ* and *τε* link such recast clauses, while *δή* can press a question or urge a command without corresponding to a separate Italian word.

Musurus is often closest to Dante in the shape of a phrase rather than in its vocabulary. *κλίβανος πρὸς κλιβάνῳ* ‘oven beside oven’ preserves the repetition of *tegghia a tegghia* but changes the utensil; *σιδηροφόρα βέλη* ‘iron-bearing missiles’ keeps the iron of *ferrati* but leaves *di pietà* unexpressed. Some departures amount to distinct readings. In *nol feci Dedalo* the speaker did not make the other man a Daedalus; in Greek *φανέντα* ‘having proved to be’ agrees with *με* ‘me’, so it is the speaker who failed to prove a Daedalus. The closing *φύσεως πίθηξ* ‘ape of nature’, on the other hand, clarifies the Italian by attachment rather than substitution: *di natura* belongs with the ape as the object of its imitation, not with the speaker as a description of what he was ‘by nature’.
