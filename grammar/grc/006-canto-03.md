# Inferno 3

## Lines 1–21 — The Gate’s Inscription and the Guide’s Reply

> 1 “Per me si va nella città dolente;  
> 2 per me si va nell’ eterno dolore;  
> 3 per me si va tra la perduta gente.  

“Through me is the way into the doleful city ; through me the way into the eternal pain; through me the way among the people lost.

> 1 Ἐνθάδ᾽ αἱ πύλαι τῆς πόλεως τῶν γόων·  
> (Here are the gates of the city of lamentations;)
>
> 2 Ἐνθάδ᾽ αἱ πύλαι τῆς αἰωνίου λύπης·  
> (Here are the gates of eternal sorrow;)
>
> 3 Ἐνθάδ᾽ ἡ χώρα τοῦ καταράτου γένους.  
> (Here is the region of the accursed race.)

- Musurus keeps the threefold opening but changes what it points to: *Ἐνθάδ᾽* ‘here’ (elided *ἐνθάδε*) replaces *per me* ‘through me’. The impersonal motion of *si va* disappears. All three Greek clauses are verbless, with an understood ‘are’ or ‘is’; *αἱ πύλαι* ‘the gates’ (nom. pl. of *πύλη*, commonly plural of a gateway) is supplied in the first two.
- *Τῆς πόλεως τῶν γόων* ‘of the city of laments’ makes the destination *nella città* a genitive dependent on *πύλαι*, and the adjective *dolente* a second genitive, *τῶν γόων* (gen. pl. of *γόος*), dependent on *πόλεως*. In *τῆς αἰωνίου λύπης* ‘of eternal sorrow’, the adjective is retained: *αἰωνίου* is gen. sg. fem. of the two-termination adjective *αἰώνιος*.
- In the third line *ἡ χώρα* ‘the region’ takes the place of *αἱ πύλαι*. For *tra la perduta gente* ‘among the lost people’ Musurus has *τοῦ καταράτου γένους* ‘of the accursed race’: another possessive genitive rather than a phrase of movement, with *καταράτου* agreeing with the neuter singular *γένους* (from *γένος*).

> 4 Giustizia mosse il mio alto Fattore;  

Justice moved my High Maker;

> 4 Τοῦ Πλάστου μου σκοπὸς ἦν Δικαιοσύνη.  
> (The purpose of my Creator was Justice.)

- *Τοῦ Πλάστου μου σκοπὸς ἦν Δικαιοσύνη* ‘my Maker’s purpose was Justice’ fronts the possessive genitive *τοῦ Πλάστου μου* (from *πλάστης* ‘fashioner’, with enclitic possessive *μου*). *Alto* has no separate equivalent.
- The Italian has an agent *Giustizia*, a transitive verb *mosse*, and an object *il mio … Fattore*. Greek supplies the noun *σκοπός* ‘aim’ and links two nominatives by *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*): Justice is identified as the aim rather than said to have moved the Maker.

> 5 fecemi la divina Potestate,  
> 6 la somma Sapienza e il primo Amore.  

Divine Power made me, Wisdom Supreme, and Primal Love.

> 5 Θεία Δύναμις ἐδημιούργησέ με,  
> (Divine Power fashioned me,)
>
> 6 Ἡ μεγίστη Σοφία, καὶ πρῶτος ἔρως.  
> (The supreme Wisdom, and primal love.)

- *Θεία Δύναμις ἐδημιούργησέ με* puts the first subject before the verb, whereas *fecemi* precedes all three subjects. *Ἐδημιούργησέ* is the augmented 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *δημιουργέω* ‘fashion’; its additional acute is due to the following enclitic accusative *με*, corresponding to the attached *-mi*.
- *Ἡ μεγίστη Σοφία, καὶ πρῶτος ἔρως* continues the series in the nominative after the singular verb. *Μεγίστη* is the fem. nom. sg. superlative of *μέγας*, agreeing with *Σοφία*; *πρῶτος* agrees with masc. *ἔρως*. Musurus keeps the single conjunction before the last member, but of the three Italian articles (*la … la … il*) only the middle one has a Greek counterpart.

> 7 Dinanzi a me non fur cose create,  
> 8 se non eterne, ed io eterno duro:  

Before me were no things created, but eternal; and eternal I endure:

> 7 Οὐδέν ποτ᾽ ἦν οὐδαμοῦ πρὶν ἐμοῦ κτίσμα,  
> (Never was there anywhere any creature before me,)
>
> 8 Πλὴν αἰωνίων, ὧν εἷς κἀγὼ τυγχάνω·  
> (Save eternal things, of which I too am one;)

- *Οὐδέν ποτ᾽ ἦν οὐδαμοῦ πρὶν ἐμοῦ κτίσμα* ‘no created thing ever existed anywhere before me’ separates neut. sg. *οὐδέν* from its noun *κτίσμα* (from *κτίζω* ‘create’). The Italian plural passive *non fur cose create* becomes existential *ἦν* with a singular noun denoting the result. *Ποτ᾽* is elided enclitic *ποτε* ‘ever’; the further negative *οὐδαμοῦ* ‘nowhere’ reinforces, rather than cancels, the negation. Neither time nor place is made so emphatic in Italian.
- *Πρὶν ἐμοῦ* uses *πρίν* prepositionally with the full genitive pronoun ‘before me’, a less usual construction than *πρό* with the genitive. *Πλὴν αἰωνίων* ‘except eternal ones’ gives *se non eterne* by *πλήν* governing a genitive plural with the noun understood.
- *Ὧν εἷς κἀγὼ τυγχάνω* ‘of which I too am one’ subordinates what is an independent assertion in *ed io eterno duro*. *Ὧν* is a partitive relative genitive dependent on *εἷς*; *κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*. *Τυγχάνω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.) with a predicate nominative has the force ‘I happen to be’, not ‘I endure’. The masculine *εἷς* presents the speaker as ‘one’; it does not agree in gender with neuter *κτίσμα* or feminine *πύλη*.

> 9 lasciate ogni speranza, voi ch’ entrate.”  

leave all hope, ye that enter.”

> 9 Ἄφες πᾶς ὧδ᾽ εἰσιὼν πᾶσαν ἐλπίδα.  
> (Abandon, every one entering here, all hope.)

- *Ἄφες* is the 2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *ἀφίημι* ‘let go’. The plural command *lasciate* and its plural addressees *voi ch’ entrate* are individualized: *πᾶς ὧδ᾽ εἰσιών* ‘each one entering here’ uses a nominative in address, with *εἰσιών* the masc. nom. sg. pres. act. participle of *εἴσειμι* ‘go in’. *Ὧδ᾽* is elided *ὧδε* ‘here’.
- The repeated adjective gives the line a correspondence absent from the Italian: *πᾶς* for each entrant, *πᾶσαν ἐλπίδα* for *ogni speranza*. The latter phrase is the fem. acc. sg. object of the imperative.

> 10 Queste parole di colore oscuro  
> 11 vid’ io scritte al sommo d’ una porta;  
> 12 per ch’io: “Maestro, il senso lor m’è duro.”  

These words, of colour obscure, saw I written above a gate; whereat I: “Master, their meaning to me is hard.”

> 10 Τοὺς λόγους τούτους διὰ χροιᾶς μελαίνης  
> (These words in dark colour)
>
> 11 Γεγραμμένους ἀναγνοὺς ἐπὶ πυλῶνος,  
> (Written upon a gateway, when I read them,)
>
> 12 Εἶπον· “ Προστάτα, σκληρὸς δοκεῖ τῶν λόγων  
> (I said: "Master, harsh seems of these words)

- *Τοὺς λόγους τούτους* ‘these words’ keeps the fronted object; Greek places the demonstrative outside the article–noun group. *Διὰ χροιᾶς μελαίνης* ‘by means of dark colour’ renders descriptive *di colore oscuro* as a phrase of means with *διά* and the genitive, attached to the writing. *Μελαίνης* is gen. sg. fem. of *μέλας*.
- *Γεγραμμένους* (acc. pl. masc. perf. pass. participle of *γράφω*) agrees with *λόγους*, as *scritte* agrees with *parole*, and describes the resulting inscription. *Ἐπὶ πυλῶνος* ‘on a gateway’ uses *ἐπί* with the genitive of position, without specifying *al sommo* ‘at the top’.
- Greek shifts the main verb from seeing to speaking: *ἀναγνούς* ‘having read’ (nom. sg. masc. second aor. act. participle of *ἀναγιγνώσκω*) subordinates *vid’ io*, while *Εἶπον* ‘I said’ (1st sg. second aor. act. ind., the aorist used with *λέγω*) supplies the verb left unspoken in *per ch’io*. The sequence of participle and finite verb does the work of *per che* without an explicit causal connective.
- *Προστάτα* is the vocative singular of *προστάτης* ‘protector’, not a literal equivalent of *Maestro*. The Greek speech runs into line 13: *σκληρὸς δοκεῖ τῶν λόγων τούτων ὁ νοῦς* ‘hard seems the meaning of these words’. Predicate *σκληρός* comes first and subject *ὁ νοῦς* last; *τῶν λόγων τούτων* spells out possessive *lor*. *Δοκεῖ* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *δοκέω*) replaces *è* with ‘seems’, leaving the experiencer *mi* unexpressed.

> 13 Ed egli a me, come persona accorta:  

And he to me, as one experienced:

> 13 „Τούτων ὁ νοῦς.“ Ὁ δ᾽ ἅτε συνετὸς ἔφη·  
> (The meaning." And he, as one prudent, spoke:)

- After the end of the preceding speech, *Ὁ δ᾽* ‘and he’ uses the article pronominally with postpositive *δέ*, elided before *ἅτε*. *Ἔφη* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *φημί*) supplies a verb of speaking for *Ed egli a me*; the recipient *a me* is not separately rendered.
- *Ἅτε συνετός* ‘as being discerning’ dispenses with the noun *persona*: *συνετός* is masc. nom. sg., referring directly to the speaker. *Ἅτε* presents discernment as an actual characteristic accounting for the reply, rather than merely comparing him to someone else.

> 14 “Qui si convien lasciare ogni sospetto;  

“Here must all distrust be left;

> 14 “Πᾶσαν ὧδ᾽ ὑπόνοιαν ἐᾷν προσήκει·  
> ("All suspicion here it behoves to leave behind;)

- *Πᾶσαν ὧδ᾽ ὑπόνοιαν ἐᾷν προσήκει* ‘all suspicion here it behoves one to leave’ retains the active infinitive of *lasciare*, not the passive construction of the English. *Προσήκει* (impersonal 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *προσήκω*) comes last and takes *ἐᾷν* (contracted pres. act. infinitive of *ἐάω*) as its complement, with an indefinite agent.
- *Πᾶσαν … ὑπόνοιαν*, the accusative object, places *ogni* first, separated from its noun by elided *ὧδ᾽* ‘here’. *Ὑπόνοια* gives *sospetto* the sense ‘suspicion, misgiving’.

> 15 ogni viltà convien che qui sia morta.  

all cowardice must here be dead.

> 15 „Πᾶν αἴσθημ᾽ ἀνανδρίας ὧδε θανέτω.  
> (Every feeling of cowardice here let die.)

- *Πᾶν αἴσθημ᾽ ἀνανδρίας* ‘every feeling of cowardice’ expands *ogni viltà*: *πᾶν* agrees with neut. nom. sg. *αἴσθημα* (final vowel elided), while *ἀνανδρίας* is a dependent gen. sg. specifying the feeling.
- *Ὧδε θανέτω* ‘here let it die’ replaces the second *convien* and its subordinate *che … sia morta* with a direct command. *Θανέτω* is the 3rd sg. second aor. act. imperative of *θνῄσκω*: it calls for the event ‘die’, rather than the state ‘be dead’.

> 16 Noi siam venuti al luogo ov’ io t’ ho detto  
> 17 che tu vedrai le genti dolorose,  
> 18 ch’ hanno perduto il ben dello intelletto.”  

We are come to the place where I told thee thou shouldst see the wretched people, who have lost the good of the intellect.”

> 16 „Εἰς τὸν τόπον ἤλθομεν, ἔνθ᾽ ἔλεγόν σε  
> (To the place we have come, where I told you)
>
> 17 „Ὄψεσθαι ψυχὰς ἀλγεινὰς καὶ δυστήνους,  
> (You would see sorrowful and wretched souls,)
>
> 18 „Ἀπολεσάσας τὸ νοητικὸν δῶρον.“  
> (Who have lost the intellectual gift.")

- *Εἰς τὸν τόπον ἤλθομεν* fronts the destination and leaves *noi* to the verb ending. *Ἤλθομεν* (1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*) gives the arrival as an event, in place of *siam venuti*. *Ἔνθ᾽* is relative *ἔνθα* ‘where’, elided before *ἔλεγον*.
- *Ἔλεγόν σε ὄψεσθαι* ‘I was saying that you would see’ recasts *io t’ ho detto che tu vedrai* as indirect discourse. *Ἔλεγον* is 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *λέγω*, with the additional accent before enclitic *σε*. The accusative *σε* is the subject of *ὄψεσθαι*, the future middle infinitive used with *ὁράω* ‘see’, not a dative recipient corresponding to *t’*. The future retains the prospect expressed by *vedrai*.
- *Ψυχὰς ἀλγεινὰς καὶ δυστήνους* ‘sorrowful and wretched souls’ replaces *genti* with *ψυχάς* and doubles the single epithet *dolorose*. Both adjectives are fem. acc. pl.; *δυστήνους* is from two-termination *δύστηνος*.
- *Ἀπολεσάσας* (fem. acc. pl. aor. act. participle of *ἀπόλλυμι* ‘lose’) agrees with *ψυχάς*, replacing the finite relative clause *ch’ hanno perduto* and marking the loss as anterior to the seeing. Its object *τὸ νοητικὸν δῶρον* ‘the intellectual gift’ interprets *ben* as a gift and changes the dependent noun *dello intelletto* into the attributive adjective *νοητικόν*.

> 19 E poichè la sua mano alla mia pose,  
> 20 con lieto volto, ond’ io mi confortai,  
> 21 mi mise dentro alle segrete cose.  

And placing his hand on mine, with a cheerful countenance that comforted me, he led me into the secret things.

> 19 Ἔπειτα δ᾽ ἐνθεὶς τὴν χεῖρα τῇ χειρί μου,  
> (And then, placing his hand in my hand,)
>
> 20 Προσώπῳ φαιδρῷ καὶ θαῤῥεῖν με ποιοῦντι,  
> (With a cheerful countenance that made me take courage,)
>
> 21 Εἰς τὰ μυστικὰ θεάματ᾽ ἤγαγέ με.  
> (Into the secret sights he led me.)

- *Ἔπειτα δ᾽ ἐνθείς* ‘and then, having put’ replaces *E poichè … pose* with a sequence adverb, elided connective *δέ*, and a circumstantial participle. *Ἐνθείς* (masc. nom. sg. second aor. act. participle of *ἐντίθημι*) shares the subject of *ἤγαγέ* and marks the gesture as prior to the leading.
- *Τὴν χεῖρα τῇ χειρί μου* ‘the hand in my hand’ gives *la sua mano* by the article alone, the possessor being clear from the subject. The dative *τῇ χειρί* completes the compound verb and repeats the noun left implicit in *alla mia*; enclitic genitive *μου* marks the second hand’s possessor. *Χεῖρα* and *χειρί* are accusative and dative singular of *χείρ*.
- *Προσώπῳ φαιδρῷ* ‘with a cheerful face’ renders *con lieto volto* by a dative of manner without a preposition. *Καὶ … ποιοῦντι* coordinates a participle with the adjective *φαιδρῷ*: both are dat. sg. neut., qualifying *προσώπῳ*. Thus *ond’ io mi confortai* becomes a second description of the face, ‘and making me take courage’. *Ποιοῦντι* is the contracted pres. act. participle of *ποιέω*; *με* is the accusative subject of its complementary infinitive *θαῤῥεῖν* (pres. act. of Attic *θαρρέω*, beside *θαρσέω*). The opposed breathings on *ῤῥ* are a traditional spelling of medial double rho.
- *Εἰς τὰ μυστικὰ θεάματ᾽ ἤγαγέ με* ‘into the secret sights he led me’ fronts the goal that follows *mi mise* in Italian. *Εἰς* with the accusative gives *dentro alle*; elided *θεάματ᾽* is *θεάματα* (acc. pl. of *θέαμα* ‘sight’), more specific than *cose*. *Ἤγαγέ* is the augmented, reduplicated second aor. act. ind., 3rd sg., of *ἄγω* ‘lead’, with an additional acute before enclitic *με*.

## Lines 22–54 — The Lament of the Blameless and Unpraised

> 22 Quivi sospiri, pianti, e alti guai  
> 23 risonavan per l' aer senza stelle,  
> 24 per ch' io al cominciar ne lagrimai.  

Here sighs, plaints, and deep wailings resounded through the starless air : it made me weep at first.

> 22 Ἐκεῖ στεναγμοί, κλαυθμοί, γόοι μυρίοι  
> (There sighs, weepings, countless lamentations)
>
> 23 Ἀντεπίαχον δι᾽ ἀέρος ἀνάστρου,  
> (Resounded through the starless air,)
>
> 24 Ἐφ᾽ οἷς δακρύειν ἐγὼ πρῶτον ἠρξάμην.  
> (At which I at first began to weep.)

- *Ἐκεῖ* ‘there’ stands first, like *Quivi*. The three sounds are nominative plurals and together supply the subject of *Ἀντεπίαχον*. Musurus drops the conjunction in *e alti guai*: *γόοι μυρίοι* ‘countless lamentations’ gives number rather than loudness, and puts the adjective after the noun.
- *Ἀντεπίαχον* is a poetic third-person plural past indicative of the *ἰάχω* ‘cry, ring out’ family; the compound suggests sound answering sound, for the imperfect *risonavan*. *δι᾽* is *διά* elided before a vowel; it takes the genitive *ἀέρος* (from *ἀήρ*) for the space traversed. The agreeing adjective *ἀνάστρου* ‘starless’ replaces *senza stelle*.
- *Ἐφ᾽ οἷς* ‘at which things’ renders *per ch’* by *ἐπί* with a causal dative; elision before the rough breathing of *οἷς* gives *φ* for *π*. The neuter plural relative gathers up the preceding sounds. *δακρύειν* (present active infinitive of *δακρύω*) depends on *ἠρξάμην* (first singular aorist middle indicative of *ἄρχομαι*). Greek thus makes ‘begin’ the finite verb and ‘weep’ its complement, the reverse of *al cominciar ne lagrimai*. *ἐγώ* retains *io*, and adverbial *πρῶτον* adds ‘at first’.

> 25 Diverse lingue, orribili favelle,  
> 26 parole di dolore, accenti d' ira,  
> 27 voci alte e fioche, e suon di man con elle,  

Strange tongues, horrible outcries, words of pain, tones of anger, voices deep and hoarse, and sounds of hands amongst them,

> 25 Γλῶσσαι ποικίλαι, φρικώδεις ὁμιλίαι,  
> (Diverse tongues, dreadful speeches,)
>
> 26 Τῆς λύπης ὀλολυγμοί, τῆς ὀργῆς τόνοι,  
> (Shrieks of grief, accents of wrath,)
>
> 27 Ὀξυφωνίαι βραγχαὶ καὶ χειρῶν κτύπος  
> (Shrill hoarse cries, and beating of hands)

- *Γλῶσσαι ποικίλαι, φρικώδεις ὁμιλίαι* reproduces both pairs of feminine plurals in *Diverse lingue, orribili favelle*, but puts *ποικίλαι* ‘various’ after its noun. *ὁμιλίαι* denotes utterances or talk, not specifically cries.
- *Τῆς λύπης ὀλολυγμοί, τῆς ὀργῆς τόνοι* fronts the dependent genitive in each pair, reversing *parole di dolore, accenti d’ ira*. *ὀλολυγμοί* are shrieks rather than articulate *parole*; *τόνοι* keeps the vocal sense of *accenti*.
- *Ὀξυφωνίαι βραγχαί* ‘hoarse shrill cries’ makes the high pitch of *voci alte* part of the noun; the feminine plural adjective describes the same cries, rather than coordinating *alte e fioche*. *χειρῶν κτύπος* ‘striking sound of hands’ likewise puts the genitive (plural of *χείρ*, not a dual) first. *καί* joins this last, singular subject to the list; there is no separate equivalent of *con elle*.

> 28 facevano un tumulto, il qual s' aggira  
> 29 sempre in quell' aria senza tempo tinta,  
> 30 come la rena quando a turbo spira.  

made a tumult, which turns itself unceasing in that air for ever dyed, as sand when [it eddies in a whirlwind].

> 28 Ἀπετέλουν θόρυβον περιδινοῦντα  
> (Made up a tumult whirling round)
>
> 29 ᾈεὶ περὶ τὸν ἄχρουν αἰθέρ᾽ ἐκεῖνον,  
> (Always about that colourless air,)
>
> 30 Ὡς ἄμμος ἐκ λαίλαπος συστρεφομένη.  
> (Like sand whirled together by a tempest.)

- *Ἀπετέλουν* (third plural imperfect active indicative of *ἀποτελέω*, with augment after the preverb) answers to *facevano*: the sounds ‘were producing’ *θόρυβον*, accusative singular. *περιδινοῦντα* is a present active participle of *περιδινέω* agreeing with that object: ‘a tumult whirling round’. It replaces the finite relative *il qual s’ aggira* without reproducing the reflexive.
- *ᾈεὶ* has the force of *ἀεί* ‘always’, for *sempre*. *περὶ τὸν ἄχρουν αἰθέρ᾽ ἐκεῖνον* gives motion ‘about that colourless air’ rather than location *in quell’ aria*. *ἄχρουν* is the contracted masculine accusative of *ἄχροος*; *αἰθέρ᾽* is elided *αἰθέρα*, from *αἰθήρ*. The demonstrative stands outside the article–adjective–noun group. ‘Colourless’ interprets the air's appearance rather than translating *tinta* ‘dyed’; *senza tempo* has no distinct equivalent.
- *Ὡς ἄμμος* supplies the comparison without a finite verb. Feminine nominative *συστρεφομένη* (present middle/passive participle of *συστρέφω*) agrees with *ἄμμος*: the sand is ‘being whirled together’. In place of the temporal clause *quando a turbo spira*, *ἐκ λαίλαπος* (genitive of *λαῖλαψ*) gives the storm as the source of the motion.

> 31 Ed io, ch' avea d' orror la testa cinta,  
> 32 dissi: “ Maestro, che è quel ch' i' odo?  
> 33 e che gente è, che par nel duol sì vinta? ”  

And I, my head begirt with horror, said : “Master, what is this that I hear? and who are these that seem so overcome with pain?”

> 31 Ἐγὼ δὲ τὸν νοῦν ὅλος ἰλιγγιάσας,  
> (And I, wholly dizzy in mind,)
>
> 32 Εἶπον· “Προστάτα, τί ἔσθ᾽ ὅπερ ἀκούω;  
> (Said: "Master, what is this that I hear?)
>
> 33 „ Καὶ τίνες οἱ πάσχοντες ὑπ᾽ ἀχθηδόνος;”  
> (And who are they that suffer under distress?")

- *Ἐγὼ δέ* reproduces *Ed io* with postpositive *δέ*. *ὅλος* is nominative, agreeing with ‘I’, though adverbial in effect (‘wholly’); *τὸν νοῦν* is an accusative of respect, ‘in mind’. *ἰλιγγιάσας* (nominative masculine singular aorist active participle of *ἰλιγγιάω*) gives a circumstance preceding *Εἶπον*: ‘having grown dizzy’. This is closer to a sense of bewilderment than to the supplied reading *d’ orror*: neither horror nor the encircling of the head is expressed.
- *Εἶπον* is the first singular second aorist active indicative used for ‘I said’. *Προστάτα*, vocative singular of *προστάτης*, addresses the *Maestro* as ‘guide’ or ‘protector’. *τί ἔσθ᾽ ὅπερ ἀκούω;* closely follows *che è quel ch’ i’ odo?*: *ἔσθ᾽* is *ἐστί* elided before a rough breathing, and the strengthened neuter relative *ὅπερ* ‘the very thing that’ is the object of present *ἀκούω*. There is no separate demonstrative for *quel*.
- *τίνες οἱ πάσχοντες* ‘who [are] the sufferers?’ has a plural interrogative, an understood copula, and an articular present active participle of *πάσχω*. These replace collective singular *che gente* and its relative clause. *ὑπ᾽ ἀχθηδόνος* ‘under the effect of distress’ uses *ὑπό* with the genitive of the cause; *par*, *sì*, and the image of being *vinta* disappear.

> 34 Ed egli a me: “ Questo misero modo  
> 35 tengon l' anime triste di coloro,  
> 36 che visser senza infamia e senza lodo.  

And he to me : “This miserable mode the dreary souls of those sustain, who lived without blame, and without praise.

> 34 Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ· “Τὴν οἰκτρὰν ταύτην τύχην  
> (And he to me: "This piteous lot)
>
> 35 „ Ἔλαχον αἱ λυπηραὶ ψυχαὶ τῶν, ὅσοι  
> (Have received the sorrowful souls of all who)
>
> 36 „ Ἔζησαν ἄνευ ψόγου κἄνευ ἐπαίνου.  
> (Lived without blame and without praise.)

- *Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ* ‘and he to me’ keeps the Italian ellipsis of ‘said’: *ὁ* is pronominal with *δέ*, and *πρός* takes the accusative of the person addressed. *Τὴν οἰκτρὰν ταύτην τύχην* keeps the object first but turns *misero modo* into a ‘piteous lot’. The placement of *ταύτην* between adjective and noun is less usual in Attic than placing the demonstrative outside the whole articular group.
- *Ἔλαχον* ‘obtained by lot’ (third plural second aorist active indicative of *λαγχάνω*) replaces present *tengon*: Greek says what lot the souls received, not what condition they sustain. Its postponed subject is *αἱ λυπηραὶ ψυχαί* ‘the sorrowful souls’.
- In *τῶν, ὅσοι* ‘of those, as many as’, *τῶν* stands substantivally in the genitive after *ψυχαί*, while masculine nominative plural *ὅσοι* is subject of *ἔζησαν* (third plural aorist active indicative of *ζάω*), for *che visser*. *ἄνευ* governs both genitives, *ψόγου* ‘censure’ and *ἐπαίνου* ‘praise’; *κἄνευ* is crasis of *καὶ ἄνευ*, retaining the repeated *senza*.

> 37 Mischiate sono a quel cattivo coro  
> 38 degli angeli che non furon ribelli,  
> 39 nè fur fedeli a Dio, ma per sè foro.  

They are mixed with that caitiff choir of the angels, who were not rebellious, nor were faithful to God; but were for themselves.

> 37 „ Εἰσὶ σύμμικτοι τῷ κακῷ χορῷ τούτῳ  
> (Mingled with this wicked band are)
>
> 38 „ Ἄγγελοι γεγονότες μήτ’ ἀποστάται  
> (Angels who were neither rebellious)
>
> 39 „ Μήτε πιστοί, πλὴν ἑαυτοῖς μόνοις φίλοι.  
> (Nor faithful, but friends to themselves alone.)

- *Εἰσὶ σύμμικτοι* replaces *Mischiate sono* with copula and adjective ‘are intermingled’; *τῷ κακῷ χορῷ τούτῳ* is the associative dative, with the demonstrative after the articular group. *τούτῳ* says ‘this’, not *quel* ‘that’.
- There is a syntactic shift across the line break. In Italian *degli angeli* depends on *coro*, and the souls are the subject of *Mischiate sono*. Greek does not write a dependent genitive ‘of angels’: *Ἄγγελοι* is nominative, naturally read back as the subject of *Εἰσὶ σύμμικτοι*. The effect is ‘mingled with this wicked choir are angels …’, reversing which group is said to be mixed with which.
- *γεγονότες* (masculine nominative plural perfect active participle of *γίγνομαι*) agrees with *Ἄγγελοι* and replaces the finite relative *che non furon … nè fur*. *μήτ᾽ … μήτε* ‘neither … nor’ negates the paired predicates in this participial characterization; the first *μήτε* is elided. *ἀποστάται* is a predicate noun ‘rebels’, *πιστοί* an adjective ‘faithful’; *a Dio* is not expressed. Adversative *πλήν* introduces *ἑαυτοῖς μόνοις φίλοι*: the reflexive dative depends on supplied *φίλοι*, making *per sè foro* into ‘friends to themselves alone’.

> 40 Cacciarli i ciel per non esser men belli,  
> 41 nè lo profondo inferno gli riceve,  
> 42 chè alcuna gloria i rei avrebber d' elli.”  

Heaven chased them forth to keep its beauty from impair; and the deep Hell receives them not, for the wicked would have some glory over them.”

> 40 „ Ἐξώσθησαν δ’ οὐρανῶν, οὓς ἐξημαύρουν,  
> (They were thrust out of the heavens, which they dimmed,)
>
> 41 „ Ἄδεκτοι δ’ ἐν τῷ βαθεῖ μένουσιν ᾍδῃ,  
> (And unreceived in deep Hell they remain,)
>
> 42 „ Ὡς μὴ πρὸς τούτων οἱ κακοὶ δοξασθῶσι.”  
> (Lest by them the wicked be glorified.")

- *Ἐξώσθησαν δ᾽ οὐρανῶν* ‘and they were driven out of the heavens’ turns the object of *Cacciarli* into the subject of a third plural aorist passive indicative (*ἐξωθέω*); *οὐρανῶν* is a genitive of separation, not the agent. *οὓς ἐξημαύρουν* ‘which they were dimming’ replaces the negative purpose *per non esser men belli* with a relative clause of actual effect. *οὕς* takes its masculine plural from ‘heavens’ but its accusative from *ἐξημαύρουν*, third plural imperfect active of *ἐξαμαυρόω*.
- *Ἄδεκτοι δ᾽ … μένουσιν* makes non-reception a continuing condition: ‘and they remain unaccepted’. The negative in *nè … riceve* passes into the predicate adjective *ἄδεκτοι*; the Italian subject becomes *ἐν τῷ βαθεῖ … ᾍδῃ*, a phrase of location with masculine dative singular *βαθεῖ* (from *βαθύς*) agreeing with *ᾍδῃ*. The Greek locates their being unaccepted ‘in deep Hades’, rather than making Hell the agent that refuses them.
- *Ὡς μὴ … δοξασθῶσι* ‘lest … be glorified’ is a negative purpose clause with the third plural aorist passive subjunctive of *δοξάζω*, not an optative or a conditional. It recasts causal *chè* and conditional *avrebber*: ‘having some glory’ becomes a passive verb, without *alcuna*. *οἱ κακοί* ‘the wicked’ is its subject, and *πρὸς τούτων* uses *πρός* with the genitive for the persons from whom the glory would come.

> 43 Ed io: “ Maestro, che è tanto greve  
> 44 a lor, che lamentar gli fa sì forte? ”  

And I : “Master what is so grievous to them, that makes them lament thus bitterly?”

> 43 Κἀγώ· “ Προστάτα, τί τὸ βαρύνον πάθος  
> (And I: "Master, what is the suffering weighing)
>
> 44 „ Αὐτοὺς καὶ ποιοῦν θρηνεῖν οὑτωσὶ σφόδρα;”  
> (Upon them that makes them lament so bitterly?")

- *Κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*; the verb of saying is again omitted. After the vocative *Προστάτα*, *τί τὸ βαρύνον πάθος* asks ‘what [is] the affliction that weighs …?’ The supplied noun *πάθος* is neuter singular; *βαρύνον* is an attributive present active participle of *βαρύνω*. Thus adjectival *greve a lor* becomes transitive ‘weighing them down’, with accusative *Αὐτούς*.
- *καὶ ποιοῦν* (neuter singular present active participle of *ποιέω*) is coordinated with *βαρύνον*, not a finite result clause corresponding to *che … fa*. *Αὐτούς* also supplies the persons made to lament; *θρηνεῖν* is the present active infinitive of *θρηνέω*. Emphatic *οὑτωσί* ‘thus’ and *σφόδρα* ‘vehemently’ concentrate the degree in *θρηνεῖν οὑτωσὶ σφόδρα*, rather than distributing it between *tanto greve* and *sì forte*.

> 45 Rispose: “ Dicerolti molto breve.  

He answered : “I will tell it to thee very briefly.

> 45 Ὁ δέ, “ Διὰ βραχέων,” εἶπεν, “ ἐρῶ σοι·  
> (And he: "In brief," he said, "I will tell you:)

- *Ὁ δέ … εἶπεν* ‘and he … said’ renders *Rispose* with pronominal *ὁ* and third singular second aorist active *εἶπεν*. Musurus inserts the reporting verb inside the speech, fronting *Διὰ βραχέων* ‘in few [words]’, an idiom with *διά* and a substantival genitive plural. There is no separate intensifier for *molto*.
- *ἐρῶ σοι* has the irregular first singular future active indicative used for ‘I shall say’ and the enclitic dative of the addressee. Of the two pronouns attached to *Dicerolti*, only *ti* is separately rendered: the thing to be told is understood.

> 46 Questi non hanno speranza di morte,  

These have no hope of death;

> 46 „ Οὐκ ἔχουσ’ οὗτοι τὴν τοῦ θανεῖν ἐλπίδα·  
> (These have not the hope of dying;)

- *Οὐκ ἔχουσ᾽ οὗτοι* fronts the negated verb rather than *Questi*: *ἔχουσ᾽* is elided *ἔχουσι*, third plural present active indicative of *ἔχω*, not a participle. *τὴν τοῦ θανεῖν ἐλπίδα* encloses the dependent genitive within article and noun. For nominal *di morte* Musurus uses an articular infinitive: *τοῦ θανεῖν* ‘of dying’, with the second aorist active infinitive of *θνῄσκω*. The aorist presents dying as an event, not as past relative to the hope.

> 47 e la lor cieca vita è tanto bassa,  
> 48 che invidiosi son d' ogni altra sorte.  

and their blind life is so mean, that they are envious of every other lot.

> 47 „ Αὐτῶν δ’ ὁ βίος ἐστὶ ταπεινὸς οὕτως,  
> (And their life is so lowly)
>
> 48 „ Ὥστε ποθοῦσι πᾶσαν ἑτέραν τύχην.  
> (That they covet every other lot.)

- *Αὐτῶν δ᾽ ὁ βίος* puts possessive *αὐτῶν* first, with postpositive, elided *δέ* for *e*. *cieca* is omitted. Predicate *ταπεινός* ‘lowly’ agrees with masculine *βίος* rather than reproducing the feminine of *vita … bassa*.
- *οὕτως, Ὥστε ποθοῦσι* corresponds to *tanto … che*: *ὥστε* with the indicative states the result as a fact. *ποθοῦσι* (third plural present active indicative of *ποθέω*) means ‘they long for’; *invidiosi son d’* thus becomes a transitive verb with accusative *πᾶσαν ἑτέραν τύχην* ‘every other lot’.

> 49 Fama di loro il mondo esser non lassa,  

Report of them the world permits not to exist;

> 49 „ Οὐκ ἔμειν’ ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ φήμη τις τούτων·  
> (No fame of them has remained in the world;)

- *Οὐκ ἔμειν᾽* is elided *οὐκ ἔμεινε* ‘did not remain’, third singular aorist active indicative of *μένω*, with augment and the *ει* of its liquid aorist. Italian has present *non lassa* with an infinitive *esser*, the world as subject and fame as the thing not suffered to exist. Greek makes the world a location (*ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ*) and the report the subject of a past-tense verb.
- *φήμη τις τούτων* ‘any report of these’ is postponed, unlike initial *Fama di loro*. Enclitic *τις* is an indefinite nominative singular agreeing with *φήμη*: under the negation, not even an unspecified report remained. *τούτων* is a genitive of the persons reported on.

> 50 misericordia e giustizia gli sdegna :  

Mercy and [Justice] disdains them

> 50 „ Οὐκ εἰσ’ ἄξιοι δικαιοσύνης κ’ οἴκτου.  
> (They are not worthy of justice and of pity.)

- *Οὐκ εἰσ᾽ ἄξιοι* ‘they are not worthy’ reverses the construction of *misericordia e giustizia gli sdegna*: the persons disdained are now the subject, and disdain is recast as a negated judgment of worth. *εἰσ᾽* is elided *εἰσί*, third plural present indicative of *εἰμί*; predicate *ἄξιοι* agrees with its unexpressed masculine plural subject.
- *δικαιοσύνης κ᾽ οἴκτου* gives the genitives governed by *ἄξιοι*. The order of the abstractions is reversed, ‘of justice and pity’; *κ᾽* is a shortened spelling of *καί* before a vowel.

> 51 non ragioniam di lor, ma guarda e passa.”  

: let us not speak of them; but look, and pass.”

> 51 „ Μὴ μελέτω σοι τούτων· βλέπε κἀπέρχου.”  
> (Take no heed of them; look and pass on.")

- *Μὴ μελέτω σοι τούτων* ‘let there be no concern to you about these’ replaces first plural hortatory *non ragioniam* with the impersonal third singular present active imperative of *μέλω*. *μή* negates the injunction; *σοι* is the dative of the person concerned, *τούτων* the genitive of the matter of concern. Caring replaces speaking, and the injunction is addressed to ‘you’ alone.
- *βλέπε κἀπέρχου* retains the two second singular commands: *βλέπε* is present active imperative of *βλέπω*, *ἀπέρχου* present middle imperative of *ἀπέρχομαι* ‘go away’. *κἀπέρχου* is crasis of *καὶ ἀπέρχου*. The turn marked by *ma* is left to the break between commands; *καί* renders the *e* joining them.

> 52 Ed io, che riguardai, vidi una insegna,  
> 53 che girando correva tanto ratta,  
> 54 che d' ogni posa mi pareva indegna ;  

And I, who looked, saw an ensign, which whirling ran so quickly that it seemed to scorn all pause;

> 52 Ἐγὼ τότ’ εἶδον ἀτενίσας σημαίαν  
> (Then, gazing intently, I saw a banner)
>
> 53 Δινοῦσαν καὶ τρέχουσαν οὕτως ὠκέως,  
> (Whirling and running so swiftly,)
>
> 54 Ὥστ᾽ ἀπαξιοῦν πᾶσαν ἐδόκει παῦλαν.  
> (That it seemed to disdain all pause.)

- *Ἐγὼ τότ᾽ εἶδον* ‘I then saw’ supplies *τότ᾽* (elided *τότε*) instead of initial *Ed*. *εἶδον* is the first singular second aorist active indicative used for ‘I saw’. Following it, *ἀτενίσας* (nominative masculine singular aorist active participle of *ἀτενίζω*) replaces the finite relative *che riguardai*: ‘having looked intently’. Anarthrous accusative *σημαίαν* is the object, for *una insegna*.
- *Δινοῦσαν καὶ τρέχουσαν* are feminine accusative singular present active participles of *δινέω* and *τρέχω*, agreeing with the banner as the object seen in motion. Italian subordinates the whirling (*girando*) to the running (*che … correva*); Greek coordinates the two as simultaneous perceptions. *οὕτως ὠκέως* ‘so swiftly’ uses the uncontracted poetic adverb *ὠκέως* from *ὠκύς*.
- *Ὥστ᾽* is elided *ὥστε*, introducing the result of *οὕτως*, again with a finite indicative: *ἐδόκει* ‘it seemed’ (third singular imperfect active of *δοκέω*). The infinitive *ἀπαξιοῦν* (present active of *ἀπαξιόω*) depends on *ἐδόκει*, not directly on *ὥστε*. Musurus interprets *d’ ogni posa … indegna* actively: the banner seemed ‘to disdain every pause’, with *πᾶσαν … παῦλαν* as the accusative object. The perceiver expressed by *mi* is left understood.

## Lines 55–87 — The Train of Shades and the Ferryman

> 55 e dietro le venia sì lunga tratta  
> 56 di gente, ch’io non avrei mai creduto,  
> 57 che morte tanta n’avesse disfatta.  

and behind it came so long a train of people, that I should never have believed death had undone so many.

> 55 Ἐξόπισθεν δὲ σειρὰ μακρὰ τοιαύτη  
> (And behind it so long a train)
>
> 56 Ἤρχετ᾽ ἀνθρώπων, ὥστ᾽ οὐδέποτ᾽ ᾠόμην  
> (Of people followed, that I had never thought)
>
> 57 Τὸν θάνατον τοσούτους ἐξαφανίσαι.  
> (Death had destroyed so many.)

- *Ἐξόπισθεν δέ* ‘and behind’ renders *e dietro le*: the adverb gives the position, but the object of *dietro*, expressed by the Italian pronoun *le*, is left to be understood. Connective *δέ* takes its usual second position.
- *σειρὰ μακρὰ τοιαύτη ἤρχετ᾽ ἀνθρώπων* ‘so long a train of people was coming’ puts the subject before the verb, unlike *venia sì lunga tratta di gente*. *σειρά* is literally a ‘cord’ or ‘chain’; the genitive plural *ἀνθρώπων*, separated from it by the verb, replaces the collective singular *gente*. *ἤρχετ᾽* is elided *ἤρχετο*, 3rd sg. imperfect middle indicative of *ἔρχομαι*, matching the continuing movement of *venia*.
- *τοιαύτη … ὥστε* correlates the description of the train with its result. After *ὥστ᾽* (elided *ὥστε*), Musurus uses an indicative of actual result: *οὐδέποτ᾽ ᾠόμην* ‘I never supposed’, with *οὐδέποτε* elided and *ᾠόμην* the 1st sg. imperfect middle indicative of *οἴομαι*. The potential force of *non avrei mai creduto* is not separately marked.
- *τὸν θάνατον τοσούτους ἐξαφανίσαι* ‘that death had made so many disappear’ is accusative and infinitive after the verb of supposing, in place of *che morte tanta n’avesse disfatta*. *τὸν θάνατον* is the infinitive’s subject; *τοσούτους*, masculine accusative plural used substantivally, is its object. The aorist active infinitive of *ἐξαφανίζω* represents the work of death as accomplished; there is no separate counterpart to partitive *n’*.

> 58 Poscia ch’io v’ebbi alcun riconosciuto,  
> 59 vidi e conobbi l’ombra di colui  
> 60 che fece per viltate il gran rifiuto.  

After I had recognised some amongst them, I [saw and knew] the shadow of him who from cowardice made the great refusal.

> 58 Ὡς δ᾽ ἔγνων τούτων τινάς, τότ᾽ ἀτενίσας  
> (When I had recognized some of them, then gazing intently)
>
> 59 Ἀνθρώπου γνωστοῦ τὴν σκιὰν εἶδον, ὅστις  
> (I saw the shade of a familiar man, who)
>
> 60 Ἔπραξ᾽ ἄρνησιν ἀγεννῶς τὴν μεγάλην.  
> (Cowardly made the great refusal.)

- Temporal *ὡς δ᾽ ἔγνων* ‘and when I recognized’ replaces *Poscia ch’io v’ebbi … riconosciuto* with a simple second aorist active indicative (1st sg. of *γιγνώσκω*). *τούτων τινάς* ‘some of these’ makes the relation explicitly partitive: genitive plural of *οὗτος* with accusative plural of indefinite *τις*, in place of Italian *v’ … alcun*.
- *τότ᾽ ἀτενίσας … εἶδον* ‘then, looking intently, I saw’ answers *ὡς* with *τότε* and supplies a preliminary act of looking. *ἀτενίσας* is the nominative masculine singular aorist active participle of *ἀτενίζω*, agreeing with the speaker; *εἶδον* is the 1st sg. second aorist active indicative serving *ὁράω*. Of *vidi e conobbi*, only the seeing remains a finite verb: recognition becomes a quality of the man in *ἀνθρώπου γνωστοῦ* ‘of a man known [to me]’, a possessive genitive placed before *τὴν σκιάν*.
- *ὅστις* ‘who’ is masculine nominative singular, referring to the man rather than the feminine *σκιάν*. *ἔπραξ᾽* (elided *ἔπραξε*, 3rd sg. aorist active indicative of *πράσσω*) takes *ἄρνησιν … τὴν μεγάλην*. This keeps the verb and abstract noun of *fece … il gran rifiuto*; the postposed article and adjective single out the refusal as ‘the great one’. The adverb *ἀγεννῶς* ‘ignobly’ replaces the causal noun phrase *per viltate*.

> 61 Incontanente intesi, e certo fui,  
> 62 che quest’era la setta dei cattivi,  
> 63 a Dio spiacenti ed a’ nemici sui.  

Forthwith I understood and felt assured, that this was the crew of caitiffs, hateful to God and to his enemies.

> 61 Εὐθὺς κατενόησα, καλῶς τ᾽ ἐπείσθην,  
> (Straightway I understood, and was well assured,)
>
> 62 Ὡς ἥδε μερὶς ἀνθρώπων ἦν δυστήνων,  
> (That this was the sect of wretched men,)
>
> 63 Ἀπεχθῶν Θεῷ καὶ Θεοῦ πολεμίοις.  
> (Hateful to God and to God's enemies.)

- *εὐθὺς κατενόησα* closely follows *Incontanente intesi*: *κατενόησα* is the 1st sg. aorist active indicative of *κατανοέω*, with the augment after the prefix. *καλῶς τ᾽ ἐπείσθην* ‘and I was thoroughly convinced’ turns *e certo fui* into an aorist passive (1st sg. of *πείθω*) qualified by an adverb. Postpositive *τε*, elided before *ἐπείσθην*, coordinates the two predicates.
- *ὡς* introduces the content of the understanding and conviction. *ἥδε μερίς* ‘this company’ joins the demonstrative to the feminine nominative noun, where *quest’era la setta* has a pronominal subject and a predicate noun. *ἦν* is 3rd sg. imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*; *ἀνθρώπων … δυστήνων* ‘of wretched people’ depends on *μερίς*, replacing substantival *dei cattivi* with a noun and adjective.
- *ἀπεχθῶν* ‘hateful’ is likewise genitive plural, agreeing with *ἀνθρώπων*, not with *μερίς*. It governs both datives, *Θεῷ* and *πολεμίοις* ‘to enemies’; *Θεοῦ* before the latter repeats the noun as a possessive genitive where *a’ nemici sui* has a possessive adjective.

> 64 Questi sciaurati, che mai non fur vivi,  
> 65 erano ignudi e stimolati molto  
> 66 da mosconi e da vespe ch’eran ivi.  

These unfortunate, who never were alive, were naked, and sorely goaded by hornets and by wasps that were there.

> 64 Οἱ μήποτ᾽ ὄντες ζωοὶ τάλανες οὗτοι  
> (These wretches, who had never been alive,)
>
> 65 Ἦσαν πάγγυμνοι καὶ νυγμοῖς τετρωμένοι  
> (Were stark naked and wounded by stings)
>
> 66 Βρυουσῶν ἐκεῖ σφηκῶν τε καὶ μυώπων.  
> (Of wasps and gadflies swarming there.)

- *οἱ μήποτ᾽ ὄντες ζωοί* ‘those never being alive’ makes *che mai non fur vivi* an articular participial expression. *ὄντες* is the nominative masculine plural present participle of *εἰμί*, with predicate adjective *ζωοί* (poetic *ζωός*, ‘alive’); *μήποτε*, elided, negatives the description. *τάλανες οὗτοι* ‘these wretches’, also nominative plural, delays the equivalent of the opening *Questi sciaurati* until after that description.
- *ἦσαν πάγγυμνοι* ‘they were entirely naked’ strengthens *erano ignudi* by the compound adjective. *νυγμοῖς τετρωμένοι* ‘wounded by stings’ shares the auxiliary *ἦσαν*: the perfect middle-passive participle of *τιτρώσκω*, nominative masculine plural, describes the resulting wounds rather than the ongoing goading of *stimolati molto*. *νυγμοῖς* is instrumental dative plural of *νυγμός*; *molto* has no distinct modifier here.
- *βρυουσῶν ἐκεῖ σφηκῶν τε καὶ μυώπων* gives the possessors or sources of the stings in the genitive, rather than attaching agents directly to the passive as in *da mosconi e da vespe*. *σφηκῶν τε καὶ μυώπων* ‘of wasps and gadflies’ reverses the order of the insects. *βρυουσῶν* (gen. fem. pl. present active participle of *βρύω*) turns *ch’eran ivi* into ‘abounding there’. Its feminine agreement sits awkwardly with the ordinarily masculine nouns *σφήξ* and *μύωψ*.

> 67 Elle rigavan lor di sangue il volto,  
> 68 che mischiato di lagrime a’ lor piedi  
> 69 da fastidiosi vermi era ricolto.  

These made their faces stream with blood, which mixed with tears was gathered at their feet by loathsome worms.

> 67 Ἐκ τῶν παρειῶν δ᾽ αὐτῶν ἔῤῥεεν αἷμα,  
> (And from their cheeks flowed blood,)
>
> 68 Ὃ τοῖς ποσὶ σύμμικτον δάκρυσι πίπτον  
> (Which, mingled with tears, falling to their feet,)
>
> 69 Ὑπὸ σκωλήκων φρικτῶν ἀπεῤῥοφᾶτο.  
> (Was sucked up by loathsome worms.)

- *ἐκ τῶν παρειῶν δ᾽ αὐτῶν ἔῤῥεεν αἷμα* ‘and from their cheeks blood flowed’ reverses the construction of *Elle rigavan lor di sangue il volto*: the insects cease to be the subject, and blood becomes the subject of an intransitive verb. *ἐκ* with the genitive marks the source; possessive *αὐτῶν* replaces *lor*, and plural *παρειῶν* ‘cheeks’ particularizes singular *volto*. *ἔῤῥεεν* is the uncontracted 3rd sg. imperfect active indicative of *ῥέω*, with initial rho doubled after the augment.
- Relative *ὅ* is neuter nominative singular, referring to *αἷμα*. Both *σύμμικτον* ‘mingled’ (a two-termination adjective) and *πίπτον* ‘falling’ (present active participle of *πίπτω*) agree with it. *δάκρυσι*, dative plural of *δάκρυον*, goes with *σύμμικτον*; *τοῖς ποσὶ*, dative plural of *πούς*, gives the place of the fall. The added *πίπτον* makes a movement out of Italian *a’ lor piedi*; the possessive is not repeated.
- *ὑπὸ σκωλήκων φρικτῶν* is the personal-agent construction, *ὑπό* with the genitive plural, for *da fastidiosi vermi*. *ἀπεῤῥοφᾶτο* ‘was being sucked up’ specifies a manner of collecting absent from *era ricolto*. It is the 3rd sg. imperfect middle-passive indicative of *ἀπορροφάω*, passive here, with internal augment, doubled rho, and contraction of the ending.

> 70 E poi che a riguardare oltre mi diedi,  
> 71 vidi gente alla riva d’un gran fiume;  

And then, as I looked onwards, I saw people on the Shore of a great River;

> 70 Εἶτα δὲ τρέψας τὰς ὄψεις ποῤῥωτέρω,  
> (And then, turning my eyes farther onward,)
>
> 71 Εἶδον παρὰ τὰς ὄχθας ποταμοῦ πλῆθος·  
> (I saw upon the banks of a river a multitude;)

- *εἶτα δὲ τρέψας τὰς ὄψεις ποῤῥωτέρω* ‘and then, turning my eyes farther’ recasts *E poi che a riguardare oltre mi diedi*. The temporal clause with a reflexive verb and infinitive becomes an aorist active participle (*τρέψας*, nom. masc. sg. of *τρέπω*) sharing the subject of *εἶδον*. *τὰς ὄψεις* ‘my eyes’ supplies its object; comparative adverb *ποῤῥωτέρω* gives *oltre* directional force. The paired breathings on double rho here and in *ἔῤῥεεν* are an older typographical convention.
- *εἶδον παρὰ τὰς ὄχθας ποταμοῦ πλῆθος* delays the object *πλῆθος* ‘a multitude’ until after the place phrase, unlike *vidi gente alla riva*. *παρά* with the accusative plural describes a position ‘along the banks’; *ποταμοῦ* is a dependent genitive. The banks become plural, while *gran* has no equivalent qualifying the river.

> 72 perch’io dissi: “Maestro, or mi concedi,  
> 73 ch’io sappia quali sono, e qual costume  
> 74 le fa parer di trapassar sì pronte,  
> 75 com’io discerno per lo fioco lume.”  

whereat I said: “Master, now grant that I may know who these are; and what usage makes them seem so ready to pass over, as I discern by the faint light.”

> 72 Εἶπον δέ· “Προστάτα, νῦν ἐπίτρεψόν μοι,  
> (And I said: "Master, now grant me)
>
> 73 „Ὅπως γνῶ, τίνες οὗτοι, καὶ τίς ἀνάγκη  
> (To know who these are, and what necessity)
>
> 74 „Αὐτοὺς ἐπείγει ταχέως διαβῆναι,  
> (Urges them swiftly to cross over,)
>
> 75 „Ὡς διὰ φωτὸς ἀμυδροῦ διακρίνω.“  
> (As through the dim light I discern.")

- *εἶπον δέ* ‘and I said’ (1st sg. second aorist active indicative) weakens the consequential link in *perch’io dissi* to simple connection. *προστάτα*, vocative singular of *προστάτης*, renders *Maestro* as ‘protector’ or ‘guide’. *νῦν ἐπίτρεψόν μοι* closely follows *or mi concedi*, with the 2nd sg. aorist active imperative of *ἐπιτρέπω* and dative of the person granted permission. The extra accent on *ἐπίτρεψόν* is due to enclitic *μοι*.
- *ὅπως γνῶ* ‘that I may know’ gives the content or end of the permission, preserving the finite subordinate clause of *ch’io sappia*. *γνῶ* is the 1st sg. second aorist active subjunctive of *γιγνώσκω*. The indirect questions are *τίνες οὗτοι* ‘who [are] these?’, with the copula omitted, and *τίς ἀνάγκη* ‘what necessity?’, with interrogative *τίς* modifying the feminine subject.
- *ἀνάγκη αὐτοὺς ἐπείγει ταχέως διαβῆναι* ‘necessity urges them to cross quickly’ interprets *costume* as constraint rather than custom. *ἐπείγει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *ἐπείγω*) takes the personal object *αὐτούς*, also the understood subject of *διαβῆναι*, second aorist active infinitive of *διαβαίνω*. The Greek drops *parer*: instead of appearing *sì pronte*, they are urged to act, and *ταχέως* modifies the crossing.
- *ὡς διὰ φωτὸς ἀμυδροῦ διακρίνω* retains the qualification *com’io discerno*. *διά* with genitive *φωτὸς ἀμυδροῦ* presents *per lo fioco lume* as the medium of perception; *διακρίνω* is 1st sg. present active indicative, with the object understood.

> 76 Ed egli a me: “Le cose ti fien conte,  
> 77 quando noi fermerem li nostri passi  
> 78 sulla trista riviera d’Acheronte.”  

And he [to me]: “The things shall be [known to] thee, when we stay our steps upon the joyless strand of Acheron.”

> 76 Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ· “Ταῦτα σὺ πάντα γνώσῃ,  
> (And he to me: "All these things you shall know,)
>
> 77 „Ὅταν ἐπιστήσωμεν ἤδη τὸ βῆμα  
> (When once we stay our steps)
>
> 78 „Ἐπ᾿ Ἀχέροντος ποταμοῦ τοῦ γοώδους.”  
> (Upon the mournful river Acheron.")

- *ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ* ‘and he to me’ matches the ellipsis of *Ed egli a me*: pronominal *ὁ* with *δέ* marks the change of speaker; *πρός* takes accusative *ἐμέ*. *ταῦτα σὺ πάντα γνώσῃ* ‘all these things you will know’ makes the Italian recipient *ti* the explicit subject *σύ* and *Le cose* the object *ταῦτα … πάντα*. *γνώσῃ* is 2nd sg. future middle indicative of *γιγνώσκω*, active in sense, replacing *fien conte* ‘will be known’.
- *ὅταν ἐπιστήσωμεν* ‘when we bring to a halt’ uses the regular prospective temporal construction, *ὅταν* (historically *ὅτε* + *ἄν*) with the subjunctive, for *quando … fermerem*. *ἐπιστήσωμεν* is 1st pl. first aorist active subjunctive of *ἐφίστημι*, transitive, with *τὸ βῆμα* ‘the step’ as object. The singular with the article replaces *li nostri passi*; *ἤδη* adds ‘by then’ or ‘at last’.
- *ἐπ᾿ Ἀχέροντος ποταμοῦ τοῦ γοώδους* places the halt ‘at the mournful river Acheron’. *ἐπί* is elided and governs the genitive; *ποταμοῦ* identifies *Ἀχέροντος* as a river, and the postposed articular adjective *τοῦ γοώδους* (gen. sg. of *γοώδης*) qualifies it. Thus the epithet of *trista riviera* passes from the bank to the river, and the noun for the bank disappears.

> 79 Allor con gli occhi vergognosi e bassi,  
> 80 temendo no ’l mio dir gli fusse grave,  
> 81 infino al fiume dal parlar mi trassi.  

Then, with eyes ashamed and downcast, fearing my words might have offended him, I kept myself from speaking till we reached the stream.

> 79 Τότε κλίνας τὰς ὄψεις μετ᾿ αἰδοῦς κάτω,  
> (Then, casting my eyes downward in shame,)
>
> 80 Καὶ φοβούμενος μὴ γένωμαι δι᾿ ὄχλου,  
> (And fearing lest I become troublesome,)
>
> 81 Ἕως ποταμοῦ τοῦ λαλεῖν ἐπαυσάμην.  
> (As far as the river I refrained from speaking.)

- *τότε κλίνας τὰς ὄψεις μετ᾿ αἰδοῦς κάτω* ‘then, lowering my eyes in shame’ makes an action out of *con gli occhi vergognosi e bassi*. *κλίνας*, nominative masculine singular aorist active participle of *κλίνω*, takes *τὰς ὄψεις* as object and *κάτω* as directional adverb. The shame belongs grammatically to the manner of lowering the eyes: elided *μετά* with *αἰδοῦς*, genitive singular of *αἰδώς*.
- *καὶ φοβούμενος* (nom. masc. sg. present middle participle of *φοβέομαι*) coordinates the continuing fear of *temendo* with the preceding act of lowering the eyes. *μὴ γένωμαι* ‘lest I become’ is a fear clause with the 1st sg. second aorist middle subjunctive of *γίγνομαι*, not an optative despite the past main verb. With idiomatic *δι᾿ ὄχλου* ‘a nuisance’, the speaker himself replaces *’l mio dir* as subject; the person inconvenienced, *gli*, is left implicit.
- *ἕως ποταμοῦ* uses *ἕως* as a preposition with the genitive to express the spatial limit *infino al fiume*. *τοῦ λαλεῖν ἐπαυσάμην* ‘I desisted from speaking’ retains the idea of withdrawal: *ἐπαυσάμην* is 1st sg. aorist middle indicative of *παύω*, with the activity relinquished in the genitive of the articular present active infinitive of *λαλέω*, answering *dal parlar*.

> 82 Ed ecco verso noi venir per nave  
> 83 un vecchio bianco per antico pelo,  
> 84 gridando: “Guai a voi, anime prave!  

And lo! an old man, white with ancient hair, comes towards us in a bark, shouting: “Woe to you, depraved spirits!

> 82 Ἀλλ᾿ ἰδοὺ δὴ πρὸς ἡμᾶς ἔρχετ᾿ ἐν σκάφει  
> (But lo, toward us comes in a skiff)
>
> 83 Γέρων λευκόθριξ, τὴν πολιὰν ἀρχαῖος,  
> (A white-haired old man, ancient in hoary locks,)
>
> 84 Κραυγάζων· “Οὐαὶ ὑμῖν, ψυχαὶ πανώλεις,  
> (Crying aloud: "Woe to you, accursed souls!)

- *ἀλλ᾿ ἰδοὺ δή* ‘but look now’ renders *Ed ecco* with a stronger break: elided *ἀλλά*, the presentative *ἰδού* (in origin a 2nd sg. aorist middle imperative), and emphatic *δή*. *πρὸς ἡμᾶς* preserves *verso noi*, but *ἔρχετ᾿* is a finite present, elided *ἔρχεται* (3rd sg. middle indicative of *ἔρχομαι*), for the infinitive *venir* after *ecco*. *ἐν σκάφει*, with the dative singular of neuter *σκάφος*, makes *per nave* a location, ‘in a boat’.
- *γέρων λευκόθριξ* keeps the subject after the verb; the compound adjective ‘white-haired’ folds *bianco … pelo* into one word. *τὴν πολιὰν ἀρχαῖος* adds ‘ancient in respect of his grey hair’: *τὴν πολιάν* is an accusative of respect, while nominative *ἀρχαῖος* qualifies the man, not the hair as *antico* does.
- *κραυγάζων*, nominative masculine singular present active participle of *κραυγάζω*, matches simultaneous *gridando*. *οὐαὶ ὑμῖν* reproduces *Guai a voi* with an interjection and dative plural. *ψυχαὶ πανώλεις* is vocative plural; *πανώλεις* (from *πανώλης*, ‘utterly ruined’ or ‘accursed’) replaces the moral adjective *prave*.

> 85 non isperate mai veder lo cielo:  

hope not ever to see Heaven:

> 85 „Οὐκ ἐλπὶς ὑμῖν οὐρανῶν ἰδεῖν φέγγος.  
> (No hope is yours to see the light of Heaven.)

- *οὐκ ἐλπὶς ὑμῖν* ‘there is no hope for you’ turns the prohibition *non isperate mai* into a statement: nominative *ἐλπίς* with an understood existential verb, *οὐκ*, and a possessive dative. There is no separate word for *mai*.
- *οὐρανῶν ἰδεῖν φέγγος* ‘to see the light of the heavens’ has a second aorist active infinitive (*ἰδεῖν*, serving *ὁράω*) completing *ἐλπίς*. The object is now neuter accusative singular *φέγγος* ‘light’; singular *lo cielo* becomes the dependent genitive plural *οὐρανῶν*, placed before the infinitive.

> 86 i’ vegno per menarvi all’altra riva,  
> 87 nelle tenebre eterne, in caldo e in gelo.  

I come to lead you to the other shore; into the eternal darkness; into fire and into ice.

> 86 „Ἥκω πορθμεύσων ὑμᾶς εἰς πέραν ὄχθην,  
> (I come to ferry you to the farther shore,)
>
> 87 „Εἰς αἰώνιον σκότος, εἰς πῦρ καὶ πάγον.  
> (Into eternal darkness, into fire and frost.)

- *ἥκω πορθμεύσων* ‘I have come to ferry’ renders *i’ vegno per menarvi*. *ἥκω* is present active indicative, 1st sg., but denotes arrival achieved; the nominative masculine singular future active participle of *πορθμεύω* expresses purpose after a verb of coming and specifies transport by water. *ὑμᾶς* is its accusative plural object, for enclitic *-vi*.
- *εἰς πέραν ὄχθην* ‘to the farther bank’ corresponds to *all’altra riva*. *πέραν*, ordinarily adverbial ‘on the far side’, serves here as a modifier of *ὄχθην*; it is *εἰς* that governs the accusative.
- *εἰς αἰώνιον σκότος, εἰς πῦρ καὶ πάγον* continues the directional construction rather than changing prepositions with *nelle … in … in*. Neuter singular *σκότος* replaces plural *tenebre*, with *αἰώνιον* agreeing in the accusative singular. *πῦρ* ‘fire’ makes *caldo* concrete, while *πάγον* ‘frost, ice’ renders *gelo*; the last two accusatives share a single *εἰς*.

## Lines 88–120 — Charon and the Crossing

> 88 E tu che sei costì, anima viva,  
> 89 partiti da cotesti, che son morti.”  

And thou who art there, alive, depart thee from these who are dead."

> 88 „Σὺ δέ, τί ζητεῖς ἐνταῦθα, ψυχὴ ζῶσα ;  
> (And you, what seek you here, living soul?)
>
> 89 „Ἀποχωρίσθητι δὴ τῶν νεκρῶν τούτων.”  
> (Separate yourself now from these dead.")

- *Σὺ δέ* sets the emphatic pronoun first: ‘you, however’. Musurus makes a question out of the Italian address: *τί ζητεῖς ἐνταῦθα* ‘what are you seeking here?’ has an interrogative object *τί* and a present active indicative, 2nd sg. of *ζητέω*, where *che sei costì* has a relative and a verb of being. *Ἐνταῦθα* locates the addressee without quite reproducing the distinction of *costì* ‘there where you are’.
- *ψυχὴ ζῶσα* is a direct address, in the nominative form rather than the distinct vocative *ψυχή* as accented in isolation being at issue: the substantive has the same form in both cases. *Ζῶσα* is the fem. sg. present active participle of *ζάω*, ‘living’, for the adjective *viva*.
- *Ἀποχωρίσθητι δή* ‘do now separate yourself’ uses the 2nd sg. aorist passive imperative of *ἀποχωρίζω* with intransitive force for reflexive *partiti*; *δή* gives the command point. The genitive of separation *τῶν νεκρῶν τούτων* ‘from these dead’ absorbs the relative predicate *che son morti* into the substantivized adjective *τῶν νεκρῶν*. The demonstrative stands outside the article–adjective group.

> 90 Ma poi ch’ei vide, ch’io non mi partiva,  
> 91 disse: “Per altra via, per altri porti  
> 92 verrai a piaggia, non qui, per passare:  
> 93 più lieve legno convien che ti porti.”  

But when he saw that I departed not, he said: "By other ways, by other ferries, not here, shalt thou pass over: a lighter boat must carry thee."

> 90 Ἀλλ᾿ ὡς εἶδέ με βραδύνοντ᾿ ἐκχωρῆσαι,  
> (But when he saw me delaying to depart,)
>
> 91 Εἶπε· “Δι᾿ ἄλλης ὁδοῦ, δι᾿ ἄλλου πόρου,  
> (He said: "By another way, by another passage,)
>
> 92 „Οὐκ ἐντεῦθεν, εὑρήσεις τρόπον ἐξόδου.  
> (Not from here, you shall find a means of exit.)
>
> 93 „ Πλὴν λεπτοτέρου χρήζεις πρὸς τοῦτο σκάφους.”  
> (Yet a lighter skiff you need for this.")

- *Ἀλλ᾿ ὡς εἶδέ με* ‘but when he saw me’ has elision of *ἀλλά* and temporal *ὡς* with *εἶδε*, 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *ὁράω*; the extra accent is due to enclitic *με*. For *ch’io non mi partiva* Greek has the accusative object with a supplementary participle after a verb of perception: *βραδύνοντ᾿* is elided *βραδύνοντα* (pres. act. ptcp., acc. masc. sg., *βραδύνω*), agreeing with *με*. Its complement *ἐκχωρῆσαι* is the aorist active infinitive of *ἐκχωρέω*: ‘delaying to withdraw’ makes a delay out of the Italian negative imperfect.
- *Εἶπε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*) resumes the main construction after the temporal clause. *Δι᾿ ἄλλης ὁδοῦ, δι᾿ ἄλλου πόρου* repeats elided *διά* with the genitive of the way by which one goes. The parallel is closer in Greek than in Italian: *ἄλλου πόρου* ‘another passage’ is singular, unlike *altri porti*, and denotes a means of crossing rather than places of embarkation.
- *Οὐκ ἐντεῦθεν* ‘not from here’ makes *non qui* expressly a matter of starting point. *Εὑρήσεις* (2nd sg. fut. act. ind. of *εὑρίσκω*) *τρόπον ἐξόδου* ‘you will find a means of exit’ replaces *verrai a piaggia … per passare*: neither the arrival nor the beach is expressed, and the purpose infinitive gives way to a genitive dependent on *τρόπον*.
- *Πλήν* ‘yet’ supplies a connective. In *λεπτοτέρου χρήζεις πρὸς τοῦτο σκάφους* the comparative adjective and its noun enclose the rest of the clause. *Χρήζεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *χρήζω*) governs the genitive *λεπτοτέρου … σκάφους* ‘of a slighter craft’; *πρὸς τοῦτο* ‘for this’ supplies its purpose. Impersonal *convien* with *che ti porti*, in which the boat does the carrying, becomes personal ‘you need’; *σκάφος* names the craft directly instead of naming its material, *legno*.

> 94 E il duca a lui: “Caron, non ti crucciare:  
> 95 vuolsi così colà, dove si puote  
> 96 ciò che si vuole; e più non dimandare.”  

And my guide to him: "Charon, vex not thyself: thus it is willed there, where what is willed can be done; and ask no more."

> 94 Προστάτης δ’ αὐτῷ· “ Χάρων, μὴ παροργίζου·  
> (And my Master to him: "Charon, be not wroth;)
>
> 95 „ Τοῦτ’ ἔστι θέλημ᾽ ἄνω τῶν δυναμένων  
> (This is the will on high of those who have power)
>
> 96 „ Ὅ,τι θέλουσι· περαιτέρω μὴ ζήτει.”  
> (To do what they will; inquire no further.")

- *Προστάτης δ’ αὐτῷ* ‘and [the] protector to him’ retains the verbless introduction and the dative of the person addressed; *προστάτης* presents *il duca* as one who stands before or protects. *Δ’* is elided postpositive *δέ*. *Χάρων* is used in address; *μὴ παροργίζου* is a negative command with the 2nd sg. present middle/passive imperative of *παροργίζω*, ‘do not be incensed’, for *non ti crucciare*.
- *Τοῦτ’ ἔστι θέλημ᾽ ἄνω* ‘this is a will on high’ makes a demonstrative subject and predicate noun out of impersonal *vuolsi così*; *τοῦτο* and *θέλημα* both lose their final vowel before a vowel. *Ἄνω* interprets *colà* as ‘on high’. Instead of the locative relative *dove* with impersonal *si puote*, *τῶν δυναμένων* is a possessive genitive plural dependent on *θέλημα*, ‘of those able’ (articular pres. mid. ptcp. of *δύναμαι*).
- *Ὅ,τι θέλουσι* ‘whatever they will’ has the neut. sg. indefinite relative *ὅ,τι*, not the conjunction *ὅτι*, and the 3rd pl. present active indicative of *θέλω*. An infinitive such as ‘to do’ is understood with *δυναμένων*. Greek thus gives both ability and volition personal, plural bearers, where Italian twice has *si*. *Περαιτέρω μὴ ζήτει* ‘inquire no further’ puts the comparative adverb first and uses the contracted 2nd sg. present active imperative of *ζητέω*, rather than the infinitive of the Italian negative command.

> 97 Quinci fur quete le lanose gote  
> 98 al nocchier della livida palude,  
> 99 che intorno agli occhi avea di fiamme rote.  

Then the woolly cheeks were quiet of the steersman on the livid marsh, who round his eyes had wheels of flame.

> 97 Τότ’ οὖν ἀτρεμεῖς τὰς πολύτριχας γνάθους  
> (Then unmoved his shaggy jaws)
>
> 98 Ἔσχεν ὁ πορθμεὺς τοῦ πελιδνοῦ τενάγους,  
> (Kept the ferryman of the livid marsh,)
>
> 99 Οὗ τοὺς ὀφθαλμοὺς περιεκύκλουν φλόγες.  
> (Around whose eyes circled flames.)

- *Τότ’ οὖν* ‘then, accordingly’ renders *Quinci* with elided *τότε* and inferential *οὖν*. *Ἀτρεμεῖς τὰς πολύτριχας γνάθους ἔσχεν* turns *fur quete le lanose gote* into ‘he held his shaggy jaws still’. *Ἔσχεν* is 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *ἔχω*; *ἀτρεμεῖς* (acc. fem. pl. of *ἀτρεμής*) is predicative, outside the article, whereas *πολύτριχας* (acc. fem. pl. of *πολύθριξ*, ‘many-haired’) is attributive. The distinction reproduces the two different functions of *quete* and *lanose*.
- Delayed *ὁ πορθμεύς* ‘the ferryman’ is the subject of *ἔσχεν*, not a possessor corresponding to *al nocchier*. *Τοῦ πελιδνοῦ τενάγους* ‘of the livid shallows’ depends on it; *τενάγους* is the contracted gen. sg. of neuter *τέναγος*.
- *Οὗ τοὺς ὀφθαλμοὺς περιεκύκλουν φλόγες* ‘whose eyes flames encircled’ uses possessive relative *οὗ* instead of subject *che*. *Περιεκύκλουν* is 3rd pl. imperfect active indicative of *περικυκλόω*, augmented after the preverb and contracted. The final nominative *φλόγες* supplies its subject; the image of *rote* passes into the encircling action rather than a noun for wheels.

> 100 Ma quell’ anime ch’eran lasse e nude,  
> 101 cangiâr colore e dibattero i denti,  
> 102 ratto che inteser le parole crude.  

But those spirits, who were foreworn and naked, changed colour and chattered with their teeth, soon as they heard the bitter words.

> 100 Αἱ ψυχαὶ δ’ αὗται γυμναὶ καὶ κεκμηκυῖαι,  
> (And these souls, naked and weary,)
>
> 101 Βρύχουσαι τοὺς ὀδόντας ἤλλαξαν χρῶμα,  
> (Gnashing their teeth, changed colour,)
>
> 102 Ὡς ἤκουσαν πορθμέως τοὺς σκληροὺς λόγους·  
> (When they heard the harsh words of the ferryman;)

- *Αἱ ψυχαὶ δ’ αὗται* ‘but these souls’ uses the demonstrative outside the article group, with *αὗται* nearer in reference than *quell’*. *Γυμναὶ καὶ κεκμηκυῖαι* reverses *lasse e nude* and dispenses with *ch’eran*: the adjective is coordinated with the nom. fem. pl. perfect active participle of *κάμνω*, denoting the resulting state ‘worn out’.
- *Βρύχουσαι τοὺς ὀδόντας* ‘gnashing their teeth’ makes *dibattero i denti* a simultaneous circumstance (pres. act. ptcp., nom. fem. pl., *βρύχω*) and places it before the sole finite predicate. *Ἤλλαξαν χρῶμα* ‘they changed colour’ closely follows *cangiâr colore*: 3rd pl. aorist active indicative of *ἀλλάσσω*, with an accusative object.
- Temporal *ὡς ἤκουσαν* (3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *ἀκούω*) renders *ratto che inteser* without a separate word for immediacy. *Πορθμέως* (gen. sg. of *πορθμεύς*) identifies the speaker explicitly before *τοὺς σκληροὺς λόγους* ‘the harsh words’, the accusative of what was heard.

> 103 Bestemmiavano Iddio e lor parenti,  
> 104 l’ umana specie, il luogo, il tempo, e il seme  
> 105 di lor semenza e di lor nascimenti.  

They blasphemed God and their parents; the human kind; the place, the time, and origin of their seed, and of their birth.

> 103 Ἐβλασφήμουν τὸν Θεὸν καὶ τοὺς γενέτας,  
> (They blasphemed God and their parents,)
>
> 104 Ἀνθρώπων γένος, τόπον, χρόνον καὶ σπέρμα  
> (The race of men, the place, the time, and the seed)
>
> 105 Ἑαυτῶν καταγωγῆς γεννήσεώς τε.  
> (Of their own lineage and of their birth.)

- *Ἐβλασφήμουν* is 3rd pl. imperfect active indicative of *βλασφημέω*, with syllabic augment and contraction, matching the continuing action of *Bestemmiavano*. The series of accusatives begins with *τὸν Θεὸν καὶ τοὺς γενέτας*; *γενέτας* (acc. pl. of *γενέτης*) means ‘begetters’, with *lor* left implicit.
- *Ἀνθρώπων γένος* replaces the adjective in *l’umana specie* with a preposed genitive, ‘the race of humans’. *Τόπον, χρόνον καὶ σπέρμα* continues the list without articles. *Σπέρμα* keeps *seme*; the related *semenza* is differentiated as *καταγωγῆς* ‘descent’.
- *Ἑαυτῶν* is the plural reflexive genitive referring to the subject, ‘their own’, shared by *καταγωγῆς γεννήσεώς τε*. These two genitives depend on *σπέρμα*; postpositive enclitic *τε* links the second to the first and accounts for the added accent on *γεννήσεώς* (gen. sg. of *γέννησις*). Greek uses singular ‘birth’ for plural *nascimenti* and does not repeat the possessive.

> 106 Poi si ritrasser tutte quante insieme,  
> 107 forte piangendo, alla riva malvagia,  
> 108 che attende ciascun uom, che Dio non teme.  

Then all of them together, sorely weeping, drew to the accursed shore, which awaits every man that fears not God.

> 106 Ἔπειτα προὐχώρησαν πᾶσαι συμμίγδην,  
> (Then they all moved forward together in a throng,)
>
> 107 Κλαίουσαι πικρῶς, πρὸς τὴν ἀποφράδ’ ὄχθην,  
> (Weeping bitterly, toward the accursed shore,)
>
> 108 Ἔνθ’ ἵξεται πᾶς, ὃς Θεὸν οὐ φοβεῖται.  
> (Where every one shall come who fears not God.)

- *Ἔπειτα προὐχώρησαν* ‘then they advanced’ gives *si ritrasser* a forward rather than a withdrawing motion. *Προὐχώρησαν* is 3rd pl. aorist active indicative of *προχωρέω*; *προὐ-* represents the coalescence of *προ-* with the syllabic augment. *Πᾶσαι* ‘all’ agrees with the feminine subject, and *συμμίγδην* ‘in a mingled throng’ strengthens *insieme*.
- *Κλαίουσαι πικρῶς* ‘weeping bitterly’ renders the gerund *piangendo* by an agreeing present active participle (nom. fem. pl. of *κλαίω*) and *forte* by an adverb of quality. *Πρὸς τὴν ἀποφράδ’ ὄχθην* is directional *πρός* with the accusative; *ἀποφράδ’* is elided *ἀποφράδα*, acc. fem. sg. of *ἀποφράς*, ‘ill-omened’, for *malvagia*.
- *Ἔνθ’ ἵξεται πᾶς* ‘where everyone will come’ reverses *che attende ciascun uom*: the shore is no longer the subject of ‘awaits’ but the destination in a local relative clause. *Ἔνθ’* is *ἔνθα* elided before *ἵξεται*; poetic *ἵξεται* is 3rd sg. future middle indicative of *ἵκω*, with active sense. Substantival *πᾶς* supplies *ciascun uom* without a noun. *Ὃς Θεὸν οὐ φοβεῖται* retains the personal relative; *φοβεῖται* is 3rd sg. present middle/passive indicative of *φοβέομαι*, governing the accusative.

> 109 Caron dimonio, con occhi di bragia  
> 110 loro accennando, tutte le raccoglie;  
> 111 batte col remo qualunque s’ adagia.  

Charon the demon, with eyes of glowing coal, beckoning them, collects them all; smites with his oar whoever lingers.

> 109 Ὁ Δαίμων Χάρων ὀφθαλμοῖς καταπύροις  
> (The demon Charon, with fiery eyes)
>
> 110 Σημαίνων, αὐτὰς συνηγέλαζε πάσας,  
> (Beckoning, herded them all together,)
>
> 111 Παίων τῇ κώπῃ τὰς σπεύδειν βραδυνούσας.  
> (Smiting with his oar those slow to hasten.)

- *Ὁ Δαίμων Χάρων* puts the designation before the name, reversing *Caron dimonio*. *Ὀφθαλμοῖς καταπύροις* is a descriptive dative, ‘with fiery eyes’; the adjective takes the place of *di bragia*. Its position also lets the eyes be understood as the means of the following *σημαίνων*.
- *Σημαίνων* (pres. act. ptcp., nom. masc. sg., *σημαίνω*) answers to *accennando* without the explicit recipient *loro*. Instead *αὐτὰς … πάσας* ‘them all’ encloses *συνηγέλαζε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *συναγελάζω*, augmented internally), ‘was herding together’. The verb is more specific than *raccoglie* and replaces the Italian narrative present with a durative past.
- *Παίων* (pres. act. ptcp. of *παίω*, agreeing with *Χάρων*) makes finite *batte* another accompanying action. *Τῇ κώπῃ* is instrumental dative. For the indefinite singular relative *qualunque s’adagia*, Greek has the articular feminine plural participle *τὰς … βραδυνούσας* (pres. act., acc., *βραδύνω*); its complementary present infinitive *σπεύδειν* supplies the pointed contrast ‘those slow to hurry’.

> 112 Come d’autunno si levan le foglie  
> 113 l’ una appresso dell’ altra, infin che il ramo  
> 114 vede alla terra tutte le sue spoglie:  

As the leaves of autumn fall off one after the other, till the branch sees all its spoils upon the ground:

> 112 Ὥσπερ ἐν φθινοπώρῳ τὰ φύλλα πίπτει  
> (Just as in autumn the leaves fall)
>
> 113 Ἀλλεπάλληλα, μέχρις ὁ κλάδος τέλος  
> (One after another, until the bough at last)
>
> 114 Ἀποδῷ τῇ γῇ στολισμὸν αὐτοῦ πάντα,  
> (Gives back to the earth all its adornment,)

- *Ὥσπερ* ‘just as’ introduces the comparison answered by *οὕτω* in 115. *Ἐν φθινοπώρῳ* is a temporal prepositional phrase, ‘in autumn’, for *d’autunno*. *Τὰ φύλλα πίπτει* ‘the leaves fall’ uses the classical singular verb with a neuter plural subject (*πίπτει*, 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *πίπτω*); the simple verb omits the notion of detachment in *si levan*.
- *Ἀλλεπάλληλα* (neut. pl. of *ἀλλεπάλληλος*) expresses *l’una appresso dell’altra* in one word, ‘successive’, agreeing in form with *φύλλα* and qualifying the manner of falling. *Μέχρις* introduces the limit ‘until’; *τέλος* is an adverbial accusative, ‘at last’.
- *Ὁ κλάδος … ἀποδῷ* ‘the branch gives back’ replaces *il ramo vede*. *Ἀποδῷ* is 3rd sg. aorist active subjunctive of *ἀποδίδωμι*, presenting the endpoint as something to be reached; Musurus has *μέχρις* with the subjunctive without the *ἄν* usual in Attic prose for such a prospective limit. *Τῇ γῇ* is consequently a recipient, not the location *alla terra*. *Στολισμὸν αὐτοῦ πάντα* ‘all its adornment’ changes plural *tutte le sue spoglie* into a collective singular; *αὐτοῦ* is a possessive genitive referring back to the branch, where an explicitly reflexive possessive was also possible.

> 115 similemente il mal seme d’ Adamo  
> 116 gittansi di quel lito ad una ad una,  
> 117 per cenni, come augel per suo richiamo.  

so one by one the evil seed of Adam cast themselves from that shore at signals, as the bird at its call.

> 115 Οὕτω τὸ κακοφυὲς Ἀδάμου σπέρμα  
> (So the evil-natured seed of Adam)
>
> 116 Ὁρμᾷ πρὸς αὐτὴν τὴν ἀκτὴν ἐπαλλήλως  
> (Darts toward that shore one after another)
>
> 117 Διὰ νευμάτων, ὡς ὄρνις πρὸς τὴν κλῆσιν·  
> (At his signs, like a bird at the call;)

- *Οὕτω τὸ κακοφυὲς Ἀδάμου σπέρμα* answers the comparison with ‘so the evil-natured seed of Adam’. *Κακοφυές* (neut. nom. sg. of *κακοφυής*) expands *mal* to ‘bad by nature’; the genitive *Ἀδάμου*, treating the name as declinable, comes between adjective and noun.
- *Ὁρμᾷ* is 3rd sg. contracted present active indicative of *ὁρμάω*, intransitive ‘rushes’. It agrees grammatically with singular *σπέρμα*, unlike plural reflexive *gittansi* with collective *seme*. *Πρὸς αὐτὴν τὴν ἀκτήν* ‘toward that very shore’ actually reverses *di quel lito* ‘from that shore’; *αὐτήν* outside the article is intensive rather than an ordinary demonstrative. The adverb *ἐπαλλήλως* renders distributive *ad una ad una* without marking gender or number.
- *Διὰ νευμάτων* ‘by means of nods’ has instrumental *διά* with the genitive for *per cenni*. *Ὡς ὄρνις πρὸς τὴν κλῆσιν* leaves the bird’s verb of motion understood, as does *come augel per suo richiamo*; directional *πρός* makes the call what the bird responds to, and the article replaces *suo*.

> 118 Così sen vanno su per l’ onda bruna,  

Thus they depart on the brown water;

> 118 Οὕτω πρὸς τὸ κελαινὸν βαίνουσι κῦμα,  
> (Thus toward the dark wave they go,)

- *Οὕτω* repeats the opening of 115 for the renewed *Così*. *Βαίνουσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *βαίνω*) returns to plural agreement with the individuals, without a separate equivalent of *sen*. *Πρὸς τὸ κελαινὸν … κῦμα* ‘toward the dark wave’ encloses the verb; the poetic adjective *κελαινός* renders *bruna*, but directional *πρός* with the accusative differs from movement along or over the water in *su per l’onda*.

> 119 ed avanti che sian di là discese,  
> 120 anche di qua nova schiera s’ aduna.  

and ere they have landed on the other shore, again a fresh crowd collects on this.

> 119 Καὶ πρὶν περαιωθῶσιν ἀντικρὺ πᾶσαι,  
> (And before they have all been ferried across to the other side,)
>
> 120 Ὅμιλος ἐκεῖ συσσωρεύεται νέος.  
> (A new throng is gathered there.)

- *Καὶ πρὶν περαιωθῶσιν* ‘and before they are ferried across’ replaces *avanti che sian … discese* with *πρίν* and the 3rd pl. aorist passive subjunctive of *περαιόω*. The passive emphasizes conveyance rather than disembarkation. This finite subjunctive after *πρίν* closely follows the Italian construction: with an affirmative main clause Attic prose would more ordinarily use *πρίν* with an infinitive. *Ἀντικρύ* ‘opposite’ stands for *di là*, and subject *πᾶσαι* ‘all’, feminine plural referring to the souls, is explicit only in Greek.
- *Ὅμιλος … νέος* ‘a new throng’ separates the noun from its agreeing adjective by *ἐκεῖ συσσωρεύεται*. The verb is 3rd sg. present middle/passive indicative of *συσσωρεύω*, ‘is massed together’, for *s’aduna*. *Ἐκεῖ* means ‘there’, not ‘on this side’: Greek does not preserve the opposition of *di là* and *di qua*. Nor is there a separate word for *anche*; recurrence is conveyed by *νέος* and the present verb.

## Lines 121–136 — Fear Turns to Desire and Dante Falls

> 121 “Figliuol mio,” disse il maestro cortese,  

“My son,” said the courteous Master,

> 121 “Τέκνον μου,” φησὶν ὁ πραΰς μοι προστάτης,  
> ("My son," says my gentle Master to me,)

- *Τέκνον μου* ‘my child’ renders *Figliuol mio* with the neuter vocative singular of *τέκνον* (identical to the nominative) and the enclitic possessive genitive *μου*. The Greek is affectionate without being a diminutive.
- *φησὶν ὁ πραΰς μοι προστάτης* ‘says the gentle guide to me’ puts a historical present for *disse*: *φησίν* is 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *φημί*, with movable *ν* before the vowel. The enclitic dative *μοι* supplies the person addressed. The subject still follows the verb, but within it attributive *πραΰς* ‘gentle’ precedes *προστάτης* ‘protector, guide’, where *cortese* follows *maestro*.

> 122 “quelli, che muoion nell’ira di Dio,  
> 123 tutti convegnon qui d’ogni paese;  

“those who die under God’s wrath, all assemble here from every country;

> 122 “Πάντες ἐν ὀργῇ τοῦ Θεοῦ τεθνηκότες  
> ("All who have died in the wrath of God)
>
> 123 „Ἐκ πάσης χώρας συναθροίζοντ᾽ ἐνταῦθα,  
> (From every land assemble here,)

- *Πάντες ἐν ὀργῇ τοῦ Θεοῦ τεθνηκότες* ‘all who have died in the wrath of God’ draws *tutti* forward to the head of the subject. In place of the resumed *quelli, che … tutti*, Greek has *πάντες* with an agreeing participle, *τεθνηκότες* (nom. masc. pl., perf. act. of *θνῄσκω*): the perfect presents the subjects as already dead, against the general present *muoion*. *ἐν* with the dative *ὀργῇ* retains the construction of *nell’ira*, with *τοῦ Θεοῦ* dependent on the noun.
- *Ἐκ πάσης χώρας* ‘from every land’ moves *d’ogni paese* before the verb; *πάσης* with an anarthrous singular has distributive force. *συναθροίζοντ᾽* is *συναθροίζονται* (3rd pl. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *συναθροίζω*), with *-αι* elided before *ἐνταῦθα*: ‘they gather’ or ‘are gathered’ for *convegnon*. The place of assembly, *ἐνταῦθα* ‘here’, comes last, rather than immediately before the expression of origin as *qui* does.

> 124 e pronti sono a trapassar lo rio,  

and they are prompt to pass the river,

> 124 „Καὶ τὸν ποταμὸν σπεύδουσι διαβῆναι·  
> (And hasten to cross the river;)

- *Καὶ τὸν ποταμὸν σπεύδουσι διαβῆναι* ‘and they hasten to cross the river’ replaces *pronti sono* with a single verb, *σπεύδουσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *σπεύδω*), suggesting haste rather than merely readiness. *διαβῆναι* is the intransitive root-aorist active infinitive of *διαβαίνω*, complementary to *σπεύδουσι*; the aorist views the crossing as a whole, not as past. Its accusative of the expanse crossed, *τὸν ποταμόν*, stands before both verbs, whereas *lo rio* follows *trapassar*.

> 125 chè la divina giustizia gli sprona  
> 126 sì che la tema si volge in disio.  

for Divine Justice spurs them so, that fear is changed into desire.

> 125 „Κεντεῖ γὰρ αὐτοὺς θεία Δικαιοσύνη,  
> (For divine Justice spurs them on,)
>
> 126 „Τὸν φόβον αὐτῶν μετατρέπουσ᾽ εἰς πόθον.  
> (Turning their fear into desire.)

- *Κεντεῖ γὰρ αὐτοὺς θεία Δικαιοσύνη* ‘for divine Justice goads them’ gives causal *chè* by postpositive *γάρ*. The verb comes first: *κεντεῖ* is the contracted 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *κεντέω*, literally ‘prick’. The accusative *αὐτούς* renders object *gli*, and the anarthrous nominative subject *θεία Δικαιοσύνη* follows instead of preceding the verb.
- *Τὸν φόβον αὐτῶν μετατρέπουσ᾽ εἰς πόθον* ‘turning their fear into longing’ dispenses with *sì che* and its finite result clause. *μετατρέπουσ᾽* is elided *μετατρέπουσα* (nom. fem. sg. pres. act. participle of *μετατρέπω*), agreeing with *Δικαιοσύνη* and expressing the effect of the goading. Where *la tema* is subject of *si volge*, *τὸν φόβον* is the object of Justice’s action; the possessive genitive *αὐτῶν* is supplied. *εἰς πόθον* retains the goal of the transformation, *in disio*.

> 127 Quinci non passa mai anima buona:  

By this way no good spirit ever passes;

> 127 „Ἐντεῦθεν οὔποτ᾽ ἔβη ψυχὴ δικαία·  
> (This way never passed a righteous soul;)

- *Ἐντεῦθεν οὔποτ᾽ ἔβη ψυχὴ δικαία* ‘from here never went a righteous soul’ retains the initial directional adverb *Quinci*, but places the entire negative *οὔποτ᾽* (elided *οὔποτε* ‘never’) before the verb, in place of *non … mai*. *ἔβη* is the augmented 3rd sg. root-aorist act. ind. of *βαίνω*: in place of the general present *passa*, Greek states that no such passage ever occurred. The postverbal, anarthrous *ψυχὴ δικαία* preserves the order of *anima buona* but specifies goodness as righteousness.

> 128 e però, se Caron di te si lagna,  
> 129 ben puoi saper omai, che il suo dir suona.”  

and hence, if Charon complains of thee, thou easily now mayest know the import of his words.”

> 128 „Εἰ δ᾽ ὁ Χάρων κατὰ σοῦ νῦν αἰτιᾶται,  
> (So if Charon now complains against you,)
>
> 129 „Νοεῖν δύνασαι, τί ποτ᾽ αὐτὸ σημαίνει.”  
> (You can understand what this signifies.")

- *Εἰ δ᾽ ὁ Χάρων κατὰ σοῦ νῦν αἰτιᾶται* ‘and if Charon now makes complaint against you’ has *εἰ* with the indicative for an open condition; postpositive *δ᾽* (elided *δέ*) connects it to the preceding statement without an explicit equivalent of inferential *però*. *ὁ Χάρων* adds the article to the name. *αἰτιᾶται* is contracted 3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *αἰτιάομαι* ‘blame, accuse’. Instead of the ordinary direct accusative of the person blamed, *κατά* with genitive *σοῦ* marks the person complained against, giving *di te* an expressly adversarial sense.
- *νῦν* goes with the complaint, shifting the time indication from *saper omai*. In the main clause *Νοεῖν δύνασαι* ‘you can understand’, the contracted pres. act. infinitive of *νοέω* precedes its governing verb *δύνασαι* (2nd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *δύναμαι*), reversing *puoi saper*. *ben* has no separate equivalent.
- *τί ποτ᾽ αὐτὸ σημαίνει* ‘what it actually signifies’ is an indirect question retaining the indicative *σημαίνει* (3rd sg. pres. act. of *σημαίνω*). Interrogative *τί* is its accusative object, strengthened by enclitic *ποτ᾽* (elided *ποτε*); neuter nominative *αὐτό* ‘it’ points back to the complaint instead of naming *il suo dir*. *σημαίνει* gives the sense rather than the sound-image of *suona*.

> 130 Finito questo, la buia campagna  
> 131 tremò sì forte, che dello spavento  
> 132 la mente di sudore ancor mi bagna.  

When he had ended, the dusky champaign trembled so violently, that the remembrance of my terror bathes me still with sweat.

> 130 Ταῦτα δ᾽ εἰπόντος, ἡ βαθύγνοφος χώρα  
> (When he had said these things, the deep-gloom region)
>
> 131 Ἐσείσθη τόσον, ὥστ᾽ ἔτι νῦν ἐκ τρόμου  
> (Shook so violently that even now from terror)
>
> 132 Ἡ μνήμη μ᾽ ὅλον ἱδρῶτι περιχέει.  
> (The memory bathes me all in sweat.)

- *Ταῦτα δ᾽ εἰπόντος* ‘and when he had said these things’ recasts the passive absolute *Finito questo* as an active genitive absolute. *εἰπόντος* is gen. masc. sg. of the second-aorist active participle belonging to *λέγω*; its subject, the speaker, is understood, not expressed by *ταῦτα*, which is its neuter plural accusative object. The aorist makes the speech anterior to the shaking; elided *δέ* supplies the transition.
- *ἡ βαθύγνοφος χώρα ἐσείσθη τόσον* ‘the deeply gloomy region was shaken so much’ puts the compound adjective *βαθύγνοφος* before *χώρα*, against *la buia campagna*. *ἐσείσθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *σείω*, with augment *ἐ-* and passive formation *-σθη-*) replaces intransitive *tremò*. Adverbial neuter accusative *τόσον* gives the degree expressed by *sì forte*.
- *ὥστ᾽ ἔτι νῦν ἐκ τρόμου … περιχέει* answers *τόσον* with *ὥστε* (elided before *ἔτι*) and a finite indicative of actual result. *ἔτι νῦν* ‘even now’ brings *ancor* forward. *ἐκ τρόμου* ‘from fright’ is causal rather than a genitive dependent on ‘memory’: Greek does not literally say “the remembrance of my terror”.
- *Ἡ μνήμη μ᾽ ὅλον ἱδρῶτι περιχέει* ‘memory drenches the whole of me with sweat’ interprets *la mente* as recollection. *μ᾽* is elided accusative *με*, with agreeing masculine *ὅλον* adding ‘all over’; *ἱδρῶτι* (dat. sg. of *ἱδρώς*) gives the liquid with which the person is drenched. *περιχέει* is the uncontracted 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *περιχέω* (contracted *περιχεῖ*), retaining the present of *bagna*.

> 133 La terra lagrimosa diede vento,  
> 134 che balenò una luce vermiglia,  
> 135 la qual mi vinse ciascun sentimento;  

The tearful ground gave out wind, [which] flashed [forth] a crimson light that conquered all my senses;

> 133 Ἄνεμος πνεύσας ἐκ τῆς δακρυογόνου  
> (A wind, blowing forth from that tear-bearing)
>
> 134 Γῆς ταύτης διήστραψε φῶς ἐρυθρόχρουν,  
> (Land, flashed forth a crimson light,)
>
> 135 Πᾶσαν αἴσθησιν ἐν ἐμοὶ καταλῦσαν·  
> (Overpowering every sense within me;)

- *Ἄνεμος πνεύσας* ‘a wind, having blown’ makes *vento* the nominative subject from the outset, rather than the object of *diede*. *πνεύσας* (nom. masc. sg. first-aorist act. participle of *πνέω*) agrees with *ἄνεμος*. The ground, subject in *La terra lagrimosa diede vento*, becomes the source of the blowing: *ἐκ τῆς δακρυογόνου γῆς ταύτης* ‘out of this tear-engendering ground’. Attributive *δακρυογόνου* characterizes the ground as productive of tears; the added demonstrative *ταύτης* stands outside the article–adjective–noun group.
- *διήστραψε φῶς ἐρυθρόχρουν* ‘flashed forth a red-hued light’ makes the flashing the main verb, with the wind as subject: Greek thus commits to the interpretation in which *che* refers to *vento* and *una luce vermiglia* is what it flashes forth. *διήστραψε* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *διαστράπτω*, with internal augment after the preverb and *-ψ-* in the sigmatic aorist. *ἐρυθρόχρουν* is the contracted neuter accusative singular of the compound adjective *ἐρυθρόχρους*, agreeing with anarthrous *φῶς*.
- *Πᾶσαν αἴσθησιν ἐν ἐμοὶ καταλῦσαν* ‘putting an end to every sensation in me’ replaces *la qual mi vinse* with an attributive-in-sense participle: *καταλῦσαν* is **neuter accusative singular**, aor. act. participle of *καταλύω*, agreeing with *φῶς*, not with feminine *αἴσθησιν*. *πᾶσαν* with the singular renders distributive *ciascun*; *ἐν ἐμοί* locates sensation ‘in me’ instead of attaching *mi* to the verb as in Italian.

> 136 e caddi, come l’uom, cui sonno piglia.  

and I fell, like one who is seized with sleep.

> 136 Ἔπεσον δ᾽ ὡς ἄνθρωπος κρατηθεὶς ὕπνῳ.  
> (And I fell like a man overcome by sleep.)

- *Ἔπεσον δ᾽* ‘and I fell’ has the augmented second-aorist act. ind. of *πίπτω*, here 1st sg., with postpositive *δέ* elided before *ὡς*. Comparative *ὡς* introduces anarthrous *ἄνθρωπος* ‘a man’ in the nominative, corresponding to the understood subject of *ἔπεσον*, for generic *come l’uom*.
- *κρατηθεὶς ὕπνῳ* ‘overcome by sleep’ turns *cui sonno piglia* from an active present relative clause into a passive participial phrase. *κρατηθείς* (nom. masc. sg. aor. pass. participle of *κρατέω*) agrees with *ἄνθρωπος* and presents the seizure as accomplished; *ὕπνῳ*, dative of the operative force, replaces the Italian grammatical subject *sonno*.

## Summary — Repetition and Grammatical Recasting

Musurus often preserves Dante’s repetitions while changing what the repeated words do. At the opening, the threefold *per me* ‘through me’ becomes *Ἐνθάδ᾽* ‘here’: the Greek keeps the insistence but replaces movement through the speaking gate with three verbless statements of place. Elsewhere the correspondence is syntactic as well as rhetorical. Repeated *senza* is answered by repeated *ἄνευ* ‘without’, each time governing a genitive; *tanto … che* finds a close counterpart in *οὕτως … ὥστε* ‘so … that’, with a finite indicative stating the result. The Italian’s elliptical changes of speaker also suit Greek idiom: *ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ* ‘and he to me’ needs no more of a verb than *Ed egli a me*. Postpositive *δέ* regularly marks such turns, while *γάρ* makes a reason explicit and *τε … καί* links paired terms.

The participle is Musurus’s most versatile means of redistributing the action. In *ἀναγνοὺς … εἶπον* ‘having read … I said’, the perception expressed by finite *vid’ io* becomes preliminary, while the act of speaking, only implied in Italian, receives the main verb. Conversely, *δινοῦσαν καὶ τρέχουσαν* ‘whirling and running’ puts two movements on an equal footing: both are present participles agreeing with the banner, where *girando correva* makes the first movement subordinate to the second. Musurus also uses the infinitive to condense finite clauses. In *ἔλεγόν σε ὄψεσθαι* ‘I was saying that you would see’, the accusative *σε* is the subject of the future infinitive, not the recipient of the telling; the construction changes, but the prospect conveyed by *vedrai* remains.

Some of the sharpest departures turn on a change of grammatical subject. Where *la tema si volge in disio* makes fear itself the subject, *μετατρέπουσα* ‘turning’ agrees with Justice and makes fear the object of her action. A lexical choice can likewise settle a reading: *ἀνάγκη* ‘necessity’ for *costume* ‘custom’ makes the question one of what impels the crossing rather than what practice governs it. Not every change clarifies a relation. *Πρὸς … τὴν ἀκτήν* ‘toward the shore’ reverses *di quel lito* ‘from that shore’, and *ἐκεῖ* ‘there’ loses the contrast of *di qua* ‘on this side’ with *di là* ‘on the other side’. Even where the sequence and cadence of the Italian phrasing remain recognizable, a preposition, a participle, or a change of subject can give the Greek a distinctly different sense.
