# Inferno 30

## Lines 1–21 — The Madness of Athamas and Hecuba

> 1 Nel tempo che Giunone era crucciata  
> 2 per Semelè contra il sangue tebano,  
> 3 come mostrò una ed altra fiata,  

At the time that Juno was incensed for Semele against the Theban blood, as she already more than once had shewn,

> 1 Ἐν καιρῷ, καθ᾽ ὃν Ἥρα δι᾽ ὀργῆς εἶχεν  
> (At the time when Juno was wroth)
>
> 2 Ἕνεκα τῆς Σεμέλης τὴν τῶν Θηβαίων  
> (Because of Semele against the Thebans')
>
> 3 Φυλήν, ὡς κατέδειξεν αὐτὸ πολλάκις,  
> (Race, as she proved it many times,)

- *Ἐν καιρῷ, καθ᾽ ὃν* ‘at a time at which’ expands *Nel tempo che*: the antecedent *καιρῷ* is dative after *ἐν*, but the masculine singular relative *ὃν* is accusative after temporal *κατά*. In *καθ᾽ ὃν* the final vowel of *κατά* is elided and the *τ* aspirated before the rough breathing.
- *Ἥρα δι᾽ ὀργῆς εἶχεν* renders *Giunone era crucciata* by the idiom *δι᾽ ὀργῆς ἔχειν τινά* ‘to be angry with someone’. *εἶχεν* is the 3rd singular imperfect active indicative of *ἔχω*: like the Italian imperfect it presents a continuing state. *διά* is elided before *ὀργῆς*; the person or group against whom the anger is directed is the direct object of *εἶχεν*, not the object of a preposition corresponding to *contra*.
- *ἕνεκα τῆς Σεμέλης* ‘on account of Semele’ renders *per Semelè*, with *ἕνεκα* placed before its genitive. It intervenes between *εἶχεν* and *τὴν τῶν Θηβαίων φυλήν* ‘the race of the Thebans’: the genitive plural of the people stands between the article and *φυλήν*, accusative singular of *φυλή*. Thus *sangue* becomes ‘race’, and the adjective *tebano* becomes a noun.
- *ὡς κατέδειξεν αὐτὸ πολλάκις* ‘as she demonstrated it often’ renders *come mostrò una ed altra fiata*. *κατέδειξεν* is the 3rd singular aorist active indicative of *καταδείκνυμι*, with the augment after the preverb. Neuter *αὐτό* supplies an object referring to the fact just stated, not to feminine *φυλήν*; *πολλάκις* replaces the enumeration of occasions with a single adverb.

> 4 Atamante divenne tanto insano  
> 5 che, veggendo la moglie con due figli  
> 6 andar carcata da ciascuna mano,  

Athamas grew so insane, that he, seeing his wife, with two sons, [go] laden on either hand,

> 4 Ἀθάμας οὕτως ἐγένετο παράφρων,  
> (Athamas became so maddened,)
>
> 5 Ὥστ᾽ ἰδὼν τὴν γυναῖκα προερχομένην,  
> (That, seeing his wife coming forth,)
>
> 6 Ἄγουσάν τε ταῖς χερσὶ τὰ δύο τέκνα,  
> (And leading with her hands the two children,)

- *Ἀθάμας οὕτως ἐγένετο παράφρων* closely follows *Atamante divenne tanto insano*. *ἐγένετο* is the 3rd singular second aorist middle indicative of *γίγνομαι*, with the predicate adjective *παράφρων* nominative singular masculine. *οὕτως* anticipates *ὥστ᾽* (elided *ὥστε*): the result construction extends to the finite indicative in line 7, expressing what actually followed.
- *ἰδών*, nominative masculine singular second aorist active participle of *ὁράω*, agrees with Athamas: ‘on seeing’ rather than the ongoing *veggendo*. After this verb of perception *τὴν γυναῖκα* is both the object seen and the subject of *προερχομένην* ‘coming forth’, accusative feminine singular present middle participle of *προέρχομαι*. Greek thus uses an agreeing participle where Italian has the infinitive *andar*; the article suffices for *la moglie* without an expressed possessive.
- *ἄγουσάν τε ταῖς χερσὶ τὰ δύο τέκνα* adds a second participle describing the wife, linked to the first by postpositive enclitic *τε*. *ἄγουσαν* is accusative feminine singular present active of *ἄγω*: she is ‘leading’ the children, not *carcata* ‘laden’. *ταῖς χερσί*, instrumental dative plural of *χείρ*, does not distribute the children one to each hand as *da ciascuna mano* does. *τὰ δύο τέκνα* is the accusative neuter plural object, with the numeral *δύο*; neither hands nor children are put in the dual, and *τέκνα* does not specify sons.

> 7 gridò: “Tendiam le reti, sì ch’ io pigli  
> 8 la leonessa e i leoncini al varco”;  

cried: "Spread we the nets, that I may take the lioness and her young lions at the pass";

> 7 Ἔκραξε· “Τὰ δίκτυα στήσωμεν, ὅπως  
> (He cried out: "Let us set the nets, so that)
>
> 8 „ Ἀγρεύσω τὴν λέαιναν καὶ λεοντίσκους.”  
> (I may catch the lioness and the lion-cubs.")

- *ἔκραξε*, 3rd singular aorist active indicative of *κράζω*, answers to *gridò* and completes the *ὥστε* clause. In the speech *τὰ δίκτυα στήσωμεν* puts the object first: ‘the nets let us set’. *στήσωμεν* is the transitive first aorist active subjunctive of *ἵστημι*, 1st plural, hortatory like *Tendiam*.
- *ὅπως ἀγρεύσω* renders purposive *sì ch’ io pigli*. *ἀγρεύσω* (from *ἀγρεύω* ‘catch in the hunt’) is 1st singular active, naturally an aorist subjunctive in this purpose clause, though identical in form to the future indicative. There is no separate pronoun for *io*.
- *τὴν λέαιναν καὶ λεοντίσκους* has a definite accusative singular ‘the lioness’ followed by an anarthrous accusative plural; the diminutive *λεοντίσκος* answers to *leoncini*. There is no Greek equivalent of *al varco*.

> 9 e poi distese i dispietati artigli,  
> 10 prendendo l’ un che avea nome Learco,  

and then stretched out his pitiless talons, grasping the one who had the name Learchus;

> 9 Εἶτ᾽ ἐξέτεινε τὰς ἀνηλεεῖς χεῖρας,  
> (Then he stretched out his pitiless hands,)
>
> 10 Καὶ λαβὼν τὸν Λέαρχον, ἕνα τῶν παίδων,  
> (And seizing Learchus, one of the boys,)

- *εἶτ᾽* is elided *εἶτα* ‘then’, without a separate *and*. *ἐξέτεινε* is the 3rd singular aorist active indicative of *ἐκτείνω*, with internal augment, answering to *distese*. *τὰς ἀνηλεεῖς χεῖρας* ‘the pitiless hands’ keeps the adjective in attributive position but replaces *artigli* ‘talons’ with ordinary ‘hands’; Greek uses the article rather than a possessive.
- *καὶ λαβὼν τὸν Λέαρχον* ‘and having taken Learchus’ converts *prendendo* into a nominative masculine singular second aorist active participle of *λαμβάνω*, preparatory to the finite verb in line 11. Greek makes the name the direct object and adds *ἕνα τῶν παίδων* ‘one of the children’ in apposition (*ἕνα*, masculine accusative singular of *εἷς*, with a partitive genitive plural). This reverses the Italian presentation, *l’ un* first, then the relative clause *che avea nome Learco*.

> 11 e rotollo, e percosselo ad un sasso;  

and whirled him, and dashed him on a rock;

> 11 Καὶ περιστρέψας κατέαξ᾽ ἐπὶ πέτρας·  
> (And whirling him round, dashed him against a rock;)

- *καὶ περιστρέψας* ‘and having whirled [him] round’ coordinates a second nominative masculine singular aorist active participle (from *περιστρέφω*) with *λαβών*. Italian coordinates two finite verbs, *rotollo, e percosselo*, each with its object pronoun; Greek subordinates the whirling to the blow and carries the object over from *τὸν Λέαρχον* without repeating it.
- *κατέαξ᾽ ἐπὶ πέτρας* ‘he smashed [him] against a rock’ has elided *κατέαξε*, 3rd singular aorist active indicative of *κατάγνυμι* ‘break in pieces’ (simple aorist *ἔαξα*), stronger than *percosse*. *ἐπί* with *πέτρας*, genitive singular of *πέτρα*, gives the surface of impact for *ad un sasso*, without an indefinite article.

> 12 e quella s’ annegò con l’ altro carco.  

and she with her other burden drowned herself.

> 12 Ἡ δὲ μήτηρ ἐπνίγη μετὰ θἀτέρου.  
> (And the mother drowned herself with the other.)

- *ἡ δὲ μήτηρ* ‘the mother, on the other hand’ makes *quella* explicit; postpositive *δέ* marks the change of subject. *ἐπνίγη* is the 3rd singular second aorist passive indicative of *πνίγω*, intransitive in sense, ‘drowned’; unlike *s’ annegò* it has no reflexive pronoun.
- *μετὰ θἀτέρου* ‘with the other [one]’ uses *μετά* with the genitive of accompaniment. *θἀτέρου* is crasis of *τοῦ ἑτέρου* (Attic *θάτερος*, ‘the other of two’), here substantival. Greek refers directly to the other child, dropping the noun *carco* that echoed *carcata*.

> 13 E quando la fortuna volse in basso  
> 14 l’ altezza de’ Troian che tutto ardiva,  
> 15 sì che insieme col regno il re fu casso,  

And when Fortune brought low the all-daring pride of the Trojans, so that the King together with his kingdom was blotted out;

> 13 Καὶ καθ’ ὃν καιρὸν ἐταπείνωσ’ ἡ Τύχη  
> (And at the time when Fortune humbled)
>
> 14 Ὕψος Τρωϊκὸν τολμηρόν, κ’ ἠφανίσθη  
> (The daring Trojan height, and there vanished)
>
> 15 Ὅ τε βασιλεὺς καὶ βασίλειον ἅμα,  
> (Both the king and his kingdom together,)

- *καὶ καθ’ ὃν καιρόν* ‘and at which time’ expands *E quando* into a relative temporal phrase, this time with the noun itself inside the phrase in the accusative. *ἐταπείνωσ᾽ ἡ Τύχη* keeps the Italian subject but places it after the verb: elided *ἐταπείνωσε*, 3rd singular aorist active indicative of *ταπεινόω* ‘bring low’, condenses *volse in basso* into one verb.
- *ὕψος Τρωϊκὸν τολμηρόν* ‘daring Trojan height’ is the anarthrous accusative neuter singular object. *ὕψος* retains the image of *l’ altezza*; the agreeing adjectives replace both *de’ Troian* and *che tutto ardiva*. In particular, *τολμηρόν* assigns daring to the ‘height’ itself and leaves *tutto* unexpressed.
- *κ’ ἠφανίσθη* ‘and vanished’ coordinates what Italian marks explicitly as a consequence with *sì che*. *κ’* is a shortened spelling of *καί* before the vowel; *ἠφανίσθη* is the 3rd singular aorist passive indicative of *ἀφανίζω* ‘make disappear’, with temporal augment. It precedes the paired subjects *ὅ τε βασιλεὺς καὶ βασίλειον*: *τε … καί* means ‘both … and’, and *ὅ* here is the nominative article accented before enclitic *τε*, not a relative pronoun.
- Where *insieme col regno il re fu casso* has the king alone as grammatical subject, Greek coordinates masculine *βασιλεύς* and neuter *βασίλειον* ‘kingdom’ as nominatives, king first, without repeating the article. The anticipatory verb is singular, agreeing with the nearer subject; final *ἅμα* retains *insieme* even after the change to coordination.

> 16 Ecuba trista, misera e cattiva,  

Hecuba, sad, miserable, and captive,

> 16 Δύστηνος, τάλαιν’, αἰχμάλωτος Ἑκάβη  
> (Wretched, miserable, captive Hecuba,)

- *δύστηνος, τάλαιν᾽, αἰχμάλωτος Ἑκάβη* delays the name until after all three epithets, without an article or a conjunction. All are nominative feminine singular: *δύστηνος* and *αἰχμάλωτος* have the same form for masculine and feminine, whereas *τάλαιν᾽* is *τάλαινα*, feminine of *τάλας*, with elision before a vowel. *δύστηνος* ‘unfortunate’ renders *trista* in terms of plight, and *αἰχμάλωτος* takes *cattiva* specifically as ‘captive’. *Ἑκάβη* is the subject of the still deferred finite verb in line 20.

> 17 poscia che vide Polissena morta,  
> 18 e del suo Polidoro in su la riva  
> 19 del mar si fu la dolorosa accorta,  

after she had seen Polyxena slain, and, forlorn, discerned her Polydorus, on the sea-strand,

> 17 Ἰδοῦσα νεκρὰν θύγατρα Πολυξένην,  
> (Seeing her daughter Polyxena dead,)
>
> 18 Υἱόν τε Πολύδωρον παρὰ θαλάσσης  
> (And her son Polydorus along the sea's)
>
> 19 Τὴν ἀκτὴν νεκρὸν τληπαθῶς ἀνευροῦσα,  
> (Shore lying dead having sorrowfully discovered,)

- *ἰδοῦσα* is the nominative feminine singular second aorist active participle of *ὁράω*, agreeing with *Ἑκάβη*. Instead of *poscia che* with finite *vide*, the participle alone marks the antecedent circumstance. *νεκρὰν θύγατρα Πολυξένην* ‘her daughter Polyxena dead’ places the predicate adjective first, rather than the name as in *Polissena morta*. *θύγατρα* is the poetic syncopated accusative singular of *θυγάτηρ* (usual Attic *θυγατέρα*); the relationship is supplied, not the possessive.
- *υἱόν τε Πολύδωρον … νεκρόν* balances the previous accusatives, now naming the relationship ‘son’ for Italian possessive *suo*. Postpositive *τε* links the second circumstance to the first. *νεκρόν* is predicative to *υἱὸν Πολύδωρον* and explicitly repeats in the masculine what the Italian only says of Polyxena.
- *παρὰ θαλάσσης τὴν ἀκτήν* renders *in su la riva del mar*. The sense is ‘along the shore of the sea’: *τὴν ἀκτήν* is accusative after *παρά*, with the dependent genitive singular *θαλάσσης* placed before the article and noun. *θάλασσα* has *σσ* where Attic normally has *ττ*.
- *τληπαθῶς ἀνευροῦσα* ‘having sorrowfully discovered’ recasts both *la dolorosa* and *si fu … accorta*. The substantivized Italian adjective becomes an adverb modifying *ἀνευροῦσα*, nominative feminine singular second aorist active participle of *ἀνευρίσκω*. Parallel to *ἰδοῦσα*, it takes a direct object, unlike the Italian pronominal verb with *del suo Polidoro*; its final position holds the two participial phrases together before the main verb.

> 20 forsennata latrò sì come cane:  

she, out of her senses, barked like a dog:

> 20 Ἐμμανὴς ὅλως, ὑλάκτησεν ὡς κύων·  
> (Wholly frenzied, barked like a dog:)

- *ἐμμανὴς ὅλως* ‘altogether frenzied’ renders *forsennata* by an adjective predicated of the subject, nominative feminine singular of the two-ending *ἐμμανής*, with an added adverb of degree. *ὑλάκτησεν*, 3rd singular aorist active indicative of *ὑλακτέω*, finally supplies the verb for *Ἑκάβη*; the initial long vowel bears the rough breathing. *ὡς κύων* gives the comparison with an anarthrous nominative singular, ‘as a dog [does]’, for *sì come cane*.

> 21 tanto il dolor le fe’ la mente torta.  

to such a degree had [the] sorrow wrung her soul.

> 21 Οὕτω τῶν φρενῶν ὑπὸ λύπης ἐξέστη!  
> (So out of her wits through grief did she go!)

- *οὕτω … ἐξέστη* ‘so far did she depart’ keeps the emphasis of initial *tanto* but changes the subject. In *il dolor le fe’ la mente torta* grief acts upon her mind; in Greek Hecuba remains the subject, and *ὑπὸ λύπης* ‘under the influence of grief’ is a prepositional expression of cause.
- *ἐξέστη* is the 3rd singular intransitive second aorist active indicative of *ἐξίστημι*, with internal augment, not the transitive first aorist *ἐξέστησε* ‘displaced’. *τῶν φρενῶν*, genitive plural of *φρήν*, is a genitive of separation: ‘she went out of her senses’. The idiom replaces singular *la mente* and dispenses with both possessive *le* and the image in *torta*.

## Lines 22–54 — The Biting Shades and the Lute-Shaped Figure

> 22 Ma nè di Tebe furie nè Troiane  
> 23 si vider mai in alcun tanto crude,  
> 24 non punger bestie, non che membra umane,  

But neither Theban Furies nor Trojan were ever seen in aught so cruel—not in stinging brutes, and much less human limbs;

> 22 Ἀλλ’ οὔτ’ Ἐριννῦς Θηβῶν οὔτε Τρῳάδες  
> (But neither Furies of Thebes nor Trojan ones)
>
> 23 Τοιάνδε ποτ’ ἔδειξαν σκληροκαρδίαν,  
> (Ever showed such hardness of heart,)
>
> 24 Ὡς μηδὲ θῆρας νύττειν, μή τί γ’ ἀνθρώπους,  
> (As not even to prick beasts, much less human beings,)

- *Ἀλλ’ οὔτ’ Ἐριννῦς Θηβῶν οὔτε Τρῳάδες* retains the adversative and the paired negatives of *Ma nè … nè*. Both *ἀλλά* and the first *οὔτε* undergo elision before a vowel. *Ἐριννῦς* is nominative plural, not singular, and *Θηβῶν* is the genitive of the plural place-name *Θῆβαι*. The two geographical descriptions are asymmetrical: ‘Furies of Thebes’ but ‘Trojan ones’, with feminine nominative plural *Τρῳάδες* answering to *Troiane*.
- *Τοιάνδε ποτ’ ἔδειξαν σκληροκαρδίαν* ‘ever showed such hardness of heart’ makes the Furies the active subject of *ἔδειξαν* (3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *δείκνυμι*), instead of the subject of passive *si vider*. Adjectival *tanto crude* becomes an accusative noun phrase: *τοιάνδε* (fem. acc. sg. of *τοιόσδε*) agrees across the verb with *σκληροκαρδίαν* and anticipates *οἵαν* in 25. *ποτ’* is elided *ποτε* ‘ever’, answering to *mai* under negation; *in alcun* has no separate equivalent.
- *Ὡς μηδὲ θῆρας νύττειν* attaches *ὡς* and a present active infinitive of *νύττω* ‘prick, sting’ to the expression of degree. This resembles a result construction, though the negative sequence follows Italian *non punger bestie, non che* closely: the point of comparison is cruelty not seen even against beasts, not that hardness of heart prevented the stinging. *μή τί γ’* (elided *γε*) marks the stronger case, ‘much less’; *ἀνθρώπους* ‘human beings’ replaces the bodily part *membra umane*. Both *θῆρας* and *ἀνθρώπους* are accusative objects of *νύττειν*.

> 25 quant' io vidi in due ombre smorte e nude,  
> 26 che mordendo correvan di quel modo,  
> 27 che il porco quando del porcil si schiude.  

as I saw [in] two shadows, pale and naked, which ran biting in the manner that a hungry swine does when he is thrust out from his sty.

> 25 Οἵαν ὅτ’ ὠχρὰς γυμνὰς σκιὰς δύ’ εἶδον,  
> (Such as when I saw two pale, naked shades,)
>
> 26 Αἳ δάκνουσαι τοιοῦτον ἔτρεχον τρόπον,  
> (Who biting ran in such a manner,)
>
> 27 Ὡς χοῖρος, ὅτ’ ἐξέρχετ’ ἐκ συφεῶνος.  
> (As a hog, when it rushes out from the sty.)

- *Οἵαν ὅτ’ … εἶδον* ‘such as when I saw …’ distributes *quant’ io vidi in* between a qualitative relative (*οἵαν*, fem. acc. sg., referring back to *σκληροκαρδίαν*) and a temporal clause (*ὅτ’* = *ὅτε*). The showing of comparable cruelty is understood; the direct object of *εἶδον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*) is not the cruelty but *ὠχρὰς γυμνὰς σκιὰς δύ’*. The adjectives precede ‘shades’, and elided *δύο* follows it. The noun and adjectives are plural, not dual.
- *Αἳ δάκνουσαι … ἔτρεχον* keeps both the relative clause and its simultaneous action: *αἵ* and the present active participle of *δάκνω* are feminine nominative plural, and *ἔτρεχον* is the augmented 3rd pl. imperfect of *τρέχω*. *τοιοῦτον … τρόπον* is an adverbial accusative of manner for *di quel modo*, completed by comparative *ὡς* for Italian *che*.
- *χοῖρος, ὅτ’ ἐξέρχετ’ ἐκ συφεῶνος* ‘a hog, when it goes out of a sty’ makes *si schiude* an intransitive verb of motion, *ἐξέρχετ’* (elided *ἐξέρχεται*, 3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *ἐξέρχομαι*). The indicative describes the characteristic action directly. *ἐκ* with genitive *συφεῶνος* repeats the idea of departure in the prefix. Neither “hungry” nor the external agency suggested by “is thrust out” is expressed in the Greek.

> 28 L' una giunse a Capocchio, ed in sul nodo  
> 29 del collo l' assannò sì che tirando  
> 30 grattar gli fece il ventre al fondo sodo.  

The one came to Capocchio, and fixed its tusks on his neck-joint, so that, dragging him, it made the solid bottom claw his belly.

> 28 Ἡ μὲν τὸν Καπόκιον κατέσχ’ ἐκ κόμβου  
> (The one seized Capocchio by the knot)
>
> 29 Τοῦ τραχήλου, καὶ δάκνουσ’ ἔσυρεν οὕτως,  
> (Of the neck, and biting dragged him so,)
>
> 30 Ὥστ’ αὐτὸν τῇ κοιλίᾳ τὴν γῆν σκαλεῦσαι.  
> (That he scraped the ground with his belly.)

- *Ἡ μὲν τὸν Καπόκιον κατέσχ’* ‘the one seized Capocchio’ uses the article pronominally, with *μέν* distinguishing one of the two shades. *κατέσχ’* is elided *κατέσχε*, 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *κατέχω*, with internal augment. Instead of a verb of arrival with *a Capocchio* followed by *l’ assannò*, Greek puts the name in the accusative as the person seized.
- *ἐκ κόμβου τοῦ τραχήλου* ‘by the knot of the neck’ reproduces *nodo del collo*, but *ἐκ* with the genitive marks the point of purchase rather than a position *in sul nodo*. *δάκνουσ’* (elided *δάκνουσα*, fem. nom. sg. pres. act. participle) subordinates the biting, without a word for tusks; conversely *ἔσυρεν* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *σύρω*) makes the dragging, Italian *tirando*, the finite, continuing action.
- *οὕτως, ὥστ’ αὐτὸν τῇ κοιλίᾳ τὴν γῆν σκαλεῦσαι* gives *sì che … gli fece* a result construction: *ὥστ’* is elided *ὥστε*, *αὐτόν* the accusative subject of *σκαλεῦσαι* (aor. act. inf. of *σκαλεύω*). It is he who scrapes *τὴν γῆν* ‘the ground’, with his belly (*τῇ κοιλίᾳ*, instrumental dative). This reverses the relation in Carlyle’s “made the solid bottom claw his belly”; *sodo* has no Greek equivalent.

> 31 E l' Aretin, che rimase tremando,  
> 32 mi disse : “Quel folletto è Gianni Schicchi,  
> 33 e va rabbioso altrui così conciando.”  

And the Aretine, who remained trembling, said to me: “That goblin is Gianni Schicchi; and, rabid, he goes thus mangling others.”

> 31 Ὁ δ’ Ἀῤῥητινός, ὅστις ἔμεινε τρέμων,  
> (And the Aretine, who remained trembling,)
>
> 32 Εἶπε· “Γιάννης Σκίκκης ἔσθ’ ὁ παράφρων  
> (Said: "Gianni Schicchi is that madman)
>
> 33 „ Οὗτος· τρέχει δ᾽ ἐν λύσσῃ σπαράσσων ἄλλους.”  
> (There; and he runs in frenzy mangling others.")

- *Ὁ δ’ Ἀῤῥητινός* introduces a new subject with the article and elided connective *δέ*. The breathing marks on medial *ῤῥ* are an older spelling of double rho. *ὅστις ἔμεινε τρέμων* uses the compound relative *ὅστις* for an explanatory *che* with a definite antecedent. *ἔμεινε* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *μένω*; nominative *τρέμων*, the present active participle of *τρέμω*, gives the state in which he remained.
- *Εἶπε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*) leaves *mi* unexpressed. *Γιάννης Σκίκκης ἔσθ’ ὁ παράφρων οὗτος* puts the predicate name first, with Greek nominative endings. *ἔσθ’* stands for *ἐστί*, elided with aspiration of *τ* before the rough breathing of *ὁ*. Article, substantivized adjective and demonstrative form the subject ‘this madman’: *παράφρων* interprets *folletto* by behaviour rather than kind of being.
- *τρέχει δ᾽ ἐν λύσσῃ σπαράσσων ἄλλους* makes generalized *va* the specific ‘runs’ (3rd sg. pres. act. ind.). *ἐν λύσσῃ* ‘in a frenzy’ replaces the adjective *rabbioso*; *σπαράσσων* (masc. nom. sg. pres. act. participle of *σπαράσσω*) turns *così conciando* into the explicit ‘tearing apart’. Its object *ἄλλους* is accusative plural for indefinite *altrui*.

> 34 “O,” diss' io lui, “se l' altro non ti ficchi  
> 35 li denti addosso, non ti sia fatica  
> 36 a dir chi è, pria che di qui si spicchi.”  

“Oh!” said I to him, “so may the other not plant its teeth on thee, be pleased to tell us who it is, ere it snatch itself away.”

> 34 Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν· “Εἴθε θἄτερον πνεῦμα  
> (And I to him: "May the other spirit)
>
> 35 „ Μὴ τοὺς ὀδόντας ἐμπήξαι σοι ! πλὴν λέγε,  
> (Not fix its teeth into you! yet tell me,)
>
> 36 „ Εἴ σ᾽ ἀρέσκει, τίς ἐστι, πρὶν ἐκχωρήσῃ.”  
> (If it pleases you, who it is, before it departs.")

- *Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν* ‘and I to him’ omits both the interjection and the verb of saying; *κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*. *Εἴθε θἄτερον πνεῦμα* turns the wish introduced by *se* into an independent wish with *εἴθε*. *θἄτερον* is the Attic crasis for *τὸ ἕτερον*, neuter nominative singular with the supplied *πνεῦμα* ‘spirit’.
- *Μὴ τοὺς ὀδόντας ἐμπήξαι σοι* has *μή*, the negative of a wish. Here *ἐμπήξαι* is 3rd sg. aor. act. **optative** of *ἐμπήγνυμι* ‘drive in’, a short form in *-αι* beside *-ειε(ν)*: the optative after *εἴθε* expresses a wish still capable of fulfilment. *τοὺς ὀδόντας* is the direct object, *σοι* the dative of the person affected, doing the work of *ti … addosso*.
- *πλὴν λέγε, εἴ σ᾽ ἀρέσκει* ‘only tell, if it pleases you’ replaces the negative courtesy *non ti sia fatica a dir* with an imperative (*λέγε*, 2nd sg. pres. act.) softened by a condition. Elided *σ᾽* is accusative *σε*, not the dative usual for the person pleased in classical *ἀρέσκω*. No recipient of ‘tell’ is specified. *τίς ἐστι* is an embedded identity question. *πρὶν ἐκχωρήσῃ* has the aorist active subjunctive (3rd sg. of *ἐκχωρέω*) for the anticipated departure, closely paralleling *pria che … si spicchi*; after a positive main clause Attic would more usually have *πρίν* with the infinitive. The prefix suggests departure without a separate *di qui*.

> 37 Ed egli a me : “Quell' è l' anima antica  
> 38 di Mirra scellerata, che divenne  
> 39 al padre, fuor del dritto amore, amica.  

And he to me: “That is the ancient spirit of flagitious Myrrha, who loved her father with more than rightful love.

> 37 Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ· “Μιαρᾶς ἐστιν αὕτη  
> (And he to me: "This is foul)
>
> 38 „ Μύῤῥας ἀρχαία σκιά, τῆς γενομένης  
> (Myrrha's ancient shade, who became)
>
> 39 „ Πατρὸς ἑαυτῆς ἀνοσίως ἑταίρας.  
> (Impiously the paramour of her own father.)

- *Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ* ‘and he to me’ matches *Ed egli a me* in leaving the verb of speech understood; *ὁ* is pronominal. In *Μιαρᾶς ἐστιν αὕτη Μύῤῥας ἀρχαία σκιά*, the feminine demonstrative *αὕτη* is subject, *ἀρχαία σκιά* predicate. Greek has ‘this’ for *Quell’* and ‘shade’ for *anima*. *μιαρᾶς* ‘polluted, foul’ is brought emphatically forward, separated by copula and subject from its genitive noun *Μύῤῥας*.
- *τῆς γενομένης … ἑταίρας* replaces the finite relative *che divenne … amica* with an articular participial phrase in apposition to the genitive name. *γενομένης* is fem. gen. sg. second aor. mid. participle of *γίγνομαι*; predicate *ἑταίρας* ‘paramour’ takes the same case. The ambiguity of *amica* is thus resolved lexically rather than by adding another noun for love.
- *πατρὸς ἑαυτῆς* ‘of her own father’ is genitive dependent on *ἑταίρας*, in place of Italian *al padre*; the feminine reflexive genitive *ἑαυτῆς* explicitly identifies the possessor. *ἀνοσίως* ‘impiously’ turns *fuor del dritto amore* into an adverb modifying the becoming.

> 40 Questa a peccar con esso così venne,  
> 41 falsificando sè in altrui forma,  

She came to sin with him disguised in alien form;

> 40 „ Ἄγος τόδ᾽ εἰργάσατο μετ᾽ αὐτοῦ, σχῆμα  
> (This abomination she wrought with him, the form)
>
> 41 „ Ἄλλης γυναικὸς δόλῳ μεταλαβοῦσα,  
> (Of another woman having deceitfully assumed,)

- *Ἄγος τόδ᾽ εἰργάσατο μετ᾽ αὐτοῦ* ‘this abomination she wrought with him’ shifts the demonstrative from the Italian subject *Questa* to the neuter accusative object *ἄγος τόδε*. *εἰργάσατο* is the augmented 3rd sg. aor. mid. ind. of deponent *ἐργάζομαι*: a completed act replaces *a peccar … venne*. *μετ᾽* is elided *μετά* with the genitive of association.
- *σχῆμα ἄλλης γυναικὸς δόλῳ μεταλαβοῦσα* ‘having deceitfully taken on another woman’s form’ specifies a woman where *altrui* is indefinite. *σχῆμα* is the object of *μεταλαβοῦσα* (fem. nom. sg. second aor. act. participle of *μεταλαμβάνω*), which agrees with the unexpressed subject. The aorist presents the assumption of shape as preliminary to the finite action; instrumental *δόλῳ* ‘by deceit’ carries the falsity in *falsificando*. There is no reflexive object corresponding to *sè*, nor a separate *così*.

> 42 come l' altro, che là sen va, sostenne,  
> 43 per guadagnar la donna della torma,  
> 44 falsificare in sè Buoso Donati,  
> 45 testando, e dando al testamento norma.”  

even as the other who there is going away, undertook, that he might gain the Lady of the troop, to disguise himself as Buoso Donati, making a testament and giving to it legal form.”

> 42 „ Ὥσπερ ἐκεῖνος, ὃς ἐκεῖ κάτω τρέχει,  
> (Even as he, who runs down yonder,)
>
> 43 „ Ἵνα κερδάνῃ τὴν ὡραίαν φορβάδα,  
> (In order to gain the fair mare,)
>
> 44 „ Πλάσας ἑαυτὸν ὡς Βουῶσον Δωνάτον,  
> (Having feigned himself as Buoso Donati,)
>
> 45 „ Διέθετο δοὺς κῦρος τῇ διαθήκῃ.”  
> (Made a will, giving validity to the testament.")

- *Ὥσπερ ἐκεῖνος, ὃς ἐκεῖ κάτω τρέχει* ‘just as that man, who runs down there’ keeps comparison and relative clause. *-περ* reinforces *ὡς*; masculine *ἐκεῖνος* replaces *l’ altro*. *τρέχει* resumes the earlier verb ‘runs’; *ἐκεῖ κάτω* adds a downward location to *là*, without the explicit motion away of *sen va*.
- *ἵνα κερδάνῃ τὴν ὡραίαν φορβάδα* translates *per guadagnar* by a final clause, *κερδάνῃ* being 3rd sg. aor. act. subj. of *κερδαίνω*. The subjunctive presents the intended gain directly even in past narration; an oblique optative was possible but not required. *φορβάδα* (acc. sg. of *φορβάς*) identifies *la donna della torma* as a mare; *ὡραίαν* ‘fair’ replaces, rather than translates, her designation as lady of the herd.
- *πλάσας ἑαυτὸν ὡς Βουῶσον Δωνάτον* ‘having fashioned himself as Buoso Donati’ reverses the distribution in *falsificare in sè Buoso Donati*: the reflexive is now the direct object, the assumed identity marked by *ὡς* with an accusative name. *πλάσας* (masc. nom. sg. aor. act. participle of *πλάσσω*) agrees with *ἐκεῖνος*. There is no governing verb for *sostenne* ‘undertook’.
- Instead the main verb is *διέθετο*, 3rd sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *διατίθημι*, with internal augment and the middle sense ‘disposed of his property by will’. Thus *testando* becomes finite, while *dando* becomes *δούς* (masc. nom. sg. aor. act. participle of *δίδωμι*). *κῦρος* is its neuter accusative object, ‘validity’ rather than literally *norma*; *τῇ διαθήκῃ* is the recipient dative, matching *al testamento* and echoing the root of *διέθετο*.

> 46 E poi che i due rabbiosi fur passati,  
> 47 sopra cui io avea l' occhio tenuto,  
> 48 rivolsilo a guardar gli altri mal nati.  

And when the furious two, on whom I had kept my eye, were passed, I turned it to observe the other ill-born spirits.

> 46 Εἶτα τῶν δύο λυσσωδῶν διαβάντων,  
> (Then when the two frenzied ones had passed by,)
>
> 47 Ἐφ᾽ οὓς εἶχον τὰς ὄψεις ἐπηρεισμένας,  
> (Upon whom I had my eyes fixed,)
>
> 48 Ἔτρεψ᾽ αὐτάς, ἵν᾽ ἴδω δυστήνους ἄλλους.  
> (I turned them, so that I might see other wretches.)

- *Εἶτα τῶν δύο λυσσωδῶν διαβάντων* gives *E poi che … fur passati* an adverb ‘then’ and a temporal genitive absolute. The numeral *δύο* is unchanged; article, substantivized adjective *λυσσωδῶν* ‘frenzied ones’ and participle are genitive plural, not dual. *διαβάντων* is the masc. gen. pl. root-aorist active participle of *διαβαίνω*: passing is complete before the narrator turns.
- *Ἐφ᾽ οὓς εἶχον τὰς ὄψεις ἐπηρεισμένας* ‘upon whom I had my eyes fixed’ keeps the direction in *sopra cui* with *ἐπί* and the accusative relative; elision before the rough breathing gives *ἐφ᾽*. *εἶχον* (here 1st sg. imperf. act. of *ἔχω*) with a predicative perfect middle/passive participle of *ἐπερείδω* expresses a maintained state, rather than simply reproducing the pluperfect *avea … tenuto*. *ἐπηρεισμένας* agrees with feminine accusative plural *τὰς ὄψεις*, plural for singular *l’ occhio*.
- *Ἔτρεψ᾽ αὐτάς* (elided *ἔτρεψα*, 1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *τρέπω*) accordingly resumes the eyes by a feminine plural pronoun instead of the singular clitic in *rivolsilo*. *ἵν᾽ ἴδω* replaces *a guardar* with a purpose clause, elided *ἵνα* and 1st sg. second aor. act. subj. of *ὁράω*. *δυστήνους ἄλλους* ‘other wretches’ renders *gli altri mal nati* by accusative plural adjectives, without an article or a literal reference to birth.

> 49 Io vidi un, fatto a guisa di liuto,  
> 50 pur ch' egli avesse avuta l' anguinaia  
> 51 tronca dal lato che l' uomo ha forcuto.  

I saw one shapen like a lute, if he had only had his groin cut short at the part where man is forked.

> 49 Τότ᾽ εἶδόν τιν᾽, ὃς σχῆμ᾽ ἂν εἶχε βαρβίτου,  
> (Then I saw one, who would have had the shape of a lute,)
>
> 50 Εἰ τετμημένον τὸν βουβῶν᾽ εἶχ᾽ ἐκ μέρους,  
> (If he had had his groin cut off on the side,)
>
> 51 Ἔνθα τἀνθρώπου διασχίζεται σῶμα.  
> (Where the body of man is cleft in two.)

- *Τότ᾽ εἶδόν τιν᾽* ‘then I saw someone’ supplies a temporal link (*τότ᾽* = *τότε*); *τιν᾽* is elided accusative singular *τινά*, the enclitic indefinite accounting for the accent on the final syllable of *εἶδόν*. Italian *fatto a guisa di liuto* becomes a relative clause, *ὃς σχῆμ᾽ ἂν εἶχε βαρβίτου* ‘who would have the shape of a barbitos’. Elided *σχῆμα* is the object of *εἶχε*; genitive *βαρβίτου* specifies whose shape, substituting a Greek stringed instrument for *liuto*.
- *ἂν εἶχε … εἰ … εἶχ᾽* pairs a counterfactual conclusion (*ἄν* with 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἔχω*) with a condition (*εἰ* and the same imperfect, elided). The condition expressed in Italian by *pur ch’ … avesse avuta* thus governs the resemblance itself in Greek; there is no separate restrictive particle for *pur*. *τετμημένον τὸν βουβῶν᾽* ‘his groin in a cut state’ has the perfect middle/passive participle of *τέμνω* as predicate to *τὸν βουβῶνα* (masc. acc. sg., elided), closely matching *l’ anguinaia tronca*.
- *ἐκ μέρους, ἔνθα* ‘at the part where’ renders *dal lato che* with a local relative adverb. *τἀνθρώπου* is crasis of *τοῦ ἀνθρώπου*, a possessive genitive separated from its head *σῶμα* by *διασχίζεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *διασχίζω*, passive in sense). Instead of *l’ uomo ha forcuto*, Greek makes ‘the human body’ the subject of ‘is split’.

> 52 La grave idropisì, che sì dispaia  
> 53 le membra con l' umor che mal converte  
> 54 che il viso non risponde alla ventraia,  

The heavy dropsy, which with its ill-digested humour so disproportions the limbs, that the visage corresponds not to the paunch,

> 52 Ὁ τυμπανόεις ὕδρωψ, ὃς παραλλάσσει,  
> (The tympanic dropsy, which alters,)
>
> 53 Τοὺς χυμοὺς παρεκτρέπων, οὕτω τὰ μέλη,  
> (Perverting the humours, the limbs in such wise,)
>
> 54 Ὥστ᾽ οὐ πρόσωπον ἀναλογεῖ κοιλίᾳ,  
> (That the face does not correspond to the belly,)

- *Ὁ τυμπανόεις ὕδρωψ* replaces *La grave idropisì* with a masculine noun and a descriptive adjective: *τυμπανόεις* is a poetic formation in *-όεις*, ‘drum-like’, evoking distension rather than translating *grave* ‘heavy’. *ὃς παραλλάσσει* has the corresponding masculine nominative relative and 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *παραλλάσσω* ‘alter’; its accusative object *τὰ μέλη* is postponed into the next line.
- *τοὺς χυμοὺς παρεκτρέπων* ‘diverting the humours’ turns *con l’ umor che mal converte* into a participial expression of means. *παρεκτρέπων* (masc. nom. sg. pres. act. participle of *παρεκτρέπω*) agrees with the relative subject; the humours are plural and directly acted upon. The compound verb expresses deviation in place of a separate *mal*.
- *οὕτω … ὥστ᾽* retains the degree and consequence of *sì … che*. Here, unlike line 30, elided *ὥστε* introduces a finite assertion of actual result: *οὐ πρόσωπον ἀναλογεῖ κοιλίᾳ*. *οὐ* negates the correspondence, *πρόσωπον* is the neuter nominative subject, and *ἀναλογεῖ* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἀναλογέω*) takes the dative of comparison *κοιλίᾳ*, closely matching *il viso non risponde alla ventraia*. Both Greek nouns lack the Italian articles; the relative description ends here, not the construction begun with *ὁ … ὕδρωψ*.

## Lines 55–87 — Master Adam’s Thirst and His Wish for Revenge

> 55 faceva a lui tener le labbra aperte,  
> 56 come l' etico fa, che per la sete  
> 57 l' un verso il mento e l' altro in su rinverte.  

made him hold his lips apart, as does the hectic patient, who for thirst curls the one lip towards the chin, and the other upwards.

> 55 Ἐβίαζ’ ἔχειν τὰ χείλη κεχηνότα,  
> (Forced him to hold his lips parted wide,)
>
> 56 Ὡς ὁ φθισικὸς τῇ δίψῃ πάσχων στρέφει  
> (As the consumptive suffering from thirst turns)
>
> 57 Τῶν χειλέων ἓν ἄνω, θἄτερον κάτω.  
> (One of his lips upward, the other downward.)

- *Ἐβίαζ’ ἔχειν* ‘forced [him] to hold’ renders *faceva a lui tener* by a verb of compulsion, not simply causation. *Ἐβίαζ’* is *ἐβίαζε*, 3rd sg. imperfect active indicative of *βιάζω*, with syllabic augment and elision of the final vowel; its subject is carried over from before line 55. The person compelled, expressed by *a lui*, is understood as the subject of the present active infinitive *ἔχειν*.
- *Τὰ χείλη κεχηνότα* ‘the lips gaping’ gives *aperte* a verbal equivalent: *κεχηνότα*, acc. neut. pl. of the perfect active participle of *χαίνω*, is predicative, agreeing with *χείλη* (contracted plural of *χεῖλος*). The perfect denotes the state in which the lips are held.
- *Ὡς ὁ φθισικὸς … στρέφει* ‘as the consumptive turns’ dispenses with the pro-verb *fa* and the following relative *che*: *στρέφει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *στρέφω*) is itself the verb of the comparison. *Ὁ φθισικός* is a substantivized adjective, like *l’ etico*; *πάσχων*, nom. masc. sg. present active participle of *πάσχω*, supplies ‘suffering’, with *τῇ δίψῃ* a dative of cause.
- *Τῶν χειλέων ἓν ἄνω, θἄτερον κάτω* puts the partitive genitive ‘of the lips’ first; *χειλέων* is the uncontracted form beside Attic *χειλῶν*. The two objects are neuter singulars, *ἕν* ‘one’ and *θἄτερον* (Attic crasis of *τὸ ἕτερον*) ‘the other’; there is no grammatical dual. Musurus reverses the order of the directions and makes them a pair of adverbs, ‘up … down’, dropping the specific *verso il mento*.

> 58 "O voi, che senza alcuna pena siete,  
> 59 (e non so io perchè), nel mondo gramo,"  

“O ye! who are exempt from every punishment (and why I know not), in this grim world,”

> 58 —“Ὑμεῖς, οἵτινες ἔρχεσθ᾽ ἀτιμωρήτως,  
> (—"You, who come without punishment,)
>
> 59 „ Ἀγνοῶ δὲ πῶς, εἰς οἰκτρὸν κόσμον τόνδε,  
> (And I know not how, into this piteous world,)

- *Ὑμεῖς, οἵτινες ἔρχεσθ’ ἀτιμωρήτως* addresses the hearers by the nominative pronoun ‘you’, without *ὦ*. *Οἵτινες* (nom. masc. pl. of *ὅστις*) characterizes them as persons who come in this way; *ἔρχεσθ’* is *ἔρχεσθε*, 2nd pl. present middle indicative of *ἔρχομαι*, elided. *Ἀτιμωρήτως* ‘unpunished’ compresses *senza alcuna pena* into an adverb.
- The change from *siete* to a verb of motion changes the syntax of *nel mondo gramo*: *εἰς οἰκτρὸν κόσμον τόνδε* is a goal, ‘into this piteous world’, not a location. The demonstrative *τόνδε* (acc. masc. sg. of *ὅδε*) makes the reference explicitly local.
- *Ἀγνοῶ δὲ πῶς* interrupts verb and goal as the Italian parenthesis interrupts the address. *Ἀγνοῶ* (1st sg. present active indicative of *ἀγνοέω*) contains the negative sense of *non so*; *δέ* is postpositive, and interrogative *πῶς* asks ‘how’, not *perchè* ‘why’.

> 60 diss' egli a noi, "guardate ed attendete  
> 61 alla miseria del maestro Adamo:  

said he to us, “look and attend to the misery of Master Adam

> 60 „ Ἴδετ᾽,” εἶπ᾽ αὐτὸς ἡμῖν, “ῥίψατε βλέμμα  
> (Look," said he to us, "cast a glance)
>
> 61 „ Οἴκτου πρὸς τὸν δυστυχῆ Μήστορ᾽ Ἀδάμον.  
> (Of pity toward the unfortunate Master Adam.)

- The Greek places *Ἴδετ’* before the reporting clause, then resumes the address with *ῥίψατε βλέμμα*. *Ἴδετ’* is elided *ἴδετε*, 2nd pl. second-aorist active imperative of *ὁράω*; *εἶπ’* is elided *εἶπε*, 3rd sg. second-aorist active indicative of *λέγω*. *Αὐτός* supplies the explicit subject corresponding to *egli*, *ἡμῖν* the dative recipient corresponding to *a noi*.
- *Ῥίψατε βλέμμα οἴκτου* ‘cast a glance of pity’ replaces *attendete alla miseria*: *ῥίψατε* is the 2nd pl. aorist active imperative of *ῥίπτω*, and *οἴκτου* a genitive qualifying the glance. Misery as the object of attention becomes pity as the quality of the look; its direction is given by *πρός* with the accusative of the person.
- *Τὸν δυστυχῆ Μήστορ’ Ἀδάμον* ‘the unfortunate Master Adam’ transfers the force of *miseria* to *δυστυχῆ* (acc. masc. sg. of *δυστυχής*). *Μήστορ’* is elided *Μήστορα*, accusative of *μήστωρ*, a poetic word for a counsellor or deviser used here for the title *maestro*; *Ἀδάμον* is likewise inflected as an accusative.

> 62 io ebbi, vivo, assai di quel ch' io volli,  
> 63 ed ora, lasso! un gocciol d' acqua bramo.  

: when alive, I had enough of what I wished; and now, alas! I crave one little drop of water.

> 62 „ Ζῶν εἶχον ἅλις πάντων, ὧν ἐβουλόμην·  
> (While living I had enough of all that I desired;)
>
> 63 „ Νῦν δ᾽ ὕδατος, φεῦ! ποθῶ σφοδρῶς ῥανίδα.  
> (And now, alas! I fiercely crave a drop of water.)

- *Ζῶν* (nom. masc. sg. present active participle of *ζάω*) takes the predicative *vivo* temporally, ‘while living’. *Εἶχον* is imperfect active indicative of *ἔχω*, here 1st sg.: the Greek presents *ebbi* as a continuing possession. *Ἅλις πάντων* ‘enough of all things’ uses an adverb of sufficiency with a partitive genitive and makes ‘all’ explicit.
- *Ὧν ἐβουλόμην* ‘that I wanted’ has the relative attracted into the genitive of *πάντων* rather than the accusative expected as object of *βούλομαι*. *Ἐβουλόμην* is 1st sg. imperfect middle indicative, with syllabic augment; like *εἶχον*, it gives past duration where the Italian has a preterite.
- *Νῦν δ’ … φεῦ!* sets elided *δέ* against the former state and substitutes an interjection for *lasso*. *Ὕδατος* is a genitive of contents, separated from *ῥανίδα* (acc. sg. of *ῥανίς*, ‘drop’) by the interjection and *ποθῶ σφοδρῶς* ‘I keenly desire’. *Ποθῶ* is the contracted 1st sg. present active indicative of *ποθέω*; the added adverb intensifies the verb rather than diminishing the drop by an adjective.

> 64 Li ruscelletti, che dei verdi colli  
> 65 del Casentin discendon giuso in Arno,  
> 66 facendo i lor canali freddi e molli,  
> 67 sempre mi stanno innanzi, e non indarno:  

The rivulets that from the verdant hills of Casentino descend into the Arno, making their channels cool and moist, stand constantly before me, and not in vain:

> 64 „ Τοὺς ῥύακας, οἵτινες ἐκ χλωρῶν λόφων  
> (The rivulets, which from the green hills)
>
> 65 „ Τοῦ Κασεντίνου καταβαίνουσ᾽ εἰς Ἄρνον,  
> (Of Casentino descend into the Arno,)
>
> 66 „ Ψυχροὺς χἁπαλοὺς ὑδραγωγοὺς ποιοῦντες,  
> (Making cool and soft watercourses,)
>
> 67 „ ᾈεὶ πρὸ τῶν ὀφθαλμῶν ἔχω καὶ πάσχω.  
> (I have ever before my eyes, and suffer.)

- *Τοὺς ῥύακας* (acc. pl. of *ῥύαξ*) opens the sentence like *Li ruscelletti*, but its case anticipates *ἔχω* three lines later: not ‘the streams stand before me’ but ‘I have the streams before my eyes’. The noun has no diminutive suffix. *Οἵτινες* is nominative as subject of the intervening relative clause despite its accusative antecedent.
- *Ἐκ χλωρῶν λόφων τοῦ Κασεντίνου* distinguishes source (*ἐκ* with the genitive) from the hills’ geographical specification (dependent genitive). *Καταβαίνουσ’* is elided *καταβαίνουσι*, 3rd pl. present active indicative of *καταβαίνω*; the downward direction of *discendon giuso* is conveyed by the compound alone. *Εἰς Ἄρνον* gives the destination in the accusative.
- *Ψυχροὺς χἁπαλοὺς ὑδραγωγοὺς ποιοῦντες* retains *facendo* as a present active participle, *ποιοῦντες* (nom. masc. pl. of *ποιέω*), agreeing with the relative subject. *Χἁπαλούς* is crasis of *καὶ ἁπαλούς*, with aspiration from the second word. The adjectives precede *ὑδραγωγούς* ‘watercourses’; the phrase can read ‘making cool and soft watercourses’, without the explicit possessive *lor*. *Ἁπαλούς* stresses softness rather than wetness in *molli*.
- *ᾈεὶ πρὸ τῶν ὀφθαλμῶν ἔχω* supplies ‘eyes’ after *πρό* with the genitive. *Καὶ πάσχω* ‘and I suffer’ turns *e non indarno* from a negated adverbial qualification into a second finite verb (1st sg. present active indicative), naming the effect.

> 68 chè l' imagine lor vie più m' asciuga,  
> 69 che il male ond' io nel volto mi discarno.  

for the image of them dries me up far more than the disease which from my visage wears the flesh.

> 68 „ Ἡ ποθητὴ γὰρ εἰκών μ᾽ ἐξαυχμοῖ μᾶλλον  
> (For the longed-for image dries me up more)
>
> 69 „ Ἢ τὸ πάθος, ὃ κατισχνοῖ μου τὴν ὄψιν.  
> (Than the malady, which wastes away my face.)

- *Ἡ ποθητὴ γὰρ εἰκών* has explanatory, postpositive *γάρ* for *chè*. *Ποθητή* ‘longed-for’ supplies an adjective echoing *ποθῶ*; there is no equivalent possessive for *lor*. *Μ’* is elided accusative *με*, object of *ἐξαυχμοῖ* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *ἐξαυχμόω*, ‘parches’).
- *Μᾶλλον ἢ τὸ πάθος* gives the ordinary comparative ‘more than the affliction’, without a separate intensifier for *vie*. *Ὃ κατισχνοῖ μου τὴν ὄψιν* recasts *ond’ io nel volto mi discarno*: the malady, represented by nominative neuter relative *ὅ*, acts directly on the face. *Κατισχνοῖ* is 3rd sg. present active indicative of *κατισχνόω* ‘makes lean’; *μου* is possessive with the accusative object *τὴν ὄψιν*. Neither the Italian first-person subject nor its reflexive construction is retained.

> 70 La rigida giustizia, che mi fruga,  
> 71 tragge cagion del loco ov' io peccai  
> 72 a metter più li miei sospiri in fuga.  

The rigid Justice, which searches me, takes occasion from the place at which I sinned, to give my sighs a quicker flight.

> 70 „ Κολάζει δέ μ᾽ ἡ σκληρὰ Δικαιοσύνη,  
> (And stern Justice chastises me,)
>
> 71 „ Ἐκ χώρας, ἔνθ᾽ ἥμαρτον, ἕλκουσα τρόπους,  
> (Drawing means from the place where I sinned,)
>
> 72 „ Ἵν᾽ ἔτι μᾶλλον αὐξάνῃ μου τοὺς στόνους.  
> (To increase my groans yet the more.)

- *Κολάζει δέ μ’ ἡ σκληρὰ Δικαιοσύνη* promotes *che mi fruga* to the main assertion, ‘and stern Justice punishes me’. *Κολάζει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *κολάζω*) precedes its subject; elided *με* is its object. The Greek specifies punishment rather than the searching or goading action of *fruga*.
- Conversely, the main verb *tragge* becomes *ἕλκουσα* (nom. fem. sg. present active participle of *ἕλκω*), agreeing with *Δικαιοσύνη*. *Ἐκ χώρας … ἕλκουσα τρόπους* is ‘drawing means from a place’, plural *τρόπους* for singular *cagion*. *Ἔνθ’* is elided relative adverb *ἔνθα* ‘where’; *ἥμαρτον* is 1st sg. second-aorist active indicative of *ἁμαρτάνω*, with temporal augment.
- *Ἵν’ ἔτι μᾶλλον αὐξάνῃ μου τοὺς στόνους* turns the Italian final infinitive into a purpose clause: elided *ἵνα* governs *αὐξάνῃ*, 3rd sg. present active subjunctive of *αὐξάνω*. *Ἔτι μᾶλλον* ‘still more’ modifies the increase; *τοὺς στόνους* ‘the groans’, with possessive *μου*, is its direct object. The figure of putting sighs to flight disappears.

> 73 Ivi è Romena, là dov' io falsai  
> 74 la lega sigillata del Batista:  
> 75 per ch' io il corpo suso arso lasciai.  

There is Romena where I falsified the alloy, sealed with the Baptist's image : for which on earth I left my body burnt.

> 73 „ Ἐνόθευσα γὰρ ἔνθα κεῖται Ῥωμένα  
> (For I debased, there where Romena lies,)
>
> 74 „ Τὸ νόμισμ᾽, ὃ Βαπτιστοῦ φέρει σφραγῖδα·  
> (The coin that bears the stamp of the Baptist;)
>
> 75 „ Διὸ τὸ σῶμα καυστὸν ἔλιπον ἄνω.  
> (Wherefore I left my body burned above.)

- *Ἐνόθευσα γὰρ ἔνθα κεῖται Ῥωμένα* reverses the hierarchy of *Ivi è Romena, là dov’ io falsai*: ‘for I adulterated [the coin] where Romena lies’. *Ἐνόθευσα* is 1st sg. aorist active indicative of *νοθεύω*, with augment; *γάρ* adds an explanatory connection. Locative *ἔνθα* introduces *κεῖται*, 3rd sg. present middle-form indicative of *κεῖμαι* ‘lie, be situated’, with *Ῥωμένα* its subject.
- The delayed object *τὸ νόμισμ’* (elided *νόμισμα*) makes *la lega* the coin rather than its alloy. *Ὃ Βαπτιστοῦ φέρει σφραγῖδα* renders passive *sigillata* by an active relative clause, ‘which bears a stamp of the Baptist’: *ὅ* is nom. neut. sg., *φέρει* 3rd sg. present active indicative, *σφραγῖδα* acc. sg. of *σφραγίς*, and *Βαπτιστοῦ* its dependent genitive.
- *Διό* ‘wherefore’ condenses *per ch’*. *Τὸ σῶμα καυστὸν ἔλιπον ἄνω* keeps *il corpo … arso lasciai* as object, predicate, and verb: *καυστόν* is a verbal adjective, here resultative, not a participle. *Ἔλιπον* is 1st sg. second-aorist active indicative of *λείπω*, with syllabic augment and the short root vowel of the aorist. Final *ἄνω* ‘above’ corresponds directly to *suso*.

> 76 Ma s' io vedessi qui l' anima trista  
> 77 di Guido o d' Alessandro o di lor frate,  
> 78 per fonte Branda non darei la vista.  

But if I could see the miserable soul of Guido here, or of Alessandro, or their brother, for Branda's fount I would not give the sight.

> 76 „ Ἀλλ᾽ εἴ γ᾽ ἔβλεπον ἐνταῦθα τὴν Γουΐδου,  
> (But if I saw here Guido's,)
>
> 77 „ Ἀλεξάνδρου τε ψυχήν, ἢ ταὐταδέλφου,  
> (And Alessandro's soul, or their brother's,)
>
> 78 „ Προειλόμην ἂν τοῦτο τῆς κρήνης Βράνδας.  
> (I would prefer that to the fountain of Branda.)

- *Ἀλλ’ εἴ γ’ ἔβλεπον ἐνταῦθα* has elided *ἀλλά* and focusing *γε*, ‘but if indeed I saw here’. *Ἔβλεπον* is 1st sg. imperfect active indicative of *βλέπω*: Greek uses a past indicative for the unreal condition where Italian uses imperfect subjunctive *vedessi*.
- *Τὴν Γουΐδου, Ἀλεξάνδρου τε ψυχήν* separates article and noun by the genitives of the names. *Τε* is an enclitic connective, ‘and’, not the first disjunctive *o*; *ἤ* does render the second *o*. *Ταὐταδέλφου* compresses *τοῦ αὐταδέλφου* by crasis, ‘of the own brother’, leaving *lor* implicit. No adjective renders *trista*.
- *Προειλόμην ἂν τοῦτο τῆς κρήνης Βράνδας* ‘I would choose this in preference to Branda’s fountain’ gives an affirmative preference for the negative exchange *non darei la vista*. *Προειλόμην* is 1st sg. second-aorist middle indicative of *προαιρέω* (middle *προαιρέομαι*, ‘choose for oneself’); the past indicative with *ἄν*, not an optative, forms the unreal result. *Τοῦτο* refers to the proposed sight without naming it; *τῆς κρήνης* is the genitive of the alternative passed over, with *Βράνδας* genitive of the name.

> 79 Dentro c' è l' una già, se l' arrabbiate  
> 80 ombre che vanno intorno dicon vero;  

One is in already, if the mad shadows that are going round speak true;

> 79 „ Ἔνδον ἔσθ᾽ ἡ μί᾽ αὐτῶν, εἴ γ᾽ αἱ λυσσώδεις  
> (One of them is within, if the raging)
>
> 80 „ Περιβαίνουσαι σκιαὶ τἀληθὲς εἶπον.  
> (Shades that go round about have spoken the truth.)

- *Ἔνδον ἔσθ’ ἡ μί’ αὐτῶν* ‘within is the one of them’ retains the initial locative *Dentro*. *Ἔσθ’* is elided *ἔστι*, with aspiration before *ἡ*; *μί’* is elided *μία*, nom. fem. sg., referring back to *ψυχή*. *Αὐτῶν* makes the partitive relation explicit. *Già* has no separate equivalent.
- *Εἴ γ’* ‘if indeed’ introduces an indicative condition. *Αἱ λυσσώδεις περιβαίνουσαι σκιαί* puts adjective and participle between article and noun: *λυσσώδεις* is nom. fem. pl. of *λυσσώδης* ‘raging’, and *περιβαίνουσαι*, the agreeing present active participle of *περιβαίνω*, replaces *che vanno intorno*.
- *Τἀληθὲς εἶπον* ‘spoke the truth’ makes *vero* a substantivized neuter accusative (*τἀληθές* by crasis from *τὸ ἀληθές*). *Εἶπον* here is 3rd pl. second-aorist active indicative of *λέγω*: the report is a past utterance rather than the present *dicon*.

> 81 ma che mi val, ch' ho le membra legate?  

but what avails it me whose limbs are tied?

> 81 „ Πλὴν τί τοὔφελος ἔχοντι δετὰ μέλη ;  
> (Yet what is the profit to one whose limbs are bound?)

- *Πλὴν τί τοὔφελος* ‘yet what [is] the benefit?’ changes *che mi val* into a nominal question with the copula understood. *Πλήν* is adversative; *τοὔφελος* is crasis of *τὸ ὄφελος*, a neuter nominative.
- *Ἔχοντι δετὰ μέλη* ‘for [me] having bound limbs’ absorbs both *mi* and *ch’ ho* into the dative of the interested person. *Ἔχοντι* is dat. masc. sg. present active participle of *ἔχω*, agreeing with an understood ‘me’; *μέλη* is contracted acc. neut. pl. of *μέλος* and *δετά* the agreeing verbal adjective from *δέω* ‘bind’. Unlike the implicit possessor, the limbs are not in the dative.

> 82 S' io fossi pur di tanto ancor leggiero,  
> 83 ch' io potessi in cent' anni andare un' oncia,  

Were I only still so light, that I could move one inch in a hundred years,

> 82 „ Ἀλλ᾽ εἰ κοῦφος οὕτως ἦν, ὥστε βαδίσαι  
> (But if I were so light as to walk)
>
> 83 „ Ἐν ἑκατὸν ἔτεσι δάκτυλον ἕνα,  
> (One finger-breadth in a hundred years,)

- *Ἀλλ’ εἰ κοῦφος οὕτως ἦν* adds an adversative and uses another unreal protasis: *ἦν* is here 1st sg. imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*, with *κοῦφος* predicate nominative. *Οὕτως* ‘so’ picks up *di tanto* and prepares *ὥστε*; the qualifications *pur* and *ancor* are not separately represented.
- *Ὥστε βαδίσαι* ‘so as to walk’ substitutes a result infinitive for *ch’ io potessi … andare*. *Βαδίσαι* is the aorist active infinitive of *βαδίζω*, with the same understood subject as *ἦν*; capacity is implied by the degree-and-result construction, not expressed by a verb meaning ‘can’.
- *Ἐν ἑκατὸν ἔτεσι* is *ἐν* with the dat. pl. of neuter *ἔτος*, qualified by the indeclinable numeral. *Δάκτυλον ἕνα* ‘one fingerbreadth’ is an accusative of distance traversed; the masculine accusative *ἕνα* agrees with the Greek unit substituted for *un’ oncia*.

> 84 io sarei messo già per lo sentiero,  
> 85 cercando lui tra questa gente sconcia,  

I had already put myself upon the road, to seek him among this disfigured people,

> 84 „ Ἐπεχείρουν ἂν ὁδεῦσαι, καὐτὸν πέριξ  
> (I would set out on the road, and round about)
>
> 85 „ Ἐν οἰκτρᾷ κοιλάδι τῇδ᾽ ἀναζητῆσαι,  
> (In this piteous valley search for him,)

- *Ἐπεχείρουν ἂν ὁδεῦσαι* ‘I would undertake to travel’ supplies the unreal apodosis: *ἐπεχείρουν* is 1st sg. imperfect active indicative of *ἐπιχειρέω*, augmented after the preverb and contracted in the ending. With *ἄν* it answers *εἰ … ἦν*. The Greek expresses an undertaking, not the already accomplished setting-out of *sarei messo già*; it omits *già* and replaces *per lo sentiero* with the verb *ὁδεῦσαι* (aorist active infinitive of *ὁδεύω*).
- *Καὐτὸν πέριξ … ἀναζητῆσαι* coordinates a second aorist active infinitive (*ἀναζητῆσαι*, from *ἀναζητέω*) under *ἐπεχείρουν*, rather than retaining the dependent gerund *cercando*. *Καὐτόν* is crasis of *καὶ αὐτόν*; *αὐτόν* is the object of the search and *πέριξ* adverbial, ‘round about’.
- *Ἐν οἰκτρᾷ κοιλάδι τῇδ’* ‘in this piteous valley’ turns *tra questa gente sconcia* from a location among persons into one within a place. *Κοιλάδι* is dat. sg. of *κοιλάς*; adjective and demonstrative agree in the feminine dative, *τῇδ’* being elided *τῇδε*. This substitution also supplies a grammatical antecedent for the description of dimensions.

> 86 con tutto ch' ella volge undici miglia,  
> 87 e men d' un mezzo di traverso non ci ha.  

though it winds round eleven miles, and is not less than half a mile across.

> 86 „ Καίπερ στάδια τὸ μὲν μῆκος ἐχούσῃ  
> (Although it has in length stadia)
>
> 87 „ Ἑκατόν, τὸ δὲ πλάτος στάδια πέντε.  
> (A hundred, and in breadth five stadia.)

- *Καίπερ … ἐχούσῃ* turns *con tutto ch’* with finite verb into a concessive participial construction. *Ἐχούσῃ* is dat. fem. sg. present active participle of *ἔχω*, agreeing across the line break with *κοιλάδι*, not a participle in a genitive absolute. The verb is ‘having’, not *volge* ‘winds round’.
- *Στάδια τὸ μὲν μῆκος … ἑκατόν, τὸ δὲ πλάτος στάδια πέντε* balances the dimensions by *μέν … δέ*, ‘in length … in breadth’. *Τὸ μῆκος* and *τὸ πλάτος* are accusatives of respect; the quantities had are expressed by *στάδια* (acc. neut. pl. of *στάδιον*) with indeclinable numerals. The first numeral, *ἑκατόν*, is delayed until after the participle; *ἐχούσῃ* is understood in the second member.
- The Greek substitutes a hundred and five stadia respectively for the two measurements in miles. In the second it gives a definite breadth rather than a lower bound: neither the negative comparison *men … non* nor the locative *ci* is reproduced.

## Lines 88–120 — Master Adam and Sinon Trade Blows and Accusations

> 88 Io son per lor tra sì fatta famiglia:  
> 89 ei m’ indussero a battere i fiorini,  
> 90 che avean ben tre carati di mondiglia.”  

Through them am I in such a crew: they induced me to stamp the florins that had three carats of alloy.”

> 88 „ Ἕνεκ᾽ αὐτῶν ἐν τοιῷδ᾽ εἰμὶ νῦν τόπῳ,  
> (On their account I am now in such a place,)
>
> 89 „ Παρακόψαι με πεισάντων τοὺς στατῆρας  
> (Who persuaded me to counterfeit the staters)
>
> 90 „ Σύγκραμ᾽ ἔχοντας ἐκ τριῶν κερατίων.”—  
> (Having an alloy of three carats."—)

- *Ἕνεκ᾽ αὐτῶν* ‘on their account’ keeps *per lor* emphatic at the front, but uses *ἕνεκα* with the genitive, eliding its final vowel before *αὐτῶν*. *ἐν τοιῷδ᾽ … τόπῳ* ‘in such a place’ substitutes the place for the company, *tra sì fatta famiglia*. The demonstrative *τοιῷδε* (dat. sg. masc. of *τοιόσδε*) agrees across *εἰμὶ νῦν* with *τόπῳ*; *νῦν* ‘now’ is supplied, while *Io* is expressed only by the first-person verb.
- *παρακόψαι με πεισάντων* ‘who persuaded me to counterfeit’ makes *ei m’indussero* a participial explanation of *αὐτῶν*, not a second independent assertion. *πεισάντων* is gen. pl. masc., aor. act. participle of *πείθω*, agreeing with that pronoun; *με* is its object and the understood subject of the fronted aor. act. infinitive *παρακόψαι* (from *παρακόπτω*). The verb already implies striking spurious money, where *battere* by itself means ‘strike’.
- *τοὺς στατῆρας* (acc. pl. of *στατήρ*) substitutes a Greek coin name for *i fiorini*. The relative clause *che avean* becomes *ἔχοντας* (pres. act. participle, acc. pl. masc., of *ἔχω*), agreeing with the coins. Its object *σύγκραμ᾽* is elided *σύγκραμα*, ‘mixture’; *ἐκ τριῶν κερατίων* specifies its composition. The Greek thus speaks of a mixture of three carats rather than ‘three carats of impurity’; *ben* has no separate equivalent.

> 91 Ed io a lui: “Chi son li due tapini,  
> 92 che fuman come man bagnate il verno,  
> 93 giacendo stretti a’ tuoi destri confini?”  

And I to him: “Who are the abject two, lying close to thy right confines, and smoking like a hand bathed in winter-time?”

> 91 Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν· “Τίνες εἰσὶν οἱ δύο,  
> (And I to him: "Who are the two,)
>
> 92 „ Οἱ καπνιῶντες ὡς χεὶρ βρεκτὴ χειμῶνος,  
> (Who are smoking like a wet hand in winter,)
>
> 93 „ Κείμενοι δὲ σύσφιγκτοι πρὸς δεξιάν σου; ”  
> (And lying pressed close together on your right?")

- *Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν* ‘and I to him’ keeps the Italian ellipsis of the verb of speaking. *κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*; *πρός* takes the accusative of the person addressed. *Τίνες εἰσὶν οἱ δύο* ‘who are the two?’ has interrogative *τίνες* as predicate. The article, pronoun and verb are plural, not dual; *tapini* is dropped.
- *οἱ καπνιῶντες* ‘the ones giving off smoke’ resumes *οἱ δύο* with article and pres. act. participle (nom. pl. masc. of *καπνιάω*), in place of *che fuman*. In *ὡς χεὶρ βρεκτὴ χειμῶνος*, *χείρ* is singular where *man bagnate* is plural; *βρεκτή* is an agreeing verbal adjective, ‘wet’, and the genitive of time *χειμῶνος* means ‘in winter’.
- *κείμενοι δὲ σύσφιγκτοι* ‘and lying pressed together’ coordinates a second description by postpositive *δέ*. *κείμενοι* (pres. mid. participle of *κεῖμαι*, nom. pl. masc.) renders *giacendo*; *σύσφιγκτοι* is a predicate adjective agreeing with the same two persons. *πρὸς δεξιάν σου* ‘on your right’ leaves ‘side’ to be understood, rather than reproducing the plural *confini*.

> 94 “Qui li trovai, e poi volta non dierno,”  
> 95 rispose, “quand’ io piovvi in questo greppo,  
> 96 e non credo che dieno in sempiterno.  

“Here I found them, when I rained into this pinfold,” he answered; “and since then they have not given a turn, and may not give, I think, to all eternity.

> 94 —“ Αὐτοὺς ὧδ᾽ εὗρον· ἀλλ᾽ ἀκίνητοι κεῖνται,”  
> (—"I found them thus; and they lie motionless,")
>
> 95 Εἶπεν, “ ἐξότ᾽ εἰς βυθὸν ἐῤῥίφθην τόνδε.  
> (He said, "since I was cast into this pit.)
>
> 96 „ Νομίζω δ᾽ οὕτω μενοῦσι δι᾽ αἰῶνος.  
> (And I think they will remain so forever.)

- *Αὐτοὺς ὧδ᾽ εὗρον* fronts ‘them’ rather than *Qui*. Elided *ὧδε* can indicate place, ‘here’, or manner, ‘thus’; *εὗρον* is the first-person singular second aorist active indicative of *εὑρίσκω*. *ἀλλ᾽ ἀκίνητοι κεῖνται* ‘but they lie motionless’ replaces *e poi volta non dierno*: a present state, with predicate adjective and third-person plural *κεῖνται*, replaces the negated past action.
- *Εἶπεν* (3rd sg., second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*) gives ‘he said’ for *rispose*. *ἐξότ᾽* is elided *ἐξότε*, ‘ever since’, making *quand’ io piovvi* the starting point of their continued immobility. *εἰς βυθὸν … τόνδε* ‘into this depth’ replaces *in questo greppo*; *ἐῤῥίφθην* (1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ῥίπτω*) makes the intransitive *piovvi* passive, ‘I was thrown’. The spelling shows the doubled rho after the augment.
- *Νομίζω δ᾽ οὕτω μενοῦσι* ‘and I think they will remain so’ turns *non credo che dieno* into a positive prediction. *μενοῦσι* is here the contracted future active indicative, 3rd pl. of *μένω*, not an Italian-style subordinate subjunctive; the finite prediction follows *νομίζω* without an expressed *ὅτι* ‘that’. *οὕτω* refers back to *ἀκίνητοι*; *δι᾽ αἰῶνος* uses elided *διά* with the genitive of duration for *in sempiterno*.

> 97 L’ una è la falsa che accusò Joseppo,  
> 98 l’ altro è il falso Sinon greco da Troia;  
> 99 per febbre acuta gittan tanto leppo.”  

One is the false *wife* who accused Joseph; the other is false Sinon, the Greek from Troy; burning fever makes them reek so strongly.”

> 97 „ Ψεύστειρ᾽ Ἰωσὴφ κατήγορός ἐσθ᾽ ἥδε·  
> (This is the false accuser of Joseph;)
>
> 98 „ Ὁ δ᾽ ἄλλος ψεύστης Σίνων, Τρωϊκὸς Ἕλλην·  
> (And the other is the liar Sinon, the Trojan Greek;)
>
> 99 „ Ἐκ θέρμης πολλῆς πέμπουσ᾽ ἀτμὸν δυσώδη.”—  
> (From intense heat they send forth a foul-smelling steam."—)

- *Ψεύστειρ᾽ Ἰωσὴφ κατήγορός ἐσθ᾽ ἥδε* puts the identifying predicates first and the pointing subject *ἥδε* ‘this woman’ last, in place of *L’una*. *ψεύστειρα* is a feminine agent noun, ‘liar’, elided before the indeclinable *Ἰωσήφ*. *κατήγορος* ‘accuser’, here feminine, replaces the finite relative *che accusò*; *Ἰωσήφ* functions as its objective genitive. *ἐσθ᾽* is *ἐστί* elided before the rough breathing of *ἥδε*, with aspiration of the final consonant. No word for “wife” is expressed.
- *ὁ δ᾽ ἄλλος ψεύστης Σίνων* balances the feminine identification with *ὁ … ἄλλος* ‘the other’, the copula now understood. Again *falso* is represented by a noun, *ψεύστης* ‘liar’. *Τρωϊκὸς Ἕλλην* renders *greco da Troia* by adjective and noun, literally ‘Trojan Greek’, not by a preposition of origin.
- *ἐκ θέρμης πολλῆς* ‘from great heat’ gives the cause corresponding to *per febbre acuta*, but makes intensity rather than acuteness explicit. *πέμπουσ᾽* is elided *πέμπουσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *πέμπω*); its object *ἀτμὸν δυσώδη* ‘ill-smelling vapour’ unpacks *leppo*. *δυσώδη* is the contracted acc. sg. masc. of *δυσώδης*. The emphasis of *tanto* falls on the heat in Greek, not on the quantity emitted.

> 100 E l’ un di lor, che si recò a noia  
> 101 forse d’ esser nomato sì oscuro,  
> 102 col pugno gli percosse l’ epa croia;  

And one of them, who took offence perhaps at being named thus darkly, smote the rigid belly of him with his fist;

> 100 Εἷς δ᾽ αὐτῶν ἐρεθισθείς, ἀκούσας ἴσως  
> (And one of them provoked, having heard perhaps)
>
> 101 Ἑαυτὸν καλούμενον λοιδόρως οὕτω,  
> (Himself named so reproachfully,)
>
> 102 Πὺξ ἔτυψ᾽ αὐτοῦ τὴν φυσητὴν γαστέρα·  
> (Struck with his fist his swollen belly;)

- *Εἷς δ᾽ αὐτῶν* closely follows *E l’un di lor*, with a partitive genitive after the numeral. For *che si recò a noia … d’esser nomato*, Musurus subordinates two participles to the eventual blow: *ἐρεθισθείς* ‘provoked’ (aor. pass. of *ἐρεθίζω*) and *ἀκούσας* ‘having heard’ (aor. act. of *ἀκούω*), both nom. sg. masc. The hearing is supplied; *ἴσως* ‘perhaps’ follows it rather than preceding the account of the naming.
- *ἑαυτὸν καλούμενον* ‘himself being called’ is an accusative and agreeing supplementary participle after the verb of perception. The reflexive refers back to *εἷς*; *καλούμενον* is pres. pass. participle of *καλέω*, presenting the naming as what he heard in progress. *λοιδόρως οὕτω* interprets *sì oscuro* as ‘so insultingly’, not literally ‘obscurely’.
- *Πὺξ ἔτυψ᾽* ‘he struck with his fist’ replaces *col pugno* by the adverb *πύξ*. *ἔτυψ᾽* is elided *ἔτυψε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *τύπτω*). Italian *gli … l’epa* has the affected person in the dative; Greek makes him the possessor, *αὐτοῦ τὴν … γαστέρα*. *φυσητήν* ‘inflated’ interprets the condition of the belly rather than translating the hardness of *croia*.

> 103 quella sonò, come fosse un tamburo;  

it sounded like a drum;

> 103 Ἡ δ᾽ ἤχησεν οἷον δὴ τύμπανον· τότε  
> (And it resounded just like a drum; then)

- *Ἡ δ᾽ ἤχησεν* ‘and it resounded’ uses the feminine article pronominally to resume *γαστέρα*, as *quella* resumes *epa*. *ἤχησεν* is the augmented 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἠχέω*. *οἷον δὴ τύμπανον* ‘just like a drum’ compresses *come fosse un tamburo* into a comparison without a verb: *οἷον* introduces the likeness and *δή* stresses it. There is no Greek optative corresponding to *fosse*. Line-final *τότε* ‘then’ already introduces the answering blow.

> 104 e mastro Adamo gli percosse il volto  
> 105 col braccio suo che non parve men duro,  

and Master Adam smote him in the face with his arm, that did not seem less hard,

> 104 Μήστωρ Ἀδάμος ἔπαισ᾽ αὐτοῦ τὴν ὄψιν  
> (Master Adam smote his face)
>
> 105 Τῷ βραχίονι μετ᾽ οὐχ ἧττονος βίας,  
> (With his arm with no less violence,)

- *Μήστωρ Ἀδάμος* recasts *mastro Adamo* with the poetic noun *μήστωρ* ‘counsellor, deviser’ in the position of a title, and a Greek nominative ending for the name. *ἔπαισ᾽* is elided *ἔπαισε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *παίω*). As in line 102, *αὐτοῦ τὴν ὄψιν* ‘his face’ replaces Italian *gli … il volto* with a possessive genitive and an accusative object.
- *τῷ βραχίονι* (dat. sg. of *βραχίων*) is an instrumental dative for *col braccio suo*; the article suffices without an expressed possessive. *μετ᾽ οὐχ ἧττονος βίας* ‘with no less force’ replaces the relative *che non parve men duro*: there is no verb of seeming, and the comparison concerns the force of the stroke rather than the hardness of the arm. Elided *μετά* governs the genitive; *ἧττονος* agrees with *βίας*, and *οὐχ* is the form of the negative before a rough breathing.

> 106 dicendo a lui: “Ancor che mi sia tolto  
> 107 lo mover, per le membra che son gravi,  
> 108 ho io il braccio a tal mestiere sciolto.”  

saying to him: “Though I am kept from moving by my weighty limbs, I have an arm free for such necessity.”

> 106 Λέγων πρὸς αὐτόν· “ Καίτοι στερηθεὶς πάμπαν  
> (Saying to him: "Although wholly deprived)
>
> 107 „ Τῆς κινήσεως τῶν βαρέων μελῶν μου,  
> (Of the movement of my heavy limbs,)
>
> 108 „ Πλὴν ἔχω πρὸς τοῦτ᾽ ἐλευθέραν τὴν χεῖρα.”  
> (Yet I have my hand free for this.")

- *Λέγων πρὸς αὐτόν* reproduces *dicendo a lui* with the pres. act. participle of *λέγω*, nom. sg. masc., and *πρός* with the accusative. *καίτοι στερηθεὶς πάμπαν* ‘although wholly deprived’ makes *Ancor che mi sia tolto* a concessive participial phrase. *στερηθείς* (aor. pass. of *στερέω*) agrees with the speaker as subject; poetic *πάμπαν* ‘altogether’ is supplied.
- *τῆς κινήσεως* ‘of movement’ is the genitive of deprivation governed by *στερηθείς*, replacing the Italian substantivized infinitive *lo mover*. *τῶν βαρέων μελῶν μου* depends in turn on *κινήσεως*: ‘the movement of my heavy limbs’. Thus *per le membra che son gravi* ceases to be a separate expression of cause. *βαρέων* (gen. pl. neut. of *βαρύς*) is attributive to *μελῶν* (from *μέλος*); *μου* supplies the possessor.
- *πλὴν ἔχω* ‘yet I have’ explicitly resumes the main assertion after the concession. *ἐλευθέραν τὴν χεῖρα* places the adjective in predicate position, ‘I have my hand free’, for *ho io il braccio … sciolto*. *χείρ* can include the arm, but differs from *βραχίων* above. *πρὸς τοῦτ᾽* (elided *τοῦτο*, acc. neut. sg.) ‘for this’ leaves *a tal mestiere* to be inferred from the demonstrative.

> 109 Ond’ ei rispose: “Quando tu andavi  
> 110 al foco non l’ avei tu così presto;  
> 111 ma sì e più l’ avei quando coniavi.”  

Thereat he answered: “When thou wast going to the fire, thou hadst it not so ready; but as ready, and more, when thou wast coining.”

> 109 Ὁ δ᾽ ὑπέλαβεν· “ Ἀλλ᾽ ὁπότ᾽ ἐπορεύου  
> (And he retorted: "Yet when you were going)
>
> 110 „ Εἰς τὴν πυράν, οὐχ οὕτως ἑτοίμην εἶχες,  
> (To the pyre, you did not have it so ready,)
>
> 111 „ Ὡς πρὸ πάντων ὅτ᾽ ἐνομισματοκόπεις.”  
> (As above all when you were coining money.")

- *Ὁ δ᾽ ὑπέλαβεν* ‘but he retorted’ uses the article pronominally to mark the change of speaker; *ὑπέλαβεν* is 3rd sg., second aor. act. ind. of *ὑπολαμβάνω*, with the augment internal to the compound. *ἀλλ᾽ ὁπότ᾽ ἐπορεύου* ‘but when you were going’ adds an adversative at the opening of the speech. Both *ἀλλά* and *ὁπότε* are elided; *ἐπορεύου* is 2nd sg. imperf. mid. ind. of deponent *πορεύομαι*, matching the ongoing *andavi*.
- *εἰς τὴν πυράν* ‘to the pyre’ specifies *al foco* as a destination. *οὐχ οὕτως ἑτοίμην εἶχες* ‘you did not have it so ready’ keeps the construction of *non l’avei tu così presto*, but omits the object pronoun. Predicate *ἑτοίμην* is feminine accusative because the understood object is now *χεῖρα*; *εἶχες* is the 2nd sg. imperfect of *ἔχω*.
- *ὡς … ὅτ᾽ ἐνομισματοκόπεις* completes *οὐχ οὕτως* with a comparison, ‘not so … as when you were minting money’. For the renewed assertion *ma sì e più l’avei*, Greek neither repeats ‘had’ nor says ‘more ready’; *πρὸ πάντων* ‘above all’ singles out the occasion. *ὅτ᾽* is elided *ὅτε*; *ἐνομισματοκόπεις* is 2nd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *νομισματοκοπέω*, incorporating ‘coin’ into the verb.

> 112 E l’ idropico: “Tu di’ ver di questo;  
> 113 ma tu non fosti sì ver testimonio,  
> 114 là ’ve del ver a Troia fosti richiesto.”  

And he of the dropsy: “In this thou sayest true; but thou wast not so true a witness there, when questioned of the truth at Troy.”

> 112 Ὁ δ᾽ ὑδρωπικός· “Ἀληθῶς εἶπας ταῦτα·  
> (And the dropsical one: "Truly you have spoken this;)
>
> 113 „ Ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἀληθὴς ἦσθα μάρτυς ἐν Τροίᾳ,  
> (But you were not a true witness in Troy,)
>
> 114 „ Ὅτε τὴν ἀλήθειαν εἰπεῖν ἐκλήθης.”—  
> (When you were called upon to speak the truth."—)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ ὑδρωπικός* ‘and the dropsical one’ reproduces *E l’idropico* with a substantivized adjective and no verb of saying. *Ἀληθῶς εἶπας ταῦτα* ‘you said these things truly’ turns *ver* into an adverb and *di questo* into the neut. pl. direct object *ταῦτα*. *εἶπας* is the 2nd sg. aor. act. ind. with the *-ας* ending beside Attic *εἶπες*: the preceding speech is treated as complete, unlike the present *di’*.
- *ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἀληθὴς ἦσθα μάρτυς ἐν Τροίᾳ* ‘but you were not a truthful witness in Troy’ preserves the predicate noun and the play on ‘true’, but drops the degree word *sì*. *ἦσθα* is the Attic 2nd sg. imperfect of *εἰμί*; *ἀληθής* agrees with nominative *μάρτυς*. *ἐν Τροίᾳ* is brought forward from the next line; there is no separate *là*.
- *ὅτε τὴν ἀλήθειαν εἰπεῖν ἐκλήθης* ‘when you were called upon to tell the truth’ substitutes temporal *ὅτε* for the Italian local relative *’ve*. *ἐκλήθης* (2nd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *καλέω*) takes the second aor. act. infinitive *εἰπεῖν*, whose understood subject is ‘you’; *τὴν ἀλήθειαν* is its object. The Italian *del ver … fosti richiesto* does not expressly say ‘to speak’. The succession *ἀληθῶς*, *ἀληθής*, *ἀλήθειαν* varies the same root across adverb, adjective and noun.

> 115 “S’ io dissi ’l falso, tu falsasti il conio,”  
> 116 disse Sinone, “e son qui per un fallo,  
> 117 e tu per più che alcun altro demonio.”  

“If I spoke false, thou too didst falsify the coin,” said Sinon; “and I am here for one crime, and thou for more than any other Demon.”

> 115 “ Εἰ ψεῦδος εἶπον, καὶ σὺ νόμισμ᾽ ἐψεύσω,”  
> ("If I spoke falsehood, you too falsified coin,")
>
> 116 Εἶπε Σίνων· “πλὴν δι᾽ ἓν εἰμ᾽ ὧδε σφάλμα·  
> (Said Sinon; "yet I am here for one offence,)
>
> 117 „ Σὺ δὲ διὰ πλείον᾽ ἢ πᾶς ἄλλος δαίμων.”—  
> (While you for more than any other demon."—)

- *Εἰ ψεῦδος εἶπον* ‘if I spoke a falsehood’ uses *εἰ* with the 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. *εἶπον*, an open condition about the past, not a counterfactual. *ψεῦδος* is a neuter accusative object for *’l falso*. *καὶ σύ* ‘you too’ makes the answering subject emphatic; *νόμισμ᾽* is elided *νόμισμα* ‘coin’, the object of *ἐψεύσω* (2nd sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ψεύδω*). The middle more ordinarily means ‘tell a lie’; taking ‘coin’ as what one falsified stretches the construction towards *falsasti il conio* and retains the Italian echo of *falso* in the verb.
- *Εἶπε Σίνων* keeps the intervening speech tag. *πλὴν δι᾽ ἓν εἰμ᾽ ὧδε σφάλμα* ‘yet for one offence I am here’ gives adversative *πλήν* for connective *e*. *διά* with the accusative expresses cause; *ἕν* (neut. acc. sg. of *εἷς*) is separated from its noun *σφάλμα* ‘error, offence’ by elided *εἰμί* and locative *ὧδε*.
- *σὺ δὲ διὰ πλείον᾽* ‘but you for more’ leaves the verb and ‘here’ understood. Elided *πλείονα* is neut. acc. pl., with ‘offences’ supplied from singular *σφάλμα*. *ἢ πᾶς ἄλλος δαίμων* expands *che alcun altro demonio* as ‘than every other demon’: the nominative is the subject of an understood parallel clause, not a second accusative governed by *διά*.

> 118 “Ricorditi, spergiuro, del cavallo,”  
> 119 rispose quel ch’ avea enfiata l’ epa;  
> 120 “e siati reo che tutto il mondo sallo.”  

“Bethink thee, perjurer, of the horse,” answered he who had the inflated paunch; “and be it a torture to thee that all the world knows thereof.”

> 118 —“Σὺ δ᾽, ἐπίορκε, μέμνησο τὸν σὸν ἵππον,”  
> (—"And you, perjurer, remember your horse,")
>
> 119 Ὑπέλαβ᾽ ὁ γαστέρα φυσητὴν ἔχων·  
> (Retorted he who had the swollen belly;)
>
> 120 “ Λάβε δὴ ποινὴν γνωστὴν παντὶ τῷ κόσμῳ.”—  
> ("Receive then a punishment known to all the world."—)

- *Σὺ δ᾽, ἐπίορκε* ‘but you, perjurer’ adds an emphatic subject and answering *δέ* before the vocative. *μέμνησο* is the 2nd sg. perfect mid. imperative of *μιμνῄσκω*, with present force, ‘keep in mind’. Where *Ricorditi … del cavallo* uses a reflexive and *di*, Greek gives the thing remembered in the accusative, *τὸν σὸν ἵππον*, and adds the possessive ‘your’.
- *Ὑπέλαβ᾽* is elided *ὑπέλαβε*, the same verb of reply as in line 109. *ὁ γαστέρα φυσητὴν ἔχων* ‘the one having an inflated belly’ replaces *quel ch’ avea* with article and pres. act. participle (nom. sg. masc. of *ἔχω*). Its present is relative to the time of the reply. *γαστέρα φυσητήν*, its accusative object and agreeing adjective, repeats the description of line 102.
- *Λάβε δὴ ποινήν* ‘receive, then, a punishment’ turns the wish *siati reo* into a direct command: *λάβε* is the 2nd sg. second aor. act. imperative of *λαμβάνω*, and *δή* presses the injunction. The final change is syntactic as well as verbal. In *che tutto il mondo sallo*, the world's knowing ‘it’ is itself to cause pain; in *ποινὴν γνωστὴν παντὶ τῷ κόσμῳ*, *γνωστήν* agrees with *ποινήν*, and *παντὶ τῷ κόσμῳ* is dative with ‘known’. What the Greek calls ‘known to the whole world’ is thus the punishment.

## Lines 121–148 — The Quarrel and the Rebuke

> 121 “A te sia rea la sete onde ti crepa,”  
> 122 disse il Greco, “la lingua, e l’ acqua marcia  
> 123 che il ventre innanzi gli occhi sì t’ assiepa.”  

“To thee be torture the thirst that cracks thy tongue,” replied the Greek, “and the foul water which makes that belly such a hedge before thy eyes.”

> 121 —“Ἡ δίψ᾽ ἔστω σοι ποινή· διαῤῥαγείη,”  
> (—"May thirst be your punishment; may it burst,")
>
> 122 Εἶπεν Ἕλλην, “ σὴ γλῶσσα, τό τε σηπῶδες  
> (Said the Greek, "your tongue, and the putrid)
>
> 123 „ Ὕδωρ, ὃ σὺν τῇ γαστρὶ φράττει σὴν ὄψιν.”—  
> (Water, which with your belly hedges up your face."—)

- *Ἡ δίψ᾽ ἔστω σοι ποινή* ‘let thirst be a punishment to you’ keeps the jussive force of *sia* with *ἔστω* (3rd sg. pres. imperative of *εἰμί*). The article marks *δίψ᾽* (elided *δίψα*, nom. sg.) as subject; the anarthrous predicate noun *ποινή* replaces the adjective *rea*. Enclitic *σοι*, dative of the person affected, lacks the emphasis of initial *A te*.
- Musurus detaches *onde ti crepa … la lingua* from *la sete* and makes it a second wish: *διαῤῥαγείη … σὴ γλῶσσα* ‘may your tongue burst’. The unaugmented *διαῤῥαγείη* is the 3rd sg. second aor. pass. optative of *διαρρήγνυμι*, intransitive in sense; the optative without *ἄν* expresses a wish. The spelling *ῤῥ* marks doubled rho. As in Italian, the subject is delayed until after the speech tag *Εἶπεν Ἕλλην* ‘said the Greek’ (*εἶπεν*, 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*); possessive *σή* replaces the personal dative *ti*.
- *τό τε σηπῶδες ὕδωρ* ‘and the putrid water’ resumes the construction of *ἔστω … ποινή* across the intervening wish. Postpositive *τε* supplies *e*; the adjective *σηπῶδες* (nom. neut. sg. of *σηπώδης*) precedes its noun. The relative *ὃ* is nom. neut. sg., subject of *φράττει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *φράττω* ‘block’). Dante's *il ventre* becomes an accompaniment, *σὺν τῇ γαστρί* ‘together with the belly’; *innanzi gli occhi sì t’ assiepa* becomes the direct-object construction *φράττει σὴν ὄψιν* ‘blocks your sight’, without a separate equivalent for *sì*.

> 124 Allora il monetier: “Così si squarcia  
> 125 la bocca tua per mal dir come suole:  

Then the coiner : “Thus thy jaw gapes wide, as usual, to speak ill

> 124 Ὁ δὲ παραχαράκτης· “ Οὕτως ἂν χάσκοι  
> (And the counterfeiter: "So would it gape,)
>
> 125 „ Εὐρὺ στόμα σὸν κακολογοῦν, ὡς ἔθος·  
> (Your wide mouth speaking evil, as is your wont;)

- *Ὁ δὲ παραχαράκτης* ‘and the counterfeiter’ leaves the verb of speaking unexpressed, like *Allora il monetier*. Postpositive *δέ* marks the change of speaker rather than translating *allora* temporally; *παραχαράκτης* specifies falsification, not merely coining.
- *Οὕτως ἂν χάσκοι* ‘so might it gape’ retains initial *Così* but gives *si squarcia* a potential construction: *χάσκοι*, 3rd sg. pres. act. optative of the intransitive *χάσκω*, with *ἄν*. *Εὐρὺ στόμα σόν* ‘your wide mouth’ is the delayed neut. sg. subject; *εὐρύ* makes the width explicit. *κακολογοῦν* (pres. act. participle of *κακολογέω*, nom. neut. sg., contracted from *κακολογέον*) agrees with *στόμα*: ‘speaking ill’, concurrent action rather than *per* with an infinitive. *ὡς ἔθος* ‘as is customary’ substitutes a noun with an understood copula for personal *come suole*.

> 126 chè s’ i’ ho sete ed umor mi rinfarcia,  
> 127 tu hai l’ arsura e il capo che ti duole,  

: for if I have thirst, and moisture stuffs me, thou hast the burning, and the head that pains thee;

> 126 „ Εἰ δ᾽ ἐγὼ διψῶ καὶ χυμός μ᾽ ἀνοιδαίνει,  
> (For if I thirst and humour swells me up,)
>
> 127 „ Σύ γε φλεγμονεῖς καὶ κεφαλαλγεῖς σφόδρα,  
> (You indeed burn with fever and have a fierce headache,)

- *Εἰ δ᾽ ἐγὼ διψῶ* ‘but if I thirst’ uses connective *δέ* (elided before *ἐγώ*) rather than a causal equivalent of *chè*. *διψῶ* (1st sg. pres. act. ind. of *διψάω*) turns *ho sete* into a verb. In the second limb of the condition, *χυμός* ‘fluid’ retains *umor* as subject, and *μ᾽ ἀνοιδαίνει* ‘swells me up’ retains its transitive syntax (*μ᾽* = *με*; *ἀνοιδαίνει*, 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἀνοιδαίνω*).
- *Σύ γε* ‘you, at any rate’ answers *ἐγώ*, with restrictive or emphatic *γε* sharpening the contrast. The apodosis has two 2nd sg. present indicatives, *φλεγμονεῖς* (from *φλεγμονέω* ‘be fevered’) for *hai l’ arsura*, and *κεφαλαλγεῖς* (from *κεφαλαλγέω* ‘have a headache’) for *il capo che ti duole*. The compound absorbs both the noun and the relative clause; *σφόδρα* ‘severely’ adds degree.

> 128 e per leccar lo specchio di Narcisso,  
> 129 non vorresti a invitar molte parole.”  

and to make thee lap the mirror of Narcissus thou wouldst not require many words of invitation.”

> 128 „ Ὥσθ᾽, ἵνα τὸ κάτοπτρον λείξῃς Ναρκίσσου,  
> (So that, to lick the mirror of Narcissus,)
>
> 129 „ Οὐχὶ προτροπῶν πολλῶν ἔσται σοι χρεία.”—  
> (You will have no need of many urgings."—)

- *Ὥσθ᾽* (elided *ὥστε*, with aspiration of the final stop before *ἵνα*) makes Italian connective *e* explicitly consecutive: ‘so that … you will have no need’. Inside this result clause, *ἵνα … λείξῃς* replaces *per leccar* with a finite purpose clause; *λείξῃς* is the 2nd sg. aor. act. subjunctive of *λείχω*. *τὸ κάτοπτρον* is its accusative object; *Ναρκίσσου*, the dependent possessive genitive, is separated from it by the verb.
- *Οὐχὶ … ἔσται σοι χρεία* ‘there will be no need for you’ replaces the personal conditional *non vorresti* with a future assertion (*ἔσται*, 3rd sg. fut. ind. of *εἰμί*) and a dative of the person in need. Emphatic *οὐχί* negates the assertion. *προτροπῶν πολλῶν* ‘of many urgings’ is a gen. pl. complement of *χρεία*: *a invitar* supplies the sense of the noun, while *parole* has no separate equivalent.

> 130 Ad ascoltarli er’ io del tutto fisso,  
> 131 quando il maestro mi disse: “Or pur mira!  
> 132 che per poco è che teco non mi risso.”  

I was standing all intent to hear them, when the Master said to me : “Now keep looking, a little longer and I quarrel with thee!”

> 130 Ὅλως ἀτενὴς ἤμην αὐτοὺς ἀκούων,  
> (I was wholly intent listening to them,)
>
> 131 Ὅτ᾽ εἶπέ μοι Προστάτης· “ Σαυτῷ νῦν πρόσχες·  
> (When my Master said to me: "Now look to yourself;)
>
> 132 „ Μικροῦ δεῖν πρὸς σὲ χαλεπαίνων ἂν εἴην.”  
> (A little more and I should be angry with you.")

- *Ὅλως ἀτενὴς ἤμην* ‘wholly intent I was’ corresponds closely to *er’ io del tutto fisso*, with the degree adverb and predicate adjective brought forward. *ἤμην* is the later Greek 1st sg. imperfect of *εἰμί* (Attic *ἦ*). *αὐτοὺς ἀκούων* ‘listening to them’ replaces initial *Ad ascoltarli* with a final circumstantial participle (*ἀκούων*, pres. act., nom. masc. sg.). The accusative of the persons echoes *-li*; for persons listened to, the genitive is usual in Attic.
- *Ὅτ᾽ εἶπέ μοι Προστάτης* ‘when the guide said to me’ has elided *ὅτε*, an aorist interrupting the preceding imperfect, and enclitic dative *μοι*, which accounts for the additional accent on *εἶπέ*. *Προστάτης* ‘one who stands before, protector’ substitutes a title of guidance for *il maestro*. *Σαυτῷ νῦν πρόσχες* ‘now attend to yourself’ interprets *Or pur mira!* as a direct admonition rather than the ironic invitation “Now keep looking”. *πρόσχες* is the 2nd sg. second aor. act. imperative of *προσέχω* ‘give heed’, governing *σαυτῷ*, the contracted reflexive dative (*σεαυτῷ*).
- *Μικροῦ δεῖν* ‘almost’, literally ‘to lack little’, is an idiomatic absolute infinitive (*δεῖν*, pres. act. of *δέω*, with gen. *μικροῦ*). It renders *per poco è che … non* without copying the negative. *πρὸς σὲ χαλεπαίνων ἂν εἴην* ‘I might be angry with you’ is periphrastic: *χαλεπαίνων* (pres. act. participle, nom. masc. sg.) with *εἴην* (1st sg. pres. optative of *εἰμί*) and potential *ἄν*. Anger directed at someone (*πρός* + acc.) replaces the reciprocal implication of *teco … mi risso*.

> 133 Quand’ io ‘l senti’ a me parlar con ira,  
> 134 volsimi verso lui con tal vergogna,  
> 135 ch’ ancor per la memoria mi si gira.  

When I heard him speak to me in anger, I turned towards him with such shame, that it comes over me again as I but think of it.

> 133 Ἰδὼν δ’ ὅτι μοι προσεφθέγγετ’ ὀργίλως,  
> (And seeing that he addressed me angrily,)
>
> 134 Μετ’ ἐντροπῆς πρὸς αὐτὸν τοιᾶσδ’ ἐστράφην,  
> (With such shame toward him I turned,)
>
> 135 Ὥστ’ εἰσέτ’ ἐν τῇ μνήμῃ φυλάσσεταί μου.  
> (That it is still kept in my memory.)

- *Ἰδὼν δ’ ὅτι* ‘and seeing that’ replaces temporal *Quand’ io ‘l senti’* with a circumstantial second aor. act. participle of *ὁράω* (nom. masc. sg.) and a declarative clause: perception is construed as recognition of a fact. *μοι προσεφθέγγετ’ ὀργίλως* ‘he was addressing me angrily’ has a finite imperfect for *parlar*: *προσεφθέγγετ’* is elided *προσεφθέγγετο*, 3rd sg. imperf. mid. ind. of deponent *προσφθέγγομαι*, with the augment after the preverb. *μοι* is its dative complement; the adverb replaces *con ira*.
- *Μετ’ ἐντροπῆς … τοιᾶσδ’* ‘with such shame’ takes the genitive after elided *μετά*; *τοιᾶσδ’* (elided *τοιᾶσδε*, gen. fem. sg. of *τοιόσδε*) agrees across the intervening *πρὸς αὐτόν*. *ἐστράφην* (1st sg. second aor. pass. ind. of *στρέφω*) is intransitive, ‘I turned’, for reflexive *volsimi*.
- *Ὥστ’ εἰσέτ’ … φυλάσσεται* ‘so that it is still preserved’ answers *τοιᾶσδε* with *ὥστε* and an indicative of actual result. *εἰσέτ’* is elided *εἰσέτι* ‘still’; *φυλάσσεται* is 3rd sg. pres. pass. ind. of *φυλάσσω*. The shame is preserved *ἐν τῇ μνήμῃ … μου* ‘in my memory’, with possessive genitive *μου* delayed past the verb: a state of retention replaces the recurrent movement of *per la memoria mi si gira*.

> 136 E quale è quei che suo dannaggio sogna,  
> 137 che sognando desidera sognare,  
> 138 sì che quel ch’ è, come non fosse, agogna:  

And as one who dreams of something hurtful to him, and dreaming wishes it a dream, so that he longs for that which is, as if it were not

> 136 Ὡς δ’ ὃς δυστυχὲς ὄναρ ὁρᾷ καθ’ ὕπνους,  
> (And like him who sees an evil dream in his sleep,)
>
> 137 Ἐν ὀνείρῳ τ’ ὢν ὀνειρώττειν ἐλπίζει,  
> (And being in a dream hopes he is dreaming,)
>
> 138 Ποθῶν ὡς ἀνύπαρκτον ὅπερ δὴ βλέπει,  
> (Longing for what he sees as though it were not,)

- *Ὡς δ’ ὃς … ὁρᾷ* ‘and as one who sees’ uses *ὡς* to open the comparison and *ὅς* without an expressed antecedent for *quei che*. *δυστυχὲς ὄναρ* ‘an unhappy dream’ is the acc. neut. sg. object of *ὁρᾷ* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ὁράω*). Where *suo dannaggio sogna* makes the harm the object of dreaming, Greek makes unhappiness a quality of the dream and adds *καθ’ ὕπνους* ‘in sleep’ (*κατά* elided and aspirated before rough breathing, with acc. pl.).
- *Ἐν ὀνείρῳ τ’ ὢν* ‘and being in a dream’ expands *sognando* into a prepositional phrase and the pres. participle of *εἰμί*, nom. masc. sg.; *τ’* is connective *τε*. *ὀνειρώττειν ἐλπίζει* ‘hopes he is dreaming’ retains the repeated dream vocabulary but substitutes hope for *desidera*. The present active infinitive of *ὀνειρώττω* (Attic *-ττ-* for *-σσ-*) has the same understood subject as *ἐλπίζει*.
- *Ποθῶν* ‘longing’ (contracted pres. act. participle of *ποθέω*, nom. masc. sg.) makes *sì che … agogna* an accompanying disposition rather than a finite result clause. Its object *ὅπερ δὴ βλέπει* is ‘the very thing he sees’, not *quel ch’ è*: *ὅπερ* (acc. neut. sg.) is an emphatic relative, further pointed by *δή*. Predicative *ὡς ἀνύπαρκτον* ‘as nonexistent’ anticipates that object and replaces *come non fosse* without a finite hypothetical verb.

> 139 tal mi fec’ io, non potendo parlare,  
> 140 che desiava scusarmi, e scusava  
> 141 me tuttavia, e nol mi credea fare.  

: such grew I, who, without power to speak, wished to excuse myself and all the while excused, and did not think that I was doing it.

> 139 Τοιοῦτος ἤμην, μηδὲν ἰσχύων λέγειν.  
> (Such was I, having no power to speak.)
>
> 140 Ἐπόθουν γὰρ συγγνώμην αἰτεῖν, καὶ πάντως  
> (For I longed to ask pardon, and altogether)
>
> 141 ᾜτουν μέν, ἀλλὰ ποιεῖν τοῦτ’ οὐκ ᾠόμην.  
> (Was asking it, yet thought I was not doing so.)

- *Τοιοῦτος ἤμην* ‘such I was’ completes the comparison begun by *ὡς*; an imperfect of state replaces *tal mi fec’ io*. *μηδὲν ἰσχύων λέγειν* ‘being able to say nothing’ closely follows *non potendo parlare*: *ἰσχύων* is a pres. act. participle of *ἰσχύω* with complementary pres. act. infinitive *λέγειν*. Negative *μηδέν* can be taken as the object of *λέγειν*, rather than a simple negation of ‘being able’.
- *Ἐπόθουν γάρ* ‘for I longed’ begins an explanatory sentence in place of Italian *che*; *ἐπόθουν* is the contracted 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ποθέω*. *συγγνώμην αἰτεῖν* ‘to ask pardon’ replaces reflexive *scusarmi* with a pres. act. infinitive of *αἰτέω* and an explicit accusative object. The repeated verb in *ᾜτουν* (1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of the same verb, with temporal augment) carries that object over into the next clause.
- *καὶ πάντως ᾜτουν μέν, ἀλλά …* ‘and indeed I was asking, but …’ gives *tuttavia* a confirmatory rather than explicitly temporal equivalent in *πάντως*. Concessive *μέν* prepares the correction with *ἀλλά*. *ποιεῖν τοῦτ’ οὐκ ᾠόμην* ‘I did not think I was doing this’ fronts the pres. act. infinitive and its object (*τοῦτ’* = *τοῦτο*) but places the negation on the governing verb, as in *nol mi credea fare*. *ᾠόμην* is 1st sg. imperf. mid. ind. of deponent *οἴομαι*; the infinitive needs no expressed subject.

> 142 “Maggior difetto men vergogna lava,”  
> 143 disse il maestro, “che il tuo non è stato :  
> 144 però d’ ogni tristizia ti disgrava;  

“Less shame washes off a greater fault than thine has been,” said the Master : “therefore unload thee of all sorrow

> 142 “Ἥσσων ἐντροπὴ μεῖζον ἐκπλύνει σφάλμα  
> ("Less shame washes away a greater fault)
>
> 143 „ Ἤπερ ἦν τὸ σόν,” εἶπεν οὐμὸς Προστάτης·  
> (Than yours was," said my Master;)
>
> 144 “Διὸ πᾶσά σου θλίψις ἤδη παυσάσθω.  
> ("Wherefore let all your distress now cease.)

- *Ἥσσων ἐντροπὴ μεῖζον ἐκπλύνει σφάλμα* brings the subject ‘less shame’ forward from Dante's *Maggior difetto men vergogna lava*. *ἥσσων* (nom. fem. sg.; Attic also *ἥττων*) modifies *ἐντροπή*; *μεῖζον* (acc. neut. sg., comparative of *μέγας*) modifies *σφάλμα* across *ἐκπλύνει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἐκπλύνω* ‘wash out’).
- *Ἤπερ ἦν τὸ σόν* ‘than yours was’ supplies the comparison after *μεῖζον*; *ἤπερ* is comparative *ἤ* strengthened by *-περ*. The article makes the neuter possessive substantive, with *σφάλμα* understood. *ἦν* is 3rd sg. imperfect, not a perfect corresponding to *è stato*; the redundant comparative negative of *che il tuo non è stato* is not reproduced. In *εἶπεν οὐμὸς Προστάτης*, *οὐμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός* ‘my’: the possessive is supplied for Italian *il*.
- *Διὸ πᾶσά σου θλίψις ἤδη παυσάσθω* ‘therefore let all your distress now cease’ shifts the command from the person (*ti disgrava*) to the distress. *πᾶσα … θλίψις* is nominative subject, *σου* possessive genitive; *παυσάσθω* is 3rd sg. aor. mid. imperative of *παύω*, the middle meaning ‘cease’. *διό* renders inferential *però*, while *ἤδη* ‘now’ is supplied.

> 145 e fa ragion ch’ io ti sia sempre allato,  
> 146 se più avvien che fortuna t’ accoglia,  
> 147 ove sia gente in simigliante piato:  
> 148 chè voler ciò udire è bassa voglia.”  

; and count that I am always at thy side, should it again fall out that Fortune brings thee where people are in similar contests : for the wish to hear it is a vulgar wish.”

> 145 „ Ἡγοῦ δ’ ᾀεὶ παρὰ σοὶ παρίστασθαί με,  
> (And deem that I ever stand beside you,)
>
> 146 „ Ἤν ποτ’ ἐκ τύχης εὑρεθῇς ἐν ἀνθρώποις  
> (If ever by chance you find yourself among men)
>
> 147 „ Τοιᾷδ’ ἔριδι διαπληκτιζομένοις·  
> (Wrangling with one another in such strife;)
>
> 148 „ Ἀγεννὲς γὰρ τὸ θέλειν τοιαῦτ’ ἀκούειν.”  
> (For it is ignoble to wish to hear such things.")

- *Ἡγοῦ δ’ … παρίστασθαί με* ‘and consider me to stand beside you’ replaces *fa ragion ch’ io … sia* with accusative and infinitive after *ἡγοῦ* (contracted 2nd sg. pres. mid. imperative of deponent *ἡγέομαι*). *με* is the subject of *παρίστασθαι*, pres. mid. infinitive of *παρίστημι*; the additional accent on its final syllable is due to the following enclitic. *ᾀεί* is a poetic spelling of *ἀεί* ‘always’. *παρὰ σοὶ* ‘beside you’ has the static dative and reinforces the local sense already present in the compound verb.
- *Ἤν ποτ’ … εὑρεθῇς* ‘if ever you should be found’ uses conditional *ἤν* (= *ἐάν*, from *εἰ* with *ἄν*) and *εὑρεθῇς*, 2nd sg. aor. pass. subjunctive of *εὑρίσκω*. Elided *ποτε* gives indefinite time rather than an explicit ‘again’. *ἐκ τύχης* ‘by chance’ turns *fortuna* from subject into an adverbial phrase; the person whom Fortune brings becomes the subject of the passive. *ἐν ἀνθρώποις* ‘among people’ replaces the local clause *ove sia gente*.
- *Τοιᾷδ’ ἔριδι διαπληκτιζομένοις* ‘wrangling in such a dispute’ qualifies *ἀνθρώποις*. *διαπληκτιζομένοις* (pres. mid. participle of *διαπληκτίζομαι*, dat. masc. pl.) expresses reciprocal action where Italian has *gente in … piato*. *τοιᾷδ’* is elided *τοιᾷδε* (dat. fem. sg. of *τοιόσδε*), agreeing with *ἔριδι* (from *ἔρις*), a dative of circumstance.
- *Ἀγεννὲς γὰρ τὸ θέλειν τοιαῦτ’ ἀκούειν* ‘for to wish to hear such things is ignoble’ gives the reason with postpositive *γάρ*. The articular pres. act. infinitive *τὸ θέλειν* is the subject; *ἀκούειν* completes it, and elided *τοιαῦτ’* (*τοιαῦτα*, acc. neut. pl.) expands singular *ciò* to ‘such things’. The neuter predicate adjective *ἀγεννές* (from *ἀγεννής*) stands first, with the copula understood: Greek thus keeps *voler* as a verb but dispenses with its Italian nominal echo in *bassa voglia*.

## Summary — Shifting the Weight of the Sentence

Musurus often keeps Dante’s sequence of actions while changing which action carries the finite verb. Hecuba’s *vide* and *si fu … accorta* become the agreeing aorist participles *ἰδοῦσα* and *ἀνευροῦσα*, leaving *ὑλάκτησεν* ‘she barked’ as the first finite verb with Hecuba as subject. The movement can also go the other way: for *l’assannò … tirando*, Musurus subordinates the bite in the present participle *δάκνουσα* and makes the dragging the main action, *ἔσυρεν* ‘was dragging’. In both passages the choice of participle matters: the aorists present what preceded the main action, the present what accompanied it.

The Greek stays especially close to the Italian where degree leads into consequence. Musurus repeatedly answers *tanto* or *sì … che* with *οὕτως … ὥστε*, but varies the construction that follows: the finite *ἔκραξε* ‘he cried out’ states what actually happened, whereas *αὐτὸν … σκαλεῦσαι* ‘for him to scrape’ gives the result an infinitive with its own accusative subject. Elsewhere *ἵνα* with the subjunctive makes the purpose of an Italian infinitive explicit, and a past indicative with *ἄν* supplies the unreal conclusion expressed by an Italian conditional. In dialogue, by contrast, Musurus can be as elliptical as Dante: *κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν* ‘and I to him’ needs no verb of saying. Postpositive *δέ* marks a change of speaker, *γε* singles out the answering *σύ* ‘you’, and *γάρ* sometimes supplies an explanatory link that the Italian leaves implicit.

Musurus keeps some verbal echoes by changing their grammatical form. Dante’s repeated *ver* at lines 112–114 becomes *ἀληθῶς*, *ἀληθής*, *ἀλήθειαν* ‘truly’, ‘truthful’, ‘truth’: the same root does duty as adverb, adjective and noun. Concrete terms are more freely exchanged: *liuto* becomes *βάρβιτος*, *fiorini* become *στατῆρες*, and *la donna della torma* is identified outright as *τὴν ὡραίαν φορβάδα* ‘the fair mare’. Occasionally a grammatical change amounts to a different reading. At line 120, where Dante makes the world’s knowledge itself a torment, *γνωστήν* ‘known’ agrees with *ποινήν* ‘punishment’: it is the punishment that everyone knows. Similarly, *σαυτῷ νῦν πρόσχες* ‘now attend to yourself’ gives *Or pur mira!* an explicit reflexive object. The rebuke remains, but its ironic invitation to go on looking becomes a direct command to turn one’s attention elsewhere.
