# Inferno 31

## Lines 1–18 — The Healing Tongue and the Terrible Horn

> 1 Una medesma lingua pria mi morse,  
> 2 sì che mi tinse l' una e l' altra guancia,  
> 3 e poi la medicina mi riporse.  

One and the same tongue first wounded me so that it tinged _with_ blushes both my cheeks, and then held forth the medicine to me.

> 1 Ἡ γλῶσσ᾽, ἥτις μοι πληγὴν ἔδωκε πρῶτον,  
> (The tongue, which first gave me a wound,)
>
> 2 Κἀμφοτέρας παρειὰς ἠρύθρηνέ μοι,  
> (And reddened both my cheeks,)
>
> 3 Εἶτ᾽ αὐτὴ πάλιν ἴασίν μοι παρέσχεν.  
> (Then itself again afforded me healing.)

- *Ἡ γλῶσσ᾽, ἥτις …* ‘the tongue which …’: instead of making *una medesma lingua* the subject of both *morse* and *riporse*, Musurus introduces the first action in a relative clause and reserves the main verb for the second. *γλῶσσ᾽* is elided *γλῶσσα* (nom. sg. fem.); *ἥτις* (nom. sg. fem. of *ὅστις*) refers to this particular tongue, with much the force of simple *ἥ*.
- *μοι πληγὴν ἔδωκε πρῶτον* ‘gave me a wound first’ replaces the verb *mi morse* ‘bit me’ with *ἔδωκε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *δίδωμι*), an accusative of the thing given, and the enclitic dative *μοι* of the recipient. *πρῶτον*, the neuter singular used adverbially, comes last, whereas *pria* precedes *mi morse*.
- *Κἀμφοτέρας παρειὰς ἠρύθρηνέ μοι* ‘and reddened both my cheeks’: *κἀμφοτέρας* is crasis of *καὶ ἀμφοτέρας*. The conjunction coordinates a second verb under *ἥτις*, in place of the Italian result clause *sì che mi tinse*. *ἀμφοτέρας* agrees with *παρειάς* in the accusative feminine plural, not the dual: one adjective expresses *l’ una e l’ altra*. *ἠρύθρηνε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐρυθραίνω*, with temporal augment) specifies the colour that *tinse* leaves implicit. The extra acute in *ἠρύθρηνέ μοι* is due to the following enclitic; *μοι* marks the person affected, hence the possessor of the cheeks.
- *Εἶτ᾽ αὐτὴ πάλιν ἴασίν μοι παρέσχεν* ‘then it itself in turn afforded me healing’: *εἶτ᾽* is elided *εἶτα*; intensive *αὐτή* (nom. sg. fem.) takes up *ἡ γλῶσσα* and supplies the emphasis of *medesma*. *πάλιν* reinforces the reversal. *παρέσχεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *παρέχω*) governs *μοι* and *ἴασιν* (acc. sg. of *ἴασις*): the act of healing replaces the remedy *la medicina*, and affording it replaces holding it forth.

> 4 Così od' io che soleva la lancia  
> 5 d' Achille e del suo padre esser cagione  
> 6 prima di trista e poi di buona mancia.  

Thus I have heard that the lance of Achilles, and of his father, used to be occasion first of sad and then of healing gift.

> 4 Οὕτως εἰώθει, καθὰ λόγος, τὸ δόρυ  
> (Thus was wont, as the story goes, the spear)
>
> 5 Ἀχιλλέως καὶ πατρὸς αἴτιον εἶναι  
> (Of Achilles and his father to be the cause)
>
> 6 Πρῶτον ὀδύνης, ἀλλ᾽ εἶτα θεραπείας.  
> (First of pain, but afterward of cure.)

- *Οὕτως εἰώθει, καθὰ λόγος* ‘thus it was wont, as the report goes’: *οὕτως* answers to *Così*, but *od’ io che* no longer governs the sentence. The first-person report becomes a parenthesis, *καθὰ λόγος*, with *καθά* ‘according as’ and an understood ‘is’; the habitual action becomes the main assertion. *εἰώθει* is the 3rd sg. pluperfect active indicative of *ἔθω*: as the perfect *εἴωθα* means ‘am accustomed’, the pluperfect gives the past sense of *soleva*.
- *τὸ δόρυ Ἀχιλλέως καὶ πατρὸς αἴτιον εἶναι* follows the sequence *la lancia / d’ Achille e del suo padre esser cagione*. *τὸ δόρυ* (nom. sg. neut.) is the subject of *εἰώθει*; the coordinated possessive genitives *Ἀχιλλέως* (from *Ἀχιλλεύς*) and *πατρός* (from *πατήρ*) depend on it, with *suo* understood. *εἶναι* is the present infinitive of *εἰμί*, complementary to *εἰώθει*; predicate *αἴτιον* ‘responsible for’ agrees with *δόρυ* in the nominative, rather than standing as a noun like *cagione*.
- *Πρῶτον ὀδύνης, ἀλλ᾽ εἶτα θεραπείας* ‘first of pain, but then of cure’: the two dependent genitives (singular of *ὀδύνη*, *θεραπεία*) name the effects directly. Neither *mancia* nor its qualifying adjectives are translated separately. Elided *ἀλλ᾽* (*ἀλλά*) makes the contrast more explicit than *e poi*.

> 7 Noi demmo il dosso al misero vallone,  
> 8 su per la ripa che il cinge d' intorno  
> 9 attraversando senza alcun sermone.  

We turned our back to the wretched valley, up by the bank that girds it round, crossing without any speech.

> 7 Ἐτρέψαμεν τὰ νῶτα πρὸς τὴν κοιλάδα  
> (We turned our backs toward the valley)
>
> 8 Τὴν οἰκτράν, ἄνω τῆς συγκυκλούσης ὄχθης,  
> (The piteous one, up along the encircling bank,)
>
> 9 Ἣν ἐν σιωπῇ διαβαίνοντες ἦμεν.  
> (Which in silence we were crossing.)

- *Ἐτρέψαμεν τὰ νῶτα πρὸς τὴν κοιλάδα / Τὴν οἰκτράν* ‘we turned our backs toward the valley, the piteous one’: *ἐτρέψαμεν* (1st pl. aor. act. ind. of *τρέπω*) expresses the turn where Italian has *demmo* ‘gave’; its ending suffices for *Noi*. Greek has plural *τὰ νῶτα* for singular *il dosso*. *πρός* with the accusative specifies the direction in which the backs are turned. The adjective *οἰκτράν* agrees with *κοιλάδα* (acc. sg. of *κοιλάς*) in the second attributive position, with the article repeated, rather than preceding the noun as *misero* does.
- *ἄνω τῆς συγκυκλούσης ὄχθης* ‘up along the encircling bank’ closely follows *su per la ripa*. *ἄνω* is used prepositionally with the genitive; by itself that construction can mean ‘above’, rather than indicating a route along something. *συγκυκλούσης* (gen. sg. fem. pres. act. participle of *συγκυκλόω*) is attributive to *ὄχθης*. The compound participle compresses *che il cinge d’ intorno* and leaves its object, the valley, unexpressed.
- *Ἣν ἐν σιωπῇ διαβαίνοντες ἦμεν* ‘which we were crossing in silence’: the relative *ἥν* refers back to the bank but is accusative as object of *διαβαίνοντες*. Thus *attraversando* becomes the predicate of a relative clause: *διαβαίνοντες* (nom. pl. masc. pres. act. participle of *διαβαίνω*) with *ἦμεν* (1st pl. impf. ind. of *εἰμί*) is a periphrastic imperfect. *ἐν σιωπῇ*, with the dative of *σιωπή*, expresses positively the manner that *senza alcun sermone* expresses by exclusion.

> 10 Quivi era men che notte e men che giorno,  
> 11 sì che il viso m' andava innanzi poco;  

Here was less than night and less than day, so that my sight went little _way_ before me;

> 10 Ὧδε δ᾽ ἦν ἧττον νυκτός, ἧττόν θ᾽ ἡμέρας,  
> (And here it was less than night, and less than day,)
>
> 11 Ὥστ᾽ οὐ τὰς ὄψεις ἔτεινον ἂν μακράν που·  
> (So that I could not stretch my sight anywhere far;)

- *Ὧδε δ᾽ ἦν ἧττον νυκτός, ἧττόν θ᾽ ἡμέρας* preserves the unusual comparison *Quivi era men che notte e men che giorno* almost word for word. *ὧδε* is locative, ‘here’; postpositive *δ᾽* (elided *δέ*) adds a transition. With impersonal *ἦν* (3rd sg. impf. of *εἰμί*), repeated *ἧττον* is the Attic comparative form (neuter singular, used adverbially); *νυκτός* and *ἡμέρας* are genitives of comparison replacing *che* with each noun. Enclitic *τε* joins the second phrase: *θ᾽* shows elision and aspiration of the surviving consonant before rough-breathed *ἡμέρας*.
- *Ὥστ᾽ οὐ τὰς ὄψεις ἔτεινον ἂν μακράν που* ‘so that I could not extend my sight anywhere far’: *ὥστ᾽* (elided *ὥστε*) introduces the consequence, but the finite verb with *ἄν* gives it a potential, qualified force. *ἔτεινον* is here 1st sg. impf. act. ind. of *τείνω*: the speaker becomes the subject and *τὰς ὄψεις* (acc. pl. of *ὄψις*) his object, whereas *il viso* itself is the subject of *m’ andava*. Negated ‘far’ replaces positive *poco*; adverbial *μακράν* expresses distance and enclitic *που* indefinite place, without a separate equivalent for *innanzi*.

> 12 ma io senti' sonare un alto corno,  
> 13 tanto ch' avrebbe ogni tuon fatto fioco,  

but I heard a high horn sound so _loudly_, that it would have made any thunder weak;

> 12 Ἀλλ᾽ ἤκουσ᾽ ἠχοῦν κέρας ὀξέως οὕτως,  
> (But I heard a horn sounding so sharply,)
>
> 13 Ὥστε κεραυνοῦ πάταγον ἀποπνίγειν.  
> (That it drowned out the crash of a thunderbolt.)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ ἤκουσ᾽ ἠχοῦν κέρας* ‘but I heard a horn sounding’: both *ἀλλά* and *ἤκουσα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀκούω*) are elided. The perception verb takes *κέρας* (acc. sg. neut.) with the supplementary participle *ἠχοῦν* (pres. act. of *ἠχέω*, contracted, acc. sg. neut.), rather than the Italian infinitive *sonare*; the participle precedes its noun as *sonare* precedes *un alto corno*.
- *ὀξέως οὕτως, Ὥστε … ἀποπνίγειν* ‘so sharply as to drown out’: the adverb *ὀξέως* transfers *alto* from the horn to the quality of its sounding; *οὕτως … ὥστε* renders *tanto ch’*. Unlike the finite result construction in line 11, *ὥστε* here governs a present active infinitive (*ἀποπνίγειν*, from *ἀποπνίγω*, literally ‘choke off’). It characterizes the sound by what it is sufficient to do, without reproducing the conditional perfect *avrebbe … fatto*.
- *κεραυνοῦ πάταγον* ‘the crash of a thunderbolt’ divides *tuon* into a sound (*πάταγον*, acc. sg., object of the infinitive) and its source (*κεραυνοῦ*, gen. sg.). There is no separate *ogni*, and drowning out the crash is stronger than making it *fioco*.

> 14 che, contra sè la sua via seguitando,  
> 15 dirizzò gli occhi miei tutti ad un loco:  

which directed my eyes, [that followed its course against itself], all to one place:

> 14 Καὶ δὴ πρὸς τοῦ κέρατος στραφεὶς τὸν κρότον,  
> (And indeed turning toward the sound of the horn,)
>
> 15 Ὅλως ἀπηύθυν’ ἐκεῖ τὰς ἐμὰς ὄψεις.  
> (Wholly I directed my eyes thither.)

- *Καὶ δὴ … στραφείς* ‘and then, turning …’: Musurus abandons the relative construction *che … dirizzò*, in which the sound directs the eyes. *καὶ δή* introduces a new assertion with the speaker as subject. *στραφείς* (nom. sg. masc., second aor. pass. participle of *στρέφω*) agrees with that subject; passive in form, it denotes his own turn, preliminary to the main action.
- *πρὸς τοῦ κέρατος στραφεὶς τὸν κρότον* means ‘turning toward the noise of the horn’: despite the word order, directional *πρός* governs accusative *τὸν κρότον*, not genitive *τοῦ κέρατος*, which qualifies the noise. Musurus gives the direction implicit in *contra sè la sua via seguitando* rather than translating the path, the reflexive, or the act of following it back.
- *Ὅλως ἀπηύθυν’ ἐκεῖ τὰς ἐμὰς ὄψεις* ‘I directed my gaze wholly thither’: *ἀπηύθυν’* is elided *ἀπηύθυνα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀπευθύνω*, augmented after the preverb), replacing third-person *dirizzò*. *ὅλως* modifies the directing, whereas *tutti* agrees with *gli occhi*. *ἐκεῖ* reduces *ad un loco* to an adverb of place; *τὰς ἐμὰς ὄψεις* has the possessive adjective within the article–noun group, in contrast to postposed *miei*.

> 16 dopo la dolorosa rotta, quando  
> 17 Carlo Magno perdè la santa gesta,  
> 18 non sonò sì terribilmente Orlando.  

after the dolorous rout, when Charlemain had lost the holy emprise, [Roland] did not sound _with his_ so terribly.

> 16 Μετὰ τὴν οἰκτρὰν τροπήν, καθ᾽ ἣν ἀγῶνος  
> (After the piteous rout, in which of the struggle—)
>
> 17 Πανιέρου Κάρολος Μέγας ἐσφάλη,  
> (All-holy—Charlemain failed,)
>
> 18 Οὐ κλαγγὴν δεινὴν τοιάνδ᾽ ἤχησ’ Ὀρλάνδος.  
> (Not so terrible a blast did Roland sound.)

- *Μετὰ τὴν οἰκτρὰν τροπήν* keeps the initial time phrase: *μετά* with the accusative means ‘after’, and *τροπή* ‘turning, rout’ renders *rotta*. *καθ᾽ ἥν* ‘during which’ replaces temporal *quando* with a relative referring to *τροπήν*: *κατά* is elided and its *τ* aspirated before rough-breathed *ἥν* (acc. sg. fem.).
- *ἀγῶνος / Πανιέρου Κάρολος Μέγας ἐσφάλη* ‘Charles the Great failed of an all-holy struggle’: *πανιέρου* (gen. sg. masc. of *πανίερος*) intensifies *santa* and agrees across the line break with *ἀγῶνος* (from *ἀγών*), an interpretation of *gesta* as an undertaking or contest. The whole phrase is brought forward before the nominative subject *Κάρολος Μέγας*. In place of transitive *perdè* with a direct object, *ἐσφάλη* (3rd sg. second aor. pass. ind. of *σφάλλω*) means ‘was foiled, failed’, with the genitive of that which he failed to achieve.
- *Οὐ κλαγγὴν δεινὴν τοιάνδ᾽ ἤχησ’ Ὀρλάνδος* ‘no such terrible blast did Roland sound’: instead of modifying *sonò* with *sì terribilmente*, Musurus gives *ἤχησ’* (elided *ἤχησε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἠχέω*) an accusative of the sound produced. *δεινήν* and *τοιάνδ᾽* (elided *τοιάνδε*, acc. sg. fem. of *τοιόσδε*) qualify *κλαγγήν*; ‘such’ makes the comparison that Italian marks by degree. The subject *Ὀρλάνδος* remains emphatically last, as does *Orlando*.

## Lines 19–51 — Towers Seen as Giants

> 19 Poco portai in là volta la testa,  
> 20 che mi parve veder molte alte torri ;  

Short while had I kept [my head turned] in that direction, when I seemed to see many lofty towers;

> 19 Μικρόν τι τὴν κεφαλὴν ἐκεῖσ᾽ ἐπάρας,  
> (Having lifted my head thither a little,)
>
> 20 Ἐδόκουν ὁρᾷν πολλοὺς ὑψηλοὺς πύργους,  
> (I seemed to see many lofty towers,)

- *Μικρόν τι* is an adverbial accusative, ‘a little’ (*τι*, enclitic neut. sg. of *τις*, makes the extent indefinite). Italian *poco* measures the time the head remained turned; the Greek can more readily be heard as measuring the movement itself. *ἐκεῖσ᾽* is elided *ἐκεῖσε*, specifically ‘thither’, for *in là*.
- *τὴν κεφαλὴν ἐκεῖσ᾽ ἐπάρας* ‘having raised my head thither’ replaces *portai in là volta la testa*: the head is the object of *ἐπάρας* (nom. masc. sg., aor. act. participle of *ἐπαίρω*), not a noun qualified by a participle meaning ‘turned’. The circumstantial participle makes the movement anterior to the seeing, dispensing with a conjunction for *che*.
- *Ἐδόκουν ὁρᾷν* ‘I seemed to see’ is personal: *ἐδόκουν* (1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *δοκέω*) takes the contracted present active infinitive of *ὁράω* with the same understood subject. Italian has the impersonal preterite *mi parve* and a dative experiencer. *πολλοὺς ὑψηλοὺς πύργους* retains the order of *molte alte torri*, with both adjectives accusative masculine plural like their noun.

> 21 ond'io : “ Maestro, di', che terra è questa ?”  

whereat I: "Master! say, what town is this?"

> 21 Κ᾽ εἶπον· “Προστάτα, τίς ἔσθ᾽ ἡ πόλις αὕτη;”  
> (And I said: “Protector, what is this city?”)

- *Κ᾽ εἶπον* ‘and I said’ supplies the verb left out of *ond'io*, but not the explicit pronoun or the consequential force of *onde*. *Κ᾽* is poetic elision of *καί*; *εἶπον* is the 1st sg. second aorist active indicative used with *λέγω*. There is no separate imperative for *di'*.
- *Προστάτα*, vocative singular of *προστάτης*, substitutes ‘protector’ for *Maestro*. In *τίς ἔσθ᾽ ἡ πόλις αὕτη;*, *τίς* is the interrogative predicate, while *ἡ πόλις αὕτη* is the subject, with the demonstrative outside the article–noun group. *πόλις* specifies the urban sense of *terra*. *ἔσθ᾽* represents *ἐστί* with elision and aspiration of *τ* before the rough breathing of *ἡ*.

> 22 Ed egli a me : “ Però che tu trascorri  
> 23 per le tenebre troppo dalla lungi,  
> 24 avvien che poi nel 'maginare aborri.  

And he to me: "Because thou traversest the darkness too far off, it follows that thou errest in thy imagining.

> 22 Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ· “Διότι θέλεις βλέπειν  
> (And he to me: “Because you wish to look)
>
> 23 „ Διὰ τῶν ζόφων μακράν, πέρα τοῦ μέτρου,  
> („ Through the gloom afar, beyond measure,)
>
> 24 „ Συμβαίνει σοι πλανᾶσθαι τὴν φαντασίαν.  
> („ It happens that your imagination is led astray.)

- *Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ* ‘and he to me’ keeps the ellipsis of a verb of speaking in *Ed egli a me*. *ὁ δέ* uses the article pronominally to mark the change of speaker; *πρός* takes the accusative of the person addressed.
- Causal *Διότι* introduces *θέλεις βλέπειν* ‘you wish to look’ (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *θέλω* with pres. act. infinitive of *βλέπω*). Musurus interprets *trascorri* as an attempt to see too far, supplying both the visual verb and the element of volition. *διὰ τῶν ζόφων* keeps the plural of *per le tenebre*, with *διά* and the genitive of the medium traversed; *μακράν* ‘afar’ and *πέρα τοῦ μέτρου* ‘beyond measure’ distinguish distance from excess in *troppo dalla lungi*.
- *Συμβαίνει σοι πλανᾶσθαι τὴν φαντασίαν* retains impersonal *avvien* as *συμβαίνει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *συμβαίνω*), but substitutes a dative and infinitive for *che* with a finite verb. *πλανᾶσθαι* is the contracted pres. mid./pass. infinitive of *πλανάω*, ‘to go astray’ or ‘to be led astray’. Taking *τὴν φαντασίαν* as its accusative subject gives ‘it happens to you that the imagination is led astray’; taking *σοι* as identifying the person who errs leaves *τὴν φαντασίαν* as an accusative of respect, closer to *nel 'maginare aborri*. *poi* has no separate equivalent.

> 25 Tu vedrai ben, se tu là ti congiungi,  
> 26 quanto il senso s'inganna di lontano :  

Thou shalt see right well, [if] thou arrivest there, how much the sense at distance is deceived:

> 25 „ Ἀλλ᾽ ὄψει καλῶς, ὅταν ᾽κεῖ προσπελάσῃς,  
> („ But you shall see clearly, when you draw near thither,)
>
> 26 „ Ὅσον αἴσθησις ἀπατᾶται μακρόθεν.  
> („ How much the sense is deceived from afar.)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ ὄψει καλῶς* adds adversative *ἀλλά*, elided before the vowel, to *Tu vedrai ben*. *ὄψει* is 2nd sg. future middle indicative of the suppletive future *ὄψομαι* of *ὁράω*, active in meaning; the subject pronoun is not expressed.
- *ὅταν ᾽κεῖ προσπελάσῃς* ‘when you draw near there’ makes *se* temporal rather than conditional. *ὅταν* (*ὅτε* with *ἄν*) takes the aorist active subjunctive *προσπελάσῃς* (2nd sg. of *προσπελάζω*) for a prospective event, not an optative. *᾽κεῖ* is *ἐκεῖ* with loss of the initial vowel. The simple active verb of approach replaces reflexive *ti congiungi*.
- *Ὅσον* is an accusative of degree introducing what will be seen. *αἴσθησις*, an anarthrous abstract noun, renders *il senso*; *ἀπατᾶται* (3rd sg. contracted pres. mid./pass. ind. of *ἀπατάω*) gives *s'inganna* a passive sense. *μακρόθεν*, placed last, has the force ‘from a distance’.

> 27 però alquanto più te stesso pungi.”  

therefore spur thee somewhat more."

> 27 „ Διὸ σπεῦσον τὸ βῆμα μικρόν τι πρόσω.”  
> („ Therefore hasten your step a little forward.”)

- *Διό* ‘wherefore’ renders inferential *però*. *σπεῦσον* (2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *σπεύδω*) takes *τὸ βῆμα* as its object: ‘quicken your step’ replaces *te stesso pungi*, with neither a reflexive nor a verb of spurring.
- *μικρόν τι πρόσω* ‘a little forward’ repeats the indefinite measure of line 19. The adverb *πρόσω* gives a direction where *più* gives a comparative degree.

> 28 Poi caramente mi prese per mano  
> 29 e disse : “ Pria che noi siam più avanti,  
> 30 acciocchè il fatto men ti paia strano,  

Then lovingly he took me by the hand, and said: "Ere we go farther, that the reality may seem less strange to thee,

> 28 Εἶτ᾽ εὐμενῶς τῆς χειρὸς ἐλάβετό μου  
> (Then kindly he took me by the hand,)
>
> 29 Λέγων· “Πρὶν ἡμεῖς προβῶμεν περαιτέρω,  
> (Saying: “Before we advance further,)
>
> 30 „ Ὅπως τὸ πρᾶγμα ξένον ἧττόν σοι δόξῃ,  
> („ So that the thing may seem less strange to you,)

- *Εἶτ᾽* is *εἶτα* elided, ‘then’; *εὐμενῶς* ‘kindly’ renders *caramente*. *τῆς χειρὸς ἐλάβετό μου* uses *λαμβάνομαι* with the genitive of what one takes hold of. *ἐλάβετο* is 3rd sg. second aorist middle indicative of *λαμβάνω*; the additional accent in *ἐλάβετό* is due to enclitic *μου*, which supplies the possessor of the hand where Italian has *mi … per mano*.
- *Λέγων*, nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *λέγω*, makes *e disse* an accompaniment to the taking of the hand. *Πρὶν ἡμεῖς προβῶμεν περαιτέρω* replaces *siam più avanti* with movement, ‘we advance farther’, retaining emphatic *ἡμεῖς* for *noi*. *προβῶμεν* is 1st pl. root-aorist active subjunctive of *προβαίνω*. Bare *πρίν* with the subjunctive is less characteristically Attic here than an infinitival construction for ‘before we advance’.
- Purpose *Ὅπως* takes *δόξῃ* (3rd sg. aor. act. subj. of *δοκέω*). *τὸ πρᾶγμα* is its subject, *ξένον* the predicate adjective, and *σοι* the dative of the person judging. Comparative adverb *ἧττον* qualifies *ξένον* but follows it, reversing *men … strano*; its additional accent in *ἧττόν σοι* marks the following enclitic.

> 31 sappi che non son torri, ma giganti,  

know, they are not towers, but Giants;

> 31 „ Ἴσθι Γίγαντας, ἀλλ᾽ οὐχὶ πύργους ὄντας.  
> („ Know that they are Giants, and not towers.)

- *Ἴσθι* is the 2nd sg. imperative of *οἶδα*, a perfect with present meaning, ‘know’. In place of *che … son*, it takes a supplementary participle: *ὄντας* (acc. masc. pl., pres. act. of *εἰμί*) agrees with the understood persons who are the object of knowledge. *Γίγαντας* and *πύργους* are predicate accusatives.
- *Γίγαντας, ἀλλ᾽ οὐχὶ πύργους* gives the positive identification first, reversing *non son torri, ma giganti*. *ἀλλ᾽* is elided *ἀλλά*; *οὐχί* is the strengthened negative.

> 32 e son nel pozzo intorno dalla ripa  
> 33 dall'umbilico in giuso tutti e quanti.”  

and are in the well, around its bank, from the navel downwards all of them."

> 32 „ Ἔγκλειστοι δ᾽ εἰσὶν ἐν τῷ φρέατι πέριξ  
> („ And they are shut within the well round about)
>
> 33 „ Τῆς ὄχθης ἀπ᾽ ὀμφαλοῦ καὶ κάτω πάντες.”  
> („ The bank, from the navel downward all of them.”)

- *Ἔγκλειστοι δ᾽ εἰσίν* ‘and they are shut in’ adds a predicate adjective (nom. masc. pl.) to bare *e son*; *δ᾽* is elided postpositive *δέ*. *ἐν τῷ φρέατι* is static, with the dative singular of the neuter *φρέαρ*. *πέριξ* governs the genitive *τῆς ὄχθης* across the line division, ‘round the bank’; Greek does not reproduce the preposition in *dalla ripa* separately.
- *ἀπ᾽ ὀμφαλοῦ καὶ κάτω* ‘from the navel and down’ has elided *ἀπό* with a genitive of starting point followed by a directional adverb. Final *πάντες*, nominative in agreement with the subject of *εἰσίν*, compresses *tutti e quanti*.

> 34 Come, quando la nebbia si dissipa,  
> 35 lo sguardo a poco a poco raffigura  
> 36 ciò che cela il vapor che l'aere stipa:  

As when a mist is vanishing, the eye by little and little reshapes that which the air-crowding vapour hides;

> 34 Ὡς δ᾽ ὅτ᾽ ἄρχετ᾽ ὀμίχλη σκεδαζομένη,  
> (And as when a mist begins to scatter,)
>
> 35 Τὸ βλέμμα διακρίνει βαθμηδὸν ὅ, τι  
> (The gaze distinguishes by degrees whatever)
>
> 36 Ἀτμὸς ὁ πυκνούμενος ἀέρι κρύπτει,  
> (The thickening vapour hides in the air,)

- *Ὡς δ᾽ ὅτ᾽* ‘and as when’ brings together comparative *ὡς*, connective *δέ*, and temporal *ὅτε*, the last two elided. *ἄρχετ᾽ ὀμίχλη σκεδαζομένη* expands *la nebbia si dissipa* to ‘a mist begins to disperse’. *ἄρχετ᾽* is poetic elision of *ἄρχεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *ἄρχω* in the sense ‘begin’); *σκεδαζομένη* is the complementary pres. mid./pass. participle of *σκεδάζω*, nom. fem. sg. with *ὀμίχλη*.
- *Τὸ βλέμμα διακρίνει βαθμηδόν* ‘the gaze distinguishes by degrees’ interprets *raffigura* as coming to discern, and replaces repeated *a poco a poco* by one adverb. *ὅ, τι* is the neut. sg. indefinite relative of *ὅστις*, ‘whatever’, not conjunctive *ὅτι*. It is accusative object of *κρύπτει*, and the whole relative clause supplies the object of *διακρίνει*, without a separate demonstrative for *ciò*.
- *Ἀτμὸς ὁ πυκνούμενος ἀέρι* makes the vapour the subject of *κρύπτει* ‘hides’. The following article marks *πυκνούμενος* (nom. masc. sg. contracted pres. mid./pass. participle of *πυκνόω*) as a qualification of *ἀτμός*. In *che l'aere stipa* the vapour crowds the air; in Greek the vapour itself thickens, with *ἀέρι* (dat. sg. of *ἀήρ*) indicating the medium.

> 37 così forando l'aura grossa e scura,  
> 38 più e più appressando in ver la sponda,  
> 39 fuggìemi errore, e cresce' mi paura.  

so whilst piercing through that gross and darksome air, more and more approaching towards the brink, error [fled] from me, and [my] fear [increased].

> 37 Οὕτως, ἐφ’ ὅσον σχίζων τὸ παχὺ νέφος  
> (So, as cleaving the thick cloud)
>
> 38 Καὶ μέλαν βάδην προὐχώρουν πρὸς τὴν ὄχθην,  
> (And dark I advanced step by step toward the bank,)
>
> 39 Ἔφευγ᾽ ἡ πλάνη, προσήγγιζε δ᾽ ὁ φόβος.  
> (Error fled away, and fear drew near.)

- *Οὕτως* answers *ὡς*, as *così* answers *Come*. *ἐφ’ ὅσον* ‘insofar as, while’ has *ἐπί* elided and aspirated before the rough breathing. It introduces a clause with finite *προὐχώρουν*; within it *σχίζων* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *σχίζω*) renders simultaneous *forando*. *τὸ παχὺ νέφος καὶ μέλαν* gives ‘the thick cloud and dark’, delaying the second adjective and substituting neuter *νέφος* for *aura*.
- *βάδην προὐχώρουν* ‘I was advancing step by step’ replaces *più e più appressando*: an adverb of gait does the work of the repetition, and the imperfect presents the approach as continuing. *προὐχώρουν* is 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *προχωρέω*, with crasis of the prefix vowel and syllabic augment (*προ-ε-* > *πρου-*) and contraction in the ending. *πρὸς τὴν ὄχθην* uses *πρός* with the accusative of the goal.
- *Ἔφευγ᾽ ἡ πλάνη, προσήγγιζε δ᾽ ὁ φόβος* balances two 3rd sg. imperfect active indicatives: elided *ἔφευγε* from *φεύγω* and augmented *προσήγγιζε* from *προσαγγίζω*. Error ‘was fleeing’, but fear ‘was drawing near’, not growing as in *cresce'*. The subjects follow their verbs, and neither Italian *mi* is expressed.

> 40 Però che, come in su la cerchia tonda  
> 41 Montereggion di torri si corona :  

For as on its round wall Montereggione crowns itself with towers:

> 40 Ὡς γὰρ στέφεται πέριξ πύργοις τὸ τεῖχος  
> (For as the wall is crowned round about with towers,)
>
> 41 Τὸ κυκλοτερὲς τοῦ Μόντος—Ῥεγιῶνος,  
> (The circular one, of Montereggione,)

- *Ὡς γάρ* gives comparative *ὡς* followed by explanatory, postpositive *γάρ* for *Però che, come*. In *στέφεται πέριξ πύργοις τὸ τεῖχος* the wall, not the town, is the subject: *στέφεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *στέφω*) is naturally passive, ‘is crowned’; adverbial *πέριξ* adds ‘all round’, and *πύργοις* is an instrumental dative for *di torri*.
- *τὸ τεῖχος τὸ κυκλοτερές* ‘the wall, the circular one’ uses the repeated article in second attributive position; *κυκλοτερές* is nom. neut. sg. of *κυκλοτερής*. *τοῦ Μόντος—Ῥεγιῶνος* recasts the Italian subject *Montereggion* as a dependent genitive, the place to which the wall belongs.

> 42 così la proda che il pozzo circonda  
> 43 torreggiavan di mezza la persona  
> 44 gli orribili giganti, cui minaccia  
> 45 Giove del cielo ancora, quando tuona.  

so with half their bodies, the horrible giants, whom Jove from heaven still threatens when he thunders, turreted the [bank] which compasses the pit.

> 42 Οὕτω τὸ περικυκλοῦν τὸ φρέαρ χεῖλος  
> (So on the rim encircling the well)
>
> 43 Ἐπύργουν πέριξ μέχρι σώματος μέσου  
> (Towered round about, up to the middle of their body,)
>
> 44 Οἱ φρικώδεις Γίγαντες, οὓς οὐρανόθεν  
> (The dreadful Giants, whom from heaven)
>
> 45 Ἔτ᾽ ἀπειλεῖ Ζεύς, ἐκ νεφελῶν βροντήσας.  
> (Jove still threatens, thundering from the clouds.)

- *Οὕτω* answers *ὡς*. *τὸ περικυκλοῦν τὸ φρέαρ χεῖλος* is the fronted accusative object of *ἐπύργουν*: between the article and *χεῖλος* ‘rim’ stands *περικυκλοῦν* (acc. neut. sg. contracted pres. act. participle of *περικυκλόω*) with its own object *τὸ φρέαρ*. This attributive participle replaces finite *che il pozzo circonda*.
- *Ἐπύργουν* is 3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *πυργόω*, ‘furnish with towers’, contracted from an ending in *-οον*. Like *torreggiavan* it takes the rim as object and delays its subject, *Οἱ φρικώδεις Γίγαντες*. Adverbial *πέριξ* repeats the notion ‘round about’; *μέχρι σώματος μέσου* gives a limit, ‘as far as mid-body’, with *μέχρι* governing the genitive, instead of Italian *di mezza la persona*.
- *οὓς … ἀπειλεῖ Ζεύς* uses the acc. masc. pl. relative as the direct object of ‘threatens’, following Italian *cui minaccia* more closely than the usual Attic *ἀπειλέω* with a dative of the person threatened. *οὐρανόθεν* ‘from heaven’ renders *del cielo* by the poetic source suffix *-θεν*; *ἔτ᾽* is elided *ἔτι*, ‘still’.
- *ἐκ νεφελῶν βροντήσας* supplies ‘from clouds’ (*ἐκ* with gen. pl. of *νεφέλη*). *βροντήσας*, nom. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *βροντάω*, agrees with *Ζεύς*: the temporal circumstance of *quando tuona* becomes a participle presenting the thundering as an act, rather than an explicitly recurrent present-tense clause.

> 46 Ed io scorgeva già d'alcun la faccia,  
> 47 le spalle e il petto, e del ventre gran parte,  
> 48 e per le coste giù ambo le braccia.  

And already I discerned the face of one, the shoulders and the breast, and great part of the belly, and down along his sides both arms.

> 46 Ἤδη παρετήρησά τινος τὴν ὄψιν,  
> (Already I observed the face of one,)
>
> 47 Ὤμους καὶ στῆθος καὶ γαστρὸς μέγα μέρος,  
> (Shoulders and chest and a great part of the belly,)
>
> 48 Καὶ πρὸς τοῖς πλευροῖς τοὺς βραχίονας ἄμφω.  
> (And along his sides both his arms.)

- *Ἤδη παρετήρησά τινος τὴν ὄψιν* keeps *già* and the possessive-before-object order of *d'alcun la faccia*, but drops *Ed io* as separate words. *παρετήρησα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *παρατηρέω*, augmented after the prefix) gives ‘I observed’ for imperfect *scorgeva*. Its final additional accent is due to enclitic *τινος*, gen. sg. of indefinite *τις*, ‘of one’; *τὴν ὄψιν* is its accusative object.
- *Ὤμους καὶ στῆθος καὶ γαστρὸς μέγα μέρος* continues the objects without the Italian articles: acc. pl. *ὤμους*, acc. neut. sg. *στῆθος*, and *μέγα μέρος*, with partitive *γαστρός* (gen. sg. of *γαστήρ*) placed first as in *del ventre gran parte*.
- *πρὸς τοῖς πλευροῖς* uses *πρός* with a static dative, ‘beside the flanks’, for *per le coste giù*, omitting the downward direction. *τοὺς βραχίονας ἄμφω* reverses *ambo le braccia*: *ἄμφω* is the nominative/accusative dual form ‘both’, here accusative in function beside an ordinary accusative plural, not a dual noun.

> 49 Natura certo, quando lasciò l'arte  
> 50 di sì fatti animali, assai fe' bene,  
> 51 per torre tali esecutori a Marte ;  

Nature certainly, when she left off the art of making animals like these, did very well, in taking away such executioners from Mars;

> 49 Ὄντως ἡ φύσις, ὅτ᾽ ἔπαυσε τὸν γόνον  
> (Truly Nature, when she stopped the breeding)
>
> 50 Τοιούτων ζώων, καλῶς διενοήθη,  
> (Of such creatures, planned well,)
>
> 51 Στερήσασα τὸν Ἄρην τοιούτων ὅπλων.  
> (Having deprived Mars of such weapons.)

- *Ὄντως ἡ φύσις* places the adverb ‘truly’ before the articular subject, reversing *Natura certo*. Temporal *ὅτ᾽* is elided *ὅτε*; *ἔπαυσε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *παύω*) is transitive, ‘stopped’. Its object *τὸν γόνον* ‘the breeding, production’ replaces *l'arte*, so the Greek needs no separate expression for making. *τοιούτων ζώων*, gen. neut. pl., depends on *γόνον*, retaining the qualification in *di sì fatti animali*.
- *καλῶς διενοήθη* ‘she judged well’ interprets *assai fe' bene* as a judgment rather than a deed; there is no distinct equivalent for *assai*. *διενοήθη* is 3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. in form, active in sense, of deponent *διανοέομαι*, with augment after the prefix.
- *Στερήσασα* (nom. fem. sg. aor. act. participle of *στερέω*) agrees with *ἡ φύσις*. Italian *per torre* can express purpose; the Greek circumstantial participle instead gives the act in which the good judgment consisted. *τὸν Ἄρην* is the accusative of the person deprived, *τοιούτων ὅπλων* the genitive of what is taken away. This reverses the distribution in *torre tali esecutori a Marte*, where the things removed are the direct object, and changes personal *esecutori* into instrumental *ὅπλα*, ‘weapons’.

## Lines 52–84 — Nimrod’s Stature and Speech

> 52 e s'ella d'elefanti e di balene  
> 53 non si pente, chi guarda sottilmente  
> 54 più giusta e più discreta la ne tiene:  

and if she repents her not of Elephants and Whales, whoso subtly looks, therein regards her as more just and prudent:

> 52 Εἰ δ᾽ ἐλέφαντας γεννῶσα καὶ φαλαίνας  
> (And if in bringing forth elephants and whales)
>
> 53 Οὐ μεταμέλετ’, ὀρθῶς φρονοῦσι πᾶσι  
> (She does not repent, to all who think rightly)
>
> 54 Τοῦτο δίκαιον δοκεῖ καὶ πρέπον ἅμα.  
> (This seems both just and fitting at once.)

- *Εἰ δ᾽ … οὐ μεταμέλετ᾽* keeps the condition *e s’ella … non si pente*: *δέ*, elided before *ἐλέφαντας*, connects the clause to what precedes; *οὐ* negatives an indicative, not a hypothetical subjunctive. The elided *μεταμέλετ᾽* is read in context as present middle *μεταμέλεται*, third singular, used personally, ‘she does not repent’, rather than the familiar impersonal *μεταμέλει* with a dative of the person.
- *ἐλέφαντας γεννῶσα καὶ φαλαίνας* supplies the act of bringing forth: *γεννῶσα* is the contracted feminine nominative singular present active participle of *γεννάω*, agreeing with the unexpressed subject corresponding to *ella*. The animals, genitival complements with *di* in Italian, become accusative plural objects of the participle.
- *ὀρθῶς φρονοῦσι πᾶσι* ‘to all who judge rightly’ replaces the singular subject *chi guarda sottilmente*. *φρονοῦσι* is here a present active participle of *φρονέω*, dative masculine plural with *πᾶσι*, not a third-person plural finite verb. The dative marks the judges in *τοῦτο … δοκεῖ* ‘this seems to them’. Instead of their deeming her *più giusta e più discreta*, this seems *δίκαιον … καὶ πρέπον ἅμα*, ‘just and fitting at once’: two positive predicates, the second a neuter singular participle of *πρέπω*.

> 55 chè dove l'argomento della mente  
> 56 s'aggiunge al mal volere ed alla possa,  
> 57 nessun riparo vi può far la gente.  

for where [the instrument] of [the] mind is joined to evil will and potency, men can make no defence against it.

> 55 Ὅταν γὰρ νοὸς δύναμις συνυπάρξῃ  
> (For when power of mind coexists)
>
> 56 Τῇ κακεντρεχεῖ τοῦ σώματος ἰσχύϊ,  
> (With the malicious strength of the body,)
>
> 57 Ἀμυντήριον οὐδὲν ἀνθρώποις ἔσται.  
> (No defence will there be for men.)

- Postpositive *γάρ* gives the reason (*chè*); *ὅταν* (*ὅτε* with *ἄν*) makes *dove* temporal and general, ‘whenever’. *συνυπάρξῃ* is third singular aorist active subjunctive of *συνυπάρχω*, ‘comes to coexist’, in place of the Italian present *s’aggiunge*. Its subject *νοὸς δύναμις* is ‘power of mind’, not an instrument; *νοός* is the uncontracted genitive singular of *νόος* (Attic contracted *νοῦ*).
- *τῇ κακεντρεχεῖ τοῦ σώματος ἰσχύϊ* combines *al mal volere ed alla possa* into one dative complement of *συνυπάρξῃ*: ‘with the malicious strength of the body’. *κακεντρεχεῖ* is dative feminine singular of *κακεντρεχής*, qualifying *ἰσχύϊ*, not a separate noun for will; *τοῦ σώματος* expressly makes the strength bodily. The diaeresis in *ἰσχύϊ*, dative of *ἰσχύς*, keeps the two vowels distinct.
- *ἀμυντήριον οὐδέν* ‘no means of defence’ is the subject of *ἔσται*, third singular future of *εἰμί*. Italian *la gente* becomes the dative plural *ἀνθρώποις*, ‘there will be none for human beings’; the active *può far* and the reference back in *vi* have no separate equivalents.

> 58 La faccia sua mi parea lunga e grossa  
> 59 come la pina di San Pietro a Roma;  

His face seemed to me as long and large as the pine of St Peter’s at Rome,

> 58 Ἡ δ᾽ ὄψις αὐτοῦ μακρά μοι καὶ παχεῖα,  
> (And his face, to me long and thick,)
>
> 59 Ὡς ὁ Ῥώμῃ τοῦ Πέτρου κῶνος, ἐδόκει·  
> (Seemed like the pine-cone of Peter at Rome;)

- *ἡ δ᾽ ὄψις αὐτοῦ* replaces the possessive adjective of *la faccia sua* with a genitive pronoun. *μακρά … καὶ παχεῖα* are feminine nominative singular predicates agreeing with *ὄψις*; *μοι* is the dative of the perceiver. Musurus postpones *ἐδόκει*, third singular imperfect active of *δοκέω*, until after the comparison, whereas *mi parea* precedes the adjectives.
- *ὡς ὁ Ῥώμῃ τοῦ Πέτρου κῶνος* identifies *la pina* by its shape, ‘like the cone’. The local dative *Ῥώμῃ* and possessive genitive *τοῦ Πέτρου* both stand between article and noun; there is no separate equivalent for *San*.

> 60 ed a sua proporzione eran l'altr'ossa;  

and his other bones were in proportion to it;

> 60 Ἴσης δ᾽ ἀναλογίας ἦν ὀστᾶ τἆλλα·  
> (And in equal proportion were his other bones;)

- *ἴσης δ᾽ ἀναλογίας* ‘of equal proportion’ is a predicate genitive, substituting equality for the explicit reference back in *a sua proporzione*. *ἦν*, third singular imperfect of *εἰμί*, takes the neuter plural subject *ὀστᾶ τἆλλα*, in the usual Greek singular construction with a neuter plural. *ὀστᾶ* is contracted from *ὀστέα*; *τἆλλα* is crasis of *τὰ ἄλλα*, ‘the rest’.

> 61 sì che la ripa, ch'era perizoma  
> 62 dal mezzo in giù, ne mostrava ben tanto  
> 63 di sopra, chè di giungere alla chioma  
> 64 tre Frison s'averian dato mal vanto:  

so that the bank, which was an apron from his middle downwards, shewed us certainly so much of him above, that three Friezelanders had vainly boasted to have reached his hair:

> 61 Ὥστε τὸ χεῖλος, ὅπερ περιεζώννυ  
> (So that the rim, which girdled him)
>
> 62 Ἐκ μέσων κάτω, διάστασιν πρὸς τἄνω  
> (From his middle downward, a span upward)
>
> 63 Ἐδείκνυ τόσην, ὥστε μέχρι τῆς κόμης  
> (Showed so great, that up to his hair)
>
> 64 Τρεῖς ἂν ἐπαρκέσειαν Φρίσσονες μόλις·  
> (Three Friezelanders would scarcely suffice;)

- The first *ὥστε* introduces an actual result with the indicative *ἐδείκνυ*, third singular imperfect active of *δείκνυμι*. Its subject *τὸ χεῖλος* ‘the lip, rim’ renders *la ripa*. In the intervening relative, *ὅπερ* is emphatic neuter nominative singular, ‘which very rim’; *περιεζώννυ*, third singular imperfect active of *περιζώννυμι*, turns *era perizoma* from a nominal predicate into an action, ‘girdled [him]’. *ἐκ μέσων κάτω* gives its extent, ‘from the middle down’.
- *διάστασιν πρὸς τἄνω … τόσην* ‘so great an extent upwards’ supplies a noun for *ben tanto di sopra*: *διάστασιν* is the object of *ἐδείκνυ*, with *τόσην* in agreement across the verb. *τἄνω* is crasis of *τὰ ἄνω*. What is shown is a distance, with no expressed *di lui* or recipient.
- The second *ὥστε* correlates with *τόσην*. *τρεῖς ἂν ἐπαρκέσειαν Φρίσσονες μόλις* means ‘three Frisians would scarcely suffice’: *ἐπαρκέσειαν* is third plural aorist active optative of *ἐπαρκέω*, with the expanded ending *-ειαν*; *ἄν* makes it potential. *μέχρι τῆς κόμης* ‘as far as the hair’ gives the endpoint without a verb for *giungere*. *μόλις* qualifies their sufficiency; it is not a translation of a surviving verb of boasting. The conditional perfect *s’averian dato mal vanto* is thus substantially recast.

> 65 però ch'io ne vedea trenta gran palmi  
> 66 dal loco in giù, dov'uom s'affibbia il manto.  

for downwards from the place where a man buckles on his mantle, I saw thirty large spans of him.

> 65 Τριάκοντα γὰρ σπιθαμὰς κατεμέτρουν  
> (For thirty spans I measured)
>
> 66 Ἐκ περόνης τῆς χλαίνης ἄχρι τοῦ χείλους.  
> (From the clasp of his cloak down to the rim.)

- *τριάκοντα γὰρ σπιθαμὰς κατεμέτρουν* ‘for I measured thirty spans’ retains the explanation and the imperfect, but turns seeing into measuring. *κατεμέτρουν* is first singular imperfect active of *καταμετρέω*, with augment after the preverb and contraction of the ending. *σπιθαμάς* is its accusative plural object; neither *gran* nor the partitive *ne* is separately expressed.
- *ἐκ περόνης τῆς χλαίνης ἄχρι τοῦ χείλους* defines both limits by prepositions with the genitive, ‘from the clasp of the cloak as far as the rim’. The clasp replaces *dal loco … dov’uom s’affibbia il manto*, removing the indefinite human subject and reflexive verb; the named lower limit takes the place of *in giù*.

> 67 “Rafel mai amech zabi almi,”  
> 68 cominciò a gridar la fiera bocca,  
> 69 cui non si convenian più dolci salmi.  

“Rafel mai amech zabi almi,” began to shout the savage mouth, for which no sweeter psalmody [was] fit.

> 67 “Ῥαφὲλ μάϊ ἀαμὲχ ζαβὶ ἀάλμι,”  
> (“Rafel mai aamech zabi aalmi,”)
>
> 68 Ἤρξατο κράζων τὸ θηριῶδες στόμα,  
> (The savage mouth began to cry out,)
>
> 69 Ἡδίους ψαλμοὺς οὐ δυνάμενον ᾄδειν.  
> (Being unable to sing sweeter psalms.)

- *Ῥαφὲλ μάϊ ἀαμὲχ ζαβὶ ἀάλμι* transcribes rather than translates. Initial *Ῥ* has the conventional rough breathing; the diaeresis of *μάϊ* separates its vowels, and the doubled initial alphas in *ἀαμέχ* and *ἀάλμι* go beyond the Italian spelling.
- *ἤρξατο*, third singular aorist middle indicative of *ἄρχω*, renders *cominciò*. For *a gridar* Musurus uses *κράζων*, present active participle of *κράζω*, rather than an infinitive. Its masculine nominative singular treats the crier as a person: it does not formally agree with the delayed grammatical subject *τὸ θηριῶδες στόμα*, which is neuter.
- *ἡδίους ψαλμοὺς οὐ δυνάμενον ᾄδειν* does agree with *στόμα*: *δυνάμενον* is neuter nominative singular present middle participle of *δύναμαι*, governing the present active infinitive *ᾄδειν*. The contracted comparative *ἡδίους* is accusative masculine plural with *ψαλμούς*. The Italian relative *cui non si convenian* expresses suitability, whereas the participle expresses inability, ‘unable to sing sweeter psalms’.

> 70 E il duca mio ver lui: “Anima sciocca,  
> 71 tienti col corno, e con quel ti disfoga  
> 72 quand'ira o altra passion ti tocca.  

And towards him my Guide: “Stupid soul! keep to thy horn; and vent thyself with that, when rage or other passion touches thee.

> 70 Οὑμὸς δ᾽ Ἡγήτωρ αὐτῷ· “Ψυχὴ παράφρων,  
> (And my Guide to him: “Foolish soul,)
>
> 71 „ Ἔχου κέρατος, καὶ δι᾽ αὐτοῦ βουκόλει  
> („ Keep to your horn, and with it soothe)
>
> 72 „ Σαυτόν, ἢν ὀργὴ κινῇ σ᾽ ἢ πάθος ἄλλο·  
> („ Yourself, if wrath or other passion moves you;)

- *οὑμὸς δ᾽ Ἡγήτωρ αὐτῷ* keeps the omission of a verb of speaking in *E il duca mio ver lui*. *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*; the dative *αὐτῷ* marks the addressee rather than direction. *ψυχὴ παράφρων* is a vocative address, ‘distraught soul’, with a stronger suggestion of derangement than *sciocca*.
- *ἔχου κέρατος* ‘hold to the horn’ uses the second singular present middle imperative of *ἔχω* with the genitive of the thing held. *δι᾽ αὐτοῦ* ‘by means of it’ renders *con quel*. *βουκόλει σαυτόν* turns *ti disfoga* into ‘soothe yourself’: *βουκόλει* is second singular present active imperative of *βουκολέω*, with the contracted reflexive accusative *σαυτόν* for *σεαυτόν*.
- *ἢν … κινῇ σ᾽* ‘if … stirs you’ makes *quand’ … ti tocca* conditional. *ἤν* is a form of *ἐάν*, governing *κινῇ*, third singular present active subjunctive of *κινέω*; *σ᾽* is elided *σε*. The alternative subjects are joined by a different particle, disjunctive *ἤ*: *ὀργὴ … ἢ πάθος ἄλλο*.

> 73 Cercati al collo, e troverai la soga  
> 74 che il tien legato, o anima confusa,  
> 75 e vedi lui che il gran petto ti doga.”  

Search on thy neck, and thou wilt find the belt that holds it tied, O soul confused, and see [the horn] itself that girdles thy huge breast.”

> 73 „ Ζήτει περὶ τὸν λαιμόν, καὶ τὸν ἱμάντα,  
> („ Search about your neck, and the strap,)
>
> 74 „ Ψυχὴ παραπλήξ, ᾧ δετόν ἐσθ᾽, εὑρήσεις,  
> („ Crazed soul, by which it is bound, you will find,)
>
> 75 „ Ὃν βλέπε κυκλοῦντά σου τὸ μέγα στῆθος.”  
> („ Which see encircling your great chest.”)

- *ζήτει περὶ τὸν λαιμόν* ‘search about the neck’ has the second singular present active imperative of *ζητέω* and *περί* with the accusative. The article suffices for the addressee’s own neck; there is no reflexive corresponding to *-ti*. *τὸν ἱμάντα*, accusative singular of *ἱμάς* ‘strap’, is put well before *εὑρήσεις*, second singular future active indicative of *εὑρίσκω*, rather than after *troverai*.
- Between object and verb come the address *ψυχὴ παραπλήξ* ‘demented soul’ and *ᾧ δετόν ἐσθ᾽* ‘with which it is bound’. *ᾧ* is instrumental dative singular referring to the strap; neuter *δετόν* is a verbal adjective predicated of the understood horn. Thus *che il tien legato* becomes a state, not an action of the strap. *ἐσθ᾽* is *ἐστί* elided before the rough breathing of *εὑρήσεις*, with aspiration of the final consonant.
- *ὃν βλέπε κυκλοῦντα* ‘which see encircling’ settles the reference differently from the supplied “[the horn] itself”: masculine accusative *ὅν* resumes *ἱμάντα*, not neuter *κέρας*. The present active participle *κυκλοῦντα* agrees with this object of *βλέπε* (second singular present imperative of *βλέπω*); it replaces the finite relative *che … ti doga*. Possessive *σου* goes with its object *τὸ μέγα στῆθος*.

> 76 Poi disse a me: “Egli stesso s'accusa;  

Then he said to me: “He accuses himself;

> 76 Ἔπειτ᾽ εἶπε πρὸς ἐμέ. “Δῆλός ἐσθ᾽ οὗτος·  
> (Then he said to me: “He stands revealed;)

- *ἔπειτ᾽ εἶπε πρὸς ἐμέ* closely follows *Poi disse a me*: *ἔπειτα* is elided, *εἶπε* is third singular second aorist active indicative in the paradigm of *λέγω*, and *πρός* takes the full accusative pronoun. *δῆλός ἐσθ᾽ οὗτος* gives the result rather than the act of *Egli stesso s’accusa*: ‘this one is manifest’. *δῆλος* is the predicate, *οὗτος* the demonstrative subject; *ἐστί* again undergoes elision and aspiration before a rough breathing.

> 77 questi è Nembrotto, per lo cui mal coto  
> 78 pure un linguaggio nel mondo non s'usa.  

this is Nimrod, through whose ill [thought] one language is not still used in the world.

> 77 „ Νεμβρώδης ἐστί, δι᾽ οὗ τὴν κακὴν τόλμην  
> („ He is Nimrod, through whose wicked daring)
>
> 78 „ Οὐ μία μόνη γλῶσσ᾽ ἐν κόσμῳ λαλεῖται.  
> („ Not one language alone is spoken in the world.)

- *Νεμβρώδης ἐστί* ‘he is Nimrod’ has a predicate nominative, with the demonstrative corresponding to *questi* already used in the preceding clause. In *δι᾽ οὗ τὴν κακὴν τόλμην*, *διά* governs the accusative *τὴν κακὴν τόλμην* to express cause; *οὗ* is the possessive genitive relative, ‘through whose evil daring’, not the object of *διά*. The interposed relative closely follows *per lo cui mal coto*, but ‘daring’ interprets rather than reproduces ‘thought’.
- *οὐ μία μόνη γλῶσσ᾽ … λαλεῖται* takes *pure un linguaggio … non* as ‘not one language alone’; *μία* and *μόνη* both agree with nominative *γλῶσσα*, elided before *ἐν*. *λαλεῖται*, third singular present passive of *λαλέω*, specifies speaking in place of the broader *s’usa*. *ἐν κόσμῳ* has the local dative without an article.

> 79 Lasciamlo stare, e non parliamo a voto:  

Let us leave him standing, and not speak in vain:

> 79 „ Ἐῶμεν αὐτόν, μηδ᾽ ὁμιλῶμεν μάτην·  
> („ Let us leave him, and not speak in vain;)

- *ἐῶμεν αὐτόν, μηδ᾽ ὁμιλῶμεν μάτην* matches the two Italian first-person plural exhortations with present active hortatory subjunctives, from *ἐάω* and *ὁμιλέω*. *ἐάω* with the accusative by itself means ‘leave him alone’, without an infinitive for *stare*. Elided *μηδέ* ‘and let us not’ carries the negative appropriate to the exhortation; adverbial *μάτην* renders *a voto*.

> 80 chè così è a lui ciascun linguaggio,  
> 81 come il suo ad altrui ch'a nullo è noto.”  

for every language is to him, as to others his which no one understands.”

> 80 „ Πᾶσα γὰρ γλῶσσα πρὸς αὐτόν ἐστιν οὕτως,  
> („ For every language is to him just so,)
>
> 81 „ Ὡς ἡ τοῦδε πρὸς ἄλλους, ἄγνωστος πᾶσιν.”  
> („ As his is to others, unknown to all.”)

- *πᾶσα γὰρ γλῶσσα* ‘for every language’ replaces *chè … ciascun linguaggio*, with distributive *πᾶσα* before an anarthrous singular. *πρὸς αὐτόν ἐστιν οὕτως, ὡς … πρὸς ἄλλους* closely follows the Italian comparison *così è a lui … come … ad altrui*. Greek twice uses *πρός* with the accusative for the person in relation to whom the statement holds, moving *οὕτως* to stand just before its correlative *ὡς*.
- *ἡ τοῦδε* ‘the [language] of this man’ supplies the feminine article with *γλῶσσα* understood; *τοῦδε* is genitive singular of *ὅδε*. *ἄγνωστος πᾶσιν* compresses *ch’a nullo è noto* into an adjective with a dative, ‘unknown to all’. *ἄγνωστος*, though ending in *-ος*, is feminine here, agreeing with the understood language; the negative has shifted from the pronoun to the adjective.

> 82 Facemmo adunque più lungo viaggio  
> 83 volti a sinistra; ed al trar d'un balestro  
> 84 trovammo l'altro assai più fiero e maggio.  

We therefore journeyed on, turning to the left; and, a crossbow-shot off, we found [the next] far more fierce and large.

> 82 Εἶτ᾽ ἔτι πρόσω προέβημεν στραφέντες  
> (Then still further onward we advanced, turning)
>
> 83 Ἐπ᾽ ἀριστερά, καὶ μέχρι βολῆς τόξου  
> (To the left, and at a bowshot's distance)
>
> 84 Εὕρομεν ἄλλον φρικτότερον καὶ μείζω.  
> (We found another, more dreadful and larger.)

- *εἶτ᾽ ἔτι πρόσω προέβημεν* ‘then we advanced still farther’ makes *adunque* sequential and turns *facemmo … più lungo viaggio* into a verb of motion with adverbs. *εἶτ᾽* is elided *εἶτα*; *προέβημεν* is first plural root aorist active indicative of *προβαίνω*, augmented after *προ-*.
- *στραφέντες ἐπ᾽ ἀριστερά* renders *volti a sinistra*: *στραφέντες* is nominative masculine plural second aorist passive participle of *στρέφω*, passive in form but intransitive here, ‘having turned’. *ἐπί*, elided before *ἀριστερά*, takes a directional accusative, the neuter plural used for the left side.
- *μέχρι βολῆς τόξου* gives a limit of distance, ‘as far as a bowshot’: *μέχρι* governs *βολῆς*, on which *τόξου* depends. The ordinary bow replaces the specific *balestro*. *εὕρομεν* is first plural second aorist active indicative of *εὑρίσκω*, with *ευ-* rather than the Attic augmented *ηυ-*. *ἄλλον* ‘another’ lacks the definiteness of *l’altro*. Its accusative masculine singular comparatives are *φρικτότερον* ‘more dreadful’ and *μείζω*, the contracted alternative to *μείζονα* from *μείζων*; neither has a separate intensifier for *assai*.

## Lines 85–117 — Ephialtes in Chains and the Approach to Antæus

> 85 A cinger lui, qual che fosse il maestro  
> 86 non so io dir, ma ei tenea succinto  
> 87 dinanzi l' altro, e dietro il braccio destro  

Who and what the master could be that girt him thus, I cannot tell; but he had his right arm pinioned down behind, and the other before,

> 85 Τίς ὁ Κύριος, ὁ τόνδ᾽ ὧδε δεσμεύσας,  
> (Who the Master was who bound this one thus,)
>
> 86 Εἰπεῖν οὐκ ἔχω· πλὴν προσδέτους τὰς χεῖρας  
> (I cannot say; only his arms bound fast)
>
> 87 Εἶχε, τὴν μὲν ἔμπροσθεν, τὴν δ᾽ αὖ ὀπίσω,  
> (He had, the one in front, and the other behind,)

- Musurus makes the Italian opening, *A cinger lui, qual che fosse il maestro*, a question of identity dependent on ‘I cannot say’: *Τίς ὁ Κύριος* ‘who the master [was]’. The interrogative *τίς* is the predicate, the articular *ὁ Κύριος* the subject. There is no expressed copula and no Greek mood corresponding to the subjunctive *fosse*. *Κύριος* suggests ‘master’ in the sense of ‘lord’, not specifically a master craftsman.
- The repeated article in *ὁ τόνδ᾽ ὧδε δεσμεύσας* attaches a participial description to *ὁ Κύριος*: ‘the one who bound this man thus’. *δεσμεύσας* is the nominative masculine singular aorist active participle of *δεσμεύω*; *τόνδ᾽* is elided *τόνδε*, accusative of *ὅδε*. Thus the Italian infinitive *cinger* becomes a completed act predicated of the master, with *ὧδε* supplying ‘in this manner’.
- *Εἰπεῖν οὐκ ἔχω* ‘I cannot say’ fronts the second aorist active infinitive of *λέγω*. *ἔχω* with an infinitive expresses ability; there is no separate equivalent of emphatic *io* in *non so io dir*. Adversative *πλήν* then introduces a new clause, with its verb *εἶχε* (third singular imperfect active of *ἔχω*) postponed to the next line.
- *προσδέτους τὰς χεῖρας εἶχε* ‘he had his hands bound fast’ puts the verbal adjective *προσδέτους* in predicate position, agreeing with the feminine accusative plural *χεῖρας* of *χείρ*. Greek uses the plural, not the dual, then distinguishes the two by the singulars *τὴν μὲν … τὴν δ᾽ αὖ* ‘the one … the other in turn’. The order *ἔμπροσθεν … ὀπίσω* follows *dinanzi … dietro*, but the Greek drops the specification *destro* and uses one noun for both limbs.

> 88 d' una catena, che il teneva avvinto  
> 89 dal collo in giù, sì che in su lo scoperto  
> 90 si ravvolgeva infino al giro quinto.  

with a chain which held him clasped from the neck downwards, and on the uncovered part went round to the fifth turn.

> 88 Ἁλύσει, δι᾽ ἧς ὢν ἐκ λαιμοῦ καὶ κάτω  
> (With a chain, by which, being from the neck downward)
>
> 89 Πεπεδημένος, τἀσκεπὲς εἶχε μέρος  
> (Fettered, he had the uncovered part)
>
> 90 Τοῦ σώματος πεντάκις ἀμφειλιγμένον.  
> (Of his body five times wound round.)

- *Ἁλύσει*, dative singular of *ἅλυσις*, gives the instrument of the binding without a preposition. In *δι᾽ ἧς* ‘by means of which’, elided *διά* takes the feminine genitive singular relative pronoun. Italian makes the chain the subject of *il teneva avvinto*; Greek makes the man the subject of *ὢν … πεπεδημένος* ‘being in a fettered state’. The present participle of *εἰμί* and the perfect middle-passive participle of *πεδάω* are both nominative masculine singular.
- *ἐκ λαιμοῦ καὶ κάτω* gives the starting point with *ἐκ* and the genitive, then the direction with an adverb, ‘from the neck and down’. Instead of introducing a result with *sì che* and following the winding of the chain, Musurus continues the relative clause with the finite *εἶχε*: ‘he had the uncovered part of his body wound round’.
- *τἀσκεπὲς* is crasis for *τὸ ἀσκεπές*, the neuter accusative singular of *ἀσκεπής* agreeing with *μέρος*. The partitive genitive *τοῦ σώματος* makes explicit what *lo scoperto* leaves implicit. *ἀμφειλιγμένον*, a perfect middle-passive participle, agrees with *μέρος* as its predicate; the perfect presents the completed wrapping. Adverbial *πεντάκις* ‘five times’ replaces the ordinal and endpoint of *infino al giro quinto*.

> 91 "Questo superbo voll' esser esperto  
> 92 di sua potenza contra il sommo Giove,"  
> 93 disse il mio duca, "ond' egli ha cotal merto.  

“This proud spirit willed to try his power against high Jove,” saith my Guide; “whence he has such reward.

> 91 “Ἤθελ᾽ ὁ παράτολμος ὅδ᾽ ἀντιτάξαι  
> (“This reckless one wished to pit)
>
> 92 „ Τὴν ἰσχὺν αὐτοῦ κατὰ Διὸς μεγίστου,”  
> („ His strength against most mighty Jove,”)
>
> 93 Εἶπεν οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ, “ὅθεν ἡ τίσις.  
> (Said my Guide, “whence comes the vengeance.)

- *Ἤθελ᾽* is elided *ἤθελε*, third singular imperfect active of *ἐθέλω*: Dante’s preterite *voll’* becomes an expression of continuing wish. *ὁ παράτολμος ὅδ᾽* ‘this overbold one’ has a substantivized adjective followed by the demonstrative *ὅδε*, elided before the infinitive. It interprets *superbo* as excessive daring rather than simply pride.
- *ἀντιτάξαι τὴν ἰσχὺν αὐτοῦ* ‘to set his strength in opposition’ replaces *esser esperto di sua potenza*: the aorist active infinitive of *ἀντιτάσσω* takes ‘strength’ as its direct object rather than making it the complement of ‘experienced’. *κατὰ Διὸς μεγίστου* renders *contra il sommo Giove* with adversative *κατά* and the genitive; *Διός* is the oblique stem form of *Ζεύς*, and *μεγίστου* the superlative of *μέγας*.
- *Εἶπεν* is the third singular second aorist active indicative of *λέγω*, matching *disse*. *οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ* ‘my guide’ has crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*. In *ὅθεν ἡ τίσις* ‘whence the retribution’, the relative adverb retains the inference of *ond’*, but there is no verb of having, personal subject, or counterpart to *cotal*: the noun *τίσις* alone interprets *merto* as punishment.

> 94 Fialte ha nome; e fece le gran prove,  
> 95 quando i giganti fer paura ai Dei;  
> 96 le braccia ch' ei menò, giammai non move."  

Ephialtes is his name; and he made the great endeavours, when the giants made the Gods afraid; the arms he agitated then, he never moves.”

> 94 „ Ἐφιάλτης τοὔνομα· μεγάλα δ᾽ ἔργα  
> („ Ephialtes is his name; and great deeds)
>
> 95 „ Ἔπραξ᾽ ἐν τῇ πρὸς θεοὺς Γιγάντων μάχῃ·  
> („ He wrought in the battle of the Giants against the gods;)
>
> 96 „ Δετὰς δ᾽ ἔχει τὰς χεῖρας, ἅς ποτ᾽ ἐκίνει.”  
> („ And bound are the hands which once he moved.”)

- *Ἐφιάλτης τοὔνομα* retains the name first, but dispenses with *ha*. *τοὔνομα* is crasis of *τὸ ὄνομα*: the compressed naming formula can be understood as ‘Ephialtes [is] the name’. *μεγάλα δ᾽ ἔργα ἔπραξ᾽* ‘and great deeds he did’ separates object and verb across the line ending; *ἔπραξ᾽* is elided *ἔπραξε*, third singular aorist active of *πράσσω*. *ἔργα* makes *prove* deeds accomplished rather than trials of strength.
- *ἐν τῇ πρὸς θεοὺς Γιγάντων μάχῃ* ‘in the Giants’ battle against gods’ condenses the temporal clause *quando i giganti fer paura ai Dei* into a prepositional phrase. The genitive plural *Γιγάντων* identifies those fighting, while *πρός* with accusative *θεούς* marks their opponents. Both modifiers stand between the article and dative singular *μάχῃ*; the effect on the gods expressed by *fer paura* disappears.
- *Δετὰς δ᾽ ἔχει τὰς χεῖρας* ‘but he has his hands bound’ again uses a predicate adjective with *ἔχει*. This positive statement of their present condition replaces *giammai non move*, losing the explicit ‘never’. The relative clause now comes last: *ἅς* is feminine accusative plural, object of *ἐκίνει* (third singular imperfect active of *κινέω*). *ποτ᾽*, elided *ποτε* ‘once’, and the imperfect contrast former activity with the present state in place of Dante’s preterite *menò*.

> 97 Ed io a lui: "S' esser puote, io vorrei  
> 98 che dello ismisurato Briareo  
> 99 esperienza avesser gli occhi miei."  

And I to him: “If it were possible, I should wish my eyes might have experience of the immense Briareus.”

> 97 Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν· “ Εἰ δυνατόν, ἐπόθουν  
> (And I to him: “If it were possible, I should wish)
>
> 98 „ Ἰδέαν Βριάρεω τοῦ πελωρίου  
> („ A sight of monstrous Briareus)
>
> 99 „ Ἔχειν ἀκριβῆ τινα τὰς ἐμὰς ὄψεις.”  
> („ That my eyes might have, an exact one.”)

- *Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν* ‘and I to him’ reproduces the Italian ellipsis of a verb of speaking; *κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*. *Εἰ δυνατόν* ‘if possible’ likewise leaves the copula unexpressed, with the impersonal neuter singular *δυνατόν* for *s’ esser puote*.
- *ἐπόθουν*, first singular imperfect active of *ποθέω*, literally ‘I was longing’, conveys the softened wish of *vorrei* without an optative or a potential construction with *ἄν*. The content of the wish is accusative and infinitive: *τὰς ἐμὰς ὄψεις* ‘my eyes’ is the subject of present active *ἔχειν*, not its object. This replaces Italian *che* with the imperfect subjunctive *avesser*.
- The object *Ἰδέαν … ἀκριβῆ τινα* is spread across two lines: ‘a certain distinct view’. *τινα* is the enclitic indefinite, feminine accusative singular like *ἰδέαν* and *ἀκριβῆ* (from *ἀκριβής*). Musurus thus makes *esperienza* specifically visual and adds the notion of distinctness. *Βριάρεω* is the genitive of *Βριάρεως*, with the Attic-declension ending *-ω*; *τοῦ πελωρίου* ‘the monstrous one’ agrees with it. Dante’s epithet *ismisurato* precedes the name; Greek postpones it, while keeping ‘my eyes’ emphatically last.

> 100 Ond' ei rispose: "Tu vedrai Anteo  
> 101 presso di qui, che parla ed è disciolto,  
> 102 che ne porrà nel fondo d' ogni reo.  

Whereat he answered: “Thou shalt see Antæus near at hand, who speaks, and is unfettered, who will put us into the bottom of all guilt.

> 100 Ὁ δ᾽ ὑπέλαβεν· “Ὄψει δὴ τὸν Ἀνταῖον  
> (And he replied: “You shall indeed see Antæus)
>
> 101 „ Ἐγγὺς ὧδ᾽ ὁμιλοῦντα καὶ λυτὸν ὄντα,  
> („ Near by here, who speaks and is unbound,)
>
> 102 „ Ὃς εἰς ἀβύσσου τὸν βυθὸν ἡμᾶς ἄξει·  
> („ Who will bring us into the depth of the abyss;)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ ὑπέλαβεν* ‘and he took up [the reply]’ uses pronominal *ὁ* with postpositive *δέ* for the change of speaker, without a separate relative adverb for *Ond’*. *ὑπέλαβεν* is the third singular second aorist active of *ὑπολαμβάνω*, with the augment after the prefix. *Ὄψει* is second singular future middle of *ὁράω*, active in sense, with accusative *τὸν Ἀνταῖον*; *δή* gives the prediction an assurance not separately expressed in *tu vedrai*.
- *Ἐγγὺς ὧδ᾽* ‘near, here’ renders *presso di qui* by two adverbs, the second elided *ὧδε*. For *che parla ed è disciolto*, Greek has participles agreeing with the object of perception: *ὁμιλοῦντα* (present active of *ὁμιλέω*, ‘conversing’) and *λυτὸν ὄντα* (‘being unbound’, verbal adjective with the present participle of *εἰμί*), all masculine accusative singular.
- The second Italian *che* does become a finite relative: *Ὃς … ἡμᾶς ἄξει* ‘who will lead us’, with nominative subject, accusative object, and third singular future active of *ἄγω*. *εἰς ἀβύσσου τὸν βυθόν* puts the genitive of *ἄβυσσος* before the articular accusative governed by *εἰς*. ‘Into the depth of the abyss’ gives *nel fondo d’ ogni reo* an entirely spatial rendering, with nothing for *ogni* or for guilt.

> 103 Quel che tu vuoi veder più là è molto,  
> 104 ed è legato e fatto come questo,  
> 105 salvo che più feroce par nel volto."  

He whom thou desirest to see is far beyond; and is tied and shaped like this one, save that he seems in aspect more ferocious.”

> 103 „ Ὃν δ᾽ ἰδεῖν ποθεῖς, ἔστ᾽ ἐκεῖ ποῤῥωτέρω·  
> („ And he whom you long to see is yonder further off;)
>
> 104 „ Δέσμιος δ᾽ ἐστὶ καὶ παρόμοιος τούτῳ,  
> („ And bound he is, and like unto this one,)
>
> 105 „ Πλὴν θηριωδέστερος ὡς πρὸς τὴν ὄψιν.”  
> („ Save that he is more savage in his look.”)

- *Ὃν δ᾽ ἰδεῖν ποθεῖς* ‘but whom you long to see’ omits an expressed antecedent for *Quel*. *ὅν* is accusative as the object of *ἰδεῖν*, the second aorist active infinitive of *ὁράω*, although the person understood is subject of the main verb. *ποθεῖς* (second singular present active of *ποθέω*) echoes *ἐπόθουν* rather than using a neutral verb of willing for *vuoi*.
- *ἔστ᾽ ἐκεῖ ποῤῥωτέρω* ‘is there farther off’ has elided *ἔστι* and a comparative adverb from *πόρρω*. The printed *ῤῥ* marks the traditional breathings on double rho. The comparative conveys *più là*, but there is no distinct intensifier for *molto*.
- *Δέσμιος … καὶ παρόμοιος τούτῳ* substitutes two predicate adjectives with *ἐστί* for *è legato e fatto come questo*. *τούτῳ*, dative masculine singular of *οὗτος*, supplies the standard of likeness after *παρόμοιος*; *fatto* has no separate equivalent.
- *Πλὴν θηριωδέστερος* ‘except [that he is] more beastlike’ uses the masculine nominative comparative of *θηριώδης*. Italian *par* qualifies the statement as one of seeming; Greek omits that verb and limits the comparison instead by *ὡς πρὸς τὴν ὄψιν* ‘as regards appearance’, broader than *nel volto*.

> 106 Non fu tremuoto già tanto rubesto,  
> 107 che scotesse una torre così forte,  
> 108 come Fialte a scotersi fu presto.  

No mighty earthquake ever shook a tower so violently, as Ephialtes forthwith shook himself.

> 106 Οὔποτε σεισμὸς ὡς μάλιστα φρικώδης  
> (No earthquake ever, however most dreadful,)
>
> 107 Πύργον διεκλόνησεν οὕτω βιαίως,  
> (Shook a tower so violently,)
>
> 108 Ὡς ὅτ᾽ ἐξαίφνης ἐσάλευσ᾽ Ἐφιάλτης.  
> (As when suddenly Ephialtes shook himself.)

- *Οὔποτε σεισμὸς … πύργον διεκλόνησεν* ‘never did an earthquake shake a tower’ replaces *Non fu tremuoto … che scotesse* with one transitive indicative clause. *διεκλόνησεν* is third singular aorist active of *διακλονέω*, augmented within the compound. An actual shaking is the grammatical comparison, rather than the Italian existential construction followed by a subjunctive.
- *ὡς μάλιστα φρικώδης* gives *tanto rubesto* a different emphasis: ‘dreadful in the highest degree’. *φρικώδης* agrees with *σεισμός*; *ὡς μάλιστα* intensifies it, with concessive force in context, ‘however dreadful’. The comparison proper is *οὕτω βιαίως … ὡς* ‘so violently … as’, answering to *così forte … come*.
- *ὡς ὅτ᾽ ἐξαίφνης* ‘as when suddenly’ adds temporal *ὅτε*, elided, and interprets *fu presto* as suddenness. *ἐσάλευσ᾽* is elided *ἐσάλευσε*, third singular aorist active of *σαλεύω*, used without an object for ‘shook’. There is no morphological middle or reflexive pronoun corresponding to *scotersi*. The subject *Ἐφιάλτης* is delayed to the end.

> 109 Allor temett' io più che mai la morte,  
> 110 e non v' era mestier più che la dotta,  
> 111 s' io non avessi viste le ritorte.  

Then more than ever I dreaded death; and nothing else was wanted for it but the fear, had I not seen his bands.

> 109 Τότ᾽ ἔσχον, ὡς οὔποτε, θανάτου τρόμον,  
> (Then I felt, as never before, terror of death,)
>
> 110 Καὶ τοῦτ᾽ ἂν ἐξήρκεσε πρὸς τὸ θανεῖν με,  
> (And this would have sufficed to make me die,)
>
> 111 Εἰ μὴ δέσμιον ἁλύσεσιν ἑώρων.  
> (Had I not seen him bound with chains.)

- *Τότ᾽ ἔσχον … θανάτου τρόμον* ‘then I felt terror of death’ renders *Allor temett’ io … la morte* by a noun with a supporting verb. *τότ᾽* is elided *τότε*; *ἔσχον* is first singular second aorist active of *ἔχω*. *θανάτου* is an objective genitive depending on accusative *τρόμον*. Parenthetic *ὡς οὔποτε* ‘as never [before]’ conveys *più che mai* without a comparative.
- *τοῦτ᾽ ἂν ἐξήρκεσε* ‘this would have sufficed’ reverses the negative wording *non v’ era mestier più che la dotta*. Elided *τοῦτο* points back to the terror; *ἄν* with the third singular aorist active indicative of *ἐξαρκέω* marks the unreal consequence. *πρὸς τὸ θανεῖν με* states explicitly what it would have sufficed for: *πρός* governs the articular second aorist active infinitive of *θνῄσκω*, whose subject is accusative *με*.
- *Εἰ μὴ … ἑώρων* supplies the negative condition with *εἰ μή* and the first singular imperfect active indicative of *ὁράω*, ‘if I did not see’, rather than formally matching the pluperfect subjunctive *non avessi viste*. Instead of taking the bands as the direct object of sight, Greek predicates *δέσμιον* ‘bound’ of an understood masculine accusative ‘him’; *ἁλύσεσιν*, dative plural of *ἅλυσις*, names the instrument.

> 112 Noi procedemmo più avanti allotta,  
> 113 e venimmo ad Anteo, che ben cinqu' alle,  
> 114 senza la testa, uscía fuor della grotta.  

We then proceeded farther on, and reached Antæus, who full five ells, besides the head, forth issued from the cavern.

> 112 Εἶτα προβάντες εἰσέτι περαιτέρω  
> (Then advancing still further onward)
>
> 113 Ἤλθομεν παρ᾽ Ἀνταῖον, ἔχοντα πήχεις  
> (We came to Antæus, who had cubits)
>
> 114 Πέντε, κεφαλῆς ἄνευ, φρέατος ἔξω.  
> (Five, without his head, outside the well.)

- *Εἶτα προβάντες … ἤλθομεν* makes *procedemmo … e venimmo* one finite verb with a prior action expressed by a participle. *προβάντες* is the masculine nominative plural second aorist active participle of *προβαίνω*, agreeing with the subject of *ἤλθομεν*, first plural second aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι*. Initial *εἶτα* does the work of postponed *allotta*; *εἰσέτι περαιτέρω* ‘still farther’ expands *più avanti*.
- *παρ᾽ Ἀνταῖον* uses elided *παρά* with the accusative for motion to a person. Accusative *ἔχοντα*, present active participle of *ἔχω*, agrees with *Ἀνταῖον* and replaces the finite relative *che … uscía*. Greek gives a measurement rather than a verb of projecting: *πήχεις πέντε* ‘five cubits’, with the accusative plural of *πῆχυς* and the indeclinable numeral postponed across the line break. There is no separate word for intensive *ben*.
- Both qualifications have their governing word last: *κεφαλῆς ἄνευ* ‘without the head’ and *φρέατος ἔξω* ‘outside the well’. *ἄνευ* and *ἔξω* take the genitive, the latter here the irregular genitive singular of *φρέαρ*. The static ‘outside’ replaces *uscía fuor*, and ‘well’ interprets rather than reproduces *grotta*.

> 115 "O tu, che nella fortunata valle,  
> 116 che fece Scipion di gloria reda  
> 117 quando Annibal co' suoi diede le spalle,  

“O thou! who in the fateful valley, which made Scipio heir of glory when Hannibal retreated with his hosts,

> 115 “Ὦ σὺ φίλ᾽, ὅς ποτ᾽ ἐν εὐμοίρῳ κοιλάδι,  
> (“O you friend, who once in the fortunate valley,)
>
> 116 „ Ἔνθα Σκιπίων ἤρατο μέγα κλέος,  
> („ Where Scipio won great renown,)
>
> 117 „ Ὁπότ᾽ ἔτρεψεν αὐτῷ τὰ νῶτ᾽ Ἀννίβας,  
> („ When Hannibal turned his back to him,)

- *Ὦ σὺ φίλ᾽* adds a term of address to *O tu*: *σύ* is the emphatic second-person pronoun, *φίλ᾽* the elided masculine vocative singular *φίλε*. *ὅς* is nominative, referring to the addressee and opening a clause still unfinished at line 117. *ποτ᾽* (elided *ποτε*) supplies ‘once’; *ἐν εὐμοίρῳ κοιλάδι* matches the Italian locative phrase with *ἐν* and the dative. *εὐμοίρῳ*, feminine dative singular of the two-termination adjective *εὔμοιρος*, renders *fortunata* as ‘favoured by fortune’.
- *Ἔνθα Σκιπίων ἤρατο μέγα κλέος* ‘where Scipio won great renown’ replaces the relative subject *che* with the relative adverb *ἔνθα*. The valley is no longer the grammatical agent of *fece*: *Σκιπίων* becomes nominative subject, and *ἤρατο*, third singular aorist middle of *αἴρω*, presents the renown as something he gained for himself. Neuter accusative *μέγα κλέος* is the direct object; ‘great’ is supplied and the predicate *reda* ‘heir’ disappears.
- *Ὁπότ᾽* is elided temporal *ὁπότε*, here with an indicative for a particular past occasion, not an optative of repeated past action. *ἔτρεψεν … τὰ νῶτ᾽* ‘turned his back’ keeps the bodily idiom of *diede le spalle* but changes ‘gave’ to the third singular aorist active of *τρέπω*. *τὰ νῶτ᾽* is elided neuter accusative plural *τὰ νῶτα*; *αὐτῷ* adds the person to whom the back was turned. *Ἀννίβας* is the postponed nominative subject, with no equivalent of *co’ suoi*.

## Lines 118–145 — Antæus Stoops and Sets the Poets Down

> 118 recasti già mille leon per preda,  

didst take of old a thousand lions for thy prey;

> 118 „ Ἤνεγκας δὴ χιλίων λεόντων λείαν·  
> („ Carried off indeed a prey of a thousand lions;)

- *Ἤνεγκας* (2nd sg. aor. act. ind. of *φέρω*, from the irregular aorist *ἤνεγκα*) keeps the sense ‘carried off’ of *recasti*. Postpositive *δή* lends emphasis, ‘indeed’; it does not itself mean ‘of old’, and the past reference of *già* is left to the verb.
- *χιλίων λεόντων λείαν* makes *λείαν* ‘booty’ (acc. sg. of *λεία*) the direct object, with a preceding genitive specifying what the booty consisted of. In *mille leon per preda* the lions are the direct object and *per preda* says in what capacity they were taken.

> 119 e che, se fossi stato all' alta guerra  
> 120 de' tuoi fratelli, ancor par ch' e' si creda  
> 121 che avrebber vinto i figli della terra;  

and through whom, hadst thou been at the high war of thy brethren, it seems yet to be believed that the sons of earth had conquered;

> 119 „ Εἰ δ᾽ ἐν τῷ δεινῷ πολέμῳ σὺ παρῆσθα  
> („ And if in the terrible war you had been present)
>
> 120 „ Τῶν σῶν ἀδελφῶν, ὡς πᾶς ἔτι νομίζει,  
> („ Of your brothers, as everyone still thinks,)
>
> 121 „ Βεβαίως ἂν ἐνίκων οἱ τῆς Γῆς παῖδες·  
> („ Surely the sons of Earth would have conquered;)

- *Εἰ δ᾽ … σὺ παρῆσθα* ‘but if you had been present’ starts an independent conditional period. Elided *δέ* links it to the preceding assertion without reproducing the relative connection of *e che*. *παρῆσθα* is the 2nd sg. imperf. ind. of *πάρειμι* ‘be present’, with the older ending *-σθα*; the explicit *σύ* singles out the addressee. Greek uses a past indicative after *εἰ* where *se fossi stato* uses a pluperfect subjunctive.
- *ἐν τῷ δεινῷ πολέμῳ … τῶν σῶν ἀδελφῶν* has *ἐν* with the dative for attendance ‘at’ the war; *δεινῷ* ‘terrible’ interprets rather than literally translates *alta*. The genitive *τῶν σῶν ἀδελφῶν*, with the possessive adjective inside the article group, is separated from *πολέμῳ* by the subject and verb.
- *ὡς πᾶς ἔτι νομίζει* ‘as everyone still thinks’ turns *ancor par ch’ e’ si creda* into a parenthesis. *πᾶς* is substantival, nom. masc. sg., with the singular active *νομίζει*; the appearance of belief becomes a direct attribution of belief.
- *Βεβαίως ἂν ἐνίκων* ‘certainly they would have prevailed’ supplies an adverb of assurance. *ἐνίκων* is here 3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of contracted *νικάω*; with *ἄν* it gives the unreal consequence, though its aspect is not the completed victory of *avrebber vinto*. *οἱ τῆς Γῆς παῖδες* encloses the possessive genitive between article and noun; *Γῆς* names Earth rather than merely denoting ground.

> 122 mettine giù, e non ten venga schifo,  
> 123 dove Cocito la freddura serra.  

set us down—and be not shy to do it—where the cold locks up Cocytus.

> 122 „ Καταβίβασον ἡμᾶς (μὴ δ᾽ ἀρνηθείης)  
> („ Set us down below (and do not refuse))
>
> 123 „ Ἔνθ᾽ ὁ παγετὸς τὸν Κωκυτὸν σκληρύνει·  
> („ Where the frost hardens Cocytus;)

- *Καταβίβασον* (2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of causative *καταβιβάζω*) gathers *metti … giù* into one verb, ‘make descend’, with *ἡμᾶς* as its object. Parenthetic *μὴ δ᾽ ἀρνηθείης* shifts *non ten venga schifo* from aversion to refusal: ‘and may you not refuse’. *ἀρνηθείης* is the 2nd sg. aor. optative of *ἀρνέομαι*, passive in form but active in sense; *μή* with the independent optative softens the injunction into a wish.
- *Ἔνθ᾽* is *ἔνθα* ‘where’, elided before *ὁ*. *ὁ παγετός* ‘the frost’ is the subject of *σκληρύνει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *σκληρύνω*) and *τὸν Κωκυτόν* its object. Thus *serra* ‘locks up’ becomes ‘hardens’, and the cause precedes rather than follows the name of the river.

> 124 Non ci far ire a Tizio, nè a Tifo;  

Do not make us go to Tityos nor Typhon;

> 124 „ Μὴ δ᾽ εἰς Τιτυὸν ἡμᾶς ἄγ᾽ ἢ Τυφῶνα.  
> („ And do not send us to Tityos or Typhon.)

- *Μὴ δ᾽ … ἄγ᾽* gives the prohibition with *μή* and *ἄγε*, 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *ἄγω*, elided before *ἤ*. The single transitive verb ‘lead’ with *ἡμᾶς* replaces causative *far ire*, ‘make go’.
- *εἰς Τιτυὸν … ἢ Τυφῶνα* uses *εἰς* with personal accusatives for the destination, closely following *a Tizio*. The second name shares the preposition across the intervening pronoun and verb. Simple disjunctive *ἤ* ‘or’ falls within the scope of *μή* rather than repeating the negative as *nè* does.

> 125 questi può dar di quel che qui si brama:  
> 126 però ti china, e non torcer lo grifo.  

this man can give of that which here is longed for: therefore bend thee, and curl not thy lip in scorn.

> 125 „ Κλῖνον δή, καὶ μὴ τὰς ὄψεις ἀποστρέψῃς.  
> („ Stoop then, and do not turn your eyes away.)
>
> 126 „ Οὗτος, ὃ ποθεῖς ὧδε, δύναται δοῦναι,  
> („ This man can give what you long for here,)

- Musurus reverses the clauses. *Κλῖνον δή* ‘stoop, then’ puts the appeal first; *κλῖνον* is 2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *κλίνω*, used without an object where *ti china* is reflexive. *δή* urges compliance rather than explicitly marking the inference expressed by *però*.
- *μὴ τὰς ὄψεις ἀποστρέψῃς* ‘do not turn your eyes away’ changes both the gesture and its register: the plural *ὄψεις* (acc. of *ὄψις*) replaces *lo grifo*, and *ἀποστρέψῃς* (2nd sg. aor. act. subj. of *ἀποστρέφω*) with *μή* forbids the act.
- *Οὗτος … δύναται δοῦναι* ‘this man is able to give’ frames the object clause with the demonstrative subject and its predicate. *δύναται* (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *δύναμαι*) takes *δοῦναι*, aor. act. infinitive of *δίδωμι*. *ὃ ποθεῖς ὧδε* ‘what you desire here’ is a relative clause without an expressed antecedent: *ὃ* is neut. acc. sg., object of *ποθεῖς* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ποθέω*). Greek personalizes impersonal *si brama* and dispenses with partitive *di quel*.

> 127 Ancor ti può nel mondo render fama:  
> 128 ch' ei vive e lunga vita ancor aspetta,  
> 129 se innanzi tempo grazia a sè nol chiama."  

He can [yet] restore thy fame on earth: for he lives, and still awaits long life, so Grace before the time call him not unto herself.”

> 127 „ Ἔτι σοι φήμην ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ παρέχων·  
> („ Still granting you renown in the world;)
>
> 128 „ Ζῇ γάρ, καὶ μακρὸν βίον εἰσέτ᾽ ἐλπίζει,  
> („ For he lives, and still hopes for a long life,)
>
> 129 „ Ἢν μὴ καλέσῃ πάρωρον Θεία χάρις.”  
> („ Unless Divine grace call him untimely.”)

- *Ἔτι σοι φήμην ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ παρέχων* ‘still granting you renown in the world’ continues the preceding sentence. *παρέχων* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *παρέχω*) agrees with *οὗτος* and explains what the giving consists in; Musurus does not repeat the finite verb of ability in *Ancor ti può*. *σοι* is the dative recipient and *φήμην* the accusative thing granted; *παρέχων* does not specify that the renown is restored.
- *Ζῇ γάρ* reproduces the causal link of *ch’ ei vive* with postpositive *γάρ* and the contracted 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ζάω*. *μακρὸν βίον εἰσέτ᾽ ἐλπίζει* retains the accusative ‘a long life’ but renders *aspetta* as ‘hopes for’; poetic *εἰσέτι* ‘still’ is elided before the verb.
- *Ἢν μὴ καλέσῃ* ‘unless [she] should call’ uses *ἤν*, a contracted equivalent of *ἐάν*, with the 3rd sg. aor. act. subj. of *καλέω* for a future contingency. *πάρωρον* ‘untimely’ compresses *innanzi tempo* into an adjective used predicatively of the understood person called, with the force of an adverb in English. *Θεία χάρις*, the postponed nominative subject, adds ‘divine’; neither the object pronoun nor the destination *a sè* is separately expressed.

> 130 Così disse il maestro; e quegli in fretta  
> 131 le man distese e prese il duca mio,  
> 132 ond' Ercole sentì già grande stretta.  

Thus spake the Master; and he in haste stretched forth the hands, whence Hercules of old did feel great stress, and took my Guide.

> 130 Ταῦτ᾽ εἶπεν ὁ Προστάτης· αὐτὸς δὲ σπεύσας  
> (Thus spoke the Protector; and he, hastening,)
>
> 131 Ἔλαβ᾽ Ἡγήτορά μου, χεῖρας ἐκτείνας,  
> (Took my Guide, stretching out his hands,)
>
> 132 Ὧν Ἡρακλῆς ᾔσθετο τὸ μέγα σθένος.  
> (Of which Hercules felt the great might.)

- *Ταῦτ᾽ εἶπεν ὁ Προστάτης* ‘these things said the Protector’ replaces adverbial *Così* with elided *ταῦτα*, neut. acc. pl. as object of *εἶπεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*). *Προστάτης* interprets *maestro* in terms of his charge rather than his teaching.
- *αὐτὸς δὲ σπεύσας ἔλαβ᾽ Ἡγήτορά μου, χεῖρας ἐκτείνας* makes ‘took’ the one finite action. *αὐτός* marks the change of subject corresponding to *quegli*; *σπεύσας* and *ἐκτείνας* (nom. masc. sg. aor. act. participles of *σπεύδω* and *ἐκτείνω*) give its circumstances: ‘having made haste … having stretched out his hands’. The latter is placed after *ἔλαβ᾽* (elided *ἔλαβε*, second aor. of *λαμβάνω*) though the reaching precedes the taking. *Ἡγήτορα*, acc. sg. of *ἡγήτωρ* ‘leader’, bears an additional accent before enclitic possessive *μου*.
- *Ὧν Ἡρακλῆς ᾔσθετο τὸ μέγα σθένος* ‘whose great strength Heracles felt’ relates *ὧν* (gen. pl.) to *χεῖρας*: it modifies *σθένος*, rather than expressing source as *ond’* does. *ᾔσθετο* is 3rd sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *αἰσθάνομαι*, with neut. acc. sg. *τὸ μέγα σθένος* as object. Strength replaces the specific *stretta* ‘grip’; *già* has no separate equivalent.

> 133 Virgilio, quando prender si sentio,  
> 134 disse a me: "Fatti in qua, sì ch' io ti prenda";  

Virgil, when he felt their grasp, said to me: “Come here, that I may take thee”;

> 133 Βιργίλιος δ᾽ ἑαυτὸν ἰδὼν ληφθέντα  
> (And Virgil, seeing himself seized,)
>
> 134 Εἶπέ μοι· “Πρόσελθ᾽ ἐγγύς, ἵνα σε λάβω·”  
> (Said to me: “Come close, that I may take you;”)

- *Βιργίλιος δ᾽ ἑαυτὸν ἰδὼν ληφθέντα* ‘and Virgil, seeing himself seized’ substitutes a circumstantial participle for *quando … si sentio*. *ἰδών* is nom. masc. sg. second aor. act. participle of *ὁράω*; its reflexive object *ἑαυτόν* takes a supplementary participle *ληφθέντα* (acc. masc. sg. aor. pass. of *λαμβάνω*). The Italian perceiver feels the taking; the Greek perceiver sees himself as already taken.
- *Εἶπέ μοι* has the enclitic dative of the addressee, hence the additional accent on *εἶπέ*. *Πρόσελθ᾽ ἐγγύς* ‘come near’ replaces reflexive *Fatti in qua* with *πρόσελθε* (elided 2nd sg. second aor. act. imperative of *προσέρχομαι*) reinforced by an adverb. *ἵνα σε λάβω* makes *sì ch’ io ti prenda* explicitly final: *λάβω* is 1st sg. second aor. act. subj. of *λαμβάνω*, not a present indicative.

> 135 poi fece sì che un fascio er' egli ed io.  

then of himself and me he made one bundle.

> 135 Καὶ σύμπλεγμ’ ἓν ἐποίησ’ αὐτοῦ τ’ ἐμοῦ τε.  
> (And made one clasp of himself and of me.)

- *Καὶ σύμπλεγμ’ ἓν ἐποίησ’ αὐτοῦ τ’ ἐμοῦ τε* ‘and made one interlacing of him and me’ replaces *poi fece sì che … er’ egli ed io* with a transitive construction. Elided *σύμπλεγμα* (neut. acc. sg.) and agreeing numeral *ἕν* are the direct object of *ἐποίησε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ποιέω*, likewise elided); the image is of things clasped together rather than tied in a *fascio*.
- *αὐτοῦ τ’ ἐμοῦ τε* specifies the two constituents in the genitive, linked by paired enclitic *τε … τε*, the first elided. *αὐτοῦ* refers back to the maker without the explicit reflexive form *ἑαυτοῦ*; *ἐμοῦ* is the full, stressed pronoun, paired with it rather than enclitic.

> 136 Qual pare a riguardar la Carisenda  
> 137 sotto il chinato, quando un nuvol vada  
> 138 sopr' essa sì che ella incontro penda:  

Such as the Carisenda seems to one’s view, beneath the leaning side, when a cloud is going over it so, that it hangs [in the contrary direction]:

> 136 Ὡς δοκεῖ τῷ βλέποντι τὴν Καρισένδαν  
> (As it seems to one looking at the Carisenda)
>
> 137 Ἐξ οὗπερ νεύει μέρους, ἐπ’ αὐτῆς νέφους  
> (From the side toward which it leans, when a cloud above it)
>
> 138 Βαίνοντος, ὅτι κατεναντίον ῥέπει,  
> (Passes, that it leans the opposite way,)

- *Ὡς δοκεῖ τῷ βλέποντι τὴν Καρισένδαν* begins ‘as it seems to the one looking at the Carisenda’. Where *la Carisenda* is the subject of *pare*, *τὴν Καρισένδαν* is the accusative object of *βλέποντι* (dat. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *βλέπω*). The article makes the participle substantival, and the dative identifies the person to whom the appearance presents itself.
- *Ἐξ οὗπερ νεύει μέρους* ‘from the side to which it inclines’ defines the observer’s viewpoint rather than translating *sotto* as ‘under’. *ἐξ* governs gen. sg. *μέρους*; *οὗπερ* has the relative genitive assimilated to *μέρους*, with enclitic *-περ* emphasizing the precise side. Finite *νεύει* ‘it inclines’ unpacks substantival *il chinato*.
- *ἐπ’ αὐτῆς νέφους βαίνοντος* replaces *quando un nuvol vada* with a genitive absolute: *νέφους* (gen. neut. sg. of *νέφος*) is the subject of *βαίνοντος* (pres. act. participle of *βαίνω*). Elided *ἐπί* with gen. fem. sg. *αὐτῆς* means ‘over it’, referring to the tower, not the cloud.
- *ὅτι κατεναντίον ῥέπει* gives the content of impersonal *δοκεῖ*, ‘that it inclines in the opposite direction’, rather than reproducing consecutive *sì che* with another subjunctive. *κατεναντίον* interprets *incontro* directionally; *ῥέπει* is 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ῥέπω*.

> 139 tal parve Anteo a me che stava a bada  
> 140 di vederlo chinare, e fu tal ora  
> 141 ch' io avrei volut' ir per altra strada;  

such Antæus seemed to me who stood watching to see him bend; and [it was so terrible a moment, that] I should have wished to go by other road;

> 139 Οὕτω πως ἐδόκει μοι ῥέπειν Ἀνταῖος,  
> (Even so seemed Antæus to me to lean,)
>
> 140 Αὐτὸν βλέποντι, καὶ τῇ στιγμῇ δὴ ’κείνῃ  
> (As I watched him, and at that very moment)
>
> 141 Δι’ ἄλλης ὁδοῦ προῃρούμην ἰέναι.  
> (By another road I would have chosen to go.)

- *Οὕτω πως* ‘somewhat in this way’ answers *Ὡς* as *tal* answers *Qual*; enclitic *πως* qualifies the likeness. *ἐδόκει μοι ῥέπειν Ἀνταῖος* now uses personal *δοκέω*: postponed nominative *Ἀνταῖος* is subject of the imperfect *ἐδόκει* and understood subject of pres. act. infinitive *ῥέπειν*. The repeated verb transfers the apparent inclination of the tower directly to Antæus.
- *Αὐτὸν βλέποντι* ‘as I looked at him’ has *βλέποντι* in the dative agreeing with *μοι*, and *αὐτόν* as its object. It recalls *τῷ βλέποντι* but compresses *che stava a bada di vederlo*: the sustained watching survives, not the separate expression of waiting to see.
- *καὶ τῇ στιγμῇ δὴ ’κείνῃ* ‘and at that very moment’ replaces *e fu tal ora ch’* with a temporal dative, not a degree-and-result clause. *δή* emphasizes the point of time; *’κείνῃ* shows prodelision of the initial vowel of *ἐκείνῃ*, dat. fem. sg. agreeing with *στιγμῇ*.
- *Δι’ ἄλλης ὁδοῦ προῃρούμην ἰέναι* fronts the route, *διά* with the genitive, where *per altra strada* comes last. *προῃρούμην* (1st sg. imperf. mid. ind. of *προαιρέω*, with internal augment) means ‘I preferred’; it takes *ἰέναι*, pres. act. infinitive of *εἶμι* ‘go’. Without *ἄν*, the Greek states the preference felt then rather than formally reproducing the conditional perfect *avrei voluto*.

> 142 ma lievemente al fondo che divora  
> 143 Lucifero con Giuda ci sposò;  

but gently on the deep, which swallows Lucifer with Judas, he [set] us [down];

> 142 Ἀλλ’ εἰς τὸν βυθὸν ἡμᾶς ἔθηκ’ ἠπίως,  
> (But gently he set us down into the abyss,)
>
> 143 Ὃς Ἑωσφόρον κ’ Ἰούδαν καταδάπτει·  
> (Which devours Lucifer and Judas;)

- *Ἀλλ’ εἰς τὸν βυθὸν ἡμᾶς ἔθηκ’ ἠπίως* puts the destination first and *ἠπίως* ‘gently’ last, reversing the prominence of *lievemente*. *εἰς* with the accusative marks motion into the deep; *ἔθηκ’* is elided *ἔθηκε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *τίθημι*). With *ἡμᾶς* as object it interprets *ci sposò* as ‘set us down’; unlike Dante, Musurus completes the main clause before the relative.
- *Ὃς Ἑωσφόρον κ’ Ἰούδαν καταδάπτει* makes the relative *ὅς* nom. masc. sg., agreeing in gender and number with *βυθόν* but taking its case as subject of *καταδάπτει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *καταδάπτω* ‘devour’). *Ἑωσφόρον* renders *Lucifero* by the Greek name ‘Dawn-bringer’; *κ’* is shortened *καί*, making *Ἰούδαν* a second accusative object rather than a companion introduced by *con*.

> 144 nè sì chinato lì fece dimora,  
> 145 e come albero in nave si levò.  

nor lingered there thus bent, but raised himself as in a ship the mast.

> 144 Οὐδ’ ἐπὶ μακρὸν ἔμεινεν οὕτω κλίνων,  
> (Nor did he remain long thus stooping,)
>
> 145 Ἀλλ’ ὡς ἱστὸς τῆς νηὸς εὐθὺς ὠρθώθη.  
> (But like the mast of a ship straightway he rose upright.)

- *Οὐδ’ ἐπὶ μακρὸν ἔμεινεν* ‘nor did he stay for long’ renders *nè … fece dimora* by *οὐδέ* elided and *ἔμεινεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *μένω*). *ἐπὶ μακρόν*, with a substantival neuter accusative, specifies duration; *lì* is not rendered separately. *οὕτω κλίνων*, nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *κλίνω*, presents the continuing posture where *sì chinato* uses a past participle.
- *Ἀλλ’ ὡς ἱστὸς τῆς νηὸς εὐθὺς ὠρθώθη* replaces the final connective *e* with adversative *ἀλλά* and adds *εὐθύς* ‘at once’. *ἱστός* specifies the nautical sense of *albero*; *τῆς νηός* ‘of the ship’ makes *in nave* possessive rather than locative. *νηός* is the Ionic and poetic gen. sg. of *ναῦς* (Attic *νεώς*).
- *ὠρθώθη* is 3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ὀρθόω*, with temporal augment of initial *ο-* to *ω-*. The passive has intransitive force, ‘came upright’, in place of reflexive *si levò*.

## Summary — The Same Turn in a Different Syntax

Musurus often preserves the order in which Dante’s words make their effect, even when he changes the construction that holds them together. The paired *ὡς … οὕτω* follows *come … così*, and *Ὀρλάνδος*, like *Orlando*, is held back to the end of the comparison. At the opening, however, the two actions of *una medesma lingua* no longer have equal grammatical weight: *ἥτις* introduces the wounding in a relative clause, while intensive *αὐτή*, ‘itself’, resumes the subject for the healing. The emphasis of *medesma* thus falls just where the action is reversed.

The participle is Musurus’s most flexible means of recasting a clause. It can make an Italian finite verb subordinate, as when *λέγων*, ‘saying’, replaces *e disse*, but the reverse is also possible: *διαβαίνοντες ἦμεν*, ‘we were crossing’, gives *attraversando* a finite, durative predicate of its own. After verbs of perception the participle often describes the object as perceived: in *ἠχοῦν κέρας*, ‘a horn sounding’, it follows the case of the noun while retaining the word order of *sonare un alto corno*. Particles likewise distinguish relations that Italian expresses less specifically. Postpositive *δέ* marks a change of speaker and *γάρ* a reason; *ὅταν* with the subjunctive turns conditional *se* into prospective ‘when’, whereas *ἤν* with the subjunctive can turn *quando* into ‘if’.

Some of the most revealing choices concern who performs an action or what a phrase refers to. Where the sound directs Dante’s eyes, Greek *στραφείς*, ‘having turned’, makes the listener turn and direct his own gaze. Masculine *ὅν* identifies *ἱμάντα*, ‘strap’, rather than neuter *κέρας*, ‘horn’, as what encircles the chest. Elsewhere the departure is lexical: *ὅπλα*, ‘weapons’, makes instruments of the personal *esecutori*, while *προσήγγιζε*, ‘was drawing near’, replaces *cresce’* so that fear approaches just as error flees. Musurus’s closeness to Dante is therefore not always best measured word for word: he may alter a verb, its subject, or its construction precisely where he preserves—or sharpens—the relation between two phrases.
