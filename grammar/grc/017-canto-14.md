# Inferno 14

## Lines 1–18 — From Scattered Leaves to Barren Sand

> 1 Poichè la carità del natio loco  
> 2 mi strinse, raunai le fronde sparte,  
> 3 e rende' le a colui ch' era già fioco.  

The love of my native place constraining me, I gathered up the scattered leaves; and gave them back to him, who was already hoarse.

> 1 Ἐκ συμπαθείας πρὸς τὴν φίλην πατρίδα  
> (Out of compassion for the dear fatherland,)
>
> 2 Συλλέξας τὰ σποράδην πεσόντα φύλλα,  
> (Having gathered the leaves that had fallen scattered about,)
>
> 3 Ἀπέδωκα τῷ βραγχῶς ἤδη φωνοῦντι.  
> (I gave them back to him who was already speaking hoarsely.)

- *Ἐκ συμπαθείας πρὸς τὴν φίλην πατρίδα* ‘out of sympathy for the dear fatherland’ replaces the causal clause *Poichè la carità del natio loco mi strinse*. Instead of making love the subject of a verb of constraint, Musurus gives the speaker’s motive with *ἐκ* and the genitive *συμπαθείας*. The object of the feeling, expressed by *del* in Italian, is introduced by *πρός* with the accusative. *Πατρίδα* (acc. sg. of *πατρίς*) itself conveys *natio loco*; *φίλην* ‘dear’ adds an epithet, not a possessive.
- *Συλλέξας* (nom. masc. sg., aor. act. participle of *συλλέγω*) ‘having gathered’ subordinates *raunai* to the act of giving back. *Τὰ σποράδην πεσόντα φύλλα* is its accusative object: *πεσόντα* (acc. neut. pl., second aor. act. participle of *πίπτω*) agrees with *φύλλα*. Where *sparte* describes the leaves as scattered, the Greek adds the act of falling and expresses their distribution by the adverb *σποράδην* ‘here and there’.
- *Ἀπέδωκα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀποδίδωμι*, with internal augment) is the sole finite verb, rendering *rende’*. There is no need for either the conjunction *e* or a pronoun repeating the leaves as object. *Τῷ βραγχῶς ἤδη φωνοῦντι* ‘to the one already speaking hoarsely’ renders *a colui ch’ era già fioco*: the article and present active participle of *φωνέω* (dat. masc. sg.) together name the recipient. The predicate adjective becomes an adverb of manner, and the present stem presents the speaking as in progress at the time of the gift.

> 4 Indi venimmo al fine, ove si parte  
> 5 lo secondo giron dal terzo, e dove  
> 6 si vede di giustizia orribil arte.  

Then we came to the limit, where the second round is separated from the third, and where is seen [a] fearful [device] of justice.

> 4 Κεῖθεν ἀφίγμεθ᾽ εἰς χῶρον, ὃς χωρίζει  
> (Thence we came to a place which separates)
>
> 5 Τὸν περίβολον τὸν δεύτερον τοῦ τρίτου,  
> (The second enclosure from the third,)
>
> 6 Ἔνθ᾽ ἔργ᾽ ἦν ἰδεῖν φρικτὰ δικαιοσύνης.  
> (Where dreadful works of justice were to be seen.)

- *Κεῖθεν* ‘from there’, the shorter poetic equivalent of *ἐκεῖθεν*, gives *Indi* local rather than merely temporal force. *Ἀφίγμεθ᾽* is *ἀφίγμεθα* (1st pl. perf. mid. ind. of *ἀφικνέομαι*, ‘we have arrived’), with final *-α* elided before *εἰς*; the perfect presents the arrival as reached, where *venimmo* is a simple past. *Εἰς χῶρον* ‘to a place’ is less specific than *al fine*.
- *Ὃς χωρίζει* ‘which separates’ makes that place the subject of an active present (3rd sg. of *χωρίζω*), where *ove si parte* has a local relative and a passive or intransitive sense. *Τὸν περίβολον τὸν δεύτερον* ‘the second enclosure’ is now accusative object; the repeated article puts the ordinal in the second attributive position. *Τοῦ τρίτου* is the genitive of separation corresponding to *dal terzo*, with *περιβόλου* understood.
- *Ἔνθ᾽* ‘where’ resumes the local construction of *e dove* without a conjunction. It is *ἔνθα* elided before *ἔργ᾽*, itself *ἔργα* elided before *ἦν*. In *ἦν ἰδεῖν* ‘one could see’, the imperfect of *εἰμί* is impersonal with the second aorist active infinitive *ἰδεῖν* (of *ὁράω*); the present *si vede* becomes a possibility of seeing in past time. *Ἔργα … φρικτὰ δικαιοσύνης* ‘dreadful works of justice’ replaces singular *orribil arte* with the things wrought: *ἔργα* is accusative object of the infinitive, *φρικτά* agrees across the intervening verbs, and *δικαιοσύνης* retains the dependent genitive.

> 7 A ben manifestar le cose nuove,  
> 8 dico che arrivammo ad una landa,  
> 9 che dal suo letto ogni pianta rimuove.  

To make the new things clear, I say we reached a plain which from its bed repels all plants.

> 7 Λέγω πρὸς ἐξήγησιν τῶν νέων τούτων  
> (I say, in explanation of these new)
>
> 8 Πραγμάτων, ὡς ἤλθομεν εἰς πεδιάδα  
> (Things, that we came unto a plain)
>
> 9 Ἐσᾳεὶ φυτοῦ παντὸς ἐστερημένην.  
> (Forever deprived of every plant.)

- *Λέγω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.) brings *dico* forward. *Πρὸς ἐξήγησιν* ‘for an explanation’ renders the purpose infinitive *A ben manifestar* by *πρός* with an accusative action noun; *ben* has no separate equivalent. *Τῶν νέων τούτων πραγμάτων* ‘of these new things’ is an objective genitive depending on *ἐξήγησιν*. *Νέων* agrees with *πραγμάτων*, not with *ἐξήγησιν*; *τούτων* adds a demonstrative to *le cose nuove*.
- *Ὡς ἤλθομεν* ‘that we came’ introduces the content of *λέγω*, like *che arrivammo*. *Ἤλθομεν* is the augmented 1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*; *εἰς πεδιάδα* (acc. sg. of *πεδιάς*) gives the goal without an article corresponding to *una*.
- *Ἐσᾳεὶ φυτοῦ παντὸς ἐστερημένην* ‘for ever bereft of every plant’ turns the active relative *che dal suo letto ogni pianta rimuove* into a description of the plain’s condition. *Ἐστερημένην* (acc. fem. sg., perfect middle-passive participle of *στερέω*, passive in sense) agrees with *πεδιάδα* and takes the genitive of deprivation *φυτοῦ παντός*. The perfect marks a resulting state; the adverb *ἐσᾳεί* adds permanence. Neither the plain’s agency nor *dal suo letto* is retained.

> 10 La dolorosa selva l'è ghirlanda  
> 11 intorno, come il fosso tristo ad essa;  
> 12 quivi fermammo i passi a randa a randa.  

The dolorous wood is a garland to it round about, as to the wood the dismal fosse; here we stayed our [steps] close to its very edge.

> 10 Ταύτην περιστοιχίζει στεφάνου δίκην  
> (This is encircled, after the manner of a garland,)
>
> 11 Ὁ λυπρὸς δρυμών, ὡς τοῦτον λυπρὰ τάφρος.  
> (By the mournful wood, as that is by a mournful trench.)
>
> 12 Ἔστημεν ἐπὶ ποδὸς ἐκεῖ πρὸς τἄκρα·  
> (We halted there on foot at the very edge;)

- *Ταύτην περιστοιχίζει στεφάνου δίκην* ‘this it surrounds in the manner of a garland’ fronts the object: *ταύτην* (acc. fem. sg.) points back to *πεδιάδα*. The subject, *ὁ λυπρὸς δρυμών* ‘the mournful wood’, follows in the next line. *Περιστοιχίζει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *περιστοιχίζω*) incorporates *intorno*; the predicate noun of *l’è ghirlanda* becomes an explicit comparison, *στεφάνου δίκην*, with adverbial accusative *δίκην* ‘in the manner of’ following its genitive.
- *Ὡς τοῦτον λυπρὰ τάφρος* ‘as a mournful trench [surrounds] this’ leaves *περιστοιχίζει* understood. *Τοῦτον* (acc. masc. sg.) now points to *δρυμών*, taking the gender of the Greek noun rather than that of *selva* in *ad essa*. *Λυπρὰ τάφρος* is nominative feminine singular, the new subject; the repetition of *λυπρός* effaces the distinction between *dolorosa* and *tristo*.
- *Ἔστημεν* (1st pl. intransitive second aor. act. ind. of *ἵστημι*) ‘we came to a stand’ replaces transitive *fermammo i passi*. *Ἐπὶ ποδός* ‘on foot’ has the genitive singular of *πούς*, not an object meaning ‘steps’. *Ἐκεῖ* renders *quivi*; *πρὸς τἄκρα* ‘at the extremities’ uses *πρός* with the accusative and crasis of *τὰ ἄκρα*. It gives the position conveyed by *a randa a randa* without the emphatic doubling.

> 13 Lo spazzo era un' arena arida e spessa,  
> 14 non d' altra foggia fatta che colei,  
> 15 che fu da' piè di Caton già soppressa.  

The ground was a sand, dry and thick, not different in its fashion from that which once was trodden by the feet of Cato.

> 13 Τοὔδαφος ἦν ἐκ ξηρᾶς καὶ λεπτῆς ἄμμου,  
> (The ground was of dry and fine sand,)
>
> 14 Οὐχ ἑτέρας φύσεως, ἀλλ᾽ οἵαν ἤδη  
> (Of no other nature than such as once)
>
> 15 Διεπάτησαν οἱ τοῦ Κάτωνος πόδες.  
> (The feet of Cato trod upon.)

- *Τοὔδαφος* is crasis of *τὸ ἔδαφος*, nominative neuter singular, subject of *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*). Instead of directly identifying *lo spazzo* with *un’ arena*, *ἐκ ξηρᾶς καὶ λεπτῆς ἄμμου* ‘of dry and fine sand’ specifies its material by *ἐκ* with the genitive. Both adjectives agree with feminine *ἄμμου*: *ξηρᾶς* corresponds to *arida*, but *λεπτῆς* describes fineness rather than the thickness of *spessa*.
- *Οὐχ ἑτέρας φύσεως* ‘not of another nature’ replaces *non d’ altra foggia fatta* with a genitive of quality, omitting a participle for *fatta*. *Οὐχ* is the form of the negative before the rough breathing of *ἑτέρας*. *Ἀλλ᾽* (elided *ἀλλά*) introduces the positive counterpart; *οἵαν* ‘such as’ (acc. fem. sg. of *οἷος*) refers to sand, but takes its case as object of the following verb. There is no separate demonstrative for *colei*.
- *Διεπάτησαν οἱ τοῦ Κάτωνος πόδες* ‘the feet of Cato trod’ reverses the passive construction *fu da’ piè di Caton … soppressa*. *Διεπάτησαν* (3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *διαπατέω*) has the augment after the preverb. The feet are nominative plural subjects, with the possessive genitive *τοῦ Κάτωνος* between their article and noun. *Ἤδη*, placed before the verb, gives *già* its past reference.

> 16 O vendetta di Dio, quanto tu dei  
> 17 esser temuta da ciascun che legge  
> 18 ciò che fu manifesto agli occhi miei!  

O vengeance of God! how shouldst thou be feared by every one who reads what was revealed to my eyes!

> 16 Ὦ Θεία Δίκη, πῶς φοβεῖσθαί σ᾽ ὀφείλει  
> (O Divine Justice, how ought to fear Thee)
>
> 17 Πᾶς ἄνθρωπος, ὅς ποτ’ ἂν ἀναγνοὺς τύχῃ  
> (Every man who may ever chance to read)
>
> 18 Τὰ τοῖς ἐμοῖς ὀφθαλμοῖς ἐμφανισθέντα!  
> (The things manifested to my eyes!)

- *Ὦ Θεία Δίκη* ‘O Divine Justice’ retains the apostrophe but replaces *vendetta* with *Δίκη* and the dependent *di Dio* with the agreeing vocative adjective *Θεία*. *Πῶς* gives exclamatory *quanto* as ‘how’, but the grammatical bearer of the obligation changes: not ‘thou oughtest to be feared’, but *φοβεῖσθαί σ᾽ ὀφείλει πᾶς ἄνθρωπος* ‘every person ought to fear thee’.
- *Φοβεῖσθαι* is the present middle-passive infinitive of contracted *φοβέομαι*, active in sense and taking the accusative *σ᾽* (elided enclitic *σε*). Its additional final accent is due to the enclitic. *Ὀφείλει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ὀφείλω*) governs the infinitive; its postponed nominative subject *πᾶς ἄνθρωπος* thus represents the agent introduced by *da* in Italian.
- *Ὅς ποτ᾽ ἂν ἀναγνοὺς τύχῃ* expands the simple present relative *che legge*. *Ὅς* is nominative masculine singular; *ποτ᾽* is elided enclitic *ποτε* ‘ever’. In this indefinite relative, *ἄν* goes with *τύχῃ* (3rd sg. second aor. act. subjunctive of *τυγχάνω*, not an optative). *Ἀναγνούς* (nom. masc. sg., root-aor. act. participle of *ἀναγιγνώσκω*) is supplementary to *τυγχάνω*: the participle supplies the substantive action, ‘read’, and the finite verb the qualification ‘happen to’.
- *Τὰ τοῖς ἐμοῖς ὀφθαλμοῖς ἐμφανισθέντα* ‘the things made manifest to my eyes’ is the object of *ἀναγνούς*. Article and participle replace *ciò che fu manifesto*: *ἐμφανισθέντα* (acc. neut. pl., aor. pass. participle of *ἐμφανίζω*) makes the Italian collective expression explicitly plural and verbal. The intervening *τοῖς ἐμοῖς ὀφθαλμοῖς* retains the recipient of the manifestation in the dative, with the possessive adjective *ἐμοῖς* in attributive position.

## Lines 19–51 — Naked Souls Beneath the Rain of Fire

> 19 D' anime nude vidi molte gregge,  
> 20 che piangean tutte assai miseramente,  
> 21 e parea posta lor diversa legge.  

I saw many herds of naked souls, who were all lamenting very miserably; and there seemed imposed upon them a diverse law.

> 19 Ψυχῶν γὰρ γυμνῶν εἶδον πολλὰς ἀγέλας,  
> (For of naked souls I saw many herds,)
>
> 20 Λίαν μὲν δεινοπαθῶς θρηνούσας πάσας,  
> (All lamenting most grievously,)
>
> 21 Διάφορος δ᾽ ἑκάσταις ἐτέθη νόμος.  
> (And a different law was set for each.)

- *Ψυχῶν γὰρ γυμνῶν* keeps *D’ anime nude* before the verb: the genitive plural depends on *πολλὰς ἀγέλας* ‘many herds’, the accusative object of *εἶδον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*). Postpositive *γάρ* ‘for’ supplies a connection not expressed in the Italian line.
- *θρηνούσας πάσας* ‘all lamenting’ replaces the finite relative *che piangean tutte* with a present active participle of *θρηνέω*. Both words are feminine accusative plural, agreeing grammatically with *ἀγέλας*, not with genitive *ψυχῶν*. *λίαν* intensifies the adverb *δεινοπαθῶς*, as *assai* intensifies *miseramente*.
- *μέν … δέ* balances the lamentation against the difference of law; *δ᾽* is elided before *ἑκάσταις*. *διάφορος … νόμος* is the nominative subject of *ἐτέθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *τίθημι*), with adjective and noun enclosing the clause. For *parea posta lor* ‘seemed imposed on them’ Musurus asserts ‘was imposed’ and makes *lor* distributive: *ἑκάσταις*, feminine dative plural, ‘for each [group]’.

> 22 Supin giaceva in terra alcuna gente,  
> 23 alcuna si sedea tutta raccolta,  
> 24 ed altra andava continuamente.  

Some were lying supine upon the ground; some sitting all crouched up; and others roaming incessantly.

> 22 Αἱ μὲν ὑπτίως ἔκειντο, τινὲς δ᾽ ἄλλαι  
> (Some lay supine upon their backs, while others)
>
> 23 Ἐκάθηντο συμμίγδην ὀκλαδὸν πᾶσαι·  
> (Sat huddled together, all crouching;)
>
> 24 Ἕτεραι δ᾽ ἐβάδιζον ἀδιαλείπτως.  
> (And others walked about unceasingly.)

- *αἱ μέν … τινὲς δ᾽ ἄλλαι … ἕτεραι δ᾽* divides the feminine plural subjects into sets, in place of the three singular collectives *alcuna gente, alcuna, altra*. *ὑπτίως* ‘on their backs’ is an adverb for *supin*; *ἔκειντο* is the 3rd pl. imperfect of the stative *κεῖμαι* ‘lie’. There is no separate equivalent of *in terra*.
- *τινὲς … ἄλλαι* ‘certain others’ anticipates *ἐκάθηντο* (3rd pl. imperf. of *κάθημαι*). *πᾶσαι* agrees with the sitters; where *tutta raccolta* describes the gathered-up posture, Musurus uses both *συμμίγδην* ‘all together, intermixed’ and the adverb of posture *ὀκλαδόν* ‘squatting’.
- *ἐβάδιζον* (3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *βαδίζω*) makes *andava* specifically walking; *ἀδιαλείπτως* ‘without interruption’ renders *continuamente*. All three imperfects describe continuing states or actions.

> 25 Quella che giva intorno era più molta,  
> 26 e quella men che giaceva al tormento,  
> 27 ma più al duolo avea la lingua sciolta.  

Those that moved about were much more numerous; and those that were lying in the torment were fewer, but uttered louder cries of pain.

> 25 Αἱ πέριξ βαδίζουσαι πλείονες ἦσαν,  
> (Those walking round about were more numerous,)
>
> 26 Ὅσαι δ᾽ ἐν ταῖς βασάνοις ἔκειντ᾽, ἐλάττους·  
> (And fewer were they who lay in the torments;)
>
> 27 Ἀλλ᾽ εἶχον γλῶσσαν πρὸς λύπην λαλιστέραν.  
> (But they had a tongue more vocal in grief.)

- *αἱ πέριξ βαδίζουσαι* ‘the ones walking round about’ is an articular present active participle, feminine nominative plural, for *Quella che giva intorno*. Predicate *πλείονες* ‘more numerous’ with *ἦσαν* (3rd pl. imperf. of *εἰμί*) expresses *più molta* by a single comparative of *πολύς*.
- *ὅσαι δ᾽ … ἔκειντ᾽* ‘but as many as lay’ introduces the other set with a quantitative relative, feminine nominative plural. *ἔκειντ᾽* is *ἔκειντο* elided before *ἐλάττους*, the contracted nominative plural comparative ‘fewer’; *ἦσαν* is understood. *ἐν ταῖς βασάνοις* is locative and plural for singular *al tormento*.
- *ἀλλ᾽ εἶχον γλῶσσαν* ‘but they had a tongue’ retains *ma … avea la lingua*, including the distributive singular object. *εἶχον* is 3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *ἔχω*. *λαλιστέραν*, feminine accusative singular comparative of *λάλος*, turns *più … sciolta* into ‘more talkative’; *πρὸς λύπην* ‘in respect of grief’ follows the relation expressed by *al duolo*, rather than specifying the loudness of the cries.

> 28 Sopra tutto il sabbion d'un cader lento  
> 29 piovean di foco dilatate falde,  
> 30 come di neve in alpe senza vento.  

Over all the great sand, falling slowly, rained dilated flakes of fire, like *those* of snow in Alps without a wind.

> 28 Ἐπὶ τοῦ ψαμαθῶνος παντὸς βραδέως  
> (Over all the sandy expanse slowly)
>
> 29 Εὐρεῖαι κατέπιπτον φλογῶν τολύπαι,  
> (Broad flakes of flame were falling down,)
>
> 30 Ὡς νηνεμίης νιφάδες εἰς τὰς Ἄλπεις.  
> (Like snowflakes in a windless calm upon the Alps.)

- *ἐπὶ τοῦ ψαμαθῶνος παντός* ‘over the whole sandy tract’ uses *ἐπί* with the genitive for *sopra*; postposed *παντός* qualifies *ψαμαθῶνος* (gen. sg. of *ψαμαθών*). *βραδέως* converts *d’un cader lento* into an adverb modifying *κατέπιπτον* (3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *καταπίπτω*): the flakes ‘were falling down’, without a separate verb for *piovean*.
- *εὐρεῖαι … φλογῶν τολύπαι* ‘broad tufts of flames’ renders *di foco dilatate falde*. The feminine nominative plural adjective *εὐρεῖαι* (from *εὐρύς*) is separated from its noun by the verb and the dependent genitive; *τολύπαι* ordinarily denotes balls or tufts of wool.
- After comparative *ὡς*, *νιφάδες* ‘snowflakes’ supplies a nominative counterpart to *τολύπαι* where *come di neve* leaves the noun understood. *νηνεμίης* is the Ionic genitive singular of *νηνεμίη* (Attic *νηνεμία*), literally ‘of a windless calm’, a nominal rendering of *senza vento*. *εἰς τὰς Ἄλπεις* gives a destination to the implied falling, rather than the setting expressed by *in alpe*.

> 31 Quali Alessandro in quelle parti calde  
> 32 d' India vide sopra lo suo stuolo  
> 33 fiamme cadere infino a terra salde;  

As the flames which Alexander, in those hot regions of India, saw fall upon his host, entire to the ground;

> 31 Οἵασπερ Ἀλέξανδρος ἐν θερμαῖς χώραις  
> (Even such as Alexander in the hot regions)
>
> 32 Ἰνδιῶν εἶδεν ἐπὶ τῶν στρατευμάτων  
> (Of India saw upon his armies,)
>
> 33 Φλόγας πιπτούσας μέχρι γῆς ἀνημμένας,  
> (Flames falling still kindled even to the ground,)

- *οἵασπερ* ‘of just such a kind as’ is a qualitative relative reinforced by *-περ*, feminine accusative plural in agreement with the delayed *φλόγας*. Like *Quali … fiamme*, the relative phrase spans the intervening account of seeing. *ἐν θερμαῖς χώραις Ἰνδιῶν* has a locative dative plural and a dependent genitive plural ‘of the Indies’; *quelle* has no demonstrative counterpart.
- *Ἀλέξανδρος … εἶδεν … φλόγας πιπτούσας* uses the accusative and supplementary participle after a verb of perception, where *vide … fiamme cadere* uses an infinitive. *εἶδεν* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*; *πιπτούσας* is pres. act. ptcp. of *πίπτω*, feminine accusative plural. *ἐπὶ τῶν στρατευμάτων* ‘over the forces’ uses the genitive plural for singular *sopra lo suo stuolo*, leaving possession implicit.
- *μέχρι γῆς* ‘as far as earth’ takes the genitive. *ἀνημμένας*, feminine accusative plural perfect middle-passive participle of *ἀνάπτω*, agrees with *φλόγας*: ‘still alight’ interprets *salde* as persistence of the kindled state, rather than wholeness of shape.

> 34 per ch'ei provvide a scalpitar lo suolo  
> 35 con le sue schiere, per ciò che il vapore  
> 36 me' si stingueva mentre ch'era solo:  

whereat he with his legions took care to tramp the soil, for the fire was more easily extinguished while alone:

> 34 Ὥστε τῷ στρατῷ λακπατεῖν ἐπιτάξαι  
> (So that he commanded his army to trample)
>
> 35 Τοὔδαφος, ὅπως τὸ πῦρ κατασβεσθείη  
> (The ground, that the fire might be extinguished)
>
> 36 Ῥᾷον, ἐφ’ ὅσον ἔμενεν ἔτι μόνον,  
> (More easily, while it still remained alone:)

- *ὥστε … ἐπιτάξαι* ‘so as to give orders’ replaces consequent *per ch’ei provvide* with a result clause containing the aorist active infinitive of *ἐπιτάσσω*; its unexpressed subject is Alexander. *τῷ στρατῷ* is the dative recipient of the order, and the army is the understood agent of present active infinitive *λακπατεῖν* ‘to tread with the feet’. Thus *con le sue schiere* becomes the force ordered to act, not a phrase of accompaniment.
- *τοὔδαφος*, accusative object of *λακπατεῖν*, is crasis of *τὸ ἔδαφος* ‘the ground’. *ὅπως τὸ πῦρ κατασβεσθείη* changes the explanation *per ciò che il vapore … si stingueva* into a purpose, ‘so that the fire might be extinguished’. *κατασβεσθείη* is 3rd sg. aor. pass. opt. of *κατασβέννυμι*, the optative appropriate to a purpose conceived in past time; neuter *τὸ πῦρ* is its subject.
- *ῥᾷον*, comparative adverb ‘more easily’, gives *me’* the sense ‘with less difficulty’. *ἐφ’ ὅσον* ‘for as long as’ has *ἐπί* elided with aspiration before the rough breathing. *ἔμενεν* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *μένω*) and *ἔτι* ‘still’ emphasize continuance beyond *era*; predicate *μόνον* agrees with *πῦρ*.

> 37 tale scendeva l’ eternale ardore;  

so fell the eternal heat,

> 37 Τοιάδ’ ἡ πτῶσις φλογῶν ἦν αἰωνίων.  
> (Such was the falling of the eternal flames.)

- *τοιάδ’ ἡ πτῶσις … ἦν* ‘such was the falling’ resumes *οἵασπερ*: *τοιάδ’* is elided feminine nominative singular *τοιάδε*, predicate to *ἡ πτῶσις*. Musurus nominalizes *scendeva* and supplies copular *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. of *εἰμί*); *φλογῶν … αἰωνίων* ‘of eternal flames’ makes singular *l’eternale ardore* a plural subjective genitive dependent on the action noun.

> 38 onde l'arena s'accendea, com'esca  
> 39 sotto focile, a doppiar lo dolore.  

by which the sand was kindled, like *tinder* under *flint* and steel, redoubling the pain.

> 38 Ἡ δ’ ἄμμος ἐξήπτετο, καθάπερ ὕσκα  
> (And the sand kindled, just like tinder)
>
> 39 Ὑπὸ χάλυβα, τὴν βάσανον διπλοῦσα.  
> (Beneath the steel, doubling the torment.)

- *ἡ δ’ ἄμμος ἐξήπτετο* ‘and the sand was catching fire’ substitutes connective *δέ* for causal *onde*. *ἐξήπτετο* is 3rd sg. imperf. middle-passive ind. of *ἐξάπτω*, corresponding to intransitive *s’accendea*. *καθάπερ ὕσκα* ‘just as tinder’ introduces a nominative comparison with the sand.
- *ὑπὸ χάλυβα* ‘under steel’ has the accusative of *χάλυψ*, not the genitive of a personal agent; it names the steel of *focile*, with no separate word for flint. *τὴν βάσανον διπλοῦσα* ‘doubling the torment’ replaces *a doppiar lo dolore* with a circumstantial present active participle of *διπλόω*, feminine nominative singular agreeing with *ἄμμος*.

> 40 Senza riposo mai era la tresca  
> 41 delle misere mani, or quindi or quinci  
> 42 iscotendo da sè l'arsura fresca.  

Ever restless was the dance of miserable hands, now here, now there, shaking off the fresh burning.

> 40 Ἀκαταπαύστως τὰς ταλαιπώρους χεῖρας  
> (Without ceasing, their wretched hands)
>
> 41 Ἀνετίνασσον νῦν ἔνθεν, νῦν ἐκεῖθεν,  
> (They shook out now on this side, now on that,)
>
> 42 Ἑκάστην νέαν ἀποσείουσαι φλόγα·  
> (Shaking off each fresh flame.)

- *ἀκαταπαύστως* ‘unceasingly’ replaces *Senza riposo mai*. Instead of making *la tresca* the subject of *era*, Musurus makes the sufferers the unexpressed subject of *ἀνετίνασσον* (3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *ἀνατινάσσω*, augmented after the prefix), ‘they kept tossing’. *τὰς ταλαιπώρους χεῖρας*, feminine accusative plural, is its direct object rather than a genitive corresponding to *delle misere mani*.
- *νῦν ἔνθεν, νῦν ἐκεῖθεν* reproduces the alternation *or quindi or quinci* with directional adverbs ‘now from this side, now from that’. *ἀποσείουσαι* (pres. act. ptcp. of *ἀποσείω*, fem. nom. pl.) renders *iscotendo* and agrees with the implied souls, not with accusative *χεῖρας*. The prefix conveys shaking ‘off’ without a separate *da sè*; *ἑκάστην νέαν … φλόγα* ‘each fresh flame’ makes collective *l’arsura fresca* a succession of individual flames.

> 43 Io cominciai: “Maestro tu che vinci  
> 44 tutte le cose, fuor che i demon duri,  
> 45 che all' entrar della porta incontro uscinci,  

I began: “Master, thou who conquerest all things, save the hard Demons, that came forth against us at the entrance of the gate,

> 43 Ἐγὼ δ’ ἠρξάμην· “ Προστάτα, σύ γ’ ὁ πάντα  
> (And I began: “Protector, you indeed who all things)
>
> 44 „ Νικήσας, ἐκτὸς τῶν τολμηρῶν δαιμόνων  
> („ Have conquered, except the daring demons)
>
> 45 „ Τῶν ἀντιστάντων ἡμῖν πρόσθεν τῆς πύλης,  
> („ Who withstood us before the gate,)

- *ἐγὼ δ’ ἠρξάμην* keeps emphatic *Io* and adds connective *δέ*. *ἠρξάμην* is 1st sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἄρχομαι* ‘begin’, with the speech itself supplying what was begun. *προστάτα*, vocative singular of *προστάτης*, addresses *Maestro* as ‘protector’.
- *σύ γ’ ὁ πάντα νικήσας* ‘you indeed, the one who has conquered all things’ replaces present relative *tu che vinci* with an articular aorist active participle of *νικάω*, masculine nominative singular in apposition to *σύ*. *γ’* is focusing *γε* elided before *ὁ*; *πάντα* is the neuter accusative plural object.
- *ἐκτὸς τῶν τολμηρῶν δαιμόνων* ‘except for the daring demons’ puts the exception in the genitive and interprets *duri* as boldness. Repeated article *τῶν* introduces attributive *ἀντιστάντων* (masc. gen. pl., second aor. act. ptcp. of *ἀνθίστημι*), ‘who stood against’; *ἡμῖν* is its dative complement, answering to *-ci* in *uscinci*. Opposition replaces the movement of *incontro uscinci*, and *πρόσθεν τῆς πύλης* ‘in front of the gate’ replaces *all’ entrar della porta* with a position.

> 46 chi è quel grande che non par che curi  
> 47 l'incendio, e giace dispettoso e torto  
> 48 sì che la pioggia non par che il maturi?”  

who is that great spirit, who seems to care not for the fire, and lies disdainful and contorted, so that the rain seems not to ripen him?”

> 46 „ Τίς ἔσθ’ ὁ μέγας, ὃς οὐ δοκεῖ φροντίζων  
> („ Who is that mighty one, who seems not to care)
>
> 47 „ Περὶ τοῦ πυρός, ἀλλὰ λυσσόων κεῖται,  
> („ About the fire, but lies raging,)
>
> 48 „ Ὥστ’ οὐκ ἐμάλαξ’ αὐτὸν ὁ φλογῶν ὄμβρος;”  
> („ So that the rain of flames has not softened him?”)

- *τίς ἔσθ’ ὁ μέγας* ‘who is the great one?’ uses a substantivized adjective for *quel grande*, with an article rather than a demonstrative. *ἔσθ’* represents *ἔστι* elided before rough-breathed *ὁ*, with *τ* aspirated to *θ*.
- *ὃς οὐ δοκεῖ φροντίζων περὶ τοῦ πυρός* follows *che non par che curi l’incendio*. *φροντίζων* is the masculine nominative singular present active participle of *φροντίζω*, agreeing with relative *ὅς*; *περί* with the genitive specifies what concerns him. The participle after *δοκεῖ* is unusual: classical personal *δοκέω* ‘seem’ would ordinarily take an infinitive here.
- *ἀλλὰ λυσσόων κεῖται* ‘but he lies raging’ makes *e* adversative and concentrates *dispettoso e torto* into an attitude, without a distinct word for bodily contortion. *λυσσόων* is a poetic expanded form corresponding to contracted *λυσσῶν*, pres. act. ptcp. of *λυσσάω*, masculine nominative singular; *κεῖται* is 3rd sg. pres. ind. of *κεῖμαι*.
- *ὥστ’ οὐκ ἐμάλαξ’ αὐτὸν ὁ φλογῶν ὄμβρος* makes *sì che* a result clause with the indicative. *ὥστ’* is elided *ὥστε*; *ἐμάλαξ’* is *ἐμάλαξε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *μαλάσσω*), ‘did not soften him’. This interprets *maturi* rather than translating the ripening image and drops the second *par che*. The postponed subject *ὁ φλογῶν ὄμβρος* specifies ‘the rain of flames’, where the Italian has simply *la pioggia*.

> 49 E quel medesmo, che si fue accorto  
> 50 ch'io domandava il mio duca di lui,  
> 51 gridò: “Qual io fui vivo, tal son morto.  

And he himself, remarking that I asked my Guide concerning him, exclaimed: “What I was living, that am I dead.

> 49 Αὐτὸς δ’ ἐκεῖνος, ὡς ἔοικ’, ἐννοήσας,  
> (And he himself, as it seemed, perceiving)
>
> 50 Ὡς αὐτοῦ πέρι τὸν Ἡγήτορ’ ἠρώτων,  
> (That I was asking my Guide about him,)
>
> 51 Ἔκραξ’· “ Εἰμὶ νῦν νεκρός, οἷος ζῶν ἤμην.  
> (Cried out: “I am now, dead, such as I was living.)

- *αὐτὸς δ’ ἐκεῖνος* ‘but that very one’ renders *E quel medesmo*: intensive *αὐτός* reinforces the demonstrative. *ὡς ἔοικ’* ‘as it seems’ adds a parenthetic qualification; *ἔοικ’* is elided *ἔοικε*, a 3rd sg. perfect active indicative with present sense.
- *ἐννοήσας*, masculine nominative singular aorist active participle of *ἐννοέω*, turns *che si fue accorto* into a circumstance anterior to the cry. The second *ὡς* introduces what he perceived, ‘that’. *αὐτοῦ πέρι* fronts *di lui*, with the genitive before its preposition (hence the recessive accent in *πέρι*). *τὸν Ἡγήτορ’* is elided *τὸν Ἡγήτορα*, accusative of *ἡγήτωρ*, the person questioned; *mio* is not separately rendered. *ἠρώτων* is 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἐρωτάω*, retaining the ongoing action of *domandava*.
- *ἔκραξ’* is elided *ἔκραξε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *κράζω*), corresponding to *gridò*. *εἰμὶ νῦν νεκρός, οἷος ζῶν ἤμην* reverses the order of *Qual io fui vivo, tal son morto*: the present assertion comes first, with added *νῦν* ‘now’ and no explicit demonstrative for *tal*. *οἷος* is a predicate qualitative relative, masculine nominative singular; *ζῶν*, the contracted present active participle of *ζάω*, gives ‘while living’ against predicate adjective *νεκρός* ‘dead’. *ἤμην* is the later 1st sg. imperfect of *εἰμί* ‘I was’, in place of classical Attic *ἦ*.

## Lines 52–84 — Capaneus’s Defiance and the Stony Stream

> 52 Se Giove stanchi il suo fabbro da cui  
> 53 crucciato prese la folgore acuta,  
> 54 onde l'ultimo dì percosso fui,  

Though Jove weary out his smith, from whom in anger he took the sharp bolt with which on my last day I was transfixed;

> 52 „ Κἂν Ζεὺς ἐπείγῃ τὸν ἑαυτοῦ χαλκέα,  
> („ Though Jove press his own smith hard,)
>
> 53 „ Οὗ τὸν ὀξὺν κεραυνὸν λαβὼν ὀργίλως  
> („ From whom in wrath he took the sharp thunderbolt)
>
> 54 „ Ἔβαλε μ’ ἐν ἐσχάτῃ ζωῆς ἡμέρᾳ·  
> („ And struck me on the last day of my life;)

- *Κἂν Ζεὺς ἐπείγῃ* ‘even if Zeus presses hard’ makes *Se Giove stanchi* explicitly concessive. *Κἄν* is *καὶ ἐάν* in crasis, with the grave accent here before a following word; it takes *ἐπείγῃ*, 3rd sg. present active subjunctive of *ἐπείγω*. The present envisages the pressure as continuing; the verb describes the urging rather than its result, exhaustion.
- *τὸν ἑαυτοῦ χαλκέα* ‘his own smith’ has the genitive singular reflexive *ἑαυτοῦ* between article and noun, referring *suo* back to Zeus. *Χαλκέα* is the accusative singular of *χαλκεύς*. The relative *οὗ*, genitive singular referring to the smith, denotes the source of the bolt, without a preposition corresponding to *da*.
- Musurus makes a single relative clause out of Dante’s two: *τὸν ὀξὺν κεραυνὸν λαβὼν ὀργίλως ἔβαλε μ’* ‘having angrily taken the sharp thunderbolt, he struck me’. *Λαβών*, nominative masculine singular second-aorist active participle of *λαμβάνω*, subordinates *prese* to *ἔβαλε*, 3rd sg. second-aorist active indicative of *βάλλω*. The participle shares the finite verb’s subject, Zeus; the adverb *ὀργίλως* replaces the subject-describing *crucciato*. There is no second relative corresponding to instrumental *onde*: Zeus remains the agent, and the subject of passive *percosso fui* becomes the elided accusative *μ’* (*με*).
- *ἐν ἐσχάτῃ ζωῆς ἡμέρᾳ* ‘on the last day of life’ uses *ἐν* with the temporal dative. *Ἐσχάτῃ* agrees with *ἡμέρᾳ* across the supplied dependent genitive *ζωῆς*; whose life it is needs no possessive.

> 55 o s' egli stanchi gli altri a muta a muta  
> 56 in Mongibello alla fucina negra,  
> 57 chiamando : ‘ Buon Vulcano, aiuta aiuta,’  
> 58 sì com'ei fece alla pugna di Flegra,  

and though he weary out the others, one by one, at the black forge in Mongibello, crying: ‘Help, help, good Vulcan!’ as he did at the strife of Phlegra;

> 55 „ Κἂν ἀμοιβαδὸν τοὺς μέλανας ἐργάτας  
> („ And though in relays the black workmen)
>
> 56 „ Ἐργοδιωκτῇ τῆς Αἰτναίας καμίνου,  
> („ He drive hard at Etna's furnace,)
>
> 57 „ Ὡς ποτ’ ἔπραξεν ἐν τῇ Φλεγραίᾳ μάχῃ  
> („ As once he did in the battle of Phlegra,)
>
> 58 „ Κράζων· ‘“Ἥφαιστε, βοήθει δή, βοήθει!”’  
> („ Crying out: ‘“Vulcan, help now, help!”’)

- Repeated *κἄν* replaces *o s’* ‘or if’ with another ‘even if’. *Ἀμοιβαδόν* ‘by turns’ condenses *a muta a muta*. *Τοὺς μέλανας ἐργάτας* ‘the black workmen’, accusative plural object of *ἐργοδιωκτῇ*, supplies a noun for *gli altri* but drops ‘other’; *μέλανας* transfers *negra* from the forge to the men.
- *Ἐργοδιωκτῇ*, 3rd sg. present active subjunctive of *ἐργοδιωκτέω*, is specifically ‘drive at work’, not a repetition of *ἐπείγῃ*. *Τῆς Αἰτναίας καμίνου* ‘of the Aetnaean furnace’ identifies the workmen by their workplace; the genitive noun phrase replaces two Italian locative expressions, with an adjective for *in Mongibello*.
- Musurus reverses lines 57 and 58: *ὡς ποτ’ ἔπραξεν* ‘as he once did’ precedes the cry. *Ποτ’* is elided *ποτε*; *ἔπραξεν* is the augmented 3rd sg. first-aorist active indicative of *πράσσω*, with movable *ν*. *Ἐν τῇ Φλεγραίᾳ μάχῃ* renders *alla pugna di Flegra* with *ἐν* and a dative noun and adjective.
- *Κράζων* ‘crying out’, nominative masculine singular present active participle of *κράζω*, keeps the concurrent action of *chiamando* and, in its new position, can describe how Zeus acted *once*. *Ἥφαιστε* is the vocative of the Greek name *Ἥφαιστος*; *Buon* is omitted. Repeated *βοήθει* is the 2nd sg. present active imperative of *βοηθέω*, with emphatic *δή* after the first imperative: ‘do help, help!’

> 59 e me saetti di tutta sua forza,  
> 60 non ne potrebbe aver vendetta allegra.”  

and hurl at me with all his might, yet should he not thereby have joyful vengeance.”

> 59 „ Κἂν τ’ ἐπ’ ἐμὲ τοξεύσῃ βέλεσι πᾶσιν,  
> („ And though he shoot at me with all his bolts,)
>
> 60 „ Οὐκ ἂν ἐκδικήσεως χαρμονὴν ἕξοι.”  
> („ He would not have the gladness of vengeance.”)

- *Κἂν τ’ ἐπ’ ἐμὲ τοξεύσῃ* gives *e me saetti* a concessive conjunction of its own, as well as connective *τ’* (elided *τε*). *Ἐπ’* is *ἐπί* elided before emphatic accusative *ἐμέ*, marking the target. *Τοξεύσῃ* is 3rd sg. first-aorist active subjunctive of *τοξεύω*, presenting the shooting as a whole, unlike the continuing pressure on the smiths. Instrumental *βέλεσι πᾶσιν* ‘with all his missiles’ (dative plural of *βέλος*, *πᾶς*) makes *di tutta sua forza* a matter of the missiles expended rather than the strength exerted.
- *Οὐκ ἂν … ἕξοι* finally supplies the main clause for the successive concessions. *Ἕξοι* is 3rd sg. **future** active optative of *ἔχω* (future *ἕξω*); *ἄν* marks the potential force of *potrebbe*. The future optative is unusual here: the ordinary Classical potential optative with *ἄν* is present or aorist. There is no separate equivalent for *ne*.
- *Ἐκδικήσεως χαρμονήν* ‘gladness of vengeance’ reverses the grammatical relation in *vendetta allegra*: gladness is now the accusative object of ‘have’, vengeance the dependent genitive singular of *ἐκδίκησις*.

> 61 Allora il duca mio parlò di forza  
> 62 tanto ch'io non l' avea sì forte udito :  

Then my Guide spake with a force such as I had not heard before:

> 61 Τότ’ εἶπεν οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ μετὰ σθένους,  
> (Then spoke my Guide with vehemence,)
>
> 62 Ὅπως οὐ πώποτ’ ἤκουσ’ αὐτοῦ λαλοῦντος·  
> (Such as I had never heard him speaking:)

- *Τότ’ εἶπεν οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ* follows the Italian order ‘then spoke my Guide’. *Τότ’* is elided *τότε*; *εἶπεν* is the 3rd sg. second-aorist active indicative used for *λέγω*. *Οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, with the article’s rough breathing retained; the possessive precedes nominative *Ἡγήτωρ*. *Μετὰ σθένους*, with the genitive singular of *σθένος*, gives the manner of speaking.
- *Ὅπως οὐ πώποτ’ ἤκουσ’ αὐτοῦ λαλοῦντος* ‘as I never yet heard him speaking’ makes *tanto ch’ … sì forte* a comparison of manner, without either degree word. *Ὅπως* is modal, not purposive. *Πώποτ’* is elided *πώποτε*, ‘ever yet’; *ἤκουσ’* is elided *ἤκουσα*, 1st sg. first-aorist active indicative of *ἀκούω*, for the Italian pluperfect *avea … udito*. The person heard is genitive *αὐτοῦ*, with agreeing present active participle *λαλοῦντος* of *λαλέω*: this is a perception construction, not a genitive absolute.

> 63 “ O Capaneo, in ciò che non s' ammorza  
> 64 la tua superbia, se' tu più punito :  

“O Capaneus! in that thy pride remains unquenched, thou art punished more:

> 63 “Ὦ Καπανεῦ, δι’ αὐτὴν τὴν ἄκαμπτόν σου  
> (“O Capaneus, through your very unbending)
>
> 64 „ Ἀλαζονείαν οὕτω σκληρῶς νῦν πάσχεις·  
> („ Arrogance you now suffer so harshly;)

- *Ὦ Καπανεῦ* has the vocative singular of *Καπανεύς*. *Δι’ αὐτὴν τὴν ἄκαμπτόν σου ἀλαζονείαν* ‘because of this very unbending arrogance of yours’ replaces *in ciò che non s’ ammorza la tua superbia* with *διά* (elided) and an accusative of cause. Intensive *αὐτήν* stands outside the article; attributive *ἄκαμπτον* makes the negative verbal image ‘is not quenched’ a quality, ‘unbending’. Its form is feminine accusative singular of the two-termination adjective *ἄκαμπτος*; the extra accent in *ἄκαμπτόν* is due to enclitic possessive *σου*.
- *Οὕτω σκληρῶς νῦν πάσχεις* ‘you now suffer so harshly’ changes passive *se’ tu … punito* to the active intransitive *πάσχεις*, 2nd sg. present indicative of *πάσχω*. Degree and manner replace comparative *più*; *νῦν* makes the present time explicit.

> 65 nullo martirio, fuor che la tua rabbia,  
> 66 sarebbe al tuo furor dolor compito.”  

no torture, except thy own raving, would be pain proportioned to thy fury.”

> 65 „ Οὐ γὰρ ἔστι βάσανος, πλὴν τῆς σῆς λύσσης,  
> („ For there is no torment, save your own rage,)
>
> 66 „ Μᾶλλον ἀξία τῆς σῆς μανίας ἄλλη.”  
> („ Other, more worthy of your madness.”)

- *Οὐ γὰρ ἔστι βάσανος* ‘for there is no torment’ adds explanatory, postpositive *γάρ*. *Ἔστι* is present indicative of *εἰμί*: Musurus asserts in the indicative what Dante puts conditionally in *sarebbe*. *Πλὴν τῆς σῆς λύσσης* ‘except your rage’ uses prepositional *πλήν* with the genitive, for *fuor che la tua rabbia*.
- *Μᾶλλον ἀξία τῆς σῆς μανίας ἄλλη* ‘another more worthy of your madness’ completes the interrupted construction. Delayed *ἄλλη* agrees with feminine nominative *βάσανος*; predicate *ἀξία* governs the genitive of what the torment matches, where Italian has *al tuo furor*. Rather than translate *dolor compito* as a noun and adjective, Musurus makes the torment itself ‘worthy’ and adds comparative *μᾶλλον*. Thus the Greek denies that any other torment is **more** fitting.

> 67 Poi si rivolse a me con miglior labbia,  
> 68 dicendo : “Quel fu l' un de' sette regi  
> 69 ch' assiser Tebe ; ed ebbe, e par ch' egli abbia,  
> 70 Dio in disdegno, e poco par che il pregi ;  

Then to me he turned with gentler lip, saying: “That was the one of the seven kings who laid siege to Thebes; and he held, and seems to hold, God in defiance and prize him lightly;

> 67 Εἶτα πρὸς ἐμὲ στραφεὶς ἠπιοφρόνως  
> (Then turning toward me mildly,)
>
> 68 Εἶπεν· “Ὅδ’ ἦν εἷς τῶν ἑπτὰ βασιλέων  
> (He said: “This was one of the seven kings)
>
> 69 „ Τῶν ἐπὶ Θήβας, ἔχων καὶ δοκῶν ἔχειν  
> („ Against Thebes, having and seeming to have)
>
> 70 „ Ἀσεβῆ πρὸς τὸ θεῖον ὑπεροψίαν·  
> („ Impious disdain toward the Deity;)

- *Εἶτα πρὸς ἐμὲ στραφεὶς ἠπιοφρόνως εἶπεν* ‘then, having turned towards me mildly, he said’ exchanges the ranks of *si rivolse* and *dicendo*. *Στραφείς* is the nominative masculine singular second-aorist passive participle of *στρέφω*, intransitive in sense; the turning precedes finite *εἶπεν*. *Πρός* with accusative *ἐμέ* gives the direction. The adverb *ἠπιοφρόνως* describes a gentle disposition, with neither the bodily expression nor the comparative of *con miglior labbia*.
- *Ὅδ’ ἦν εἷς τῶν ἑπτὰ βασιλέων* ‘this one was one of the seven kings’ substitutes near demonstrative *ὅδε* (elided) for *Quel*, and imperfect *ἦν* for *fu*. Predicate *εἷς* takes a partitive genitive; *ἑπτά* is indeclinable. Repeated genitive plural article *τῶν* attaches *ἐπὶ Θήβας* ‘against Thebes’ attributively to *βασιλέων*. The preposition with the accusative plural place-name conveys hostile direction, but no verb specifies a siege as *assiser* does.
- *Ἔχων καὶ δοκῶν ἔχειν* ‘having and seeming to have’ replaces *ed ebbe, e par ch’ egli abbia* with two nominative masculine singular present active participles referring to *ὅδε*. The first is contemporary with *ἦν*, rather than independently past like *ebbe*; the second does not independently mark Dante’s change to the speaker’s present. *Δοκῶν*, contracted from *δοκέων* (*δοκέω*), takes a present active infinitive with the same understood subject instead of a finite *che*-clause.
- The shared object of *ἔχων* and *ἔχειν* is delayed: *ἀσεβῆ πρὸς τὸ θεῖον ὑπεροψίαν* ‘impious disdain towards the divine’. *Ἀσεβῆ* is contracted accusative feminine singular of *ἀσεβής*, agreeing with *ὑπεροψίαν* across the prepositional phrase. Substantivized neuter *τὸ θεῖον* is the object of *πρός*, not of ‘have’. The single expression of disdain absorbs *e poco par che il pregi* rather than giving ‘prize’ a second verb.

> 71 ma, come io dissi lui, li suoi dispetti  
> 72 sono al suo petto assai debiti fregi.  

but, as I told him, his revilings are ornaments that well befit his breast.

> 71 „ Ἀλλ’, ὡς αὐτῷ νῦν εἶπον, τὴν αὑτοῦ λύσσαν  
> („ But, as I just told him, his own rage)
>
> 72 „ Ἔσχηκεν ὧδε νῦν ποινὴν ἀποχρῶσαν.  
> („ He has here now as a sufficient punishment.)

- *Ἀλλ’, ὡς αὐτῷ νῦν εἶπον* closely follows *ma, come io dissi lui*, with *ἀλλά* elided, dative *αὐτῷ* for the addressee and 1st sg. second-aorist active indicative *εἶπον*. Supplied *νῦν* with the aorist gives ‘just now’.
- *Τὴν αὑτοῦ λύσσαν ἔσχηκεν ὧδε νῦν ποινὴν ἀποχρῶσαν* ‘he has got his own rage here now as a sufficient punishment’ interprets rather than reproduces *li suoi dispetti … assai debiti fregi*. The Italian subject becomes a singular accusative object, *λύσσαν*, recalling line 65; rough-breathed *αὑτοῦ* is the contracted reflexive *ἑαυτοῦ*, ‘his own’. Capaneus becomes the subject of *ἔσχηκεν*, 3rd sg. perfect active indicative of *ἔχω*, denoting a resulting possession; *ὧδε νῦν* adds place and time.
- *Ποινήν* is a predicate accusative, what he has his rage **as**; *ἀποχρῶσαν*, accusative feminine singular present active participle of *ἀποχράω* ‘suffice’, qualifies it. ‘Sufficient punishment’ takes the place of the due ornaments; there is no Greek breast or ornament.

> 73 Or mi vien dietro, e guarda che non metti  
> 74 ancor li piedi nell' arena arsiccia,  
> 75 ma sempre al bosco li ritieni stretti.”  

Now follow me, and see thou place not yet thy feet upon the burning sand; but always keep them back close to the wood.”

> 73 „ Ἀλλ’ ἕπου μοι νῦν, ὅρα τε μὴ τοὺς πόδας  
> („ But follow me now, and take care lest your feet)
>
> 74 „ Ἐπὶ τῆς αἰθομένης τυχὸν θῇς ἄμμου·  
> („ You chance to set upon the burning sand;)
>
> 75 „Ἀλλ᾿ ἐγγὺς ᾀεὶ τοῦ δάσους αὐτοὺς ἔχε.”  
> („But keep them always close to the wood.”)

- *Ἀλλ’ ἕπου μοι νῦν* ‘but follow me now’ uses *ἀλλά* to turn to the command. *Ἕπου* is the 2nd sg. present middle imperative of *ἕπομαι*, which takes the dative of the person followed, here enclitic *μοι*, rather than a directional expression like *mi vien dietro*.
- *Ὅρα τε μὴ τοὺς πόδας … τυχὸν θῇς* ‘and see that you do not happen to put your feet …’ has connective *τε* and contracted *ὅρα*, 2nd sg. present active imperative of *ὁράω*. After ‘take care’, *μή* introduces the act to be avoided, with *θῇς*, 2nd sg. aorist active subjunctive of *τίθημι*. *Τυχόν*, neuter singular second-aorist active participle of *τυγχάνω* used adverbially, adds ‘perchance’, not the temporal ‘yet’ of *ancor*.
- *Ἐπὶ τῆς αἰθομένης … ἄμμου* uses *ἐπί* with the genitive for the surface on which the feet would rest. *Αἰθομένης*, genitive feminine singular present middle/passive participle of *αἴθω*, means ‘burning’, an ongoing condition for *arsiccia*.
- *Ἀλλ᾿ ἐγγὺς ᾀεὶ τοῦ δάσους αὐτοὺς ἔχε* ‘but always keep them near the wood’ gives *stretti* no separate adjective. *Ἐγγύς* governs genitive singular *τοῦ δάσους* (from neuter *δάσος*), despite the intervening ‘always’, printed *ᾀεὶ* here (usual Attic *ἀεί*). *Αὐτούς* resumes masculine plural *πόδας*, like Italian *li*; *ἔχε*, 2nd sg. present active imperative of *ἔχω*, enjoins continued keeping.

> 76 Tacendo divenimmo là ove spiccia  
> 77 fuor della selva un picciol fiumicello,  
> 78 lo cui rossore ancor mi raccapriccia.  

In silence we came to where there gushes forth from the wood a little rivulet, the redness of which still makes me shudder.

> 76 Διὰ σιγῆς ἤλθομεν ἔνθ᾿ ἀναβλύει  
> (In silence we came to where there gushes forth)
>
> 77 Ἐκτὸς τοῦ δρυμοῦ μικρὸν ποτάμιόν τι,  
> (Out of the forest a little stream,)
>
> 78 Οὗτινος ἡ ᾿ρυθρότης ἔτι φρικοῖ με.  
> (Whose redness still makes me shudder.)

- *Διὰ σιγῆς* ‘in silence’ substitutes a phrase of manner, *διά* with genitive *σιγῆς*, for the subject’s action in *Tacendo*. *Ἤλθομεν* is the augmented 1st pl. second-aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι*. Elided local adverb *ἔνθ’* (*ἔνθα*) does duty for *là ove*, without a separate demonstrative.
- *Ἀναβλύει ἐκτὸς τοῦ δρυμοῦ μικρὸν ποτάμιόν τι* keeps *spiccia* in the present (3rd sg. active indicative of *ἀναβλύω*) and postpones the subject as in Italian. *Ἐκτός* with the genitive marks the emergence outside the wood. *Μικρόν* qualifies diminutive *ποτάμιον*, retaining both indications of smallness in *picciol fiumicello*. All three words of *μικρὸν ποτάμιόν τι* are nominative neuter singular; enclitic indefinite *τι* adds indefiniteness and occasions the extra accent on *ποτάμιόν*.
- *Οὗτινος ἡ ᾿ρυθρότης ἔτι φρικοῖ με* ‘whose redness still makes me shudder’ follows the Italian clause closely. *Οὗτινος*, genitive singular of compound relative *ὅστις*, refers to *ποτάμιον* and depends on the noun for redness, serving here like simple relative *οὗ*. *Ἡ ᾿ρυθρότης* shows prodelision: it is the initial vowel of *ἐρυθρότης*, not the preceding article’s vowel, that is lost. *Φρικοῖ*, contracted 3rd sg. present active indicative of *φρικόω*, is transitive and causative with enclitic accusative *με*.

> 79 Quale del Bulicame esce un ruscello,  
> 80 che parton poi tra lor le peccatrici,  
> 81 tal per l' arena giù sen giva quello.  

As from the Bulicame issues [a] streamlet, which the sinful women share amongst themselves: so this ran down across the sand.

> 79 Οἷος δ᾿ ὁ ῥύαξ ῥέει τοῦ Βουλικάμου,  
> (And like as the stream flows from Bulicame,)
>
> 80 Ὅνπερ ἑαυταῖς διανέμουσ᾿ αἱ πόρναι,  
> (Which the harlots divide among themselves,)
>
> 81 Οὕτως ἔῤῥεεν αὐτὸ διὰ τῆς ἄμμου.  
> (So did this flow across the sand.)

- *Οἷος δ’ ὁ ῥύαξ ῥέει τοῦ Βουλικάμου* sets up the comparison with nominative masculine *οἷος* agreeing with *ὁ ῥύαξ*. The stream is definite in Greek, and noun and verb are cognate in sense, ‘the stream flows’, for *esce un ruscello*. *Ῥέει* is the uncontracted, poetic 3rd sg. present active indicative of *ῥέω* (Attic *ῥεῖ*). The bare genitive *τοῦ Βουλικάμου* can attach to the stream as ‘of Bulicame’; understood with the verb as its source, it gives *del Bulicame* without an explicit ‘from’. *Δ’* is connective *δέ* elided.
- *Ὅνπερ* is the masculine accusative singular relative *ὅν* strengthened by *-περ*, object of *διανέμουσ’* (elided *διανέμουσι*, 3rd pl. present active indicative of *διανέμω*). Feminine dative plural reflexive *ἑαυταῖς* gives the recipients, ‘to themselves’, for reciprocal *tra lor*. *Αἱ πόρναι* specifies the women’s designation more narrowly than *le peccatrici*; *poi* is not translated.
- *Οὕτως* ‘thus’ answers *οἷος*, using an adverb for the second member of *Quale … tal*. *Ἔῤῥεεν* is the uncontracted 3rd sg. imperfect active indicative of *ῥέω* (contracted *ἔρρει*); the initial rho doubles after the syllabic augment, here with the older paired breathings. The imperfect retains the ongoing motion of *giva*. Nominative neuter *αὐτό* resumes *ποτάμιον*, not masculine *ῥύαξ*; *διὰ τῆς ἄμμου* ‘through the sand’ has no separate equivalent for directional *giù*.

> 82 Lo fondo suo ed ambo le pendici  
> 83 fatt' eran pietra, e i margini da lato :  
> 84 per ch'io m' accorsi che il passo era lici.  

Its bottom and both its shelving banks were petrified, and also the margins near it: whereby I discerned that our passage lay there.

> 82 Αὐτοῦ δ᾿ ἡ κοίτη καὶ τὰ πλευρώματ᾿ ἄμφω  
> (And its bed and both its banks)
>
> 83 Τό τ᾿ ἐκτὸς χεῖλος ἦσαν ὅλως ἐκ λίθου·  
> (And the outer margin were wholly of stone;)
>
> 84 Ἐνόησα δ᾿ ἡμᾶς δεῖν περᾷν ἐκεῖθεν.  
> (Whereby I perceived that we must cross by that way.)

- *Αὐτοῦ δ’ ἡ κοίτη* fronts the possessive genitive, ‘its bed’, for *Lo fondo suo*. *Καὶ τὰ πλευρώματ’ ἄμφω* renders *ed ambo le pendici* as ‘and the two sides’. *Πλευρώματ’* is elided *πλευρώματα*, nominative neuter **plural** of *πλεύρωμα*; *ἄμφω* is the nominative/accusative **dual** ‘both’, here accompanying a plural noun rather than a dual.
- *Τό τ’ ἐκτὸς χεῖλος* ‘and the outer rim’ joins a third subject by elided *τε* and turns plural *i margini da lato* into a collective singular. Adverb *ἐκτός* stands attributively between article and noun. The plural imperfect *ἦσαν* of *εἰμί* gathers the subjects together, including the rim, which in Italian follows the predicate. *Ὅλως ἐκ λίθου* ‘wholly of stone’ is a predicate of material (*ἐκ* with genitive), a state rather than the change expressed by *fatt’ eran pietra*.
- *Ἐνόησα δ’* ‘and I perceived’, 1st sg. first-aorist active indicative of *νοέω*, renders reflexive *m’ accorsi* by a simple active; connective *δέ* leaves the inference in *per ch’* implicit. *Ἡμᾶς δεῖν περᾷν* replaces finite *che il passo era* with indirect statement and two infinitives: impersonal present *δεῖν* ‘to be necessary’, from *δεῖ*, takes *περᾷν*, contracted present active infinitive of *περάω*, with accusative *ἡμᾶς* as the subject of crossing. Thus the existence of a passage becomes a necessity for **us** to pass. *Ἐκεῖθεν*, properly ‘from there’ or ‘by that way’, gives a route rather than the static location of *lici*.

## Lines 85–117 — The Stream and the Old Man’s Tears

> 85 “Tra tutto l’ altro ch’io t’ho dimostrato,  

“Amidst all the rest that I have shown thee,

> 85 “Ἐκ πάντων ἄλλων, ὅσα κατέδειξά σοι,  
> (“Of all other things which I have shown you,)

- *Ἐκ πάντων ἄλλων* ‘out of all other things’ renders *Tra tutto l’ altro*. The Italian collective singular becomes a neuter plural; *ἐκ* with the genitive sets out the whole from which one thing will be distinguished.
- *ὅσα κατέδειξά σοι* ‘as many things as I showed you’ renders *ch’io t’ho dimostrato*. The quantitative relative *ὅσα* is accusative neuter plural, the direct object of *κατέδειξα*, not genitive like its antecedent. *Κατέδειξα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *καταδείκνυμι*, with the augment after the preverb) treats the showing as a completed act. The ending supplies *io*; the dative enclitic *σοι* supplies *t’* and accounts for the additional accent in *κατέδειξά*.

> 86 poscia che noi entrammo per la porta,  
> 87 lo cui sogliare a nessuno è negato,  

since we entered by the gate whose threshold is denied to none,

> 86 „Ἐξ οὗπερ εἰσήλθομεν διὰ τῆς πύλης,  
> („Since we entered through the gate)
>
> 87 „Δι᾿ ἧς οὐδενὶ κωλύετ᾿ εἰσιέναι,  
> („Through which entry is forbidden to no one,)

- *Ἐξ οὗπερ* ‘ever since’ is temporal: *ἐξ* is *ἐκ* before a vowel, *οὗ* the genitive relative, and the attached particle *-περ* makes the starting point precise. *Εἰσήλθομεν* (1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *εἰσέρχομαι*) gives *noi entrammo* in one word; *διὰ τῆς πύλης* uses the genitive for passage through.
- Italian *lo cui sogliare* makes the threshold the subject of *è negato*. Greek omits the threshold and repeats the construction of passage: *δι᾿ ἧς* ‘through which’, with the feminine relative referring to *πύλης*. *Οὐδενί* is dative singular of *οὐδείς*, corresponding to *a nessuno*. *Κωλύετ᾿ εἰσιέναι* is impersonal, ‘it is forbidden to enter’: *κωλύετ᾿* is elided *κωλύεται* (3rd sg. pres. pass. ind. of *κωλύω*), and *εἰσιέναι* the present infinitive of *εἴσειμι* ‘go in’. The dative with this impersonal phrasing keeps the Italian construction rather than making the person the subject of ‘is prevented’.

> 88 cosa non fu dagli tuoi occhi scorta  
> 89 notabil come lo presente rio,  
> 90 che sopra sè tutte fiammelle ammorta.”  

thy eyes have discerned nothing so notable as the present stream, which quenches all the flames above it.”

> 88 „Οὐδὲν τοῖς σοῖς ὀφθαλμοῖς ἦν τόσου λόγου  
> („Nothing has been to your eyes of such account)
>
> 89 „Ἄξιον, ὅσον τὸ ποτάμιον τοῦτο,  
> („Worthy as this little stream,)
>
> 90 „Οὗτινος ἔνδον σβέννυνται φλόγες πᾶσαι.”  
> („Within which all flames are quenched.”)

- *Οὐδὲν τοῖς σοῖς ὀφθαλμοῖς ἦν ... ἄξιον* ‘nothing was ... worthy in your eyes’ recasts *cosa non fu dagli tuoi occhi scorta*: the eyes are no longer agents of perception but a dative of judgment. *Οὐδέν* is the neuter singular subject; *ἦν* is the 3rd sg. imperfect of *εἰμί*. There is no separate verb for *scorta*.
- *Τόσου λόγου ἄξιον, ὅσον* ‘worthy of so much account as’ expands *notabil come*. *Ἄξιον* agrees with *οὐδέν* and governs the genitive *τόσου λόγου*; *ὅσον* answers the expression of degree. *Τὸ ποτάμιον τοῦτο* replaces *lo presente rio* with a diminutive, ‘this little river’; the demonstrative stands outside the article–noun group.
- *Οὗτινος ἔνδον* ‘within which’ has the genitive singular of the compound relative *ὅστις* with postposed *ἔνδον*. This differs spatially from *sopra sè*. Italian makes the stream the agent of *ammorta*; Greek makes *φλόγες πᾶσαι* ‘all flames’ the subject of *σβέννυνται* (3rd pl. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *σβέννυμι*), ‘are extinguished’. The diminutive force of *fiammelle* is absent here.

> 91 Queste parole fur del duca mio :  

These were words of my Guide:

> 91 Τοιοίδ᾿ ἦσαν οἱ λόγοι τοὐμοῦ Προστάτου.  
> (Such were the words of my Protector.)

- *Τοιοίδ᾿ ἦσαν οἱ λόγοι* ‘of such a kind were the words’ substitutes a qualitative demonstrative for *Queste parole*. *Τοιοίδ᾿* is elided *τοιοίδε*, nominative masculine plural of *τοιόσδε*, predicative to *οἱ λόγοι*; *ἦσαν* is the 3rd pl. imperfect of *εἰμί*, against the Italian preterite *fur*.
- *Τοὐμοῦ Προστάτου* ‘of my protector’ renders *del duca mio*. *Τοὐμοῦ* is crasis of *τοῦ ἐμοῦ*, with the possessive before the noun; *προστάτου* is the genitive singular of the masculine first-declension noun *προστάτης*. The word stresses one who stands before or protects, rather than the act of leading.

> 92 per che il pregai che mi largisse il pasto  
> 93 di cui largito m’aveva il disio.  

wherefore I prayed him to bestow on me the food, for which he had bestowed the appetite.

> 92 Ἐμοῦ δὲ δεηθέντος, ὅπως πληρώσῃ  
> (And when I besought him to satisfy)
>
> 93 Ὃν ταῦτα λέγων ἐνήγειρέ μοι πόθον,  
> (The desire which by saying these things he had awakened in me,)

- *Ἐμοῦ δὲ δεηθέντος* ‘and when I had asked’ turns the main verb *il pregai* into a genitive absolute dependent on the ensuing ‘he said’. *Δεηθέντος* is genitive masculine singular of the aorist passive-form participle of *δέομαι*, active in sense. *Δέ* connects rather than explicitly giving the consequence expressed by *per che*; the person asked is understood.
- *Ὅπως πληρώσῃ ... πόθον* ‘that he might satisfy a longing’ gives the substance of the request. *Πληρώσῃ* is 3rd sg. aor. act. subj. of *πληρόω*: the subjunctive is retained, not changed to an oblique optative after a past request. Greek drops both *il pasto* and the repetition *largisse ... largito*: what is to be supplied in Italian becomes a desire to be fulfilled in Greek.
- *Ὃν ταῦτα λέγων ἐνήγειρέ μοι πόθον* places the relative *ὅν* before its noun *πόθον*. Both are accusative masculine singular: *πόθον* belongs with *πληρώσῃ*, while *ὅν* is the object of *ἐνήγειρε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐγείρω* compounded with *ἐν-*, ‘roused’). *Μοι* marks the person affected. *Ταῦτα λέγων* ‘in saying these things’ supplies the means, with a nominative present active participle agreeing with the speaker; the aorist replaces the pluperfect *m’aveva*.

> 94 “In mezzo mar siede un paese guasto,”  
> 95 diss’ egli allora, “che s’ appella Creta,  

“In the middle of the sea lies a waste country,” he then said, “which is named Crete,

> 94 “Μέσον θαλάσσης ἐστὶν ἐρείπιός τις,”  
> (“In the midst of the sea there is a wasted”)
>
> 95 Εἶπέ μοι τότε, “χώρα, τοὔνομα Κρήτη,  
> (He said to me then, “country, named Crete,)

- *Μέσον θαλάσσης* ‘in mid-sea’ uses *μέσον* adverbially with a genitive rather than translating *in* by a preposition. Existential *ἐστίν* replaces *siede*. *Ἐρείπιός τις ... χώρα* ‘a certain ruined country’ separates adjective and indefinite from their noun by the reporting clause. *Ἐρείπιος* is a two-termination adjective, here nominative feminine singular despite its ending; enclitic *τις* gives it the additional accent.
- *Εἶπέ μοι τότε* ‘he said to me then’ replaces *diss’ egli allora*: *εἶπε* is the 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative used for *λέγω*. The subject pronoun disappears, while a recipient, enclitic *μοι*, is supplied. Greek interrupts the description before *χώρα*; Italian has already completed *un paese guasto* before the interruption.
- *Τοὔνομα Κρήτη* ‘Crete by name’ compresses the relative clause *che s’ appella Creta*. *Τοὔνομα* is crasis of *τὸ ὄνομα*, accusative of respect; *Κρήτη* is nominative, in apposition to *χώρα*.

> 96 sotto il cui rege fu già il mondo casto.  

under whose King the world once was chaste.

> 96 „ Ὑφ᾿ ἧς τὸν βασιλέ᾽ ἦν ὁ κόσμος σώφρων.  
> („ Under whose king the world was chaste.)

- *Ὑφ᾿ ἧς τὸν βασιλέ᾽* means ‘under the king of which’: *ἧς* is a possessive genitive referring to *Κρήτη*, not the complement of *ὑπό*. The latter governs accusative *τὸν βασιλέα* in the sense ‘under the rule of’. *Ὑφ᾿* shows elision and aspiration before the rough breathing; *βασιλέ᾽* loses its final *-α* before *ἦν*.
- *Ἦν ὁ κόσμος σώφρων* keeps *fu ... il mondo casto* as a copular predication. *Σώφρων*, nominative masculine singular, suggests ‘temperate, self-controlled’ as well as ‘chaste’. The imperfect *ἦν* describes a state; *già* has no separate equivalent.

> 97 Una montagna v’è, che già fu lieta  
> 98 d’ acque e di fronde, che si chiamò Ida;  
> 99 ora è diserta come cosa vieta.  

A mountain is there, called Ida, which once was glad with waters and with foliage; now it is deserted like an antiquated thing.

> 97 „ Ἔστιν ὄρος ἐκεῖ τι πάλαι μὲν πλῆρες  
> („ There is a mountain there, of old full)
>
> 98 „ Ὑδάτων καὶ πρασιῶν, οὗ τοὔνομ᾽ Ἴδη,  
> („ Of waters and greenery, whose name is Ida,)
>
> 99 „ Ἔρημον δὲ νῦν, ὡς γεγηρακὸς πρᾶγμα.  
> („ But now deserted, like an outworn thing.)

- *Ἔστιν ὄρος ἐκεῖ τι* ‘there is a certain mountain there’ makes the existential and local senses of *v’è* explicit. Enclitic *τι* agrees with neuter *ὄρος* across the intervening *ἐκεῖ*. *Πάλαι μὲν πλῆρες ... ἔρημον δὲ νῦν* balances ‘formerly full’ against ‘but now deserted’: *μέν ... δέ* sets the two predicate adjectives against one another without repeating a copula for each.
- *Πλῆρες ὑδάτων καὶ πρασιῶν* changes *lieta d’ acque e di fronde* from ‘glad’ to ‘full’. *Πλῆρες* is nominative neuter singular of *πλήρης* and governs the two genitive plurals; *πρασιῶν*, from *πρασιά* ‘garden bed, plot’, is not a literal word for leaves. *Οὗ τοὔνομ᾽ Ἴδη* ‘whose name [is] Ida’ replaces past *si chiamò* by a verbless relative clause. Here *τοὔνομ᾽* is both contracted by crasis and elided, and *τὸ ὄνομα* is the subject.
- *Ὡς γεγηρακὸς πρᾶγμα* ‘like a thing grown old’ takes *vieta* in the sense ‘outworn’. *Γεγηρακός* is nominative neuter singular of the reduplicated perfect active participle of *γηράσκω*: the perfect expresses the resulting state, in place of the Italian adjective.

> 100 Rea la scelse già per cuna fida  
> 101 del suo figliuolo; e, per celarlo meglio,  
> 102 quando piangea, vi facea far le grida.  

Rhea of old chose it for the faithful cradle of her son; and the better to conceal him, when he wept, caused cries to be made on it.

> 100 „ Ὡς πιστὴν αὐτὸ κοιτίδ᾽ εἵλετο Ῥέα  
> („ As a trusty cradle Rhea chose it)
>
> 101 „ Τοῦ παιδὸς αὑτῆς, ὅν, ἵνα ῥᾷον κρύψῃ  
> („ For her child, and the more easily to hide him)
>
> 102 „ Κλαίοντα, βοὰς ἀναπέμπεσθ᾽ ἐποίει.  
> („ When weeping, she caused loud cries to be sent up.)

- *Ὡς πιστὴν αὐτὸ κοιτίδ᾽ εἵλετο Ῥέα* fronts the capacity in which the mountain was chosen. *Αὐτό*, accusative neuter singular, resumes *ὄρος*, whereas *πιστὴν ... κοιτίδ᾽* is a feminine predicate accusative (*κοιτίδα*, from *κοιτίς*, elided). *Εἵλετο* is 3rd sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *αἱρέω*: the middle means ‘chose for herself’. *Ῥέα* comes last rather than first, and *già* is not separately rendered.
- *Τοῦ παιδὸς αὑτῆς* ‘of her own child’ depends on ‘cradle’. *Παιδός* is genitive singular of *παῖς*, without the diminutive of *figliuolo*; rough-breathed *αὑτῆς* is the contracted reflexive *ἑαυτῆς*, referring to Rhea. The connecting relative *ὅν* ‘whom’ takes up the child as object inside *ἵνα ῥᾷον κρύψῃ* ‘so that she might hide him more easily’. Italian *per celarlo meglio* becomes a purpose clause with *κρύψῃ* (3rd sg. aor. act. subj. of *κρύπτω*) and the comparative adverb *ῥᾷον*.
- *Κλαίοντα*, accusative masculine singular present active participle of *κλαίω*, agrees with *ὅν*: ‘while he was crying’ renders the finite *quando piangea*. In *βοὰς ἀναπέμπεσθ᾽ ἐποίει*, *βοάς* is the accusative subject of the present passive infinitive *ἀναπέμπεσθαι*, ‘cries to be sent up’; *ἐποίει* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ποιέω*) preserves the repeated causation of *facea far*. *Ἀναπέμπεσθ᾽* elides final *-αι*; the Italian locative *vi* is left implicit.

> 103 Dentro dal monte sta dritto un gran veglio,  
> 104 che tien volte le spalle inver Damiata,  
> 105 e Roma guata sì come suo speglio.  

Within the mountain stands erect a great Old Man, who keeps his shoulders turned towards Damietta, and looks at Rome as if it were his mirror.

> 103 „ Τοῦ δ᾽ ὄρους ἐντὸς ἵσταται γέρων μέγας,  
> („ And within the mountain stands a great Old Man,)
>
> 104 „ Τὰ νῶτ᾽ ἐστραμμέν᾽ ἔχων πρὸς Δαμιάταν,  
> („ Having his back turned toward Damietta,)
>
> 105 „ Καὶ βλέπων πρὸς τὴν Ῥώμην, ὡς ἐν κατόπτρῳ.  
> („ And looking toward Rome as in a mirror.)

- *Τοῦ δ᾽ ὄρους ἐντός* ‘and within the mountain’ places the genitive before its governing local adverb. *Ἵσταται* (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *ἵστημι*) is intransitive, ‘stands’; *dritto* has no separate adjective. *Γέρων μέγας* keeps the postposed subject of *sta dritto un gran veglio*.
- The relative with two finite predicates, *che tien ... e ... guata*, becomes two present participles agreeing with *γέρων*: *ἔχων* ‘having’ and *βλέπων* ‘looking’. *Τὰ νῶτ᾽ ἐστραμμέν᾽ ἔχων* keeps the Italian ‘have + object + participle’. *Νῶτ᾽* is elided *νῶτα*, accusative neuter plural ‘back’; *ἐστραμμέν᾽* is elided *ἐστραμμένα*, the agreeing perfect middle/passive participle of *στρέφω*, ‘in a turned position’. *Πρὸς Δαμιάταν* marks the direction with the accusative.
- *Βλέπων πρὸς τὴν Ῥώμην* replaces Italian *Roma guata*, a direct object, with a second directional *πρός*. *Ὡς ἐν κατόπτρῳ* ‘as in a mirror’ uses a local dative after *ἐν*: it gives the manner of looking instead of calling Rome *suo speglio*. The possessive is not expressed.

> 106 La sua testa è di fin’ oro formata,

His head is shapen of fine gold, his arms and his breast are pure silver;

> 106 „ Ἐκ χρυσοῦ τὴν κεφαλὴν ἀκραιφνοῦς ἔχει,

> 107 „ Ἐξ ἀργύρου δὲ βραχίονας καὶ στῆθος·

- *Ἐκ χρυσοῦ τὴν κεφαλὴν ἀκραιφνοῦς ἔχει* ‘of unalloyed gold he has his head’ changes the passive *La sua testa è ... formata* to active possession. *Τὴν κεφαλήν* is the object of *ἔχει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind.); its article suffices for the possessive. The intervening accusative separates genitive *χρυσοῦ* from its adjective *ἀκραιφνοῦς* (from *ἀκραιφνής*). *Ἐκ* with the genitive denotes material.
- *Ἐξ ἀργύρου δὲ βραχίονας καὶ στῆθος* continues the construction: ‘and of silver [he has] arms and breast’. *Βραχίονας* is accusative plural of *βραχίων*, not dual; *στῆθος* is accusative neuter singular. Italian changes to a copula, *son*, and qualifies the silver as *puro*; Greek does neither.

> 108 poi è di rame infino alla forcata;  

then he is of brass to the cleft;

> 108 „ Ἔπειτ᾽ ἐστὶν ἐκ χαλκοῦ μέχρις ὀσφύος,  
> („ Then he is of bronze as far as the waist,)

- *Ἔπειτ᾽ ἐστὶν ἐκ χαλκοῦ* ‘next he is of bronze’ keeps the sequence and the change to a copula; *ἔπειτ᾽* is elided *ἔπειτα*. *Χαλκός* can denote copper or bronze and renders *rame*. *Μέχρις ὀσφύος* uses a genitive of limit after *μέχρις*; *ὀσφύος*, from *ὀσφύς* ‘loin, waist’, gives a different anatomical expression from *alla forcata* ‘to the fork’.

> 110 salvo che il destro piede è terra cotta,  
> 111 e sta in su quel, più che in su l’ altro, eretto.

> („ And of choice iron from thence downward,)
>

> („ Upon which he leans more than on the other.)

- *Ἐξ ἐκλεκτοῦ σιδήρου τ᾽ ἐκεῖθεν κάτω* puts the material first and direction last. *Ἐκλεκτοῦ* agrees with *σιδήρου*, ‘selected iron’; *τ᾽* is elided connective *τε*, and *ἐστίν* carries over from line 108. *Ἐκεῖθεν* already means ‘from there’; *κάτω* supplies *in giuso*. There is no equivalent of *tutto*.
- *Πλὴν τοῦ δεξιοῦ ποδὸς ὄντος πηλίνου* replaces the finite exception *salvo che ... è* with *πλήν* ‘except’ and a genitive participial phrase. *Ὄντος* (gen. masc. sg. pres. participle of *εἰμί*) and its predicate *πηλίνου* agree with *ποδός*, from *πούς*. *Πήλινος* means ‘made of clay’; *cotta* is not separately expressed.
- *Ἐφ᾽ οὗπερ ἐρείδεται* ‘on which very one he supports himself’ makes *e sta in su quel* a relative clause. *Ἐφ᾽* is *ἐπί* elided and aspirated before *οὗπερ*, genitive masculine singular with specifying *-περ*. *Ἐρείδεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *ἐρείδω*) conveys the bearing of weight rather than translating *eretto*. *Πλέον* is an adverbial neuter comparative, ‘more’; *θἀτέρου*, crasis of *τοῦ ἑτέρου*, gives the genitive of comparison instead of repeating the preposition as in *che in su l’ altro*.

> 112 Ciascuna parte, fuor che l’ oro, è rotta  
> 113 d’ una fessura che lagrime goccia,  
> 114 le quali accolte foran quella grotta.

> 112 „ Διασχιδὲς πλὴν χρυσοῦ πᾶν ἐστι μέρος

> („ By a fissure, from which a tear drips,)
>

> („ Which, gathering together, bores through the mountain.)

- *Διασχιδὲς ... πᾶν ἐστι μέρος* fronts the predicate ‘split’, a neuter singular adjective agreeing with *πᾶν ... μέρος*. Italian *Ciascuna parte ... è rotta* likewise describes a state, but with a participle. *Πλὴν χρυσοῦ* renders the parenthesis *fuor che l’ oro* with *πλήν* and a bare genitive.
- *Διὰ χαράδρας* ‘by a cleft’ uses *διά* with the genitive; *χαράδρα*, a word for a gully or chasm, renders *fessura*. *Ἐξ ἧς σταλάζει δάκρυ* ‘from which a tear drips’ makes the cleft the source, not the subject as in *che lagrime goccia*. *Σταλάζει* is 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *σταλάζω*, here intransitive; *δάκρυ* is its nominative neuter singular subject, against plural *lagrime*.
- That singular controls the next clause: *ὃ συναθροισθὲν διατιτραίνει*, ‘which, once gathered, bores through’. *Ὅ* and *συναθροισθέν* are nominative neuter singular; the latter is the aorist passive participle of *συναθροίζω*, expressing collection prior to the action of the present verb. Italian has plural *le quali accolte foran*. *Τοὖρος*, accusative object, is crasis of *τὸ ὄρος* ‘the mountain’: Musurus substitutes the mountain for *quella grotta* and drops the demonstrative.

> 115 Lor corso in questa valle si diroccia;  
> 116 fanno Acheronte, Stige e Flegetonta;  

Their course descends from rock to rock into this valley; they form Acheron, Styx, and Phlegethon,

> 115 „Ἐκ δὲ τούτων τῶν ῥοῶν ἐν τῇ κοιλάδι  
> („And from these streams, in the valley,)
>
> 116 „ Σχηματίζετ’ Ἀχέρων, Στὺξ καὶ Φλεγέθων.  
> („ Are formed Acheron, Styx, and Phlegethon.)

- *Ἐκ δὲ τούτων τῶν ῥοῶν* ‘and from these streams’ turns the subject *Lor corso* into a prepositional phrase of origin dependent on the next line. *Τούτων* is demonstrative rather than possessive, and *ῥοῶν* is genitive plural of *ῥοή*. *Ἐν τῇ κοιλάδι* ‘in the valley’ is static, with the dative of *κοιλάς*. The descent expressed by *si diroccia* has no corresponding verb.
- *Σχηματίζετ᾿ Ἀχέρων, Στὺξ καὶ Φλεγέθων* reverses active *fanno* and its three objects: the names are nominative subjects of *σχηματίζεται* (elided; 3rd sg. pres. pass. ind. of *σχηματίζω*), ‘is formed’. The preceding singular verb agrees with the first of the coordinated subjects, *Ἀχέρων*, rather than taking plural agreement with all three.

> 117 poi sen van giù per questa stretta doccia  

then, by this narrow conduit, go down

> 117 „ Δι’ αὔλακος δὲ στενῆς βαίνουσαι κάτω  
> („ Then going downward through a narrow channel,)

- *Δι᾿ αὔλακος δὲ στενῆς* ‘and through a narrow channel’ fronts the route. *Δι᾿* is elided *διά* with the genitive; *στενῆς* shows that *αὔλακος* (from *αὖλαξ*, ‘furrow, channel’) is treated as feminine. Postpositive *δέ* marks continuation without an explicit temporal adverb for *poi*; *questa* is not expressed.
- *Βαίνουσαι κάτω* ‘going down’ replaces finite *sen van giù* with the present active participle of *βαίνω* and a directional adverb. The nominative feminine plural picks up the streams, *ῥοαί*, by sense from genitive *ῥοῶν* in line 115, rather than agreeing with the three river names. Unlike the Italian, the Greek leaves the motion participial at the end of the extract.

## Lines 118–142 — The Rivers and the Unburnt Path

> 118 infin là dove più non si dismonta;  
> 119 fanno Cocito; e qual sia quello stagno,  
> 120 tu il vederai: però qui non si conta.”  

to where there is no more descent; they form Cocytus, and thou shalt see what kind of lake that is: here therefore I describe it not.”

> 118 „ Αἱ ῥοαὶ μέχρι τοῦ βυθοῦ τῆς ἀβύσσου,  
> („ The streams as far as the depth of the abyss,)
>
> 119 „ Ποιοῦσι τὸν Κωκυτόν· ἡ λίμνη δ’ αὕτη  
> („ Make Cocytus; and that lake,)
>
> 120 „ Τίς ἐστιν, ὄψει· τὰ νῦν οὐδὲν ἐρῶ σοι.”  
> („ What it is, you shall see; for now I will tell you nothing.”)

- *Αἱ ῥοαὶ μέχρι τοῦ βυθοῦ τῆς ἀβύσσου* ‘the streams as far as the bottom of the abyss’: Musurus supplies a nominative plural subject for *fanno* and replaces the impersonal relative clause *là dove più non si dismonta* with a limit expressed by *μέχρι* + genitive. *Τῆς ἀβύσσου* depends in turn on *τοῦ βυθοῦ*. There is no Greek negative here: ‘the bottom’ does the work of ‘no further’.
- *Ποιοῦσι τὸν Κωκυτόν* follows *fanno Cocito* closely: *ποιοῦσι* is the contracted 3rd plural present active indicative of *ποιέω*, with the river name as its accusative object.
- *Ἡ λίμνη δ’ αὕτη τίς ἐστιν, ὄψει* ‘and this lake, what it is, you will see’ fronts the subject of the indirect question, whereas *qual sia quello stagno* fronts the interrogative. *Αὕτη* is the feminine nominative singular demonstrative in the usual position outside the article–noun group; ‘this’ replaces *quello*. Predicative *τίς* agrees with *λίμνη*, and indicative *ἐστιν* replaces subjunctive *sia*. *Ὄψει* is the 2nd singular future middle indicative used as the future of *ὁράω*; its object is the question itself, without a pronoun resuming it like *il* in *tu il vederai*.
- *Τὰ νῦν οὐδὲν ἐρῶ σοι* ‘for the present I shall tell you nothing’ makes *qui* temporal rather than local. *Τὰ νῦν* is adverbial; *οὐδέν* is the neuter accusative object of *ἐρῶ*, the 1st singular future of ‘say’, and *σοι* is the dative of the person addressed. The impersonal present *non si conta* thus becomes a personal future; *però* has no expressed equivalent.

> 121 Ed io a lui: “Se il presente rigagno  
> 122 si deriva così dal nostro mondo,  
> 123 perchè ci appar pure a questo vivagno?”  

And I to him: “If the present rill thus flows down from our world, why does it appear to us only on this bank?”

> 121 Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν· “ Εἴπερ ὁ ῥύαξ οὗτος,  
> (And I to him: “If this stream,)
>
> 122 „ Ὡς φῄς, ἐκ τοῦ παρ’ ἡμῖν πηγάζει κόσμου,  
> („ As you say, takes its source from our world,)
>
> 123 „ Πῶς οὐ φαίνεται πλὴν εἰς τὸ χεῖλος τόδε;”  
> („ Why does it not appear except at this border?”)

- *Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν* reproduces *Ed io a lui*, including the absence of a verb of speaking. *Κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*; *πρός* takes the accusative of the person addressed.
- *Εἴπερ ὁ ῥύαξ οὗτος, ὡς φῄς* ‘if indeed this stream, as you say’: *-περ* reinforces the supposition introduced by *Se*. The post-articular demonstrative *οὗτος* renders the adjective *presente*, while *così* becomes a parenthetic clause. *Φῄς* is the 2nd singular present indicative of *φημί*.
- *Ἐκ τοῦ παρ’ ἡμῖν πηγάζει κόσμου* ‘springs from the world where we are’: active intransitive *πηγάζει* (3rd singular present of *πηγάζω*) expresses origin rather than the onward flow of *si deriva*. *Ἐκ* governs *τοῦ … κόσμου* across the intervening verb; attributive *παρ’ ἡμῖν* ‘among us’, with elision of *παρά* before the dative, replaces possessive *nostro*.
- *Πῶς οὐ φαίνεται πλὴν εἰς τὸ χεῖλος τόδε;* ‘how is it not visible except at this edge?’ recasts restrictive *pur* as the combination *οὐ … πλήν*. *Φαίνεται* is the 3rd singular present middle/passive of *φαίνω*, here intransitive ‘appears’; the persons to whom it appears (*ci*) are left unexpressed. *Εἰς* + accusative has the local force of Italian *a*, rather than its usual Classical directional force. *Τόδε* is neuter accusative singular of *ὅδε*, agreeing with *χεῖλος*.

> 125 e, tutto che tu sii venuto molto  
> 126 pur a sinistra giù calando al fondo,  
> 127 non se’ ancor per tutto il cerchio volto:  

And he to me: “Thou knowest that the place is round; and though thou hast come far, always to the left, descending towards the bottom, thou hast not yet turned through the entire circle:

> 124 Πρὸς ἐμὲ δ’ αὐτός· “ Οἶδας, ὅτι κυκλώδης  
> (And he to me: “You know that circular)
>
> 125 „ Ὁ τόπος οὗτος· σὺ δ’ ἐν τῇ καταβάσει,  
> („ Is this place; and you, in your descent,)
>
> 126 „ Καίτοι πρὸς τἀριστερὰ πολὺ βαδίσας,  
> („ Although you have walked far toward the left,)
>
> 127 „ Οὔπω διῆλθες ὁλόκληρον τὸν κύκλον.  
> („ Have not yet traversed the entire circle.)

- *Πρὸς ἐμὲ δ’ αὐτός* reverses the order of *Ed egli a me*: the full accusative *ἐμέ* stands after the preposition, postpositive *δέ* is elided before *αὐτός*, and the speaker is identified by nominative *αὐτός*. Again the verb of speaking is understood.
- *Οἶδας, ὅτι κυκλώδης ὁ τόπος οὗτος* ‘you know that this place is circular’: *οἶδας* is the analogical 2nd singular beside Attic *οἶσθα* of *οἶδα*, a perfect with present meaning. *Ὅτι* introduces its content. Musurus adds *οὗτος* to *il loco* and puts the predicate adjective *κυκλώδης* first, without an expressed copula.
- *Σὺ δ’ ἐν τῇ καταβάσει* ‘and you, in the descent’ introduces the main subject before the concession. The dative noun after *ἐν* replaces *giù calando*; the goal *al fondo* is not separately expressed. *Καίτοι … βαδίσας* replaces *tutto che tu sii venuto* with a concessive participle: *βαδίσας* is nominative masculine singular, aorist active of *βαδίζω*, in agreement with *σύ*.
- *Πρὸς τἀριστερὰ πολὺ βαδίσας* ‘having walked far toward the left’: *τἀριστερά* is crasis of *τὰ ἀριστερά*, the substantivized neuter plural after directional *πρός*. *Πολύ* is an adverbial accusative of extent; *pur* ‘always’ is not separately represented.
- *Οὔπω διῆλθες ὁλόκληρον τὸν κύκλον* ‘you have not yet traversed the circle entire’ changes *non se’ … volto* to a single active verb. *Διῆλθες* is the 2nd singular second aorist indicative of *διέρχομαι*, with augment after the preverb. Instead of *per tutto il cerchio*, Greek has an accusative of the space traversed; *ὁλόκληρον* stands outside the article, emphasizing entirety.

- *Εἴ τί σοι λοιπὸν ἀναφαίνεται νέον* ‘if, then, anything new appears to you’: adverbial *λοιπόν* takes up inferential *per che*. Indefinite *τι* is the neuter nominative subject, separated from its adjective *νέον* by the dative and verb; its printed accent in *τί σοι* is due to the following enclitic, not to interrogative force. *Ἀναφαίνεται*, 3rd singular present middle/passive of *ἀναφαίνω*, renders *apparisce*; singular *σοι* replaces plural *n’*.

Impersonal *δεῖ* governs present infinitive *δοκεῖν* (*δοκέω*), whose accusative subject is *σε*. Poetic *τεθηπότα* is the masculine accusative singular perfect active participle of *τέθηπα* ‘stand amazed’; it agrees with *σε* and expresses a state, not the act of bringing wonder.

- *Περὶ ταύτης οὐδὲν γὰρ εἶπας* ‘for about this latter you said nothing’ resolves *dell’ un* explicitly: feminine genitive *ταύτης*, governed by *περί*, refers to *Λήθη*. - *Πρὸς τὸ λουσθῆναι* renders the purpose infinitive *a lavarsi* by *πρός* with an articular infinitive in the accusative. *Λουσθῆναι* is the aorist passive infinitive of *λούω*, literally ‘to be washed’, where Italian marks the souls as washing themselves.
- *Ὅταν τὸ πταῖσμα μετάνοι’ ἐξαλείφῃ* ‘whenever repentance wipes out the fault’ makes *μετάνοι’* (elided *μετάνοια*, nominative singular) the agent and *τὸ πταῖσμα* the accusative object. Italian instead qualifies *colpa* with *pentuta* and makes it the subject of passive *è rimossa*. *Ὅταν* (*ὅτε* + *ἄν*) takes the 3rd singular present active subjunctive *ἐξαλείφῃ* (*ἐξαλείφω*) in an indefinite temporal clause.

> 139 Poi disse: “Omai è tempo da scostarsi  
> 140 dal bosco; fa che di retro a me vegne;  

Then he said: “Now it is time to quit the wood; see that thou follow me;

> 139 Εἶτ’ εἶπε· “Καιρὸς ἐξελθεῖν τοῦ δρυμῶνος·  
> (Then he said: “It is time to go forth from the forest;)
>
> 140 „ Σοὶ δ’ ἕπεσθαί μοι κατόπι νῦν μελέτω.  
> („ And let it now be your care to follow behind me.)

- *Εἶτ’ εἶπε* ‘then he said’ retains *Poi disse*: *εἶτ’* is elided *εἶτα*, and *εἶπε* is the 3rd singular second aorist active indicative of ‘say’. *Καιρὸς ἐξελθεῖν τοῦ δρυμῶνος* ‘[it is] time to go out of the wood’ omits the copula of *è tempo*. *Ἐξελθεῖν*, second aorist active infinitive of *ἐξέρχομαι*, has no augment in the infinitive; the genitive of separation *τοῦ δρυμῶνος* corresponds to *dal bosco*.
- *Σοὶ δ’ ἕπεσθαί μοι κατόπι νῦν μελέτω* ‘and let it now be your care to follow behind me’ recasts *fa che … vegne*. Instead of a 2nd person command with a subordinate subjunctive, *μελέτω* is a 3rd singular present active imperative of *μέλω*, with the person concerned in the emphatic dative *σοί* and the action of concern expressed by an infinitive. *Νῦν* puts the equivalent of *Omai* in this second clause.
- *Ἕπεσθαι* is the present middle infinitive of *ἕπομαι* ‘follow’, governing dative *μοι*; its additional final accent in *ἕπεσθαί μοι* is due to the enclitic. Adverbial *κατόπι* makes *di retro* explicit, while ‘follow’ itself replaces *vegne* ‘come’.

> 141 li margini fan via, che non son arsi,  
> 142 e sopra loro ogni vapor si spegne.”  

the margins which are not burning, form a path and over them all fire is quenched.”

> 141 „ Ὁδευτόν ἐστι τὸ χεῖλος, ὡς οὐ καῖον·  
> („ The margin is passable, since it does not burn;)
>
> 142 „ Πᾶς γὰρ ἀτμὸς ἐπ’ αὐτοῦ σβέννυται πίπτων.”  
> („ For every vapour falling upon it is quenched.”)

- *Ὁδευτόν ἐστι τὸ χεῖλος* ‘the edge is traversable’ substitutes a predicate verbal adjective in *-τός* for *fan via*. The substantivized idea of a path thus becomes a property of *τὸ χεῖλος*, neuter nominative singular in place of plural *li margini*.
- *Ὡς οὐ καῖον* ‘since it does not burn’ interprets *che non son arsi* causally. *Καῖον* is the present active participle of *καίω*, neuter nominative singular agreeing with *χεῖλος*: the Italian passive form is replaced by an active description. *Ὡς* presents the participial phrase as the reason given for traversability.
- *Πᾶς γὰρ ἀτμὸς ἐπ’ αὐτοῦ σβέννυται πίπτων* ‘for every vapour is quenched as it falls upon it’ changes coordinating *e* to explanatory *γάρ*. *Πᾶς … ἀτμός* keeps the singular of *ogni vapor* and its noun ‘vapour’, rather than the prose rendering “all fire”. *Ἐπ’ αὐτοῦ* (*ἐπί* + genitive) adapts *sopra loro* to the singular *χεῖλος*. *Σβέννυται* is the 3rd singular present middle/passive indicative of *σβέννυμι*, corresponding to *si spegne*; the added *πίπτων*, present active participle of *πίπτω*, agrees with *ἀτμός* and makes the attendant action of falling explicit.

## Summary — From Action to Attribute

Musurus often keeps Dante’s order of actions while changing which one receives a finite verb. At the opening, *raunai* becomes the aorist participle *συλλέξας* ‘having gathered’, leaving *ἀπέδωκα* ‘I gave back’ as the main assertion. At lines 67–68 the redistribution runs the other way: *si rivolse* becomes *στραφείς* ‘having turned’, while *dicendo* becomes *εἶπεν* ‘he said’. Present and perfect participles also let Musurus replace a relative clause with a description. The most striking instance is *ἐστερημένην* ‘bereft’: where Dante’s plain actively repels every plant, the Greek perfect describes its resulting condition, and *ἐσᾳεί* ‘for ever’ adds a permanence that Dante leaves implicit.

Particles often make relations explicit without reproducing the Italian connective. In the grouping of the souls, *μέν … δέ* distinguishes one set from another; in the description of Ida, the same pair sets former abundance against present desolation. Repeated *κἄν* ‘even if’, with the subjunctive, gives each of Capaneus’s concessions its own opening; the answering *ἂν … ἕξοι* ‘he would have’ is notable for its unusual future optative. Musurus can also change the relation itself: *ὅπως* with *κατασβεσθείη* ‘might be extinguished’ turns Dante’s explanation of why the trampling worked into a statement of its purpose.

The Greek sometimes follows Italian syntax closely enough to preserve a distinctive turn: *τὰ νῶτ᾽ ἐστραμμέν᾽ ἔχων* ‘having his back turned’ keeps the object and resultative participle of *tien volte le spalle*. Elsewhere Musurus gives the sense he finds in an image rather than the image itself. *Μαλάσσω* ‘soften’ interprets *maturare* ‘ripen’; *ποινὴν ἀποχρῶσαν* ‘a sufficient punishment’ replaces the ironic *debiti fregi* ‘due ornaments’, making the judgment explicit and dispensing with the breast they adorn. Such departures are not always metaphorical. At line 90, *ἔνδον* ‘within’ replaces *sopra sè* ‘above itself’, although the closing *ἐπ᾽ αὐτοῦ* ‘upon it’ restores the spatial relation at the point where Dante himself returns to it.
