# Inferno 22

## Lines 1–18 — Horsemen, Signals, and the Demons’ March

> 1 Io vidi già cavalier muover campo,  
> 2 e cominciare stormo, e far lor mostra,  
> 3 e talvolta partir per loro scampo;  

I have ere now seen horsemen moving camp, and commencing the assault, and holding their muster, and at times retiring to escape;

> 1 Εἶδον ἱππεῖς ἐν τάξει πορευομένους  
> (I saw horsemen marching in order,)
>
> 2 Καὶ μάχης ἀρχομένους, πομπῇ τ᾽ ἰόντας,  
> (And beginning battle, and going in parade,)
>
> 3 Ἐνίοτε δὲ φυγῇ παλινδρομοῦντας·  
> (And sometimes running back in flight;)

- *Εἶδον ἱππεῖς* ‘I saw horsemen’ begins with the verb: *εἶδον* is the suppletive second aorist active indicative of *ὁράω*, here 1st singular. Greek needs no separate subject corresponding to *Io*, and gives *già* no separate equivalent. *Ἱππεῖς* is accusative plural of *ἱππεύς*, identical in form to the nominative plural.
- Italian has *vidi* with *cavalier* and a series of infinitives; Greek has the regular construction of direct perception, *εἶδον* with an object and participles agreeing with it. *Πορευομένους*, *ἀρχομένους*, *ἰόντας*, and *παλινδρομοῦντας* are all masculine accusative plural; their present stems present the actions as in progress when seen.
- *Ἐν τάξει πορευομένους* ‘marching in formation’ interprets *muover campo* as an ordered movement of the men: *ἐν* with the dative singular of *τάξις* supplies the manner, but there is no word for *campo*. *Πορευομένους* is present middle of *πορεύομαι*. In *μάχης ἀρχομένους* ‘beginning battle’, the middle of *ἄρχω* in the sense ‘begin’ takes a genitive where *cominciare stormo* has a direct object.
- *Πομπῇ τ᾽ ἰόντας* ‘and going in procession’ turns *far lor mostra* into another verb of motion: *ἰόντας* is the present active participle of *εἶμι* ‘go’, with *πομπῇ* as a dative of manner. The possessive *lor* disappears. Postpositive *τε*, elided before *ἰόντας*, links this action to the preceding one.
- *Ἐνίοτε δὲ φυγῇ παλινδρομοῦντας* ‘and sometimes running back in flight’ places *ἐνίοτε* first, with connective *δέ* second. *Παλινδρομοῦντας* is the contracted present active participle of *παλινδρομέω*. It makes the backward movement more explicit than *partir*; the dative of manner *φυγῇ* replaces the purpose phrase *per loro scampo*.

> 4 corridor vidi per la terra vostra,  
> 5 o Aretini, e vidi gir gualdane,  
> 6 ferir torneamenti, e correr giostra,  

coursers have I seen upon your land, O Aretines! and seen the march of foragers, the shock of tournaments and race of jousts,

> 4 Ἐκδρόμους εἶδον διὰ τῆς ὑμῶν χώρας,  
> (Foragers I saw across your land,)
>
> 5 Ἀῤῥητῖνοι, καὶ στροφὰς ἐν ἱππασίαις·  
> (O Aretines, and wheelings in cavalry charges;)
>
> 6 Εἶδον ἱππικοὺς καὶ γυμνικοὺς ἀγῶνας,  
> (I saw equestrian and athletic contests,)

- *Ἐκδρόμους εἶδον* preserves the object-first order of *corridor vidi*. *Ἐκδρόμους* (accusative plural of *ἔκδρομος*, used substantivally) denotes men who run or sally out; Musurus takes *corridor* as raiders. *Διὰ τῆς ὑμῶν χώρας* gives *per* its spatial sense ‘through’, with *διά* and the genitive. The possessive genitive *ὑμῶν* ‘of you’ stands between article and noun, in contrast to postposed *vostra*.
- *Ἀῤῥητῖνοι* is a vocative plural, in the same position across the line break as *o Aretini*, but without a vocative particle. The successive smooth and rough breathings on doubled *ῤῥ* are a traditional spelling of medial double rho.
- *Καὶ στροφὰς ἐν ἱππασίαις* ‘and turns in cavalry exercises’ is a second object of *εἶδον*. Where *vidi gir gualdane* has a second verb of seeing and an infinitive of motion, Greek gives the movement itself a noun, *στροφάς* (accusative plural of *στροφή*), qualified by *ἐν* with the dative plural of *ἱππασία*. This is not the same interpretation as “the march of foragers”: it is in *Ἐκδρόμους*, earlier, that Musurus has named the raiders.
- The repeated *Εἶδον* now introduces line 6. *Ἱππικοὺς καὶ γυμνικοὺς ἀγῶνας* ‘equestrian and athletic contests’ has one accusative plural noun (*ἀγών*) with two coordinated adjectives. In place of the distinct actions *ferir* and *correr*, each with its own kind of contest, Musurus names two classes of contest; *γυμνικούς* introduces a specifically athletic category.

> 7 quando con trombe, e quando con campane,  
> 8 con tamburi e con cenni di castella,  
> 9 e con cose nostrali e con istrane:  

now with trumpets, and now with bells, with drums and castle-signals, and with native things and foreign:

> 7 Ποῦ μὲν σάλπιγξι, ποῦ δὲ κωδώνων ψόφοις,  
> (Now with trumpets, now with the din of bells,)
>
> 8 Διὰ τυμπάνων καὶ σημείων ἐκ πύργων,  
> (With drums and signals from towers,)
>
> 9 Ὀργάνων τ᾽ ἀλλοδαπῶν ἡμεδαπῶν τε·  
> (And with instruments both foreign and native;)

- *Ποῦ μὲν … ποῦ δέ* gives a spatial or distributive turn to temporal *quando … quando*: ‘in one place … in another’. The accented *ποῦ* is ordinarily interrogative ‘where?’, as distinct from enclitic indefinite *που*; it is the paired particles *μέν … δέ* that make the alternatives clear here.
- *Σάλπιγξι* is the dative plural of *σάλπιγξ*, without movable *ν*; the dative alone expresses instrumental *con*. In *κωδώνων ψόφοις* ‘with sounds of bells’, it is the added *ψόφοις* (dative plural of *ψόφος*) that carries this construction; the bells are in the dependent genitive plural.
- Musurus varies the expression of *con* by switching to *διὰ τυμπάνων καὶ σημείων* ‘by means of drums and signals’, *διά* with coordinated genitives. *Ἐκ πύργων* specifies the source of the signals, ‘from towers’, for the less explicit relation in *cenni di castella*.
- *Ὀργάνων* ‘instruments’, still governed by *διά*, narrows the indefinite *cose*. *Ἀλλοδαπῶν ἡμεδαπῶν τε* ‘foreign and of our own land’ reverses *nostrali … istrane*. Both adjectives are genitive neuter plural agreeing with *ὀργάνων*; the first *τ᾽* (elided *τε*) adds the instruments to the list, the final *τε* links the two adjectives.

> 10 nè già con sì diversa cennamella  
> 11 cavalier vidi muover, nè pedoni,  
> 12 nè nave a segno di terra o di stella.  

but never yet to so uncouth a cornet saw I cavaliers nor footmen move, nor ship by mark of land or star.

> 10 Ἀλλ᾽ οὔποτ᾽ εἶδον αὐλῷ τοιῷδ᾽ ἐκτόπῳ  
> (But never did I see to so strange a pipe)
>
> 11 Ἱππέα κινούμενον οὔτε πεζίτην,  
> (Either horseman moving or foot-soldier,)
>
> 12 Οὔτε πλοῖον ὑπ᾽ ἄστρων ἢ γῆς σημείων.  
> (Or ship by signs of stars or of land.)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ οὔποτ᾽ εἶδον* ‘but never did I see’ puts the verb before the phrase about the instrument, rather than after it as in *nè già con sì diversa cennamella / cavalier vidi*. Both *ἀλλά* and *οὔποτε* lose their final vowel before a vowel. Adversative *ἀλλά* marks the turn from all the things seen to the one never seen.
- *Αὐλῷ τοιῷδ᾽ ἐκτόπῳ* ‘to a pipe of this strange sort’ is an instrumental dative singular. *Αὐλός* renders *cennamella* as a pipe; *ἔκτοπος*, literally ‘out of place’, takes *diversa* to mean ‘outlandish’, not simply ‘different’. *Τοιῷδ᾽* is elided *τοιῷδε*, from the qualitative demonstrative *τοιόσδε*; the demonstrative and adjective both agree with *αὐλῷ*.
- *Ἱππέα κινούμενον* returns to the object and participle construction after *εἶδον*: *ἱππέα* is accusative singular of *ἱππεύς*, and *κινούμενον* is the present middle/passive participle of *κινέω*, here intransitive in sense, ‘moving’. The singular gives an individual instance in place of the collective reference of *cavalier*; *πεζὶτην* (*πεζίτης*, ‘foot-soldier’) likewise stands in the singular for plural *pedoni*.
- After *οὔποτε*, repeated *οὔτε* extends the negation to foot-soldier and ship. *Πλοῖον* is accusative neuter singular; the thought of moving continues without repetition of the participle (which would require neuter agreement if expressed). *Ὑπ᾽* is *ὑπό* elided before smooth-breathed *ἄστρων*. *Ὑπὸ … σημείων* with the genitive suggests movement guided by signs; *ἄστρων ἢ γῆς* ‘of stars or of land’ depends on *σημείων*. Musurus pluralizes both *segno* and *stella* and reverses *terra o … stella*.

> 13 Noi andavam con li dieci dimoni;  

We went with the ten Demons:

> 13 Μετὰ τῶν δέκα προὐχωροῦμεν δαιμόνων·  
> (With the ten demons we were advancing;)

- *Μετὰ τῶν δέκα … δαιμόνων* fronts the accompaniment expressed by *con li dieci dimoni*. *Μετά* takes the genitive; the article and *δαιμόνων* (genitive plural of *δαίμων*) enclose the indeclinable numeral *δέκα*, but the verb intervenes before the noun.
- *Προὐχωροῦμεν* ‘we were advancing’ is 1st plural imperfect active indicative of *προχωρέω*. The internal augment after *προ-* coalesces with its vowel (*προ-ε-* → *προὐ-*); the ending also shows the regular contraction of an *-έω* verb. The imperfect matches *andavam*, while the prefix makes forward movement explicit. The personal ending suffices for *Noi*.

> 14 ahi fiera compagnia! ma nella chiesa  
> 15 coi santi ed in taverna coi ghiottoni.  

ah, hideous company! but, ‘In church with saints, and with guzzlers in the tavern.’

> 14 Ὦ φρικτῆς συνοδίας! πλήν—σὺν ἁγίοις  
> (O dreadful company! yet—with saints)
>
> 15 Ἐν ναῷ, σὺν λαιμάργοις ἐν καπηλείῳ.—  
> (In church, with gluttons in a tavern.—)

- *Ὦ φρικτῆς συνοδίας!* ‘O dreadful company!’ uses *ὦ* with an exclamatory genitive, not a vocative addressing the company. Both *φρικτῆς* and *συνοδίας* are feminine genitive singular; *συνοδία* expresses companionship on a journey.
- *Πλήν* stands independently as the adversative ‘yet’ for *ma*, not as a preposition governing a genitive. What follows is verbless, as in Italian. Musurus makes the two halves parallel: *σὺν ἁγίοις ἐν ναῷ, σὺν λαιμάργοις ἐν καπηλείῳ* puts companions before location both times, reversing *nella chiesa / coi santi* but retaining the order of *coi ghiottoni* relative to its location only in sense, not in position: Italian has *in taverna* first there too.
- *Σύν* governs the substantivized masculine dative plurals *ἁγίοις* ‘holy ones’ and *λαιμάργοις* ‘gluttons’; *ἐν* governs the dative singulars *ναῷ* and *καπηλείῳ*. All four nouns or substantives are anarthrous. The comma and repeated prepositions do the work of *ed*, without a Greek coordinating conjunction.

> 16 Pure alla pegola era la mia intesa,  
> 17 per veder della bolgia ogni contegno,  
> 18 e della gente ch’ entro v’ era incesa.  

Yet my intent was on the pitch, to see each habit of the chasm and of the people that were burning in it.

> 16 Ἐγὼ δ᾽ ἀτενὴς πρὸς τὴν πίσσαν προσεῖχον,  
> (And I kept my gaze intently on the pitch,)
>
> 17 Ὅπως ἂν ἴδω τἄνδον τοῦ βόθρου πάντα  
> (So that I might see all things inside the pit)
>
> 18 Καὶ τοὺς ἐν αὐτῷ συγκατακαιομένους.  
> (And those who were burning together within it.)

- *Ἐγὼ δ᾽ … προσεῖχον* turns *era la mia intesa* into a personal action, ‘but I was paying attention’. *Ἐγώ* is explicit and contrastive; postpositive *δέ*, elided before *ἀτενής*, renders the transition *Pure*. *Προσεῖχον* is 1st singular imperfect active indicative of *προσέχω*, with the augment of *ἔχω* (*εἶχον*) inside the compound.
- *Ἀτενής* ‘intent, fixed’ is a nominative singular adjective predicated of the speaker, not an adverb modifying the verb. *Πρὸς τὴν πίσσαν* gives the direction of attention by *πρός* with the accusative for *alla pegola*. No noun meaning ‘gaze’ is expressed.
- *Ὅπως ἂν ἴδω* ‘so that I may see’ replaces the purpose infinitive *per veder* with a finite purpose clause. *Ἴδω* is 1st singular second aorist active subjunctive of *ὁράω*, from the same aorist stem as *εἶδον* but without the indicative augment. *Ὅπως* with *ἄν* can introduce a prospective purpose with the subjunctive; the past main verb does not require a change to the optative.
- *Τἄνδον* is crasis of *τὰ ἔνδον*: the neuter plural article substantivizes the adverb ‘within’. *Τοῦ βόθρου* specifies the pit whose interior is meant, and the postponed accusative neuter plural *πάντα* gathers up the object, ‘all that is inside the pit’. This replaces *ogni contegno* with things observed, rather than naming a habit or manner.
- *Καὶ τοὺς ἐν αὐτῷ συγκατακαιομένους* adds a second direct object of *ἴδω*. Instead of *della gente* depending on *contegno*, Greek has ‘and those … being burned’, an articular present passive participle, masculine accusative plural, of *συγκατακαίω*. The participle replaces the relative clause *ch’ entro v’ era incesa* and presents the burning as ongoing; the prefix *συγ-* adds ‘together’. Within the article-participle group, *ἐν αὐτῷ* ‘in it’ refers back to masculine *βόθρος*.

## Lines 19–51 — The Sinners Surface and One Is Caught

> 19 Come i delfini, quando fanno segno  
> 20 ai marinar con l' arco della schiena,  
> 21 che s' argomentin di campar lor legno:  

As dolphins, when with the arch of the back they make sign to mariners that they may prepare to save their ship:

> 19 Ὡς οἱ δελφῖνες προμηνύοντες ζάλην  
> (As dolphins foretelling a storm)
>
> 20 Ναυτιλομένοις τῇ κυρτώσει τῶν νώτων  
> (To sailors by the arching of their backs)
>
> 21 Πρὸς τὴν ἔγκαιρον ἀσφάλειαν τοῦ πλοίου,  
> (For the timely safety of the ship,)

- *Ὡς οἱ δελφῖνες προμηνύοντες ζάλην* ‘as dolphins foretelling a storm’ opens a comparison answered by *Οὕτως* in 22. For the finite temporal clause *quando fanno segno*, Musurus has *προμηνύοντες* (nom. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *προμηνύω*), agreeing with *δελφῖνες*. There is no finite verb in this half of the comparison. The prefix *προ-* makes the sign a warning in advance; the accusative *ζάλην* supplies what it foretells.
- *Ναυτιλομένοις* ‘to those at sea’ renders *ai marinar* by a substantival present middle participle (dat. masc. pl.; the verb is also spelled *ναυτίλλομαι*). This dative names the recipients; *τῇ κυρτώσει* (dat. sg. of *κύρτωσις*) is instrumental, ‘by the arching’. The action noun replaces *l’ arco*, and the genitive plural *τῶν νώτων* distributes the Italian singular *della schiena* over the dolphins.
- *Πρὸς τὴν ἔγκαιρον ἀσφάλειαν τοῦ πλοίου* ‘for the timely safety of the ship’ turns *che s’ argomentin di campar lor legno* from a purpose clause with subjunctive and dependent infinitive into *πρός* with an accusative of purpose. Timeliness is expressed by the attributive adjective *ἔγκαιρον*; there is no separate verb for the sailors’ exertion. *Πλοίου* names the ship directly instead of using *legno* ‘wood’, and the article does duty for *lor*.

> 22 talor così ad alleggiar la pena  
> 23 mostrava alcun dei peccatori il dosso,  
> 24 e nascondeva in men che non balena.  

so now and then, to ease the punishment, some sinner showed his back and hid in less time than it lightens.

> 22 Οὕτως ἔσθ᾽ ὅτε πρὸς κουφισμὸν τοῦ πάθους  
> (So sometimes, for relief of their suffering,)
>
> 23 Τῶν ἁμαρτωλῶν ἐδείκνυ τις τὴν ῥάχιν,  
> (One of the sinners would show his back,)
>
> 24 Ἐκρύπτετο δ᾽ ἀστραπῆς τάχιον πάλιν.  
> (And hide himself again swifter than lightning.)

- *Οὕτως ἔσθ᾽ ὅτε* takes up *talor così* in reverse order: first the answering ‘so’, then the idiom ‘there are times when’. *Ἔσθ᾽* is *ἔστι* with final vowel elided and *τ* aspirated before the rough breathing of *ὅτε*. *Πρὸς κουφισμὸν τοῦ πάθους* ‘for relief of the suffering’ again uses *πρός* with an action noun for an Italian purposive infinitive, *ad alleggiar la pena*.
- *Τῶν ἁμαρτωλῶν ἐδείκνυ τις τὴν ῥάχιν* fronts the partitive genitive belonging to indefinite *τις* ‘one’, where Italian has *alcun dei peccatori* together after the verb. *Ἐδείκνυ* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *δείκνυμι*) gives the recurrent action of *mostrava*; with a part of the body the article in *τὴν ῥάχιν* suffices for ‘his’.
- *Ἐκρύπτετο* (3rd sg. imperf. mid./pass. ind. of *κρύπτω*) gives *nascondeva* the sense ‘hid himself’, not ‘hid his back’. Postpositive *δ᾽* (elided *δέ*) marks the turn, and *πάλιν* ‘again’ supplies the return movement. *Ἀστραπῆς τάχιον* ‘faster than lightning’ replaces the finite comparison *in men che non balena* with a genitive of comparison and the comparative neuter *τάχιον* used adverbially; the Italian comparative *non* has no Greek counterpart.

> 25 E come all' orlo dell' acqua d' un fosso  
> 26 stanno i ranocchi pur col muso fuori,  
> 27 sì che celano i piedi e l' altro grosso :  

And as at the edge of the water of a ditch, the frogs stand only with their muzzles out, so that they hide their feet and other bulk:

> 25 Ὡς δὲ παρὰ τὸ χεῖλος ὑδάτων τάφρου  
> (And as along the brink of the waters of a ditch)
>
> 26 Προΐσχουσ᾽ οἱ βάτραχοι τὸ ῥύγχος ἔξω,  
> (Frogs thrust their snout outside,)
>
> 27 Κρύπτοντες πόδας καὶ πᾶν τὸ λοιπὸν σῶμα,  
> (Hiding their feet and all the rest of their body,)

- *Ὡς δὲ παρὰ τὸ χεῖλος ὑδάτων τάφρου* ‘and as along the brink of the waters of a ditch’ retains the succession of nouns in *all’ orlo dell’ acqua d’ un fosso*. *Παρά* with the accusative gives the line along which the frogs are ranged; *ὑδάτων* (gen. pl. of *ὕδωρ*) depends on *χεῖλος*, and *τάφρου* on *ὑδάτων*.
- *Προΐσχουσ᾽ οἱ βάτραχοι τὸ ῥύγχος ἔξω* substitutes an active verb, ‘the frogs hold their snout out’, for *stanno i ranocchi pur col muso fuori*. *Προΐσχουσ᾽* is elided *προΐσχουσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *προΐσχω*); the diaeresis keeps the vowels of the prefix and stem distinct. *Τὸ ῥύγχος* is distributive singular, as is *il muso*. There is no separate ‘only’ for *pur*.
- *Κρύπτοντες* (nom. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *κρύπτω*) agrees with *βάτραχοι*: the concealment is simultaneous with holding the snout out, rather than a result introduced by *sì che*. Its objects are *πόδας* (acc. pl. of *πούς*) and *πᾶν τὸ λοιπὸν σῶμα* ‘the whole remaining body’, which makes *l’ altro grosso* explicit.

> 28 sì stavan d' ogni parte i peccatori;  

thus stood on every hand the sinners;

> 28 Τοιάδ᾽ ἦν πέριξ ἁμαρτωλῶν ἡ στάσις.  
> (Such was the posture of the sinners all around.)

- *Τοιάδ᾽ ἦν πέριξ ἁμαρτωλῶν ἡ στάσις* ‘such was the sinners’ posture all around’ converts *sì stavan* into a copula and a noun. *Τοιάδ᾽* is elided feminine singular *τοιάδε*, the predicate of *ἡ στάσις*; hence singular *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. of *εἰμί*) in place of plural *stavan*. *Πέριξ* is adverbial for *d’ ogni parte*; *ἁμαρτωλῶν*, now possessive genitive rather than subject, precedes its noun.

> 29 ma come s' appressava Barbariccia,  
> 30 così si ritraean sotto i bollori.  

but as Barbariccia approached, they instantly retired beneath the seething.

> 29 Ἀλλ᾽ ἅμ᾽ ἐλθόντος ἐγγὺς Βαρβαρικίου,  
> (But as soon as Barbariccia came near,)
>
> 30 Ἐβυθίσθησαν εἰς τὴν βράζουσαν πίσσαν.  
> (They plunged down into the boiling pitch.)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ ἅμ᾽ ἐλθόντος ἐγγὺς Βαρβαρικίου* ‘but just as Barbariccia came near’ replaces *come s’ appressava Barbariccia* with a genitive absolute. *Ἐλθόντος* (gen. masc. sg. second aor. act. participle of *ἔρχομαι*) agrees with the Hellenized *Βαρβαρικίου*; the aorist takes the approach as an event, while *ἐγγύς* supplies ‘near’. Both *ἀλλά* and *ἅμα* are elided. *Ἅμα* closely connects the actions without an answering word for *così*.
- *Ἐβυθίσθησαν* (3rd pl. aor. pass. ind. of *βυθίζω*) is passive in form, ‘were submerged’, with the effect of intransitive ‘sank’. The two Italian imperfects describing what regularly happened thus give way to two aorist forms. *Εἰς τὴν βράζουσαν πίσσαν* gives a destination, ‘into the boiling pitch’, for *sotto i bollori*: *βράζουσαν* (acc. fem. sg. pres. act. participle of *βράζω*) qualifies the supplied noun *πίσσαν* (Attic *πίτταν*).

> 31 Io vidi, ed anco il cor me n' accapriccia,  
> 32 uno aspettar così, com' egli incontra  
> 33 che una rana rimane ed altra spiccia;  

I saw, and my heart still shudders thereat, one linger so, as it will happen that one frog remains while the other spouts away;

> 31 Εἶδον, ἀλλ᾽ ἀκμὴν ἡ καρδία μου τρέμει,  
> (I saw—yet still my heart trembles—)
>
> 32 Τινὰ βραδὺν μένοντα, καθὼς συμβαίνει  
> (One lingering behind, just as happens)
>
> 33 Βατράχοις τὸν μὲν ἵστασθαι, τὸν δὲ φεύγειν.  
> (With frogs, that one stays while another flees.)

- *Εἶδον* (here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*) waits, like *Io vidi*, for its object until the next line. In the parenthesis *ἀλλ᾽ ἀκμὴν ἡ καρδία μου τρέμει*, elided *ἀλλά* gives a stronger turn than *ed*; *ἀκμήν*, originally an accusative noun, has its later adverbial sense ‘still’. *Μου* is the enclitic possessive genitive. *Τρέμει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *τρέμω*) simply says ‘trembles’, without an equivalent for the backward reference in *ne*.
- *Τινὰ βραδὺν μένοντα* ‘someone slow to move, remaining’ has an accusative object and a supplementary participle after a verb of perception: *μένοντα* (acc. masc. sg. pres. act. of *μένω*) agrees with *τινά*. Italian instead has *uno* with the infinitive *aspettar*. The adjective *βραδύν* characterizes the person; there is no separate adverb for *così*.
- *Καθὼς συμβαίνει βατράχοις* ‘as happens with frogs’ puts the frogs first as a generic dative plural with impersonal *συμβαίνει*. In place of the finite clause after *incontra*, the specification is infinitival: *τὸν μὲν ἵστασθαι, τὸν δὲ φεύγειν*. The articles are pronominal accusative subjects, contrasted by *μέν … δέ*, ‘the one … the other’; *ἵστασθαι* (pres. mid. inf. of *ἵστημι*) gives ‘stay in place’, *φεύγειν* (pres. act. inf. of *φεύγω*) the less specific ‘flee’ for *spiccia*.

> 34 e Graffiacan, che gli era più d' incontra,  
> 35 gli arroncigliò le impegolate chiome,  
> 36 e trassel su, che mi parve una lontra.  

and Graffiacane, who was nearest to him, hooked his pitchy locks and haled him up, so that to me he seemed an otter.

> 34 Γραφικάτης δὲ παρευρεθεὶς πλησίον  
> (And Graffiacane, happening to be close by,)
>
> 35 Ἐξήρπασ’ αὐτὸν τῆς πισσαλοιφοῦς κόμης,  
> (Snatched him up by his pitch-anointed hair,)
>
> 36 Ἀνύψωσέ τε, καθάπερ ἐνυδρίδα.  
> (And hoisted him up, just like an otter.)

- *Γραφικάτης δὲ παρευρεθεὶς πλησίον* renders the name as a Greek masculine nominative and the relative clause *che gli era più d’ incontra* as a participial phrase. *Παρευρεθείς* (nom. masc. sg. aor. pass. of *παρευρίσκω*) is literally ‘having been found’, hence ‘happening to be’; *πλησίον* ‘nearby’ lacks both the comparison in *più* and an expressed reference to ‘him’.
- *Ἐξήρπασ᾽ αὐτὸν τῆς πισσαλοιφοῦς κόμης* ‘snatched him by the pitch-smeared hair’ shifts the direct object from *le impegolate chiome* to *αὐτόν*. The part seized is genitive, with singular collective *κόμης* and its agreeing compound adjective *πισσαλοιφοῦς*. *Ἐξήρπασ᾽* is elided *ἐξήρπασε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐξαρπάζω*, augmented after the prefix); it conveys forcible seizure but not the hook implied in *arroncigliò*.
- *Ἀνύψωσέ τε* ‘and lifted [him] up’ continues with a second aorist indicative in the sense of a second completed action (*ἀνύψωσε*, 3rd sg. first aor. act. of *ἀνυψόω*; the extra accent is due to enclitic *τε*). *Καθάπερ ἐνυδρίδα* ‘just like an otter’ reduces *che mi parve una lontra* to a comparison: *ἐνυδρίδα* (acc. sg. of *ἐνυδρίς*) corresponds in case to the object lifted, with neither a verb of seeming nor a first-person observer expressed.

> 37 Io sapea già di tutti e quanti il nome,  
> 38 sì li notai quando furono eletti,  
> 39 e poi che si chiamaro attesi come.  

I already knew the name of every one, so well I noted them as they were chosen, and when they called each other, listened how.

> 37 ᾜδειν τοὔνομα τῶν δαιμόνων ἑκάστου,  
> (I knew the name of each of the demons,)
>
> 38 Ἐπεὶ τῆς αὐτῶν ἐκλογῆς μνείαν εἶχον,  
> (Since I kept remembrance of their selection,)
>
> 39 Προσεῖχόν τ’ αὐτοῖς ἀλλήλους προσφωνοῦσιν·  
> (And I paid heed to them as they called to one another:)

- *ᾜδειν* is the 1st sg. pluperfect indicative of *οἶδα*, whose perfect has present sense ‘know’; thus the pluperfect gives the past state *sapea*, without a separate *già*. *Τοὔνομα* is crasis of *τὸ ὄνομα*. The genitives in *τῶν δαιμόνων ἑκάστου* work at two levels: ‘the name of each’, and ‘each of the demons’. *Ἑκάστου* makes *tutti e quanti* distributive, and *δαιμόνων* supplies the noun.
- *Ἐπεὶ τῆς αὐτῶν ἐκλογῆς μνείαν εἶχον* gives a reason, ‘since I retained a remembrance of their selection’, for *sì li notai quando furono eletti*. *Εἶχον* is here 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἔχω*, with *μνείαν* its object and *τῆς … ἐκλογῆς* the genitive of what is remembered. The event of the passive temporal clause becomes the noun *ἐκλογῆς*; *αὐτῶν* denotes those selected.
- *Προσεῖχόν τ᾽ αὐτοῖς ἀλλήλους προσφωνοῦσιν* ‘and I attended to them addressing one another’ replaces the temporal clause and *attesi come* with a single construction. *Προσεῖχον* (1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *προσέχω*, with internal augment) governs dative *αὐτοῖς*; *προσφωνοῦσιν* is an agreeing dat. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *προσφωνέω*, not a finite verb here. Reciprocal *ἀλλήλους* is its accusative object. *Τ᾽* is elided enclitic *τε*; there is no separate ‘how’.

> 40 " O Rubicante, fa che tu gli metti  
> 41 gli unghioni addosso sì che tu lo scuoi,"  
> 42 gridavan tutti insieme i maledetti.  

“O Rubicante, see thou plant thy clutches on him, and flay him!” shouted together all the accursed crew.

> 40 —“Ὦ Ῥουβικάντα, τῇ σῇ βάλλ’ αὐτὸν ἅρπῃ  
> (—“O Rubicante, strike him with your hook)
>
> 41 „ Ἐπὶ τῶν νώτων, ἐκδερμάτισον τοῦτον,” —  
> („ Upon the back, flay this fellow,”—)
>
> 42 Ἔκραζον σύναμ’ οἱ κατάρατοι πάντες.  
> (All the accursed ones cried out together.)

- *Ὦ Ῥουβικάντα* marks direct address with *ὦ* and a masculine first-declension vocative in *-α*. *Τῇ σῇ βάλλ᾽ αὐτὸν ἅρπῃ* separates attributive *τῇ σῇ* from its noun *ἅρπῃ*, instrumental dative singular, ‘with your hook’. Thus the plural *gli unghioni*, the object to be laid on him, becomes a single implement; *αὐτόν* is the direct object of ‘strike’.
- *Βάλλ᾽* is elided *βάλλε* (2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *βάλλω*). *Ἐπὶ τῶν νώτων* localizes *addosso* as ‘on the back’, with *ἐπί* and the genitive. *Ἐκδερμάτισον τοῦτον* ‘flay this one’ has a 2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *ἐκδερματίζω* and a demonstrative object. The two direct commands replace *fa che … metti* and the subordinate *sì che tu lo scuoi*; the aorist presents the flaying as a whole.
- *Ἔκραζον* (here 3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *κράζω*) retains the imperfect *gridavan*. *Σύναμ᾽* is elided adverbial *σύναμα* ‘together’; *οἱ κατάρατοι* uses the adjective substantivally for *i maledetti*, with *πάντες* ‘all’ outside the article-adjective group.

> 43 Ed io: " Maestro mio, fa, se tu puoi,  
> 44 che tu sappi chi è lo sciagurato  
> 45 venuto a man degli avversari suoi."  

And I: “Master, learn if thou canst, who is that piteous wight, fallen into the hand of his adversaries.”

> 43 Κἀγώ· “Προστάτα, σπεῦσον, εἰ δυνατόν σοι,  
> (And I: “Master, hasten, if you are able,)
>
> 44 „ Μαθεῖν, τίς ποτ’ ἂν ὁ τάλας οὗτος εἴη  
> („ To learn who this wretched man may be)
>
> 45 „ Ὁ ταῖς χερσὶν ἐμπεσὼν ἐχθρῶν τοιούτων.”  
> („ Who has fallen into the hands of such foes.”)

- *Κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*, retaining the verbless *Ed io*. Vocative *Προστάτα* (from *προστάτης*) gives ‘protector’ for *Maestro mio*, without the possessive. *Σπεῦσον* (2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *σπεύδω*) introduces urgency in place of *fa* and governs the infinitive in the next line. Parenthetic *εἰ δυνατόν σοι* ‘if [it is] possible for you’ uses a neuter predicate adjective, an understood copula, and a dative instead of personal *se tu puoi*.
- *Μαθεῖν* (second aor. act. inf. of *μανθάνω*) means ‘find out’, in place of *che tu sappi*. Its indirect question *τίς ποτ᾽ ἂν ὁ τάλας οὗτος εἴη* shifts the plain indicative *chi è* to a potential optative: *εἴη* is 3rd sg. pres. opt. of *εἰμί*, with *ἄν*, ‘who might he be?’ Elided enclitic *ποτε* adds ‘ever’ to interrogative *τίς*. *Ὁ τάλας οὗτος* is ‘this wretched one’, with the demonstrative outside the articular group.
- *Ὁ … ἐμπεσών* is an articular participle in apposition, ‘the one who fell’ (nom. masc. sg. second aor. act. of *ἐμπίπτω*), for *venuto*. *Ταῖς χερσίν* (dat. pl. of *χείρ*) depends on the compound verb: ‘fell into the hands’. *Ἐχθρῶν τοιούτων* supplies the possessors of the hands but substitutes qualitative ‘of such enemies’ for *degli avversari suoi*.

> 46 Lo duca mio gli s' accostò allato,  
> 47 domandollo ond' ei fosse, e quei rispose:  

My Guide drew close to [his side] and asked him whence he came; and he replied:

> 46 Οὑμὸς δ’ Ἡγήτωρ ἐγγὺς αὐτοῦ προσῆλθεν  
> (And my Guide drew near to him,)
>
> 47 Ἐρωτήσας, πόθεν ἦν· ὁ δ’ ἀπεκρίθη·  
> (Having asked whence he was; and he answered:)

- *Οὑμὸς δ᾽ Ἡγήτωρ* ‘and my Guide’ has crasis of *ὁ ἐμός* in *οὑμός* and elision of postpositive *δέ*. *Ἐγγὺς αὐτοῦ προσῆλθεν* renders *gli s’ accostò allato* by ‘came near him’, with *ἐγγύς* governing the genitive and *προσῆλθεν* the internally augmented 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *προσέρχομαι*. There is no noun for ‘side’.
- *Ἐρωτήσας* (nom. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *ἐρωτάω*) agrees with *Ἡγήτωρ*. Musurus attaches the asking to the approach as a participial circumstance instead of making it the next finite verb as in *domandollo*; no second object pronoun is expressed. In *πόθεν ἦν* ‘whence he was’, the indirect question uses an imperfect indicative rather than the subjunctive of *ond’ ei fosse*.
- *Ὁ δ᾽ ἀπεκρίθη* gives *e quei rispose* by the pronominal use of the article with *δέ* to change subjects, ‘and he in turn’. *Ἀπεκρίθη* is 3rd sg. aor. passive in form, with the active sense ‘answered’ of *ἀποκρίνομαι*.

> 48 " Io fui del regno di Navarra nato.  

“I was born in the kingdom of Navarre.

> 48 “Ἐν βασιλείῳ Ναυάῤῥας ἐγεννήθην·  
> (“In the kingdom of Navarre was I born;)

- *Ἐν βασιλείῳ Ναυάῤῥας* construes *del regno* as a birthplace, with locative *ἐν* and dative singular *βασιλείῳ*, rather than a genitive of origin. *Ναυάῤῥας* is the dependent genitive; the smooth and rough breathings on successive rhos represent a traditional spelling of medial double rho.
- *Ἐγεννήθην* (1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *γεννάω*) renders *Io fui … nato* in a single final verb. The augment and passive aorist suffix mark a past event, and the ending makes an independent subject pronoun unnecessary.

> 49 Mia madre a servo d' un signor mi pose,  

My mother placed me as servant of a lord;

> 49 „ Εἰς κύριον μ’ ἔθηκεν ἡ μήτηρ δοῦλον,  
> („ My mother placed me as servant to a lord,)

- *Εἰς κύριον μ᾽ ἔθηκεν ἡ μήτηρ δοῦλον* separates the two roles: *εἰς κύριον* ‘to a lord’ gives the destination, and *δοῦλον* ‘as a slave’ is a predicate accusative agreeing with *μ᾽* (elided *με*). This is not the Italian grouping *a servo d’ un signor*, where the lord depends grammatically on *servo*. The directional *εἰς* with a person is less idiomatic in classical prose than a construction expressing the person to whom someone is entrusted.
- *Ἔθηκεν* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *τίθημι*, with the singular active aorist stem *θηκ-*. The subject *ἡ μήτηρ* follows it; the article with the kinship noun leaves *mia* to be inferred.

> 50 chè m' avea generato d' un ribaldo  
> 51 distruggitor di sè e di sue cose.  

for she had borne me to a ribald waster of himself and of his substance.

> 50 „ Τεκοῦσά μ’ ἐξ εὐτελοῦς τινος ἀνθρώπου,  
> („ Having borne me to a worthless sort of man,)
>
> 51 „ Ὀλετῆρος ἑαυτοῦ κοὐσίας πάσης.  
> („ A destroyer of himself and of all his substance.)

- *Τεκοῦσά μ᾽* ‘having borne me’ replaces the explicitly causal *chè m’ avea generato* with a circumstantial participle referring back to *ἡ μήτηρ*. *Τεκοῦσα* is nom. fem. sg. second aor. act. of *τίκτω*: the aorist participle here expresses an act prior to the placing, without a finite pluperfect. Its extra accent is due to enclitic *μ᾽*.
- *Ἐξ εὐτελοῦς τινος ἀνθρώπου* gives paternal origin with *ἐξ* and the genitive for *d’ un ribaldo*. The single abusive noun becomes adjective, indefinite, and noun, ‘of a certain worthless man’: *εὐτελοῦς* is gen. masc. sg. of *εὐτελής*, *τινος* the enclitic genitive of *τις*.
- *Ὀλετῆρος* (gen. sg. of poetic *ὀλετήρ* ‘destroyer’) stands in apposition to *ἀνθρώπου*, as *distruggitor* does to *ribaldo*. The genitives dependent on this agent noun name what he destroyed: reflexive *ἑαυτοῦ* ‘himself’ and *κοὐσίας πάσης* ‘and all [his] substance’. *Κοὐσίας* is crasis of *καὶ οὐσίας*; singular *οὐσία* collects *sue cose* into ‘property’, with possession implicit and *πάσης* adding totality.

## Lines 52–84 — The Hooks and the Question About Gomita

> 52 Poi fui famiglio del buon re Tebaldo;  

Then I was domestic with the good king Thibault;

> 52 „ Εἶτ’ ἄνακτι Τυπάλδῳ συνήθης ἤμην,  
> („ Afterward I was an intimate of King Thibault,)

- *Εἶτ’* is *εἶτα* ‘then’, elided before *ἄνακτι*. Musurus puts the king first: *ἄνακτι Τυπάλδῳ* ‘to King Thibault’ (dat. sg. of poetic *ἄναξ*, with the Hellenized name in apposition) depends on *συνήθης* ‘familiar with’. There is no equivalent of *buon*.
- *συνήθης ἤμην* ‘I was an intimate’ renders *fui famiglio*: a predicate adjective (nom. sg. masc.) replaces the noun denoting household service. *ἤμην* is the later middle-form imperfect, 1st sg. of *εἰμί* (Attic *ἦ*), presenting a continuing relationship.

> 53 quivi mi misi a far baratteria,  
> 54 di che io rendo ragione in questo caldo.”  

here I set myself to doing barratry, of which I render reckoning in this heat."

> 53 „ Κἀκεῖ κλωπείας ἠρξάμην μηχανᾶσθαι,  
> („ And there I began to contrive thefts,)
>
> 54 „ Ὧν δίδωμι νῦν ἐν πυρὶ τῷδε δίκην.”  
> („ For which I now pay the penalty in this fire.”)

- *Κἀκεῖ* is crasis for *καὶ ἐκεῖ* ‘and there’. In *κλωπείας ἠρξάμην μηχανᾶσθαι*, the object *κλωπείας* ‘thefts’ (acc. pl. of *κλωπεία*) comes before both verbs: the specific *baratteria* becomes plural acts of theft, and *far* becomes ‘devise’. *ἠρξάμην* (1st sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἄρχω*) takes the complementary present middle infinitive *μηχανᾶσθαι* (contracted, from *μηχανάομαι*), ‘I began to devise’.
- *Ὧν δίδωμι … δίκην* ‘for which I pay the penalty’ replaces *di che io rendo ragione*. The relative *ὧν* is gen. pl., looking back to *κλωπείας* and specifying the offences for which satisfaction is given; *δίδωμι* is 1st sg. pres. act. ind. The idiomatic object *δίκην* is held to the end. *νῦν* ‘now’ is supplied; *ἐν πυρὶ τῷδε* ‘in this fire’ makes *in questo caldo* concrete (*πυρί*, dat. sg. of *πῦρ*; *τῷδε*, dat. sg. neut. of *ὅδε*).

> 55 E Ciriatto, a cui di bocca uscia  
> 56 d’ ogni parte una sanna come a porco,  
> 57 gli fe’ sentir come l’ una sdrucia.  

And Ciriatto, from whose mouth on either side came forth a tusk as from a hog, made him feel how one of them did rip.

> 55 Καὶ Καριάτος ἐκ στόματος προβάλλων  
> (And Ciriatto, thrusting forth from his mouth)
>
> 56 Ἑκατέρωθεν, ὡς σῦς, ὀξὺν ὀδόντα,  
> (On either side, like a boar, a sharp tusk,)
>
> 57 Ὅσον ἦν εἷς ἐξ αὐτῶν τομὸς ἐδείκνυ.  
> (Showed how cutting one of them was.)

- In place of the relative *a cui di bocca uscia*, with the tusk as subject of an intransitive verb, *Καριάτος ἐκ στόματος προβάλλων* makes Ciriatto the agent: *προβάλλων* ‘putting forward’ (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *προβάλλω*) agrees with the subject of the main verb. *ἐκ στόματος* has the gen. sg. of *στόμα*, without article or possessive.
- *Ἑκατέρωθεν* ‘on either side’ distributes the singular object *ὀξὺν ὀδόντα* ‘a sharp tooth’ (acc. sg. masc. of *ὀξύς*, *ὀδούς*); *ὡς σῦς* is a nominative comparison, ‘like a pig’, for *come a porco*. The sharpness is explicit in Greek. Although two teeth are implied, Musurus uses no dual.
- *Ὅσον ἦν εἷς ἐξ αὐτῶν τομός* ‘how cutting one of them was’ is the content of *ἐδείκνυ* ‘he showed’ (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *δείκνυμι*). *ὅσον* marks degree; *εἷς* is the subject of *ἦν*, *ἐξ αὐτῶν* partitive, and *τομός* a predicate adjective. Thus *come l’una sdrucia* changes from action to quality; *gli fe’ sentir* loses both its explicit recipient and its causative infinitive.

> 58 Tra male gatte era venuto il sorco;  

Amongst evil cats the mouse had come;

> 58 Ἐν μέσῳ κακῶν αἰλούρων ὁ μῦς ἦλθεν·  
> (Among wicked cats the mouse had come;)

- *Ἐν μέσῳ κακῶν αἰλούρων* ‘in the midst of wicked cats’ retains the fronted position of *Tra male gatte*: locative *ἐν* + dat. *μέσῳ* is completed by the gen. pl. *κακῶν αἰλούρων*. *ὁ μῦς* ‘the mouse’ precedes *ἦλθεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*); the simple past replaces the anterior *era venuto*.

> 59 ma Barbariccia il chiuse con le braccia,  
> 60 e disse: “State in là, mentr’ io lo inforco.”  

but Barbariccia locked him in his arms, and said: "Stand off whilst I enfork him!"

> 59 Ἀλλ’ ἔλαβ’ ἀγκὰς αὐτὸν Βαρβαρικίας,  
> (But Barbariccia clasped him in his arms,)
>
> 60 Εἶπέ τε· “ Στῆτε, διαπερῶ ’γὼ τοῦτον.”  
> (And said: “Stand back, I am piercing this one.”)

- *Ἀλλ’ ἔλαβ’ ἀγκὰς αὐτὸν Βαρβαρικίας* puts the subject last. *ἀλλ’* and *ἔλαβ’* show elision of *ἀλλά* and *ἔλαβε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λαμβάνω*). Poetic *ἀγκάς* is adverbial, ‘in the arms’: ‘took him in his arms’ replaces *il chiuse con le braccia*.
- *Εἶπέ τε* ‘and he said’ joins the two aorists with enclitic *τε* (hence the additional accent on *εἶπε*, from *λέγω*). *Στῆτε* ‘take your stand, stop’ is the 2nd pl. intransitive second aor. act. imperative of *ἵστημι*; *in là* has no separate rendering.
- *διαπερῶ ’γὼ τοῦτον* ‘I shall run this one through’ is an independent assertion, not a temporal clause like *mentr’ io lo inforco*. *διαπερῶ* is naturally read as the contracted future of *διαπείρω* ‘pierce through’; *’γώ* is *ἐγώ* with prodelision of its initial vowel. Expressed subject and demonstrative object (*τοῦτον*, acc. sg. masc. of *οὗτος*) are more pointed than an unexpressed subject and a simple object pronoun.

> 61 Ed al maestro mio volse la faccia:  
> 62 “Domanda,” disse, “ancor se più desii  
> 63 saper da lui, prima ch’ altri il disfaccia.”  

And turning his face to my Master: "Ask on," he said, "if thou wouldst learn more from him, before some other undo him."

> 61 Εἶτα δὲ στραφεὶς πρὸς τὸν ἐμὸν Προστάτην  
> (And then, turning toward my Master,)
>
> 62 Εἶπεν· “ Ἐρώτα πάλιν, εἰ πλέον θέλεις  
> (He said: “Ask again, if you wish more)
>
> 63 „ Μαθεῖν παρ’ αὐτοῦ, πρὶν ὄληται πανώλης.”  
> („ To learn from him, before he utterly perishes.”)

- *Εἶτα δὲ στραφείς* ‘then, having turned’ converts finite *volse* into a circumstantial participle subordinate to *Εἶπεν*. *στραφείς* is nom. sg. masc., second aor. pass. of *στρέφω*, intransitive in sense; *la faccia* drops out. *πρὸς τὸν ἐμὸν Προστάτην* uses *πρός* + accusative of direction and an attributive possessive, ‘toward my protector’.
- *Ἐρώτα* is the contracted 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *ἐρωτάω*; *πάλιν* takes *ancor* as ‘again’. *εἰ πλέον θέλεις μαθεῖν παρ’ αὐτοῦ* ‘if you wish to learn more from him’ has *εἰ* with the present indicative, *μαθεῖν* (second aor. act. inf. of *μανθάνω*) complementary to *θέλεις*, and *παρά* (elided) + genitive of the personal source. *πλέον* is the neuter comparative ‘more’.
- *πρὶν ὄληται πανώλης* ‘before he perishes utterly’ suppresses the indefinite agent of *prima ch’ altri il disfaccia*. *ὄληται* (3rd sg. second aor. mid. subj. of *ὄλλυμι*) is intransitive; the prospective limit is expressed by a finite subjunctive after *πρίν*, without *ἄν*, rather than the usual infinitive after an affirmative main clause. *πανώλης* ‘utterly lost’ is a predicate adjective (nom. sg. masc.), not an adverb.

> 64 Lo duca dunque: “Or di’, degli altri rii  
> 65 conosci tu alcun che sia Latino  
> 66 sotto la pece?” E quegli: “Io mi partii  

The Guide therefore: "Now say, of the other sinners knowest thou any that is a Latian, beneath the pitch?" And he: "I parted

> 64 Ὁ δ’ Ἡγήτωρ· “ Λέγε νῦν· ἐνόχων ἄλλων  
> (And the Guide: “Tell me now: of the other culprits)
>
> 65 „ Γινώσκεις τίν’ ἆρά γε Λατῖνον ὄντα  
> („ Do you know anyone, then, who is a Latian)
>
> 66 „ Ὑπὸ τὴν πίσσαν; ” Ὁ δέ· “ Διεχωρίσθην  
> („ Beneath the pitch?” And he: “I parted)

- *Ὁ δ’ Ἡγήτωρ* supplies no verb of speaking, like *Lo duca dunque*; elided *δέ* marks the change of speaker rather than an inference. *Λέγε νῦν* ‘say now’ uses the 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *λέγω*.
- *ἐνόχων ἄλλων* ‘of other guilty persons’ is a fronted partitive genitive with *τίν’*, elided indefinite *τινα* (acc. sg.); its accent before enclitic *ἆρά γε*’s particle group should not be taken to make the question ‘whom do you know?’ Rather, *ἆρα* marks a yes-or-no question, with *γε* lending emphasis: ‘do you know any one …?’
- *Γινώσκεις … Λατῖνον ὄντα* uses the 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *γιγνώσκω* in the spelling *γινώσκω*, and a supplementary participle after a verb of knowing. *ὄντα* (acc. sg. masc. pres. participle of *εἰμί*) agrees with the indefinite object, and *Λατῖνον* is its predicate: ‘know anyone to be a Latian’, for the relative subjunctive *che sia Latino*. *ὑπὸ τὴν πίσσαν* closely follows *sotto la pece*; the accusative is notable for a position beneath, rather than motion under.
- *Ὁ δέ* ‘and he’, again without a speech verb, introduces *Διεχωρίσθην* ‘I was separated’ (1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *διαχωρίζω*, with internal augment). The Italian pronominal *mi partii* thus has passive form but intransitive force in Greek.

> 67 poco è da un, che fu di là vicino;  

just now from one who was a neighbour of theirs [on the other side];

> 67 „ Πρὸ μικροῦ τινος, ὃς ἦν γείτονος χώρας·  
> („ A little while ago from one who was of a neighbouring land;)

- *Πρὸ μικροῦ* ‘a little while ago’ is *πρό* + genitive with time understood, for *poco è*. *τινος* ‘from someone’ is a separate genitive, of separation with *Διεχωρίσθην*. In *ὃς ἦν γείτονος χώρας* the relative is nominative subject, and the predicate genitive expresses origin or affiliation: ‘who was of a neighbouring country’. *γείτονος* (gen. sg. of *γείτων*) qualifies *χώρας*. Musurus thus attaches the idea of proximity to a supplied ‘country’, without separately rendering *di là* or naming whose neighbour the man was.

> 68 così foss’ io ancor con lui coperto,  
> 69 ch’ io non temerei unghia nè uncino.”  

would I still were covered with him, for I should not fear claw nor hook!"

> 68 „ Εἴθ’ ἔτ’ ἤμην σὺν αὐτῷ κεκαλυμμένος·  
> („ Would that I were still covered together with him,)
>
> 69 „ Οὐ γὰρ ἅρπας κὤνυχας νῦν ἐδεδοίκειν.”  
> („ For I should not now be fearing hooks and claws.”)

- *Εἴθ’ ἔτ’ ἤμην* ‘if only I were still’ uses *εἴθε* with imperfect indicative, not optative, for an unreal present wish (*così foss’ io ancor*). Both particles are elided: *εἴθε* before *ἔτι*, *ἔτι* before *ἤμην*. *σὺν αὐτῷ* has the dative of accompaniment; *κεκαλυμμένος* (nom. sg. masc. perf. mid./pass. participle of *καλύπτω*, reduplicated) denotes the resulting state, ‘under cover’.
- Postpositive *γάρ* interprets *ch’* as explanatory. *ἅρπας κὤνυχας* ‘hooks and claws’ reverses *unghia nè uncino*, pluralizes both objects, and coordinates them positively within the scope of *οὐ*. *κὤνυχας* is crasis of *καὶ ὄνυχας* (acc. pl. of *ὄνυξ*).
- *νῦν ἐδεδοίκειν* ‘I should now be afraid’ uses the 1st sg. pluperfect active indicative corresponding to *δέδοικα* ‘I fear’ (from *δείδω*). The tense has the past value of a present-state verb; the unreal condition is implicit in the wish, and the expected modal *ἄν* is absent. *νῦν* is supplied.

> 70 E Libicocco: “Troppo avem sofferto,”  
> 71 disse, e presegli il braccio col ronciglio,  
> 72 sì che, stracciando, ne portò un lacerto.  

And Libicocco cried: "Too much have we endured!" and with the hook seized his arm, and mangling carried off a part of brawn.

> 70 Καὶ Λιβίκοκκος· “ Ἀνοχῆς ἡμῖν ἅλις,”  
> (And Libicocco: “We have had enough of forbearance,”)
>
> 71 Εἶπε, καὶ λαβὼν τοῦ βραχίονος, ἅρπῃ,  
> (Said he, and seizing his arm, with his hook,)
>
> 72 Αὐτὸν σπαράττων, τμῆμα σαρκὸς ἐξεῖλε·  
> (Tearing him, took out a piece of flesh;)

- *Καὶ Λιβίκοκκος … Εἶπε* places ‘said’ after the direct speech as in Italian. *Ἀνοχῆς ἡμῖν ἅλις* ‘enough for us of forbearance’ is verbless: *ἅλις* ‘enough’ takes the gen. sg. of the abstract noun *ἀνοχή*, and the Italian first-person subject becomes dative *ἡμῖν*. The perfect *avem sofferto* is not reproduced; *troppo* becomes an assertion of sufficiency.
- *λαβὼν τοῦ βραχίονος* ‘taking hold of the arm’ makes finite *prese* a circumstantial second aor. act. participle (nom. sg. masc. of *λαμβάνω*); the part grasped is genitive, and its possessor need not be expressed. *ἅρπῃ* (dat. sg. of *ἅρπη*) is instrumental, ‘with a hook’, placed between the grasping and the tearing.
- *Αὐτὸν σπαράττων* ‘tearing him’ gives *stracciando* an explicit personal object; the present active participle of *σπαράττω* marks action accompanying the main aorist. *τμῆμα σαρκὸς ἐξεῖλε* ‘he took out a piece of flesh’ has a genitive of the material or whole (*σαρκός*, from *σάρξ*) and the 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἐξαιρέω*. The prefix expresses removal; Italian *ne* has no separate pronominal equivalent.

> 73 Draghignazzo anco i volle dar di piglio  
> 74 giuso alle gambe; onde il decurio loro  
> 75 si volse intorno intorno con mal piglio.  

Draghignazzo, he too, wished to have a catch at the legs below; whereat their Decurion wheeled around around with evil aspect.

> 73 Βάλλειν δ’ αὐτὸν ἤθελε καὶ Δραγιγνάζος  
> (And Draghignazzo too wished to strike him)
>
> 74 Κάτω τῶν σκελῶν· ἀλλ’ ὁ δέκαρχος τότε  
> (Down on the legs; but the decurion then)
>
> 75 Κύκλῳ περιέστρεψε φοβερὸν βλέμμα.  
> (Rolled around in a circle a terrifying glance.)

- *Βάλλειν δ’ αὐτὸν ἤθελε καὶ Δραγιγνάζος* puts the complementary infinitive first and the subject last: ‘and Draghignazzo too wanted to strike him’. *βάλλειν* (pres. act. inf. of *βάλλω*) interprets *dar di piglio* as a blow rather than a grasp; *αὐτόν* is direct object where Italian has *i* ‘at him’. *ἤθελε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἐθέλω*) presents an ongoing intention for *volle*; additive *καί* focuses the following name.
- *Κάτω τῶν σκελῶν* couples *κάτω* with the gen. pl. of *σκέλος*. Literally the prepositional use suggests ‘below the legs’; it is the Italian combination *giuso alle gambe* that gives the intended sense ‘down at the legs’. The plural, not the dual, is used.
- *ἀλλ’ ὁ δέκαρχος τότε* ‘but the decurion then’ substitutes contrast and time for consequential *onde* and omits *loro*. *Κύκλῳ περιέστρεψε φοβερὸν βλέμμα* ‘he turned a fearsome look about in a circle’ transfers the turning from the man (*si volse*) to his look: *περιέστρεψε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *περιστρέφω*, internally augmented) is transitive, with acc. sg. neut. *φοβερὸν βλέμμα* as object. Adverbial dat. *κύκλῳ* and prefix *περι-* together suggest *intorno intorno*; *con mal piglio* becomes the quality of the look.

> 76 Quand’ elli un poco rappaciati foro,  
> 77 a lui che ancor mirava sua ferita,  
> 78 domandò il duca mio senza dimoro:  

When they were somewhat pacified, my Guide without delay asked him that still kept gazing on his wound:

> 76 Τότε δὲ μικρὸν αὐτῶν εἰρηνευσάντων,  
> (And then, when they had grown a little peaceful,)
>
> 77 Τὸν ἔνοχον, ὃς τὴν πληγὴν ἔθ᾽ ἑώρα,  
> (The culprit, who was still gazing at his wound,)
>
> 78 Ἠρώτησ᾽ οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ ἀμελλήτως·  
> (My Guide asked without delay:)

- *Τότε δὲ μικρὸν αὐτῶν εἰρηνευσάντων* replaces *Quand’ … rappaciati foro* with ‘then, when they had quieted down’. *αὐτῶν εἰρηνευσάντων* is a genitive absolute, with the gen. pl. masc. aor. act. participle of intransitive *εἰρηνεύω*, not a passive. The aorist places the settling before the question; *μικρόν* is an adverbial accusative of degree.
- *Τὸν ἔνοχον* ‘the guilty man’ makes *a lui* a named accusative object of ‘asked’. The relative *ὅς* is nominative subject of *ἑώρα* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ὁράω*, with the distinctive augment *ἑω-*). *τὴν πληγήν* ‘the wound’ uses the article where Italian has possessive *sua*. *ἔθ’* is *ἔτι* elided before rough-breathed *ἑώρα*, with aspiration of the remaining dental.
- *Ἠρώτησ’ οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ ἀμελλήτως* follows the Italian order of main verb, subject, and expression of promptness. *Ἠρώτησ’* is elided *ἠρώτησε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐρωτάω*); *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, ‘my’. The adverb *ἀμελλήτως* ‘without delaying’ replaces *senza dimoro*.

> 79 “Chi fu colui, da cui mala partita  
> 80 di’ che facesti per venire a proda?”  

"Who was he, from whom thou sayest that thou madest an ill departure to come ashore?"

> 79 “Τίς ἦν ἐκεῖνος, οὗτινος ἐχωρίσθης,  
> (“Who was that man from whom you parted,)
>
> 80 „ Ὡς λέγεις, κακῶς, ἐξοκείλας ἐνθάδε ;”  
> („ Ill-starred, as you say, to run aground here?”)

- *Τίς ἦν ἐκεῖνος* ‘who was that man?’ has predicate interrogative *τίς*, imperfect *ἦν*, and demonstrative subject. *οὗτινος* (gen. sg. masc. of the compound relative *ὅστις*) is a genitive of separation with *ἐχωρίσθης* (2nd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *χωρίζω*, ‘you parted’). The Greek makes *mala partita … facesti* a verb qualified by adverbial *κακῶς* ‘to your hurt’.
- *Ὡς λέγεις* ‘as you say’ is parenthetic, rather than a governing *di’ che* clause. *ἐξοκείλας* (nom. sg. masc. aor. act. participle of *ἐξοκέλλω*, ‘run aground’) agrees with the implicit ‘you’. It expresses the attendant event rather than explicitly marking purpose as *per venire a proda* does; directional *ἐνθάδε* ‘hither’ replaces the noun *proda*.

> 81 Ed ei rispose: “Fu frate Gomita,  
> 82 quel di Gallura, vasel d’ ogni froda,  

And he answered: "It was Friar Gomita, he of Gallura, vessel of every fraud,

> 81 Ὁ δ᾽ ἀπεκρίθη· “Κεῖνος ἦν Φρα-Γομίτας  
> (And he answered: “That was Fra Gomita,)
>
> 82 „ Ὁ τῆς Γαλούρας, σκεῦος ἁπάντων δόλων.  
> („ He of Gallura, a vessel of all frauds.)

- *Ὁ δ’ ἀπεκρίθη* ‘and he answered’ has the 3rd sg. aor. passive in form, active in sense, of *ἀποκρίνομαι* (beside classical middle aorist *ἀπεκρίνατο*). *Κεῖνος* ‘that man’, the shorter poetic form of *ἐκεῖνος*, supplies a subject for *ἦν* where *Fu* stands alone; *Φρα-Γομίτας* retains the Italian title in the name, with a Greek masculine nominative ending.
- *Ὁ τῆς Γαλούρας* ‘the one of Gallura’ closely follows *quel di Gallura*: the masculine article allows the genitive of place to identify the man without a repeated noun. *σκεῦος ἁπάντων δόλων* is a second apposition, neut. sg. *σκεῦος* ‘vessel’ with gen. pl. of contents, ‘of all deceits’, for Italian distributive singular *d’ ogni froda*. Greek closes the sentence here rather than continuing with *che*.

> 83 ch’ ebbe i nimici di suo donno in mano,  
> 84 e fe’ sì lor, che ciascun se ne loda:  

who had his master's enemies in hand, and did so to them that they all praise him for it:

> 83 „ Ἐν χερσὶν εἶχεν ἐχθροὺς ἑοῦ κυρίου·  
> („ He had in his hands the enemies of his lord,)
>
> 84 „ Τοιόσδ᾽ ἦν αὐτοῖς, ὥστ᾽ ἀρέσκειν ἑκάστῳ·  
> („ And was such to them as to please each one;)

- *Ἐν χερσὶν εἶχεν* ‘in his hands he held’ begins an independent clause, fronting the phrase that ends the Italian relative clause. *χερσίν* is dat. pl. of *χείρ* with movable *ν*; *εἶχεν* is 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἔχω*, a state rather than the bounded *ebbe*. *ἐχθρούς* is the accusative object; *ἑοῦ κυρίου* ‘of his own lord’ depends on it. *ἑοῦ* is the gen. sg. masc. of the poetic third-person possessive adjective *ἑός*, agreeing with *κυρίου* but referring possession back to the subject.
- *Τοιόσδ’ ἦν αὐτοῖς* ‘he was such toward them’ replaces *fe’ sì lor*: elided *τοιόσδε* is a predicate demonstrative adjective (nom. sg. masc.), and *αὐτοῖς* a dative of relation. Conduct expressed by ‘do’ becomes a quality expressed by ‘be’.
- *ὥστ’ ἀρέσκειν ἑκάστῳ* ‘so as to please each one’ completes the demonstrative with a consecutive infinitive (*ὥστε* elided; *ἀρέσκειν*, pres. act. inf. of *ἀρέσκω*). Its understood subject is still Gomita. Italian *ciascun*, subject of the finite *se ne loda*, becomes *ἑκάστῳ*, dat. sg. of the person pleased: the favourable response is retained, but neither praise nor its pronominal complement is expressed directly.

## Lines 85–117 — The Whistle and Alichino’s Challenge

> 85 denar si tolse, e lasciolli di piano,  
> 86 sì com’ei dice; e negli altri uffici anche  
> 87 barattier fu non picciol, ma soprano.  

money took he for himself, and dismissed them smoothly, as he says; and in his other offices besides, he was no petty but a sovereign barrator.

> 85 „ Χρήματα λαβὼν ἀφῆκ᾽ αὐτοὺς ἀνέτως,  
> („ Having taken money, he let them go freely,)
>
> 86 „ Ὡς αὐτὸς λέγει· κἀν λειτουργίαις ἄλλαις  
> („ As he himself says; and in other offices)
>
> 87 „ Ἦν οὐχὶ μικρός, ἀλλ᾽ ἔξοχος φηλήτης.  
> („ He was no petty, but an eminent swindler.)

- *Χρήματα λαβών* ‘having taken money’ keeps *denar* first but subordinates *si tolse* to the verb of dismissal. *Χρήματα* is the accusative plural of *χρῆμα*, regularly plural in the sense ‘money’; *λαβών* is the nominative masculine singular second-aorist active participle of *λαμβάνω*. The Italian *si* ‘for himself’ has no separate equivalent.
- *ἀφῆκ᾽ αὐτοὺς ἀνέτως* ‘he let them go freely’ renders *e lasciolli di piano*. *ἀφῆκ᾽* is elided *ἀφῆκε*, third singular of the augmented κ-aorist active indicative of *ἀφίημι*; the Italian enclitic *-lli* becomes the independent accusative plural *αὐτούς*. The adverb *ἀνέτως* qualifies the manner of release.
- *ὡς αὐτὸς λέγει* follows *sì com’ei dice*, with intensive *αὐτός* ‘he himself’. *κἀν* is crasis of *καὶ ἐν*; *λειτουργίαις ἄλλαις* is an anarthrous dative plural, ‘in other public functions’, for *negli altri uffici anche*. The additive *καί* comes before the phrase rather than after it.
- *ἦν οὐχὶ μικρός, ἀλλ᾽ ἔξοχος φηλήτης* puts the copula first and the predicate noun last, reversing the disposition of *barattier fu non picciol, ma soprano*. *ἦν* is third singular imperfect of *εἰμί*, for the Italian preterite *fu*; *οὐχί* is an emphatic negative, and *ἀλλ᾽* is elided *ἀλλά*. Both adjectives are nominative masculine singular with *φηλήτης* ‘cheat’: the Greek gives eminence in cheating rather than a term specific to corrupt officeholding.

> 88 Usa con esso donno Michel Zanche  
> 89 di Logodoro; ed a dir di Sardigna  
> 90 le lingue lor non si sentono stanche.  

With him keeps company Don Michel Zanche of Logodoro; and in speaking of Sardinia the tongues of them do not feel weary.

> 88 „ Σύνεστιν αὐτῷ Κυρὸς Μιχαὴλ Ζάγκης  
> („ With him is Don Michel Zanche)
>
> 89 „ Ὁ Λογοδώρου, καὶ περὶ Σαρδοῦς νήσου  
> („ Of Logodoro, and concerning the island of Sardinia)
>
> 90 „ Ἀναπαύδητοι λαλοῦσ᾽ αὐτῶν αἱ γλῶσσαι.  
> („ Untiringly their tongues speak.)

- *Σύνεστιν αὐτῷ* ‘associates with him’ keeps the order of *Usa con esso*. *σύνεστιν* is third singular present indicative of *σύνειμι* ‘be with’, with movable *-ν*; the simple dative *αὐτῷ* supplies the associate to the compound verb.
- *Κυρὸς Μιχαὴλ Ζάγκης* gives *donno* a Greek title, leaves *Μιχαήλ* indeclinable, and gives *Ζάγκης* a Greek nominative ending. The masculine nominative article in *ὁ Λογοδώρου* attaches the genitive of origin to the name: ‘the one from Logodoro’.
- *περὶ Σαρδοῦς νήσου* ‘about the island of Sardinia’ replaces *a dir di Sardigna* with a topic phrase, *περί* governing the genitive; *Σαρδοῦς* is the genitive of *Σαρδώ*, and *νήσου* makes ‘island’ explicit. The act of speaking is transferred to *λαλοῦσ᾽*, elided *λαλοῦσι*, third plural present active indicative of *λαλέω*.
- *ἀναπαύδητοι … αὐτῶν αἱ γλῶσσαι* ‘untiring … their tongues’ makes the Italian negative reflexive predicate *non si sentono stanche* a positive verb with a predicate adjective. *ἀναπαύδητοι* is nominative feminine plural in agreement with *αἱ γλῶσσαι*, not an adverb, though it gives the manner of speaking. The possessive genitive *αὐτῶν* precedes the article and noun, reversing *le lingue lor*.

> 91 O me! vedete l’altro che digrigna;  

Oh me! see that other grinning;

> 91 „ Φεῦ ! βλέπετε τὸν ἄλλον πῶς ἐπιβρύχει·  
> („ Alas! see how the other grinds his teeth!)

- *Φεῦ* is an indeclinable cry corresponding to *O me!* without a pronoun. *βλέπετε* is the second plural present active imperative of *βλέπω*. In *τὸν ἄλλον πῶς ἐπιβρύχει* ‘the other, how he gnashes’, the accusative object of ‘see’ anticipates the understood subject of *ἐπιβρύχει* (third singular present active indicative of *ἐπιβρύχω*). Exclamatory *πῶς* ‘how’ replaces the Italian relative *che*.

> 92 io direi anco; ma io temo ch’ello  
> 93 non s’apparecchi a grattarmi la tigna.”  

I would say more; but fear he is preparing to claw my scurf.”

> 92 „ Πλείον᾽ ἂν εἶπον· ἀλλὰ φοβοῦμαι, μήπως  
> („ More would I tell; but I fear lest)
>
> 93 „ Αἴφνης ἐφορμήσας μου ξύσῃ τὴν κνύζαν.”  
> („ Suddenly rushing upon me he scratch my itch.”)

- *Πλείον᾽ ἂν εἶπον* ‘I would have said more’ makes *anco* an object, elided *πλείονα*, neuter accusative plural of the comparative *πλείων*. *εἶπον* is the first singular second-aorist active indicative used for *λέγω*. With postpositive *ἄν* it suggests an unrealized utterance, rather than the present possibility of *io direi anco*; it is not an optative.
- *φοβοῦμαι, μήπως … ξύσῃ* ‘I fear lest … he scratch’ gives the present middle of *φοβέομαι* (first singular) a fear clause with the third singular aorist active subjunctive of *ξύω*. *μήπως* marks the apprehended possibility; it does not negate ‘scratch’, just as *non* in *temo ch’ello non s’apparecchi* does not negate what is feared.
- *αἴφνης ἐφορμήσας* ‘suddenly rushing upon [me]’ replaces preparation (*s’apparecchi*) with an onset preceding the scratching. *ἐφορμήσας* is a nominative masculine singular aorist active participle of *ἐφορμάω*, with the same subject as *ξύσῃ*. In *μου ξύσῃ τὴν κνύζαν*, enclitic *μου* gives the affected person as possessor of *τὴν κνύζαν* ‘the itch’, where *grattarmi la tigna* has a personal clitic and names the scalp complaint.

> 94 E il gran proposto, volto a Farfarello  
> 95 che stralunava gli occhi per ferire,  
> 96 disse: “Fatti in costà, malvagio uccello.”  

And their great Marshal, turning to Farfarello, who rolled his eyes to strike, said: “Off with thee, villanous bird!”

> 94 Ὁ δ᾽ ἀρχηγέτης στραφεὶς πρὸς Φαρφαρέλλον  
> (And the commander, turning toward Farfarello,)
>
> 95 Περιστροβοῦντ᾽ ὀφθαλμοὺς πρὸς τὸ πατάξαι  
> (Rolling his eyes about in order to strike,)
>
> 96 Εἶπεν· “Ἄπαγ᾽ ἐκεῖθεν, κακόφρον ὄρνι.”  
> (Said: “Begone from there, evil-minded bird.”)

- *ὁ δ᾽ ἀρχηγέτης* ‘and the commander’ incorporates the rank of *il gran proposto* in a single noun; *δ᾽* is postpositive *δέ* elided. *στραφείς*, nominative masculine singular second-aorist passive participle of *στρέφω*, is intransitive in sense, ‘having turned’, like *volto*. *πρὸς Φαρφαρέλλον* marks orientation with *πρός* and the accusative.
- *περιστροβοῦντ᾽ ὀφθαλμούς* replaces *che stralunava gli occhi* with a participle agreeing with *Φαρφαρέλλον*: *περιστροβοῦντ᾽* is elided *περιστροβοῦντα*, accusative masculine singular present active participle of *περιστροβέω*, with *ὀφθαλμούς* its object. *πρὸς τὸ πατάξαι* renders *per ferire* by *πρός* with the accusative articular infinitive of purpose; *πατάξαι* is the aorist active infinitive of *πατάσσω*.
- *εἶπεν* ‘said’ resumes the subject *ἀρχηγέτης*. *ἄπαγ᾽* is elided *ἄπαγε*, second singular present active imperative of *ἀπάγω*, used absolutely as a dismissal. *ἐκεῖθεν* strictly means ‘from there’, whereas *in costà* indicates where to move to. *κακόφρον ὄρνι* ‘evil-minded bird’ has two vocatives, of *κακόφρων* and *ὄρνις* respectively.

> 97 “Se voi volete vedere o udire,”  
> 98 ricominciò lo spaurato appresso,  
> 99 “Toschi o Lombardi, io ne farò venire.  

"If you wish to see or hear Tuscans or Lombards,” the frightened sinner then resumed, “I will make them come.

> 97 — “Εἰ δὴ νῦν βούλεσθ᾽ ὑμεῖς ἰδεῖν κἀκοῦσαι,”  
> (—“If indeed you now wish to see and hear,”)
>
> 98 Ὁ καταπλὴξ ἔνοχος ἤρξατο πάλιν,  
> (The terrified culprit began again,)
>
> 99 “Τούσκους ἢ Λογγοβάρδους ἐγὼ καλέσω·  
> (“Tuscans or Lombards I will summon;)

- *Εἰ δὴ νῦν βούλεσθ᾽ ὑμεῖς* ‘if indeed you now wish’ uses an open condition with the present middle indicative. *βούλεσθ᾽* is *βούλεσθε* (second plural of *βούλομαι*) with elision and aspiration of the final dental before rough-breathed *ὑμεῖς*. The emphatic pronoun follows the verb rather than preceding it as *voi* does; *δή* and *νῦν* add emphasis and immediacy.
- *ἰδεῖν κἀκοῦσαι* ‘to see and hear’ coordinates two aorist active infinitives, the second aorist *ἰδεῖν* of *ὁράω* and *ἀκοῦσαι* of *ἀκούω*. *κἀκοῦσαι* is crasis of *καὶ ἀκοῦσαι*: Greek has ‘and’ for the first *o* but retains the second in *Τούσκους ἢ Λογγοβάρδους*. These accusative plurals are delayed across the reporting clause as in Italian.
- *ὁ καταπλὴξ ἔνοχος* ‘the terrified culprit’ supplies a noun for substantival *lo spaurato*; *καταπλήξ* is a nominative singular adjective. *ἤρξατο πάλιν* ‘began again’ distributes *ricominciò* between the third singular aorist middle indicative of *ἄρχω* and an adverb. *ἐγὼ καλέσω* ‘I shall summon’ expresses *io ne farò venire* with an emphatic subject and the first singular future active indicative of *καλέω*: a single transitive verb replaces the causative and infinitive, without a separate equivalent of *ne*.

> 100 Ma stien le male branche un poco in cesso,  
> 101 sì ch’ei non teman delle lor vendette;  

But let the [evil claws hold back] a little, that they may not fear their vengeance;

> 100 „Ἀλλὰ στήτωσαν οἱ Κακώνυχες ἄπο,  
> („But let the Evil-claws stand aloof,)
>
> 101 „Ἵνα μὴ τούτων ἐκδίκησιν φοβῶνται.  
> („So that they may not fear the vengeance of these.)

- *ἀλλὰ στήτωσαν* ‘but let them stand’ renders jussive *Ma stien* with the third plural second-aorist active imperative of *ἵστημι*, intransitive, ‘let them take their stand’. *οἱ Κακώνυχες* makes *le male branche* a masculine plural designation, ‘the evil-clawed ones’. *ἄπο* is the accented, separately used adverb ‘away’, not a preposition governing a noun; it gives the force of *in cesso* without expressing *un poco*.
- *ἵνα μὴ … φοβῶνται* renders *sì ch’ei non teman* as negative purpose, with the third plural present middle subjunctive of *φοβέομαι*. *τούτων* ‘of these’ points back to *οἱ Κακώνυχες*, distinguishing them from the unexpressed subject of ‘fear’; *ἐκδίκησιν* is an accusative singular abstract noun for plural *vendette*.

> 102 ed io, sedendo in questo loco stesso,  
> 103 per un ch’io son, ne farò venir sette,  
> 104 quand’ io sufolerò, com’è nostr’ uso  
> 105 di fare allor che fuori alcun si mette.”  

and I, sitting in this same place, for one that I am, will make seven come, on whistling as is our wont to do when any of us gets out.”

> 102 „Ἐγὼ δέ, καίπερ ἑστὼς ἐν τόπῳ τῷδε,  
> („And I, though standing in this place,)
>
> 103 „Ἀνθ᾽ ἑνὸς ἐμοῦ γ᾽ ὄντος ἑπτὰ προσοίσω,  
> („In place of my single self will bring seven forward,)
>
> 104 „Ἅμα συρίξας, ὥς ἐστιν ἡμῖν ἔθος  
> („As soon as I whistle, as is our custom)
>
> 105 „Πράττειν, ὅταν τις ἡμῶν ἐκτὸς προβαίνῃ.”—  
> („To do, whenever one of us comes outside.”—)

- *ἐγὼ δέ* marks the change of subject. *καίπερ ἑστώς* ‘although standing’ makes *sedendo* explicitly concessive and changes the posture. *ἑστώς* is the short nominative masculine singular perfect active participle of *ἵστημι*, with present stative sense. *ἐν τόπῳ τῷδε* uses the locative dative and the proximal demonstrative *ὅδε* ‘this here’ rather than a separate equivalent of *stesso*.
- *ἀνθ᾽ ἑνὸς ἐμοῦ γ᾽ ὄντος* recasts *per un ch’io son* as ‘in place of one, being only myself’. *ἀνθ᾽* is *ἀντί* elided and aspirated before *ἑνός*, masculine genitive singular of *εἷς*. *ἐμοῦ … ὄντος* gives the first-person subject and the present participle of *εἰμί* in the genitive, closely following the Italian explanation of ‘one’; *γ᾽* is the focusing enclitic *γε* elided. *ἑπτὰ προσοίσω* ‘I shall bring seven forward’ substitutes a transitive verb for *ne farò venir*: *προσοίσω* is the irregular first singular future active of *προσφέρω*, with indeclinable *ἑπτά* as object.
- *ἅμα συρίξας* ‘as soon as I whistle’ replaces *quand’ io sufolerò* with a nominative masculine singular aorist active participle of *συρίζω*. Its time is relative to *προσοίσω*, not past in relation to the speaker. *ὥς ἐστιν ἡμῖν ἔθος πράττειν* closely follows *com’è nostr’ uso di fare*: the dative *ἡμῖν* indicates whose custom it is, and the present active infinitive *πράττειν* specifies the custom.
- *ὅταν τις ἡμῶν ἐκτὸς προβαίνῃ* turns *allor che fuori alcun si mette* into an indefinite temporal clause, ‘whenever one of us steps outside’. *ὅταν* combines *ὅτε* and modal *ἄν* and takes the subjunctive (*προβαίνῃ*, third singular present active of *προβαίνω*). *τις* has the explicit partitive genitive *ἡμῶν*; *ἐκτός* is adverbial, and the Greek verb of motion needs no reflexive.

> 106 Cagnazzo a cotal motto levò il muso,  
> 107 crollando il capo, e disse: “Odi malizia  
> 108 ch’ egli ha pensata per gittarsi giuso.”  

Cagnazzo at these words raised his snout, shaking his head, and said: “Hear the malice he has contrived, to throw himself down!”

> 106 Κογνάζος τότε μυκτῆρ᾽ ἄρας καὶ σείων  
> (Cagnazzo then, lifting his snout and shaking)
>
> 107 Κεφαλὴν εἶπεν· “Ἄκουσον πονηρίαν,  
> (His head, said: “Hear the malice)
>
> 108 „Ἣν ἐπενόησ᾽, ἵν᾽ ὑποδύῃ κάτω.”  
> („Which he has devised, so that he may dive down below.”)

- *Κογνάζος τότε* substitutes ‘then’ for *a cotal motto*. *μυκτῆρ᾽ ἄρας* ‘having raised his snout’ subordinates *levò il muso* to *εἶπεν*: *μυκτῆρ᾽* is elided *μυκτῆρα*, accusative singular of *μυκτήρ*; *ἄρας* is the nominative masculine singular aorist active participle of *αἴρω*. By contrast *σείων κεφαλήν* has the present active participle of *σείω*, concurrent with ‘said’, like *crollando il capo*. Neither body part takes an article in the Greek.
- *ἄκουσον πονηρίαν* follows *Odi malizia* with the second singular aorist active imperative of *ἀκούω* and an accusative of the thing heard. *ἣν ἐπενόησ᾽* ‘which he devised’ uses a feminine accusative relative agreeing with *πονηρίαν*; *ἐπενόησ᾽* is elided *ἐπενόησε*, third singular aorist active indicative of *ἐπινοέω*, augmented after the prefix, for the Italian compound perfect *ha pensata*.
- *ἵν᾽ ὑποδύῃ κάτω* expands *per gittarsi giuso* into a purpose clause. *ἵν᾽* is elided *ἵνα*; *ὑποδύῃ* is third singular present active subjunctive of *ὑποδύω*, used intransitively, ‘slip under’. *κάτω* retains the downward direction of *giuso*, but there is no separate reflexive.

> 109 Ond’ei, ch’avea lacciuoli a gran divizia,  
> 110 rispose: “Malizioso son io troppo,  
> 111 quand’io procuro a’ miei maggior tristizia.”  

Whereat he, who had artifices in great store, replied: “Too malicious indeed! when I contrive for my companions greater sorrow.”

> 109 Ἀλλ᾽ ἐκεῖνος, ὅστις ἦν πολὺς ἐν τέχναις,  
> (But he, who was rich in wiles,)
>
> 110 Ἀπεκρίθη· “Πονηρὸς εἴμ᾽ ἐγὼ πάνυ,  
> (Answered: “Very malicious indeed am I,)
>
> 111 „Ὅτ᾽ ἐπάγω τοῖς ἐμοῖς μεγίστας λύπας.”  
> („When I bring the greatest sorrows upon my own folk.”)

- *ἀλλ᾽ ἐκεῖνος* ‘but that one’ substitutes contrast for the connective *Ond’ei*. *ὅστις ἦν πολὺς ἐν τέχναις* ‘who was abundant in devices’ uses the characterizing relative *ὅστις* and a predicate adjective with *ἐν* and the dative of sphere. Where *avea lacciuoli a gran divizia* predicates possession and uses the image ‘snares’, *πολύς* characterizes the man and *τέχναις* names his devices directly.
- *ἀπεκρίθη* is third singular aorist passive in form but active in sense, ‘answered’, from *ἀποκρίνομαι*. *πονηρὸς εἴμ᾽ ἐγὼ πάνυ* repeats *πονηρίαν* through its cognate adjective, as *Malizioso* echoes *malizia*. *εἴμ᾽* is elided *εἰμί*, first singular present; the strong subject pronoun follows it as in *son io*. *πάνυ* means ‘very’ or ‘indeed’, not necessarily the excess of *troppo*.
- *ὅτ᾽ ἐπάγω* ‘when I bring upon’ has elided *ὅτε* with the first singular present active indicative of *ἐπάγω*. *τοῖς ἐμοῖς* ‘upon my own people’ is the substantival possessive in the dative plural, closely matching *a’ miei*. *μεγίστας λύπας*, accusative feminine plural, replaces comparative singular *maggior tristizia* with a superlative and a countable plural, ‘the greatest sorrows’.

> 112 Alichin non si tenne, e di rintoppo  
> 113 agli altri, disse a lui: “Se tu ti cali,  

Alichino held in no longer, and in opposition to the others said to him: “If thou stoop,

> 112 Οὐ μὴν κατέσχεν ἑαυτὸν Ἀλικῖνος,  
> (Alichino, however, did not restrain himself,)
>
> 113 Ἄλλοις τ᾽ ἀντιστὰς εἶπεν· “Ἤν ὑποδύῃς,  
> (And opposing the others said: “If you dive down,)

- *οὐ μὴν κατέσχεν ἑαυτόν* ‘he did not, however, restrain himself’ uses adversative *μήν* with *οὐ*, not a double negative. *κατέσχεν* is third singular second-aorist active indicative of *κατέχω*, with the augment after the prefix; *ἑαυτόν* is its masculine singular reflexive object, for *si*. *Ἀλικῖνος* moves from the head of the Italian clause to the end of the Greek one.
- *ἄλλοις τ᾽ ἀντιστάς* ‘and having opposed the others’ turns *di rintoppo agli altri* into a participial construction. *τ᾽* is elided postpositive *τε*; *ἀντιστάς* is the nominative masculine singular intransitive second-aorist active participle of *ἀνθίστημι*, governing the dative *ἄλλοις*. *εἶπεν* has no expressed equivalent of *a lui*.
- *ἤν* is a contracted form of conditional *ἐάν*, not the imperfect *ἦν*. It introduces a prospective condition with *ὑποδύῃς*, second singular present active subjunctive of *ὑποδύω*. For *Se tu ti cali* Musurus thus repeats the verb of line 108, now addressed directly to its subject.

> 114 io non ti verrò dietro di galoppo,  
> 115 ma batterò sopra la pece l’ali;  

I will not follow thee at gallop, but beat my wings above the pitch;

> 114 „Οὐ διώξω σ᾽ ὄπισθεν ἀπὸ ῥυτῆρος,  
> („I will not pursue you behind at full gallop,)
>
> 115 „Ἀλλὰ σείσω πτέρυγας ἐπὶ τῆς πίσσης.  
> („But I will beat my wings over the pitch.)

- *οὐ διώξω σ᾽ ὄπισθεν* ‘I shall not pursue you from behind’ gives the future apodosis. *διώξω* is first singular future active indicative of *διώκω*, taking elided accusative *σ᾽* (*σε*) where *ti verrò dietro* has a verb of coming. *ὄπισθεν* retains *dietro* adverbially; *ἀπὸ ῥυτῆρος*, with the genitive singular of *ῥυτήρ* ‘rein’, is an idiom for going at full speed, corresponding to *di galoppo*.
- *ἀλλὰ σείσω πτέρυγας* ‘but I shall shake wings’ balances the two future verbs as in Italian. *σείσω* is first singular future active indicative of *σείω*; *πτέρυγας*, accusative plural of *πτέρυξ*, is anarthrous, unlike *l’ali*. The object precedes *ἐπὶ τῆς πίσσης*, where *ἐπί* with the genitive locates the beating of the wings over the pitch.

> 116 lascisi il colle, e sia la ripa scudo  
> 117 a veder se tu sol più di noi vali.”  

let the height be left, and be the bank a screen, to see if thou alone prevailest over us.”

> 116 „Λόφον ἀφέντες ἄγωμεν πρὸς τὴν ὄχθην,  
> („Leaving the ridge, let us go toward the bank,)
>
> 117 „Ἵν’ ἴδῃς, εἰ σὺ μόνος ἡμῶν ἰσχύεις.”  
> („That you may see whether you alone are stronger than we.”)

- *λόφον ἀφέντες* ‘having left the ridge’ replaces impersonal or passive jussive *lascisi il colle* with an active participle: *ἀφέντες* is nominative masculine plural, second-aorist active participle of *ἀφίημι*, agreeing with the implied ‘we’ of *ἄγωμεν*. The latter is a first plural present active hortatory subjunctive of *ἄγω*, used intransitively, ‘let us go’.
- *πρὸς τὴν ὄχθην* ‘toward the bank’ makes *la ripa* the destination of that movement. The Greek does not reproduce *sia … scudo*: it substitutes an action by ‘us’ for an injunction about what the bank is to be.
- *ἵν᾽ ἴδῃς* ‘so that you may see’ gives *a veder* a definite second-person subject; *ἴδῃς* is the second singular second-aorist active subjunctive of *ὁράω*. *εἰ* then introduces an indirect yes-or-no question with *ἰσχύεις*, second singular present active indicative of *ἰσχύω*. *σὺ μόνος* reproduces *tu sol*, and *ἡμῶν* is most naturally partitive with *μόνος*: ‘whether you alone of us are strong’. Where *più di noi vali* compares one with the rest, Musurus sets him apart from the rest, without a comparative, beside an unmodified verb meaning ‘be strong’.

## Lines 118–151 — The Navarrese Escapes and the Devils Grapple

> 118 O tu che leggi, udirai nuovo ludo!  

O Reader, thou shalt hear new sport!

> 118 Σὺ δ’ ἀναγνῶστα, νέον ἀγῶν’ ἀκούσεις.  
> (Now you, O reader, shall hear of a new contest.)

- *Σὺ δ’ ἀναγνῶστα* ‘and you, reader’ brings the subject pronoun to the front and turns the relative *tu che leggi* into a noun of address: *ἀναγνῶστα* is the vocative singular of *ἀναγνώστης*. There is no separate equivalent of the exclamatory *O*; elided *δέ* connects the address to what precedes.
- *νέον ἀγῶν’ ἀκούσεις* puts the object before the verb, reversing *udirai nuovo ludo*. *ἀγῶν’* is elided *ἀγῶνα*, accusative singular of *ἀγών*: ‘contest’ gives *ludo* a more competitive sense than “sport.” *ἀκούσεις* is the second singular future active indicative of *ἀκούω*, corresponding directly to *udirai*.

> 119 Ciascun dall’ altra costa gli occhi volse;  
> 120 quei prima, ch’ a ciò fare era più crudo.  

All turned their eyes toward the other side, he first who had been most unripe for doing it.

> 119 Ἑκατέρωθεν ὄμματ’ ἔστρεψαν πάντες,  
> (On either side all turned their eyes,)
>
> 120 Πρῶτος δ’ ὁ φανεὶς μᾶλλον ἰσχυρογνώμων.  
> (And first he who had shown himself the most stubborn.)

- *Ἑκατέρωθεν* ‘from either side’ is not directional in the same way as *dall’ altra costa*, here rendered “toward the other side.” Greek puts this adverb first and the subject *πάντες* ‘all’ last; Dante begins with the distributive singular *Ciascun*. Accordingly *ἔστρεψαν* is third plural aorist active indicative of *στρέφω*, against singular *volse*. *ὄμματ’* is elided *ὄμματα*, the neuter plural object; as in Italian, whose eyes they are needs no possessive.
- *Πρῶτος δ’ ὁ φανεὶς μᾶλλον ἰσχυρογνώμων* ‘and first [was] the one who showed himself more obstinate’ has an understood verb. *πρῶτος* is predicative; *ὁ φανείς* is an articular nominative masculine singular participle, aorist passive in form from *φαίνω*, intransitive in sense. *ἰσχυρογνώμων* is its predicate, modified by comparative *μᾶλλον*. Musurus thus interprets *più crudo* as resistance of will, drops *a ciò fare*, and substitutes a manifestation of character for the state expressed by *era*.

> 121 Lo Navarrese ben suo tempo colse,  
> 122 fermò le piante a terra, ed in un punto  
> 123 saltò, e dal proposto lor si sciolse.  

The Navarrese chose well his time; planted his soles upon the ground, and in an instant leapt and from their purpose freed himself.

> 121 Καιροῦ δὲ λαβόμενος ὁ Ναυαῤῥαῖος  
> (And seizing his opportunity, the Navarrese)
>
> 122 Ἤρεισ’ ἐπὶ γῆς πόδας, κἀναπηδήσας  
> (Planted his feet upon the ground, and leaping up)
>
> 123 Σκιρτηδὸν αὐτῶν τὰς προσβολὰς διέδρα.  
> (At a bound escaped their assaults.)

- *Καιροῦ δὲ λαβόμενος ὁ Ναυαῤῥαῖος* ‘and the Navarrese, taking hold of the opportunity’ subordinates *ben suo tempo colse* to the next verb. *λαβόμενος* is the nominative masculine singular second aorist middle participle of *λαμβάνω*; the middle in the sense ‘take hold of’ governs the fronted genitive *καιροῦ*. *καιρός* itself denotes the opportune moment, without separate words for *ben* or *suo*. In the ethnic name *Ναυαῤῥαῖος* the two breathings mark medial double rho in an older spelling convention.
- *Ἤρεισ’ ἐπὶ γῆς πόδας* ‘he braced his feet on the ground’ renders *fermò le piante a terra*: *ἤρεισ’* is elided *ἤρεισε*, third singular aorist active indicative of *ἐρείδω*, with temporal augment. The accusative *πόδας* gives the whole feet rather than the soles; *ἐπί* with the genitive specifies the supporting surface.
- *κἀναπηδήσας* is crasis for *καὶ ἀναπηδήσας*; the aorist active participle of *ἀναπηδάω*, agreeing with the Navarrese, makes *saltò* preliminary to the main verb. *σκιρτηδόν* ‘at a bound’ emphasizes manner rather than the time specified by *in un punto*. *αὐτῶν τὰς προσβολὰς διέδρα* ‘he escaped their assaults’ puts the possessive genitive before the object of *διέδρα* (third singular root aorist active indicative of *διαδιδράσκω*). Thus *dal proposto lor si sciolse*, reflexive with a phrase of separation, becomes an active verb taking the attacks themselves as object.

> 124 Di che ciascun di colpa fu compunto,  
> 125 ma quei più che cagion fu del difetto;  
> 126 però si mosse, e gridò: “Tu se’ giunto!”  

Thereat each was stung [with guilt]; but he most who had been cause of the mistake; he therefore started forth, and shouted: “Thou’rt caught!”

> 124 Αἴφνης ἕκαστος πρὸς τοῦτο κατεπλάγη,  
> (Suddenly each one was astounded at this,)
>
> 125 Ὁ δ’ ὑπαίτιος ἔτι μᾶλλον τῶν ἄλλων·  
> (And he who was to blame even more than the others;)
>
> 126 Ἐφ’ ᾧ κινηθεὶς ἔκραξεν· “Ἔχομαι σου.”  
> (Whereat, roused, he cried out: “I have hold of you!”)

- *Αἴφνης ἕκαστος πρὸς τοῦτο κατεπλάγη* ‘suddenly each was astounded at this’ retains the distributive singular *ciascun*. *πρὸς τοῦτο* expresses the occasion (*Di che*); *αἴφνης* supplies suddenness. *κατεπλάγη*, third singular aorist passive indicative of *καταπλήσσω*, expresses shock, not the prick of guilt in *di colpa fu compunto*.
- *ὁ δ’ ὑπαίτιος ἔτι μᾶλλον τῶν ἄλλων* ‘but the one responsible still more than the others’ carries *κατεπλάγη* over without repeating it. The substantivized adjective *ὁ ὑπαίτιος* replaces *quei … che cagion fu del difetto*; the mistake is not separately named. Greek expands *più* with intensive *ἔτι* and the genitive of comparison *τῶν ἄλλων*.
- *Ἐφ’ ᾧ* ‘whereupon’ is *ἐπί* with the dative neuter relative, its final vowel elided and its consonant aspirated before the rough breathing. *κινηθείς*, nominative masculine singular aorist passive participle of *κινέω*, subordinates *si mosse* to *ἔκραξεν*, third singular aorist active indicative of *κράζω*. *Ἔχομαι σου* ‘I have hold of you’ uses the first singular present middle of *ἔχω* with a genitive of the person grasped: it reverses the perspective of *Tu se’ giunto!*, which makes the person caught the subject.

> 127 Ma poco i valse, chè l’ ale al sospetto  
> 128 non potero avanzar; quegli andò sotto,  
> 129 e quei drizzò, volando suso, il petto:  

But little it availed [him]; for wings could not outspeed the terror; the sinner went under; and he, flying, raised up his breast:

> 127 Πλὴν μάτην· οὐ γὰρ αἱ πτέρυγες τὸν φόβον  
> (Yet in vain; for his fear his wings in no way)
>
> 128 Ὑπερέβησαν. Ὁ μὲν ὑπέδυ κάτω,  
> (Outstripped. The one dived down below,)
>
> 129 Ὁ δ’ ἱπτάμενος ἠνώρθωσε τὸ στῆθος.  
> (And the other, flying, lifted up his breast.)

- *Πλὴν μάτην* ‘but in vain’ compresses *Ma poco i valse* into two adverbs, with neither finite verb nor personal object. In *οὐ γὰρ αἱ πτέρυγες τὸν φόβον ὑπερέβησαν*, postpositive *γάρ* gives the reason; *τὸν φόβον* interprets *al sospetto* as the fear to be outstripped. *ὑπερέβησαν* is third plural root aorist active indicative of *ὑπερβαίνω*, with the augment inside the compound. ‘Did not outstrip’ replaces the modal *non potero avanzar* with a statement of outcome.
- *Ὁ μὲν … ὁ δ’* ‘the one … the other’ makes the contrast between *quegli* and *quei* explicit. *ὑπέδυ* is the third singular intransitive root aorist of *ὑποδύω*, ‘went under’; *κάτω* reinforces the downward direction. *ἱπτάμενος*, present middle participle of *ἵπταμαι*, agrees with the second subject and gives the simultaneous action of *volando*. *ἠνώρθωσε τὸ στῆθος* ‘raised his breast upright’ renders *drizzò … il petto*; *ἠνώρθωσε* is third singular aorist active indicative of *ἀνορθόω*. The upward direction of *suso* is not separately expressed.

> 130 non altrimenti l’ anitra di botto,  
> 131 quando il falcon s’ appressa, giù s’ attuffa,  
> 132 ed ei ritorna su crucciato e rotto.  

not otherwise the duck suddenly dives down, when the falcon approaches, and he returns up angry and defeated.

> 130 Οὐκ ἄλλως ἀναπηδῶσ’ ἡ νῆσσα, κίρκου  
> (Not otherwise, leaping up, the duck, the falcon)
>
> 131 Ἐγγὺς φανέντος, ἑαυτὴν ἐμβυθίζει·  
> (Appearing near, plunges herself down,)
>
> 132 Ὁ δ’ ἵπτατ’ ἄνω κατηφής, πλήρης τ’ αἴσχους.  
> (While the other flew upward downcast and full of shame.)

- *Οὐκ ἄλλως* closely reproduces *non altrimenti*. *ἀναπηδῶσ’*, however, is elided *ἀναπηδῶσα*, nominative feminine singular present active participle of *ἀναπηδάω*, agreeing with *ἡ νῆσσα*: ‘springing up’ adds a movement rather than translating temporal *di botto*. *νῆσσα* has *-σσ-* where specifically Attic spelling has *-ττ-*.
- *κίρκου ἐγγὺς φανέντος* ‘the hawk having appeared near’ is a genitive absolute replacing *quando il falcon s’ appressa*. *φανέντος* is the genitive masculine singular aorist passive participle of *φαίνω*, intransitive as in line 120; *ἐγγύς* is adverbial. *ἑαυτὴν ἐμβυθίζει* renders *giù s’ attuffa* with the full feminine reflexive object and a third singular present active verb, ‘plunges herself in’.
- *Ὁ δ’ ἵπτατ’ ἄνω* ‘but the other flew upward’ uses the article pronominally. *ἵπτατ’* represents elided *ἵπτατο*, third singular imperfect middle of *ἵπταμαι* without a marked augment: the Greek shifts from the present to a past description, and does not distinguish returning from flying. *κατηφής, πλήρης τ’ αἴσχους* substitutes ‘downcast and full of shame’ for *crucciato e rotto*. Both adjectives are nominative predicates of the bird; elided *τε* links them, and *πλήρης* takes the genitive *αἴσχους*.

> 133 Irato Calcabrina della buffa,  
> 134 volando dietro gli tenne, invaghito  
> 135 che quei campasse per aver la zuffa.  

Calcabrina, furious at the trick, kept flying after him, desirous that the sinner might escape, to have a quarrel.

> 133 Ὀργισθεὶς Καλκαβρίνας ἐπὶ τῇ πράξει,  
> (Calcabrina, enraged at the deed,)
>
> 134 Πτὰς ὄπισθεν ὥρμησεν ἐπ’ αὐτὸν χαίρων,  
> (Flying behind, rushed upon him, rejoicing)
>
> 135 Τῆς κείνου φυγῆς οὔσης αἰτίας νείκους.  
> (That the other's escape was a cause for a quarrel.)

- *Ὀργισθεὶς Καλκαβρίνας ἐπὶ τῇ πράξει* preserves the opening order of *Irato Calcabrina della buffa*. *ὀργισθείς*, nominative masculine singular aorist passive participle of *ὀργίζω*, means ‘having become angry’; *ἐπί* with the dative gives the ground of the anger. *πρᾶξις* is the neutral ‘deed’, not specifically a *buffa* ‘trick’.
- *Πτὰς ὄπισθεν ὥρμησεν ἐπ’ αὐτόν* ‘having flown behind, he rushed at him’ alters the aspect of *volando dietro gli tenne*. *πτάς* is the poetic nominative masculine singular root aorist active participle associated with *πέτομαι*; *ὥρμησεν* is third singular aorist active indicative of *ὁρμάω*, with temporal augment. *ὄπισθεν* gives the position, while *ἐπ’ αὐτόν* gives a direction more aggressive than merely following.
- *χαίρων* ‘rejoicing’, a present active participle of *χαίρω*, replaces *invaghito*. The accompanying circumstance *τῆς κείνου φυγῆς οὔσης αἰτίας νείκους* is a genitive absolute: *φυγῆς* is its subject, *οὔσης* the feminine singular present participle of *εἰμί*, and *αἰτίας* its predicate, governing *νείκους* ‘of strife’. *κείνου* is the shortened poetic form of possessive *ἐκείνου*. Where *che quei campasse per aver la zuffa* presents escape as desired and the quarrel as its purpose, Greek treats ‘his escape being cause for strife’ as a circumstance already giving pleasure; it uses neither a purpose clause nor an optative.

> 136 E come il barattier fu disparito,  
> 137 così volse gli artigli al suo compagno,  
> 138 e fu con lui sopra il fosso ghermito.  

And, when the barrator had disappeared, he turned his talons on his fellow, and was clutched with him above the ditch.

> 136 Ὡς οὖν ἐγένετ᾽ ἀφανὴς ὁ φηλήτης,  
> (So when the swindler had vanished from sight,)
>
> 137 Ἔτρεψ᾽ ὄνυχας κατὰ τοῦ συνεταίρου,  
> (He turned his claws against his companion,)
>
> 138 Κἀπὶ τοῦ βόθρου μετ᾽ αὐτοῦ συνεπλάκη.  
> (And over the pit grappled together with him.)

- *Ὡς οὖν ἐγένετ’ ἀφανὴς ὁ φηλήτης* ‘when, then, the cheat became invisible’ answers to *E come il barattier fu disparito*. Temporal *ὡς* introduces an indicative clause; continuative *οὖν* is postpositive. Elided *ἐγένετο* is third singular second aorist middle of *γίγνομαι*, with predicate adjective *ἀφανής*. *φηλήτης* is a cheat or impostor rather than specifically a *barattier*.
- *Ἔτρεψ’ ὄνυχας κατὰ τοῦ συνεταίρου* begins the main clause without a correlative for *così*. *ἔτρεψ’* is elided *ἔτρεψε*, third singular aorist active indicative of *τρέπω*; *ὄνυχας* is the accusative plural of *ὄνυξ*. *κατά* with the genitive makes *al suo compagno* expressly hostile, ‘against his associate’; the possessive is understood.
- *Κἀπὶ τοῦ βόθρου* (crasis of *καὶ ἐπί*) fronts the position *sopra il fosso*. *μετ’ αὐτοῦ συνεπλάκη* keeps *con lui*, but *συνεπλάκη*, third singular second aorist passive of *συμπλέκω*, has reciprocal force with the companion phrase: ‘he grappled with him’, rather than simply suffering a grip.

> 139 Ma l’ altro fu bene sparvier grifagno  
> 140 ad artigliar ben lui, e ambo e due  
> 141 cadder nel mezzo del bollente stagno.  

But the other was indeed a sparrowhawk to claw him well; and both dropt down into the middle of the boiling pond.

> 139 Ὁ δ᾽ ἕτερος ὢν ἀλκίμαχος ἱέραξ  
> (But the other, being a fierce-fighting hawk,)
>
> 140 Ἥρπαξ᾽ αὐτὸν ὄνυξιν· ὥστ᾽ ἐν τῷ μέσῳ  
> (Seized him with his talons; so that into the middle)
>
> 141 Ἔπεσον ἄμφω τῆς καχλαζούσης πίσσης·  
> (Of the bubbling pitch they both fell;)

- *Ὁ δ’ ἕτερος ὢν ἀλκίμαχος ἱέραξ* ‘but the other, being a stout-fighting hawk’ turns finite *fu* into *ὤν*, nominative masculine singular present participle of *εἰμί*, with predicate nominative *ἱέραξ*. *ἀλκίμαχος* expresses fighting quality rather than precisely reproducing *grifagno*; *ἱέραξ* is less specific than *sparvier*. Neither *bene* nor the following *ben* has a separate adverb.
- *Ἥρπαξ’ αὐτὸν ὄνυξιν* makes *ad artigliar … lui* the main assertion: ‘he seized him with talons’. Elided *ἥρπαξε* is third singular aorist active indicative of *ἁρπάζω*; *ὄνυξιν* is instrumental dative plural of *ὄνυξ*. Greek thus distributes the sense of *artigliar* between a verb of seizure and its instrument.
- *ὥστ’ … ἔπεσον* replaces coordinating *e* with elided consecutive *ὥστε* and an indicative of actual result. *ἔπεσον* is third plural second aorist active of *πίπτω*; *ἄμφω* ‘both’ is the nominative dual, here with a plural rather than a dual verb, compressing *ambo e due*. *ἐν τῷ μέσῳ … τῆς καχλαζούσης πίσσης* describes the place of the fall with *ἐν* and the dative rather than a directional phrase. *καχλαζούσης*, genitive feminine singular present active participle of *καχλάζω*, modifies *πίσσης*: the ‘bubbling pitch’ names the substance where *bollente stagno* names the pond.

> 142 Lo caldo sghermitor subito fue;  
> 143 ma però di levarsi era niente,  
> 144 sì aveano inviscate l’ ale sue.  

The heat at once unclutched them; but rise they could not, their wings were so beglued.

> 142 Ἡ καῦσις δ᾽ αὐτοὺς διεχώρισ᾽ εὐθέως·  
> (And the burning heat parted them straightway;)
>
> 143 Ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ εἶχον πῶς ἑαυτοὺς ἀνορθῶσαι,  
> (But they had no way to raise themselves up,)
>
> 144 Ὡς ἔχοντες τὰ πτερὰ πεπισσωμένα.  
> (Since they had their wings smeared with pitch.)

- *Ἡ καῦσις δ’ αὐτοὺς διεχώρισ’ εὐθέως* ‘and the burning parted them immediately’ makes the effect a transitive verb with an expressed object. Dante instead makes *Lo caldo* the subject of *fue* with agent noun *sghermitor* as predicate. Elided *διεχώρισε* is third singular aorist active indicative of *διαχωρίζω*; *εὐθέως* retains *subito*.
- *Ἀλλ’ οὐκ εἶχον πῶς ἑαυτοὺς ἀνορθῶσαι* recasts impersonal *di levarsi era niente* as personal ‘they had no way to raise themselves’. *εἶχον* is third plural imperfect active indicative of *ἔχω*; *πῶς* introduces the question of means with the aorist active infinitive *ἀνορθῶσαι* from *ἀνορθόω*. The active infinitive with plural reflexive *ἑαυτούς* corresponds to reflexive *levarsi*.
- *Ὡς ἔχοντες τὰ πτερὰ πεπισσωμένα* gives *sì aveano inviscate l’ ale sue* as a causal participial phrase rather than a finite clause of degree. *ὡς* with nominative plural *ἔχοντες* presents the circumstance as the explanation. *πεπισσωμένα*, accusative neuter plural perfect passive participle of *πισσόω*, is predicative to *τὰ πτερά* after *ἔχοντες*: they have their wings in the resulting state of being coated with pitch. The article suffices for *sue*.

> 145 Barbariccia, con gli altri suoi dolente,  
> 146 quattro ne fe’ volar dall’ altra costa  
> 147 con tutti i raffi, ed assai prestamente  
> 148 di qua, di là discesero alla posta;  

Barbariccia with the rest lamenting, made four of them fly over to the other coast with all their drags; and most rapidly on this side, on that, they descended to the stand;

> 145 Βαρβαρικίας σὺν τοῖς ἄλλοις οἰκτείρων,  
> (Barbariccia, grieving along with the others,)
>
> 146 Τέσσαρας εἰς τὴν πέραν ἔπεμψεν ὄχθην  
> (Sent four across to the opposite bank)
>
> 147 Μετὰ δικράνων πάντων, καὶ ταχὺ μάλα  
> (With all their pitchforks, and very swiftly)
>
> 148 Ἔνθεν κἀκεῖθεν ἀπῆλθον εἰς τὸν τόπον,  
> (From this side and that they went down to the post,)

- *Βαρβαρικίας σὺν τοῖς ἄλλοις οἰκτείρων* closely keeps the order of *Barbariccia, con gli altri suoi dolente*. *σύν* governs the dative plural; there is no possessive for *suoi*. *οἰκτείρων*, nominative masculine singular present active participle of *οἰκτείρω*, agrees with Barbariccia alone and suggests ‘pitying’, without expressing whom he pities.
- *Τέσσαρας εἰς τὴν πέραν ἔπεμψεν ὄχθην* ‘he sent four to the bank beyond’ simplifies causative *quattro ne fe’ volar*: *ἔπεμψεν* is third singular aorist active indicative of *πέμπω*, with no verb of flight, and masculine accusative *τέσσαρας* stands alone without a partitive for *ne*. *πέραν* functions attributively between article and noun, here separated from *ὄχθην* by the verb. *τέσσαρας*, like *νῆσσα* above, shows *-σσ-* rather than Attic *-ττ-*.
- *μετὰ δικράνων πάντων* uses *μετά* with the genitive of accompaniment; *πάντων* agrees with *δικράνων* ‘two-pronged forks’, a more specific shape than *raffi*. *καὶ ταχὺ μάλα* gives *ed assai prestamente* as adverbial neuter *ταχύ* followed by its intensifier *μάλα*.
- *Ἔνθεν κἀκεῖθεν* ‘from here and from there’ (crasis of *καὶ ἐκεῖθεν*) treats *di qua, di là* as points of departure. *ἀπῆλθον*, third plural second aorist active indicative of *ἀπέρχομαι*, means ‘went off’, without the downward motion of *discesero*. *εἰς τὸν τόπον* ‘to the place’ gives the destination but not the particular sense of an assigned position in *alla posta*.

> 149 porser gli uncini verso gl’ impaniati,  
> 150 ch’ eran già cotti dentro dalla crosta;  

they stretched their hooks towards the limed *pair*, who were already scalded within the crust;

> 149 Ἅρπας τ᾽ ἔτεινον πρὸς τοὺς πισσηλιμμένους,  
> (And stretched out their hooks toward the pitch-smeared ones,)
>
> 150 Ἐντὸς τῆς πλακὸς ἑψητοὺς ὄντας ἤδη·  
> (Who were already boiled within the crust;)

- *Ἅρπας τ’ ἔτεινον* ‘and they extended hooks’ fronts the object, accusative plural of *ἅρπη*, and adds connective *τε*, elided before the verb. *ἔτεινον* is third plural imperfect active indicative of *τείνω*: the reaching is presented in progress, against Italian preterite *porser*. *πρὸς τοὺς πισσηλιμμένους* retains the direction of *verso gl’ impaniati*. The article substantivizes the accusative masculine plural perfect passive participle: its compound combines pitch with the notion of being smeared (*ἀλείφω*), replacing the image of birdlime in *impaniati*.
- *Ἐντὸς τῆς πλακὸς ἑψητοὺς ὄντας ἤδη* ‘being already boiled inside the slab’ replaces relative *ch’ eran già cotti* with a participle agreeing with the preceding accusative plural. *ὄντας* is present active from *εἰμί*, with predicate verbal adjective *ἑψητούς* ‘boiled’; final *ἤδη* corresponds to *già*. *ἐντός* governs the genitive singular *πλακός* (from *πλάξ*, a flat layer or slab) and moves *dentro dalla crosta* to the head of the phrase.

> 151 e noi lasciammo lor così impacciati.  

and we left them thus embroiled.

> 151 Κατελίπομεν δ᾽ αὐτοὺς ἐμπλέκτους οὕτω.  
> (And we left them thus entangled.)

- *Κατελίπομεν δ’ αὐτοὺς ἐμπλέκτους οὕτω* begins with the verb rather than *e noi*: postpositive *δέ* supplies the connective, and the first plural ending alone expresses the subject. *κατελίπομεν* is second aorist active indicative of *καταλείπω*, with internal augment and the aorist stem *λιπ-*. *αὐτούς* is the object and *ἐμπλέκτους* its predicate adjective, ‘entangled’; Greek postpones *οὕτω* ‘thus’ where Dante puts *così* before *impacciati*.

## Summary — From Clause to Participle

Musurus often keeps Dante’s order of perception while changing the grammar of what is perceived. The object-first *corridor vidi* remains object-first in *ἐκδρόμους εἶδον* ‘raiders I saw’; but where Dante follows *vidi* with infinitives, Musurus makes participles agree with the person seen. The present participles of the opening catalogue hold the movements in view as they happen. Elsewhere an aorist participle marks a preliminary act: *καιροῦ λαβόμενος* ‘having seized the opportunity’ subordinates what Dante gives its own finite verb in *ben suo tempo colse*. When the subject changes, a genitive absolute can take the place of a temporal clause: *κίρκου ἐγγὺς φανέντος* ‘a hawk having appeared nearby’ renders *quando il falcon s’appressa*, changing an approach in progress into the moment of appearance.

Purpose shows how readily Musurus varies his constructions even where Dante repeats one. An Italian infinitive of purpose can become *πρός* with an action noun, as in *πρὸς κουφισμὸν τοῦ πάθους* ‘for relief of the suffering’, or with an articular infinitive, as in *πρὸς τὸ πατάξαι* ‘in order to strike’. Elsewhere *ἵνα* with the subjunctive makes the agent more definite than the infinitive does: *a veder* becomes *ἵν’ ἴδῃς* ‘so that you may see’. Particles likewise clarify relations left to word order or repetition. Postpositive *δέ* often marks a change of speaker without adding a verb of speech; *ὁ μὲν … ὁ δέ* ‘the one … the other’ sets the two subjects more distinctly against each other than *quegli … quei*. Elision and crasis are frequent, sometimes drawing a whole connective phrase into one form, as in *κἀν* ‘and in’.

Musurus is often closest to Dante in an echo rather than in an individual word: *πονηρίαν* and *πονηρός* preserve the retort that turns *malizia* back on itself as *Malizioso*. Elsewhere a lexical choice amounts to an interpretation. *Προμηνύοντες ζάλην* ‘foretelling a storm’ specifies what *fanno segno* leaves the reader to infer, while *κλωπείας* ‘thefts’ and *φηλήτης* ‘cheat’ lose the reference to corrupt officeholding in *baratteria* and *barattier*. Even a concrete image may disappear while its noun survives: Musurus keeps *la ripa* as *τὴν ὄχθην* ‘the bank’, but makes it the destination of a movement rather than the *scudo* ‘shield’ it is to serve as. The close verbal correspondence makes such shifts especially clear: a familiar noun or sequence remains in place, but its grammatical role changes what the reader sees.
