# Inferno 32

## Lines 1–21 — Harsh Verse and a Warning Underfoot

> 1 S' io avessi le rime aspre e chiocce,  
> 2 come si converrebbe al tristo buco,  
> 3 sopra il qual pontan tutte l' altre rocce,  

If I had rhymes rough and hoarse, as would befit the dismal hole, on which all the other rocky steeps converge and weigh,

> 1 Εἰ τοὺς στίχους βραγχώδεις καὶ στρυφνοὺς εἶχον,  
> (If I had verses hoarse and harsh,)
>
> 2 Ὡς βαράθρῳ γοερῷ τοιῷδ᾿ ἁρμόζει,  
> (As befits such a mournful pit,)
>
> 3 Ἐφ᾿ οὗπερ πάντες οἱ λοιποὶ κρημνοὶ κεῖνται,  
> (Upon which all the other precipices rest,)

- *Εἰ … εἶχον* renders *S’ io avessi*: Greek uses *εἰ* with the imperfect active indicative (*εἶχον*, 1st sg. of *ἔχω*, with irregular augment) where Italian uses the imperfect subjunctive. The subject pronoun is unnecessary; the verb comes at the end of the line, enclosing its object within the condition. The answering *ἄν* comes in line 4.
- *τοὺς στίχους βραγχώδεις καὶ στρυφνούς* gives ‘verses hoarse and harsh’ for *le rime aspre e chiocce*. *Στίχους* denotes lines of verse without specifying rhyme. Both adjectives are accusative masculine plural (*βραγχώδης*, *στρυφνός*); they follow the noun without a repeated article, describing the quality the speaker would have his verses possess. Musurus reverses the Italian adjectives: the word for hoarseness now comes first.
- *Ὡς … ἁρμόζει* ‘as is fitting’ turns the conditional *come si converrebbe* into an unqualified present (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἁρμόζω*). *βαράθρῳ γοερῷ τοιῷδ᾿* is the dative of that for which the verses are fitting: *βάραθρον* ‘pit, chasm’ for *buco*, *γοερός* ‘mournful’ for *tristo*. The demonstrative *τοιῷδ᾿* (elided *τοιῷδε*, dat. sg. neut. of *τοιόσδε*) adds ‘of such a kind’ rather than reproducing the Italian article.
- *Ἐφ᾿ οὗπερ* ‘upon which precisely’ renders *sopra il qual*: *ἐπί* loses its final vowel, and its *π* becomes *φ* before the rough breathing of *οὗ* (gen. sg. neut. of *ὅς*, referring to *βάραθρον*); *-περ* reinforces the relative. *πάντες οἱ λοιποὶ κρημνοί* ‘all the remaining cliffs’ is the nominative subject. *κεῖνται* (3rd pl. pres. ind. of *κεῖμαι*) gives *pontan* a stative interpretation: the cliffs ‘rest upon’ the pit, without a separate word for their convergence.

> 4 io premerei di mio concetto il suco  
> 5 più pienamente; ma perch' io non l' abbo,  

I should press out the juice of my conception more fully; but since I have them not,

> 4 Τὴν διάνοιαν τὴν ἐμὴν ἂν ἐδήλουν  
> (I would declare my thought)
>
> 5 Πληρέστερον· ἀλλ᾿ ὡς ὢν ἐλλιπὴς τούτου,  
> (More fully; but since I am lacking in this,)

- *Τὴν διάνοιαν τὴν ἐμήν* ‘my thought’ replaces *di mio concetto il suco*: the thought itself, not its juice, becomes the direct object. *ἐμήν* (acc. sg. fem. of *ἐμός*) stands in the second attributive position, with the article repeated. *ἂν ἐδήλουν* ‘I would make clear’ completes the unreal condition: *ἐδήλουν* is the augmented, contracted imperfect active indicative, 1st sg. of *δηλόω*. The verb of expression replaces the physical *premerei*, while the comparative adverb *πληρέστερον*, carried into line 5, closely answers *più pienamente*.
- *ἀλλ᾿ ὡς ὢν ἐλλιπὴς τούτου* ‘but as one deficient in this’ renders *ma perch’ io non l’ abbo*. After elided *ἀλλά*, *ὡς* introduces a circumstantial participle giving the speaker’s stated reason. *ὤν* (nom. sg. masc. pres. participle of *εἰμί*) agrees with the subject of the following *ἄρχομαι*; *ἐλλιπής* is its predicate. The negative verb of possession becomes a positive adjective of deficiency governing the genitive *τούτου*. The demonstrative points to the requisite quality as ‘this’, rather than referring back to the verses in the plural.

> 6 non senza tema a dicer mi conduco:  

not without fear I bring myself to tell *thereof*:

> 6 Οὐκ ἄνευ δέους ἄρχομαι νῦν τοῦ λόγου·  
> (Not without fear do I now begin the tale;)

- *Οὐκ ἄνευ δέους* keeps both the position and the double negative expression of *non senza tema*. *οὐκ* is the form of the negative before a smooth-breathed vowel; *ἄνευ* governs *δέους*, the contracted genitive singular of neuter *δέος*.
- *ἄρχομαι νῦν τοῦ λόγου* ‘I now begin the account’ replaces *a dicer mi conduco*. *ἄρχομαι* (1st sg. pres. mid. ind. of *ἄρχω*) takes a genitive of what one begins. Thus the Italian infinitive becomes the noun *λόγου*, and the reflexive expression of bringing oneself to speak becomes an idiomatic Greek middle. *νῦν* is supplied.

> 7 chè non è impresa da pigliare a gabbo  
> 8 descriver fondo a tutto l' universo,  
> 9 nè da lingua che chiami mamma e babbo.  

for to describe the bottom of all the Universe is not an enterprise for being taken up in sport, nor for a tongue that cries *mamma* and *papa*.

> 7 Οὐ γὰρ εὐχερὲς ἔργον ἢ παιδιά τις  
> (For it is no easy task or any child's play)
>
> 8 Περιγράψαι πᾶν τοῦ Σύμπαντος τὸ βάθος  
> (To describe all the bottom of the Universe)
>
> 9 Γλώσσῃ δὴ τραυλιζούσῃ Μάμμα καὶ Πάππα.  
> (With a tongue babbling Mamma and Pappa.)

- *Οὐ γὰρ εὐχερὲς ἔργον ἢ παιδιά τις* ‘for [it is] not an easy task or a kind of play’ supplies no finite copula. Postpositive *γάρ* renders causal *chè*. *εὐχερές* (nom. sg. neut. of *εὐχερής*) qualifies *ἔργον*, while *παιδιά τις* is a second predicate nominative, with enclitic *τις* agreeing with feminine *παιδιά* ‘play’. Musurus draws ‘easy’ from the sense of *da pigliare a gabbo* but does not translate *pigliare*: treating the undertaking lightly becomes classifying it as play.
- *Περιγράψαι* (aor. act. inf. of *περιγράφω*) is the postponed subject, like *descriver*; the aorist takes the act of describing as a whole. In *πᾶν τοῦ Σύμπαντος τὸ βάθος*, *πᾶν* (acc. sg. neut. of *πᾶς*) agrees across the intervening genitive with *τὸ βάθος*: ‘the whole depth of the universe’. In *fondo a tutto l’ universo*, by contrast, *tutto* qualifies *universo*. *τοῦ Σύμπαντος* is the genitive of substantivized neuter *τὸ σύμπαν* ‘the whole, the universe’.
- *Γλώσσῃ δὴ τραυλιζούσῃ* ‘with a tongue indeed stammering’ attaches an instrumental dative to *περιγράψαι*, rather than continuing the Italian parallel *da pigliare … nè da lingua*. There is no second negative conjunction; *δή* gives emphasis. *τραυλιζούσῃ* (dat. sg. fem. pres. act. participle of *τραυλίζω*) agrees with *γλώσσῃ*: the relative *che chiami* becomes a participle specifying the manner of utterance. *Μάμμα καὶ Πάππα* give the sounds uttered directly.

> 10 Ma quelle Donne aiutino il mio verso,  
> 11 ch' aiutaro Anfion a chiuder Tebe,  
> 12 sì che dal fatto il dir non sia diverso.  

But may those Ladies help my verse, who helped Amphion with *walls* to close in Thebes; so that my words may not be diverse from the fact.

> 10 Ἀλλ᾿ εἴησάν μοι βοηθοί γ᾿ αἱ Παρθένοι,  
> (Yet may the Maidens be helpers to me,)
>
> 11 Ὡς Ἀμφίονι τειχιστῇ Θηβῶν ἦσαν,  
> (As they were to Amphion, wall-builder of Thebes,)
>
> 12 Ἵν᾿ οἱ λόγοι μὴ φανῶσ᾿ ἥττονες τοὔργου.—  
> (So that my words may not prove inferior to the deed.—)

- *Ἀλλ᾿ εἴησάν μοι βοηθοί γ᾿ αἱ Παρθένοι* ‘but may the Maidens be helpers to me’ renders *Ma quelle Donne aiutino il mio verso*. *εἴησαν* (3rd pl. pres. opt. of *εἰμί*) is an independent optative of wish, without *ἄν*, for the Italian exhortatory subjunctive. The extra accent in *εἴησάν μοι* is due to enclitic *μοι*, the dative singular of the person helped: the speaker replaces his verse. *βοηθοί* is a predicate nominative, emphasized by *γ᾿* (elided *γε*); the postponed subject *αἱ Παρθένοι* specifies ‘the Maidens’ rather than ‘those Ladies’.
- *Ὡς … ἦσαν* ‘as they were’ changes the relative *ch’ aiutaro* into a comparison, with *βοηθοί* understood again. *ἦσαν* (3rd pl. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*) describes their being helpers rather than a completed act of helping. *Ἀμφίονι* is dative like *μοι*; *τειχιστῇ* (dat. sg. of *τειχιστής*) stands in apposition to it. *τειχιστῇ Θηβῶν* ‘wall-builder of Thebes’, with the city name in the genitive plural, compresses *a chiuder Tebe* into a title and makes the wall-building explicit.
- *Ἵν᾿ οἱ λόγοι μὴ φανῶσ᾿ ἥττονες τοὔργου* ‘so that the words may not prove inferior to the deed’ renders *sì che dal fatto il dir non sia diverso*. *ἵν᾿* is elided *ἵνα*, introducing a purpose clause with negative *μή*; *φανῶσ᾿* is elided *φανῶσι*, 3rd pl. second aor. pass. subj. of *φαίνω*, intransitive ‘appear, prove’. Plural *οἱ λόγοι* replaces the substantivized infinitive *il dir*. Predicate *ἥττονες* (nom. pl. masc. of comparative *ἥττων*) makes difference into inferiority; it takes a genitive of comparison, *τοὔργου*, by crasis for *τοῦ ἔργου*.

> 13 O sopra tutte mal creata plebe,  
> 14 che stai nel loco, onde 'l parlare è duro,  
> 15 me' foste state qui pecore o zebe!  

O ye beyond all *others*, miscreated rabble, who are in the place, to speak of which is hard, better had ye here *on earth* been sheep or goats!

> 13 Ὦ κατάρατος φυλὴ πρὸ πασῶν ἄλλων,  
> (O accursed race above all others,)
>
> 14 Ἥτις ἐν δυσφάτῳ τῷδ᾿ ἔγκεισαι τόπῳ·  
> (That lies in this unspeakable place;)
>
> 15 Κρεῖττον ἦν ὑμᾶς εἶναι πρόβατα κ᾽ αἶγας.—  
> (Better had it been for you to be sheep and goats.—)

- *Ὦ κατάρατος φυλή* ‘O accursed race’ renders *O … mal creata plebe*: *κατάρατος* means ‘under a curse’, not literally ‘ill-created’; collective *φυλή* gives a people or tribe rather than a rabble. The phrase is vocative in function; the feminine of the two-termination adjective *κατάρατος* keeps *-ος*. *πρὸ πασῶν ἄλλων* ‘before all others’ follows the noun, reversing the placement of *sopra tutte*; *πρό* takes the genitive, and feminine *πασῶν* implies other groups of the same kind.
- *Ἥτις … ἔγκεισαι* ‘who lie in’ uses the feminine singular compound relative *ἥτις* (from *ὅστις*) for *che*; the verb is second person because the antecedent is addressed, as in *che stai*. *ἔγκεισαι* (2nd sg. pres. ind. of *ἔγκειμαι*) is reinforced by locative *ἐν* with the dative. *δυσφάτῳ τῷδ᾿ … τόπῳ* ‘in this hard-to-speak-of place’ turns the entire *onde ’l parlare è duro* into an adjective of *τόπῳ*. *τῷδ᾿* is elided *τῷδε* (dat. sg. masc. of *ὅδε*); the verb intervenes before the noun.
- *Κρεῖττον ἦν ὑμᾶς εἶναι* ‘it was better for you to be’ recasts *me’ foste state* impersonally. *κρεῖττον* is the neuter singular comparative of *κρείττων*, used predicatively with *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*). An imperfect of such an evaluative expression can convey an unrealized alternative without *ἄν*. *ὑμᾶς* is the accusative plural subject of the present infinitive *εἶναι*: the address shifts from the collective singular to its members. *πρόβατα κ᾽ αἶγας* are predicate accusatives (neut. pl. of *πρόβατον*, fem. pl. of *αἴξ*); shortened poetic *κ᾽* for *καί* substitutes ‘and’ for *o*. *qui* has no counterpart.

> 16 Come noi fummo giù nel pozzo scuro  
> 17 sotto i piè del gigante, assai più bassi,  
> 18 ed io mirava ancora all' alto muro,  

When we were down in the dark pit, under the Giant's feet, much lower, and I still was gazing at the high wall,

> 16 Εἰς τὸ ζοφερὸν κατέβημεν δὴ φρέαρ,  
> (Down into the gloomy well we then descended,)
>
> 17 Ὑπὸ Γίγαντος πόδας καὶ πλέον κάτω·  
> (Beneath the Giant's feet and further below;)
>
> 18 Κἀγὼ θεωρῶν ἔτι τὸ τείχους ὕψος  
> (And I, still gazing at the height of the wall,)

- *Εἰς τὸ ζοφερὸν … φρέαρ* ‘into the gloomy well’ replaces the position expressed by *giù nel pozzo scuro* with motion, *εἰς* governing the accusative singular of neuter *φρέαρ*. *κατέβημεν* (1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *καταβαίνω*, with augment after the prefix) means ‘we descended’, not ‘we were’. Musurus puts this in a main clause, with emphatic *δή* before the delayed noun; there is no conjunction answering temporal *Come*.
- *Ὑπὸ Γίγαντος πόδας* ‘under a Giant’s feet’ takes *πόδας* (acc. pl. of *πούς*) with *ὑπό* after the verb of motion; the possessive genitive *Γίγαντος* precedes the feet, reversing *i piè del gigante*. Neither Greek noun has an article. *καὶ πλέον κάτω* ‘and further down’ supplies a connective and uses adverbs, not an adjective agreeing with the speakers as *bassi* does. *πλέον* is the neuter singular comparative of *πολύς* used adverbially.
- *Κἀγὼ θεωρῶν ἔτι* ‘and I, still gazing’ begins the next main construction. *κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*; the pronoun marks the change from plural to singular. *θεωρῶν* (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *θεωρέω*) replaces the finite imperfect *mirava* and expresses action simultaneous with *Ἤκουσα* in line 19. *τὸ τείχους ὕψος* ‘the height of the wall’ makes the quality in *alto* the head noun: *τὸ* belongs to accusative *ὕψος*, while intervening *τείχους* is the contracted genitive singular of *τεῖχος*.

> 19 dicere udimmi: “Guarda come passi:  

I heard a *voice* say to me: “Look how thou passest:

> 19 Ἤκουσά του λέγοντος· “Ὅρα πῶς βαίνεις·  
> (Heard someone saying: “Watch how you walk;)

- *Ἤκουσά του λέγοντος* ‘I heard someone saying’ renders *dicere udimmi*. *ἤκουσα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀκούω*, with temporal augment) gains an accent on its final syllable before enclitic *του*, the short genitive singular of indefinite *τις*, not the article *τοῦ*. Greek uses the genitive of the person heard with a supplementary participle: *λέγοντος* (gen. sg. masc. pres. act. of *λέγω*) agrees with *του*. This is not a genitive absolute. Greek makes the indefinite speaker explicit but supplies neither a noun for “voice” nor an equivalent of recipient *-mi*.
- *Ὅρα πῶς βαίνεις* closely follows *Guarda come passi*: *ὅρα* (2nd sg. pres. act. imper. of *ὁράω*) governs an indirect question of manner with interrogative *πῶς*, not comparative *ὡς*. *βαίνεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *βαίνω*) means ‘you step, you walk’.

> 20 fa sì che tu non calchi con le piante  
> 21 le teste de' fratei miseri lassi.”  

take care that with thy soles thou tread not on the heads of the weary wretched brothers.”

> 20 „ Πρόσεχε δὴ μὴ τοῖς σοῖς ποσὶ συνθλάσῃς  
> („ Take heed now lest with your feet you crush)
>
> 21 „ Τὰς κεφαλὰς ἀδελφῶν πολυτλημόνων.”  
> („ The heads of much-enduring brothers.”)

- *Πρόσεχε δὴ μή … συνθλάσῃς* ‘take heed, then, lest you crush’ renders *fa sì che tu non calchi*. *πρόσεχε* (2nd sg. pres. act. imper. of *προσέχω*) commands continuing attention, with *δή* lending urgency. The preventive clause has *μή* and *συνθλάσῃς* (2nd sg. aor. act. subj. of *συνθλάω*): the aorist views the feared act as a whole. ‘Crush’ names the possible result rather than the act of treading.
- *τοῖς σοῖς ποσί* ‘with your feet’ is an instrumental dative for *con le piante*. *ποσί* is dat. pl. of *πούς*; *σοῖς* (dat. pl. masc. of *σός*) adds an explicit possessive between article and noun. *Τὰς κεφαλάς* remains the direct object, as in *le teste*, and likewise opens the last line.
- *ἀδελφῶν πολυτλημόνων* ‘of much-enduring brothers’ keeps the possessive relation of *de’ fratei* but omits its article. *πολυτλημόνων* (gen. pl. masc. of poetic *πολυτλήμων*) agrees with *ἀδελφῶν*: one compound adjective gathers *miseri lassi* into the idea of suffering much, without separately specifying weariness.

## Lines 22–54 — The Lake of Ice and the Clashing Shades

> 22 Per ch’ io mi volsi e vidimi davante  
> 23 e sotto i piedi un lago, che per gelo  
> 24 avea di vetro e non d’ acqua sembiante.  

Whereat I turned myself, and saw before me and beneath my feet a lake, which through frost had the semblance of glass and not of water.

> 22 Στραφεὶς δ᾽ εἶδον ἔμπροσθεν ἐμοῦ καὶ κάτω  
> (Turning round, I saw before me and beneath)
>
> 23 Τῶν ποδῶν λίμνην, ἐκ τοῦ πάγους ὑέλῳ  
> (My feet a lake, which through the frost to glass)
>
> 24 Μᾶλλον ἤπερ ὕδατι παραπλησίαν.  
> (Rather than to water bore a likeness.)

- *Στραφεὶς δ᾽ εἶδον* ‘turning, I saw’ subordinates *io mi volsi* to *vidimi*. *Στραφείς* is the nom. masc. sg. second aorist passive participle of *στρέφω*, passive in form but intransitive here; *εἶδον* is the first-person singular second aorist indicative used with *ὁράω*. The connective *δέ*, elided before *εἶδον*, does not express the full causal force of *Per ch’*. There is no reflexive pronoun with the verb of seeing.
- *ἔμπροσθεν ἐμοῦ καὶ κάτω τῶν ποδῶν* makes the two relations explicit by placing a genitive after each local adverb: ‘in front of me and below the feet’. The article with *ποδῶν* suffices where English needs a possessive. *λίμνην*, accusative object of *εἶδον*, follows both expressions, as *un lago* does in Italian.
- Instead of the relative clause *che … avea … sembiante*, Greek has the adjective *παραπλησίαν* ‘resembling’, acc. fem. sg. agreeing with *λίμνην*. It governs the datives *ὑέλῳ* (from *ὕελος* ‘glass’) and *ὕδατι* (from *ὕδωρ*). *μᾶλλον ἤπερ* ‘rather than’ turns *di vetro e non d’ acqua* into a comparison; *ἐκ τοῦ πάγους* gives the cause, *per gelo*, as the source of the resemblance.

> 25 Non fece al corso suo sì grosso velo  
> 26 di verno la Danoia in Osteric,  
> 27 nè Tanai là sotto il freddo cielo,  

Never did the Danube of Austria make so thick a veil for his course in winter, nor *the Don* afar beneath the frigid sky,

> 25 Οὔποτε παχύτερος πέπλος χειμῶνος  
> (No thicker veil of winter ever)
>
> 26 Δουνάβεως ῥοῦν ἐκάλυψ᾽ ἐν Αὐστρίᾳ,  
> (Covered the stream of the Danube in Austria,)
>
> 27 Οὔτε Τάναϊν ὑπ᾽ αἰθέρα κρυμώδη,  
> (Nor the Don under its icy sky,)

- *Οὔποτε παχύτερος πέπλος … ἐκάλυψ᾽* ‘never did a thicker veil cover’ changes both subject and verb: Dante’s river *fece … velo*, but Musurus’ *πέπλος* itself acts on *ῥοῦν* ‘stream, course’ (contracted acc. sg. of *ῥόος*, Attic *ῥοῦς*). *ἐκάλυψ᾽* is *ἐκάλυψε*, augmented third-person singular aorist active indicative of *καλύπτω*, with final vowel elided. *χειμῶνος* can be taken with *πέπλος*, ‘a veil of winter’, though the genitive also admits the temporal sense ‘in winter’ of *di verno*.
- *Δουνάβεως* is the possessive genitive dependent on *ῥοῦν*; *ἐν Αὐστρίᾳ* is explicitly locative, like *in Osteric*. With *οὔτε Τάναϊν* ‘nor the Don’, the verb of covering is understood again; *Τάναϊν* is accusative, not a second subject parallel to Dante’s *Danoia*. *ὑπ᾽* is elided *ὑπό*, here with accusative *αἰθέρα κρυμώδη* ‘beneath a frosty sky’; *κρυμώδη* is the contracted masc. acc. sg. of *κρυμώδης*. There is no separate word for *là*.

> 28 com’ era quivi: chè, se Tambernic  
> 29 vi fosse su caduto o Pietrapana,  
> 30 non avria pur dall’ orlo fatto cric.  

as there was here: for if Tambernic had fallen on it, or Pietrapana, it would not even at the edge have given a creak.

> 28 Οἷος ἐνταῦθ᾽ ἦν· εἰ γὰρ ἔπιπτεν ὧδε  
> (Than was here; for if there fell upon it)
>
> 29 Τὸ Ταβέρνικον, εἴτ᾽ Ἀπουάνα Πέτρα,  
> (Tabernicum, or Petra Apuana,)
>
> 30 Οὔτε τὸ τῆς πλακὸς ἂν ἔκρικεν ἄκρον.  
> (Not even the edge of the sheet would have cracked.)

- *Οἷος ἐνταῦθ᾽ ἦν* ‘of such a kind as there was here’ retains the qualitative relative corresponding to *com’ era quivi*: *οἷος* is nom. masc. sg., referring to *πέπλος*. The fit with comparative *παχύτερος* is less exact than with Italian *sì grosso*: the Greek starts with ‘thicker’ but finishes with ‘such as’. *ἐνταῦθ᾽* is *ἐνταῦθα* elided before *ἦν* (third sg. imperfect of *εἰμί*).
- *εἰ γὰρ ἔπιπτεν ὧδε* ‘for if there fell here’ renders *chè, se … vi fosse su caduto*. Postpositive *γάρ* falls inside the conditional clause; *ἔπιπτεν* is third sg. imperfect active indicative of *πίπτω*, not a pluperfect. *ὧδε* supplies the local reference, without a distinct equivalent for *su*. The alternative subjects follow: *Τὸ Ταβέρνικον* is neuter singular; *εἴτ᾽* is elided *εἴτε*, here introducing just the alternative, and *Ἀπουάνα Πέτρα* recasts the second name as adjective and noun, ‘Apuan Rock’.
- The condition has *εἰ* with a past indicative, and *ἄν* with a past indicative in the unreal consequence. In *οὔτε τὸ τῆς πλακὸς ἂν ἔκρικεν ἄκρον*, *οὔτε* has the force of ‘not even’, and *ἔκρικεν* is an augmented past form of the sound-verb *κρίζω* (compare the aorist *ἔκρικα*). The subject is now *τὸ … ἄκρον* ‘the edge’, enclosing the dependent genitive *τῆς πλακός* ‘of the slab’ (from *πλάξ*). Dante’s *dall’ orlo* thus becomes the thing that would make the noise, and his quoted sound *cric* becomes a verb.

> 31 E come a gracidar si sta la rana  
> 32 col muso fuor dell’ acqua, quando sogna  
> 33 di spigolar sovente la villana:  

And as the frog to croak, sits with his muzzle out of the water, when the [peasant-woman] oft dreams that she is gleaning:

> 31 Ὥσπερ δὲ κρώζων ὁ βάτραχος τὸ ῥύγχος  
> (And just as the croaking frog its snout)
>
> 32 Ἔχει τῆς λίμνης ἐκτός, ὅτε κατ᾽ ὄναρ  
> (Holds out of the pond, when in a dream)
>
> 33 Σταχυολογεῖν δοκεῖ ποθ᾽ ἡ χωρῖτις,  
> (The peasant woman at times imagines she is gleaning,)

- *Ὥσπερ δέ* opens a comparison answered by *οὕτως* in 34. *κρώζων*, nom. masc. sg. present active participle of *κρώζω*, makes *a gracidar* a simultaneous action, ‘croaking’. In place of *si sta la rana col muso fuor*, *ὁ βάτραχος τὸ ῥύγχος ἔχει* gives the frog an active verb and its snout as direct object. *τῆς λίμνης ἐκτός* places the governing adverb after its genitive, ‘outside the lake’, specifying *acqua* as the body of water.
- *ὅτε κατ᾽ ὄναρ … δοκεῖ* ‘when in a dream … she seems [to herself]’ distributes *quando sogna* between a temporal conjunction, an adverbial phrase (*κατά* elided before *ὄναρ*), and a finite verb. *σταχυολογεῖν* ‘to glean ears of grain’ is a contracted present active infinitive, with the same subject as third sg. present *δοκεῖ*. The subject *ἡ χωρῖτις* ‘the countrywoman’ is reserved to the end, like *la villana*. Enclitic *ποτέ*, written *ποθ᾽* by elision and aspiration before *ἡ*, means ‘at some time’, not expressly *sovente* ‘often’.

> 34 livide, insin là dove appar vergogna,  
> 35 eran l’ ombre dolenti nella ghiaccia,  
> 36 mettendo i denti in nota di cicogna.  

so, livid, up to where *the hue of shame* appears, *the doleful shades* were in the ice, sounding with their teeth like storks.

> 34 Οὕτως αἱ σκιαὶ πελιδναὶ καὶ γοώδεις  
> (So the shades, livid and mournful,)
>
> 35 Μέχρι παρειῶν ἦσαν ἐντὸς τοῦ πάγου,  
> (Up to their cheeks were within the ice,)
>
> 36 Ὀδόντας πλαταγοῦσαι πελαργῶν δίκην.  
> (Clattering their teeth like storks.)

- *Οὕτως αἱ σκιαὶ πελιδναὶ καὶ γοώδεις* brings *l’ ombre* forward and coordinates the two descriptions, ‘livid and lamenting’, as nom. fem. pl. adjectives. Dante separates *livide* from *dolenti* and attaches the latter directly to *ombre*. *μέχρι παρειῶν* ‘as far as the cheeks’ replaces the clause *insin là dove appar vergogna* with the bodily location Musurus understands it to indicate; *μέχρι* governs the genitive.
- *ἦσαν ἐντὸς τοῦ πάγου* ‘they were within the ice’ uses third pl. imperfect *ἦσαν* and *ἐντός* with genitive. *ὀδόντας πλαταγοῦσαι*, with acc. pl. of *ὀδούς* and nom. fem. pl. present active participle of *πλαταγέω*, agrees with *σκιαί*: ‘clattering their teeth’. The verb supplies the sound for which Dante has *in nota*; *πελαργῶν δίκην* ‘in the manner of storks’ uses the accusative *δίκην* adverbially after a genitive, and pluralizes *cicogna*.

> 37 Ognuna in giù tenea volta la faccia;  
> 38 da bocca il freddo e dagli occhi il cor tristo  
> 39 tra lor testimonianza si procaccia.  

Each held his face turned downwards; by the mouth their cold, and by the eyes *the sorrow* of their hearts is testified amongst them.

> 37 Τὴν ὄψιν εἶχεν ἑκάστη πρὸς τὰ κάτω·  
> (Each one held its face turned downward;)
>
> 38 Ἐκ στόματος τὸ ψῦχος, ἀπὸ δ’ ὀμμάτων  
> (By the mouth the cold, and from the eyes)
>
> 39 Ἡ τῆς καρδίας θλίψις ἐμαρτυρεῖτο.  
> (The grief of the heart was testified.)

- *Τὴν ὄψιν εἶχεν ἑκάστη* fronts the object ‘the face’ and postpones *ἑκάστη* ‘each’, feminine singular referring back to *σκιαί*. *εἶχεν*, third sg. imperfect of *ἔχω*, represents *tenea*; the direction *πρὸς τὰ κάτω* ‘toward the parts below’ suffices for *in giù … volta*, without a participle for ‘turned’.
- *Ἐκ στόματος τὸ ψῦχος, ἀπὸ δ᾽ ὀμμάτων ἡ τῆς καρδίας θλίψις* balances two expressions of source, each with the genitive, varying the preposition where Dante varies *da* and *dagli*. *δέ* is elided. *il cor tristo* ‘the sad heart’ becomes *ἡ … θλίψις* ‘the grief’, with *τῆς καρδίας* a genitive dependent on the abstract noun.
- *ἐμαρτυρεῖτο*, contracted third sg. imperfect middle/passive indicative of *μαρτυρέω*, is passive in sense: ‘was being attested’. It stands in the singular beside the nearer subject *θλίψις*, with the same predicate understood of *ψῦχος*. It replaces present *testimonianza si procaccia* with a single verb in the descriptive imperfect; *tra lor* has no expressed equivalent.

> 40 Quand’ io ebbi d’ intorno alquanto visto,  
> 41 volsimi a’ piedi, e vidi due sì stretti  
> 42 che il pel del capo avieno insieme misto.  

When I had looked round awhile, I turned towards my feet; and saw two so pressed *against each other*, that they had the hair of their heads intermixed.

> 40 Μικρόν τι πέριξ τὰς ὄψεις τείνας, κᾆτα  
> (Having stretched my gaze a little round about, then)
>
> 41 Πρὸς πόδας, εἶδον δύο πλησίον οὕτως,  
> (Toward my feet, I saw two so close together,)
>
> 42 Ὥστε συμμεμιγμένας εἶχον τὰς κόμας.  
> (That they had their hair mingled one with another.)

- *Μικρόν τι πέριξ τὰς ὄψεις τείνας* ‘having directed my gaze a little way around’ recasts *Quand’ io ebbi … visto* as an anterior participle. *τείνας* is nom. masc. sg. first aorist active of *τείνω*; it takes the plural *τὰς ὄψεις* ‘the looks’ as object. *μικρόν τι* is adverbial accusative, ‘some little [extent]’, with enclitic indefinite *τι*; *πέριξ* is adverbial ‘round about’.
- *κᾆτα* is crasis of *καὶ εἶτα* ‘and then’. *πρὸς πόδας* gives the second direction of the gaze, but *volsimi* has no separate finite equivalent: the main verb is *εἶδον* ‘I saw’. *δύο πλησίον οὕτως* ‘two so near’ replaces *due sì stretti* by an adverb of proximity rather than a word for pressure.
- *ὥστε … εἶχον* gives an actual result with the indicative, following *sì … che … avieno*. *εἶχον* is here third pl. imperfect, not dual despite *δύο*. *συμμεμιγμένας*, acc. fem. pl. perfect middle/passive participle of *συμμίγνυμι*, is predicative with *τὰς κόμας*: ‘they had their locks in a mingled state’. The compound expresses *insieme*; *del capo* is implicit in ‘locks’.

> 43 “Ditemi voi, che sì stringete i petti,”  
> 44 diss’ io, “chi siete.” E quei piegaro i colli;  
> 45 e poi ch’ ebber li visi a me eretti,  

“Tell me, ye who thus *together* press your bosoms,” said I, “who you are.” And they bended their necks; and when they had raised their faces towards me,

> 43 “Ὑμεῖς οἱ στήθη σφίγγοντες, εἴπατέ μοι,”  
> (“You who press your breasts together, tell me,”)
>
> 44 Ἔκραξα, “τίνες ἐστέ;” Τότ’ οὖν αὐχένας  
> (I cried out, “who are you?” Then, their necks)
>
> 45 Κάμψαντες, ἀνέτειναν πρός με τὸ βλέμμα·  
> (Bending, they lifted up their gaze toward me;)

- *Ὑμεῖς οἱ στήθη σφίγγοντες* ‘you who press breasts’ puts the address before the command. The article and nom. masc. pl. present active participle of *σφίγγω* stand in apposition to the emphatic pronoun, in place of the Italian relative clause; *στήθη* is acc. neut. pl. of *στῆθος*. Neither *sì* nor the mutual direction of the pressure is separately expressed.
- *εἴπατέ μοι* is second pl. aorist active imperative (the *εἶπα* form belonging to *λέγω*) with enclitic dative ‘to me’, hence the extra accent on the verb. *ἔκραξα*, first sg. aorist active of *κράζω*, heightens *diss’ io* to ‘I cried out’. *τίνες ἐστέ;* gives *chi siete* as a direct question, with interrogative *τίνες* and second pl. present of *εἰμί*.
- *Τότ᾽ οὖν* ‘then, accordingly’ elides *τότε*. Musurus reverses the Italian syntactic hierarchy: finite *piegaro* becomes *αὐχένας κάμψαντες* ‘having bent their necks’ (nom. masc. pl. aorist active participle of *κάμπτω*), while temporal *poi ch’ ebber … eretti* becomes the main assertion *ἀνέτειναν πρός με τὸ βλέμμα* ‘they raised their gaze toward me’. *ἀνέτειναν* is third pl. aorist active of *ἀνατείνω*, with internal augment; singular *βλέμμα* replaces plural *visi*.

> 46 gli occhi lor, ch’ eran pria pur dentro molli,  
> 47 gocciar su per le labbra, e il gelo strinse  
> 48 le lagrime tra essi, e riserrolli.  

their eyes, which only inwardly were moist before, gushed at the lids, and the frost bound fast the tears between them, and closed them up again.

> 46 Τὰ δ’ ὄμματ’ αὐτῶν, ὑγρὰ πρὶν ἔνδον ὄντα,  
> (And their eyes, which before were wet within,)
>
> 47 Ἔσταζον ἐκ βλεφάρων· εἶτα δ’ ὁ πάγος  
> (Dripped from their eyelids; and then the frost)
>
> 48 Τὰ δάκρυα συμπήξας ἔκλεισε πάλιν.  
> (Congealed the tears and locked them shut again.)

- *Τὰ δ᾽ ὄμματ᾽ αὐτῶν* begins a new coordinated clause, with *δέ* and neut. pl. *ὄμματα* both elided. *ὑγρὰ πρὶν ἔνδον ὄντα* ‘previously being moist within’ renders the relative clause by a supplementary description agreeing throughout with ‘eyes’: *ὄντα* is the nom. neut. pl. present participle of *εἰμί*. *πρίν* is adverbial ‘before’; *ἔνδον* renders *dentro*, without a separate restrictive particle for *pur*.
- *ἔσταζον ἐκ βλεφάρων* ‘were dripping from the eyelids’ interprets *labbra* as the rims of the eyes, not the lips of the mouth, and makes the lids the source of the drops. *ἔσταζον* is third pl. imperfect active of *στάζω*, durative against Italian preterite *gocciar*; the plural verb with a neuter plural subject differs from the usual Attic singular construction.
- *εἶτα δ᾽ ὁ πάγος τὰ δάκρυα συμπήξας ἔκλεισε πάλιν* ‘and then the frost, having congealed the tears, shut [the eyes] again’ subordinates the first of Dante’s coordinated actions. *συμπήξας* is nom. masc. sg. aorist active participle of *συμπήγνυμι*, agreeing with *πάγος*; *ἔκλεισε* is third sg. aorist active of *κλείω*. The object of the finite verb, explicit in Italian *-lli*, must be recovered from *ὄμματα*; *πάλιν* gives the force of *ri-* in *riserrolli*. The compound participle conveys binding together but does not specify *tra essi*.

> 49 Con legno legno mai spranga non cinse  
> 50 forte così: ond’ ei, come due becchi,  
> 51 cozzaro insieme, tant’ ira li vinse.  

Wood with wood no cramp did ever gird so strongly: wherefore *they*, like two he-goats, butted one another; such rage came over them.

> 49 Οὔποτ’ ἔσφιγξε γόμφος ξύλον σὺν ξύλῳ  
> (Never did clamp bind wood with wood)
>
> 50 Οὑτωσὶ σφοδρῶς· ὅθεν, ὡς δύο τράγοι,  
> (So violently; wherefore, like two he-goats,)
>
> 51 Ἀλλήλους συνέκρουον· τόσον ἐλύσσων!  
> (They clashed together; so fiercely did they rage!)

- *Οὔποτ᾽ ἔσφιγξε γόμφος ξύλον σὺν ξύλῳ* moves *mai* to the head as elided *οὔποτε* ‘never’. *ἔσφιγξε* is third sg. aorist active of *σφίγγω*, the verb used of the breasts in 43; *γόμφος* ‘peg, bolt’ is the subject corresponding to *spranga*. *ξύλον* is its accusative object, *σὺν ξύλῳ* an accompanying dative: the repetition of *legno* survives, with the order of the object and prepositional phrase reversed.
- *οὑτωσὶ σφοδρῶς* ‘so very forcibly’ renders *forte così* with two adverbs; *οὑτωσί* is the emphatic deictic form of *οὕτω(ς)* with *-ί*. Relative adverb *ὅθεν* ‘whence, wherefore’ corresponds closely to *ond’*. *ὡς δύο τράγοι* has a nominative plural, not a dual, in the comparison.
- *ἀλλήλους συνέκρουον* ‘they were striking one another’ replaces adverbial *insieme* with the reciprocal accusative. *συνέκρουον* is third pl. imperfect active of *συγκρούω*, with internal augment, rather than an aorist corresponding to *cozzaro*. *τόσον ἐλύσσων* ‘so fiercely were they raging’ makes the men, rather than *ira*, the subject: *τόσον* is an accusative of degree, *ἐλύσσων* the contracted third pl. imperfect active of *λυσσάω*. There is no separate equivalent of *li vinse*.

> 52 Ed un, ch’ avea perduti ambo gli orecchi  
> 53 per la freddura, pur col viso in giue  
> 54 disse: “Perchè cotanto in noi ti specchi?  

And one, who had lost both ears by the cold, with his face still downwards said: “Why art thou looking so much at us?

> 52 Καί τις, ὃς ὤτων ἐστέρητ’ ἀμφοτέρων  
> (And one, who had been deprived of both his ears)
>
> 53 Ὑπὸ τοῦ ψύχους, κλίνων τὴν ὄψιν κάτω  
> (By the cold, bending his face downward)
>
> 54 Εἶπε· “Τί βλέπεις ἡμᾶς, ὡς ἐν κατόπτρῳ;  
> (Said: “Why do you gaze at us as in a mirror?)

- *Καί τις, ὃς ὤτων ἐστέρητ᾽ ἀμφοτέρων* ‘and someone who had been deprived of both ears’ changes active *avea perduti* to a passive state resulting from loss. *τις* is indefinite, *ὅς* the nom. masc. sg. relative subject; *ἐστέρητ᾽* is elided *ἐστέρητο*, third sg. pluperfect middle/passive of *στερέω*. The genitive of deprivation *ὤτων* (gen. pl. of *οὖς*) is qualified across the verb by gen. pl. *ἀμφοτέρων*, not a dual.
- *ὑπὸ τοῦ ψύχους* makes the cold the cause of the deprivation, with *ὑπό* and genitive as after a passive. *κλίνων τὴν ὄψιν κάτω* ‘inclining his face downward’ turns *col viso in giue* into a nom. masc. sg. present active participle of *κλίνω*, simultaneous with *εἶπε*, third sg. second aorist active ‘said’. The present participle conveys the continuing posture, though *pur* has no independent equivalent.
- *Τί βλέπεις ἡμᾶς, ὡς ἐν κατόπτρῳ;* uses adverbial interrogative *τί* ‘why?’ with second sg. present active *βλέπεις* and direct accusative *ἡμᾶς*. Where *in noi ti specchi* has a reflexive verb and a prepositional complement, the Greek says ‘look at us’ and adds a comparison, *ὡς ἐν κατόπτρῳ* ‘as in a mirror’ (locative dative of *κάτοπτρον*). It thus retains the mirror sense of *specchi*, absent from the English rendering, but not the degree expressed by *cotanto*.

## Lines 55–87 — The Shades in the Ice and the Stranger’s Reproach

> 55 Se vuoi saper chi son cotesti due,  

If thou desirest to know who are these two,

> 55 „ Εἰ θέλεις μαθεῖν, τίνες οἱ δύο ’κεῖνοι,  
> („ If you wish to learn who those two are,)

- *Εἰ θέλεις μαθεῖν* follows *Se vuoi saper* closely: *εἰ* takes the present indicative *θέλεις* (2nd sg. of *θέλω*), which governs *μαθεῖν*, the second aorist active infinitive of *μανθάνω*. The infinitive means ‘to learn, find out’ rather than ‘to know’.
- *τίνες οἱ δύο ’κεῖνοι* is the indirect question after *μαθεῖν*. The interrogative *τίνες* is nominative plural and predicative; the copula expressed by *son* is understood. *οἱ δύο ’κεῖνοι* means ‘those two’: *’κεῖνοι* has lost the initial vowel of *ἐκεῖνοι* by prodelision. The postposed demonstrative stands outside the article, as normally in Greek; it does not mark proximity to the person addressed as specifically as *cotesti* does. The agreement is plural, not dual.

> 56 la valle onde Bisenzio si dichina  
> 57 del padre loro Alberto e di lor fue.  

the valley whence the Bisenzio descends was theirs and their father Albert’s.

> 56 „ Ἡ κοιλάς, ἔνθεν Βισέντιον ἐκρέει,  
> („ The valley whence the Bisenzio flows down)
>
> 57 „ Ὑπέκειτ᾽ αὐτοῖς πατρί τ᾽ αὐτῶν Ἀλβέρτῳ.  
> („ Belonged to them and to their father Albert.)

- *Ἡ κοιλάς, ἔνθεν Βισέντιον ἐκρέει* preserves the order of *la valle onde Bisenzio si dichina*. *ἔνθεν* is a relative adverb of source, ‘whence’; *Βισέντιον* is the neuter nominative subject of *ἐκρέει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *ἐκρέω*, uncontracted). ‘Flows out’ replaces the reflexive ‘slopes down’: the Greek prefix marks departure rather than descent.
- *Ὑπέκειτ᾽ αὐτοῖς πατρί τ᾽ αὐτῶν Ἀλβέρτῳ* is literally ‘was subject to them and to their father Albert’. *ὑπέκειτ᾽* is elided *ὑπέκειτο*, 3rd sg. imperfect of *ὑπόκειμαι*, with the augment after the preverb. Where *del padre loro Alberto e di lor fue* uses possessive phrases with ‘was’, the Greek verb governs datives, *αὐτοῖς* and *πατρί*, in reverse order. Postpositive *τε*, elided before *αὐτῶν*, coordinates them; *αὐτῶν* is possessive, *Ἀλβέρτῳ* in apposition to *πατρί*.

> 58 D’ un corpo usciro; e tutta la Caina  
> 59 potrai cercare, e non troverai ombra  
> 60 degna più d’ esser fitta in gelatina:  

They issued from one body; and thou mayest search the whole Caïna, and shalt not find a shade more worthy to be fixed in gelatine:

> 58 „ Μιᾶς ἐξῆλθον γαστρός· Καΐναν δ᾽ ὅλην  
> („ From one womb they issued; and all Caïna)
>
> 59 „ Ἢν περιέλθῃς, σκιὰν οὐκ ἂν εὑρήσοις  
> („ If you should go through, you would not find a shade)
>
> 60 „ Ἀξιωτέραν πηκτὴν γενέσθ᾽ ἐν πάγῳ·  
> („ More worthy to be fixed in the ice;)

- *Μιᾶς ἐξῆλθον γαστρός* makes *D’ un corpo* specifically ‘from one womb’. The genitive of source is divided around *ἐξῆλθον* (3rd pl. second aorist active indicative of *ἐξέρχομαι*); *μιᾶς* is the feminine genitive of *εἷς*, agreeing with *γαστρός*.
- *Καΐναν δ᾽ ὅλην Ἢν περιέλθῃς* fronts the entire object ‘all Caïna’ before the conditional conjunction. *ἤν* is a contracted form of *ἐάν*; *περιέλθῃς* is the 2nd sg. second aorist active subjunctive of *περιέρχομαι*, ‘go all round’. The two coordinated Italian futures, *potrai cercare, e non troverai*, thus become a supposition and its consequence.
- *σκιὰν οὐκ ἂν εὑρήσοις* puts ‘a shade’ first and makes the consequence potential, ‘you would not find’. *εὑρήσοις* is specifically a future active optative (2nd sg. of *εὑρίσκω*), not the aorist *εὕροις*: the potential use of *ἄν* with this future form is unusual in Attic and retains something of *troverai*.
- *Ἀξιωτέραν πηκτὴν γενέσθ᾽ ἐν πάγῳ* distributes *degna più d’ esser fitta* between the comparative adjective *ἀξιωτέραν* ‘more worthy’ and its infinitival complement. *γενέσθ᾽* is *γενέσθαι* (second aorist middle infinitive of *γίγνομαι*) with poetic elision of final *-αι*; predicate *πηκτήν* ‘set fast, congealed’ and *ἀξιωτέραν* are both feminine accusative singular, referring to *σκιάν*. The result is ‘to become fixed in ice’: *ἐν πάγῳ* interprets *in gelatina* rather than reproducing its image.

> 61 non quelli, a cui fu rotto il petto e l’ ombra  
> 62 con esso un colpo per la man d’ Artù;  

not him, whose breast and shadow at one blow were pierced by Arthur’s hand;

> 61 „ Οὐ τὸν στῆθος καὶ σκιὰν διαπαρέντα  
> („ Not him whose breast and shadow were pierced through)
>
> 62 „ Ἑνὶ βλήματι διὰ χειρὸς Ἀρθούρου,  
> („ By a single thrust from the hand of Arthur,)

- *Οὐ τὸν στῆθος καὶ σκιὰν διαπαρέντα* replaces *non quelli, a cui fu rotto il petto e l’ ombra* with an articular participle continuing the accusative construction after ‘find’. *διαπαρέντα* (masc. acc. sg., second aorist passive participle of *διαπείρω*) agrees with *τόν*: it is the man who was ‘pierced through’, with *στῆθος καὶ σκιάν* accusatives of respect, ‘as to breast and shadow’. The Italian instead has a relative dative of the person, *a cui*.
- *Ἑνὶ βλήματι* ‘by a single stroke’ is an instrumental dative; *ἑνί* is the neuter dative of *εἷς*, agreeing with *βλήματι*. It interprets *con esso un colpo* as one stroke for both. *διὰ χειρὸς Ἀρθούρου* closely follows *per la man d’ Artù*: *διά* with the genitive marks the means through which the stroke was delivered, and *Ἀρθούρου* depends on *χειρός*.

> 63 non Focaccia; non questi, che m’ ingombra  
> 64 col capo sì ch’ io non veggio oltre più,  
> 65 e fu nomato Sassol Mascheroni:  

not Focaccia; not this one, who so obstructs me with his head that I see no farther, and who was named Sassol Mascheroni:

> 63 „ Οὐ Φωκακκίαν, οὐ τὸν ἀπείργοντά με  
> („ Not Focaccia, not him who hinders me)
>
> 64 „ Τῷ τῆς κεφαλῆς ὄγκῳ προσβλέπειν πέραν,  
> („ With the bulk of his head from looking beyond,)
>
> 65 „ Καλούμενον δὲ Σάσολιν Μασκερώνην.  
> („ And who is called Sassol Mascheroni.)

- *Οὐ Φωκακκίαν, οὐ τὸν ἀπείργοντά με* continues the series of negated accusatives. For *non questi, che m’ ingombra* the article with *ἀπείργοντα* (masc. acc. sg. present active participle of *ἀπείργω*) gives ‘not the one preventing me’; *με* is its object.
- *Τῷ τῆς κεφαλῆς ὄγκῳ προσβλέπειν πέραν* adds a noun to *col capo*: ‘by the bulk of his head’, with instrumental dative *ὄγκῳ* enclosing the attributive genitive with its article. *προσβλέπειν* is a present active infinitive specifying what the speaker is prevented from doing, *πέραν* an adverb, ‘beyond’. Thus the affirmative infinitive after a verb of hindrance does the work of the negative finite result clause *sì ch’ io non veggio oltre più*.
- *Καλούμενον δὲ Σάσολιν Μασκερώνην* continues the description with a second masculine accusative participle, present passive of *καλέω*, ‘called’, rather than the finite past passive *fu nomato*. The two parts of the name are predicate accusatives agreeing in case with the person named.

> 66 se Tosco se’, ben sa’ omai chi fu.  

if thou beest a Tuscan, well knowest thou now who he was.

> 66 „ Εἰ σὺ Τοῦσκος εἶ, καλῶς, τίς ποτ᾽ ἦν, οἶσθα·  
> („ If you are a Tuscan, you know well who he was;)

- *Εἰ σὺ Τοῦσκος εἶ* puts the explicit subject *σύ* before the predicate noun, reversing *se Tosco se’*; *εἶ* is the present indicative, 2nd sg. of *εἰμί*. *καλῶς* renders *ben*, but is separated from its verb by the indirect question *τίς ποτ᾽ ἦν*, ‘who he was’. *ποτ᾽* is elided enclitic *ποτε*, lending emphasis to the question or looking back to an earlier time; it does not literally render *omai* ‘by now’.
- *οἶσθα*, placed last, is the inherited 2nd sg. perfect active indicative of *οἶδα*, with present sense ‘you know’; *ἦν* in the embedded question is imperfect, corresponding to *fu*.

> 67 E perchè non mi metti in più sermoni,  
> 68 sappi ch’ io fui il Camicion de’ Pazzi,  
> 69 ed aspetto Carlin che mi scagioni.”  

And that thou mayest not put me to further speech, know that I was Camicion de’ Pazzi, and am waiting for Carlino to excuse me.”

> 67 „ Ἵν᾽ εἰς ἀνάγκην μὴ μ᾽ ἄγῃς πλείω λέγειν,  
> („ And so that you may not compel me to say more,)
>
> 68 „ Ἴσθι νῦν μ᾽ ὄντα Καμικίονα Πάζον·  
> („ Know now that I am Camicion de' Pazzi;)
>
> 69 „ Πρὸς συγγνώμην μου περιμένω Καρλῖνον.”  
> („ To excuse me I await Carlino.”)

- *Ἵν᾽ εἰς ἀνάγκην μὴ μ᾽ ἄγῃς πλείω λέγειν* expands *perchè non mi metti in più sermoni* to ‘lest you bring me under necessity to say more’. Elided *ἵνα* introduces a purpose clause, hence negative *μή* and *ἄγῃς* (2nd sg. present active subjunctive of *ἄγω*); *μ᾽* is elided *με*. *λέγειν* specifies the compulsion; *πλείω*, contracted neuter accusative plural of the comparative *πλείων*, is its object, ‘more things’. There is no separate introductory ‘and’.
- *Ἴσθι νῦν μ᾽ ὄντα Καμικίονα Πάζον* renders *sappi ch’ io fui* by a participial clause after a verb of knowledge. *ἴσθι* here is the 2nd sg. imperative of *οἶδα*, ‘know’; *με* is the accusative subject of *ὄντα* (masc. acc. sg. present participle of *εἰμί*), and the name is predicative, likewise accusative. Musurus adds *νῦν* and substitutes present identity for Italian *fui*.
- *Πρὸς συγγνώμην μου περιμένω Καρλῖνον* puts the purpose first: ‘with a view to my pardon’. *πρός* with the accusative noun *συγγνώμην* replaces *che mi scagioni*, losing the explicit verb with Carlino as its subject; *μου* identifies the person to be pardoned. *περιμένω* is 1st sg. present active indicative, governing *Καρλῖνον* directly; the clause follows without the Italian *ed*.

> 70 Poscia vid’ io mille visi, cagnazzi  
> 71 fatti per freddo: onde mi vien riprezzo,  
> 72 e verrà sempre, de’ gelati guazzi.  

Afterwards I saw a thousand visages, made doggish by the cold: whence shuddering comes over me, and always will come, *when I think of* the frozen fords.

> 70 Εἶτα πρόσωπα χίλι᾽ εἶδον ἰώδη  
> (Then I saw a thousand faces turned livid)
>
> 71 Ἐκ ψύχους πάντα· φρίκην δέ μοι παρέχει,  
> (From the cold, all of them; and a shudder brings upon me,)
>
> 72 Παρέξει τ᾽ ᾀεί, τῆς λίμνης τῆσδ᾽ ἡ μνήμη.  
> (And ever will bring, the memory of this lake.)

- *Εἶτα πρόσωπα χίλι᾽ εἶδον ἰώδη Ἐκ ψύχους πάντα* moves ‘a thousand faces’ before *εἶδον* (1st sg. second aorist active indicative of *ὁράω*) and omits the explicit *io*. *χίλι᾽* is elided *χίλια*; it, *ἰώδη* (from *ἰώδης*), and the added *πάντα* all agree with neuter accusative plural *πρόσωπα*. The adjective *ἰώδη* ‘violet-coloured, livid’ takes *cagnazzi* as a description of colour, not of canine appearance. There is no participle for *fatti*: the faces are seen already livid, *ἐκ ψύχους* ‘from cold’, with causal *ἐκ* and the genitive.
- *φρίκην δέ μοι παρέχει, Παρέξει τ᾽ ᾀεί, τῆς λίμνης τῆσδ᾽ ἡ μνήμη* changes what ‘comes over me’ into the object of a transitive verb: ‘the memory of this lake gives me a shudder, and will always give [one]’. *παρέχει* and *παρέξει* are 3rd sg. present and future active indicative of *παρέχω*, sharing accusative *φρίκην*, recipient dative *μοι*, and the delayed subject *ἡ μνήμη*. Elided *τε* links the future to the present; *ᾀεί* is the printed form of the adverb ‘always’.
- Where *onde* links the shudder back to the sight, Greek uses connective *δέ* and expressly supplies ‘memory’. *τῆς λίμνης τῆσδ᾽* is the objective genitive dependent on *μνήμη*, with elided feminine genitive demonstrative *τῆσδε*: singular ‘of this lake’ replaces *de’ gelati guazzi*, without an adjective for *gelati*.

> 73 E mentre che andavamo in ver lo mezzo,  
> 74 al quale ogni gravezza si raduna,  
> 75 ed io tremava nell’ eterno rezzo,  

And as we were going towards the middle at which all weight unites, and I was shivering in the eternal shade,

> 73 Ἐν ᾧ δ᾽ ἐβαδίζομεν ἐπὶ τὸ μέσον,  
> (While we were walking toward the middle,)
>
> 74 Πρὸς ὅπερ ἐπιῤῥέπει βαρύτης πᾶσα,  
> (Toward which all gravity inclines,)
>
> 75 Ἐγὼ δ’ ἔτρεμον ἐν αἰωνίῳ ζόφῳ,  
> (And I was trembling in the eternal gloom,)

- *Ἐν ᾧ δ᾽ ἐβαδίζομεν ἐπὶ τὸ μέσον* renders *E mentre che andavamo in ver lo mezzo*. *ἐν ᾧ*, literally ‘in which [time]’, functions as ‘while’; postpositive *δέ* is elided. *ἐβαδίζομεν* is 1st pl. imperfect active indicative of *βαδίζω*, with syllabic augment, and *ἐπί* with the accusative of the substantivized neuter *μέσον* expresses direction.
- *Πρὸς ὅπερ ἐπιῤῥέπει βαρύτης πᾶσα* makes *al quale* directional, ‘toward which’. *ὅπερ* is the neuter accusative relative with strengthening *-περ*, referring to *τὸ μέσον*. *ἐπιῤῥέπει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *ἐπιρρέπω*, with the two rhos separately marked in this spelling) means ‘inclines toward’, not reflexive *si raduna* ‘gathers’. The postposed subject *βαρύτης πᾶσα* is singular and abstract, ‘all weight’.
- *Ἐγὼ δ’ ἔτρεμον ἐν αἰωνίῳ ζόφῳ* keeps emphatic *io* as *ἐγώ*, with connective *δέ*. *ἔτρεμον* (1st sg. imperfect active indicative of *τρέμω*) matches the continuing *tremava*. *ἐν* with the dative marks the surroundings; *ζόφος* interprets *rezzo* as darkness or gloom rather than cool shade.

> 76 se voler fu o destino, o fortuna,  
> 77 non so; ma passeggiando tra le teste,  
> 78 forte percossi il piè nel viso ad una.  

whether it was will, or destiny or chance, I know not; but, walking amid the heads, I hit my foot violently against the face of one.

> 76 Ἢ θέλησις ἦν, ἢ σύμπτωσις, ἢ τύχη,  
> (Whether it was will, or chance, or fortune,)
>
> 77 Οὐκ οἶδ’, ἀλλ’ ἐν κεφαλῶν μέσῳ προβαίνων  
> (I know not; but advancing amidst the heads)
>
> 78 Τῷ ποδί τινος προσέκρουσα τὴν ὄψιν·  
> (With my foot I struck someone's face;)

- *Ἢ θέλησις ἦν, ἢ σύμπτωσις, ἢ τύχη, Οὐκ οἶδ’* keeps the alternatives in advance of ‘I do not know’. The first *ἤ* begins a series ‘either … or … or’, rather than translating interrogative *se* with *εἰ*. *ἦν* is the 3rd sg. imperfect of *εἰμί*. The substantivized infinitive *voler* becomes the noun *θέλησις*; *σύμπτωσις* ‘coincidence’ gives a different sense from *destino*, while *τύχη* corresponds to *fortuna*. *οἶδ’* is elided *οἶδα*, perfect in form, present in meaning.
- *ἀλλ’ ἐν κεφαλῶν μέσῳ προβαίνων* retains the circumstantial construction of *ma passeggiando tra le teste*: *προβαίνων* (nom. masc. sg. present active participle of *προβαίνω*) agrees with the implicit ‘I’ and denotes simultaneous movement. *ἐν … μέσῳ* ‘in the midst’ encloses its dependent genitive plural *κεφαλῶν*.
- *Τῷ ποδί τινος προσέκρουσα τὴν ὄψιν* redistributes the complements of *percossi il piè nel viso ad una*: ‘with my foot I struck someone’s face’. *τῷ ποδί* is instrumental dative, *τὴν ὄψιν* accusative object, and indefinite genitive *τινος* depends on *ὄψιν* despite their separation. *προσέκρουσα* is 1st sg. aorist active indicative of *προσκρούω*, augmented after *πρός*. Greek does not separately translate *forte*.

> 79 Piangendo mi sgridò: “Perchè mi peste?  

Weeping it cried out to me: “Why tramplest thou on me?

> 79 Ὁ δὲ κλαίων ἔκραξε· “Τί μ’ ἐπικρούεις;  
> (And he weeping cried out: “Why do you strike me?)

- *Ὁ δὲ κλαίων ἔκραξε* gives an explicit masculine subject, ‘and he, weeping, cried out’: *ὁ δέ* resumes the person just struck, and *κλαίων* is a circumstantial present active participle (nom. masc. sg. of *κλαίω*) corresponding to *Piangendo*. *ἔκραξε* is 3rd sg. aorist active indicative of *κράζω*; it expresses the cry rather than the rebuke in *sgridò*, and has no expressed equivalent of the first *mi*.
- *Τί μ’ ἐπικρούεις;* retains the direct object of *mi peste*: *μ’* is elided *με*. *τί* is the adverbial accusative ‘why?’, and *ἐπικρούεις* (2nd sg. present active indicative of *ἐπικρούω*) means ‘strike upon’, without the specific suggestion of trampling.

> 80 se tu non vieni a crescer la vendetta  
> 81 di Mont’ Aperti, perchè mi moleste?”  

If thou comest not to increase the vengeance for Montaperti, why dost thou molest me?”

> 80 „ Εἰ μὴ τὴν ἐκδίκησιν αὐξήσων ἦλθες  
> („ If you have not come to increase the vengeance)
>
> 81 „ Τοῦ Μονταπέρτου, τί σύ με βασανίζεις;” —  
> („ Of Montaperti, why do you torment me?”—)

- *Εἰ μὴ τὴν ἐκδίκησιν αὐξήσων ἦλθες* substitutes ‘if you did not come intending to increase the vengeance’ for *se tu non vieni a crescer la vendetta*. The present of coming becomes *ἦλθες* (2nd sg. second aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι*); the purpose infinitive becomes *αὐξήσων* (nom. masc. sg. future active participle of *αὐξάνω*), a regular purpose construction with a verb of motion. *μή* negatives the supposition introduced by *εἰ*.
- *Τοῦ Μονταπέρτου* retains *di Mont’ Aperti* as a dependent genitive, now separated from *τὴν ἐκδίκησιν* by participle and verb. *τί σύ με βασανίζεις;* adds explicit, emphatic *σύ* to the repeated ‘why?’ and chooses the stronger *βασανίζεις* ‘torment’ (2nd sg. present active indicative of *βασανίζω*) for *moleste*.

> 82 Ed io: “Maestro mio, or qui m’ aspetta,  
> 83 sì ch’ io esca d’ un dubbio per costui;  
> 84 poi mi farai, quantunque vorrai, fretta.”  

And I: “My Master! now wait me here, that I may rid me of a doubt respecting him; then shalt thou, however much thou pleasest, make me haste.”

> 82 Κἀγώ· “Προστάτα, πρόσμενέ με νῦν ὧδε,  
> (And I: “Master, wait for me now here,)
>
> 83 „ Ἵν’ ἀπορίαν τὴν περὶ τούτου λύσω·  
> („ So that I may resolve my doubt concerning this one;)
>
> 84 „ Εἶτα δέ μ’ ἐπισπεύσεις ὅπως ἂν βούλῃ.”  
> („ And afterwards you shall hasten me on as you please.”)

- *Κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*, ‘and I’, with the verb of speaking unexpressed as in *Ed io*. *Προστάτα*, vocative singular of *προστάτης*, chooses ‘protector, leader’ for *Maestro mio*, without the possessive. *πρόσμενέ με* is a 2nd sg. present active imperative of *προσμένω* with accusative object; the second accent on the imperative is occasioned by enclitic *με*. *νῦν ὧδε* retains *or qui* but follows the command.
- *Ἵν’ ἀπορίαν τὴν περὶ τούτου λύσω* treats *sì ch’ io esca* as purpose, with elided *ἵνα* and *λύσω*, here 1st sg. aorist active subjunctive of *λύω*. Instead of ‘get out of a doubt’, the speaker will ‘solve a difficulty’. The postposed articular qualifier *τὴν περὶ τούτου* identifies which difficulty; *περί* with the genitive takes *per costui* as ‘concerning this man’.
- *Εἶτα δέ μ’ ἐπισπεύσεις ὅπως ἂν βούλῃ* compresses *mi farai … fretta* into *μ’ ἐπισπεύσεις* ‘you will hurry me on’ (2nd sg. future active indicative of *ἐπισπεύδω*). Final-position *ὅπως ἂν βούλῃ* is an indefinite clause of manner, ‘in whatever way you wish’, rather than the degree expressed by *quantunque*. *βούλῃ* is 2nd sg. present middle subjunctive of the deponent *βούλομαι*, with modal *ἄν*, not a future like *vorrai*.

> 85 Lo duca stette; ed io dissi a colui  
> 86 che bestemmiava duramente ancora:  
> 87 “Qual se’ tu, che così rampogni altrui?”  

The master stood; and to that *shade*, which still kept bitterly reviling, I said: “What art thou, who thus reproachest others?”

> 85 Ἔστη μὲν ὁ Προστάτης· ἐγὼ δὲ τότε  
> (The Master stood still; and I then)
>
> 86 Εἶπον τῷ πικρῶς ἔτι με βλασφημοῦντι·  
> (Said to him who was still bitterly reviling me:)
>
> 87 “Τίς οὖν εἶ σύ, τοὺς ἄλλους οὕτω προσβάλλων;”  
> (“Who then are you, who thus rail at others?”)

- *Ἔστη μὲν ὁ Προστάτης· ἐγὼ δὲ τότε Εἶπον* balances the two subjects by postpositive *μέν … δέ*. *ἔστη* (3rd sg. intransitive root aorist active indicative of *ἵστημι*) means ‘came to a standstill’, matching *stette*; *ὁ Προστάτης* repeats the title used in the vocative rather than introducing a new term for *duca*. *τότε* adds ‘then’ to the second clause; *εἶπον* is the 1st sg. second aorist of the verb of saying.
- *τῷ πικρῶς ἔτι με βλασφημοῦντι* renders *a colui che bestemmiava duramente ancora* by a substantivized present active participle (dat. masc. sg. of *βλασφημέω*), dative of the addressee after *εἶπον*. Its present stem denotes abuse still in progress at the time of speaking, where Italian uses the imperfect. *πικρῶς* ‘bitterly’ and *ἔτι* ‘still’ precede it; *με* makes explicit the object of the abuse.
- *Τίς οὖν εἶ σύ* takes *Qual se’ tu* as a question of identity, ‘who, then, are you?’, with predicate nominative *τίς*, expressed subject *σύ*, and added inferential *οὖν*. *τοὺς ἄλλους οὕτω προσβάλλων* replaces the finite relative *che così rampogni altrui* with a participle agreeing with *σύ*: *προσβάλλων* (nom. masc. sg. present active of *προσβάλλω*) gives ‘assailing’, with accusative plural *τοὺς ἄλλους* for indefinite *altrui* and adverb *οὕτω* for *così*.

## Lines 88–120 — Bocca Named Against His Will

> 88 “Or tu chi se’, che vai per l’ Antenora  
> 89 percotendo,” rispose, “altrui le gote  
> 90 sì che, se fossi vivo, troppo fora?”  

“Nay, who art thou,” he answered, “that through the Antenora goest, smiting the cheeks of others; so that, if thou wert alive, it were too much?”

> 88 — “Ἀλλὰ σὺ τίς εἶ, βαίνων δι’ Ἀντηνείρας,  
> (—“And who are you, walking through Antenora,)
>
> 89 „ Κρούων τ’,” ἐπεῖπε, “τὰς παρειὰς τῶν ἄλλων,  
> („ And striking,” he replied, “the cheeks of others,)
>
> 90 „ Ὥστε, κἂν ζῶν ᾖς, ὑπεράγαν ἂν εἴη;” —  
> („ So that, even if you were alive, it would be too much?”—)

- *Ἀλλὰ σὺ τίς εἶ* ‘but you, who are you?’ gives *Or* an adversative turn and keeps the emphatic *tu* as *σύ*, unnecessary for person alongside *εἶ* (2nd sg. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*). *τίς* is the interrogative predicate, nominative like *σύ*.
- *βαίνων δι’ Ἀντηνείρας, κρούων τ’* ‘going through Antenora and striking’ replaces the relative clause *che vai* and its dependent gerund *percotendo* with two coordinated present active participles, nom. masc. sg. of *βαίνω* and *κρούω*, agreeing with *σύ*. *δι’* is *διά* elided before the genitive of the place traversed; postpositive *τε*, elided to *τ’* before *ἐπεῖπε*, couples the second participle to the first.
- *ἐπεῖπε* ‘he said in reply’ (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἐπιλέγω*, with the suppletive stem of *εἶπον*) interrupts the question at *rispose*. *τὰς παρειὰς τῶν ἄλλων* puts the direct object first and turns *altrui* into an explicit possessive genitive plural, ‘the cheeks of the others’.
- After resultative *ὥστε*, *κἂν ζῶν ᾖς* ‘even if you are alive’ is concessive: *κἄν* represents *καὶ ἐάν*, and *ᾖς* is 2nd sg. pres. subj. of *εἰμί*, with predicative *ζῶν* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *ζάω*). This leaves the condition open rather than reproducing the remoteness of *se fossi vivo*. The separate *ἄν* in *ὑπεράγαν ἂν εἴη* belongs to the potential optative *εἴη* (3rd sg. pres. of *εἰμί*): ‘it would be altogether too much’, with the intensive adverb *ὑπεράγαν* for *troppo*.

> 91 “Vivo son io, e caro esser ti puote,”  
> 92 fu mia risposta, “se domandi fama,  
> 93 ch’ io metta il nome tuo tra l’ altre note.”  

“I am alive,” was my reply; “and if thou seekest fame, it may be precious to thee, that I put thy name among the other notes.”

> 91 — “Ἐγὼ ζῶν εἰμί, καί σοι χαρίεν ἔσται,”  
> (—“I am alive, and it may be pleasing to you,”)
>
> 92 Ὑπέλαβον, “εἰ φήμην ἀγαπᾷς, ὅπως  
> (I answered, “if you love fame, that)
>
> 93 „ Ἐν ᾠδαῖς ἐμαῖς μνήσωμ’ ὀνόματός σου.”  
> („ In my songs I should make mention of your name.”)

- *Ἐγὼ ζῶν εἰμί* answers in the very words of the condition, but puts *ἐγώ* first where *Vivo son io* puts *io* last. *καί σοι χαρίεν ἔσται* ‘and it will be pleasing to you’ replaces *caro esser ti puote*: a definite future, *ἔσται* (3rd sg. fut. ind. of *εἰμί*), for the modal *puote*, with dative of the person pleased and neuter singular predicate adjective *χαρίεν* (from *χαρίεις*).
- *ὑπέλαβον* ‘I took up the speech, replied’ (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὑπολαμβάνω*, augmented after the preverb) converts *fu mia risposta* into a verb of speaking. *εἰ φήμην ἀγαπᾷς* ‘if you love fame’ makes *domandi* a disposition rather than a request; *ἀγαπᾷς* is 2nd sg. contracted pres. act. ind. of *ἀγαπάω*.
- *ὅπως … μνήσωμ’ ὀνόματός σου* specifies what will be pleasing. *ὅπως* with the subjunctive, ordinarily purposive, does service for the explanatory *che*. *μνήσωμ’* is elided *μνήσωμαι* (1st sg. aor. mid. subj. of *μιμνήσκω*); ‘make mention of’ governs the genitive *ὀνόματος*, with possessive *σου*. Thus the name is no longer the direct object of *metta*. *ἐν ᾠδαῖς ἐμαῖς* ‘in songs of mine’ interprets *tra l’altre note*: *ἐν* takes the dative, and *ἐμαῖς* supplies ownership in place of *altre*.

> 94 Ed egli a me: “Del contrario ho io brama;  
> 95 levati quinci, e non mi dar più lagna:  
> 96 chè mal sai lusingar per questa lama.”  

And he to me: “The contrary is *what* I long for; take thyself away! and pester me no more: for thou ill knowest *how* to flatter on this icy slope.”

> 94 Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ· “Τοὐναντίον ἐπόθουν·  
> (And he to me: “The opposite I longed for;)
>
> 95 „ Ἄπιθ᾽ ἐντεῦθεν, μὴ δέ με βλάπτε πλέον·  
> („ Depart from here, and hurt me no more;)
>
> 96 „ Κακῶς οἶδας θωπεύειν ἐν βόθρῳ τῷδε.” —  
> („ Ill do you know how to flatter in this pit.”—)

- *ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ* ‘and he to me’ keeps the ellipsis of a verb of saying in *Ed egli a me*. *ὁ* is pronominal before the connective *δέ*. *τοὐναντίον* (crasis of *τὸ ἐναντίον*) is the substantivized neuter adjective, accusative object of *ἐπόθουν* ‘I was longing for’ (1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ποθέω*). The Italian has a present verb and noun, *ho io brama*, with *del contrario* dependent on the noun.
- *ἄπιθ’ ἐντεῦθεν* ‘go away from here’ uses the elided 2nd sg. pres. imperative *ἄπιθι* of *ἄπειμι* ‘go away’ for reflexive *levati*. *μὴ δέ με βλάπτε πλέον* ‘and do not hurt me any longer’ replaces *non mi dar più lagna* with a prohibition addressed to an ongoing action: *μή* with the 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *βλάπτω*, accusative *με*, and adverbial comparative *πλέον*.
- *κακῶς οἶδας θωπεύειν* ‘badly do you know how to flatter’ closely follows *mal sai lusingar*. *οἶδας* is the alternative to Attic *οἶσθα*, 2nd sg. of the perfect *οἶδα* with present meaning; *θωπεύειν* is its complementary pres. act. infinitive. Musurus leaves causal *chè* unexpressed. *ἐν βόθρῳ τῷδε* renders *per questa lama* as ‘in this pit’, with static *ἐν* and the proximal demonstrative *ὅδε*, both noun and demonstrative in the dative.

> 97 Allor lo presi per la cuticagna,  
> 98 e dissi: “E’ converrà che tu ti nomi,  
> 99 o che capel qui su non ti rimagna.”  

Then I seized him by the afterscalp, and said: “It will be necessary that thou name thyself, or that not a hair remain upon thee here!”

> 97 Αὐτὸν δὴ τότε τῆς χαίτης ἐλαβόμην  
> (Then I seized him by the hair of his head,)
>
> 98 Λέγων· “Ἀνάγκη τὸ σὸν ὄνομ᾽ εἰπεῖν μοι·  
> (Saying: “You must needs tell me your name;)
>
> 99 „ Ἄλλως, οὐ μενεῖ θρὶξ ἐπὶ κεφαλῆς σου.”  
> („ Otherwise, not a hair shall remain upon your head.”)

- *αὐτὸν δὴ τότε τῆς χαίτης ἐλαβόμην* ‘him, then, I took by the hair’ fronts the person seized. *ἐλαβόμην* (1st sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *λαμβάνω*) takes accusative *αὐτόν* with a genitive of the part grasped, *τῆς χαίτης*. The latter denotes the hair rather than specifically *la cuticagna*, the back of the head. *τότε* gives *allor*, reinforced by *δή*.
- *λέγων* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *λέγω*) subordinates *e dissi* to the seizure. *ἀνάγκη* ‘there is necessity’, with *ἐστί* understood, replaces future *converrà* and takes an infinitive instead of a *che*-clause: *τὸ σὸν ὄνομ’ εἰπεῖν μοι* ‘to tell me your name’. *εἰπεῖν* is second aor. act. infinitive; elided *ὄνομα* with possessive *σόν* unpacks reflexive *ti nomi*, and *μοι* supplies the recipient.
- *ἄλλως, οὐ μενεῖ θρὶξ ἐπὶ κεφαλῆς σου* ‘otherwise a hair will not remain on your head’ makes *o che … non ti rimagna* an independent prediction. *μενεῖ* is the contracted 3rd sg. fut. act. ind. of *μένω*; singular *θρίξ* is its subject. *ἐπί* with genitive *κεφαλῆς* makes *qui su* anatomically explicit, and *σου* replaces possessive *ti*.

> 100 Ond’ egli a me: “Perchè tu mi dischiomi,  
> 101 nè ti dirò ch’ io sia, nè mostrerolti,  
> 102 se mille fiate in sul capo mi tomi.”  

Whence he to me: “Even if thou unhair me, I will not tell thee who I am; nor shew it thee, though thou fall foul upon my head a thousand times.”

> 100 Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπεν· “Ἐπεὶ τὴν κόμην μ᾽ ἀποτίλλεις,  
> (And he said: “Even though you pluck out my hair,)
>
> 101 „ Οὐκ ἐρῶ σοι, τίς εἰμι, κοὐδέν σοι δείξω,  
> („ I will not tell you who I am, nor show you anything,)
>
> 102 „ Κἂν χιλιάκις τὴν κεφαλήν μοι παίσῃς.” —  
> („ Even if a thousand times you strike my head.”—)

- *ὁ δ’ εἶπεν* ‘and he said’ supplies the verb missing from *Ond’ egli a me*, but neither the consequential *onde* nor the addressee is separately rendered. *ἐπεὶ τὴν κόμην μ’ ἀποτίλλεις* gives *Perchè tu mi dischiomi* a present indicative, *ἀποτίλλεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. of *ἀποτίλλω*). *ἐπεί* ordinarily means ‘since’ or ‘when’; the concessive force of the rejoinder is contextual, not marked here by *καί*. Greek names both the hair, *τὴν κόμην*, and the affected person, *μ’* (elided accusative *με*).
- *οὐκ ἐρῶ σοι, τίς εἰμι* ‘I shall not tell you who I am’ uses the contracted future of the verb of saying (*ἐρῶ*, 1st sg. act. ind.) and an indirect question retaining the present indicative, against Italian subjunctive *ch’ io sia*. *κοὐδέν* is crasis of *καὶ οὐδέν*: *κοὐδέν σοι δείξω* ‘and I shall show you nothing’ replaces the particular object of *mostrerolti* with a negative indefinite; *δείξω* is 1st sg. fut. act. ind. of *δείκνυμι*.
- *κἂν χιλιάκις τὴν κεφαλήν μοι παίσῃς* explicitly marks concession with *καὶ ἐάν* and the subjunctive. *χιλιάκις* ‘a thousand times’ condenses *mille fiate* into an adverb; *παίσῃς* (2nd sg. aor. act. subj. of *παίω*) interprets *in sul capo mi tomi* as striking. *τὴν κεφαλήν* is now the direct object, *μοι* the dative of the person affected.

> 103 Io avea già i capelli in mano avvolti,  
> 104 e tratti glien’ avea più d’ una ciocca,  
> 105 latrando lui con gli occhi in giù raccolti;  

I already had his hair coiled on my hand, and had plucked off more than one tuft of it, he barking and keeping down his eyes,

> 103 Εἶχον δ᾽ ἐν χειρὶ τὰς τρίχας εἰλιγμένας,  
> (And I had his hairs coiled in my hand,)
>
> 104 Καὶ μαλλοὺς οὐκ ὀλίγους ἀπεσπασμένους,  
> (And not a few tufts torn away,)
>
> 105 Αὐτοῦ σὺν στροφαῖς ὀμμάτων ὑλακτοῦντος,  
> (While he was barking with rolling eyes,)

- *εἶχον δ’ ἐν χειρὶ τὰς τρίχας εἰλιγμένας* follows *Io avea … i capelli in mano avvolti*: *εἶχον* (1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἔχω*) takes an object and a predicate participle. *εἰλιγμένας* (acc. fem. pl. perf. mid./pass. participle of *ἑλίσσω*) agrees with *τὰς τρίχας* and describes the resulting state. *ἐν χειρί* has *χείρ* in the dative singular, without article or possessive; *già* has no separate equivalent.
- *καὶ μαλλοὺς οὐκ ὀλίγους ἀπεσπασμένους* continues the construction under the same *εἶχον*: ‘and no few locks torn away’. *ἀπεσπασμένους* (acc. masc. pl. perf. mid./pass. participle of *ἀποσπάω*) predicates a state of *μαλλούς* where *tratti glien’ avea* expresses a completed act. The litotes *οὐκ ὀλίγους* strengthens *più d’una*; the Italian pronominal elements identifying whose hair and of what are not repeated.
- *αὐτοῦ … ὑλακτοῦντος* renders *latrando lui* by a genitive absolute, with its own subject and the gen. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *ὑλακτέω*. Inside it, *σὺν στροφαῖς ὀμμάτων* ‘with turnings of the eyes’ substitutes movement for *con gli occhi in giù raccolti*: *σύν* governs dat. pl. *στροφαῖς*, and *ὀμμάτων* is gen. pl. of *ὄμμα*. The downward direction disappears.

> 106 quando un altro gridò: “Che hai tu, Bocca?  
> 107 non ti basta sonar con le mascelle,  
> 108 se tu non latri? qual diavol ti tocca?”  

when another cried: “What ails thee, Bocca? is it not enough for thee to chatter with thy jaws, but thou must bark too? what Devil is upon thee?”

> 106 Ὅτ᾽ ἄλλος τις ἔκραξε· “Τί πάσχεις, Βόκκα ;  
> (When another cried out: “What ails you, Bocca?)
>
> 107 „ Οὐκ ἀρκεῖ σοι πλαταγεῖν τὰς σιαγόνας  
> („ Is it not enough for you to clatter your jaws)
>
> 108 „ Ὑλαγμῶν ἄνευ ; Τίς σε πιέζει Δαίμων ;”  
> („ Without barking? What Demon torments you?”)

- *ὅτ’ ἄλλος τις ἔκραξε* closely reproduces *quando un altro gridò*. *ὅτ’* is elided *ὅτε*; indefinite *τις* gives *ἄλλος* the sense ‘someone else’. *ἔκραξε* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *κράζω*. *τί πάσχεις, Βόκκα;* turns *Che hai tu* into ‘what are you suffering?’ (*πάσχεις*, 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *πάσχω*), with the name in direct address.
- *οὐκ ἀρκεῖ σοι πλαταγεῖν τὰς σιαγόνας* uses impersonal *ἀρκεῖ* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἀρκέω*) with the dative and a subject infinitive, much as in *non ti basta sonar*. *πλαταγεῖν* (pres. act. inf. of *πλαταγέω*) ‘make clatter’ takes ‘the jaws’ as accusative object instead of instrumental *con le mascelle*. Initial *οὐκ* invites assent.
- *ὑλαγμῶν ἄνευ* ‘without barkings’ turns the negative conditional *se tu non latri* into a prepositional phrase; *ἄνευ* follows the genitive plural it governs. *τίς σε πιέζει Δαίμων;* separates interrogative *τίς* from its noun *Δαίμων* by object and verb. *πιέζει* ‘presses, besets’ (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *πιέζω*) is stronger than *tocca*.

> 109 “Omai,” diss’ io, “non vo’ che tu favelle,  
> 110 malvagio traditor, chè alla tua onta  
> 111 io porterò di te vere novelle.”  

“Now,” said I, “accursed traitor! I do not want thee to speak; for to thy shame I will bear true tidings of thee.”

> 109 — “Νῦν,” ὑπέλαβον, “οὐ θέλω λαλεῖν σ᾽ ἔτι,  
> (—“Now,” I answered, “I do not want you to speak any more,)
>
> 110 „ Κατάρατε προδότα· πρὸς σὴν δ᾽ αἰσχύνην  
> („ Accursed traitor; for to your shame)
>
> 111 „ Κομιῶ τὰς σὰς ἀληθεῖς ἀγγελίας.”  
> („ I will bear back true tidings of you.”)

- *νῦν … ἔτι* divides the force of *Omai* between ‘now’ and, under negation, ‘any longer’. *ὑπέλαβον* again means ‘I replied’. *οὐ θέλω λαλεῖν σ’ ἔτι* replaces *non vo’ che tu favelle* with an accusative and infinitive: elided *σέ* is the subject of *λαλεῖν* (pres. act. inf. of *λαλέω*), distinct from the subject of *θέλω*.
- *κατάρατε προδότα* is vocative singular in both words (*κατάρατος*, *προδότης*): ‘accursed’ for *malvagio*. *πρὸς σὴν δ’ αἰσχύνην* ‘and to your shame’ makes the consequence explicit with *πρός* and the accusative. Connective *δέ*, elided before *αἰσχύνην*, replaces causal *chè*.
- *κομιῶ* (1st sg. Attic contracted fut. act. ind. of *κομίζω*) ‘I shall carry, bring back’ renders *io porterò*. In *τὰς σὰς ἀληθεῖς ἀγγελίας* both adjectives are attributive, acc. fem. pl.; possessive *σάς* stands for topical *di te*, so ‘your true tidings’ here means ‘true reports about you’.

> 112 “Va via,” rispose, “e ciò che tu vuoi, conta;  
> 113 ma non tacer, se tu di qua entr’ eschi,  
> 114 di quei ch’ ebbe or così la lingua pronta.  

“Go away!” he answered; “and tell what pleases thee; but be not silent, if thou gettest out from hence, respecting him, who now had his tongue so ready.

> 112 Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπ᾽· “Ἄπιθι, διηγοῦ τ᾽ εἴ τι θέλεις·  
> (And he said: “Go your way, and tell whatever you wish;)
>
> 113 „ Ἀλλὰ μὴ παραλίπῃς, ἤν ποτ᾽ ἐξέλθῃς  
> („ But do not omit, if ever you come out)
>
> 114 „ Ἐντεῦθεν, τὸν ἄθυρον δείξαντα γλῶσσαν.  
> („ From here, him who showed an unbridled tongue.)

- *ὁ δ’ εἶπ’* places the speech introduction before the command rather than after it; *εἶπ’* is elided *εἶπε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind.). *ἄπιθι* again renders ‘go away’. *διηγοῦ* (2nd sg. pres. mid. imperative of *διηγέομαι*) gives *conta* as ‘recount’; elided connective *τ’* joins the commands. *εἴ τι θέλεις* is literally ‘if you wish anything’, not the definite relative construction of *ciò che tu vuoi*: *τι* is enclitic and indefinite, accounting for the accent on *εἴ*.
- *ἀλλὰ μὴ παραλίπῃς … τόν* ‘but do not pass over … the man’ recasts *non tacer … di quei* with a transitive verb. *παραλίπῃς* is 2nd sg. second aor. act. subj. of *παραλείπω*, prohibitive with *μή*. The intervening *ἤν ποτ’ ἐξέλθῃς ἐντεῦθεν* retains the Italian interruption but carries *ἐντεῦθεν* ‘from here’ over into line 114. *ἤν* is a contracted form of conditional *ἐάν*; elided *ποτε* adds ‘ever’; *ἐξέλθῃς* is 2nd sg. second aor. act. subj. of *ἐξέρχομαι*.
- *τὸν ἄθυρον δείξαντα γλῶσσαν* is ‘the one who displayed an unguarded tongue’. Article *τόν* substantivizes *δείξαντα* (acc. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *δείκνυμι*), replacing *quei ch’ebbe*. Between article and participle stands *ἄθυρον*, agreeing not with the man but with *γλῶσσαν* (acc. fem. sg.; *ἄθυρος* is a two-termination adjective). Literally ‘without a door’, it interprets readiness as lack of restraint; *or così* is not separately expressed.

> 115 Ei piange qui l’ argento de’ Franceschi.  

Here he laments the Frenchman’s silver.

> 115 „ Ὧδ᾿ οὗτος κλαίει τἀργύριον τῶν Γάλλων.”  
> („ Here he bewails the silver of the French.”)

- *ὧδ’ οὗτος κλαίει* puts the place adverb first: *ὧδ’* is elided *ὧδε* ‘here’, *οὗτος* ‘this man’ a more pointed subject than *Ei*. *κλαίει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *κλαίω*) takes the thing bewailed as its direct object, as does *piange*. *τἀργύριον* is crasis of *τὸ ἀργύριον* ‘the silver, the money’; possessive *τῶν Γάλλων* is plural, like *de’ Franceschi*.

> 116 ‘Io vidi,’ potrai dir, ‘quel da Duera  
> 117 là dove i peccatori stanno freschi.’  

‘Him of Duera,’ thou canst say, ‘I saw there, where the sinners stand pinched in ice.’

> 116 ‘“Εἶδον,”’ εἰπεῖν δύνασαι, ‘“τὸν Δαδουέραν,  
> (‘“I saw,”’ you may say, ‘“him of Duera,)
>
> 117 ‚ „ Ἔνθ᾿ ἁμαρτωλοὶ ψυχροὶ διατελοῦσιν.”’  
> (‚ „ Where the cold sinners abide.”’)

- *εἶδον … τὸν Δαδουέραν* preserves the Italian order of *Io vidi … quel da Duera*. *εἶδον* is here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*, with no separate *ἐγώ*. *τὸν Δαδουέραν* fuses the preposition *da* with the name and inflects the result as a masculine singular accusative, with the article for *quel*.
- Parenthetic *εἰπεῖν δύνασαι* ‘you can say’ uses a complementary second aor. act. infinitive and *δύνασαι* (2nd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *δύναμαι*); the ability is present rather than future as in *potrai dir*.
- *ἔνθ’* (elided *ἔνθα*, relative ‘where’) does duty for both *là* and *dove*. *ἁμαρτωλοὶ ψυχροὶ διατελοῦσιν* follows *i peccatori stanno freschi* with ‘sinners continue cold’: *ψυχροί* is a predicate adjective agreeing with the anarthrous subject; *διατελοῦσιν* (3rd pl. contracted pres. act. ind. of *διατελέω*) emphasizes continuance. Neither a verb of freezing nor a noun for ice is supplied.

> 118 Se fossi domandato altri chi v’ era  
> 119 tu hai da lato quel di Beccheria,  
> 120 di cui segò Fiorenza la gorgiera.  

Shouldst thou be asked who else was there, thou hast beside thee the Beccheria whose gorge was slit by Florence.

> 118 „ Ἢν δ᾿ ἐρωτηθῇς, τίνες ἄλλοι συνῆσαν,  
> („ And if you are asked who else were with him,)
>
> 119 „ Ἔχεις ἐγγύς σου τὸν Βεκκερίαν, οὗπερ  
> („ You have close beside you Beccheria, whose)
>
> 120 „ Τὸν λαιμὸν ἀπέτεμεν ἡ Φλωρεντία.  
> („ Throat Florence severed.)

- *ἢν δ’ ἐρωτηθῇς* ‘and if you are asked’ uses contracted *ἐάν* with *ἐρωτηθῇς* (2nd sg. aor. pass. subj. of *ἐρωτάω*) in place of *Se fossi domandato*. The prospective condition leads to a present indicative main clause. *τίνες ἄλλοι συνῆσαν* ‘what others were together [with him]?’ makes the indirect question explicitly plural. *συνῆσαν* is 3rd pl. imperf. ind. of *σύνειμι* ‘be with’; its preverb conveys association where *v’era* expresses location.
- *ἔχεις ἐγγύς σου τὸν Βεκκερίαν* closely follows *tu hai da lato quel di Beccheria*. *ἔχεις* is 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἔχω*; *ἐγγύς* ‘near’ governs genitive *σου*. The Italian designation with *di* becomes an articular personal name in the accusative.
- *οὗπερ τὸν λαιμὸν ἀπέτεμεν ἡ Φλωρεντία* keeps the active construction of *di cui segò Fiorenza la gorgiera*, but puts the subject last. *οὗπερ* is the possessive gen. masc. sg. of the relative *ὅσπερ*, strengthened by *-περ*; it depends on *τὸν λαιμόν* ‘the throat’, not on the verb. *ἀπέτεμεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἀποτέμνω*, augmented after *ἀπ-*) means ‘cut off’, without the specific suggestion of sawing in *segò*.

## Lines 121–139 — Two Heads in the Ice

> 121 Gianni de' Soldanier credo che sia  
> 122 più là con Ganellone e Tribaldello,  

Gianni de’ Soldanier, I think, is farther on, with Ganellone, and Tribaldello

> 121 „ Γιάννην Σολδανεῖρον μικρὸν πρὸς οἶμαι  
> („ Gianni de' Soldanier a little further on, I think,)
>
> 122 „ Εἶναι σὺν Γανελλῶνι καὶ Τριβαλδέλλῳ,  
> („ Is, with Ganellone and Tribaldello,)

- *Γιάννην Σολδανεῖρον … οἶμαι εἶναι* turns *Gianni de' Soldanier credo che sia* into accusative and infinitive after a verb of thinking. Both names are accusative singular, the subject of *εἶναι* (present infinitive of *εἰμί*); the family designation is inflected as a name rather than introduced by a preposition corresponding to *de'*. *οἶμαι* is the contracted first-person singular present middle indicative of *οἴομαι*. It comes late, between the indication of place and the infinitive, but governs the construction.
- *μικρὸν πρὸς* gives the sense ‘a little farther on’: *μικρόν* is a neuter accusative of extent, and *πρός* stands adverbially, without a nominal complement. The qualification ‘a little’ is supplied; there is no separate demonstrative adverb for *là*. *σὺν Γανελλῶνι καὶ Τριβαλδέλλῳ* follows *con Ganellone e Tribaldello*, with both names in the dative governed by *σύν*.

> 123 ch' aprì Faenza quando si dormia."  

who unbarred Faenza when it slept.”

> 123 „ Ὃς ἠνέῳξε νυκτὸς τὴν Φαυεντίαν.”—  
> („ Who opened Faenza in the night.”—)

- *ὃς ἠνέῳξε … τὴν Φαυεντίαν* follows *ch' aprì Faenza*: the relative *ὅς* refers to the preceding dative *Τριβαλδέλλῳ* but is itself nominative masculine singular, subject of *ἠνέῳξε* (third-person singular aorist active indicative of *ἀνοίγω*, in an augmented form). Greek, like Italian, makes the city itself the direct object; it does not supply a word for gates.
- *νυκτός*, genitive singular of *νύξ*, is a genitive of time, ‘by night’. Musurus thus interprets rather than reproduces *quando si dormia*: neither the impersonal *si* nor the imperfect verb of sleeping has a Greek counterpart.

> 124 Noi eravam partiti già da ello,  
> 125 ch' io vidi due ghiacciati in una buca  
> 126 sì che l' un capo all' altro era cappello;  

We had already left him, when I saw two frozen in one hole so *closely*, that the one head was a cap to the other;

> 124 Τὴν σκιὰν ἡμῶν ἤδη λιπόντων ταύτην,  
> (When we had already left this shade behind,)
>
> 125 Εἶδον ἐν ὀπῇ δύο πεπαγωμένους,  
> (I saw two frozen in one hole,)
>
> 126 Ὧν ἑνὸς ἡ κεφαλὴ πρὸς τὴν θατέρου  
> (Of whom the head of the one upon that of the other)

- *τὴν σκιὰν ἡμῶν ἤδη λιπόντων ταύτην* means ‘when we had already left this shade’. *ἡμῶν … λιπόντων* is a genitive absolute: *λιπόντων* is the genitive masculine plural of the second-aorist active participle of *λείπω*. Its action is anterior to *εἶδον*; *ἤδη* retains *già*. Instead of *partiti … da ello*, with a complement of separation, Greek has a transitive participle whose object is *τὴν σκιὰν … ταύτην*. The demonstrative stands outside the article–noun group, separated from it here by the entire participial construction.
- *εἶδον* (first-person singular second-aorist active indicative of *ὁράω*) is now the main verb, not introduced by a counterpart to *ch'*. *ἐν ὀπῇ* ‘in a hole’ precedes its object *δύο πεπαγωμένους*. The latter participle, accusative masculine plural perfect middle-passive of *πήγνυμι*, denotes the resulting frozen state. Musurus uses the plural, not a dual, with *δύο*.
- For consecutive *sì che*, Musurus begins a relative description, *ὧν ἑνὸς ἡ κεφαλή* ‘of whom the head of one’: *ὧν* is partitive genitive plural, *ἑνός* possessive genitive singular. *πρὸς τὴν θατέρου* expresses the position of this head against the other’s; *τὴν* is accusative after *πρός*, with *κεφαλήν* understood. *θατέρου* is the crasis of *τοῦ ἑτέρου*. The predicate corresponding to *era cappello* is deferred into Greek line 127.

> 127 e come il pan per fame si manduca,  
> 128 così il sovran li denti all' altro pose  
> 129 là 've il cervel si giunge con la nuca.  

and as bread is chewed for hunger, so the uppermost put his teeth into the other there where the brain joins with the nape.

> 127 Ἦν ὡς πῖλός τις· ὡς δ᾿ ἐκ πείνης τις δάκνει  
> (Was like a cap; and as from hunger one bites)
>
> 128 Τὸν ἄρτον, οὕτως ἔπηξ᾿ αὐτὸς ὀδόντας  
> (Bread, so did he fix his teeth)
>
> 129 Μέσον αὐχένος κἀγκεφάλου θατέρου.  
> (Between the neck and the brain of the other.)

- *ἦν ὡς πῖλός τις* first completes the preceding relative clause: ‘was like a sort of cap’. *ἦν* is third-person singular imperfect of *εἰμί*; *πῖλός τις* is nominative, with enclitic indefinite *τις*. Where Dante identifies the head with a cap, Musurus explicitly compares it to one.
- *ὡς δ᾿ ἐκ πείνης τις δάκνει τὸν ἄρτον, οὕτως …* preserves *come … così*. *δ᾿* is elided *δέ*; *ἐκ* with genitive *πείνης* gives hunger as the cause. Instead of *il pan … si manduca*, the indefinite nominative *τις* supplies a personal subject, *δάκνει* (third-person singular present active indicative of *δάκνω*) specifies biting, and the bread becomes its accusative object.
- *ἔπηξ᾿ αὐτὸς ὀδόντας* ‘he himself planted teeth’ has elided *ἔπηξε*, third-person singular aorist active indicative of *πήγνυμι*. The verb is stronger than *pose* and repeats, in an active sense, the root of *πεπαγωμένους*. Nominative *αὐτός* points back to the biter without translating the positional distinction in *il sovran*; *ὀδόντας* is accusative plural of *ὀδούς*, without article or possessive.
- *μέσον αὐχένος κἀγκεφάλου θατέρου* replaces the finite local clause *là 've il cervel si giunge con la nuca* with ‘between the neck and brain of the other’. Neuter accusative *μέσον* functions prepositionally with the two genitives, reversing Dante’s order; *κἀγκεφάλου* is crasis for *καὶ ἐγκεφάλου*. The possessor *θατέρου* thus appears at the end rather than as the personal complement *all' altro*.

> 130 Non altrimenti Tideo si rose  
> 131 le tempie a Menalippo per disdegno,  
> 132 che quei faceva il teschio e l' altre cose.  

Not otherwise did Tydeus gnaw the temples of Menalippus for rage, than he the skull and the other parts.

> 130 Οὐκ ἄλλως Τυδεὺς λυσσῶν τοὺς Μελανίππου  
> (Not otherwise did Tydeus in his rage gnaw)
>
> 131 Ἐτραγάλισε κροτάφους, ἤπερ οὗτος  
> (The temples of Melanippus, than this one)
>
> 132 Τό τε κρανίον κατέτρωγε καὶ τἄνδον.  
> (Was devouring both the skull and what was within.)

- *οὐκ ἄλλως … ἤπερ* closely follows *Non altrimenti … che*. *ἤπερ* is comparative *ἤ* reinforced by *-περ*. *Τυδεύς* is nominative; agreeing *λυσσῶν*, contracted present active participle of *λυσσάω*, makes *per disdegno* an accompanying state, ‘raging’, rather than a prepositional expression of motive.
- *τοὺς Μελανίππου ἐτραγάλισε κροτάφους* puts the possessive genitive of *Μελάνιππος* between the article and *κροτάφους*, accusative plural of *κρόταφος*; the verb itself intervenes before the noun. Thus *le tempie a Menalippo* becomes ‘Melanippus’ temples’. *ἐτραγάλισε* (third-person singular aorist active indicative of *τραγαλίζω*, ‘gnaw’) needs no counterpart to Italian *si* in *si rose*.
- *οὗτος … κατέτρωγε* ‘this one was gnawing away’ retains the imperfect of *faceva* but replaces the Italian substitute verb with a specific transitive verb (*κατέτρωγε*, third-person singular imperfect active indicative of *κατατρώγω*, with the augment after the preverb). *τό τε κρανίον … καὶ τἄνδον* coordinates two objects by *τε … καί*. *τἄνδον*, crasis of *τὰ ἔνδον*, uses the neuter plural article to substantivize an adverb: *l' altre cose* is interpreted specifically as ‘the things inside’.

> 133 " O tu, che mostri per sì bestial segno  
> 134 odio sopra colui che tu ti mangi,  
> 135 dimmi il perchè," diss' io; "per tal convegno,  

“O thou! who by such brutal token shewest thy hate on him whom thou devourest, tell me why,” I said; “on this condition,

> 133 “Ὦ σύ, δεικνύων τοιῷδε θηρὸς τρόπῳ  
> (“O you, who show in such a beastly manner)
>
> 134 „ Μῖσος πρὸς ὃν νῦν καταβιβρώσκεις ὧδε,”  
> („ Hatred toward him whom you are now devouring thus,”)
>
> 135 Εἶπον, “λέγε μοι τὴν αἰτίαν, ἐφ᾽ ὅρῳ  
> (I said, “tell me the reason, on this condition,)

- *ὦ σύ, δεικνύων … μῖσος* replaces *O tu, che mostri … odio* with direct address and a present active participle of *δείκνυμι*, nominative masculine singular in agreement with *σύ*. *τοιῷδε … τρόπῳ* is a dative of manner, ‘in such a way as this’; the intervening *θηρός* (genitive singular of *θήρ*) makes it ‘a beast’s way’. The Greek describes a manner of acting rather than naming a *segno*.
- *πρὸς ὃν νῦν καταβιβρώσκεις ὧδε* ‘towards the one whom you are now devouring thus’ compresses *sopra colui che tu ti mangi*: *πρός* gives the direction of *μῖσος*, with no expressed demonstrative antecedent, and accusative masculine singular *ὅν* is the object of *καταβιβρώσκεις* (second-person singular present active indicative of *καταβιβρώσκω*). Greek adds *νῦν* and *ὧδε* but no equivalent of *ti*.
- *εἶπον* (first-person singular second-aorist active indicative, ‘I said’) precedes the command instead of following it as *diss' io* does. *λέγε μοι τὴν αἰτίαν* uses a second-person singular present active imperative of *λέγω*, an enclitic dative of the addressee, and a noun, ‘the cause’, for the substantivized *il perchè*. *ἐφ᾽ ὅρῳ* ‘on condition’ takes the dative; *ἐπί* loses its final vowel and its *π* is aspirated before the rough breathing of *ὅρῳ*.

> 136 che se tu a ragion di lui ti piangi,  
> 137 sappiendo chi voi siete, e la sua pecca,  
> 138 nel mondo suso ancor io te ne cangi,  

that if thou with reason complainest of him, I, knowing who ye are and his offence, may yet repay thee in the world above,

> 136 „ Ὡς, αὐτὸν εἴ τι δικαίως ἐπιμέμφῃ,  
> („ That, if you have any just complaint against him,)
>
> 137 „ Γνούς, τίνες ἐστέ, καὶ τἀδίκημα τούτου,  
> („ Knowing who you are, and his offence,)
>
> 138 „ Ἀποδώσω σοι δίκην ἐν ζώντων κόσμῳ,  
> („ I will render justice to you in the world of the living,)

- *ὡς* introduces the terms after *ἐφ᾽ ὅρῳ*, much as *che* introduces them after *per tal convegno*. In *αὐτὸν εἴ τι δικαίως ἐπιμέμφῃ*, *αὐτόν* is brought forward even before *εἰ*: the person blamed is an accusative object, not a complement like *di lui*. *ἐπιμέμφῃ* is second-person singular present middle indicative of *ἐπιμέμφομαι* in an open condition; *δικαίως* renders *a ragion*, and enclitic *τι* qualifies the complaint, ‘in any respect’.
- *γνούς* (nominative masculine singular second-aorist active participle of *γιγνώσκω*) renders *sappiendo* as ‘having learned’, prior to the promised act. Its subject is the implicit first-person subject of *ἀποδώσω*. It governs both the indirect question *τίνες ἐστέ* ‘who you are’ (interrogative predicate nominative plural and second-person plural present of *εἰμί*) and the coordinated accusative *τἀδίκημα τούτου* ‘this one’s wrongdoing’. *τἀδίκημα* is crasis of *τὸ ἀδίκημα*; the demonstrative genitive specifies the possessor more explicitly than *sua*.
- *ἀποδώσω σοι δίκην* is a definite future undertaking in place of the subjunctive *te ne cangi*: *ἀποδώσω* is first-person singular future active indicative of *ἀποδίδωμι*, with dative recipient *σοι* and an explicit object *δίκην*, ‘justice’ or ‘recompense’. There is no separate equivalent for *ne* or *ancor*. *ἐν ζώντων κόσμῳ* interprets *nel mondo suso* as ‘in the world of the living’: *κόσμῳ* is locative dative after *ἐν*, and *ζώντων* is a substantivally used genitive masculine plural present active participle of *ζάω*.

> 139 se quella, con ch' io parlo, non si secca."  

if that, wherewith I speak, be not dried up.”

> 139 „ Ἢν μὴ ξηρανθῇ γλῶσσ’ ἥδ᾽, ᾗ φθέγγομαί σοι.”  
> („ If this tongue with which I speak to you does not dry up.”)

- *ἢν μὴ ξηρανθῇ* introduces a prospective condition: *ἤν* is a contracted equivalent of *ἐάν*, with negative *μή* and third-person singular aorist passive subjunctive of *ξηραίνω*. The passive ‘be dried up’ replaces *si secca*, and the verb precedes its subject instead of closing the condition.
- *γλῶσσ᾿ ἥδ᾽* ‘this tongue’ names what Italian leaves implicit in *quella* and changes the demonstrative to a proximal one. *γλῶσσ᾿* is elided *γλῶσσα*, the Attic form of which is *γλῶττα*; *ἥδ᾽* is elided *ἥδε*, nominative feminine singular of *ὅδε*. The delayed relative clause *ᾗ φθέγγομαί σοι* gives *con ch' io parlo* by an instrumental feminine dative *ᾗ* and the first-person singular present middle indicative of *φθέγγομαι*, ‘utter speech’. The added dative *σοι* makes the addressee explicit; its enclisis accounts for the additional accent on *φθέγγομαί*.

## Summary — Recasting Dante’s Syntax in Greek

Musurus often keeps the order of Dante’s thought while changing its grammatical form. The opening *S’ io avessi … io premerei* remains a condition followed by its consequence, but Greek uses *εἰ* with the imperfect indicative *εἶχον* in the first clause and *ἄν* with the imperfect *ἐδήλουν* in the second. The syntax is closer than the imagery: pressing out *il suco* ‘the juice’ of a conception becomes making the thought clear. Elsewhere the choice of mood changes the force of the Italian. In *κἂν ζῶν ᾖς* ‘even if you are alive’, the subjunctive leaves open what *se fossi vivo* presents more remotely; the separate *ἄν* with optative *εἴη* still gives the consequence as ‘it would be’.

Participles repeatedly redistribute the weight of a sentence. In *στραφεὶς … εἶδον* ‘having turned, I saw’, the first of two coordinated Italian verbs becomes an aorist participle marking the action prior to the act of seeing. By contrast, *θεωρῶν* ‘gazing’ makes the continuing action of *mirava* simultaneous with the main verb. Where the subject changes, *latrando lui* finds a close syntactic counterpart in the genitive absolute *αὐτοῦ … ὑλακτοῦντος* ‘while he was barking’. The article gives the participle another use: *τῷ … βλασφημοῦντι* ‘to the one reviling’ renders both the antecedent and the finite relative of *a colui che bestemmiava*, with the dative itself marking the addressee. Likewise, *οἶμαι εἶναι* ‘I think [him] to be’, with the name in the accusative, replaces *credo che sia* without disturbing the Italian placement of the name first.

The particles sometimes preserve a correspondence and sometimes supply one. Musurus keeps *come … così* in *ὡς … οὕτως* ‘as … so’, but uses *μέν … δέ* to distinguish two subjects that Dante links simply with *ed*. His lexical choices are similarly selective. *Μέχρι παρειῶν* ‘up to the cheeks’ names the place that Dante indicates by the appearance of shame; *ἰώδη* ‘violet-coloured’ reads *cagnazzi* as a colour rather than a canine likeness. Yet an image can also survive a change of construction: in *ὡς ἐν κατόπτρῳ* ‘as in a mirror’, Musurus draws the mirror out of the reflexive verb *ti specchi* and makes it an explicit comparison.
