# Inferno 1

## Lines 1–21 — The Dark Wood and the Sunlit Hill

> 1 Nel mezzo del cammin di nostra vita  
> 2 mi ritrovai per una selva oscura,  
> 3 che la diritta via era smarrita.  

In the middle of the journey of our life I [came to] myself in a dark wood [where] the straight way was lost.

> 1 Ἐν τῷ μέσῳ τῆς τρίβου τοῦ βίου τούτου  
> (In the middle of the path of this life)
>
> 2 Εὑρέθην ἐν σκοτεινῷ τινι δρυμῶνι·  
> (I found myself in a dark forest;)
>
> 3 Τῆς γὰρ εὐθείας ὁδοῦ παρεξετράπην.  
> (For I had strayed from the straight road.)

- *Ἐν τῷ μέσῳ τῆς τρίβου* closely follows *Nel mezzo del cammin*: *ἐν* governs the dative of the substantivized neuter *τὸ μέσον* ‘the middle’, which takes the partitive genitive *τῆς τρίβου* ‘of the path’. *τοῦ βίου τούτου* depends in turn on *τρίβου*: *di nostra vita* becomes ‘of this life’, with the demonstrative *τούτου* (gen. sg. masc. of *οὗτος*) outside the article–noun group.
- *Εὑρέθην* ‘I was found’ (1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *εὑρίσκω*) renders the reflexive *mi ritrovai*. *per una selva oscura* becomes a phrase of position, *ἐν σκοτεινῷ τινι δρυμῶνι*: the adjective precedes the noun, and enclitic *τινι* (dat. sg. of indefinite *τις*) gives ‘in a certain dark thicket’. *δρυμῶνι* is the dative singular of masculine *δρυμών*, the noun resumed in lines 4–7.
- *Τῆς γὰρ εὐθείας ὁδοῦ παρεξετράπην* ‘for I turned aside from the straight road’ takes *che* as causal rather than local (“[where]”). *γάρ* is postpositive. The Italian makes the road the subject of *era smarrita*; Greek fronts the road as a genitive of separation and makes the speaker the subject. *παρεξετράπην* is 1st sg. second aor. pass. ind. of *παρεκτρέπω*, intransitive in sense, with the augment inside the compound.

> 4 Ahi quanto a dir qual era è cosa dura  
> 5 questa selva selvaggia ed aspra e forte,  
> 6 che nel pensier rinnova la paura!  

Ah! how hard a thing it is to tell what a wild, and rough, and stubborn wood this was, which in my thought renews the fear!

> 4 Ὤ, πόσον δεινὸν εἰπεῖν ὡς ἦν ἐκεῖνος  
> (Oh, how terrible to tell how that)
>
> 5 Ὁ δρυμὼν τραχύς, ἄγριος καὶ συνήρης,  
> (Forest was rough, savage, and tangled,)
>
> 6 Οὗπερ ἡ μνήμη τὸν φόβον διεγείρει!  
> (The very memory of which rouses fear!)

- *Ὤ, πόσον δεινὸν εἰπεῖν* renders *Ahi quanto a dir … è cosa dura* without a noun for *cosa* or an expressed copula: ‘oh, how dreadful [it is] to tell’. *πόσον* marks degree; *δεινόν* is neuter singular predicative, and the subject infinitive *εἰπεῖν* is the second aorist active used with *λέγω*.
- *ὡς ἦν ἐκεῖνος ὁ δρυμὼν τραχύς, ἄγριος καὶ συνήρης* gives the substance of what is hard to tell: ‘how rough, wild, and tangled that wood was’. *ἦν* is 3rd sg. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*. Distal *ἐκεῖνος* replaces *questa* and precedes the articular noun across the line break. The three nominative masculine singular adjectives are predicates: *τραχύς* and *ἄγριος* reverse the order of *selvaggia ed aspra*, while *συνήρης* understands *forte* as describing a wood close-grown or tangled. The repetition of sound and root in *selva selvaggia* has no Greek counterpart.
- *Οὗπερ ἡ μνήμη τὸν φόβον διεγείρει* ‘the very memory of which stirs up fear’ changes the subject of *che … rinnova*: not the wood but *ἡ μνήμη* acts. *οὗπερ* is the genitive singular masculine relative, referring to *δρυμών* and depending on *μνήμη*, with emphatic *-περ*. *nel pensier* thus becomes a noun in the nominative, and *διεγείρει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *διεγείρω*) expresses arousal rather than explicit renewal.

> 7 Tanto è amara, che poco è più morte:  

So bitter is it, that scarcely more is death:

> 7 Ὄντως ἐστὶν ἧττόν τι πικρὸς θανάτου.  
> (Indeed it is scarcely less bitter than death.)

- *Ὄντως ἐστὶν ἧττόν τι πικρὸς θανάτου* replaces *Tanto … che* and its result clause with a single comparison: ‘truly it is a little less bitter than death’. *ὄντως* asserts rather than intensifies; masculine *πικρός* agrees with the understood *δρυμών*, where *amara* agreed with *selva*.
- *ἧττον* is a comparative adverb ‘less’, qualified by adverbial indefinite *τι* ‘somewhat’ (its enclisis accounts for the additional accent in *ἧττόν τι*). The small excess expressed by *poco è più morte* thus becomes a small deficiency on the side of the wood; *θανάτου* is a genitive of comparison, not the subject of a second verb.

> 8 ma per trattar del ben ch' i' vi trovai,  
> 9 dirò dell' altre cose, ch' io v' ho scorte.  

but to treat of the good that I there found, I will relate the other things that I discerned.

> 8 Ἀλλ᾿ ὅ,τι καλὸν εὗρον ἐξειπεῖν μέλλων,  
> (Yet, meaning to tell of the good I found there,)
>
> 9 Περὶ τῶν ἄλλων, ὅσ’ εἶδον ἐκεῖ, λέξω.  
> (I will speak of the other things I saw there.)

- *Ἀλλ᾿ … ἐξειπεῖν μέλλων* converts *ma per trattar* from a purpose phrase into ‘but intending to tell fully’. *μέλλων* (nom. sg. masc., pres. act. participle of *μέλλω*) agrees with the unexpressed subject of *λέξω* and governs the second aorist active infinitive *ἐξειπεῖν*. *Ἀλλ᾿* is *ἀλλά* elided before a vowel.
- *ὅ,τι καλὸν εὗρον* ‘whatever good I found’ is the object of *ἐξειπεῖν*. The free relative *ὅ,τι* (acc. sg. neut. of *ὅστις*, distinct from the conjunction *ὅτι*) is the object within its clause of *εὗρον*, here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *εὑρίσκω*. This replaces the definite antecedent and relative in *del ben ch’ i’ vi trovai*; there is no separate equivalent here for *vi*.
- *Περὶ τῶν ἄλλων … λέξω* preserves the topic construction of *dirò dell’ altre cose* but puts *λέξω* (1st sg. fut. act. ind. of *λέγω*) last. *περί* takes the genitive; neuter plural *τῶν ἄλλων* needs no noun for *cose*. *ὅσ’ εἶδον ἐκεῖ* ‘all that I saw there’ has elided *ὅσα* (acc. pl. neut. of *ὅσος*), object of *εἶδον*, the second aorist active indicative used with *ὁράω*, here 1st sg. *ἐκεῖ* expressly renders *v’* in *ch’ io v’ ho scorte*; the Italian compound perfect becomes a simple aorist.

> 10 I' non so ben ridir com' io v' entrai;  

I cannot rightly tell how I entered it,

> 10 Οὐκ ἔχω καλῶς εἰπεῖν, πῶς ποτ’ εἰσῆλθον·  
> (I cannot well tell how I ever entered it;)

- *Οὐκ ἔχω καλῶς εἰπεῖν* ‘I cannot well say’ uses *ἔχω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.) with an infinitive to express ability, rather than translating *so* by a verb of knowing. *καλῶς* answers to *ben*; simple *εἰπεῖν* does not reproduce the prefix of *ridir*. *οὐκ* is the form of the negative before a smooth-breathed vowel.
- *πῶς ποτ’ εἰσῆλθον* is an indirect question after *εἰπεῖν*. Enclitic *ποτε*, elided before *εἰσῆλθον*, adds ‘ever’ to interrogative *πῶς*. *εἰσῆλθον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *εἰσέρχομαι*) conveys motion in by its prefix; *v’* in *com’ io v’ entrai* has no separate local equivalent.

> 11 tant' era pien di sonno in su quel punto,  
> 12 che la verace via abbandonai.  

so full of sleep was I about the moment that I left the true way.

> 11 Οὕτως ὑπνώττων ἤμην τῇ στιγμῇ ᾽κείνῃ,  
> (So drowsy was I at that moment)
>
> 12 Καθ’ ἣν τῆς ἀληθινῆς ἀπέστην τρίβου.  
> (When I departed from the true path.)

- *Οὕτως ὑπνώττων ἤμην* ‘so drowsing was I’ replaces *tant’ era pien di sonno*: the adjective and its complement become *ὑπνώττων*, nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *ὑπνώττω*, with Attic *-ττ-* for *-σσ-*. With *ἤμην* it describes an ongoing state. The 1st sg. imperfect copula *ἤμην* is later usage rather than the ordinary classical Attic *ἦ*.
- *τῇ στιγμῇ ᾽κείνῃ* ‘at that instant’ renders *in su quel punto* by a bare dative of time. *᾽κείνῃ* is *ἐκείνῃ* (dat. sg. fem.) with prodelision of the initial vowel after *στιγμῇ*. *Καθ’ ἣν* ‘at which’ resumes the instant: *κατά* takes the accusative feminine relative, and its final *τ* becomes *θ* on elision before the rough breathing. Greek thereby attaches the clause explicitly to the time expression rather than making it a result of *οὕτως*.
- *τῆς ἀληθινῆς ἀπέστην τρίβου* renders transitive *la verace via abbandonai* by ‘I withdrew from the true path’. *ἀπέστην* is 1st sg. intransitive second aor. act. ind. of *ἀφίστημι*, with internal augment; it governs the genitive of separation, splitting *τῆς ἀληθινῆς … τρίβου*.

> 13 Ma poi ch' io fui al piè d' un colle giunto,  
> 14 là dove terminava quella valle,  
> 15 che m' avea di paura il cor compunto,  

But after I had reached the foot of a Hill there, where that valley ended, which had pierced my heart with fear,

> 13 Ἀλλ’ ὅτ’ ἦλθον εἰς λόφου τινὸς τοὺς πόδας,  
> (But when I came to the foot of a hill,)
>
> 14 Ἔνθ᾽ ἦν τὸ τέρμα τῆς κοιλάδος ἐκείνης,  
> (Where lay the end of that valley)
>
> 15 Ἣ τὴν καρδίαν κατέπληξέ μοι φόβῳ,  
> (Which had struck my heart with fear,)

- *Ἀλλ’ ὅτ’ ἦλθον* ‘but when I came’ replaces *Ma poi ch’ io fui … giunto* with a temporal conjunction and simple aorist, without an explicit word for ‘after’. Both *ἀλλά* and *ὅτε* are elided. *ἦλθον* is here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*.
- *εἰς λόφου τινὸς τοὺς πόδας* ‘to the feet of a certain hill’ keeps the bodily noun of *al piè d’ un colle* but makes it plural. *εἰς* governs accusative *τοὺς πόδας* (from *πούς*), not the adjacent *λόφου*: the possessive genitive with indefinite *τινός* is placed between preposition and object.
- *Ἔνθ᾽ ἦν τὸ τέρμα τῆς κοιλάδος ἐκείνης* ‘where the end of that valley was’ compresses *là dove* into relative adverb *ἔνθα*, elided before *ἦν*. Italian *terminava* becomes copula plus nominative *τὸ τέρμα*; the former subject becomes dependent genitive *τῆς κοιλάδος ἐκείνης* (from *κοιλάς*, fem.).
- *Ἣ τὴν καρδίαν κατέπληξέ μοι φόβῳ* ‘which struck my heart with fear’ keeps the valley as agent. Relative *ἥ* is feminine singular like *κοιλάδος* but nominative as subject of *κατέπληξε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *καταπλήσσω*). The simple aorist replaces *avea … compunto*, striking replaces piercing, and instrumental *φόβῳ* renders *di paura*. Enclitic *μοι*, the dative of the person affected, marks the heart as the speaker’s, much as *m’* does in *m’ avea … il cor compunto*; it gives the preceding verb its additional accent.

> 16 guardai in alto, e vidi le sue spalle  
> 17 vestite già de' raggi del pianeta,  
> 18 che mena dritto altrui per ogni calle.  

I looked up and saw its shoulders already clothed with the rays of the Planet that leads men straight on every road.

> 16 Ὑψώσας ὄμματ’ εἶδον ἤδη τὰ νῶτα  
> (Raising my eyes I saw already its)
>
> 17 Αὐτῆς ἀκτῖνας φοροῦντα τοῦ πλανήτου  
> (Shoulders wearing the rays of the planet)
>
> 18 Τοῦ πάντας πανταχόσε φωταγωγοῦντος.  
> (That guides all men everywhere with its light.)

- *Ὑψώσας ὄμματ’ εἶδον* ‘having raised my eyes, I saw’ subordinates the first of the coordinated verbs *guardai in alto, e vidi*. *ὑψώσας* is nom. sg. masc. aor. act. participle of *ὑψόω*, agreeing with the subject of *εἶδον*. Its object is poetic plural *ὄμματ’*, elided *ὄμματα* (acc. pl. neut. of *ὄμμα*); Italian names the direction of the gaze rather than the eyes.
- *ἤδη τὰ νῶτα Αὐτῆς* places *già* before the object of seeing and renders *le sue spalle* by ‘its back’, literally the neuter plural ‘backs’. The possessive genitive *αὐτῆς* is feminine: it cannot regularly refer to masculine *λόφος*. The available feminine antecedent is *κοιλάς*, whereas Italian *sue* agrees with *spalle* and does not mark the gender of the possessor.
- *ἀκτῖνας φοροῦντα τοῦ πλανήτου* turns *vestite … de’ raggi del pianeta* into ‘wearing the rays of the planet’. *φοροῦντα* (pres. act. participle of *φορέω*, acc. pl. neut.) agrees with *τὰ νῶτα* in a participial complement to *εἶδον*, describing what they were seen doing. Accusative plural *ἀκτῖνας* (from *ἀκτίς*) is its direct object; *τοῦ πλανήτου* (gen. sg. masc. of *πλανήτης*) depends on *ἀκτῖνας* across the participle.
- *Τοῦ πάντας πανταχόσε φωταγωγοῦντος* replaces finite relative *che mena* with a second-attributive-position participle: the repeated article and *φωταγωγοῦντος* (gen. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *φωταγωγέω*) agree with *πλανήτου*. *πάντας* ‘everyone’ supplies a personal direct object for indefinite *altrui*; directional *πανταχόσε* ‘in every direction’ replaces *per ogni calle*. The compound verb specifies guidance by light; neither *dritto* nor the noun *calle* has a distinct equivalent.

> 19 Allor fu la paura un poco queta,  
> 20 che nel lago del cor m' era durata  
> 21 la notte, ch' i' passai con tanta pieta.  

Then the fear was somewhat calmed, which had continued in the lake of my heart the night that I passed so piteously.

> 19 Τότε μικρόν τι κατέπαυσεν ὁ φόβος,  
> (Then a little subsided the fear)
>
> 20 Ὁ τοὺς κρουνούς μοι παγώσας τῆς καρδίας  
> (That had frozen the springs of my heart)
>
> 21 Τὴν νύκτ’ ἐκείνην, ἣν διῆλθον ἐν φρίκαις.  
> (Through that night which I had passed in terrors.)

- *Τότε μικρόν τι κατέπαυσεν ὁ φόβος* ‘then the fear subsided a little’ retains *Allor* but postpones the subject. *μικρόν τι* is an adverbial neuter accusative of extent, with indefinite *τι*, for *un poco*. *κατέπαυσεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *καταπαύω*) is used intransitively in place of *fu … queta*, copula plus adjective.
- *Ὁ τοὺς κρουνούς μοι παγώσας τῆς καρδίας* converts the relative *che … m’ era durata* into an attributive participle with repeated article: *ὁ … παγώσας* (nom. sg. masc. aor. act. participle of *παγόω*) agrees with *ὁ φόβος*. The change is not merely one of tense: instead of fear persisting ‘in the lake’ it is fear ‘having frozen the springs’. *τοὺς κρουνούς* is a direct object, not a locative; *τῆς καρδίας* depends on it across the intervening words, and possessive dative *μοι* answers to *m’*.
- *Τὴν νύκτ’ ἐκείνην* renders *la notte* as an accusative of duration with the demonstrative ‘that’ added; *νύκτ’* is elided *νύκτα*, acc. sg. of *νύξ*. *ἣν διῆλθον* retains the relative construction of *ch’ i’ passai*: *ἥν* is the accusative time traversed, and *διῆλθον* is 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *διέρχομαι*. *ἐν φρίκαις* ‘in shudders’ uses *ἐν* with the dative plural of *φρίκη* to express the attendant state; it interprets *pieta* in terms of terror and gives no separate equivalent for *tanta*.

## Lines 22–54 — Looking Back and Meeting the Three Beasts

> 22 E come quei, che con lena affannata  
> 23 uscito fuor del pelago alla riva,  
> 24 si volge all' acqua perigliosa, e guata:  

And as he, who with panting breath has escaped from the deep sea to the shore, turns to the dangerous water and gazes:

> 22 Ὡς δ’ ὅτε τις βαρέως ἀσθμαίνων ἔξω  
> (And as one who, panting heavily, out)
>
> 23 Τοῦ πελάγους, ἐξελθὼν ἐπὶ τὴν ὄχθην,  
> (Of the sea, having come forth upon the shore,)
>
> 24 Στρέφων πρὸς τὸ σφαλερὸν κῦμ’ ἀτενίζει,  
> (Turns and gazes at the perilous wave,)

- *Ὡς δ’ ὅτε τις* ‘and as when someone’ gives *E come quei, che* an epic form of introduction: *ὡς* marks the comparison, *ὅτε* introduces the scene, and indefinite *τις* supplies its subject. Postpositive *δέ*, elided before *ὅτε*, does the work of initial *E*.
- *βαρέως ἀσθμαίνων* ‘panting heavily’ turns the noun phrase *con lena affannata* into an adverb and a present active participle (nom. masc. sg. of *ἀσθμαίνω*). By contrast, *ἐξελθών* (second aor. act. participle of *ἐξέρχομαι*, in the same case) presents *uscito* as an action already accomplished. *ἔξω τοῦ πελάγους* ‘out of the sea’ reinforces the outward direction of *ἐξ-*; *ἐπὶ τὴν ὄχθην*, with the accusative of destination, renders *alla riva*.
- Of the two coordinated presents *si volge … e guata*, only the second remains finite: *στρέφων … ἀτενίζει* ‘turning … he gazes intently’. *στρέφων* is present active, used without an expressed object where Italian uses a reflexive. *πρὸς τὸ σφαλερὸν κῦμ’* gives the direction of the turn and the gaze; *κῦμ’* is elided *κῦμα* (acc. neut. sg.), a ‘wave’ rather than the less specific *acqua*.

> 25 così l' animo mio, che ancor fuggiva,  
> 26 si volse indietro a rimirar lo passo,  
> 27 che non lasciò giammai persona viva.  

so my mind, which still was fleeing, turned back to see the pass that no one ever left alive.

> 25 Οὕτως ἡ διαφεύγουσ’ ἔτι ψυχή μου  
> (So my soul, still fleeing,)
>
> 26 Στρέφουσ’ ὀπίσω τὸν πόρον ἐθεώρει,  
> (Turned back to look upon the pass)
>
> 27 Ὃν κατέλιπεν ἄνθρωπος ζῶν οὐδέπω.  
> (Which no living man has ever left behind.)

- *Οὕτως* answers *ὡς* as *così* answers *come*. In *ἡ διαφεύγουσ’ ἔτι ψυχή μου*, the present active participle *διαφεύγουσ’* (elided *διαφεύγουσα*, nom. fem. sg.) stands between article and noun: *che ancor fuggiva* becomes an attributive phrase, ‘my still-fleeing soul’. *μου* is the enclitic possessive genitive.
- *Στρέφουσ’ ὀπίσω τὸν πόρον ἐθεώρει* reverses the Italian ranking of the actions: *si volse indietro* becomes a circumstantial participle, *στρέφουσ’* (for *στρέφουσα*, agreeing with *ψυχή*), while the infinitive *rimirar* becomes the main verb, *ἐθεώρει* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *θεωρέω*), ‘was looking at’. *τὸν πόρον*, ‘the passage’, is its accusative object.
- *Ὃν κατέλιπεν ἄνθρωπος ζῶν οὐδέπω* makes a grammatical choice obscured by Italian *che*: *ὅν* is accusative, the passage **left behind**, not the subject that lets someone go. *κατέλιπεν* is the 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *καταλείπω*, with internal augment. *ἄνθρωπος ζῶν* ‘a human being alive’ is its nominative subject (*ζῶν*, contracted present participle of *ζάω*); final *οὐδέπω* negates the clause with ‘not yet’ rather than *non … giammai* ‘never’.

> 28 Poi ch' ei posato un poco il corpo lasso,  
> 29 ripresi via per la piaggia diserta,  
> 30 sì che il piè fermo sempre era il più basso.  

After I had rested my wearied body [a short while], I took the way again along the desert strand, so that the [right] foot always was the lower.

> 28 Ἄνεσιν δέ τινα δοὺς τῷ σώματί μου,  
> (Having given some rest to my body,)
>
> 29 Ἐπανέλαβον τὴν ὁδὸν τῆς ἐρήμου,  
> (I resumed the way of the desert,)
>
> 30 Ὥστ᾽ ἦν ποῦς στεῤῥότερος ὁ κατωτέρω.  
> (So that the firmer foot was always the lower.)

- *Ἄνεσιν δέ τινα δοὺς τῷ σώματί μου* ‘and having given my body some respite’ replaces the finite temporal clause *Poi ch’ ei posato*. *δούς* (nom. masc. sg., aor. act. participle of *δίδωμι*) takes an accusative of the thing given and a dative of the recipient. Indefinite *τινα* qualifies *ἄνεσιν* and thus renders *un poco* as an amount of rest; *μου* supplies a possessive, but nothing separately renders *lasso*.
- *Ἐπανέλαβον* ‘I took up again’ is the 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἐπαναλαμβάνω*, with augment after the prefixes. *τὴν ὁδόν* renders *via* as its direct object. In place of *per la piaggia diserta* comes a dependent genitive, *τῆς ἐρήμου* ‘of the wilderness’: the adjective is substantivized and the strand is not named.
- *Ὥστ’ ἦν* (elided *ὥστε* with the imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*) presents *sì che* as an actual result. In *ποῦς στεῤῥότερος ὁ κατωτέρω*, *στεῤῥότερος* (nom. masc. sg. comparative of *στερρός*; the doubled rho is printed with breathings) means ‘firmer’, not “right”. *ὁ κατωτέρω* uses the article with a comparative adverb of place, ‘the one farther down’. The two expressions are equated; no adverb explicitly renders *sempre*.

> 31 Ed ecco, quasi al cominciar dell' erta,  
> 32 una lonza leggiera e presta molto,  
> 33 che di pel maculato era coperta.  

And behold, almost at the commencement of the steep, a Leopard, light and very nimble, which was covered with spotted [hair].

> 31 Ἀλλ᾽ ἰδοὺ σχεδὸν ἐν ἀρχῇ τῆς ἀνόδου  
> (But behold, almost at the beginning of the ascent,)
>
> 32 Πάρδαλίς τις ἐλαφρὰ καὶ ταχυτάτη,  
> (A nimble and very swift leopard,)
>
> 33 Κεκαλυμμένη δέρματι πολυστίκτῳ,  
> (Covered with a dappled hide,)

- *Ἀλλ’ ἰδού* renders *Ed ecco* with a more adversative connective (*ἀλλά*, elided) and the conventional presentative *ἰδού*, historically a second-person singular aorist middle imperative. *σχεδὸν ἐν ἀρχῇ τῆς ἀνόδου* closely follows *quasi al cominciar dell’ erta*, but *ἀρχῇ* is a dative noun rather than a substantivized infinitive, and *ἄνοδος* names the ascent rather than the steep ground.
- *Πάρδαλίς τις* ‘a certain leopard’ is nominative, with enclitic *τις* for *una*; the additional accent on *πάρδαλίς* is due to the enclitic. *ἐλαφρά* and *ταχυτάτη* agree in the feminine singular. The latter, superlative of *ταχύς*, is intensive, ‘very swift’, for adjective plus adverb in *presta molto*.
- *Κεκαλυμμένη* (nom. fem. sg., reduplicated perfect middle/passive participle of *καλύπτω*, passive in sense) gives the resulting state without a relative clause or an equivalent of *era*. *δέρματι πολυστίκτῳ* is dative of covering, ‘with a many-spotted hide’, for *di pel maculato*.

> 34 E non mi si partia dinanzi al volto;  
> 35 anzi impediva tanto il mio cammino,  
> 36 ch' io fui per ritornar più volte volto.  

And it went not from before my face; nay, so impeded my way, that I had often turned to go back.

> 34 Ἀκίνητος ἵστατο κατέναντί μου,  
> (Stood motionless before me,)
>
> 35 Τοσοῦτό τ᾽ ἐκώλυε τὴν πρόοδόν μου,  
> (And so hindered my advance)
>
> 36 Ὥστ᾽ ἐπειράθην ἐπιστρέψαι πολλάκις.  
> (That many times I tried to turn back.)

- *Ἀκίνητος ἵστατο κατέναντί μου* ‘it stood motionless opposite me’ gives *non mi si partia dinanzi al volto* positive form. *ἀκίνητος* is a two-ending adjective, here nom. fem. sg., predicative to *πάρδαλις*; *ἵστατο* is the 3rd sg. imperf. middle of *ἵστημι*, intransitive. *κατέναντι* governs genitive *μου*: the person, not specifically the face, is named.
- *Τοσοῦτό τ’ ἐκώλυε τὴν πρόοδόν μου* ‘and so much hindered my advance’ substitutes connective *τε* (elided) for corrective *anzi*. *τοσοῦτο* is an adverbial neuter accusative anticipating *ὥστε*; *ἐκώλυε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *κωλύω*) keeps the durative force of *impediva*. *πρόοδος* makes *cammino* the act of advancing.
- *Ὥστ’ ἐπειράθην ἐπιστρέψαι πολλάκις* again has *ὥστε* with an indicative of actual result. *ἐπειράθην* (1st sg. aor. passive in form, of *πειράω*, here ‘I tried’) takes the aorist active infinitive *ἐπιστρέψαι*, ‘to turn back’. What is asserted as repeatedly actual is the **attempt**: Musurus neither asserts completed returns nor reproduces the verbal echo of *ritornar … volte volto*.

> 37 Tempo era dal principio del mattino;  
> 38 e il sol montava in su con quelle stelle  
> 39 ch' eran con lui, quando l' amor divino  
> 40 mosse da prima quelle cose belle;  

The time was at the beginning of the morning; and the sun was mounting up with those stars, which were with him when Divine Love first moved those fair things:

> 37 Καιρὸς δ᾽ ἦν, καθ᾽ ὃν ἡ πρωΐα διηύγει,  
> (It was the hour when morning was breaking,)
>
> 38 Ὁ δ᾽ ἥλιος ὑψοῦτο μετὰ τῶν ἄστρων,  
> (And the sun was rising with those stars)
>
> 39 Οἷς συνυπῆρχεν, ὁ θεῖος ὁπότ᾽ ἔρως  
> (Which were with it when Divine Love)
>
> 40 Τὰ καλὰ ᾽κεῖνα πρῶτον ἐκίνησ᾽ ἔργα.  
> (First set in motion those fair works.)

- *Καιρὸς δ’ ἦν, καθ’ ὃν ἡ πρωΐα διηύγει* ‘and it was the hour at which morning was growing light’ expands *dal principio del mattino* into a relative clause. *καθ’ ὅν* is temporal *κατά* with the accusative, elided and aspirated before the rough breathing. *ἡ πρωΐα* is subject of *διηύγει* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *διαυγέω*, with temporal augment in the verbal stem).
- *Ὁ δ’ ἥλιος ὑψοῦτο* ‘and the sun was rising’ has the contracted 3rd sg. imperf. middle/passive of *ὑψόω*, literally ‘was being raised’, for active *montava in su*. *μετὰ τῶν ἄστρων* expresses accompaniment by *μετά* with the genitive; there is an article but no demonstrative for *quelle*.
- *Οἷς συνυπῆρχεν* ‘with which he was present’ reverses *ch’ eran con lui*: the sun remains the singular subject, the stars become the dative neuter plural relative governed by the *συν-* compound. *συνυπῆρχεν* is the 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *συνυπάρχω*.
- *ὁ θεῖος ὁπότ’ ἔρως* interposes the temporal conjunction *ὁπότ’* (elided *ὁπότε*, ‘when’) between adjective and noun in *l’ amor divino*. It introduces a definite past event with *ἐκίνησ’* (elided *ἐκίνησε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *κινέω*), not an iterative optative. *πρῶτον* is adverbial for *da prima*. *τὰ καλὰ ’κεῖνα … ἔργα* supplies ‘works’ for *cose* and postpones the noun; *’κεῖνα* is *ἐκεῖνα* with prodelision of its initial vowel.

> 41 sì che a bene sperar m' eran cagione  
> 42 di quella fera alla gaietta pelle  
> 43 l' ora del tempo, e la dolce stagione:  

so that the hour of time and the sweet season [caused me to have good hope of that animal with the gay skin];

> 41 Καὶ δή μοι χρηστὰς ὑπέφαινον ἐλπίδας  
> (And indeed good hopes were held out to me)
>
> 42 Τοῦ τε θηρίου τὸ ποικιλόχρουν δέρμα  
> (By both the many-coloured hide of the beast)
>
> 43 Ὥρα τ᾽ ἔαρος ἡμέρας τε πρωΐα·  
> (And the season of spring and the morning of the day;)

- *Καὶ δή* ‘and indeed’ makes an emphatic continuation rather than formally subordinating the consequence as *sì che* does. *μοι χρηστὰς ὑπέφαινον ἐλπίδας* ‘they held out good hopes to me’ changes *a bene sperar m’ eran cagione* into a transitive verb with a dative recipient and an accusative plural object: adverb *bene* becomes attributive *χρηστάς*. *ὑπέφαινον* is 3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *ὑποφαίνω*, with internal augment.
- The postponed subjects change the construction of *di quella fera alla gaietta pelle*. Instead of making the beast the object of hope, Musurus makes *τοῦ … θηρίου τὸ ποικιλόχρουν δέρμα* ‘the beast’s many-coloured hide’ one of the things that inspire hope. *τοῦ θηρίου* is possessive genitive; *ποικιλόχρουν* is the contracted nom. neut. sg. of *ποικιλόχροος*, agreeing with *δέρμα*.
- The repeated enclitic *τε* links the hide, *ὥρα … ἔαρος* ‘the season of spring’, and *ἡμέρας … πρωΐα* ‘the morning of the day’; *τ’* before *ἔαρος* is elided. *ἔαρος* (gen. sg. of *ἔαρ*) specifies the season rather than translating *dolce*. The two temporal subjects thus occur in the reverse of the order *l’ ora del tempo, e la dolce stagione*.

> 44 ma non sì, che paura non mi desse  
> 45 la vista, che m' apparve, d' un leone.  

yet not so, but that I feared at the sight, which appeared to me, of a Lion.

> 44 Ἀλλ᾽ οὐχ οὕτως, ὡς μή μ᾽ ἔμφοβον γενέσθαι  
> (Yet not so much as to keep me from being afraid)
>
> 45 Ἐκ τῆς θέας λέοντος ἐπιφανέντος.  
> (At the sight of a lion that appeared.)

- *Ἀλλ’ οὐχ οὕτως, ὡς μή μ’ ἔμφοβον γενέσθαι* ‘but not to such a degree as for me not to become afraid’ retains the two negatives of *ma non sì, che paura non mi desse*. *οὐχ* negates the degree expressed by *οὕτως* (the aspirated spelling precedes a rough breathing); *μή* negates the infinitive in the consecutive *ὡς* construction. *μ’* (elided *με*) is its accusative subject, *ἔμφοβον* its predicate adjective, and *γενέσθαι* the second aorist middle infinitive of *γίγνομαι*.
- Thus *paura … mi desse* ‘should give me fear’ is recast from the experiencer’s side. *Ἐκ τῆς θέας* gives the cause, ‘from the sight’, instead of making *la vista* subject. In *λέοντος ἐπιφανέντος*, *λέοντος* is the genitive object of seeing, and *ἐπιφανέντος* (gen. masc. sg., second aor. passive participle of *ἐπιφαίνω*, intransitive in sense) agrees with it: it is the lion ‘that appeared’, whereas *che m’ apparve* qualifies *la vista*. The recipient of the appearance is not expressed again.

> 46 Questi parea che contra me venesse  
> 47 con la testa alta, e con rabbiosa fame,  
> 48 sì che parea che l' aer ne temesse;  

He seemed coming upon me with head erect, and furious hunger; so that the air seemed to [have fear] thereat;

> 46 Οὗτος πρὸς ἐμὲ καθορμήσειν ἐδόκει,  
> (He seemed about to rush upon me,)
>
> 47 Ὑψαύχην, πειναλέος, λυσσώδης οὕτως,  
> (With head held high, famished, so raging)
>
> 48 Ὥστε φρίσσοντα φαίνεσθαι τὸν ἀέρα·  
> (That the air itself seemed to shudder;)

- *Οὗτος πρὸς ἐμὲ καθορμήσειν ἐδόκει* ‘this one seemed about to charge at me’ renders *Questi parea che contra me venesse* by a personal *δοκέω* construction. *ἐδόκει* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind.) shares its subject with *καθορμήσειν*, future active infinitive of *καθορμάω*: the charge is prospective, not simply in progress. *πρὸς ἐμέ* uses the full pronoun after the preposition.
- Three nominative masculine singular adjectives describe the subject: *ὑψαύχην* ‘high-necked’ condenses *con la testa alta*; *πειναλέος* ‘famished’ and *λυσσώδης* ‘raging’ distribute *con rabbiosa fame* between two predicates of the lion rather than making the rage a quality of hunger.
- *οὕτως, ὥστε φρίσσοντα φαίνεσθαι τὸν ἀέρα* corresponds to *sì che parea che l’ aer ne temesse*, but has a result infinitive rather than two finite clauses. *τὸν ἀέρα* is the postponed accusative subject of present middle/passive *φαίνεσθαι* ‘to appear’; *φρίσσοντα* (acc. masc. sg., pres. act. participle of *φρίσσω*) agrees with it as a supplementary participle, ‘to appear to be shuddering’. The bodily verb replaces *temesse*, and *ne* has no separate equivalent.

> 49 ed una lupa, che di tutte brame  
> 50 sembiava carca nella sua magrezza,  
> 51 e molte genti fe' già viver grame.  

and a She-wolf, that looked full of all cravings in her leanness; and has ere now made many live in sorrow.

> 49 Καὶ λύκαινά τις, ἣ μεστὴ πόθων πάντων  
> (And a she-wolf, who full of all)
>
> 50 Ἐδόκει καὶ παντοίων, κάτισχνος οὖσα,  
> (And every kind of craving seemed, being gaunt,)
>
> 51 Πολλούς τ᾽ ἐποίησ᾽ ἤδη ζῆν παναθλίους.  
> (And has already made many live in utter misery.)

- *Καὶ λύκαινά τις* ‘and a certain she-wolf’ retains the loosely appended noun phrase *ed una lupa*, without supplying a new main verb. The feminine nominative *ἣ* is the subject within the relative clause. In *μεστὴ πόθων πάντων … καὶ παντοίων*, *μεστή* ‘full’ is predicate to that subject and governs the genitive of contents; *πάντων* and added *παντοίων*, both gen. masc. pl. with *πόθων*, distinguish ‘all’ from ‘of every kind’.
- *Ἐδόκει* ‘she seemed’ links the subject to *μεστή*, with ‘to be’ understood, for *sembiava carca*. *κάτισχνος οὖσα* ‘though she was gaunt’ makes *nella sua magrezza* a circumstantial phrase: *οὖσα* is the nom. fem. sg. present participle of *εἰμί*, with predicate *κάτισχνος*, a two-ending adjective.
- *Πολλούς τ’ ἐποίησ’ ἤδη ζῆν παναθλίους* follows the Italian causative *molte genti fe’ già viver grame* closely. *τ’* connects another predicate within the relative clause; *ἐποίησ’* is elided *ἐποίησε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ποιέω*), with *ἤδη* for *già*. Substantival *πολλούς* ‘many people’ is the accusative subject of *ζῆν* (contracted pres. act. infinitive of *ζάω*); *παναθλίους* is its predicate adjective, masculine plural in place of Italian feminine *grame* and intensified by *παν-*.

> 52 Questa mi porse tanto di gravezza  
> 53 con la paura, che uscia di sua vista,  
> 54 ch' io perdei la speranza dell' altezza.  

She brought such heaviness upon me with the terror of her aspect, that I lost the hope of ascending.

> 52 Αὕτη μοι τόσην ἐνεποίησε νάρκην,  
> (She wrought such numbness in me,)
>
> 53 Τρόμον ἀφιεῖσ᾽ ἐκ τῶν αὐτῆς ὀμμάτων,  
> (Casting dread from her eyes,)
>
> 54 Ὥστ᾽ ἀπηλπίσθην τὴν κορυφὴν ἱκέσθαι·  
> (That I despaired of reaching the summit;)

- *Αὕτη μοι τόσην ἐνεποίησε νάρκην* follows *Questa mi porse tanto di gravezza* in placing the demonstrative subject and the recipient first. *ἐνεποίησε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐμποιέω*, internally augmented) means ‘produced in’, with *μοι* for the person affected. Instead of a quantity followed by *di gravezza*, *τόσην* directly qualifies its accusative noun *νάρκην* ‘numbness’.
- *Τρόμον ἀφιεῖσ’ ἐκ τῶν αὐτῆς ὀμμάτων* ‘sending out dread from her eyes’ shifts agency: in *con la paura, che uscia di sua vista* the fear itself issues forth. *ἀφιεῖσ’* is elided *ἀφιεῖσα* (nom. fem. sg., pres. act. participle of *ἀφίημι*), agreeing with *αὕτη* and taking *τρόμον* as object. *ἐκ* governs gen. pl. *ὀμμάτων* (from *ὄμμα*), making *vista* specifically the eyes; *αὐτῆς* is possessive.
- *Ὥστ’ ἀπηλπίσθην* answers *τόσην* with an actual result, the 1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ἀπελπίζω* ‘I was brought to despair’. This single verb replaces *perdei la speranza*. Its complement *τὴν κορυφὴν ἱκέσθαι* ‘to reach the summit’ resolves *dell’ altezza* into a goal and an action: *ἱκέσθαι* is the second aorist middle infinitive of poetic *ἱκνέομαι*, with the summit in the accusative and the same understood subject as *ἀπηλπίσθην*.

## Lines 55–87 — The Beast’s Repulse and the Recognition of Virgil

> 55 E quale è quei, che volentieri acquista,  
> 56 e giugne il tempo che perder lo face,  
> 57 che in tutti i suoi pensier piange e s'attrista:  

And as one who is eager in gaining, and, when the time arrives that makes him lose, weeps and afflicts himself in all his thoughts:

> 55 Ὡς δέ τις χαίρων ἐν ταῖς ἐπικερδείαις,  
> (And as one who rejoices in his gains,)
>
> 56 Ἀλλ᾽, ἐφεστῶτος τοῦ καιροῦ τῆς ζημίας,  
> (Yet, when the time of loss comes upon him,)
>
> 57 Ἄχθετ᾽ ἐν τοῖς λογισμοῖς αὐτοῦ καὶ κλαίει,  
> (Grieves in all his thoughts and weeps,)

- *Ὡς δέ τις* ‘and as someone’ opens a comparison answered by *Τοιόνδ᾽* in 58. Postpositive *δέ* renders *E*; indefinite *τις* does the work of *quei, che* without a relative clause. *χαίρων* (pres. act. participle, nom. sg. masc., of *χαίρω*) makes *volentieri acquista* a circumstance of the subject: ‘rejoicing in his gains’, with *ἐν* and the dative plural *ταῖς ἐπικερδείαις*, rather than ‘willingly acquires’.
- *Ἀλλ᾽* (elided *ἀλλά*) makes the contrast sharper than the second *e*. *ἐφεστῶτος τοῦ καιροῦ* is a temporal genitive absolute: *ἐφεστῶτος* is the gen. sg. masc. of the intransitive perfect active participle of *ἐφίστημι*, ‘having come upon him, being at hand’. *τῆς ζημίας* depends on *καιροῦ*: ‘the occasion of loss’ replaces *il tempo che perder lo face*, with neither a causative verb nor an expressed object corresponding to *lo*.
- *Ἄχθετ᾽* is elided *ἄχθεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *ἄχθομαι*), ‘is distressed’; *κλαίει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *κλαίω*) is ‘weeps’. The two finite verbs complete *ὡς … τις* and reverse *piange e s’attrista*. *ἐν τοῖς λογισμοῖς αὐτοῦ* follows *in … i suoi pensier*, with a possessive genitive but no separate equivalent of *tutti*.

> 58 tal mi fece la bestia senza pace,  
> 59 che, venendomi incontro, a poco a poco  
> 60 mi ripingeva là dove il sol tace.  

such that restless beast made me, which coming against me, by little and little drove me back to where the Sun is silent.

> 58 Τοιόνδ᾽ ἡ θὴρ ἄκαμπτος ἐποίησέ με,  
> (Such did the unyielding beast make me,)
>
> 59 Ἣ κατὰ μικρὸν ἀντίον μου χωροῦσα  
> (Who, advancing little by little against me,)
>
> 60 Ἐξώθει μ᾽ ἔνθ᾽ ἥλιος σιγηλὸς μένει.  
> (Was driving me back to where the sun stays silent.)

- *Τοιόνδ᾽* (elided *τοιόνδε*, acc. sg. masc. of *τοιόσδε*) is predicative to *με*: ‘such did the beast make me’. *ἐποίησέ* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ποιέω*, with an additional accent before enclitic *με*. *ἡ θὴρ ἄκαμπτος* uses the feminine article with *θήρ* and a two-termination adjective: ‘the beast, unbending’ interprets *la bestia senza pace* as relentless rather than literally without rest.
- *Ἣ* (nom. sg. fem. of *ὅς*) takes up *θήρ* as subject. *χωροῦσα* (pres. act. participle, nom. sg. fem., of *χωρέω*) renders the simultaneous gerund *venendomi*; *ἀντίον* with genitive *μου* gives the direction ‘against me’. Greek puts distributive *κατὰ μικρόν* ‘little by little’ before the participle, within the description of the advance.
- *Ἐξώθει* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἐξωθέω*) preserves the continuing action of *ripingeva*, though the prefix suggests pushing *out*, not specifically *back*. *μ᾽* is elided *με*; *ἔνθ᾽* is *ἔνθα*, its final consonant aspirated before *ἥλιος*. A single local adverb does duty for *là dove*. *σιγηλὸς μένει* ‘remains silent’ divides *tace* between a predicate adjective agreeing with *ἥλιος* and a verb of continuance.

> 61 Mentre ch' io rovinava in basso loco,  
> 62 dinanzi agli occhi mi si fu offerto  
> 63 chi per lungo silenzio parea fioco.  

Whilst I was rushing downwards, there appeared before my eyes one who seemed hoarse from long silence.

> 61 Πλανωμένου μου δ᾽ ἐν ταῖς κατωφερείαις,  
> (While I was wandering down the lower slopes,)
>
> 62 Παρέστηκέ τις πρὸ τῶν ἐμῶν ὀμμάτων,  
> (There appeared before my eyes one)
>
> 63 Ὃς μακρὸν σιγῶν λιπόφθογγος ἐδόκει.  
> (Who from long silence seemed faint of voice.)

- *Πλανωμένου μου* is a temporal genitive absolute (pres. mid./pass. participle, gen. sg. masc., of *πλανάω*, with *μου* as subject): ‘while I was wandering’ loses the precipitous motion of *rovinava*. *ἐν ταῖς κατωφερείαις* ‘on the downward slopes’ substitutes a plural place-expression with static *ἐν* for *in basso loco*. Elided, postpositive *δ᾽* links the sentence to what precedes.
- *Παρέστηκέ τις* ‘someone has taken his stand beside me’ replaces the presentation *mi si fu offerto … chi* with an indefinite subject and the intransitive perfect active of *παρίστημι* (3rd sg., *παρέστηκε*, additionally accented before *τις*). The perfect presents the figure as now present. *πρὸ τῶν ἐμῶν ὀμμάτων* renders *dinanzi agli occhi*, supplying the possessive adjective inside the article–noun group rather than a separate dative for *mi*.
- *Ὃς … ἐδόκει* ‘who seemed’ is an explicit relative clause with *τις* as antecedent; *ἐδόκει* is 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *δοκέω*. Predicative *λιπόφθογγος* specifies *fioco* as ‘faint of voice’. *μακρὸν σιγῶν* ‘being silent a long time’ turns *per lungo silenzio* into a circumstantial participle (pres. act., nom. sg. masc., of *σιγάω*) with an adverbial accusative of duration; the causal force is inferred.

> 64 Quand' io vidi costui nel gran diserto,  
> 65 “Miserere di me,” gridai a lui,  
> 66 “qual che tu sii, od ombra, od uomo certo.”  

When I saw him in the great desert, I cried: “Have pity on me, whate’er thou be, whether shade or veritable man!”

> 64 Ὡς δ᾽ εἶδον αὐτὸν ἐν τοιᾷδ᾽ ἐρημίᾳ,  
> (When I saw him in such a desert,)
>
> 65 Εἶπον ἀνακραυγάσας· “Ἐλέησόν με,  
> (I cried aloud and said: "Have pity on me,)
>
> 66 „ Οἷός ποτ᾽ εἶ, ζῶν ἄνθρωπος ἢ σκιά τις.”—  
> (Whoever you may be, living man or shade."—)

- Temporal *Ὡς* with *εἶδον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*) renders *Quand’ io vidi*. *αὐτόν* is less demonstrative than *costui*. *ἐν τοιᾷδ᾽ ἐρημίᾳ* ‘in such a solitude’ substitutes quality for the size expressed by *gran*: *τοιᾷδ᾽* is elided *τοιᾷδε*, dat. sg. fem. of *τοιόσδε*.
- *Εἶπον ἀνακραυγάσας* ‘I said, crying out’ moves the reporting words before the quotation. *εἶπον* is 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of the verb ‘say’; *ἀνακραυγάσας* is the nom. sg. masc. aor. act. participle of *ἀνακραυγάζω*. The participle carries the force of *gridai*; *a lui* is left implicit. *Ἐλέησόν με* uses the 2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *ἐλεέω* with a direct accusative, not the construction of *Miserere di me*.
- *Οἷός ποτ᾽ εἶ* ‘of whatever sort you are’ uses qualitative *οἷος*, strengthened in indefiniteness by elided enclitic *ποτε*, with the 2nd sg. pres. indicative of *εἰμί* rather than the subjunctive of *qual che tu sii*. *ζῶν ἄνθρωπος ἢ σκιά τις* reverses the alternatives, makes *uomo certo* specifically a ‘living man’ (*ζῶν*, pres. act. participle of *ζάω*), and adds indefinite *τις* to ‘shade’. Both alternatives are predicate nominatives.

> 67 Risposemi : “Non uomo, uomo già fui,  

He answered me: “Not man, a man I once was;

> 67 —“Οὐκέτ᾽ ἄνθρωπος,” εἶπεν, “ἄνθρωπος ἤμην·  
> (—"No longer man," he said, "a man I was;)

- *Οὐκέτ᾽ ἄνθρωπος … ἄνθρωπος ἤμην* keeps the repeated predicate noun and the unexpressed present copula of *Non uomo, uomo già fui*. Elided *οὐκέτι* ‘no longer’ makes the temporal contrast explicit in the first member; *ἤμην* is a postclassical 1st sg. imperfect of *εἰμί* (Attic *ἦ*), expressing the former state without a separate word for *già*. Parenthetic *εἶπεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind.) moves *Risposemi* inside the speech, reduces ‘answered’ to ‘said’, and omits the recipient.

> 68 e li parenti miei furon Lombardi,  
> 69 Mantovani per patria ambo e dui.  

and my parents were Lombards, and both of Mantua by country.

> 68 „ Οἱ δ᾽ ἐμοὶ γονεῖς ὑπῆρξαν Λογγοβάρδοι,  
> (And my parents were Lombards,)
>
> 69 „Καὶ Μαντυανοὶ τὴν πατρίδ᾽ ἦσαν ἄμφω·  
> (And both were Mantuans by homeland;)

- *Οἱ δ᾽ ἐμοὶ γονεῖς* corresponds closely to *e li parenti miei*, with elided postpositive *δέ* and the possessive adjective between article and noun. *ὑπῆρξαν* (3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *ὑπάρχω*) is copular with predicate nominative *Λογγοβάρδοι*, ‘were Lombards’.
- *Καὶ Μαντυανοὶ τὴν πατρίδ᾽ ἦσαν ἄμφω* expands the Italian apposition into a second coordinated predication: *ἦσαν* is 3rd pl. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*. *τὴν πατρίδ᾽* (elided *πατρίδα*, from *πατρίς*) is accusative of respect, ‘Mantuan as regards homeland’. The single dual form *ἄμφω* renders the emphatic *ambo e dui*; it stands in apposition to a plural subject with a plural verb, not a dual verb.

> 70 Nacqui sub Julio, ancorchè fosse tardi,  
> 71 e vissi a Roma sotto il buono Augusto,  
> 72 al tempo degli Dei falsi e bugiardi.  

I was born *sub Julio*, though it was late; and lived at Rome under the good Augustus, in the time of the false and lying Gods.

> 70 „Ἐγενόμην ὀψέπερ ἐπ᾽ Ἰουλίου,  
> (I was born late under Julius,)
>
> 71 „Ἐν Ῥώμῃ δ᾽ ἐπ᾽ εὐμενοῦς ἔζησ᾽ Αὐγούστου  
> (And lived in Rome under gracious Augustus)
>
> 72 „Ἐν χρόνοις θεῶν ψευδῶν καὶ ψευδομένων.  
> (In the times of false and lying gods.)

- *Ἐγενόμην* (1st sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *γίγνομαι*) renders *Nacqui*. *ὀψέπερ* compresses *ancorchè fosse tardi*: the emphasis of *-περ* on *ὀψέ* ‘late’ suggests the qualification without a subordinate verb. *ἐπ᾽ Ἰουλίου* uses elided *ἐπί* with the genitive of the person in whose time something occurred.
- *Ἐν Ῥώμῃ* gives location with the dative. *δ᾽ ἐπ᾽ εὐμενοῦς ἔζησ᾽ Αὐγούστου* repeats the temporal *ἐπί* construction for *sotto il buono Augusto*, but separates *εὐμενοῦς* (gen. sg. masc. of *εὐμενής*, ‘well-disposed’) from *Αὐγούστου* by *ἔζησ᾽*, elided *ἔζησα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ζάω*).
- *Ἐν χρόνοις* ‘in times’ pluralizes *al tempo*. Of the two genitive plural modifiers of *θεῶν*, *ψευδῶν* is an adjective (from *ψευδής*), whereas *ψευδομένων* is a present middle participle (from *ψεύδομαι*): *falsi e bugiardi* becomes ‘false and speaking falsely’, with an audible repetition of the root.

> 73 Poeta fui, e cantai di quel giusto  
> 74 figliuol d' Anchise, che venne da Troia,  
> 75 poi che il superbo Ilion fu combusto.  

A poet I was; and sang of that just son of Anchises, who came from Troy after proud Ilium was burnt.

> 73 „Ποιητὴς ἤμην καὶ τὸν δίκαιον ᾖδον  
> (A poet I was, and sang of the righteous)
>
> 74 „Υἱὸν Ἀγχίσου, τοῦ Τροίηθεν ἐλθόντος,  
> (Son of Anchises, who came from Troy)
>
> 75 „Ἐπεὶ τὸ κλεινὸν Ἴλιον κατεκαύθη.  
> (After renowned Ilium was burned.)

- *Ποιητὴς ἤμην* preserves the initial predicate of *Poeta fui*. *τὸν δίκαιον … Υἱόν* ‘the righteous son’ drops the demonstrative force of *quel* and is the direct object of *ᾖδον* (here 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ᾄδω*). Greek thus has ‘I sang the son’, not *cantai di*; the verb intervenes between adjective and noun, and its imperfect presents the singing as an activity rather than as a completed act.
- *Ἀγχίσου* is the possessive genitive of *Ἀγχίσης*. *Τροίηθεν* is a poetic directional adverb: the epic form of the place-name and the suffix *-θεν* together give ‘from Troy’. *τοῦ … ἐλθόντος* is an articular second aor. act. participle of *ἔρχομαι*, gen. sg. masc., replacing finite *che venne*. There is a case mismatch with the intended *Υἱόν*: grammatically the participle attaches to genitive *Ἀγχίσου*; to modify the accusative ‘son’ it would be *τὸν … ἐλθόντα*.
- Temporal *Ἐπεί* with *κατεκαύθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *κατακαίω*, with internal augment) renders *poi che … fu combusto*. *τὸ κλεινὸν Ἴλιον* is the neuter nominative subject: *κλεινόν* ‘renowned’ selects the sense of distinction rather than pride in *superbo*.

> 76 Ma tu, perchè ritorni a tanta noia?  

But thou, why returnest thou to such disquiet?

> 76 „Σὺ δὲ πῶς εἰς ἔρημον ὧδε προήχθης;  
> (But you, how have you been brought here to a desert?)

- *Σὺ δέ* keeps the emphatic pronoun and contrast of *Ma tu*. *πῶς* asks ‘how’, rather than literally ‘why’; *προήχθης* (2nd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *προάγω*, with the augment after the prefix) asks how one ‘was brought on’, not why one is *returning*. *εἰς ἔρημον ὧδε* ‘here into a desert’ gives the motion a concrete destination (*ἔρημον* used substantivally after *εἰς*). It recalls the Greek of 64 rather than translating *tanta noia* as a degree of distress.

> 77 perchè non sali il dilettoso monte,  
> 78 ch' è principio e cagion di tutta gioia?”  

why ascendest not the delectable mountain, which is the beginning and the cause of all gladness?”

> 77 „Πῶς οὐκ ἀνέβης εἰς τὸ θυμῆρες ὄρος,  
> (Why did you not ascend the delightful mountain,)
>
> 78 „Τὴν ἀρχὴν αἰτίαν τε χαρμονῆς πάσης;  
> (The beginning and cause of all gladness?")

- *Πῶς οὐκ ἀνέβης* ‘how is it you did not go up?’ again uses *πῶς* for *perchè*, but this time with an active verb: *ἀνέβης* is the 2nd sg. root aor. act. ind. of *ἀναβαίνω*. The aorist asks about a step not taken rather than the present *non sali*. *εἰς* + accusative makes the mountain the goal; poetic *θυμῆρες* (acc. sg. neut. of *θυμήρης*) renders *dilettoso*, ‘pleasing to the heart’.
- *Τὴν ἀρχὴν αἰτίαν τε* replaces relative *ch’ è* and its two predicates with nouns in accusative apposition to *τὸ … ὄρος*: ‘the beginning and cause’. Postpositive *τε* coordinates *αἰτίαν* with *ἀρχήν*. *χαρμονῆς πάσης* is a dependent genitive singular common to both, reversing the order of *tutta gioia*.

> 79 “Or se' tu quel Virgilio, e quella fonte,  
> 80 che spande di parlar sì largo fiume?”  
> 81 risposi lui con vergognosa fronte.  

“Art thou then that Virgil, and that fountain which pours abroad so rich a stream of speech?” I answered him, with bashful front.

> 79 —„Σὺ γοῦν εἶ Βιργίλιος, ἡ πηγὴ ᾽κείνη,  
> (—"Are you then Virgil, that fountain)
>
> 80 „Ἐξ ἧς ἐκρέει ποταμὸς εὐφραδείας;”  
> (From which flows forth a river of eloquence?")
>
> 81 Ἀπεκρίθην μετ᾽ αἰδοῦς κεφαλὴν κλίνας.  
> (I answered, bowing my head with reverence.)

- *Σὺ γοῦν εἶ Βιργίλιος* fronts the emphatic pronoun; *γοῦν* ‘then, at any rate’ makes *Or* inferential. The first *quel* is not separately translated. *ἡ πηγὴ ᾽κείνη* ‘that fountain’ stands in apposition without the Italian *e*; *᾽κείνη* shows prodelision (loss of the initial vowel of *ἐκείνη*, not fusion with the preceding noun). The demonstrative stands outside the article–noun group.
- *Ἐξ ἧς ἐκρέει ποταμὸς εὐφραδείας* changes the syntax of *che spande di parlar sì largo fiume*: the fountain is no longer the subject but the source (*ἐξ* + gen. sg. fem. relative *ἧς*), and *ποταμός* is the subject of *ἐκρέει* (uncontracted 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἐκρέω*), ‘flows out’. *εὐφραδείας* defines what the river consists of, ‘eloquence’ rather than simply ‘speech’; *sì largo* has no separate equivalent.
- *Ἀπεκρίθην* is 1st sg. aor. in passive form of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, active in sense, ‘I answered’; *lui* is not expressed. *μετ᾽ αἰδοῦς* (elided *μετά* with gen. sg. of *αἰδώς*) gives the manner, ‘with diffidence’ or ‘with reverence’. *κεφαλὴν κλίνας* adds a gesture instead of qualifying *fronte*: *κλίνας* is a nom. sg. masc. aor. act. participle of *κλίνω*, ‘having bowed my head’, with the possessor understood.

> 82 “O degli altri poeti onore e lume,  

“O glory, and light of other poets!

> 82 “Ὦ τῶν ἄλλων ποιητῶν δόξα καὶ φέγγος,  
> ("O glory and light of the other poets,)

- *Ὦ … δόξα καὶ φέγγος* closely follows the paired nouns of address (*δόξα*, fem., and *φέγγος*, neut., both voc. sg.). The shared dependent genitive *τῶν ἄλλων ποιητῶν* stands before both, as does *degli altri poeti*; *δόξα* renders *onore* as ‘glory’ or ‘renown’.

> 83 vagliami il lungo studio e il grande amore,  
> 84 che m' ha fatto cercar lo tuo volume.  

May the long zeal avail me, and the great love, that made me search thy volume.

> 83 „Εὔνους εἴης μοι τῷ μακρὸν τὴν σὴν βίβλον  
> (Be gracious to me, who long have studied)
>
> 84 „Μελετήσαντι λίαν τ᾽ ἀγασαμένῳ.  
> (Your book and greatly admired it.)

- *Εὔνους εἴης μοι* ‘may you be well-disposed to me’ recasts *vagliami*: the person addressed, not the study and love, becomes the subject of the wish. *εἴης* is the 2nd sg. pres. optative of *εἰμί*, an independent optative of wish without *ἄν*; *εὔνους* is the contracted predicate adjective, nom. sg. masc., and *μοι* is the dative of the person favoured.
- *τῷ … Μελετήσαντι … ἀγασαμένῳ* expands *μοι* in apposition: ‘to me, the one who studied and admired’. The shared article and both participles are dat. sg. masc.; *μελετήσαντι* is aor. active of *μελετάω*, *ἀγασαμένῳ* aor. middle of *ἄγαμαι*. *μακρόν* ‘for long’ is adverbial with the first; *λίαν* ‘greatly’ modifies the second, and elided postpositive *τ᾽* (*τε*) coordinates them.
- *τὴν σὴν βίβλον* ‘your book’ precedes the participles as their shared object. Study and love are thus actions directed at the book, not the subjects of *m’ ha fatto cercar*: the Italian causative relative clause disappears, and admiration takes the place of *amore*.

> 85 Tu se' lo mio maestro, e il mio autore;  

Thou art my master and my author;

> 85 „Σύ μοι διδάσκαλος εἶ, σύ τ᾽ ἀρχηγέτης,  
> (You are my teacher, you my author,)

- *Σύ μοι διδάσκαλος εἶ, σύ τ᾽ ἀρχηγέτης* repeats *σύ* where the Italian repeats the possessive *mio*. *μοι* is a dative of relation, ‘teacher to me’, with its force carried over to the second predicate; *εἶ* (2nd sg. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*) is understood after the second *σύ*. Elided *τε* joins the predicates. *διδάσκαλος* gives *maestro* directly, but *ἀρχηγέτης* specifies *autore* as an ‘originator’ or ‘leader’.

> 86 tu se' solo colui, da cui io tolsi  
> 87 lo bello stile, che m' ha fatto onore.  

thou alone art he from whom I took the good style that hath done me honour.

> 86 „Σὺ μόνος, ἐξ οὗ τὸ καλλιεπὲς ὕφος  
> (You alone from whom I received the fair)
>
> 87 „Παρέλαβον τὸ τιμὴν ἀπενεγκόν μοι.  
> (Style that has brought me honour.)

- *Σὺ μόνος* leaves the copula and any equivalent of *colui* understood: ‘you alone [are he]’. *ἐξ οὗ* (gen. sg. masc. relative after *ἐκ*) follows *da cui*, while the subject *io* is expressed only by the ending of *Παρέλαβον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *παραλαμβάνω*, with internal augment), ‘I received’.
- *τὸ καλλιεπὲς ὕφος* (acc. sg. neut.) is the object placed before *Παρέλαβον*, rather than after the verb as in *tolsi lo bello stile*. *καλλιεπές*, from *καλλιεπής*, means specifically ‘beautiful in diction’. The repeated article in *τὸ τιμὴν ἀπενεγκόν μοι* marks a further attribute of *ὕφος*, in place of finite *che m’ ha fatto onore*: *ἀπενεγκόν* is the acc. sg. neut. second aor. act. participle of *ἀποφέρω*, ‘which brought’, with *τιμήν* as its object and *μοι* as recipient.

## Lines 88–120 — The Beast and the Other Road

> 88 Vedi la bestia, per cui io mi volsi;  

See the beast from which I turned back;

> 88 „Ἰδὲ τὴν θῆρα τὴν ἐκδιώξασάν με·  
> (Behold the beast that has driven me back;)

- *Ἰδέ* is the 2nd sg. second aorist active imperative of *ὁράω*, from the stem *ἰδ-*; like *Vedi*, it directs the hearer’s attention to an object in the accusative. Musurus treats *θήρ* as feminine throughout: here *τὴν θῆρα*, acc. sg., corresponds to *la bestia*.
- *τὴν ἐκδιώξασάν με* means ‘the one that drove me away’. The repeated article puts the participle in attributive position after its noun. *ἐκδιώξασαν* is the feminine acc. sg. aorist active participle of *ἐκδιώκω*; its additional accent comes before enclitic *με*. In *per cui io mi volsi*, the speaker is the subject of the turning and the beast its cause; in Greek the beast acts directly on the speaker, now the object *με*.

> 89 aiutami da lei, famoso saggio,  

help me from her, thou famous sage;

> 89 „ Βοήθησόν μοι, σοφὲ δεδοξασμένε·  
> (Help me, renowned sage,)

- *Βοήθησόν μοι* has an aorist active imperative, 2nd sg. of *βοηθέω*, with the dative of the person helped, not an accusative as in *aiutami*. The final accent of *Βοήθησόν* is additional before enclitic *μοι*. There is no separate rendering of *da lei*.
- *σοφὲ δεδοξασμένε* reverses *famoso saggio*: both words are masculine vocative singular. *σοφέ* is the adjective *σοφός* used as a noun; *δεδοξασμένε*, perfect middle-passive participle of *δοξάζω*, is passive in sense, ‘held in honour’. Its perfect expresses an attained standing rather than an act of praise.

> 90 ch'ella mi fa tremar le vene e i polsi."  

for she makes my veins and pulses tremble."

> 90 „ Ἐμποιεῖ γάρ μοι φλεψὶ καὶ σφυγμοῖς τρόμον.“ —  
> (For she makes my veins and pulses tremble."—)

- Postpositive *γάρ* renders causal *ch’*. *Ἐμποιεῖ* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *ἐμποιέω*) takes *τρόμον*, ‘trembling’, as its direct object: instead of the causative *fa tremar* with infinitive, the Greek says ‘produces trembling’.
- *μοι* marks the person affected and supplies the possessive sense of *mi*; *φλεψὶ καὶ σφυγμοῖς* specifies where the trembling is produced. These are dative plurals of *φλέψ* and *σφυγμός*, without the articles of *le vene e i polsi*.

> 91 "A te convien tenere altro viaggio,"  

"Thou must take another road,"

> 91 — “Ὁδὸν ἑτέραν τραπῆναί σε προσήκει,”  
> (—"It behoves you to turn to another road,"—)

- *Ὁδὸν ἑτέραν* ‘another road’ comes first, whereas *altro viaggio* comes last. *ὁδόν* is an accusative of the way taken with *τραπῆναι*, the second aorist passive infinitive of *τρέπω*, intransitive in sense, ‘to turn aside’.
- Impersonal *προσήκει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *προσήκω*) answers to *convien*. Where Italian has *a te* and an infinitive with understood subject, Greek makes *σε* the explicit accusative subject of *τραπῆναι*: ‘it befits you to turn’.

> 92 rispose, poi che lagrimar mi vide,  

he answered, when he saw me weeping,

> 92 Ἀπεκρίνατο θεωρῶν με θρηνοῦντα,  
> (He answered, seeing me weeping,—)

- *Ἀπεκρίνατο* is the 3rd sg. aorist middle indicative of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, ‘answer’, with the augment after the preverb. It corresponds directly to *rispose*.
- *θεωρῶν με θρηνοῦντα* replaces *poi che lagrimar mi vide* with two present active participles. *θεωρῶν* (nom. masc. sg. of *θεωρέω*) agrees with the person answering; *θρηνοῦντα* (acc. masc. sg. of *θρηνέω*) agrees with *με* and completes the verb of perception: ‘seeing me lament’. The first participle presents the seeing as attendant on the answer rather than explicitly anterior to it; the second replaces the Italian infinitive.

> 93 "se vuoi campar d'esto loco selvaggio:  

"if thou desirest to escape from this wild place:

> 93 “Ἵν᾽ ἐξέλθῃς τῆς χθονὸς ἀγρίας τῆσδε.  
> ("If you would escape from this savage land.)

- *Ἵν᾽ ἐξέλθῃς* makes *se vuoi campar* a purpose clause, ‘in order that you may get out’. *ἵν᾽* is *ἵνα* with final vowel elided; *ἐξέλθῃς* is the 2nd sg. second aorist active subjunctive of *ἐξέρχομαι*. There is no verb of wishing and no conditional protasis.
- *τῆς χθονὸς ἀγρίας τῆσδε* is a genitive of separation with the compound verb. Poetic *χθών*, ‘land, earth’, replaces *loco*. The adjective *ἀγρίας* and the demonstrative *τῆσδε* (gen. fem. sg. of *ὅδε*) agree with *χθονός*; the demonstrative corresponding to *esto* stands last, outside the article–noun group.

> 94 chè questa bestia, per la qual tu gride,  
> 95 non lascia altrui passar per la sua via,  
> 96 ma tanto lo impedisce, che l' uccide;  

because this beast, for which thou criest, lets not men pass her way; but so entangles that she slays them;

> 94 „ Ἡ γὰρ θὴρ αὕτη, δι᾽ ἣν κράζεις, τοὺς ἄλλους  
> (For this beast, on account of which you cry out, does not)
>
> 95 „ Οὐκ ἐᾷ τὴν ἑαυτῆς διελθεῖν τρίβον,  
> (Permit others to pass along her path,)
>
> 96 „ Ἀλλ᾽ ἀπείργει μέχρις ἂν αὐτοὺς φονεύσῃ·  
> (But hinders them until she slays them;)

- *Ἡ γὰρ θὴρ αὕτη* gives *chè questa bestia* as an independent explanation with postpositive *γάρ*. *αὕτη* stands outside the articular noun, the normal position of a demonstrative. *δι᾽ ἣν κράζεις* closely follows *per la qual tu gride*: elided *διά* with the feminine accusative relative pronoun expresses cause; *κράζεις* is 2nd sg. present active indicative of *κράζω*.
- *τοὺς ἄλλους* ‘the others’ makes indefinite *altrui* explicitly plural. It is placed before *οὐκ ἐᾷ* and serves as the accusative subject of *διελθεῖν* after that verb of permission. *ἐᾷ* is the contracted 3rd sg. present active indicative of *ἐάω*; *διελθεῖν* is the second aorist active infinitive of *διέρχομαι*. The path traversed is accusative, *τὴν ἑαυτῆς ... τρίβον*, not a prepositional phrase as in *per la sua via*. The feminine reflexive genitive *ἑαυτῆς* refers possession back to the beast.
- *Ἀλλ᾽ ἀπείργει* ‘but keeps out’ leaves its object understood. Instead of the degree and result construction *tanto ... che*, *μέχρις ἂν αὐτοὺς φονεύσῃ* gives a temporal limit, ‘until she kills them’. *ἄν* with the aorist active subjunctive *φονεύσῃ* (3rd sg. of *φονεύω*) marks the prospective endpoint; plural *αὐτούς* resumes *τοὺς ἄλλους* in place of singular *l’*.

> 97 ed ha natura sì malvagia e ria,  
> 98 che mai non empie la bramosa voglia,  
> 99 e dopo il pasto ha più fame che pria.  

and has a nature so perverse and vicious, that she never satiates her craving appetite; and after feeding, she is hungrier than before.

> 97 „ Οὕτω τε κακοποιὸν κέκτηται φύσιν,  
> (And so malignant a nature does she possess)
>
> 98 „ Ὥστ᾽ οὐδὲν αὐτῆς ἀπλήστους πληροῖ πόθους,  
> (That nothing fills her insatiable desires,)
>
> 99 „ Καὶ κορεσθεῖσα πλέον ἢ πρὶν λιμώττει.  
> (And once sated she hungers more than before.)

- *Οὕτω τε ... Ὥστ᾽* preserves the degree and result relation of *sì ... che*; connective *τε* supplies *ed*. *κακοποιόν* ‘harm-doing’ combines *malvagia e ria* in one adjective, feminine acc. sg. of two-termination *κακοποιός*, agreeing with *φύσιν*. *κέκτηται*, 3rd sg. perfect middle indicative of *κτάομαι*, denotes present possession as the result of acquisition and so renders present *ha*.
- After elided *ὥστε*, the indicative *πληροῖ* (contracted 3rd sg. present active of *πληρόω*) asserts the result as a fact. *οὐδέν* ‘nothing’ is its subject, replacing temporal *mai* and changing the agent: not ‘she never fills’ but ‘nothing fills’. *αὐτῆς ἀπλήστους ... πόθους* makes *la bramosa voglia* plural, ‘her insatiable desires’; *αὐτῆς* is possessive genitive, *πόθους* the accusative object.
- *κορεσθεῖσα*, nom. fem. sg. aorist passive participle of *κορέννυμι*, agrees with the beast: ‘after being sated’ says more than *dopo il pasto*. *λιμώττει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *λιμώττω*, with Attic *-ττ-* for *-σσ-*) substitutes a verb for *ha ... fame*; adverbial *πλέον ἢ πρίν* closely answers to *più ... che pria*.

> 100 Molti son gli animali, a cui s' ammoglia,  
> 101 e più saranno ancora, infin che il Veltro  
> 102 verrà, che la farà morir con doglia.  

The animals to which she weds herself are many; and will yet be more, until the Greyhound comes, that will make her die with pain.

> 100 „ Τὰ ζῶα δ᾽ ἐστίν, οἷς συμμίγνυται, πλεῖστα,  
> (Very many are the animals with which she mates,)
>
> 101 „ Καὶ πλείον᾽ ἔσται, μέχρις ὁ Λυκοθήρας  
> (And there shall be more, until the Wolf-hunter)
>
> 102 „ Ἐλθὼν ἐν δειναῖς ὀδύναις αὐτὴν κτείνῃ.  
> (Comes and slays her in grievous torments.)

- *Τὰ ζῶα δ᾽ ἐστίν ... πλεῖστα* puts the subject first and reserves the predicate adjective until after the relative clause, reversing the emphasis of *Molti son gli animali*. Elided *δέ* adds a connective. The neuter plural subject takes a singular verb, *ἐστίν*, in ordinary Attic usage; *πλεῖστα*, superlative of *πολύς*, has the absolute sense ‘very many’.
- *οἷς συμμίγνυται* retains the relative construction of *a cui s’ammoglia*, but the dative plural marks association, ‘with which’. *συμμίγνυται* is 3rd sg. present middle-passive indicative of *συμμίγνυμι*, here middle, ‘mates’; it does not reproduce the specifically marital verb. *πλείον᾽ ἔσται* has elided *πλείονα*, neuter plural comparative, and again a singular verb, the future of *εἰμί*. There is no separate *ancora*.
- *μέχρις ... κτείνῃ* makes the killing itself the limit, rather than the coming as in *infin che ... verrà*. *ὁ Λυκοθήρας* interprets *il Veltro* by function, ‘the wolf-hunter’. *Ἐλθών*, nom. masc. sg. second aorist active participle of *ἔρχομαι*, subordinates the coming: ‘having come’. *κτείνῃ*, 3rd sg. aorist active subjunctive of *κτείνω*, replaces causative *la farà morir* with transitive ‘kills her’. The prospective subjunctive here follows *μέχρις* without the *ἄν* of line 96. *ἐν δειναῖς ὀδύναις*, ‘in grievous pains’, expands singular *con doglia*.

> 103 Questi non ciberà terra nè peltro,  
> 104 ma sapienza, e amore, e virtute;  

He will not feed on land or pelf, but on wisdom, and love, and manfulness;

> 103 „ Οὗτος οὐ ζήσετ᾽ ἐν γαίαις καὶ χρυσίῳ,  
> (He shall not live on lands and gold,)
>
> 104 „ Ἀλλ᾽ ἐν ἀρετῇ καὶ στοργῇ καὶ σοφίᾳ.  
> (But on virtue and affection and wisdom.)

- *Οὗτος* reproduces demonstrative *Questi*. *ζήσετ᾽* is elided *ζήσεται*, 3rd sg. future middle indicative of *ζάω*, active in meaning. ‘Will live’ replaces *non ciberà*’s verb of feeding; *ἐν* with datives suggests what sustains him rather than giving food as a direct object.
- *οὐ ... ἐν γαίαις καὶ χρυσίῳ* has simple *καί* within the scope of the negative rather than a second negative like *nè*. *γαίαις* makes *terra* plural, ‘lands’; *χρυσίῳ* specifies ‘gold’ for *peltro*. *Ἀλλ᾽ ἐν* repeats the preposition after elided *ἀλλά*. Of the three datives *ἀρετῇ καὶ στοργῇ καὶ σοφίᾳ*, the first and last reverse the positions of *sapienza* and *virtute*; *στοργή* gives *amore* the more particular sense ‘affection’.

> 105 e sua nazion sarà tra Feltro e Feltro.  

and his nation shall be between Feltro and Feltro.

> 105 „ Γενετὴν δ᾽ ἕξει μεταξὺ δύο Φέλτρων,  
> (He shall have his birth between two Feltros,)

- *Γενετὴν δ᾽ ἕξει* interprets *sua nazion* as ‘birth’. The Italian possessive subject and copula become an accusative object and a personal verb, ‘he will have his birth’; *ἕξει* is 3rd sg. future active indicative of *ἔχω*. Elided postpositive *δέ* connects the clause.
- *μεταξὺ δύο Φέλτρων* condenses *tra Feltro e Feltro* to ‘between two Feltros’. *μεταξύ* governs the genitive; *δύο* is undeclined here and *Φέλτρων* is genitive plural, not dual.

> 106 Di quell' umile Italia fia salute,  
> 107 per cui morì la vergine Cammilla,  
> 108 Eurialo, e Turno, e Niso di ferute;  

He shall be the salvation of that low Italy, for which Camilla the virgin, Euryalus, and Turnus, and Nisus, died of wounds;

> 106 „ Καὶ σωτὴρ ἔσται ταπεινῆς Ἰταλίας,  
> (And shall be the saviour of humble Italy,)
>
> 107 „ Ὑπὲρ ἧς ἀπέθανε κόρη Καμίλλα,  
> (For whom died the maiden Camilla,)
>
> 108 „ Εὐρύαλος καὶ Τύρνος Νῖσός τ᾽ ἐν μάχαις.  
> (Euryalus and Turnus and Nisus in battles.)

- *Καὶ σωτὴρ ἔσται ταπεινῆς Ἰταλίας* adds an initial connective and fronts *σωτήρ*, a personal predicate nominative, ‘saviour’, for abstract *salute*. *ἔσται* is the 3rd sg. future of *εἰμί*, like *fia* in tense. *ταπεινῆς Ἰταλίας* depends on *σωτήρ*; it retains *umile* but not demonstrative *quell’*.
- *Ὑπὲρ ἧς* (gen. fem. sg. relative) specifies ‘on behalf of whom’ for *per cui*. *ἀπέθανε*, 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *ἀποθνῄσκω*, has the augment inside the compound. Like *morì*, it stands in the singular before the list of subjects and agrees with the first, *κόρη Καμίλλα*. *κόρη*, ‘maiden’, is a noun in apposition, without the article of *la vergine*.
- *Εὐρύαλος καὶ Τύρνος Νῖσός τ᾽* continues the nominatives; *τ᾽* is enclitic *τε* elided before *ἐν*, accounting for the additional accent on *Νῖσός*. *ἐν μάχαις*, ‘in battles’, substitutes the circumstances of dying for the cause *di ferute*.

> 109 questi la caccerà per ogni villa,  
> 110 fin che l' avrà rimessa nell' Inferno,  
> 111 là onde invidia prima dipartilla.  

he shall chase her through every city, till he have put her into Hell again; from which envy first set her loose.

> 109 „Οὗτος οὖν ἐκ πόλεως πάσης διώξει  
> (He then shall chase her from every city)
>
> 110 „Αὐτήν, ἕως ἂν εἰς ᾍδην ἀποπέμψῃ,  
> (Until he sends her back to Hell,)
>
> 111 „Ἔνθεν τὸ πρῶτον ἐξήγαγεν ὁ φθόνος.  
> (Whence envy first brought her forth.)

- *Οὗτος οὖν* resumes *questi* and adds inferential *οὖν*, ‘then’. *διώξει* (3rd sg. future active indicative of *διώκω*) postpones its object *αὐτήν* to the next line. *ἐκ πόλεως πάσης*, ‘out of every city’, gives *per ogni villa* a direction of expulsion rather than passage through.
- *ἕως ἂν ... ἀποπέμψῃ* is a prospective temporal clause with *ἄν* and the 3rd sg. aorist active subjunctive of *ἀποπέμπω*. Where *fin che l’avrà rimessa* has a future perfect, the Greek aorist views the sending as a single completed act at the limit. *εἰς ᾍδην* gives the destination in the accusative; *ἀποπέμπω* expresses sending away or back without a separate word for ‘again’.
- Relative adverb *ἔνθεν*, ‘whence’, combines *là onde*. Adverbial *τὸ πρῶτον* renders *prima*. *ἐξήγαγεν*, 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *ἐξάγω*, has the reduplicated aorist stem of *ἄγω* and internal augment: ‘brought out’, rather than ‘sent away’ as in *dipartilla*. Its object is understood, where Italian has enclitic *-la*; the articular subject *ὁ φθόνος* follows the verb.

> 112 Ond' io per lo tuo me' penso e discerno,  
> 113 che tu mi segui, ed io sarò tua guida,  
> 114 e trarrotti di qui per luogo eterno,  

Wherefore I think and discern *this* for thy best, that thou follow me; and I will be thy guide, and lead thee hence through an eternal place,

> 112 „Ὅθεν ἐπ’ ἀγαθῷ σοι φρονῶ καὶ κρίνω,  
> (Therefore for your good I think and judge)
>
> 113 „Ὅπως ἕπῃ μοι, κἀγὼ δέ σ’ ὁδηγήσω,  
> (That you should follow me, and I will guide you,)
>
> 114 „Ἄξω τ’ ἐντεῦθεν δι’ αἰωνίου χώρας,  
> (And lead you hence through an eternal realm,)

- *Ὅθεν*, literally ‘whence’, has the inferential force of *Ond’*. *ἐπ’ ἀγαθῷ σοι* (elided *ἐπί* with dative) means ‘for good to you’, without the comparative force of *per lo tuo me’*. *φρονῶ καὶ κρίνω*, both 1st sg. present active indicatives, retain the paired *penso e discerno*; the endings suffice without *io*.
- *Ὅπως ἕπῃ μοι* gives the course judged desirable, ‘that you should follow me’. *ἕπῃ* is 2nd sg. present middle subjunctive of deponent *ἕπομαι*, which takes a dative, here enclitic *μοι*, rather than the direct object of *mi segui*. *κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*, preserving the explicit change of subject in *ed io*. With transitional *δέ*, *σ’ ὁδηγήσω* (elided accusative *σε*; 1st sg. future active of *ὁδηγέω*) turns *sarò tua guida* into a transitive verb, ‘I shall guide you’.
- *Ἄξω τ’ ἐντεῦθεν*, ‘and I shall lead from here’, retains the future (*ἄξω*, from *ἄγω*) and renders *di qui* by one adverb. Elided postpositive *τε* joins the verbs; the object of *trarrotti* is carried over from *σ’*. *δι’ αἰωνίου χώρας* uses elided *διά* with the genitive for passage through, *per luogo eterno*; *αἰωνίου* is feminine gen. sg. of two-termination *αἰώνιος*, agreeing with *χώρας*.

> 115 ove udirai le disperate strida,  
> 116 vedrai gli antichi spiriti dolenti,  
> 117 che la seconda morte ciascun grida;  

where thou shalt hear the hopeless shrieks, shalt see the ancient spirits in pain, so that each calls for a second death;

> 115 „Ἔνθ’ ὀλολυγμοὺς ἀνέλπιδας ἀκούσῃ·  
> (Where you shall hear despairing wails;)
>
> 116 „Ὄψει τ’ ἀρχαῖα πνεύματα τετληκότα,  
> (And you shall see ancient spirits in suffering,)
>
> 117 „Τὸν δεύτερον θάνατον κραυγαῖς καλοῦντα·  
> (Calling with cries upon the second death;)

- *Ἔνθ’* is *ἔνθα*, ‘where’, elided before the rough breathing of *ὀλολυγμούς*, with consequent aspiration of *τ* to *θ*. *ὀλολυγμοὺς ἀνέλπιδας*, ‘hopeless wails’, places the adjective after its noun, both masculine acc. pl., and the whole object before *ἀκούσῃ*. The latter is 2nd sg. future middle indicative of *ἀκούω*, active in sense, answering to *udirai*.
- *Ὄψει* is likewise a 2nd sg. future middle indicative, the suppletive future of *ὁράω*, ‘you will see’; elided *τε* links it to the hearing. *ἀρχαῖα πνεύματα τετληκότα* keeps the two modifiers of *gli antichi spiriti dolenti* on either side of the noun. *τετληκότα* is the neuter acc. pl. perfect active participle of *τλάω*, ‘endure’: the perfect presents suffering as an enduring condition.
- *Τὸν δεύτερον θάνατον κραυγαῖς καλοῦντα* continues with a second participle agreeing with *πνεύματα*: *καλοῦντα* is neuter acc. pl. present active of *καλέω*. Musurus thus takes *la seconda morte* as the object called for, fronted as in Italian. He distributes *grida* between a verb of calling and instrumental *κραυγαῖς*, ‘with cries’, but does not reproduce distributive *ciascun* or give *che* a separate finite clause.

> 118 e poi vedrai color, che son contenti  
> 119 nel fuoco, perchè speran di venire,  
> 120 quando che sia, alle beate genti;  

and [then] thou shalt see those who are contented in the fire: for they hope to come, whensoever it be, amongst the blessed;

> 118 „Ὄψει τ’ ἐκείνους, οἵτινες εὐαρέστως  
> (And you shall see those who contentedly)
>
> 119 „Ἐν τῷ πυρὶ μένουσιν, ὡς προσδοκῶντες  
> (Remain in the fire, because they hope)
>
> 120 „Εἰς τὸν χῶρόν ποτ’ ἀνελθεῖν τῶν μακάρων.  
> (One day to ascend to the place of the blessed.)

- *Ὄψει τ’ ἐκείνους* repeats the preceding future and connective, with masculine acc. pl. demonstrative *ἐκείνους* for *color*; there is no separate adverb for *poi*. The relative *οἵτινες* (nom. masc. pl. of *ὅστις*) characterizes those indicated. Musurus turns the predicate *son contenti* into the adverb *εὐαρέστως*, ‘contentedly’, and supplies a verb for it to modify, *μένουσιν* (3rd pl. present active indicative of *μένω*): ‘remain in the fire’.
- *ὡς προσδοκῶντες* replaces finite causal *perchè speran*. *προσδοκῶντες* is the nom. masc. pl. present active participle of *προσδοκάω*, ‘expect’, agreeing with the subject of *μένουσιν*; *ὡς* presents the expectation as their own reason for remaining.
- *ποτ’ ἀνελθεῖν* has elided indefinite *ποτε*, ‘at some time’, for the clause *quando che sia*. *ἀνελθεῖν*, second aorist active infinitive of *ἀνέρχομαι*, completes *προσδοκῶντες* and adds upward direction to *venire*. Where *alle beate genti* makes people the goal, *εἰς τὸν χῶρον ... τῶν μακάρων* supplies a place as the goal: ‘to the place of the blessed’. *τῶν μακάρων* is the substantival genitive plural of *μάκαρ*, dependent on *χῶρον*.

## Lines 121–136 — A Worthier Guide and the Poet’s Plea

> 121 alle qua' poi se tu vorrai salire,  
> 122 anima fia a ciò di me più degna;  

then to these, if thou desirest to ascend, there shall be a spirit worthier than I to guide thee;

> 121 „Πρὸς τούτους ἔπειτ’ εἰ θέλεις ἀναβῆναι,  
> (If thereafter you wish to ascend to these,)
>
> 122 „Ἔστιν ἐμοῦ ψυχή τις ἀξιωτέρα,  
> (There is a soul worthier than I,)

- *Πρὸς τούτους ἔπειτ’* ‘to these, then’ keeps the destination at the front, as in *alle qua’ poi*. The Italian feminine relative *qua’* looks back to *genti*; the Greek masculine accusative plural demonstrative *τούτους* points to the persons rather than continuing the relative construction. *πρός* with the accusative marks the goal; *ἔπειτ’* is *ἔπειτα* with its final vowel elided before *εἰ*.
- *εἰ θέλεις ἀναβῆναι* renders *se tu vorrai salire*. Musurus uses an open condition with the present indicative *θέλεις* (2nd sg. of *θέλω*) rather than the Italian future; the personal ending suffices for *tu*. *ἀναβῆναι* is the intransitive root-aorist active infinitive of *ἀναβαίνω*: after a verb of wishing, the aorist presents the ascent as a whole, not as past.
- *Ἔστιν ἐμοῦ ψυχή τις ἀξιωτέρα* ‘there is a soul worthier than I’ replaces future *fia* with existential present *ἔστιν*, accented on the first syllable in this use. *ψυχή τις* is the nominative subject, with enclitic *τις* making the indefinite sense explicit. *ἀξιωτέρα*, nominative feminine singular comparative of *ἄξιος*, agrees with *ψυχή*; the genitive of comparison *ἐμοῦ* renders *di me* and precedes both the noun and the comparative. Nothing expressly renders *a ciò*: the respect in which the soul is worthier, made explicit by the English “to guide thee,” is left to be understood.

> 123 con lei ti lascerò nel mio partire:  

with her will I leave thee at my parting:

> 123 „Μεθ’ ἧς καταλείψω σε πρὶν ἐκχωρήσω.  
> (With whom I will leave you before I depart.)

- *Μεθ’ ἧς καταλείψω σε* turns the independent *con lei ti lascerò* into a relative clause, ‘with whom I shall leave you’. *ἧς* is genitive feminine singular after *μετά*, with *ψυχή* as antecedent; elision before the rough breathing changes final *τ* to *θ* in *μεθ’*. *καταλείψω* is the 1st sg. future active indicative of *καταλείπω*, with enclitic accusative *σε* following rather than preceding the verb.
- *πρὶν ἐκχωρήσω* ‘before I depart’ recasts *nel mio partire*: the Italian preposition and possessive with a substantivized infinitive mark the occasion, whereas *πρίν* introduces a finite clause and marks priority. *ἐκχωρήσω* (from *ἐκχωρέω*) is identical in form in the future active indicative and the 1st sg. aorist active subjunctive. The sense is prospective; after an affirmative main clause with the same subject, ordinary Attic would more naturally use *πρίν* with an infinitive.

> 124 chè quello Imperador, che lassù regna,  
> 125 perch' io fui ribellante alla sua legge,  
> 126 non vuol che in sua città per me si vegna.  

for that Emperor who reigns above, because I was rebellious to his law, wills not that I come into his city.

> 124 „Αὐτοκράτωρ γάρ, ὃς ὕπερθεν ἀνάσσει,  
> (For the Emperor who reigns above,)
>
> 125 „Ὡς τῶν ἑαυτοῦ νόμων ἀφεστηκότα,  
> (Since I stood apart from his laws,)
>
> 126 „Οὐ θέλει μ’ ἐλθεῖν εἰς τὴν ἑαυτοῦ πόλιν.  
> (Does not wish me to enter into his city.)

- *Αὐτοκράτωρ γάρ* renders *chè quello Imperador*: postpositive explanatory *γάρ* follows the nominative title, which stands without article or demonstrative. *ὃς ὕπερθεν ἀνάσσει* preserves the relative clause *che lassù regna*. *ὅς* is its nominative subject; poetic *ὕπερθεν* ‘on high’ modifies *ἀνάσσει*, 3rd sg. present active indicative of the epic and poetic *ἀνάσσω* ‘be lord, reign’.
- *Ὡς τῶν ἑαυτοῦ νόμων ἀφεστηκότα* replaces the finite *perch’ io fui ribellante alla sua legge* with *ὡς* and a causal participle, giving the reason as the ruler regards it. *ἀφεστηκότα* is accusative masculine singular of the intransitive perfect active participle of *ἀφίστημι*: ‘as one who has stood apart’. The perfect stresses the resulting estrangement. The participle agrees in advance with *μ’* in the next line. *τῶν … νόμων* is a genitive of separation, plural for Italian singular *legge*; reflexive *ἑαυτοῦ* refers to the ruler, not the speaker.
- *Οὐ θέλει μ’ ἐλθεῖν* ‘he does not wish me to come’ construes *per me si vegna* personally: instead of an impersonal *si* with *per me* ‘by my agency’, the speaker himself becomes the subject of coming. *οὐ* negates indicative *θέλει*; *μ’* is elided *με*, accusative subject of *ἐλθεῖν*, the second-aorist active infinitive of *ἔρχομαι*. *εἰς τὴν ἑαυτοῦ πόλιν* expresses motion into the city with *εἰς* and the accusative; again *ἑαυτοῦ* assigns possession to the subject of *θέλει*.

> 127 In tutte parti impera, e quivi regge,  
> 128 quivi è la sua città, e l' alto seggio:  

In all parts he rules and there [holds sway]; there is his city, and his high seat:

> 127 „Οὗτος πάντων βασιλεύς, Οὐρανῶν ἄναξ,  
> (He is king of all, lord of the Heavens,)
>
> 128 „Ἔνθ’ ἡ πόλις ἔστ’ αὐτοῦ καὶ θεῖος θρόνος.  
> (Where his city is and his divine throne.)

- *Οὗτος πάντων βασιλεύς, Οὐρανῶν ἄναξ* ‘he is king of all, lord of the heavens’ replaces the two finite verbs *impera* and *regge* with parallel predicate nominatives and an understood copula. *οὗτος* supplies an emphatic subject; *πάντων* depends on *βασιλεύς* and expresses the extent of dominion rather than place, as in *in tutte parti*. The second title follows without a conjunction: poetic *ἄναξ* takes genitive plural *Οὐρανῶν*, naming what *quivi* had only indicated.
- *Ἔνθ’ ἡ πόλις ἔστ’ αὐτοῦ καὶ θεῖος θρόνος* ‘where his city is, and a divine throne’ makes the second *quivi* a local link back to *Οὐρανῶν*. *ἔνθ’* is elided *ἔνθα*; *ἔστ’* is *ἔστι* elided before *αὐτοῦ*. The possessive genitive *αὐτοῦ* follows the verb, rather than standing next to *πόλις*. The singular verb accompanies the first nominative, with *θεῖος θρόνος* added by *καί*; anarthrous *θεῖος* ‘divine’ replaces *alto*, and *θρόνος* specifies the kind of *seggio*.

> 129 o felice colui, cui ivi elegge!"  

o happy whom he chooses for it!”

> 129 „Ὤ, μάκαρ ἐκεῖνος, ὃν ἐκεῖσ’ ἐκλέξῃ!”  
> (Oh, blessed is he whom he chooses thither!")

- *Ὤ, μάκαρ ἐκεῖνος* ‘oh, blessed is that man’ renders *o felice colui* without a copula. *μάκαρ* is a poetic adjective; despite the introductory exclamation, *ἐκεῖνος* is nominative, the subject of the implied predication, not a person addressed.
- *ὃν ἐκεῖσ’ ἐκλέξῃ* renders *cui ivi elegge*. *ὅν* is accusative masculine singular, the direct object; *ἐκεῖσ’*, elided *ἐκεῖσε*, means ‘thither’, giving *ivi* directional force. *ἐκλέξῃ* is 3rd sg. aorist middle subjunctive of *ἐκλέγω*, the middle meaning ‘choose for oneself’. The subjunctive gives the relative a prospective or indefinite sense, rather than reproducing the present indicative *elegge*; an indefinite relative of this kind in Attic prose would normally have *ἄν*.

> 130 Ed io a lui: "Poeta, io ti richieggio  
> 131 per quello Dio, che tu non conoscesti,  
> 132 acciocch' io fugga questo male e peggio,  

And I to him: “Poet, I beseech thee by that God whom thou knowest not: in order that I may escape this ill and worse,

> 130 Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν· “Ποιητά, δέομαί σου,  
> (And I to him: "Poet, I beseech you,)
>
> 131 „Πρὸς Θεοῦ σ’ ἐκλιπαρῶν, ὅνπερ οὐκ ἔγνως,  
> (Imploring you by God whom you did not know,)
>
> 132 „Φυγεῖν με τὸ κακὸν τόδ’ ἢ τούτου χεῖρον.  
> (That I may flee this evil or worse than this.)

- *Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν* reproduces *Ed io a lui*, again without a verb of speaking. *κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*; *πρός* with the accusative marks the person addressed. *Ποιητά* is the vocative singular of *ποιητής*. *δέομαί σου* renders *io ti richieggio* with the 1st sg. present middle indicative of *δέομαι*, which takes the person entreated in the genitive. Enclitic *σου* accounts for the additional accent on the verb.
- *Πρὸς Θεοῦ σ’ ἐκλιπαρῶν* ‘imploring you by God’ expands *per quello Dio*. Here *πρός* takes the genitive in an adjuration; there is no equivalent of *quello*. Musurus repeats the act of asking in *ἐκλιπαρῶν*, nominative masculine singular present active participle of *ἐκλιπαρέω*, agreeing with the speaker. Unlike *δέομαι*, it takes an accusative of the person, *σ’* for elided *σε*.
- *ὅνπερ οὐκ ἔγνως* retains the past of *che tu non conoscesti*, rather than the present of the English “whom thou knowest not.” *ὅνπερ* is the accusative relative with emphasizing particle *-περ*; *οὐκ* is the form of the negative before a smooth vowel. *ἔγνως* is the augmented 2nd sg. root-aorist active indicative of *γιγνώσκω*.
- *Φυγεῖν με* ‘for me to escape’ replaces the purpose clause *acciocch’ io fugga* with an accusative and infinitive expressing the desired outcome of the entreaty. *φυγεῖν* is the second-aorist active infinitive of *φεύγω*; *με* is its expressed subject. Its object *τὸ κακὸν τόδ’* has the demonstrative in the usual position outside the article–noun group (*τόδ’* = *τόδε*). *ἢ τούτου χεῖρον* gives ‘or something worse than this’ for *e peggio*: disjunction replaces addition, and the substantival neuter singular comparative *χεῖρον* has an explicit genitive of comparison, *τούτου*.

> 133 che tu mi meni là dov' or dicesti,  

lead me where thou now hast said,

> 133 „Ὁδήγησόν με λοιπὸν ὅπου νῦν εἶπας,  
> (Guide me then whither you have now spoken,)

- *Ὁδήγησόν με* ‘guide me’ makes *che tu mi meni*, the subjunctive clause specifying the request, a direct command. *ὁδήγησον* is the 2nd sg. aorist active imperative of *ὁδηγέω*, with an additional accent before enclitic *με*. *λοιπόν*, the neuter singular of *λοιπός* used adverbially, adds the transition ‘then’.
- *ὅπου νῦν εἶπας* closely follows *là dov’ or dicesti*, but dispenses with a separate word for *là*. The relative adverb *ὅπου* is literally ‘where’; the governing verb of guidance supplies the sense ‘whither’. *νῦν* renders *or*; *εἶπας* is the 2nd sg. aorist active indicative used with *λέγω*, with the analogical ending *-ας* beside *εἶπες*.

> 134 sì ch' io vegga la porta di San Pietro,  
> 135 e color cui tu fai cotanto mesti."  

so that I may see the Gate of St Peter, and those whom thou makest so sad.”

> 134 „Ὅπως ἴδω τὴν θύραν ἁγίου Πέτρου,  
> (That I may see the gate of Saint Peter,)
>
> 135 „Κἀκείνους, οὕς μοι λέγεις οὕτω δυστήνους.”  
> (And those whom you tell me are so wretched.")

- *Ὅπως ἴδω* gives *sì ch’ io vegga* a final construction, ‘in order that I may see’. *ἴδω* is the 1st sg. second-aorist active subjunctive belonging to *ὁράω*, without the augment of indicative *εἶδον*. *τὴν θύραν* is its accusative object; *ἁγίου Πέτρου* renders *di San Pietro* by a possessive genitive, the adjective agreeing with the name.
- *Κἀκείνους* is crasis of *καὶ ἐκείνους*; the masculine accusative plural demonstrative renders *color* as a second object of *ἴδω*. *οὕς μοι λέγεις οὕτω δυστήνους* interprets *cui tu fai cotanto mesti* as description, not causation: ‘whom you tell me to be so wretched’, rather than “whom thou makest so sad.” *λέγεις* is 2nd sg. present active indicative; dative *μοι* adds the recipient of the description. *δυστήνους* is predicative, agreeing with accusative plural *οὕς*, with ‘to be’ understood; adverbial *οὕτω* marks degree as does *cotanto*.

> 136 Allor si mosse; ed io li tenni retro.  

Then he moved; and I kept on behind him.

> 136 Τότ’ αὐτὸς ἐκίνησεν, ἐγὼ δ’ εἱπόμην.  
> (Then he set out, and I followed.)

- *Τότ’ αὐτὸς ἐκίνησεν* renders *Allor si mosse*. *τότ’* is elided *τότε*; nominative *αὐτός* singles out the first subject in contrast with the following *ἐγώ*. *ἐκίνησεν* is the augmented 3rd sg. aorist active indicative of *κινέω*, with movable *-ν*. It is used without an object in the sense ‘set out’, where the Italian uses a reflexive and Greek could have used a middle.
- *ἐγὼ δ’ εἱπόμην* ‘and I was following’ replaces *ed io li tenni retro* with a single verb of following. Postpositive *δ’* is *δέ* elided before the vowel, marking the change of subject. *εἱπόμην* is the 1st sg. imperfect middle indicative of the deponent *ἕπομαι*, with the augmented stem *εἱπ-* and its rough breathing. The imperfect presents the following as continuing, against the aorist of setting out; the person followed, normally expressed by a dative and explicit in *li*, is understood.

## Summary — From Clause to Participle

Musurus often keeps Dante’s order of images while changing which action receives a finite verb. At 16, *guardai in alto, e vidi* becomes *ὑψώσας ὄμματ’ εἶδον*, ‘having raised my eyes, I saw’: the aorist participle presents the gesture as preliminary to the seeing. At 25–26 the shift goes the other way. The finite *si volse* becomes the present participle *στρέφουσ’*, ‘turning’, while the infinitive *rimirar* becomes the imperfect *ἐθεώρει*, ‘was looking’. Participles also take over the work of relative clauses. The repeated article in *τὸ τιμὴν ἀπενεγκόν μοι* (87), ‘the one that brought me honour’, attaches the phrase to *ὕφος* just as *che m’ ha fatto onore* qualifies *stile*. Here the change of form leaves the relation between the words intact.

Small particles often determine how closely the Greek follows the Italian syntax. Postpositive *δέ* and enclitic *τε* regularly carry the connective force of *e*, while *οὕτω … ὥστε* preserves the degree and consequence of *sì … che* at 97–98. Yet at 96 the similar *tanto … che* becomes *μέχρις ἄν* with the subjunctive: the killing marks a limit, ‘until’, rather than a result. Case can settle a reading just as decisively. At 27, accusative *ὅν* makes the pass the object of *κατέλιπεν*, ‘left behind’, and the living person its subject; Italian *che* does not itself distinguish the two. At 126, *μ’ ἐλθεῖν*, ‘for me to enter’, gives *per me si vegna* a personal subject, losing the distinction between entering oneself and being the means by which another enters.

The diction ranges from poetic *Τροίηθεν*, ‘from Troy’, to the postclassical imperfect *ἤμην*, ‘I was’. A close verbal echo can keep even an unusual Italian image: *σιγηλὸς μένει*, ‘remains silent’, preserves *il sol tace* (60). Elsewhere the Greek changes not just the words but what they describe. At 19–21, fear no longer persists in *nel lago del cor*, ‘in the lake of the heart’; it acts on *τοὺς κρουνούς*, ‘the springs’, as *παγώσας*, ‘having frozen’. A quieter but equally consequential shift occurs at 41–43: *τὸ ποικιλόχρουν δέρμα*, ‘the many-coloured hide’, becomes a grammatical subject alongside the season and the hour. What in Italian identifies the beast about which the speaker hopes thus becomes, in Greek, one of his reasons for hoping.
