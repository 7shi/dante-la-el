# Inferno 19

## Lines 1–18 — The Trumpet and the Round Holes of the Third Pit

> 1 O Simon mago, o miseri seguaci,  
> 2 che le cose di Dio, che di bontate  
> 3 deono essere spose, e voi rapaci  
> 4 per oro e per argento adulterate;  

O Simon Magus! O wretched followers of his and robbers ye, who prostitute the things of God, that should be wedded unto righteousness, for gold and silver!

> 1 Ὦ μάγε Σίμων, ὦ συνοπαδοὶ φαῦλοι,  
> (O Simon Magus, O wicked companions,)
>
> 2 Οἱ τὰ θεῖα πράγματα τἀγαθοῖς μόνοις  
> (Who the sacred things, to the good alone)
>
> 3 Ἀνήκοντα, συζύγοις ὡς, ὑμεῖς φῶρες  
> (Belonging as spouses, you thieves)
>
> 4 Προαγωγεύετ᾽ ἀντὶ χρυσοῦ κἀργύρου !  
> (Prostitute for gold and silver!)

- *Ὦ μάγε Σίμων, ὦ συνοπαδοὶ φαῦλοι* keeps the two vocative cries but reverses the order within each Italian phrase: *Simon mago* becomes ‘magician Simon’, and *miseri seguaci* becomes ‘wicked followers’. *μάγε* is the vocative singular of *μάγος*; *Σίμων* retains the name’s nominative form in address. *συνοπαδοί* and *φαῦλοι* are masculine vocative plurals, identical in form to the nominatives. *φαῦλοι* makes *miseri* a judgment of character rather than a description of wretchedness.
- Where Italian begins with relative *che* and eventually reaches the finite verb *adulterate*, Greek begins with the nominative plural article *οἱ*, as if to name the offenders by what they do, but resumes the address with *ὑμεῖς φῶρες* ‘you thieves’ and a finite second-person verb. The resulting change of construction follows the interruption in *che … e voi rapaci*; *οἱ* is not the ordinary relative pronoun *οἵ*. The noun *φῶρες* (plural of *φώρ*) gives the predatory adjective *rapaci* a concrete equivalent.
- *τὰ θεῖα πράγματα* ‘the divine things’ is the accusative object of the eventual main verb; the adjective *θεῖα* replaces the genitive relation in *le cose di Dio*. The intervening relative clause *che di bontate deono essere spose* becomes *τἀγαθοῖς μόνοις ἀνήκοντα, συζύγοις ὡς* ‘belonging to the good alone, as to spouses’. *ἀνήκοντα* is the present active participle of *ἀνήκω*, accusative neuter plural agreeing with *πράγματα*. Its present sense of belonging replaces the obligation expressed by *deono*. *τἀγαθοῖς* represents *τοῖς ἀγαθοῖς* in crasis: Musurus takes abstract *bontate* personally, ‘the good’, and adds restrictive *μόνοις*. The delayed *ὡς* marks the comparison; *συζύγοις* is dative in apposition to the persons to whom the things belong, rather than a predicate of the things themselves as *spose* is.
- *προαγωγεύετ᾽* is *προαγωγεύετε* (second-person plural present active indicative of *προαγωγεύω*), with final *-ε* elided before *ἀντί*. It means ‘you procure for prostitution’, a more specific act than *adulterate*. *ἀντί* governs the genitives *χρυσοῦ κἀργύρου* and makes *per* explicitly a price of exchange; *κἀργύρου* is crasis for *καὶ ἀργύρου*.

> 5 or convien che per voi suoni la tromba,  
> 6 però che nella terza bolgia state.  

now must the trump sound for you: for ye are in the third chasm.

> 5 Νυνὶ δι᾽ ὑμᾶς ἡ σάλπιγξ ἠχησάτω,  
> (Now for you let the trumpet sound,)
>
> 6 Ἀνθ᾽ ὧν εἰς τρίτον ἐβυθίσθητε βόθρον.  
> (Because you have been plunged into the third pit.)

- Emphatic *νυνί* renders *or*. Instead of the impersonal necessity of *convien* followed by subjunctive *suoni*, Musurus gives the trumpet a direct command: *ἡ σάλπιγξ ἠχησάτω* ‘let the trumpet sound’. *ἠχησάτω* is the third-person singular aorist active imperative of *ἠχέω*, calling for the act of sounding; the initial *η* belongs to the stem, not to an augment. *δι᾽ ὑμᾶς* is elided *διά* with the second-person plural accusative, ‘on your account’, for *per voi*.
- *ἀνθ᾽ ὧν* is a causal relative expression, ‘inasmuch as’, for *però che*: *ἀντί* loses its final vowel and its *τ* becomes *θ* before the rough breathing of genitive plural *ὧν*. The state *nella terza bolgia state* becomes an event, *εἰς τρίτον ἐβυθίσθητε βόθρον* ‘you were plunged into a third pit’. *ἐβυθίσθητε* is the augmented second-person plural aorist passive indicative of *βυθίζω*; *εἰς* with the accusative marks the destination, with *τρίτον* separated from *βόθρον* by the verb.

> 7 Già eravamo alla seguente tomba  
> 8 montati, dello scoglio in quella parte  
> 9 che appunto sopra mezzo il fosso piomba.  

Already we had mounted to the following grave, on that part of the cliff which hangs right over the middle of the foss.

> 7 Τέως ἤλθομεν εἰς τὸν ἐφεξῆς τύμβον,  
> (Meanwhile we had come to the next tomb,)
>
> 8 Εἰς μέρος, ἔνθ᾽ ἡ σπιλὰς πρὸς τὸ τοῦ βόθρου  
> (To that part where the rock over the pit's)
>
> 9 Μέσον κατὰ κάθετον ὑπερεξέχει.  
> (Middle hangs directly perpendicular.)

- *τέως* ‘by then, meanwhile’ supplies the temporal setting of *già*. *ἤλθομεν* is the first-person plural second aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι*: ‘we came’ replaces the pluperfect *eravamo … montati*, leaving the ascent unexpressed. *εἰς τὸν ἐφεξῆς τύμβον* renders the destination *alla seguente tomba*; the indeclinable adverb *ἐφεξῆς* ‘next in succession’ stands attributively between article and noun.
- *εἰς μέρος* restates the destination more narrowly, ‘to a part’, without the demonstrative of *in quella parte*. Elided *ἔνθ᾽* (from *ἔνθα*) introduces a local clause, ‘where the crag … projects’. Thus *dello scoglio* no longer qualifies ‘part’: *ἡ σπιλάς* ‘the crag’ is the explicit nominative subject of *ὑπερεξέχει* (third-person singular present active indicative of *ὑπερεξέχω*).
- *πρὸς τὸ τοῦ βόθρου μέσον* means ‘towards the middle of the pit’: the genitive *τοῦ βόθρου* falls between the article and the substantivized neuter *μέσον*. For *appunto sopra … piomba*, the compound verb conveys projection over something, while *κατὰ κάθετον* ‘along a perpendicular’ makes the plumb-line precision explicit; *κάθετον* is an accusative after *κατά*.

> 10 O somma Sapienza, quanta è l'arte  
> 11 che mostri in cielo, in terra e nel mal mondo,  
> 12 e quanto giusto tua virtù comparte!  

O Wisdom Supreme, what art thou shewest in heaven, on earth and in the evil world, and how justly thy Goodness dispenses!

> 10 Ὦ μεγίστη Σοφία, πῶς ἡ σὴ τέχνη  
> (O supreme Wisdom, how your art)
>
> 11 Ἐμφαίνετ᾽ ἐν οὐρανοῖς, ἐν γῇ κἀν ᾍδου !  
> (Is manifested in the heavens, on earth, and in Hell!)
>
> 12 Πόσον δικαία πρὸς πάντας ἡ σὴ δίκη !  
> (How just toward all is your justice!)

- *ὦ μεγίστη Σοφία* keeps the address of *O somma Sapienza*: *μεγίστη*, feminine singular superlative of *μέγας*, agrees with the vocative *Σοφία*. Exclamatory *πῶς* ‘how’ redirects attention from the greatness of the art (*quanta è l’arte*) to how it appears. In place of the relative *che mostri* with Wisdom as agent, *ἡ σὴ τέχνη* ‘your art’ is the subject of *ἐμφαίνετ᾽* (elided *ἐμφαίνεται*, third-person singular present middle/passive indicative of *ἐμφαίνω*), ‘is manifest’.
- *ἐν οὐρανοῖς, ἐν γῇ* uses locative datives, plural ‘heavens’ for singular *cielo*, singular ‘earth’ for *terra*. *κἀν ᾍδου* has crasis of *καὶ ἐν*. The genitive *ᾍδου* is not governed directly by *ἐν*: the idiom is elliptical, ‘in [the house] of Hades’. The proper name replaces the descriptive *mal mondo*.
- In *πόσον δικαία πρὸς πάντας ἡ σὴ δίκη* ‘how just toward all is your justice’, *πόσον* is a neuter accusative of degree and *δικαία* a feminine nominative predicate adjective with the copula understood. Musurus drops the conjunction *e* and balances two exclamations. Instead of the action *quanto giusto tua virtù comparte*, he predicates a quality of *ἡ σὴ δίκη*: *δίκη* narrows *virtù* to ‘justice’, while *πρὸς πάντας* ‘toward all’ supplies those affected without a verb of apportioning.

> 13 Io vidi per le coste e per lo fondo  
> 14 piena la pietra livida di fori  
> 15 d' un largo tutti, e ciascuno era tondo.  

I saw the livid stone, on the sides and on the bottom, full of holes, all of one breadth; and each was round.

> 13 Ἐπὶ τῶν πλευρῶν καὶ τοῦ βάθους τὴν πέτραν  
> (Upon the sides and along the bottom the rock,)
>
> 14 Τὴν πελιδναίαν πλήθουσαν ὀπῶν εἶδον,  
> (Livid in hue, I saw full of holes,)
>
> 15 Ἴσων τὸ πλάτος καὶ περιφερῶν ὅλως.  
> (Equal in width and wholly round.)

- *ἐπὶ τῶν πλευρῶν καὶ τοῦ βάθους* fronts both locations before the object and verb. One *ἐπί* governs the two genitives, ‘on the sides and the bottom’, in place of repeated *per*. *βάθος* literally denotes ‘depth’. Italian begins with *Io vidi*; Greek leaves the subject in the ending of *εἶδον* (here first-person singular second aorist active indicative of *ὁράω*) and delays the verb until after the description of the rock.
- *τὴν πέτραν τὴν πελιδναίαν* ‘the rock, the livid one’ uses the repeated article to mark the adjective as attributive. By contrast, *πλήθουσαν* (present active participle of *πλήθω*, accusative feminine singular agreeing with *πέτραν*) is predicative after the verb of perception: ‘I saw the rock abounding in holes’. The participle replaces *piena* and takes the genitive *ὀπῶν* (from *ὀπή*) for *di fori*.
- *ἴσων τὸ πλάτος καὶ περιφερῶν ὅλως* continues to qualify *ὀπῶν*: *ἴσων* and *περιφερῶν* (from *ἴσος* and *περιφερής*) are genitive feminine plurals. *τὸ πλάτος* is an accusative of respect, ‘equal in width’, for *d’un largo tutti*. Italian then singles out each hole with *ciascuno era tondo*; Greek coordinates the two adjectives and uses *ὅλως* ‘wholly’ to qualify roundness, without a distributive pronoun or a finite verb.

> 16 Non mi parean meno ampi nè maggiori  
> 17 che quei che son nel mio bel San Giovanni  
> 18 fatti per loco de' battezzatori;  

Not less wide they seemed to me, nor larger, than those that are in my beauteous San Giovanni made for stands to the baptizers;

> 16 Οὔτε πλέον οὐδ᾽ ἧττον ἦσαν μεγάλαι  
> (Neither larger nor smaller were they)
>
> 17 Τῶν οὐσῶν ἐν καλῷ μου ναῷ τοῦ θείου  
> (Than those in my fair church of the divine)
>
> 18 Προδρόμου πρὸς τὸν τόπον τῶν βαπτιζόντων,  
> (Forerunner, at the place of the baptizers,)

- *οὔτε πλέον οὐδ᾽ ἧττον ἦσαν μεγάλαι* means ‘neither more nor less were they large’. The comparative adverbs *πλέον* and *ἧττον* modify the one positive adjective *μεγάλαι* (nominative feminine plural, referring to *ὀπαί*). This reverses the order of *meno ampi nè maggiori* and removes the distinction between width and size. *οὐδ᾽* is *οὐδέ* elided before *ἧττον*. *ἦσαν*, third-person plural imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*, keeps the past time of *parean* but asserts size directly, omitting the perceiver *mi*.
- *τῶν οὐσῶν* is a genitive of comparison, ‘than the ones being [there]’, for *che quei che son*. The article and the present participle of *εἰμί* are genitive feminine plural, with ‘holes’ understood. *ἐν καλῷ μου ναῷ* supplies ‘in my fair temple’ for *nel mio bel San Giovanni*: *καλῷ* agrees with dative *ναῷ*, and enclitic possessive *μου* follows the adjective. *τοῦ θείου Προδρόμου*, a genitive dependent on *ναῷ*, substitutes the title ‘of the divine Forerunner’ for the Italian name.
- *πρὸς τὸν τόπον τῶν βαπτιζόντων* retains a prepositional phrase, ‘for the place of those baptizing’, but supplies no participle meaning *fatti*. *πρός* with the accusative can indicate intended use; the Greek speaks of *τόπος* ‘place’ rather than explicitly of things made to serve as stands. The Italian agent noun *battezzatori* becomes *τῶν βαπτιζόντων*, the substantivized present active participle of *βαπτίζω*, genitive masculine plural dependent on *τόπον*.

## Lines 19–51 — The Burning Soles and the Confessor

> 19 l' un delli quali, ancor non è molt' anni,  
> 20 rupp' io per un che dentro vi annegava :  

one of which, not many years ago, I broke to save one that was drowning in it:

> 19 Ὧν κατέαξα πρό τινων ἐτῶν μίαν,  
> (One of which I broke some years ago,)
>
> 20 Ἵνα τίν᾽ ἐντὸς πνιγόμενον λυτρώσω·  
> (So that I might rescue someone drowning within;)

- *ὧν … μίαν* ‘of which … one’ frames the clause: *ὧν* is the partitive genitive plural of the relative pronoun, *μίαν* the feminine accusative singular of *εἷς*, object of *κατέαξα*. Where Italian has masculine *l’un delli quali*, the Greek numeral takes the gender of the openings, subsequently called *ὀπαί*.
- *κατέαξα* ‘I broke’ (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *κατάγνυμι*) is brought forward from *rupp’ io* in line 20; the person is in the verb, without a separate *io*. The unusual internal *εα* belongs to the augmented aorist of *ἄγνυμι*, a verb that historically began with digamma. *πρό τινων ἐτῶν* ‘some years ago’ uses *πρό* with the genitive of the interval; it replaces the negative expression *ancor non è molt’ anni*.
- *ἵνα … λυτρώσω* makes *per un che* explicitly final: ‘in order that I might rescue someone’. *λυτρώσω* is 1st sg. aor. act. subj. of *λυτρόω*; the subjunctive is retained after a past main verb, where a purpose optative would also be possible. *τίν᾽* is elided *τινά*, the indefinite accusative, accented before the following enclitic *ἐντός*. *πνιγόμενον* (acc. sg. masc., pres. mid./pass. participle of *πνίγω*) agrees with it: the relative clause *che dentro vi annegava* becomes a participle of action in progress, with *ἐντός* ‘inside’ doing duty for both local expressions.

> 21 e questo fia suggel ch' ogni uomo sganni.  

and be this a seal to undeceive all men.

> 21 Τόδ᾽ ἀναίρεσις ἔστω πάσης ἀπάτης.  
> (Let this be a refutation of every falsehood.)

- *τόδ᾽ … ἔστω* ‘let this be’ uses elided neuter *τόδε* as subject and the 3rd sg. pres. imperative of *εἰμί* for *questo fia*; the initial *e* has no separate equivalent. *ἀναίρεσις* ‘refutation, doing away with’ is a predicate nominative in place of the figure *suggel*.
- *πάσης ἀπάτης* ‘of all deception’ is an objective genitive with the action noun: instead of a seal *ch’ ogni uomo sganni*, Greek gives the thing to be done away with. The universality of *ogni* thus attaches to deception, not to the persons undeceived.

> 22 Fuor della bocca a ciascun soperchiava  
> 23 d'un peccator li piedi, e delle gambe  
> 24 infino al grosso; e l'altro dentro stava.  

From the mouth of each emerged a sinner's feet, and legs up to the calf; and the rest remained within.

> 22 Ἐξήρχοντ᾽ ἐξ ἑκάστης τῶν ὀπῶν τούτων  
> (There came forth out of each of these holes)
>
> 23 Οἱ πόδες τινὸς τῶν ἁμαρτωλῶν, ἕως  
> (The feet of one of the sinners, as far as)
>
> 24 Γαστροκνημίων· τὸ λοιπὸν δ᾽ ἔμεν᾽ ἔνδον.  
> (The calves; and the rest remained within.)

- *ἐξήρχοντ᾽ ἐξ ἑκάστης τῶν ὀπῶν τούτων* ‘came out of each of these openings’: *ἐξήρχοντ᾽* is elided *ἐξήρχοντο*, 3rd pl. imperf. mid. ind. of deponent *ἐξέρχομαι*, with the augment after the prefix. The repeated *ἐξ* marks the source; *ἑκάστης* is feminine genitive singular with the partitive genitive *τῶν ὀπῶν τούτων*. Greek names the openings and adds ‘these’, rather than distinguishing the *bocca* of each.
- As in Italian the verb precedes the subject, *οἱ πόδες*, but it agrees with the plural feet, in contrast to singular *soperchiava*. *τινὸς τῶν ἁμαρτωλῶν* ‘of one of the sinners’ combines a possessive genitive with a partitive one for *d’un peccator*. *ἕως γαστροκνημίων* ‘as far as the calves’ has *ἕως* as a preposition with the genitive plural; there is no separate noun corresponding to *delle gambe*.
- *τὸ λοιπὸν δ᾽ ἔμεν᾽ ἔνδον* ‘and the remainder stayed inside’ follows *e l’altro dentro stava*: *τὸ λοιπόν* is a substantivized neuter adjective, *δ᾽* elided postpositive *δέ*, and *ἔμεν᾽* elided *ἔμενε*, 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *μένω*.

> 25 Le piante erano a tutti accese intrambe:  

The soles of all were both on fire:

> 25 Ἔμπυροι δ᾽ ἦσαν αἱ πέζαι πᾶσιν ἄμφω·  
> (And on fire were both soles of all of them;)

- *ἔμπυροι δ᾽ ἦσαν αἱ πέζαι* puts the predicate ‘on fire’ first, rather than beginning with *le piante*. *ἔμπυροι* is feminine nominative plural of the two-termination adjective *ἔμπυρος*, agreeing with *αἱ πέζαι* ‘the soles’; *ἦσαν* is 3rd pl. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*. An adjective replaces the Italian participle *accese*.
- *πᾶσιν* is a dative plural of possession, closely matching *a tutti*. Final *ἄμφω* ‘both’ keeps the position of *intrambe*: the nominative/accusative dual form is attached to a plural noun, without a dual verb.

> 26 per che sì forte guizzavan le giunte,  
> 27 che spezzate averian ritorte e strambe.  

wherefore the joints quivered so strongly, that they would have snapped in pieces withes and grass-ropes.

> 26 Οἱ δ᾽ ἁρμοὶ σφοδρῶς συνεστρέφοντο τόσον,  
> (And their joints writhed so violently,)
>
> 27 Ὥστε κάλως ἂν διέῤῥηξαν καὶ πέδας.  
> (That they would have snapped cables and fetters.)

- *οἱ δ᾽ ἁρμοὶ … συνεστρέφοντο* ‘and the joints kept twisting’: connective *δέ* leaves the causal force of *per che* implicit. *συνεστρέφοντο* (3rd pl. imperf. mid./pass. ind. of *συστρέφω*, with internal augment) gives a twisting movement for *guizzavan*. *σφοδρῶς* ‘violently’ and adverbial accusative *τόσον* ‘to such a degree’ expand *sì forte*; *τόσον* points forward to *ὥστε*.
- *ὥστε … ἂν διέῤῥηξαν* expresses a hypothetical result by a finite verb, not a result infinitive: *διέῤῥηξαν* is 3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *διαρρήγνυμι*, with *ἄν* giving the unreal ‘would have burst asunder’ of *spezzate averian*. The augment follows the prefix; the two breathings in *ῤῥ* are an older spelling of doubled rho. *κάλως* here is the accusative plural of *κάλως* ‘cable’, not the adverb *καλῶς*. It and *πέδας* (acc. pl. of *πέδη*, ‘fetter’) replace the particular bindings *ritorte e strambe*.

> 28 Qual suole il fiammeggiar delle cose unte  
> 29 moversi pur su per l' estrema buccia,  
> 30 tal era lì da' calcagni alle punte.  

As the flaming of things oiled moves only on their outer surface: so was it there, from the heels to the points.

> 28 Ὡς δ᾽ ἡ φλὸξ περιφέρετ᾽ ἐπιπολαίως  
> (And as the flame moves along the surface)
>
> 29 Ἐπὶ πραγμάτων ἠλειμμένων ἐλαίῳ,  
> (Over things anointed with oil,)
>
> 30 Οὕτω πως ἦσαν ἐκ πτερνῶν ἄχρις ἄκρων.  
> (Even so were they from heels to toes.)

- *ὡς δ᾽ ἡ φλὸξ περιφέρετ᾽* ‘and as the flame moves about’ begins the *qual … tal* comparison. The verbal noun *il fiammeggiar* becomes the concrete subject *ἡ φλόξ*; *περιφέρετ᾽* is elided *περιφέρεται*, 3rd sg. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *περιφέρω*. The generic present alone conveys *suole … moversi*.
- *ἐπιπολαίως ἐπὶ πραγμάτων* ‘along the surface, over things’ replaces *pur su per l’estrema buccia* by an adverb of manner and *ἐπί* with the genitive: neither ‘only’ nor the noun ‘skin’ is separately translated. *ἠλειμμένων* (gen. pl. neut., perf. mid./pass. participle of *ἀλείφω*) agrees with *πραγμάτων* and expresses the resultant state in *unte*; *ἐλαίῳ*, dative of means, makes the oil explicit.
- *οὕτω πως ἦσαν* ‘some such way were they’ answers *ὡς*: enclitic *πως* qualifies *οὕτω*, and plural *ἦσαν* refers back to the soles rather than reproducing singular *era* and local *lì*. *ἐκ πτερνῶν ἄχρις ἄκρων* gives the two limits with genitives, literally ‘from heels as far as extremities’; *ἄκρων* is substantival, like *le punte*.

> 31 “Chi è colui, maestro, che si cruccia,  
> 32 guizzando più che gli altri suoi consorti,”  
> 33 diss'io, “e cui più rossa fiamma succia?”  

"Master! who is that who writhes himself, quivering more than all his fellows," I said, "and sucked by ruddier flame?"

> 31 “Τίς ἔσθ᾽ ὁ κινούμενος,” εἶπον, “Προστάτα,  
> (“Who is he who moves,” I said, “Protector,)
>
> 32 „ Καὶ περιστρεφόμενος πλέον τῶν ἄλλων,  
> („ And twists about more than the others,)
>
> 33 „ Ὅνπερ ἐρυθροτέρα φλὸξ περιφλέγει;”  
> („ Whom a redder flame burns all around?”)

- *τίς ἔσθ᾽ ὁ κινούμενος* ‘who is the one moving?’ uses interrogative *τίς* and an articular participle for *chi è colui … che*. *ἔσθ᾽* represents *ἐστί* with elision and aspiration of the final consonant before rough-breathed *ὁ*. *κινούμενος* (nom. sg. masc., pres. mid./pass. of *κινέω*) renders *si cruccia* as motion rather than distress.
- *εἶπον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*) brings *diss’io* forward from line 33. The vocative *Προστάτα*, from *προστάτης*, recasts *maestro* as ‘protector’. *καὶ περιστρεφόμενος* ‘and twisting about’ coordinates a second present mid./pass. participle with the first for Italian *guizzando*. *πλέον*, neuter comparative used adverbially, takes the genitive of comparison *τῶν ἄλλων*: ‘more than the others’, without the possessive or the specific *consorti*.
- *ὅνπερ ἐρυθροτέρα φλὸξ περιφλέγει* ‘whom a redder flame burns all round’: *ὅνπερ* is the emphatic masculine accusative relative, object of *περιφλέγει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind.). The flame remains the subject and *ἐρυθροτέρα* its comparative adjective, but surrounding and burning replace the sucking action of *succia*.

> 34 Ed egli a me: “Se tu vuoi ch'io ti porti  
> 35 laggiù per quella ripa che più giace,  
> 36 da lui saprai di sè e de' suoi torti.”  

And he to me: "If thou wilt have me carry thee down there, by that lower bank, thou shalt learn from him about himself and about his wrongs."

> 34 Ὁ δ᾽ ὑπέλαβεν. “Εἰ θέλεις ἀγαγεῖν σε  
> (And he replied: “If you wish me to lead you)
>
> 35 „ Κάτω πρὸς τὸ κάταντες τοῦ κρημνοῦ μέρος,  
> („ Down to the sloping part of the crag,)
>
> 36 „ Τίς ἔστ᾽ εἴσῃ παρ᾽ αὐτοῦ, καὐτοῦ τὰς πράξεις.”  
> („ You shall learn from him who he is, and his own deeds.”)

- *ὁ δ᾽ ὑπέλαβεν* ‘and he replied’ supplies a speech verb for *ed egli a me*, omitting the addressee: *ὁ* is pronominal, *ὑπέλαβεν* 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὑπολαμβάνω*. *εἰ θέλεις* retains the open condition with a present indicative. For *ch’io ti porti*, *ἀγαγεῖν σε* gives the second aor. act. infinitive of *ἄγω* with *σε* as object: ‘to lead you’. Its intended subject ‘me’, distinct from the subject of *θέλεις*, has to be understood; an explicit accusative subject would make the classical infinitive construction clearer.
- *κάτω πρὸς τὸ κάταντες τοῦ κρημνοῦ μέρος* ‘down towards the sloping part of the precipice’ interprets *per quella ripa che più giace* as a destination. *πρός* governs accusative *μέρος*; *κάταντες*, neuter singular of *κατάντης*, is attributive, and the whole *κρημνός* stands in the genitive. There is no explicit comparative for *più*.
- *τίς ἔστ᾽ εἴσῃ παρ᾽ αὐτοῦ* puts an indirect question, ‘who he is’, before *εἴσῃ* (2nd sg. fut. mid. ind. of *οἶδα*, future *εἴσομαι*). Thus *di sè* becomes the content of what will be learned, *παρ᾽ αὐτοῦ* its personal source. *καὐτοῦ* is crasis of *καὶ αὐτοῦ*; *αὐτοῦ τὰς πράξεις* is a second, accusative object of *εἴσῃ*, ‘his deeds’, without the adverse judgment in *torti*.

> 37 Ed io: “Tanto m'è bel, quanto a te piace:  
> 38 tu se' signore, e sai ch' io non mi parto  
> 39 dal tuo volere, e sai quel che si tace.”  

And I: "Whatever pleases thee, to me is grateful: thou art my lord, and knowest that I depart not from thy will; also thou knowest what is not spoken."

> 37 Κἀγώ· “ Πᾶν τἀρέσκον σοι κἀμοί γ᾽ ἀρέσκει·  
> (And I: “All that pleases you pleases me too, indeed;)
>
> 38 „ Σύ μοι κύριος, εἰδώς με σῇ βουλήσει  
> („ You are my lord, knowing me to your will)
>
> 39 „ Ἐμμένοντ᾽ ᾀεί, νοῶν τε σιωπῶντα.”  
> („ Ever abiding, and understanding me even when silent.”)

- *κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*, preserving the verbless *ed io*. *πᾶν τἀρέσκον σοι κἀμοί γ᾽ ἀρέσκει* ‘everything pleasing to you pleases me too’ replaces *tanto … quanto* by a subject and predicate from the same verb. *τἀρέσκον* is crasis of *τὸ ἀρέσκον* (articular pres. act. participle, nom. sg. neut., of *ἀρέσκω*); both participle and finite verb take the dative of the person pleased. *κἀμοί* contracts *καὶ ἐμοί*; elided *γ᾽* (*γε*) emphasizes that dative.
- *σύ μοι κύριος* ‘you are lord to me’ suppresses the copula and adds possessive *μοι*. The two occurrences of finite *sai* become nominative masculine singular participles referring to *σύ*: *εἰδώς*, perfect active of *οἶδα* with present sense, and *νοῶν*, contracted present active of *νοέω*, linked by postpositive *τε*.
- *εἰδώς με σῇ βουλήσει ἐμμένοντ᾽ ᾀεί* ‘knowing me always to abide by your will’ uses an accusative and supplementary participle after the verb of knowledge. *ἐμμένοντ᾽* is elided *ἐμμένοντα*, pres. act. participle agreeing with *με*; *ἐμμένω* governs the dative *σῇ βουλήσει*. This turns negative *non mi parto dal* into positive adherence, with poetic *ᾀεί* ‘always’. In *νοῶν τε σιωπῶντα* the accusative participle of *σιωπάω* likewise describes the understood *με*: ‘understanding me when silent’, not an impersonal relative ‘what is not said’.

> 40 Allor venimmo in su l' argine quarto;  

Then we came upon the fourth bulwark;

> 40 Εἰς τὸν τέταρτον τότ᾽ ἤλθομεν αὐλῶνα·  
> (Then we came to the fourth valley;)

- *εἰς τὸν τέταρτον τότ᾽ ἤλθομεν αὐλῶνα* keeps *allor* as elided *τότ᾽* (*τότε*) but inserts it, with the verb, between ordinal and noun. *ἤλθομεν* is 1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*. *εἰς* with the accusative marks motion into or to: *αὐλῶνα* (from *αὐλών*, ‘valley, channel’) interprets *argine* as a depression rather than a raised bank, and *in su* loses its upward or surface sense.

> 41 volgemmo, e discendemmo a mano stanca  
> 42 laggiù nel fondo foracchiato ed arto.  

we turned and descended, on the left hand, down there into the perforated and narrow bottom.

> 41 Πρὸς τἀριστερὰ κατέβημεν, στραφέντες  
> (To the left we went down, turning)
>
> 42 Παρὰ τὸ στενὸν καὶ βαθύτρητον βάθος.  
> (Along the narrow and deep-pierced depth.)

- *πρὸς τἀριστερά* ‘towards the left’ fronts the direction: *τἀριστερά* is crasis of *τὰ ἀριστερά*, a substantivized neuter plural after *πρός*, with no noun for *mano*. Of the two coordinate Italian verbs, only *κατέβημεν* (1st pl. root aor. act. ind. of *καταβαίνω*) remains finite; *στραφέντες* is a nominative masculine plural second aor. passive participle of *στρέφω*, intransitive in sense, ‘having turned’.
- *παρὰ τὸ στενὸν καὶ βαθύτρητον βάθος* uses *παρά* with the accusative for motion along, rather than *laggiù nel* for motion down into. Both adjectives agree with neuter *βάθος* ‘depth’: *στενόν* translates *arto*, while the compound *βαθύτρητον* ‘deep-pierced’ renders *foracchiato*. Their order is reversed.

> 43 Lo buon maestro ancor della sua anca  
> 44 non mi dipose, sì mi giunse al rotto  
> 45 di quei che sì piangeva con la zanca.  

The kind Master did not yet depose me from his side, till he brought me to the cleft of him who so lamented with his legs.

> 43 Ὁ Προστάτης δέ μ᾽ ἐβάσταζ᾽ ἐν ἀγκάλαις,  
> (And the Protector carried me in his arms,)
>
> 44 Ἕως ἐγγὺς τῆς ὀπῆς ἔθετ᾽ ἐκείνου,  
> (Until he set me near the hole of that one,)
>
> 45 Ὃς οὕτω σφόδρ᾽ ἔκλαιε, κινῶν τοὺς πόδας.  
> (Who wept so bitterly, moving his feet.)

- *ὁ Προστάτης δέ μ᾽ ἐβάσταζ᾽ ἐν ἀγκάλαις* ‘and the protector was carrying me in his arms’ replaces *lo buon maestro* by the earlier title, without *buon*. *μ᾽* is elided *με*, and *ἐβάσταζ᾽* elided *ἐβάσταζε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *βαστάζω*). An affirmative imperfect of continuing action does the work of *ancor … non mi dipose*; *ἐν* with dative plural *ἀγκάλαις* substitutes the arms for *anca*.
- *ἕως … ἔθετ᾽* ‘until he placed [me]’ marks an actual past limit with the indicative. *ἔθετ᾽* is elided *ἔθετο*, 3rd sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *τίθημι*: the setting down, negated in the Italian main clause, now supplies the endpoint instead of *mi giunse*. *ἐγγύς* governs genitive *τῆς ὀπῆς*, ‘near the opening’; possessive *ἐκείνου* ‘of that one’ is separated from it by the verb and supplies the antecedent of *ὅς*.
- *ὃς οὕτω σφόδρ᾽ ἔκλαιε, κινῶν τοὺς πόδας* distinguishes weeping from moving the feet. *σφόδρ᾽* is elided *σφόδρα*, reinforcing *οὕτω* for *sì*; *ἔκλαιε* is 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *κλαίω*. *κινῶν*, contracted nominative masculine singular pres. act. participle of *κινέω*, adds a simultaneous action with a plural object, in place of instrumental *con la zanca*.

> 46 “O qual che se', che 'l di su tien di sotto,  
> 47 anima trista, come pal commessa,”  
> 48 comincia' io a dir, “se puoi, fa motto.”  

"O whoe'er thou be that hast thy upper part beneath, unhappy spirit, planted like a stake!" I began to say; "if thou art able, speak."

> 46 “ Οἷός ποτ᾽ ἂν ᾖς, ὁ τἄνω φέρων κάτω,  
> (“Whoever you may be, who keep the upper part below,)
>
> 47 „ Πνεῦμα τάλαν, πεπηγὸς παττάλου δίκην,”  
> („ Wretched spirit, planted like a stake,”)
>
> 48 Ἠρξάμην λέγων, “ εἴ σοι δυνατόν, λάλει.”  
> (I began saying, “if you are able, speak.”)

- *οἷός ποτ᾽ ἂν ᾖς* ‘of whatever sort you may be’ renders *o qual che se’* without a separate interjection. The qualitative relative *οἷος* has the generalizing *ποτ᾽* (elided enclitic *ποτε*) and *ἄν* with *ᾖς*, 2nd sg. pres. subj. of *εἰμί*. The Greek marks indefiniteness by the subjunctive rather than retaining the Italian indicative.
- *ὁ τἄνω φέρων κάτω* ‘you who carry the upper parts below’ turns *che … tien* into an articular pres. act. participle, nominative masculine singular used in address. *τἄνω* is crasis of *τὰ ἄνω*, the adverb substantivized as a neuter plural object; bare *κάτω* is adverbial, as against *di sotto*.
- *πνεῦμα τάλαν* ‘wretched spirit’ is neuter singular in address, with *τάλαν* the neuter of *τάλας*. *πεπηγός* is the agreeing neuter singular second perfect active participle of *πήγνυμι*, intransitive and stative, ‘standing fixed’, for passive *commessa*. *παττάλου δίκην* uses *δίκην*, an adverbial accusative acting as a postposition with the genitive, ‘in the manner of a stake’; *πάτταλος* has Attic *ττ*.
- *ἠρξάμην λέγων* (1st sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἄρχω*, with nominative pres. act. participle of *λέγω*) substitutes the Greek participial construction ‘I began speaking’ for *comincia’ io a dir*. *εἴ σοι δυνατόν* supplies no finite verb: ‘if [it is] possible for you’, with a dative instead of personal *puoi*. *λάλει*, contracted 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *λαλέω*, replaces *fa motto* by one verb.

> 49 Io stava come il frate che confessa  
> 50 lo perfido assassin, che poi ch' è fitto  
> 51 richiama lui, per che la morte cessa;  

I stood, like the friar who is confessing a treacherous assassin that, after being fixed, recalls him and thus delay[s] the death;

> 49 Ἱστάμην ὡς ἱερεὺς διεξετάζων  
> (I stood like a priest questioning)
>
> 50 Δολοφόνον, ὃς ἐντεθαμμένος ἤδη  
> (An assassin, who, already buried,)
>
> 51 Αὐτὸν πρὸς ἀναβολὴν καλεῖ θανάτου.  
> (Calls him to delay his death.)

- *ἱστάμην* is 1st sg. imperf. mid./pass. ind. of *ἵστημι*, intransitive ‘I stood’, answering *io stava*. *ὡς ἱερεὺς διεξετάζων* ‘like a priest questioning closely’ uses an anarthrous predicate of comparison and a nominative masculine singular pres. act. participle of *διεξετάζω* for *il frate che confessa*. Both the office and the act are rendered in less specific terms.
- *δολοφόνον*, masculine accusative object of the participle, combines the killing and the treachery of *lo perfido assassin* in one compound. Relative *ὅς* takes up this accusative noun as nominative subject of *καλεῖ*. *ἐντεθαμμένος ἤδη* ‘already buried in [the ground]’ is a nominative masculine singular perf. mid./pass. participle of *ἐνθάπτω*, passive in sense: *ἤδη* and the resulting state of the perfect replace the finite temporal clause *poi ch’è fitto*. The Greek specifies burial rather than simply being fixed.
- *αὐτὸν … καλεῖ* ‘calls him’ places the object pronoun referring to the priest first, but the verb, contracted 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *καλέω*, has no prefix for the ‘back’ of *richiama*. *πρὸς ἀναβολὴν … θανάτου* converts *per che la morte cessa* into a purpose phrase, ‘for postponement of death’: *πρός* governs the accusative action noun *ἀναβολήν*, and *θανάτου*, delayed until after the verb, is its objective genitive.

## Lines 52–84 — The Mistaken Question and the Succession of Shepherds

> 52 ed ei gridò: “Sei tu già costì ritto,  
> 53 sei tu già costì ritto, Bonifazio?  

and he cried: "Art thou there already standing, Boniface? art thou there already standing?

> 52 Οὗτος δ᾽ ἔκραξε· “ Πῶς ὧδ᾽ ὄρθιος ἤδη;  
> (And he cried out: “How are you already standing upright here?)
>
> 53 „ Βονιφάκιε, πῶς ὧδ᾽ ὄρθιος ἤδη;  
> („ Boniface, how are you already standing upright here?)

- *Οὗτος δ᾽ ἔκραξε* ‘and this one cried out’ renders *ed ei gridò*: the demonstrative *οὗτος* takes up the speaker, *δ᾽* is postpositive *δέ* elided before a vowel, and *ἔκραξε* is the augmented 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *κράζω*.
- *Πῶς ὧδ᾽ ὄρθιος ἤδη;* ‘how [are you] here upright already?’ replaces the yes-or-no question *Sei tu già costì ritto* with a question of surprise introduced by *πῶς*. Both copula and pronoun are understood; *ὄρθιος* is predicate nom. sg. masc. *ὧδ᾽* is elided *ὧδε* ‘here’, rather than *costì* ‘there where you are’, and *ἤδη* ‘already’ comes emphatically last.
- Musurus repeats the whole question, but moves *Βονιφάκιε* (voc. sg. of *Βονιφάκιος*) from the end of the second question to its beginning.

> 54 Di parecchi anni mi mentì lo scritto.  

By several years the writ has lied to me.

> 54 „ Ἐψεύσθη μοι τὸ γραπτὸν κατά τιν᾽ ἔτη.  
> („ The writing has lied to me by some years.)

- *Ἐψεύσθη μοι τὸ γραπτόν* ‘the written thing proved false to me’ puts verb and dative before the subject, as in *mi mentì lo scritto*. *Ἐψεύσθη* is 3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ψεύδω*, passive in form but not ‘was lied to’ here. *Τὸ γραπτόν* is the neut. sg. verbal adjective of *γράφω* used substantivally.
- The fronted *di parecchi anni* becomes the final *κατά τιν᾽ ἔτη*: *κατά* with the accusative gives the extent of the discrepancy. *Τιν᾽* is elided *τινα*, neut. pl. agreeing with *ἔτη* (acc. pl. of *ἔτος*): ‘by some years’, less definite than *parecchi*.

> 55 Se’ tu sì tosto di quell’ aver sazio,  
> 56 per lo qual non temesti torre a inganno  
> 57 la bella Donna, e di poi farne strazio?”  

Art thou so quickly sated with that wealth, for which thou didst not fear to seize the comely Lady by deceit, and then make havoc of her?"

> 55 „ Ἐκορέσθης ἤδη σὺ τοῦ χρυσοῦ ᾽κείνου,  
> („ Are you already sated with that gold,)
>
> 56 „ Δι᾽ ὃν οὐκ ἐφοβήθης ἁρπάσαι δόλῳ  
> („ For which you did not fear to seize by guile)
>
> 57 „ Τὴν καλὴν κυρίαν, ἣν ὕβρισας εἶτα ;”  
> („ The fair lady, whom you then outraged?”)

- *Ἐκορέσθης ἤδη σὺ τοῦ χρυσοῦ ᾽κείνου* ‘were you already sated with that gold?’ replaces *se’ tu sì tosto … sazio*, a present copula and adjective, with *ἐκορέσθης* (2nd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *κορέννυμι*), denoting the attainment of satiety. *Σύ* is expressed, though the verb marks the person. *Ἤδη* ‘already’ takes the place of *sì tosto*; the genitive of that with which one is sated specifies *χρυσός* ‘gold’ for the broader *avere*. *᾽Κείνου* has lost the initial vowel of *ἐκείνου* by prodelision.
- *Δι᾽ ὅν* is *διά* with the acc. sg. masc. relative referring to *χρυσοῦ*: ‘on account of which’. *Οὐκ ἐφοβήθης* (2nd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *φοβέω*, in the sense of *φοβέομαι* ‘fear’) takes *ἁρπάσαι*, aor. act. inf. of *ἁρπάζω*, with the dative of means *δόλῳ*. Thus *torre a inganno* becomes ‘snatch by guile’, with the object *τὴν καλὴν κυρίαν* delayed to the next line.
- Where *e di poi farne strazio* coordinates a second infinitive with *torre*, *ἣν ὕβρισας εἶτα* is a new relative clause: *ἥν* is the acc. sg. fem. direct object of *ὕβρισας* (2nd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ὑβρίζω*), ‘whom you subsequently outraged’.

> 58 Tal mi fec’ io, quai son color che stanno,  
> 59 per non intender ciò ch’ è lor risposto,  
> 60 quasi scornati, e risponder non sanno.  

I became like those who stand as if bemocked, not comprehending what is answered to them, and unable to reply.

> 58 Ἐγενόμην, ὡς οἱ μὴ καλῶς νοοῦντες  
> (I became like those who, not clearly understanding)
>
> 59 Τὰ ῥηθέντ᾽ αὐτοῖς μένουσιν ἐν αἰσχύνῃ  
> (What has been said to them, remain in shame)
>
> 60 Σχεδόν, ἀμηχανοῦντες ἀποκριθῆναι.  
> (Almost, at a loss how to answer.)

- *Ἐγενόμην* (1st sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *γίγνομαι*) replaces reflexive *tal mi fec’ io* with ‘I became’; comparative *ὡς* introduces the description without a separate equivalent of *tal*. *Οἱ μὴ καλῶς νοοῦντες* ‘those not understanding clearly’ makes the failure to understand part of the designation of these people: the article substantivizes the nom. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *νοέω*. *Μή* characterizes the class; *καλῶς* is supplied. Italian instead gives *per non intender* as a reason.
- *Τὰ ῥηθέντ᾽ αὐτοῖς* ‘the things said to them’ is the object of *νοοῦντες*: *ῥηθέντ᾽* is elided *ῥηθέντα*, acc. neut. pl. aor. pass. participle used with *λέγω*. The Italian present *ciò ch’ è lor risposto* becomes an anterior utterance, not specifically an answer.
- *Μένουσιν ἐν αἰσχύνῃ σχεδόν* ‘they remain almost in shame’ gives *stanno … quasi scornati* a finite verb (*μένουσιν*, 3rd pl. pres. act. ind.) and a prepositional phrase in place of the predicate participle. *Ἀμηχανοῦντες* (nom. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *ἀμηχανέω*) adds a simultaneous circumstance, ‘being at a loss’, in place of coordinated *e … non sanno*. Its complement *ἀποκριθῆναι* is the aor. pass. inf. of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, active in meaning, ‘to answer’.

> 61 Allor Virgilio disse: “Digli tosto,  
> 62 ‘Non son colui, non son colui che credi.’ ”  
> 63 Ed io risposi come a me fu imposto.  

Then Virgil said: "Say to him quickly, ‘I am not he, I am not he whom thou thinkest.'" And I replied as was enjoined me.

> 61 Τότ᾽ εἶπε Βιργίλιος· “ Λέγ᾽ αὐτῷ τάχος·  
> (Then Virgil said: “Tell him quickly:)
>
> 62 „ Οὐκ εἴμ᾽ ἐκεῖνος, οὐκ εἴμ᾽ ἐκεῖνος, ὅνπερ  
> („ I am not he, I am not he whom)
>
> 63 „ Νομίζεις·” Ἀπεκρίθην δ᾽ ὡς διετάχθην.  
> („ You suppose.” And I answered as I was bidden.)

- *Τότ᾽ εἶπε Βιργίλιος* ‘then said Virgil’ elides *τότε* and puts the subject after the second aor. act. indicative *εἶπε*. *Λέγ᾽ αὐτῷ τάχος* corresponds closely to *digli tosto*: *λέγ᾽* is elided *λέγε*, 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative; the recipient is a separate dative, and *τάχος* is a neuter accusative used adverbially, ‘with speed’.
- *Οὐκ εἴμ᾽ ἐκεῖνος* reproduces the denial twice; *εἴμ᾽* is elided *εἰμί*, with *ἐκεῖνος* as predicate nominative. *Ὅνπερ νομίζεις* ‘the very one whom you suppose’ carries the verb into line 63. The strengthening particle *-περ* attaches to the acc. masc. sg. relative; the sense is ‘whom you suppose [me to be]’, not ‘whom you believe’ in the sense of trusting someone.
- *Ἀπεκρίθην δ᾽ ὡς διετάχθην* gives two 1st sg. aor. pass. indicatives, the first ‘I answered’ (*ἀποκρίνομαι*), the second genuinely passive ‘I was instructed’ (*διατάσσω*, augmented after the preverb). Thus impersonal *come a me fu imposto* becomes personal; *ὡς* specifies manner, and no pronoun is needed for *io*.

> 64 Per che lo spirto tutti storse i piedi;  

Whereat the spirit quite wrenched his feet;

> 64 Τότ᾽ ἔστρεψε τὸ πνεῦμα τοὺς πόδας ἄμφω·  
> (Then the spirit twisted both its feet;)

- *Τότ᾽* ‘then’ gives a temporal rather than expressly consequential link for *per che*. *Ἔστρεψε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *στρέφω*) precedes its neuter subject *τὸ πνεῦμα*; *τοὺς πόδας* is its acc. pl. object. Postposed *ἄμφω* ‘both’ is the old nom./acc. dual form, here accompanying a plural noun: Musurus takes *tutti* as specifying both feet, rather than the degree of the wrenching.

> 65 poi sospirando e con voce di pianto,  
> 66 mi disse: “Dunque che a me richiedi?  

thereafter, sighing and with voice of weeping, he said to me: "Then what askest thou of me?

> 65 Εἶτα στενάζον, οἰμωκτικῇ φωνῇ τε  
> (Then sighing, and with a lamenting voice)
>
> 66 Εἶπέ μοι· “ Λοιπὸν τί βούλει μέ σοι λέγειν ;  
> (Said to me: “What then do you wish me to tell you?)

- *Εἶτα στενάζον* follows *poi sospirando*: *στενάζον* is the nom. neut. sg. pres. act. participle of *στενάζω*, agreeing with *πνεῦμα*, not a masculine participle referring to the speaker. *Οἰμωκτικῇ φωνῇ τε* ‘and with a wailing voice’ uses an adjective and a dative of manner for *con voce di pianto*; enclitic *τε* links this phrase to the participle. Both precede *εἶπέ μοι*, where the enclitic dative accounts for the additional accent on the verb.
- *Λοιπόν* ‘then’ is a neuter adjective used adverbially as a transition. *Τί βούλει μέ σοι λέγειν;* expands *che a me richiedi?* into ‘what do you wish me to say to you?’ *Βούλει* is 2nd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *βούλομαι*; *μέ* is the accusative subject of pres. act. inf. *λέγειν*, *σοι* its recipient, and interrogative *τί* its object. The Italian recipient of the question thus becomes the prospective speaker.

> 67 Se di saper chi io sia ti cal cotanto,  
> 68 che tu abbi però la ripa corsa,  
> 69 sappi ch’ io fui vestito del gran manto;  

If to know who I am concerneth thee so much, that thou hast therefore passed the bank, learn that I was clothed with the Great Mantle;

> 67 „ Εἴπερ μαθεῖν, τίς εἰμι, σὺ ποθῶν μόνον  
> („ If you, longing solely to learn who I am,)
>
> 68 „ Τὸν περίβολον τοῦτον διῆλθες, ἴσθι  
> („ Have traversed this enclosure, know)
>
> 69 „ Ὡς τὴν ἱερὰν περιεβλήθην χλαῖναν.  
> („ That I was clothed in the sacred mantle.)

- *Εἴπερ* ‘if indeed’ strengthens *se* with *-περ*. Instead of impersonal *ti cal cotanto, che …*, Musurus makes the crossing itself the conditional verb: *σὺ … τὸν περίβολον τοῦτον διῆλθες* ‘you traversed this enclosure’. *Διῆλθες* is 2nd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *διέρχομαι*, with internal augment and a direct object. *Περίβολον* interprets *ripa* as an enclosing boundary, and *τοῦτον* supplies ‘this’. There is no corresponding result clause or subjunctive for *che tu abbi … corsa*.
- *Μαθεῖν, τίς εἰμι, σὺ ποθῶν μόνον* places the complement before *ποθῶν* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *ποθέω*, agreeing with *σύ*): ‘desiring only to learn who I am’. *Μαθεῖν* is the second aor. act. inf. of *μανθάνω*, ‘find out’ rather than stative *saper*. The indirect question retains interrogative *τίς* and the present indicative; *μόνον* expresses singleness of purpose rather than the degree expressed by *cotanto*.
- *Ἴσθι* is the 2nd sg. imperative of the perfect *οἶδα*, with present sense ‘know’; *ὡς* introduces its declarative complement. *Τὴν ἱερὰν περιεβλήθην χλαῖναν* places the verb between adjective and noun. *Περιεβλήθην* (1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *περιβάλλω*) follows *fui vestito*, but the garment stands in the retained accusative, not in a phrase corresponding to *del*. *Ἱεράν* ‘sacred’ interprets rather than translates *gran*.

> 70 e veramente fui figliuol dell’ orsa,  
> 71 cupido sì, per avanzar gli orsatti,  
> 72 che su l’ avere, e qui me misi in borsa.  

and verily I was a son of the She-bear, so eager to advance the Whelps, that I pursed wealth above, and here myself.

> 70 „ Ἄρκτου δ᾽ ὄντως ἦν υἱός· ἄπληστος δ᾽ οὕτω  
> („ And indeed I was a son of the She-Bear; and so greedy)
>
> 71 „ Πρὸς τὸ πλουτίσαι πάντας ἀρκτύλους, ὥστε  
> („ To enrich all the bear-cubs, that)
>
> 72 „ Ἐτέθην, ὡς ἔπραττον, ἐν βαλαντίῳ.  
> („ I was placed, just as I used to do, in a purse.)

- *Ἄρκτου δ᾽ ὄντως ἦν υἱός* fronts the genitive dependent on predicate *υἱός*: ‘of a bear indeed I was a son’. *Ἄρκτου* lacks the article of *dell’ orsa*, and the form of the noun alone does not distinguish male from female. *Ὄντως* renders *veramente*; *ἦν* is here the 1st sg. imperfect of *εἰμί*, in contrast to *fui*.
- *Ἄπληστος δ᾽ οὕτω πρὸς τὸ πλουτίσαι πάντας ἀρκτύλους* ‘and so insatiable to enrich all the little bears’ continues the predicate without repeating *ἦν*. *Πρός* with the accusative articular infinitive marks the end toward which the greed was directed. *Πλουτίσαι* is the transitive aor. act. inf. of *πλουτίζω*: it specifies enrichment for *avanzar*. *Ἀρκτύλους* is a diminutive; *πάντας* ‘all’ is added.
- *Οὕτω … ὥστε ἐτέθην* answers to *sì … che*: *ὥστε* with the indicative presents an actual result. *Ἐτέθην* is 1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *τίθημι*: active-reflexive *me misi* becomes ‘I was put’. *Ἐν βαλαντίῳ* (dat. sg. of *βαλάντιον*) describes the resulting position ‘in a purse’.
- *Ὡς ἔπραττον* ‘as I used to do’ compresses the first half of the Italian antithesis. *Ἔπραττον* is 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *πράττω*, with habitual force and no expressed object. The change from active past practice to present predicament is preserved through voice, but neither *l’avere* nor the contrast *su … qui* has an explicit counterpart.

> 73 Di sotto al capo mio son gli altri tratti  
> 74 che precedetter me simoneggiando,  
> 75 per le fessure della pietra piatti.  

Beneath my head are dragged the others who preceded me in simony, cowering [within] the fissure[s] of the stone.

> 73 „ Κάτω τῆς ἐμῆς κεφαλῆς εἰσι πάντες  
> („ Beneath my head are all)
>
> 74 „ Ἄλλοι πρὶν ἐμοῦ πράξαντες σιμωνίαν,  
> („ The others who before me practiced simony,)
>
> 75 „ Ἐν τῷ χάσματι τῆς πέτρας τεθλιμμένοι.  
> („ Compressed within the cleft of the rock.)

- *Κάτω τῆς ἐμῆς κεφαλῆς* has *κάτω* governing a genitive and the possessive adjective in attributive position, ‘below my head’. *Εἰσι πάντες ἄλλοι* ‘are all the others’ substitutes simple presence for the movement of *son gli altri tratti*, again adding *πάντες*.
- *Πρὶν ἐμοῦ πράξαντες σιμωνίαν* recasts *che precedetter me simoneggiando*: *πρὶν ἐμοῦ* ‘before me’ carries the idea of precedence; *πράξαντες* (nom. masc. pl. aor. act. participle of *πράσσω*, Attic *πράττω*) agrees with *ἄλλοι*. The action that Italian makes a gerund becomes the principal description of this group, expressed by verb and cognate borrowing in the sense ‘having practised simony’.
- *Ἐν τῷ χάσματι τῆς πέτρας* has a single ‘cleft’ (dat. sg. of *χάσμα*) for plural *fessure*. *Τεθλιμμένοι* ‘pressed together’ is the reduplicated nom. masc. pl. perf. mid./pass. participle of *θλίβω*, passive in sense: it describes a continuing condition, interpreting *piatti* as physical compression.

> 76 Laggiù cascherò io altresì, quando  
> 77 verrà colui ch’ io credea che tu fossi,  
> 78 allor ch’ io feci il subito dimando.  

I too shall fall down thither, when he comes for whom I took thee when I put the sudden question.

> 76 „ Ἐκεῖ περιτροπάδην κἀγὼ πεσοῦμαι,  
> („ Thither headlong I too shall fall,)
>
> 77 „ Ἅμ’ ἔλθῃ ’κεῖνος, οἷον ὑπέλαβόν σε,  
> („ As soon as he comes, whom I took you to be,)
>
> 78 „ Ὁπότ’ ἄρτι προπετῶς ἠρώτησά σε.  
> („ When just now I rashly questioned you.)

- *Ἐκεῖ περιτροπάδην κἀγὼ πεσοῦμαι* ‘there, tumbling headlong, I too shall fall’ adds the adverb *περιτροπάδην* to *laggiù cascherò*. *Κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*, answering to *io altresì*; *πεσοῦμαι* is the contracted 1st sg. fut. mid. ind. of *πίπτω*, with active intransitive meaning.
- *Ἅμ’ ἔλθῃ ’κεῖνος* ‘as soon as that one comes’ sharpens *quando verrà*. *Ἅμ᾽* is elided *ἅμα*, used as a temporal connective; *ἔλθῃ* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. subj. of *ἔρχομαι*, prospective, here without the *ἄν* usual in an indefinite future temporal clause in Attic. *’Κεῖνος* is prodelided *ἐκεῖνος*.
- *Οἷον ὑπέλαβόν σε* ‘such as I took you [to be]’ uses the qualitative relative *οἷον* predicatively with acc. *σε*. *Ὑπέλαβον* is 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὑπολαμβάνω*: Italian imperfect *credea* with finite *che tu fossi* becomes a single act of supposition with an accusative and its predicate.
- *Ὁπότ᾽ ἄρτι προπετῶς ἠρώτησά σε* ‘when just now I questioned you precipitately’ elides *ὁπότε*. *Ἄρτι* supplies the explicit nearness in time; *προπετῶς* interprets *subito* as rashness. *Feci il … dimando* becomes the single verb *ἠρώτησα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐρωτάω*) with a personal object.

> 79 Ma più è il tempo già che i piè mi cossi  
> 80 e ch’ io son stato così sottosopra,  
> 81 ch’ ei non starà piantato coi piè rossi:  

But longer is the time already, that I have baked my feet and stood inverted thus, than he shall stand planted with glowing feet:

> 79 „ Ἀλλ’ οὑμοὶ πόδες ἤδη πλείονα χρόνον  
> („ But already a longer time my feet)
>
> 80 „ Περιφλέγονται, κἀγὼ κεῖμ’ ἄνω κάτω,  
> („ Have been scorched all round, and I lie upside down,)
>
> 81 „ Ἤπερ ἐκεῖνος μενεῖ τοὺς πόδας ἔμφλοξ.  
> („ Than he shall remain with his feet ablaze.)

- *Ἀλλ’ οὑμοὶ πόδες* elides *ἀλλά* before *οὑμοί*, itself crasis of *οἱ ἐμοί* ‘my’. The feet, object of *cossi*, become the subject of *περιφλέγονται* (3rd pl. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *περιφλέγω*), ‘are being scorched all round’. Instead of *più è il tempo già che …*, *ἤδη πλείονα χρόνον* gives an accusative of duration: *πλείονα* is acc. masc. sg. comparative agreeing with *χρόνον*. Present tense with *ἤδη* includes the suffering still in progress.
- *Κἀγὼ κεῖμ’ ἄνω κάτω* ‘and I lie upside down’ coordinates a new subject by crasis (*καὶ ἐγώ*). *Κεῖμ᾽* is elided *κεῖμαι*, 1st sg. pres. ind., middle in form and stative in meaning; *ἄνω κάτω* renders *sottosopra* without a separate word for *così*.
- *Ἤπερ* ‘than’ introduces the second term of the comparison, with strengthening *-περ*. *Ἐκεῖνος μενεῖ* (3rd sg. contracted fut. act. ind. of *μένω*) renders *ei … starà*, leaving *piantato* unexpressed. *Τοὺς πόδας ἔμφλοξ* is literally ‘ablaze as to the feet’: *ἔμφλοξ* is predicate nom. sg. referring to *ἐκεῖνος*, not an adjective agreeing with *πόδας*. The accusative of respect replaces *coi piè rossi*, and fire replaces the description by colour.

> 82 chè dopo lui verrà, di più laid’ opra,  
> 83 di ver ponente un pastor senza legge,  
> 84 tal che convien che lui e me ricopra.  

for after him, from westward, there shall come a lawless Shepherd, of uglier deeds, fit to cover him and me.

> 82 „ Μετ’ αὐτὸν δ’ ἥξει ποιμὴν χείρων τὰς πράξεις,  
> („ And after him shall come a shepherd worse in his deeds,)
>
> 83 „ Ἀθέμιστος δυσμόθεν, ὃς ἡμᾶς ἄμφω  
> („ A lawless one from the west, who both of us)
>
> 84 „ Διαδέξεται. Νέος ἔσετ’ Ἰάσων,  
> („ Shall succeed. He shall be a new Jason,)

- *Μετ’ αὐτὸν δ’ ἥξει ποιμήν* ‘and after him a shepherd will come’ has successive elisions of *μετά* and *δέ*. *Μετά* with the accusative gives temporal succession; connective *δέ* is less expressly explanatory than *chè*. *Ἥξει* is 3rd sg. fut. act. ind. of *ἥκω*. Musurus brings *ποιμήν* forward from the next Italian line.
- *Χείρων τὰς πράξεις* ‘worse as to his actions’ replaces descriptive *di più laid’ opra* with a comparative nom. masc. sg. qualifying the shepherd and an acc. pl. of respect. *Ἀθέμιστος* ‘lawless’ agrees with *ποιμήν* across the line break. *Δυσμόθεν* renders *di ver ponente* in one adverb, its suffix *-θεν* marking motion from a place.
- *Ὃς ἡμᾶς ἄμφω διαδέξεται* ‘who will succeed us both’ replaces *tal che convien che lui e me ricopra* with a relative clause in the indicative. *Διαδέξεται* is 3rd sg. fut. mid. ind. of *διαδέχομαι*, governing acc. *ἡμᾶς* with dual *ἄμφω* in apposition. Succession is made explicit at the cost of the image in *ricopra* and the necessity in *convien*.
- The remainder of Greek line 84, *Νέος ἔσετ’ Ἰάσων* ‘he will be a new Jason’, already begins the next Italian sentence and has no counterpart in the English span. *Ἔσετ᾽* is elided poetic *ἔσεται*, the uncontracted 3rd sg. future of *εἰμί* (Attic *ἔσται*); *νέος* and *Ἰάσων* form the predicate nominative on either side of it.

## Lines 85–117 — The Rebuke of Avaricious Shepherds

> 85 Nuovo Jason sarà, di cui si legge  
> 86 ne' 'Maccabei'; e come a quel fu molle  
> 87 suo re, così fia a lui chi Francia regge."  

A new Jason will it be, of whom we read in Maccabees; and as to that *high priest* his king was pliant, so to this shall be he who governs France."

> 85 „ Οὗπερ ἐν Μακκαβαίοις γίνεται μνήμη.  
> („ Of whom mention is made in Maccabees.)
>
> 86 „ Ὡς δ’ ὁ βασιλεὺς μαλακὸς ἦν ἐκείνῳ,  
> („ And as the king was compliant to that one,)
>
> 87 „ Τοιοῦτος ἔσται τούτῳ Γαλλίας ἄναξ.”  
> („ So to this one shall be the king of France.”)

- The Greek excerpt begins with the relative *οὗπερ*, not with an equivalent of *Nuovo Jason sarà*. *Οὗπερ* is masculine genitive singular, the relative *οὗ* strengthened by *-περ*: ‘of whom indeed’. It belongs to *μνήμη*, ‘mention’. Where *di cui si legge* uses impersonal *si* with a verb of reading, *γίνεται μνήμη* has a nominative subject: ‘mention is made’, literally ‘mention comes about’ (*γίνεται*, 3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *γίγνομαι*, in the form *γίνομαι*). *Ἐν Μακκαβαίοις* renders *ne' Maccabei* with *ἐν* and the dative plural, without an article.
- *Ὡς δ’ … τοιοῦτος* answers *e come … così*: ‘and as … such’. Postpositive *δέ* is elided before *ὁ*. The predicative adjective *μαλακός*, literally ‘soft’, follows *molle* closely; *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*) presents as a state what Italian gives in the simple past *fu*. Greek says *ὁ βασιλεύς*, ‘the king’, without reproducing *suo*.
- *Ἐκείνῳ … τούτῳ*, ‘to that one … to this one’, are contrasting masculine datives, answering *a quel … a lui*; neither Greek nor Italian supplies a noun for the former. *Τοιοῦτος ἔσται* means ‘such will [he] be’ (*ἔσται*, 3rd sg. fut. ind. of *εἰμί*). The subject, held until the end, is poetic *Γαλλίας ἄναξ*, ‘lord of France’: a noun with a dependent genitive replaces the free relative *chi Francia regge*.

> 88 Io non so s'io mi fui qui troppo folle,  
> 89 ch' io pur risposi lui a questo metro :  

I know not if here I was too hardy, for I answered him in this strain:

> 88 Οὐκ οἶδ’, εἰ πάνυ προπετὴς ἐγενόμην·  
> (I know not whether I was altogether too bold;)
>
> 89 Ἀπεκρίθην γὰρ αὐτῷ τῷδε τῷ τρόπῳ·  
> (For I answered him in this very manner:)

- *Οὐκ οἶδ’, εἰ …*, ‘I do not know whether …’, closely follows *Io non so s’io …*. *Οἶδ’* is elided *οἶδα*, a perfect indicative, 1st singular, with present meaning; *εἰ* introduces an indirect yes-or-no question, not a condition. *Πάνυ προπετὴς ἐγενόμην* is literally ‘I proved very rash’: *πάνυ* intensifies without itself meaning ‘too’, *προπετής* is predicate nominative, and *ἐγενόμην* is the 1st sg. aor. mid. ind. of *γίγνομαι*. There is no separate *qui* or emphatic *io*.
- *Ἀπεκρίθην γὰρ αὐτῷ* takes *ch’* as explanatory: ‘for I answered him’. *Ἀπεκρίθην* is passive in aorist form (1st sg. ind. of *ἀποκρίνομαι*) but active in sense, with the person answered in the dative. *Τῷδε τῷ τρόπῳ*, ‘in this manner’, is a dative of manner: the demonstrative *τῷδε* points forward to the words that follow. There is no distinct equivalent of *pur*.

> 90 "Deh or mi di', quanto tesoro volle  
> 91 nostro Signore in prima da san Pietro,  
> 92 che gli ponesse le chiavi in balìa?  

"Ah! now tell me how much treasure our Lord required of St Peter, before he put the keys into his keeping?

> 90 “ Λέγε νῦν, ὁ Κύριος ἡμῶν τίν’ ἆρα  
> (“Tell me now, what, pray, did our Lord)
>
> 91 „ Μισθὸν ᾔτησε παρὰ τοῦ θείου Πέτρου,  
> („ Demand as payment from the divine Peter,)
>
> 92 „ Ὅτ’ ἔθετ’ ἐν ταῖς χερσὶν αὐτοῦ τὰς κλεῖδας ;  
> („ When He placed the keys in his hands?)

- *Λέγε νῦν*, ‘say now’, is a 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *λέγω* with an adverb; *Deh* and the *mi* of *mi di’* go unexpressed. After the command Greek poses the question with *τίν’ ἆρα μισθόν*, ‘what payment, then?’: *τίν’* is elided interrogative *τίνα*, accusative masculine singular agreeing with *μισθόν* across the line break; *ἆρα* is interrogative. Thus *quanto tesoro*, a question of quantity, becomes a question about a payment.
- *Ὁ Κύριος ἡμῶν*, ‘the Lord of us’, is the subject of *ᾔτησε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *αἰτέω*). The payment is accusative, the person from whom it is demanded *παρά* with the genitive, *παρὰ τοῦ θείου Πέτρου*; attributive *θείου*, ‘divine’, renders *san*.
- *Ὅτ’ ἔθετ’* expands to *ὅτε ἔθετο*, ‘when he placed’ (3rd sg. aor. mid. ind. of *τίθημι*). Instead of *in prima … che … ponesse*, with a preceding event and an imperfect subjunctive, Greek has a temporal clause with a past indicative. *Ἐν ταῖς χερσὶν αὐτοῦ τὰς κλεῖδας*, ‘the keys in his hands’, recasts *le chiavi in balìa* concretely: *χερσίν* is dative plural of *χείρ*, *κλεῖδας* accusative plural of *κλείς*.

> 93 Certo non chiese se non : 'viemmi retro.'  

Surely he demanded nought but ‘Follow me!'

> 93 „ Οὐκ ἄλλον δή, πλὴν “ ἀκολούθει μοι ” μόνον.  
> („ None other indeed, except “Follow me” alone.)

- *Οὐκ ἄλλον δή*, ‘no other payment, indeed’, leaves the verb understood; masculine accusative *ἄλλον* resumes *μισθόν*. Emphatic *δή* answers *Certo*. Greek doubles the restriction of *non … se non* with exceptive *πλήν* and adverbial *μόνον*, ‘except … alone’.
- *Ἀκολούθει μοι*, ‘follow me’, is a contracted 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *ἀκολουθέω* with its usual dative of the person followed. The verb itself conveys the relation expressed by *retro* in *viemmi retro*.

> 94 Nè Pier nè gli altri chiesero a Mattia  
> 95 oro od argento, quando fu sortito  
> 96 al loco che perdè l' anima ria.  

Nor did Peter, nor the others, ask of Matthias gold or silver, when he was chosen for the office which the guilty soul had lost.

> 94 „ Οὔτε Πέτρος οὔτ’ ἄλλοι παρὰ Ματθίου  
> („ Neither Peter nor the others from Matthias)
>
> 95 „ ᾜτησαν χρυσὸν οὔτ᾿ ἄργυρον, ὁπότε  
> („ Demanded gold or silver, when)
>
> 96 „ Ἔσχε τὴν θέσιν, ἣν εἶχεν ὁ Προδότης.  
> („ He received the office which the Traitor held.)

- *Οὔτε Πέτρος οὔτ’ ἄλλοι*, ‘neither Peter nor others’, reproduces the paired negatives of *Nè Pier nè gli altri*, but without the article of *gli altri*. *Οὔτ’* is elided *οὔτε*. The compound subject takes plural *ᾔτησαν* (3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *αἰτέω*); *παρὰ Ματθίου*, ‘from Matthias’, repeats the construction with *παρά* rather than the Italian *a Mattia*.
- *Χρυσὸν οὔτ’ ἄργυρον* gives two accusative objects, ‘gold nor silver’, continuing the negative coordination where Italian has *oro od argento*. *Ὁπότε ἔσχε τὴν θέσιν* means ‘when he obtained the position’: *ὁπότε* introduces a particular past event with the indicative, and *ἔσχε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. of *ἔχω*) makes receipt of the office the action, without translating the selection by lot in *fu sortito*.
- *Ἣν εἶχεν ὁ Προδότης*, ‘which the Traitor held’, contrasts imperfect *εἶχεν* with aorist *ἔσχε* of the same verb. The feminine accusative relative *ἥν* takes its gender from *θέσιν* and its case from *εἶχεν*. Musurus substitutes prior possession for *perdè*, loss, and an agent noun, *ὁ Προδότης*, for *l’anima ria*.

> 97 Però ti sta, chè tu se' ben punito ;  
> 98 e guarda ben la mal tolta moneta,  
> 99 ch' esser ti fece contra Carlo ardito.  

Therefore stay thou *here*, for thou art justly punished; and keep well the ill-got money, which against Charles made thee be bold.

> 97 „ Μέν᾿ ἐκεῖ λοιπόν· δικαίαν ποινὴν φέρε  
> („ Remain there then; bear a just punishment)
>
> 98 „ Νῦν, καὶ τήρει τὰ κακῶς θησαυρισθέντα,  
> („ Now, and guard your ill-gotten treasures,)
>
> 99 „ Ἅ σ᾿ ἐποίησε θρασὺν κατὰ Καρόλου.  
> („ Which made you bold against Charles.)

- *Μέν’ ἐκεῖ λοιπόν*, ‘remain there, then’, has elided *μένε* (2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *μένω*); *ἐκεῖ* supplies a place adverb, and inferential *λοιπόν* answers *Però*. The causal assertion *chè tu se’ ben punito* becomes a second command, *δικαίαν ποινὴν φέρε νῦν*, ‘bear a just punishment now’: *δικαίαν* qualifies the punishment rather than modifying the act of punishing.
- *Καὶ τήρει*, ‘and keep’, is the contracted present imperative of *τηρέω*, with no separate equivalent for *ben*. Its object *τὰ κακῶς θησαυρισθέντα*, ‘the things wrongfully amassed’, is a substantivized neuter plural aorist passive participle of *θησαυρίζω*: the singular *moneta* disappears, and the participle stresses accumulation rather than taking.
- *Ἅ σ’ ἐποίησε θρασὺν κατὰ Καρόλου*, ‘which made you bold against Charles’: neuter plural relative *ἅ* is nominative subject with a singular verb, regular Greek concord. *Σ’* is elided accusative *σε*; *θρασύν* is its predicate complement after *ἐποίησε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ποιέω*). Greek keeps the causative construction of *ti fece … ardito* but places hostile *κατά* with the genitive after the adjective.

> 100 E se non fosse che ancor lo mi vieta  
> 101 la riverenza delle somme chiavi,  
> 102 che tu tenesti nella vita lieta,  
> 103 i' userei parole ancor più gravi :  

And were it not that reverence for the Great Keys thou heldest in the glad life yet hinders me, I should use still heavier words:

> 100 „ Εἰ δὲ μή μ᾿ ἀνέστελλεν ὃ φέρω σέβας  
> („ And were I not restrained by the reverence I bear)
>
> 101 „ Πρὸς τὰς ἱερὰς κλεῖδας, ἅσπερ ἐκράτεις  
> („ Toward the sacred keys, which you held)
>
> 102 „ Ἐν τῷ γλυκερῷ βίῳ, δηκτικωτέροις  
> („ In the sweet life, even sharper)
>
> 103 „ Ἐχρησάμην ἂν λόγοις· καὶ γὰρ τὸν κόσμον  
> („ Words would I have used; for indeed the world)

- *Εἰ δὲ μή μ’ ἀνέστελλεν*, ‘but if it were not restraining me’, begins a contrary-to-fact condition: *μή* negatives the protasis, *μ’* is elided *με*, and *ἀνέστελλεν* is 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἀναστέλλω*. Greek dispenses with the introductory *se non fosse che* and with the forward-pointing object *lo* of *lo mi vieta*; *ancor* has no separate equivalent here.
- The delayed subject is *ὃ φέρω σέβας*, ‘the reverence which I bear’: the antecedent *σέβας* follows the relative clause. Although *σέβας* is nominative as subject of *ἀνέστελλεν*, *ὅ* is accusative as object of *φέρω*; the forms look alike in the neuter singular. *Πρὸς τὰς ἱερὰς κλεῖδας*, ‘towards the sacred keys’, specifies the direction of that reverence in place of *delle somme chiavi*.
- *Ἅσπερ ἐκράτεις*, ‘which you held’, has a strengthened feminine accusative plural relative and the 2nd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *κρατέω*, marking continued possession rather than the simple past *tenesti*. *Ἐν τῷ γλυκερῷ βίῳ* closely follows *nella vita lieta*, but poetic *γλυκερός* means ‘sweet’.
- *Δηκτικωτέροις ἐχρησάμην ἂν λόγοις*, ‘I would have used more biting words’, encloses the verb between adjective and noun. Both are dative plural because *χράομαι*, ‘use’, governs the dative. *Ἐχρησάμην* is 1st sg. aor. mid. ind.; with *ἄν* it gives the unreal consequence as a completed act, against Italian conditional *userei*. The comparative expresses *più gravi* as greater sharpness, without a separate *ancor*. Greek already starts the next explanation at the end of 103.

> 104 chè la vostra avarizia il mondo attrista,  
> 105 calcando i buoni e sollevando i pravi.  

for your avarice grieves the world, trampling on the good, and raising up the wicked.

> 104 „ Ἡ φλέγουσ᾿ ὑμᾶς λυπεῖ φιλαργυρία,  
> („ Is afflicted by the avarice that burns you,)
>
> 105 „ Καταπατοῦσα χρηστούς, ὑψοῦσα φαύλους.  
> („ Trampling down the worthy, exalting the wicked.)

- *Καὶ γὰρ τὸν κόσμον* in 103 leads into *ἡ φλέγουσ’ ὑμᾶς λυπεῖ φιλαργυρία*: ‘for indeed the love of money that burns you grieves the world’. *Τὸν κόσμον* is the anticipated object of contracted *λυπεῖ* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *λυπέω*). Instead of possessive *vostra*, Musurus inserts attributive *φλέγουσ’ ὑμᾶς*, ‘burning you’: elided *φλέγουσα* is the feminine nominative singular present active participle of *φλέγω*, between the article and its widely separated noun *φιλαργυρία*.
- *Καταπατοῦσα χρηστούς, ὑψοῦσα φαύλους* closely balances *calcando i buoni e sollevando i pravi*, without *e*. The two contracted present active participles (of *καταπατέω* and *ὑψόω*) agree with *φιλαργυρία* and express how it grieves the world. Their objects are masculine plural adjectives used substantivally without articles: ‘worthy people … base people’.

> 106 Di voi pastor s' accorse il Vangelista,  
> 107 quando colei, che siede sopra l' acque,  
> 108 puttaneggiar co' regi a lui fu vista ;  

Shepherds such as ye the Evangelist perceived, when she, that sitteth on the waters, was seen by him committing fornication with the kings;

> 106 „ Ὑμᾶς ἐνόει, ποιμένες, ὁ προγνώστης  
> („ Of you, O shepherds, was thinking the prophetic)
>
> 107 „ Εὐαγγελιστής, ἰδὼν τὴν καθημένην  
> („ Evangelist, when he saw her who sits)
>
> 108 „ Ἐφ᾿ ὑδάτων τε καὶ μετὰ βασιλέων  
> („ Upon the waters, and with kings)

- *Ὑμᾶς ἐνόει, ποιμένες, ὁ προγνώστης Εὐαγγελιστής* opens with the emphatic object ‘you’. *Ποιμένες* is a plural vocative in direct address; *ἐνόει* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *νοέω*) means ‘had in mind’, an interpretation of *di voi … s’accorse*. The delayed subject adds *προγνώστης*, literally ‘foreknower’, as a designation of the Evangelist.
- *Ἰδών*, nominative masculine singular second aorist active participle of *ὁράω*, means ‘on seeing’: the construction makes the Evangelist the active perceiver instead of making the woman the subject of *a lui fu vista*. *Τὴν καθημένην*, ‘the one sitting’, is the articular feminine accusative singular present participle of *κάθημαι*, object of *ἰδών*; it absorbs both *colei* and *che siede*. *Ἐφ’ ὑδάτων* is locative *ἐπί* with the genitive, its final vowel elided and its *π* aspirated before the rough breathing.
- *Τε καὶ μετὰ βασιλέων* leads into *πορνευομένην* in 109: she is seen both sitting upon waters and ‘consorting with kings’. *Μετά* with the genitive renders *co’*; present middle *πορνευομένην* (of *πορνεύομαι*) agrees with the woman in the accusative as a participle of the action perceived, where Italian uses *puttaneggiar*, an infinitive.

> 109 quella che con le sette teste nacque,  
> 110 e dalle dieci corna ebbe argomento,  
> 111 fin che virtute al suo marito piacque.  

she that was born with seven heads, and in her ten horns had a witness so long as virtue pleased her spouse.

> 109 „ Πορνευομένην, τὴν σὺν ἑπτὰ τεχθεῖσαν  
> („ Committing fornication, her who was born with seven)
>
> 110 „ Κεφαλαῖς, καὶ φέρουσαν κέρατα δέκα,  
> („ Heads, and bears ten horns,)
>
> 111 „ Ἕως ἀρετὴν ὁ σύζυγος ἠγάπα.  
> („ So long as her spouse loved virtue.)

- After *πορνευομένην*, which completes the preceding construction, *τὴν σὺν ἑπτὰ τεχθεῖσαν κεφαλαῖς* resumes the same accusative object in apposition: ‘the one born with seven heads’. The article and feminine accusative singular aorist passive participle *τεχθεῖσαν* (of *τίκτω*) replace demonstrative and finite relative clause, *quella che … nacque*. The participle separates indeclinable *ἑπτά* from *κεφαλαῖς*, dative plural governed by *σύν*.
- *Καὶ φέρουσαν κέρατα δέκα*, ‘and bearing ten horns’, coordinates a present active participle of *φέρω* with the aorist passive *τεχθεῖσαν*. *Κέρατα* is neuter accusative plural of *κέρας*, with indeclinable *δέκα*. Greek makes the horns the direct object of bearing; it does not reproduce the evidential relation in *dalle dieci corna ebbe argomento*.
- *Ἕως ἀρετὴν ὁ σύζυγος ἠγάπα*, ‘as long as the spouse loved virtue’, has temporal *ἕως* with the imperfect indicative for the duration of a past state. *Ἠγάπα* is 3rd sg. imperf. act. of *ἀγαπάω*. Musurus reverses the grammatical roles in *virtute al suo marito piacque*: *ἀρετήν* is object, *ὁ σύζυγος* subject; the definite article suffices without a possessive.

> 112 Fatto v' avete Dio d' oro e d' argento ;  

Ye have made you a god of gold and silver;

> 112 „ Θεὸν ἐκ χρυσοῦ ᾽ποιήσασθε κἀργύρου.  
> („ You have made yourselves a god of gold and silver.)

- *Θεὸν ἐκ χρυσοῦ ᾽ποιήσασθε κἀργύρου* puts the object ‘a god’ first. *᾽Ποιήσασθε* is 2nd pl. aor. mid. ind. of *ποιέω*, with the initial syllabic augment of *ἐποιήσασθε* suppressed after *χρυσοῦ* (prodelision). The middle conveys ‘made for yourselves’, answering *v’avete*, while the aorist replaces the Italian compound perfect.
- *Ἐκ* governs both genitives of material, ‘out of gold and silver’. The verb separates them; *κἀργύρου* is crasis of *καὶ ἀργύρου*. Italian repeats the preposition in *d’oro e d’argento*.

> 113 e che altro è da voi all' idolatre,  
> 114 se non ch' egli uno, e voi n' orate cento?  

and wherein do ye differ from the idolater, save that he worships one, and ye a hundred?

> 113 „ Ὑμεῖς τί διαφέρετ᾿ εἰδωλολάτρου,  
> („ How do you differ from an idolater,)
>
> 114 „ Πλὴν ὁ μὲν ἑνός, μυρίων δ᾿ ὑμεῖς λάτραι;  
> („ Save that he worships one, and you ten thousand?)

- *Ὑμεῖς τί διαφέρετ’ εἰδωλολάτρου*, ‘you, in what respect do you differ from an idolater?’, turns the impersonal comparison *che altro è da voi all’idolatre* into a personal verb. Emphatic nominative *ὑμεῖς* is subject; interrogative *τί* is an accusative of respect. *Διαφέρετ’* is elided *διαφέρετε* (2nd pl. pres. act. ind. of *διαφέρω*), with a genitive of comparison, *εἰδωλολάτρου*.
- After exceptive *πλήν*, *ὁ μὲν ἑνός, μυρίων δ’ ὑμεῖς λάτραι* shifts from the verb *orate* to the agent noun *λάτραι*, ‘worshippers’, with genitives of what is worshipped. The first half requires a singular predicate understood from the plural in the second: ‘he [a worshipper] of one, but you worshippers of ten thousand’. *Ὁ μέν* is pronominal, paired with *δέ* (elided *δ’*); *ἑνός* is the genitive of *εἷς*, and *μυρίων* increases *cento* to ‘ten thousand’. Neither half expresses the copula.

> 115 Ahi, Constantin, di quanto mal fu matre,  
> 116 non la tua conversion, ma quella dote  
> 117 che da te prese il primo ricco patre!"  

Ah Constantine! to how much ill gave birth, not thy conversion, but that dower which the first rich Father took from thee!"

> 115 „ Αἴ, Κωνσταντῖνε, πόσων κακῶν αἰτία,  
> („ Ah, Constantine, of how many evils the cause,)
>
> 116 „ Οὐχ ἡ σὴ προσήλυσις, ἀλλ’ ἡ δοθεῖσα  
> („ Not your conversion, but that which was given)
>
> 117 „ Ὑπὸ σοῦ προὶξ τῷ πρώτῳ πατρὶ πλουσίῳ!”  
> („ By you as a dowry to the first rich father!”)

- *Αἴ, Κωνσταντῖνε* matches interjection and vocative. *Πόσων κακῶν αἰτία*, ‘cause of how many evils’, replaces *di quanto mal fu matre*: exclamatory *πόσων* agrees with substantival neuter plural *κακῶν*, both genitive with the predicate noun *αἰτία*. Greek drops the maternal metaphor and leaves the past copula understood.
- *Οὐχ ἡ σὴ προσήλυσις, ἀλλ’ ἡ δοθεῖσα … προίξ* delays the contrasted subjects: ‘not your conversion, but the dowry given …’. *Οὐχ* is the form of *οὐ* before a rough-breathed vowel; *ἀλλ’* is elided *ἀλλά*. In *ἡ σὴ προσήλυσις* the possessive adjective is attributive; in the second phrase the article precedes *δοθεῖσα*, feminine nominative singular aorist passive participle of *δίδωμι*, agreeing with *προίξ*, ‘dowry’.
- *Ὑπὸ σοῦ* names the agent of the passive participle, *τῷ πρώτῳ πατρὶ πλουσίῳ* the recipient in the dative singular. Where *che da te prese il primo ricco patre* makes the Father the subject of ‘took’ and Constantine the source, Greek makes Constantine the giver and the Father the indirect object. The participle and its complements thus do the work of the Italian relative clause without a finite verb.

## Lines 118–133 — The Kicking Feet and the Gentle Ascent

> 118 E mentre io gli cantava cotai note,  
> 119 o ira o coscienza che il mordesse,  
> 120 forte springava con ambo le piote.  

And whilst I sung these notes to him, whether it was rage or conscience gnawed him, he violently sprawled with both his feet.

> 118 Ἐν ᾧ δ’ ἔψαλλον αὐτῷ τοὺς τόνους τούτους,  
> (And while I sang to him these strains,)
>
> 119 Ἢ δι᾿ ὀργήν, ἢ τύψιν τοῦ συνειδότος,  
> (Either through anger or the sting of conscience,)
>
> 120 Ἐστροφάλιζε σφοδρῶς τοὺς πόδας ἄμφω.  
> (He tossed both his feet violently.)

- *Ἐν ᾧ δ’ ἔψαλλον* renders *E mentre io gli cantava*: *ἐν ᾧ*, literally ‘in which [time]’, introduces an action in progress, and the connective *δέ* is postpositive and elided before *ἔψαλλον*. The latter is the augmented imperfect active indicative of *ψάλλω*, here 1st singular; its ending does the work of *io*. *Αὐτῷ* is the dative recipient corresponding to *gli*.
- *Τοὺς τόνους τούτους* ‘these strains’ gives *cotai note* a more directly demonstrative sense: ‘these’ rather than ‘such’. *Τούτους* is accusative masculine plural of *οὗτος*, in the regular demonstrative position outside the article–noun group.
- *Ἢ δι᾿ ὀργήν, ἢ τύψιν τοῦ συνειδότος* turns *o ira o coscienza che il mordesse* into two alternative causes. *Διά* (elided *δι᾿*) takes the accusative and is understood again after the second *ἤ*. Neither the object *il* nor the imperfect subjunctive *mordesse* has a separate counterpart: the ‘bite’ becomes *τύψιν*, accusative singular of *τύψις*, ‘a striking, a prick’, attached specifically to conscience. *Τοῦ συνειδότος* is the substantivized neuter genitive singular of the perfect active participle of *σύνοιδα*: literally ‘of that which knows with oneself’, hence ‘of conscience’.
- *Ἐστροφάλιζε σφοδρῶς* ‘he kept tossing violently’ answers to *forte springava*, with the adverb now following the verb. *Ἐστροφάλιζε* is 3rd singular imperfect active indicative of *στροφαλίζω*, with syllabic augment. Where *con ambo le piote* gives the limbs with which he kicks, Greek makes them the direct object, *τοὺς πόδας ἄμφω*. *Πόδας* is accusative plural of *πούς*; *ἄμφω* ‘both’ is the nominative/accusative dual form, here accompanying a plural noun rather than a dual.

> 121 Io credo ben che al mio duca piacesse,  
> 122 con sì contenta labbia sempre attese  
> 123 lo suon delle parole vere espresse.  

And indeed I think it pleased my Guide, with so satisfied a look did he keep listening to the sound of the true words uttered.

> 121 Οὑμὸς δ’ Ἡγήτωρ ἠρέσθη μοι, νομίζω·  
> (And my Guide was pleased with me, I think;)
>
> 122 Ἠκροᾶτο γὰρ ᾀεὶ κεχαρισμένως  
> (For he listened ever with delight)
>
> 123 Οὓς εἶπον διαῤῥήδην ἀληθεῖς λόγους.  
> (To the true words I spoke outright.)

- *Οὑμὸς δ’ Ἡγήτωρ* ‘and my Guide’ makes *al mio duca* the nominative subject. *Οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, with the rough breathing of the article retained; *δ’* is elided *δέ*. *Ἠρέσθη* is 3rd singular aorist passive indicative of *ἀρέσκω*, ‘was pleased’, with temporal augment. *Μοι* is the dative of the person with whom he was pleased: the Greek supplies ‘with me’ where the Italian leaves what pleased him to be inferred.
- *Νομίζω* ‘I think’ (1st singular present active indicative) is postponed as a parenthesis. Thus *Io credo ben che … piacesse*, with a subordinate imperfect subjunctive, becomes an indicative assertion qualified afterwards; *ben* has no separate equivalent.
- *Ἠκροᾶτο γὰρ ᾀεὶ κεχαρισμένως* makes the next clause explicitly a reason by postpositive *γάρ*, ‘for’. *Ἠκροᾶτο* is 3rd singular imperfect middle indicative of the deponent *ἀκροάομαι*, with temporal augment and contraction. The imperfect and *ᾀεί* (a spelling of *ἀεί*, ‘continually’) render *sempre attese*. *Κεχαρισμένως* is an adverb formed on *κεχαρισμένος*, the reduplicated perfect middle/passive participle of *χαρίζομαι*: delight becomes the manner of listening, and neither the visible *labbia* nor the degree *sì* is expressed.
- *Οὓς εἶπον διαῤῥήδην ἀληθεῖς λόγους* places the relative *οὕς* before its noun *λόγους*, drawing the noun into the relative clause: ‘the true words which I spoke outright’. *Οὕς* is accusative masculine plural, object of *εἶπον* (1st singular second aorist active indicative of *λέγω*); *ἀληθεῖς* agrees with *λόγους*. The accusative is thus natural **inside** the relative, though *ἀκροάομαι* ordinarily takes a genitive of what is heard. *Lo suon* drops out, and *vere espresse* is redistributed between the adjective ‘true’, the finite ‘I spoke’, and *διαῤῥήδην* ‘expressly, outright’, with the older notation of smooth and rough breathings on doubled rho.

> 124 Però con ambo le braccia mi prese,  
> 125 e poi che tutto su mi s' ebbe al petto,  
> 126 rimontò per la via onde discese;  

Therefore with both his arms he took me; and, when he had me quite upon his breast, remounted by the path where he had descended;

> 124 Ἐν βραχίοσι δ’ αὑτοῦ παραλαβών με,  
> (Then taking me up in his arms,)
>
> 125 Ἐπὶ τοῦ στήθους ὕψωσέ με, καὶ πάλιν  
> (He lifted me upon his breast, and back again)
>
> 126 Ἀνήγαγ’ εἰς τὴν ὁδόν, ὅθεν κατέβη·  
> (Carried me up to the path whence he had come down;)

- *Ἐν βραχίοσι δ’ αὑτοῦ παραλαβών με* ‘and taking me up in his arms’ replaces inferential *Però* with the weaker connective *δέ*, elided before *αὑτοῦ*. *Ἐν* with *βραχίοσι* (dative plural of *βραχίων*) describes where he takes the speaker; *ambo* is not separately expressed. The rough breathing distinguishes reflexive *αὑτοῦ* (contracted for *ἑαυτοῦ*, ‘of himself’) from *αὐτοῦ*.
- *Παραλαβών* is the nominative masculine singular second aorist active participle of *παραλαμβάνω*, with enclitic *με* as object. Italian finite *mi prese* becomes a preliminary action dependent on *ὕψωσέ με*, while temporal *poi che … s’ ebbe* becomes that main verb: ‘having taken me up, he lifted me’. *Ὕψωσε* is 3rd singular aorist active indicative of *ὑψόω*; the extra final accent in *ὕψωσέ με* is due to the enclitic. *Ἐπὶ τοῦ στήθους* gives the position ‘upon the breast’ with the genitive singular of *στῆθος*; the lifting conveys *su*, but *tutto* is not expressed.
- *Καὶ πάλιν ἀνήγαγ’* ‘and he brought [me] up again’ renders *rimontò* transitively, carrying forward the object *με*. *Ἀνήγαγ’* is elided *ἀνήγαγε*, 3rd singular second aorist active indicative of *ἀνάγω*, with the augment internal to the compound. *Εἰς τὴν ὁδόν* gives a destination, ‘to the path’, rather than the route *per la via*. *Ὅθεν* ‘whence’ renders *onde*; *κατέβη* is 3rd singular root aorist active indicative of *καταβαίνω*, the descent understood as prior without a Greek pluperfect.

> 127 nè si stancò d' avermi a sè distretto,  
> 128 sì mi portò sopra il colmo dell' arco,  
> 129 che dal quarto al quinto argine è tragetto.  

nor did he weary in holding me clasped to him, till he bore me away to the summit of the arch which is a crossway from the fourth to the fifth rampart.

> 127 Οὐδ’ ἐπαύσατο φέρων μ’ ἐν ταῖς ἀγκάλαις,  
> (Nor did he cease carrying me in his arms,)
>
> 128 Ἕως ἀφίκετ’ ἐπ’ ἄκρας τῆς γεφύρας  
> (Until he reached the summit of the bridge)
>
> 129 Ἐκ περιβόλου τετάρτου πρὸς τὸν πέμπτον.  
> (From the fourth enclosure to the fifth.)

- *Οὐδ’ ἐπαύσατο* ‘nor did he stop’ renders *nè si stancò* by cessation rather than weariness. *Οὐδ’* is elided *οὐδέ*; *ἐπαύσατο* is 3rd singular aorist middle indicative of *παύω*. The middle *παύομαι* takes a supplementary participle for the activity discontinued: *φέρων* (nominative masculine singular present active of *φέρω*) agrees with its subject and presents the carrying as continuous. *Μ’* is elided *με*; *ἐν ταῖς ἀγκάλαις* ‘in his arms’ gives a concrete equivalent for *a sè distretto*. The carrying of *mi portò* is thus already expressed before line 128.
- *Ἕως ἀφίκετ’* makes *sì mi portò* an explicit temporal limit, ‘until he arrived’. For an endpoint actually reached in the past, *ἕως* takes the indicative without *ἄν*. *Ἀφίκετ’* is elided *ἀφίκετο*, 3rd singular second aorist middle indicative of the deponent *ἀφικνέομαι*; unlike *portò*, it has no object.
- *Ἐπ’ ἄκρας τῆς γεφύρας* ‘at the highest point of the bridge’ uses elided *ἐπί* with the genitive. *Ἄκρας*, genitive feminine singular of *ἄκρος*, stands outside the article–noun group and designates the extreme part of the whole. Thus *colmo* becomes an adjective and *arco* becomes the bridge itself.
- *Ἐκ περιβόλου τετάρτου πρὸς τὸν πέμπτον* preserves the direction of *dal quarto al quinto argine* with *ἐκ* plus genitive of origin and *πρός* plus accusative of goal. *Περίβολος* denotes an enclosing circuit or wall; the masculine noun is understood after *τὸν πέμπτον*. The phrases follow *γεφύρας* without any equivalent of the finite relative *che … è tragetto*; the bridge’s function as a crossing is left implicit.

> 130 Quivi soavemente spose il carco,  
> 131 soave per lo scoglio sconcio ed erto,  
> 132 che sarebbe alle capre duro varco;  

Here he placidly set down the burden [placidly] on the rough steep cliff, which to the goats would be a painful passage;

> 130 Ἁβρῶς ἀπέθετ’ ἐκεῖ τὸ γλυκὺ βάρος  
> (Gently he set down there his sweet burden)
>
> 131 Ἐπὶ σπιλάδος ἀγρίας καὶ κρημνώδους,  
> (Upon a wild and craggy rock,)
>
> 132 Ἥτις ἄβατος ἂν ἦν καὐτοῖς τοῖς τράγοις·  
> (Which would have been impassable even for goats;)

- *Ἁβρῶς ἀπέθετ’ ἐκεῖ* ‘gently he set [it] down there’ puts the adverb corresponding to *soavemente* first and shifts *Quivi* after the verb. *Ἀπέθετ’* is elided *ἀπέθετο*, 3rd singular second aorist middle indicative of *ἀποτίθημι*, with internal augment; the middle is apt for putting down what one has been carrying.
- *Τὸ γλυκὺ βάρος* ‘the sweet burden’ makes *soave* an adjective belonging to *carco*, rather than a second adverb modifying the setting down, as in the bracketed “[placidly]”. *Γλυκύ* is the accusative neuter singular of *γλυκύς*, agreeing with *βάρος*. *Ἐπὶ σπιλάδος* ‘upon a crag’ gives the place of deposit with the genitive singular of *σπιλάς*, in place of *per lo scoglio*; *ἀγρίας* ‘wild, rugged’ and *κρημνώδους* ‘precipitous’ are both genitive feminine singular, the latter from the two-ending adjective *κρημνώδης*.
- *Ἥτις* (nominative feminine singular of *ὅστις*) takes up *σπιλάδος* as the subject of a characterizing relative. *Ἄβατος* ‘impassable’, a two-ending adjective here feminine, replaces the predicate noun phrase *duro varco*: difficulty becomes impossibility. *Ἂν ἦν* combines modal *ἄν* with the imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*, 3rd singular, giving the hypothetical consequence ‘would be’ or ‘would have been’, rather than using a potential optative.
- *Καὐτοῖς* is crasis of *καὶ αὐτοῖς*: *καί* is ascensive, ‘even’, and intensive *αὐτοῖς* agrees with *τοῖς τράγοις* in the dative masculine plural, ‘even for the goats themselves’. This retains the dative relation of *alle capre* but adds emphasis and uses the specifically male *τράγος*.

> 133 indi un altro vallon mi fu scoperto.  

thence another valley was discovered to me.

> 133 Ἐκεῖθεν δ’ αὐλῶν’ ἀνεκάλυπτον ἄλλον.  
> (And from there I discovered another valley.)

- *Ἐκεῖθεν δ’* renders *indi* by a directional adverb, ‘from there’, with connective *δέ* elided before *αὐλῶν’*. The latter is elided *αὐλῶνα*, accusative singular of *αὐλών*, ‘a glen, a valley’; *ἄλλον* agrees with it across the intervening verb. The bare noun with *ἄλλον* suffices for *un altro vallon*.
- *Ἀνεκάλυπτον* is the internally augmented imperfect active indicative of *ἀνακαλύπτω*, here 1st singular (formally also 3rd plural). Italian *mi fu scoperto* presents the valley as subject of a completed passive event and the speaker as recipient; Greek makes the speaker the subject and the valley the object, with the imperfect presenting the discovery in progress.

## Summary — From Clause to Participle

Musurus often preserves Dante’s emphasis while changing the construction that carries it. The doubled *ὦ* keeps the two opening cries, and the repeated *οὐκ εἴμ᾽ ἐκεῖνος* follows *non son colui* word for word. More often, a Greek participle takes the place of an Italian clause. In *τὴν καθημένην* ‘the one sitting’, the article and participle do the work of both *colei* and *che siede*. Elsewhere the change affects which action receives the emphasis: *στραφέντες* ‘having turned’ subordinates *volgemmo* to the descent, whereas *νοῶν … σιωπῶντα* ‘understanding me when silent’ shifts attention from *quel che si tace*, the thing left unsaid, to the silent speaker.

Greek case constructions let Musurus retain a distinction without retaining its wording. He follows *a tutti* closely with possessive *πᾶσιν*, but recasts *coi piè rossi* as *τοὺς πόδας ἔμφλοξ* ‘ablaze as to the feet’: the adjective describes the person, and the accusative limits it to the part affected. Particles likewise mark some relations more explicitly than others. *Οὕτω … ὥστε* closely matches the progression from degree to result in *sì … che*; recurrent *δέ*, by contrast, can leave the inference in *per che* or *però* unstated. A change of verbal form can be more decisive: *ἠχησάτω* ‘let it sound’ turns impersonal necessity into a command, while *ἐβυθίσθητε* ‘you were plunged’ gives an event in place of the state expressed by *state*.

Musurus’s less literal choices often show how he construes a particular word. He reads abstract *bontate* personally in *τἀγαθοῖς μόνοις* ‘to the good alone’, and takes *soave* with *carco* in *τὸ γλυκὺ βάρος* ‘the sweet burden’, rather than as a second description of how the burden is set down. Sometimes he exchanges an image for its implication: *διαδέξεται* ‘will succeed’ makes the succession in *ricopra* explicit but loses the physical sense of covering. The reverse happens with *vostra avarizia*: *φλέγουσ᾽ ὑμᾶς* ‘burning you’ turns a possessive into an action and brings the canto’s language of flame into the rebuke itself.
