# Inferno 5

## Lines 1–24 — Minos Judges the Souls and Challenges the Traveler

> 1 Così discesi del cerchio primaio  
> 2 giù nel secondo, che men loco cinghia,  
> 3 e tanto più dolor, che pugne a guaio.  

Thus I descended from the first circle down into the second, which encompasses less space, and so much greater pain, that it stings to wailing.

> 1 Οὕτως ἐκ κύκλου κατέβημεν τοῦ πρώτου  
> (Thus we descended from the first circle)
>
> 2 Εἰς τὸν δεύτερον, ἔκτασιν μὲν ἐλάττω,  
> (Into the second, smaller in extent,)
>
> 3 Ἀλλὰ πλείονα περιέχοντα γόον.  
> (But containing greater lamentation.)

- *Οὕτως* answers to initial *Così*, but *κατέβημεν* is 1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *καταβαίνω*: ‘we descended’, not the singular *discesi*. The augment follows the preverb. The descent expressed separately by *giù* is already in the verb.
- *ἐκ κύκλου … τοῦ πρώτου* ‘out of the circle, the first’ has the genitive after *ἐκ*; the verb intervenes between the noun and its identifying ordinal. *Εἰς τὸν δεύτερον* balances it with *εἰς* and the accusative of destination, leaving *κύκλον* understood just as *nel secondo* leaves *cerchio* understood.
- For the relative clause *che men loco cinghia*, Greek attaches *περιέχοντα* (pres. act. participle of *περιέχω*, acc. masc. sg.) to *τὸν δεύτερον*. The participle comes late and can govern both *ἔκτασιν … ἐλάττω* ‘a smaller extent’ and *πλείονα … γόον* ‘greater lamentation’. *ἐλάττω* is the contracted acc. fem. sg. of the Attic comparative *ἐλάττων*; *πλείονα* is acc. masc. sg. of *πλείων*.
- *μέν … ἀλλά* sets the two accusatives in contrast where Italian has *e tanto più*. *γόον* (acc. sg. of *γόος*) takes up *guaio*, the audible result of the pain, rather than *dolor* itself. Neither *tanto* nor the clause *che pugne a guaio* has a separate equivalent.

> 4 Stavvi Minos orribilmente, e ringhia ;  
> 5 esamina le colpe nell' entrata,  
> 6 giudica e manda, secondo che avvinghia.  

There Minos sits horrific, and grins: examines the crimes upon the entrance; judges, and sends according as he girds himself.

> 4 Ἵστατ’ ἐκεῖ φρικώδης καὶ βρέμων Μίνως·  
> (There stands dreadful and roaring Minos;)
>
> 5 Εἰς τὴν εἴσοδον τὰ πταίσματ’ ἐξετάζει,  
> (At the entrance he examines the offences,)
>
> 6 Δικάζει, πέμπει, καθὰ περιειλεῖται.  
> (Judges, and sends, according as he coils himself.)

- *Ἵστατ’ ἐκεῖ* ‘he stands there’ renders both elements of *Stavvi*: *ἵστατ’* is elided *ἵσταται*, 3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *ἵστημι*, intransitive. *Μίνως*, near the beginning in Italian, comes last in the Greek line. *φρικώδης* is a predicative nom. masc. sg. adjective, not an adverb like *orribilmente*; *βρέμων* ‘roaring’ (pres. act. participle of *βρέμω*, nom. masc. sg.) makes *ringhia* an accompanying action rather than a second finite verb.
- *Εἰς τὴν εἴσοδον* comes first where *nell’ entrata* comes last. With *ἐξετάζει* ‘he examines’, *εἰς* + acc. has the force of location at the entrance rather than motion into it; classical prose could use *ἐν* + dat. *τὰ πταίσματ’* is *τὰ πταίσματα* (acc. neut. pl. of *πταῖσμα*), ‘the missteps, offences’, elided before *ἐξετάζει*.
- *Δικάζει, πέμπει* retains the two 3rd sg. present active indicatives of *giudica e manda* but drops the conjunction. *καθά* ‘according as’ introduces *περιειλεῖται* (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *περιειλέω*). The middle supplies the self-involvement that *avvinghia* leaves implicit: ‘according as he coils himself’.

> 7 Dico, che quando l' anima mal nata  
> 8 li vien dinanzi, tutta si confessa ;  

I say, that when the ill-born spirit comes before him, it confesses all;

> 7 Ὅταν γὰρ ἐναγής τις ψυχὴ πρὸς τοῦτον  
> (For whenever an accursed soul before him)
>
> 8 Παρουσιασθῇ, πᾶσ’ ἐξομολογεῖται·  
> (Presents herself, she confesses wholly;)

- There is no reporting verb or equivalent of *Dico, che*. Postpositive *γάρ* links the explanation directly to the preceding verbs. *Ὅταν* (*ὅτε* with *ἄν*) and the subjunctive give *quando … vien* an explicitly recurrent sense, ‘whenever …’.
- *ἐναγής τις ψυχή* is ‘a certain accursed soul’: the indefinite *τις* replaces the generic article of *l’ anima*, and *ἐναγής* expresses the judgment in *mal nata* without an image of birth. *πρὸς τοῦτον* (acc. masc. sg. of *οὗτος*) and *παρουσιασθῇ* (3rd sg. aor. pass. subj. of *παρουσιάζω*) recast *li vien dinanzi* as ‘is presented before him’. The passive form has the contextual force ‘presents herself’; the aorist subjunctive has no augment.
- *πᾶσ’* is elided *πᾶσα*, nom. fem. sg. agreeing with *ψυχή*, not acc. neut. pl. ‘all things’. Like *tutta* it characterizes the confessing subject: ‘wholly’. *ἐξομολογεῖται* is the 3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *ἐξομολογέομαι* ‘confess’, corresponding to *si confessa*.

> 9 e quel conoscitor delle peccata  
> 10 vede qual loco d' inferno è da essa;  

and that sin-discerner sees what place in hell is for it,

> 9 Αὐτὸς δὲ γνώστης ἁμαρτημάτων πάντων,  
> (And he, knower of all sins,)
>
> 10 ᾍδου βλέπει τί μέρος ἑκάστῃ πρέπει.  
> (Sees what part of Hell befits each one.)

- *Αὐτὸς δέ* ‘and he, for his part’ marks the change of subject more distinctly than *e quel*. The anarthrous *γνώστης* ‘knower’ stands in apposition to *αὐτός*; the objective genitive *ἁμαρτημάτων πάντων* renders *delle peccata* and adds *πάντων* ‘all’ (gen. neut. pl.).
- *βλέπει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *βλέπω*) introduces an indirect question retaining the interrogative *τί*. *ᾍδου* (gen. sg. of *ᾍδης*) belongs with *μέρος* ‘part’ but is brought out before *βλέπει*: ‘of Hades he sees what part befits each’. *τί μέρος* is neut. nom. sg., the subject of *πρέπει*; *ἑκάστῃ* is the dat. fem. sg. of *ἕκαστος*, with *ψυχῇ* understood. Thus *qual loco … è da essa* becomes a construction of suitability, with distributive ‘each’ in place of the pronoun.

> 11 cignesi colla coda tante volte,  
> 12 quantunque gradi vuol che giù sia messa.  

and with his tail makes as many circles round himself as the degrees he will have it to descend.

> 11 Τοσάκις δὲ τὴν οὐρὰν συμπεριστρέφει,  
> (And so many times he winds his tail round himself,)
>
> 12 Ὅσους ἂν βαθμοὺς κάτω πεμφθῆναι θέλῃ.  
> (As many steps downward as he wishes her to be sent.)

- *Τοσάκις δέ* ‘and so many times’ brings *tante volte* to the front in anticipation of *ὅσους*. *τὴν οὐράν* ‘the tail’ is the direct object of *συμπεριστρέφει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *συμπεριστρέφω*), not an instrumental phrase like *colla coda*. The compound expresses winding about; there is no separate reflexive corresponding to *-si* in *cignesi*.
- *Ὅσους ἂν βαθμούς* ‘however many steps’ answers the adverb of frequency with a relative adjective of quantity, acc. masc. pl. agreeing with *βαθμούς*. The accusative measures the extent of descent. *ἄν* belongs with *θέλῃ* (3rd sg. pres. act. subj. of *θέλω*) in the indefinite relative clause.
- *κάτω πεμφθῆναι θέλῃ* ‘he wishes [her] to be sent down’ changes *vuol che … sia messa* from a finite complement to an infinitive governed by the verb of wishing. *πεμφθῆναι* is the aor. pass. inf. of *πέμπω*, recalling *πέμπει* in line 6; its understood subject is the soul, not the subject of *θέλῃ*.

> 13 Sempre dinanzi a lui ne stanno molte :  
> 14 vanno a vicenda ciascuna al giudizio ;  
> 15 dicono e odono, e poi son giù volte.  

Always before him stands a crowd of them; they go each in its turn to judgment; they tell, and hear; and then are whirled down.

> 13 ᾈεὶ παρίσταντ’ ἔμπροσθεν αὐτοῦ πλεῖσται  
> (Always very many stand before him,)
>
> 14 Ἀμοιβαδὸν ἑκάστη τοῦ δικασθῆναι.  
> (Each in turn to be judged.)
>
> 15 Λέγουσιν, ἀκούουσι, κρημνίζοντ᾽ εἶτα.  
> (They speak, they hear, and then are hurled down.)

- *ᾈεὶ παρίσταντ’* ‘always they stand by’ renders *Sempre … ne stanno*. *παρίσταντ’* is elided *παρίστανται*, 3rd pl. pres. mid. ind. of *παρίστημι*, intransitive. *ἔμπροσθεν* governs the genitive *αὐτοῦ* in *dinanzi a lui*. The subject comes last: *πλεῖσται* (nom. fem. pl., superlative of *πολύς*) means ‘very many [souls]’, strengthening *molte* without separately rendering partitive *ne*.
- *Ἀμοιβαδόν* ‘by turns’ renders *a vicenda*, and distributive *ἑκάστη* (nom. fem. sg.) picks out individuals from the plural subject. Greek supplies no second finite verb for *vanno*. *τοῦ δικασθῆναι* is a genitive articular infinitive of purpose: not literally ‘to judgment’ but ‘in order to be judged’. *δικασθῆναι* is aor. pass. inf. of *δικάζω*.
- *Λέγουσιν, ἀκούουσι* are 3rd pl. pres. act. indicatives of *λέγω* and *ἀκούω*; the first has movable *-ν* before a vowel. Greek drops the conjunctions of *dicono e odono, e poi* and puts *εἶτα* ‘then’ last. *κρημνίζοντ’* is elided *κρημνίζονται* (3rd pl. pres. pass. ind. of *κρημνίζω*): ‘are flung headlong’ concentrates *son giù volte* in one verb.

> 16 "O tu, che vieni al doloroso ospizio,"  

"O thou who comest to the abode of pain!"

> 16 “Ὦ σύ, τί ζητεῖς ἐν τῷδε τῷ κλαυθμῶνι;”  
> (“O you, what do you seek in this place of weeping?”)

- *Ὦ σύ* keeps the direct address *O tu*, with the nominative pronoun in a vocative function. But *τί ζητεῖς* ‘what do you seek?’ (interrogative acc. neut. sg. and 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ζητέω*) replaces the relative clause *che vieni* with a direct question.
- *ἐν τῷδε τῷ κλαυθμῶνι* ‘in this place of weeping’ makes *al doloroso ospizio* a location rather than a destination. *τῷδε* (dat. masc. sg. of *ὅδε*) points to the immediate place and stands outside the article–noun group in normal demonstrative position. *κλαυθμῶνι* (dat. sg. of *κλαυθμών*) evokes weeping instead of reproducing a noun for lodging modified by *doloroso*.

> 17 disse Minos a me, quando mi vide,  
> 18 lasciando l' atto di cotanto ufizio,  

said Minos to me, when he saw me leaving the act of that great office;

> 17 Εἶπέ μοι Μίνως, ὡς εἶδέ μ᾽, ἀνακόπτων  
> (Said Minos to me, when he saw me, interrupting)
>
> 18 Τὴν σειρὰν τηλικαύτης ὑπηρεσίας.  
> (The course of so great an office.)

- *Εἶπέ μοι Μίνως* keeps the verb first but puts the enclitic dative *μοι* before the subject, where Italian has *Minos a me*. *εἶπε* is the 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. used with *λέγω*; its additional accent here is due to the enclitic. Temporal *ὡς* renders *quando*; *εἶδέ μ’* contains the 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω* and elided accusative *με*.
- *ἀνακόπτων* ‘interrupting’ is a pres. act. participle of *ἀνακόπτω*, nom. masc. sg. Its case attaches *lasciando* unambiguously to Minos, not to the object *μ’*. Its object *τὴν σειράν* ‘the sequence, course’ recasts *l’ atto*: it is the continuity of the work that is interrupted. *τηλικαύτης ὑπηρεσίας* ‘of so great a service’ is gen. fem. sg., with the correlative adjective *τηλικοῦτος* rendering *cotanto*.

> 19 "guarda com' entri, e di cui tu ti fide ;  
> 20 non t' inganni l' ampiezza dell' entrare."  

"look how thou enterest, and in whom thou trustest; let not the wideness of the entrancy deceive thee."

> 19 “Βλέπ᾽ ἐπὶ τίνι θαῤῥεῖς, καὶ πῶς ἐμβαίνεις·  
> (“Look to whom you trust, and how you enter;)
>
> 20 „Μή σ᾽ ἀπατήσῃ τὸ πλάτος τῆς εἰσόδου.”  
> („Let not the width of the entrance deceive you.”)

- *Βλέπ’* is *βλέπε*, 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *βλέπω*, elided before *ἐπί*: ‘look to, consider’. Musurus reverses the two indirect questions governed by *guarda*. First comes *ἐπὶ τίνι θαῤῥεῖς* ‘on whom do you rely’, with *ἐπί* + interrogative dat. sg. rather than the construction *di cui*. *θαῤῥεῖς* is the contracted 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *θαρρέω* (Attic beside *θαρσέω*); the opposed breathings are a traditional spelling of medial double rho. *καὶ πῶς ἐμβαίνεις* ‘and how you enter’ then renders *com’ entri*, with *ἐμβαίνεις* the 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἐμβαίνω*.
- *Μή σ’ ἀπατήσῃ* is ‘lest it deceive you’ or ‘let it not deceive you’: *μή* with the 3rd sg. aor. act. subj. of *ἀπατάω* gives the warning corresponding to *non t’ inganni*. *σ’* is elided acc. *σε*. As in Italian the subject follows the verb: *τὸ πλάτος* (nom. neut. sg.) ‘the width’. Greek replaces the substantivized infinitive *dell’ entrare* with the genitive of a noun, *τῆς εἰσόδου* ‘of the entrance’.

> 21 E il duca mio a lui : "Perchè pur gride?  

And my guide to him: "Why criest thou too?

> 21 Προστάτης δ᾽ ἐμὸς αὐτῷ· “Τί δὴ κραυγάζεις;  
> (And my protector to him: “Why then do you cry out?)

- *Προστάτης δ’ ἐμὸς αὐτῷ* retains the verbless introduction *E il duca mio a lui*. Postpositive *δέ*, elided before *ἐμός*, corresponds to initial *E*. *προστάτης* ‘protector, one who stands before’ interprets *duca* rather than using a word specific to guiding; *ἐμός* is a possessive adjective agreeing in the nom. masc. sg., and *αὐτῷ* is the dative of the addressee.
- *Τί δὴ κραυγάζεις;* has adverbial interrogative *τί* ‘why?’ and 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. *κραυγάζεις* from *κραυγάζω* ‘cry out’. Emphatic *δή* gives *pur* the force ‘then’ or ‘indeed’, not the explicitly additive sense “too”.

> 22 Non impedir lo suo fatale andare :  

Hinder not his fated going;

> 22 „Μὴ κώλυ᾽ αὐτοῦ τὴν τολμηρὰν πορείαν.  
> („Hinder not his daring journey.)

- *Μὴ κώλυ’* is *μή* with elided *κώλυε*, 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *κωλύω*: Greek uses its ordinary negative command where Italian has *Non impedir*, a negative infinitive with imperative force.
- *αὐτοῦ τὴν τολμηρὰν πορείαν* puts the possessive genitive ‘his’ before the article; *πορείαν* (acc. fem. sg.) turns the substantivized infinitive *andare* into the noun ‘journey’. *τολμηράν* ‘daring’ is a substantive departure from *fatale*: it attributes boldness rather than predestination to the going.

> 23 vuolsi così colà, dove si puote  
> 24 ciò che si vuole, e più non dimandare."  

thus it is willed there where what is willed can be done: and ask no more."

> 23 „Τοῦτ᾽ ἔστι θέλημ᾽ ἄνω τῶν δυναμένων  
> („This is the will above of those who have power to do)
>
> 24 „Ὅ,τι θέλουσι· περαιτέρω μὴ ζήτει.”  
> („Whatever they will; inquire no further.”)

- *Τοῦτ’ ἔστι θέλημ’* expands to *τοῦτο ἔστι θέλημα*, ‘this is a will’: both neuter forms lose a final vowel before the following vowel. *τοῦτο* is the deictic subject, *θέλημα* the predicate nominative. The Italian adverb and impersonal *vuolsi così* thus become a demonstrative and a nominal predicate; *ἄνω* ‘above’ makes the direction of *colà* explicit.
- *τῶν δυναμένων* ‘of those who are able’ is a substantivized pres. mid. participle of the deponent *δύναμαι*, gen. masc. pl., dependent on *θέλημα*. Italian *dove si puote* locates an impersonal power; Greek instead names plural possessors of the will and attributes ability to them. There is no expressed infinitive ‘to do’ with *δυναμένων*: it is supplied in understanding *Ὅ,τι θέλουσι* ‘whatever they wish’. *ὅ,τι* is the neut. sg. indefinite relative, distinguished in print from the conjunction *ὅτι*; *θέλουσι* is 3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *θέλω*, personal where *si vuole* was impersonal. The echo *θέλημα … θέλουσι* preserves something of *vuolsi … si vuole* across the change of construction.
- *περαιτέρω μὴ ζήτει* ‘inquire no further’ drops the connecting *e* and fronts the comparative adverb *περαιτέρω*. *ζήτει* is the contracted 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *ζητέω*, again with prohibitive *μή* for Italian *non* + infinitive; it also repeats the verb Musurus supplied for Minos’s question in line 16.

## Lines 25–57 — The Storm of the Carnal Sinners

> 25 Ora incomincian le dolenti note  
> 26 a farmisi sentire; or son venuto  
> 27 là dove molto pianto mi percuote.  

Now begin the doleful notes to reach me; now am I come where much lamenting strikes me.

> 25 Νῦν ἄρχοντ᾽ οἰμωκτικαὶ φωναὶ καὶ θρῆνοι  
> (Now wailing voices and dirges begin)
>
> 26 Ἀκουόμενοι· νῦν ἀφικόμην ἔνθα  
> (To be heard; now I have come where)
>
> 27 Πλεῖστοι κλαυθμοὶ πλήττουσι τὰς ἀκοάς μου,  
> (Very many weeping cries strike my ears,)

- *Νῦν ἄρχοντ᾽ οἰμωκτικαὶ φωναὶ καὶ θρῆνοι* keeps the order of *Ora incomincian le dolenti note*: first the adverb, then the verb, then what begins. Musurus expands *note* into two nouns, ‘wailing voices and dirges’; *οἰμωκτικαί* agrees with the feminine *φωναί*, not with the masculine *θρῆνοι*. *Ἄρχοντ᾽* represents *ἄρχονται* (3rd pl. pres. mid. ind. of *ἄρχω*), with poetic elision of final *-αι* before a vowel.
- *Ἀκουόμενοι* (pres. pass. ptcp. of *ἀκούω*, nom. masc. pl.) completes *ἄρχοντ᾽*: ‘begin to be heard’. Greek can use a supplementary participle with ‘begin’ where Italian has *a* and an infinitive. The participle takes the gender of the nearer *θρῆνοι*. Neither the causative element of *farmisi sentire* nor the person hearing is expressed here.
- *Νῦν ἀφικόμην* repeats *νῦν* for *or*, but gives a completed arrival, ‘I came’ (1st sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *ἀφικνέομαι*), for *son venuto*. The relative adverb *ἔνθα* does the work of *là dove*. *Πλεῖστοι κλαυθμοί* ‘very many cries of weeping’ turns the mass singular *molto pianto* into a countable plural; hence *πλήττουσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *πλήττω*). Its object is not simply *me* but *τὰς ἀκοάς μου* ‘my ears’, making explicit how the blows reach the speaker.

> 28 Io venni in loco d'ogni luce muto,  
> 29 che mugghia, come fa mar per tempesta,  
> 30 se da contrari venti è combattuto.  

I came into a place void of all light, which bellows like the sea in tempest, when it is combated by warring winds.

> 28 Εἰς τόπον παντὸς φωτὸς ἐστερημένον,  
> (Into a place deprived of all light,)
>
> 29 Πλήρη μυκηθμῶν, ὡς θύελλα θαλάσσης  
> (Full of bellowings, like a tempest of the sea)
>
> 30 Ὑπ᾽ ἐναντίων ἀνέμων κοπτομένης.  
> (Lashed by contrary winds.)

- *Εἰς τόπον* ‘into a place’ takes its verb of motion from *ἀφικόμην* in 26: Musurus does not repeat *Io venni*. *Ἐστερημένον* (acc. masc. sg., perf. pass. ptcp. of *στερέω*) agrees with *τόπον* and takes the genitive of what is lacking, *παντὸς φωτός*. Where *d'ogni luce muto* says ‘mute of all light’, Greek says ‘deprived of all light’, the perfect denoting the resulting state.
- *Πλήρη μυκηθμῶν* ‘full of bellowings’ is a second description of *τόπον*: *πλήρη* is the contracted acc. masc. sg. of *πλήρης*, with a genitive of content. The finite relative *che mugghia* thus becomes an adjective and noun. *Ὡς θύελλα θαλάσσης* shifts the comparison from the sea in a storm to ‘a storm of the sea’; there is no equivalent of the pro-verb *fa* in *come fa mar*.
- *Κοπτομένης* (gen. fem. sg., pres. pass. ptcp. of *κόπτω*, ‘strike’) agrees with *θαλάσσης*, not with the nominative *θύελλα*: it is the sea that is lashed. *Ὑπ᾽* is *ὑπό* elided; the genitive *ἐναντίων ἀνέμων* names the agent. A participle describing the sea replaces the explicit condition *se … è combattuto*.

> 31 La bufera infernal, che mai non resta,  
> 32 mena gli spirti con la sua rapina;  
> 33 voltando e percotendo li molesta.  

The hellish storm, which never rests, leads the spirits with its sweep; whirling, and smiting it vexes them.

> 31 Ἀεικίνητος ἡ ταρτάρειος λαῖλαψ,  
> (The ever-moving Tartarean whirlwind,)
>
> 32 Ἐπισύρουσα τὰ πνεύματ᾽ ἐν τῇ δίνῃ,  
> (Sweeping the spirits along in its whirl,)
>
> 33 Φέρει καὶ στρέφει, πλήττει καὶ μωλωπίζει.  
> (Carries and turns, strikes and bruises them.)

- *Ἀεικίνητος ἡ ταρτάρειος λαῖλαψ* puts ‘ever-moving’ first, outside the article–noun group, as a predicate description of the whirlwind. A positive compound replaces the negative relative *che mai non resta*; the adjective *ταρτάρειος* ‘Tartarean’ particularizes *infernal*.
- *Ἐπισύρουσα* (nom. fem. sg., pres. act. ptcp. of *ἐπισύρω*) agrees with *λαῖλαψ*: ‘dragging the spirits along in the eddy’. *Τὰ πνεύματ᾽* is elided *τὰ πνεύματα*, acc. neut. pl.; *ἐν τῇ δίνῃ* makes the sweep of *con la sua rapina* a motion within a whirl, without a possessive.
- *Φέρει καὶ στρέφει, πλήττει καὶ μωλωπίζει* gives four coordinated 3rd sg. present active indicatives, ‘carries and turns, strikes and bruises’. *Φέρει* takes up *mena*; Dante’s gerunds *voltando e percotendo* become finite verbs on the same footing, and *li molesta* becomes the more bodily *μωλωπίζει*. The object already named with *ἐπισύρουσα* is understood throughout.

> 34 Quando giungon davanti alla ruina,  
> 35 quivi le strida, il compianto e il lamento;  
> 36 bestemmian quivi la virtù divina.  

When they arrive before the ruin, there the shrieks, the moanings, and the lamentation; there they blaspheme the divine power.

> 34 Ὡς δ᾽ ἂν φθάσωσιν εἰς τοῦ κρημνοῦ τὸ χεῖλος,  
> (And when they reach the brink of the precipice,)
>
> 35 Ἐκεῖ φωναί, γογγυσμοί, λυγμοὶ καὶ γόοι,  
> (There are cries, murmurings, sobs, and wails,)
>
> 36 Ἐκεῖ τὴν θείαν ἀρετὴν βλασφημοῦσι.  
> (There they blaspheme the divine power.)

- *Ὡς δ᾽ ἂν φθάσωσιν* ‘and whenever they reach’ renders *Quando giungon* as an indefinite temporal clause: *ὡς* with *ἄν* and the subjunctive marks a recurring occasion. *Δ᾽* is elided postpositive *δέ*; *φθάσωσιν* is 3rd pl. aor. act. subj. of *φθάνω*, here ‘reach’, with *εἰς* and the accusative of destination. The aorist views each arrival as a whole.
- *Εἰς τοῦ κρημνοῦ τὸ χεῖλος* ‘to the lip of the precipice’ interprets rather than reproduces *davanti alla ruina*: a brink is supplied and the genitive *τοῦ κρημνοῦ* is placed before its head *τὸ χεῖλος*.
- *Ἐκεῖ … Ἐκεῖ* preserves the repeated *quivi*, and the first clause remains verbless. Four bare nominative plurals, *φωναί, γογγυσμοί, λυγμοὶ καὶ γόοι* ‘cries, murmurings, sobs and wails’, replace the three articular terms, two of them singular, in Italian. In the next clause the Greek puts the repeated adverb first and the verb last: *τὴν θείαν ἀρετὴν βλασφημοῦσι*. *Βλασφημοῦσι* is 3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *βλασφημέω*, with a direct accusative; *ἀρετή* ‘excellence, virtue’ follows the wording of *virtù* more closely than the English “power”.

> 37 Intesi, che a così fatto tormento  
> 38 enno dannati i peccator carnali,  
> 39 che la ragion sommettono al talento.  

I learnt that to such torment [are] doomed the carnal sinners, who subject reason to lust.

> 37 Τοιαύτας δὲ πάσχουσιν ὧδε βασάνους  
> (And such torments they suffer here,)
>
> 38 Ὅσοι σαρκικὰς ἔπραξαν ἁμαρτίας,  
> (All who committed carnal sins,)
>
> 39 Ὑποτάσσοντες τὸ λογικὸν τοῖς πόθοις.  
> (Subjugating reason to their desires.)

- *Τοιαύτας δὲ πάσχουσιν ὧδε βασάνους* ‘and such torments they suffer here’ omits *Intesi* and the dependent statement introduced by *che*. *Πάσχουσιν* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *πάσχω*) states the suffering directly in place of *enno dannati* ‘are condemned’; *τοιαύτας … βασάνους* is its plural accusative object, and *ὧδε* ‘here’ is supplied.
- *Ὅσοι … ἔπραξαν* ‘all who committed’ supplies the subject by a substantive relative clause. Instead of naming *i peccator carnali*, Musurus names their acts: *σαρκικὰς … ἁμαρτίας* ‘carnal sins’ is the object of *ἔπραξαν* (3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *πράσσω*), contrasting their completed acts with their present suffering.
- *Ὑποτάσσοντες* (nom. masc. pl., pres. act. ptcp. of *ὑποτάσσω*) agrees with *ὅσοι* and replaces the finite relative *che … sommettono*. It retains the two complements: the accusative *τὸ λογικόν*, a substantivized neuter adjective, ‘the rational faculty’, and the dative *τοῖς πόθοις* ‘to desires’, plural for *al talento*.

> 40 E come gli stornei ne portan l'ali,  
> 41 nel freddo tempo, a schiera larga e piena:  
> 42 così quel fiato gli spiriti mali;  
> 43 di qua, di là, di giù, di su gli mena.  

And as their wings bear along the starlings, at the cold season, in large and crowded troop: so that blast, the evil spirits; hither, thither, down, up, it leads them.

> 40 Ὥσπερ δ’ οἱ ψᾶρες φέροντ’ ἐπὶ πτερύγων  
> (And as starlings are borne upon their wings)
>
> 41 Χειμῶνος ἀγεληδὸν πυκνοὶ κἀθρόοι,  
> (In winter in flocks, dense and crowded together,)
>
> 42 Οὕτω τἄθλια πνεύματ’ ἐκεῖν’ ἡ δίνη  
> (So those wretched spirits that whirl)
>
> 43 Ἄνω καὶ κάτω, τῇδε κἀκεῖσε φέρει.  
> (Bears up and down, hither and thither.)

- *Ὥσπερ δ᾽ … Οὕτω* answers to *E come … così*. *Οἱ ψᾶρες* ‘the starlings’ are nominative subject, not the object as in *gli stornei ne portan l'ali*. *Φέροντ᾽* is elided *φέρονται* (3rd pl. pres. pass. ind. of *φέρω*): ‘are borne upon wings’. The Italian subject *l'ali* becomes *ἐπὶ πτερύγων*, with the genitive.
- *Χειμῶνος* is a genitive of time within which, ‘in winter’, for *nel freddo tempo*. The adverb *ἀγεληδόν* ‘in flocks’ replaces *a schiera*; *πυκνοὶ κἀθρόοι* ‘dense and massed together’ describes the birds themselves, in the nom. masc. pl., rather than qualifying the flock as *larga e piena*. *Κἀθρόοι* is crasis of *καὶ ἀθρόοι*.
- *Τἄθλια πνεύματ᾽ ἐκεῖν᾽* ‘those wretched spirits’ is the fronted accusative object: *τἄθλια* is crasis of *τὰ ἄθλια*, and *πνεύματ᾽* and *ἐκεῖν᾽* elide the final vowels of *πνεύματα* and *ἐκεῖνα*. *Ἄθλια* stresses wretchedness rather than the evil of *mali*. The demonstrative goes with the spirits, not, as *quel* does, with the blast. *Ἡ δίνη*, the nominative subject, supplies ‘the whirl’ for *fiato*.
- Greek delays the one governing verb, *φέρει*, until after the directions; it needs no resumptive object corresponding to *gli* in *gli mena*. *Ἄνω καὶ κάτω, τῇδε κἀκεῖσε* puts the vertical pair first and makes it ‘up and down’, reversing *di giù, di su*. *Τῇδε* is adverbial, ‘this way’; *κἀκεῖσε* is crasis of *καὶ ἐκεῖσε*, ‘and thither’.

> 44 Nulla speranza gli conforta mai,  
> 45 non che di posa, ma di minor pena.  

No hope ever comforts them, not of rest but even of less pain.

> 44 Οὐκ ἔστιν ἐλπὶς αὐτοὺς παρηγοροῦσα,  
> (There is no hope comforting them,)
>
> 45 Οὔτ’ ἀνέσεως, οὔτε μείονος ἄλγους.  
> (Neither of respite, nor of lesser pain.)

- *Οὐκ ἔστιν ἐλπίς* ‘there is no hope’ replaces the negative subject *Nulla speranza* with a negated existential verb. *Αὐτοὺς παρηγοροῦσα* ‘comforting them’ qualifies *ἐλπίς*: *παρηγοροῦσα* is the nom. fem. sg. pres. act. ptcp. of *παρηγορέω*, with the accusative personal object *αὐτούς*. There is no separate word for *mai*.
- *Οὔτ᾽ ἀνέσεως, οὔτε μείονος ἄλγους* gives two parallel genitives dependent on ‘hope’: ‘neither of respite nor of lesser pain’. The first *οὔτε* is elided. *Ἀνέσεως* is gen. sg. of *ἄνεσις*; *μείονος* (gen. sg. of comparative *μείων*) agrees with *ἄλγους*, contracted gen. sg. of neuter *ἄλγος*. The paired negatives retain both objects but flatten the scale expressed by *non che … ma*.

> 46 E come i gru van cantando lor lai,  
> 47 facendo in aer di sè lunga riga;  
> 48 così vid' io venir, traendo guai,  
> 49 ombre portate dalla detta briga;  

And as the cranes go chanting their lays, making a long streak of themselves in the air: so I saw the shadows come, uttering wails, borne by that strife of winds;

> 46 Ὡς δ’ οἱ γέρανοι κλαγγίζοντες ποτῶνται,  
> (And as the cranes fly uttering their cries,)
>
> 47 Τὸν ἀέρα τέμνοντες ἐν μακροῖς στοίχοις,  
> (Cleaving the air in long lines,)
>
> 48 Οὕτως εἶδον τὰς σκιὰς προσερχομένας,  
> (So I saw the shades approaching,)
>
> 49 Ἑλκομένας τ’ ἐν κλαυθμοῖς ὑπὸ τῆς δίνης.  
> (And dragged amidst weeping by the whirl.)

- *Ὡς δ᾽ … Οὕτως* repeats the comparative frame. In *οἱ γέρανοι κλαγγίζοντες ποτῶνται*, the article and participle are masculine plural. *Ποτῶνται* (3rd pl. pres. mid. ind. of poetic *ποτάομαι*) specifies flight for *van*; *κλαγγίζοντες* (pres. act. ptcp. of *κλαγγίζω*) replaces *cantando lor lai* with a verb for the sound itself, ‘uttering cries’, which needs neither object nor possessive.
- *Τὸν ἀέρα τέμνοντες* ‘cleaving the air’ gives the nominative participle *τέμνοντες* (from *τέμνω*) an accusative object where Dante has the local phrase *in aer*. Conversely, *lunga riga*, the object of *facendo*, becomes *ἐν μακροῖς στοίχοις* ‘in long ranks’, a plural phrase of formation. There is no equivalent of the reflexive *di sè*.
- *Εἶδον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*, with the aorist stem seen in *ἰδεῖν*) expresses *vid' io* without a pronoun. Greek brings *τὰς σκιάς* forward from the position of *ombre* and uses the participle with a verb of perception: *προσερχομένας* (acc. fem. pl., pres. mid. ptcp. of *προσέρχομαι*) means ‘approaching’, in place of the infinitive *venir*.
- *Ἑλκομένας τ᾽* adds a second participle agreeing with the shades (pres. pass. of *ἕλκω*; *τ᾽* is elided enclitic *τε*, ‘and’). Musurus redistributes *traendo guai, ombre portate*: it is the shades that are ‘dragged’, while their wailing becomes the attendant circumstance *ἐν κλαυθμοῖς*. *Ὑπὸ τῆς δίνης* names the agent, ‘by the whirl’, without separately rendering either the strife in *briga* or *detta*.

> 50 per ch'io dissi: "Maestro, chi son quelle  
> 51 genti, che l'aura nera sì gastiga?"  

whereat I said: “Master who are those people, whom the black air thus lashes?”

> 50 Ἐγὼ δ’ εἶπον· “Προστάτα, τίνες εἴσ’ οὗτοι,  
> (And I said: “Protector, who are these,)
>
> 51 „Οὓς ὁ ζοφώδης ἀὴρ οὕτω κολάζει;” —  
> („Whom the murky air so punishes?”—)

- *Ἐγὼ δ᾽ εἶπον* ‘and I said’ retains the explicit *io* but replaces the consequential *per ch'* with connective *δέ*. *Εἶπον* is 1st sg. second aor. act. ind., the aorist used with *λέγω*. *Προστάτα*, voc. sg. of *προστάτης*, addresses a ‘protector’ or ‘leader’ rather than reproducing *Maestro*.
- *Τίνες εἴσ᾽ οὗτοι;* ‘who are these?’ has an interrogative pronoun and a demonstrative, both nom. masc. pl.; *εἴσ᾽* is elided *εἰσί*, 3rd pl. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*. *Οὗτοι* stands on its own for *quelle genti*, with a shift from ‘those’ to ‘these’. The relative *οὕς* refers back to it but is accusative as the object of *κολάζει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *κολάζω*).
- *Ὁ ζοφώδης ἀήρ* is the nominative subject, ‘the murky air’ for *l'aura nera*. *Οὕτω κολάζει* follows *sì gastiga* closely in construction, ‘thus punishes’, without specifying a physical stroke.

> 52 "La prima di color, di cui novelle  
> 53 tu vuoi saper," mi disse quegli allotta,  
> 54 "fu imperatrice di molte favelle.  

“The first of these concerning whom thou seekest to know,” he then replied, “was Empress of many tongues.

> 52 —“Ἡ πρώτη τούτων τῶν ψυχῶν, ἃς εἰδέναι  
> (—“The first of these souls, whom to know)
>
> 53 „Θέλεις,” εἶπέ μοι τότε, “πολλῶν ὑπῆρξεν  
> („You wish,” he said to me then, “was over many)
>
> 54 „Αὐτοκράτειρα λαῶν ἑτερογλώσσων.  
> („Peoples of diverse tongues empress.)

- *Ἡ πρώτη τούτων τῶν ψυχῶν* ‘the first of these souls’ makes *color* explicit as ‘souls’. The ordinal is substantival, nom. fem. sg.; the genitive is partitive. *Τούτων* stands outside the article–noun group, the regular position of a demonstrative modifying an articular noun.
- *Ἃς εἰδέναι θέλεις* ‘whom you wish to know’ turns *di cui novelle tu vuoi saper* into a direct-object construction: the feminine plural relative refers to the souls, and its accusative is governed by *εἰδέναι*. There is no noun for *novelle*. *Εἰδέναι* is the perf. act. infinitive of *οἶδα*, present in sense, and complements *θέλεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *θέλω*).
- The parenthesis *εἶπέ μοι τότε* follows the position of *mi disse quegli allotta*. *Εἶπε* is the 3rd sg. of the aorist *εἶπον*, with an additional written accent before enclitic *μοι*, the dative recipient; the subject *quegli* is left understood. *Τότε* renders *allotta*.
- *Πολλῶν ὑπῆρξεν αὐτοκράτειρα λαῶν ἑτερογλώσσων* separates *πολλῶν* from its noun *λαῶν* across verb and predicate. *Ὑπῆρξεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ὑπάρχω*, with internal augment) functions as ‘was’; *αὐτοκράτειρα* is its predicate nominative. The genitive after the title denotes those ruled. Greek unpacks *molte favelle*: ‘many’ qualifies the peoples, while *ἑτερογλώσσων* ‘of different tongues’ qualifies them in turn.

> 55 A vizio di lussuria fu sì rotta,  
> 56 che libito fe' licito in sua legge  
> 57 per torre il biasmo, in che era condotta.  

With the vice of luxury she was so broken, that she made lust and law alike in her decree, to take away the blame she had incurred.

> 55 „ Τοσοῦτο δ’ ἦν ἔκδοτος εἰς τὴν λαγνείαν,  
> („ And so given over was she to lechery,)
>
> 56 „ Ὥστ’ ἔθετο τἀσελγὲς θεμιτὸν νόμῳ,  
> („ That she made wantonness lawful by decree,)
>
> 57 „ Ἵνα μὴ ψόγον ἔχοι τῶν αὑτῆς τρόπων.  
> („ That she might have no reproach for her ways.)

- *Τοσοῦτο δ᾽ ἦν ἔκδοτος* ‘and to such a degree was she given over’ replaces *fu sì rotta* with a state: *ἦν* is 3rd sg. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*, and *ἔκδοτος* is a predicate adjective, feminine here despite its *-ος* ending. Neuter *τοσοῦτο* is adverbial, anticipating the result introduced by *ὥστε*. *Εἰς τὴν λαγνείαν* ‘to lechery’ supplies the direction of abandonment without a separate noun for *vizio*.
- *Ὥστ᾽ ἔθετο* has elided *ὥστε* with the indicative for an actual result. *Ἔθετο* is 3rd sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *τίθημι*: ‘she laid down’ or ‘ordained’. *Τἀσελγές* is crasis of *τὸ ἀσελγές*, the substantivized neuter of *ἀσελγής*, ‘what is wanton’; *θεμιτόν* ‘lawful’ is its predicate accusative. This preserves the object–predicate relation in *libito fe' licito*, though not the likeness in sound. The dative *νόμῳ* ‘by law’ replaces *in sua legge* without the possessive.
- *Ἵνα μὴ ψόγον ἔχοι* ‘in order that she might not have reproach’ recasts the positive purpose infinitive *per torre il biasmo* as a negative finite purpose clause. *Ἔχοι* (3rd sg. pres. act. opt. of *ἔχω*) is the optative of purpose after the past *ἔθετο*; *μή* negates the intended state. *Τῶν αὑτῆς τρόπων* specifies the ground of reproach, ‘for her own ways’; *αὑτῆς*, with rough breathing, is the contracted reflexive genitive *ἑαυτῆς*. This genitive phrase replaces *in che era condotta*, so that Greek names her conduct rather than expressing the prior incurring of blame.

## Lines 58–90 — The Shades Named and the Pair Summoned

> 58 Ell’ è Semiramis, di cui si legge,  
> 59 che succedette a Nino, e fu sua sposa;  

She is Semiramis, of whom we read that she succeeded Ninus, and was his spouse;

> 58 „ Ἔστι δὲ Σεμίραμις, ἥτις, ὡς λόγος,  
> („ And she is Semiramis, who, as report tells,)
>
> 59 „ Διεδέχθη τὸν αὑτῆς σύζυγον Νῖνον·  
> („ Succeeded her husband Ninus;)

- *Ἔστι δὲ Σεμίραμις* renders *Ell’ è Semiramis*: the verb supplies the third person without a separate *ell’*, and postpositive *δέ* connects this identification to what precedes. *ἥτις* (nom. sg. fem. of *ὅστις*) takes up *Σεμίραμις* as the subject of *διεδέχθη*. Thus *di cui si legge, che …* becomes a relative clause with a parenthesis, *ὡς λόγος*, ‘as the report goes’; there is no longer a verb of reading.
- *Διεδέχθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *διαδέχομαι*) is passive in form but means ‘succeeded’; the augment follows the preverb. Where *succedette a Nino* has *a*, the Greek verb takes a direct accusative, *τὸν αὑτῆς σύζυγον Νῖνον*, ‘her own husband Ninus’. This absorbs the separate assertion *e fu sua sposa* into the object phrase: *αὑτῆς* is the contracted feminine reflexive genitive (for *ἑαυτῆς*), referring to the subject, and *Νῖνον* is in apposition to *σύζυγον*.

> 60 tenne la terra, che il Soldan corregge.  

she held the land which the Soldan rules.

> 60 „ Ἦν δ’ ἄνασσα γῆς, ἧς ὁ Σουλτάνος ἄρχει.  
> („ And was queen of the land which the Sultan rules.)

- *Ἦν δ’ ἄνασσα γῆς* substitutes ‘and she was queen of a land’ for *tenne la terra*. *ἦν* is the 3rd sg. imperfect of *εἰμί*; the poetic feminine *ἄνασσα* is its predicate nominative, with the dependent genitive *γῆς*. *δ’* is *δέ* elided before a vowel.
- *ἧς ὁ Σουλτάνος ἄρχει* retains the present of *che il Soldan corregge*. The relative *ἧς* is feminine singular like *γῆς*, but its genitive case is required within its own clause by *ἄρχει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἄρχω*, ‘rule over’).

> 61 L’ altra è colei, che s’ ancise amorosa,  
> 62 e ruppe fede al cener di Sicheo;  

That other is she who slew herself in love, and broke faith to the ashes of Sichæus;

> 61 „ Ἡ δ’ ἄλλη δι’ ἔρωτα γέγον’ αὐτόχειρ,  
> („ And that other slew herself for love,)
>
> 62 „ Πρὶν δ’ ἦν ἄπιστος πρὸς Σιχαῖον θανόντα.  
> („ And earlier was faithless to dead Sichæus.)

- *Ἡ δ’ ἄλλη* (‘and the other woman’) is subject directly, without the identifying *è colei, che*. *δι’ ἔρωτα* (*διά* with the accusative of cause) makes *amorosa* an explicit reason, ‘because of love’. For reflexive *s’ ancise* Musurus has *γέγον’ αὐτόχειρ*, ‘has become her own slayer’: *γέγον’* is elided *γέγονε*, 3rd sg. perfect act. ind. of *γίγνομαι*, with the common-gender predicate nominative *αὐτόχειρ*. The perfect gives a resulting condition rather than a simple past act.
- *Πρὶν δ’ ἦν ἄπιστος* adds an order of time not marked by *e*: *πρίν* is adverbial, ‘previously’, not a conjunction. ‘She was faithless’ (imperfect copula and predicate adjective) replaces the event *ruppe fede*. *πρὸς Σιχαῖον θανόντα* gives the person toward whom she was faithless instead of *al cener di Sicheo*: *θανόντα* (acc. sg. masc., second aor. act. participle of *θνῄσκω*) agrees with *Σιχαῖον*, ‘Sichæus, who had died’.

> 63 poi è Cleopatras lussuriosa.  

next comes luxurious Cleopatra.

> 63 „ Ἡ τρίτη δ’ ἐστὶν ἀκρατὴς Κλεοπάτρα.“  
> („ And the third is unbridled Cleopatra.“)

- *Ἡ τρίτη δ’ ἐστὶν ἀκρατὴς Κλεοπάτρα* counts where *poi* only indicates succession: ‘and the third is unrestrained Cleopatra’. The article makes the feminine ordinal *τρίτη* substantival. *ἀκρατής* is a two-termination adjective, here nom. sg. fem. with *Κλεοπάτρα*; it renders *lussuriosa* by lack of self-command rather than by a word specific to lust. The Greek closes the direct speech here, not after *Tristano*.

> 64 Elena vedi, per cui tanto reo  
> 65 tempo si volse; e vedi il grande Achille,  
> 66 che con amore al fine combatteo;  

Helena [see], for whom so long a time of ill revolved; and [see] the great Achilles, who fought at last with love;

> 64 Εἶδον Ἑλένην τὴν πηγὴν παθημάτων  
> (I saw Helena, the source of calamities)
>
> 65 Πολυετῶν· τὸν μέγαν Ἀχιλλέ᾽ εἶδον,  
> (Of many years; the great Achilles I saw,)
>
> 66 Τὸν ὑπ’ ἔρωτος ὀλωλότ’ ἐπὶ τέλους.  
> (Who perished through love at the end.)

- *Εἶδον Ἑλένην … τὸν μέγαν Ἀχιλλέ᾽ εἶδον* changes *Elena vedi … e vedi il grande Achille* from an address into a first-person report. *εἶδον* is the suppletive second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*, here 1st sg.; its repetitions frame the two accusatives. *Ἀχιλλέ᾽* is the elided accusative *Ἀχιλλέα*.
- *τὴν πηγὴν παθημάτων πολυετῶν*, ‘the source of sufferings lasting many years’, stands in accusative apposition to *Ἑλένην*. It replaces the whole relative clause *per cui tanto reo tempo si volse*: *πηγή* expresses causation, *παθημάτων* (gen. pl. of *πάθημα*) turns the ill into sufferings, and *πολυετῶν* (gen. pl. of *πολυετής*) assigns the duration to them. There is no verb corresponding to *si volse*.
- *τὸν ὑπ’ ἔρωτος ὀλωλότ’ ἐπὶ τέλους* is a second attributive phrase after the name: ‘the one who in the end perished through love’. *ὀλωλότ’* is elided *ὀλωλότα*, acc. sg. masc. of the intransitive second perfect active participle of *ὄλλυμι*. The form denotes one already lost; *ὑπ’* (elided *ὑπό*) with the genitive names the effective cause. Thus *al fine* has its counterpart in *ἐπὶ τέλους*, but *con amore … combatteo* is interpreted by the outcome, not translated as fighting.

> 67 vedi Paris, Tristano”; e più di mille  
> 68 ombre mostrommi, e nominommi a dito,  
> 69 ch’ amor di nostra vita dipartille.  

[see] Paris, Tristan”; and more than a thousand shades he shewed to me, and *pointing* with his finger, named [to me those] whom love had parted from our life.

> 67 Εἶδον Πάριν, Τριστανὸν καὶ σκιὰς πλέον  
> (I saw Paris, Tristan, and shades more than)
>
> 68 Χιλίων ὀνομαστὶ κατέδειξέ μοι,  
> (A thousand he pointed out to me by name,)
>
> 69 Ἃς ἔρως ἐκ τῆς ζωῆς ἡμῶν ἀφεῖλεν.  
> (Whom love took away from this life of ours.)

- *Εἶδον Πάριν, Τριστανόν* continues the shift from *vedi* to ‘I saw’; both names are accusative. At *καὶ σκιὰς πλέον χιλίων* the object of a new clause comes before its verb and the subject changes without an expressed pronoun. *πλέον* is the neuter singular comparative used adverbially, ‘more’; *χιλίων* is genitive plural after the comparative, where Italian has *più di mille*.
- *ὀνομαστὶ κατέδειξέ μοι*, ‘he pointed out to me by name’, compresses *mostrommi, e nominommi a dito*. The adverb *ὀνομαστί* does the work of the naming verb; *κατέδειξε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *καταδείκνυμι*, with internal augment) conveys the pointing without naming the finger. The single enclitic dative *μοι* serves the whole expression and accounts for the additional accent on *κατέδειξέ*.
- *Ἃς ἔρως ἐκ τῆς ζωῆς ἡμῶν ἀφεῖλεν* closely follows *ch’ amor di nostra vita dipartille*. *ἅς* (acc. pl. fem.) looks back to *σκιάς* and is itself the direct object, with no resumptive pronoun for Italian *-lle*. *ἀφεῖλεν* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἀφαιρέω*, ‘took away’; *ἐκ* with the genitive makes the separation explicit, and possessive *ἡμῶν* follows *ζωῆς*.

> 70 Poscia ch’ io ebbi il mio dottore udito  
> 71 nomar le donne antiche e i cavalieri,  
> 72 pietà mi giunse, e fui quasi smarrito.  

After I had heard my teacher name the olden dames and cavaliers, pity [came over] me, and I was as if bewildered.

> 70 Ἐπεὶ δ’ ὀνομάζοντα τὸν ὁδηγόν μου  
> (And when my guide naming)
>
> 71 Ἤκουσα τὰς ἀρχαίας γυναῖκας κἄνδρας,  
> (I heard the women and men of old,)
>
> 72 Οἶκτος εἷλέ με, καὶ σχεδὸν ἐναρκώθην.  
> (Pity seized me, and I was almost benumbed.)

- *Ἐπεὶ δ’ … ἤκουσα* uses temporal *ἐπεί* and the 1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀκούω* for *Poscia ch’ io ebbi … udito*: the temporal conjunction orders the events without a Greek pluperfect. In *ὀνομάζοντα τὸν ὁδηγόν μου* the present active participle of *ὀνομάζω* agrees with the accusative of the person perceived, ‘my guide naming’. This participial construction replaces *il mio dottore … nomar*; *ὁδηγός* denotes a guide rather than a teacher.
- *τὰς ἀρχαίας γυναῖκας κἄνδρας* supplies the objects of the participle only after *ἤκουσα*. *κἄνδρας* is crasis of *καὶ ἄνδρας* (acc. pl. of *ἀνήρ*): the socially marked *cavalieri* becomes simply ‘men’. *ἀρχαίας* agrees in form with *γυναῖκας*.
- *Οἶκτος εἷλέ με*, ‘pity seized me’, gives *pietà mi giunse* a transitive verb, *εἷλε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *αἱρέω*), with enclitic accusative *με*. *σχεδὸν ἐναρκώθην* keeps the qualification *quasi* but replaces *fui … smarrito* with ‘I was benumbed’, 1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ναρκόω*.

> 73 Io cominciai: “Poeta, volentieri  
> 74 parlerei a que’ duo, che insieme vanno,  
> 75 e paion sì al vento esser leggieri.”  

I began : “Poet, willingly would I speak with those two that go together, and seem so light upon the wind.”

> 73 Εἶπον· “ Ποιητά, διαλεχθῆν᾽ ἐπόθουν  
> (I said: “Poet, I longed to converse)
>
> 74 „ Πρὸς τὰς σκιὰς τὰς δύο τὰς φερομένας  
> („ With those two shades that are borne)
>
> 75 „Συνδέτους οὕτως ἐλαφρῶς ἐπ’ ἀνέμου.”  
> („Joined together, so lightly upon the wind.”)

- *Εἶπον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind., ‘I said’) suppresses the inceptive sense of *Io cominciai*. *Ποιητά* is the vocative singular of *ποιητής*. *διαλεχθῆν᾽ ἐπόθουν*, ‘I desired to converse’, recasts *volentieri parlerei*: the wish is in the imperfect *ἐπόθουν* (1st sg. act. ind. of *ποθέω*), not in a potential optative with *ἄν*. Its complement *διαλεχθῆν᾽* is poetic elision of *διαλεχθῆναι*, aorist passive infinitive in form, with the conversational sense of *διαλέγομαι*.
- *πρὸς τὰς σκιὰς τὰς δύο* gives the interlocutors with *πρός* and the accusative, supplying ‘shades’ for *que’ duo*. The repeated article places *δύο* in attributive position; articles, noun and following modifiers are plural, not dual.
- *τὰς φερομένας συνδέτους οὕτως ἐλαφρῶς ἐπ’ ἀνέμου* replaces the two finite predicates *insieme vanno, e paion … esser leggieri* with one articulated participle and its qualifications. *φερομένας* (acc. pl. fem., pres. pass. participle of *φέρω*) means ‘being carried’, not actively going; *συνδέτους* (acc. pl. fem. of *σύνδετος*) describes their state, ‘joined’. *οὕτως ἐλαφρῶς*, ‘so lightly’, modifies the carrying: there is no verb of seeming. *ἐπ’* is elided *ἐπί*, here with the genitive for *al vento*.

> 76 Ed egli a me: “Vedrai, quando saranno  
> 77 più presso a noi; e tu allor li prega  
> 78 per quell’ amor che i mena; e quei verranno.”  

And he to me: “Thou shalt see when they are nearer to us; and do thou then entreat them by that love, which leads them; and they will come.”

> 76 Ὁ δ’ εἶπε· “Ταύτας, ὅταν ὦσ’ ἐγγυτέρω,  
> (And he said: “Them, when they are nearer,)
>
> 77 „Ὄψει, καὶ τότε πρὸς Ἔρωτος, ὃς ἄγει  
> („You shall see, and then by Love, who leads)
>
> 78 „Αὐτάς, προσκάλει, καὶ προσελεύσονταί σοι.”  
> („Them, summon them, and they will come to you.”)

- *Ὁ δ’ εἶπε* supplies ‘said’ in *Ed egli a me* but omits the addressee; *ὁ* with *δέ* serves as the subject pronoun. *Ταύτας* (‘these’, acc. pl. fem.) supplies an object for *Vedrai*, placed before the intervening time clause and resumed in sense by *ὄψει* (2nd sg. fut. mid. ind. of *ὁράω*, active in meaning).
- *ὅταν ὦσ’ ἐγγυτέρω* renders future *quando saranno più presso* with prospective *ὅταν* (*ὅτε* + *ἄν*) and the subjunctive: *ὦσ’* is elided *ὦσι*, 3rd pl. pres. subj. of *εἰμί*. *ἐγγυτέρω* is a comparative adverb; the standard of nearness *a noi* is understood.
- *καὶ τότε πρὸς Ἔρωτος … προσκάλει* puts the adjuration before the command. *πρός* with the genitive means ‘by’; capitalized *Ἔρωτος* personifies *quell’ amor* without reproducing the demonstrative. *ὃς ἄγει αὐτάς* is a relative clause, with masculine nominative subject referring to *Ἔρως* and feminine accusative object referring to the shades. *προσκάλει* (2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *προσκαλέω*) means ‘summon’, rather than *prega*; its object is understood. *προσελεύσονταί σοι* (3rd pl. fut. mid. ind. of *προσέρχομαι*) adds a dative of destination, ‘to you’, to *verranno*, but does not separately express *quei*.

> 79 Sì tosto come il vento a noi li piega,  
> 80 mossi la voce: “O anime affannate,  
> 81 venite a noi parlar, s’ altri nol niega.”  

Soon as the wind bends them to us, I raise[d] my voice: “O wearied souls! come to speak with us, if none denies it.”

> 79 Ὡς δ’ ἐγγὺς ἡμῶν ἤχθησαν ὑπ’ ἀνέμου,  
> (And when they were brought near us by the wind,)
>
> 80 Ὕψωσα φωνήν· “Ὦ ψυχαὶ τεθλιμμέναι,  
> (I raised my voice: “O afflicted souls,)
>
> 81 „Πρὸς ἡμᾶς ἔλθετ’, εἰ μηδὲν τὸ κωλύον.”  
> („Come to us, if there is nothing that forbids.”)

- *Ὡς δ’ ἐγγὺς ἡμῶν ἤχθησαν ὑπ’ ἀνέμου* makes *il vento … li piega* passive: ‘when they were brought near us by wind’. Temporal *ὡς* does not separately mark the immediacy of *Sì tosto*. *ἐγγύς* governs genitive *ἡμῶν*; *ἤχθησαν* (3rd pl. aor. pass. ind. of *ἄγω*, with temporal augment) replaces the Italian narrative present, and the former subject becomes *ὑπό* with the genitive.
- *Ὕψωσα φωνήν* gives *mossi la voce* as ‘I raised a voice’, with the 1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ὑψόω* and no article or possessive. *Ὦ ψυχαὶ τεθλιμμέναι* renders *O anime affannate* with a vocative plural and the agreeing perfect middle-passive participle of *θλίβω*, ‘pressed hard’, hence ‘afflicted’; the perfect presents a continuing state.
- *Πρὸς ἡμᾶς ἔλθετ’* fronts the destination. *ἔλθετ’* is elided *ἔλθετε*, 2nd pl. second aor. act. imperative of *ἔρχομαι*; *parlar* has no equivalent. *εἰ μηδὲν τὸ κωλύον* is an elliptical condition with the copula understood: ‘if there is nothing preventing [it]’. *τὸ κωλύον* is a substantivized neut. sg. pres. act. participle of *κωλύω*; neuter *μηδέν* replaces the personal *altri*, and the negative is appropriate to the supposition.

> 82 Quali colombe, dal disio chiamate,  
> 83 con l’ ali alzate e ferme al dolce nido  
> 84 vengon per l’ aer dal voler portate:  

As doves called by desire, with [raised] and steady wings [come] through the air to their loved nest, borne by their will:

> 82 Ὡς περιστεραὶ καλούμεναι ’κ τοῦ πόθου  
> (As doves, summoned by desire,)
>
> 83 Πτέρυξι στεῤῥαῖς κἀνοικταῖς πρὸς γλυκεῖαν  
> (With firm and outstretched wings toward the sweet)
>
> 84 Καλιὰν ὁμόσ’ ἵπτανται δι’ ἀέρος,  
> (Nest fly together through the air,)

- *Ὡς περιστεραὶ καλούμεναι ’κ τοῦ πόθου* opens the comparison with *ὡς*, answered by *οὕτω* in line 85. *καλούμεναι* (nom. pl. fem., pres. pass. participle of *καλέω*) agrees with *περιστεραί* and presents the summons as ongoing. *’κ* is prodelision of *ἐκ* after the preceding vowel; the genitive phrase makes desire the source of the call (*dal disio*).
- *Πτέρυξι στεῤῥαῖς κἀνοικταῖς* is an instrumental dative plural: *πτέρυξι* is from *πτέρυξ*, and both adjectives agree with it. *στεῤῥαῖς* (‘firm’, from *στερρός*, with the traditional breathings on double rho) renders *ferme* first; *κἀνοικταῖς* is crasis of *καὶ ἀνοικταῖς*, ‘and open’, for *alzate*.
- *πρὸς γλυκεῖαν καλιάν* gives the goal (*al dolce nido*) with *πρός* and accusative; the feminine *γλυκεῖαν* agrees with *καλιάν*. *ὁμόσ’ ἵπτανται δι’ ἀέρος*, ‘they fly together through air’, replaces *vengon per l’ aer*: *ὁμόσ’* is elided *ὁμόσε*, properly ‘to the same place’, and *ἵπτανται* is 3rd pl. pres. mid. ind. of *ἵπταμαι*. Flight has its own verb, but *dal voler portate* has no separate counterpart.

> 85 cotali uscir della schiera ov’ è Dido,  
> 86 a noi venendo per l’ aer maligno,  
> 87 sì forte fu l’ affettuoso grido.  

so those spirits issued from the band where Dido is, coming to us through the malignant air; such was the force of my affectuous cry.

> 85 Οὕτω λιποῦσαι τὸ πλῆθος, ἔνθα μένει  
> (So, leaving the throng where abides)
>
> 86 Διδώ, πρὸς ἡμᾶς δι’ ἀέρος λυμώδους  
> (Dido, toward us through the pestilent air)
>
> 87 Ἦλθον, ἐνδοῦσαι τῇ προσπαθεῖ φωνῇ μου.  
> (They came, yielding to my affectionate cry.)

- *Οὕτω λιποῦσαι τὸ πλῆθος* answers *ὡς* with an adverb, ‘so’, rather than the agreeing *cotali*. *λιποῦσαι* (nom. pl. fem., second aor. act. participle of *λείπω*) takes a direct object, ‘having left the throng’, for finite *uscir della schiera*. The feminine plural keeps *σκιαί* understood; no noun for “those spirits” is added.
- *ἔνθα μένει Διδώ* translates *ov’ è Dido* with the relative adverb of place and ‘abides’ rather than ‘is’; *Διδώ* is the delayed nominative subject. *πρὸς ἡμᾶς δι’ ἀέρος λυμώδους ἦλθον* makes *a noi venendo* the main assertion, reversing the Italian distribution of finite verb and participle. *ἦλθον* is here 3rd pl. second aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*. *λυμώδους* (gen. sg. of *λυμώδης*) qualifies *ἀέρος* after *διά*, giving *maligno* the sense ‘pestilential’.
- *ἐνδοῦσαι τῇ προσπαθεῖ φωνῇ μου*, ‘yielding to my affectionate voice’, recasts *sì forte fu l’ affettuoso grido* from the hearers’ side. *ἐνδοῦσαι* is nom. pl. fem., aor. act. participle of *ἐνδίδωμι*, with a dative of that to which one yields. Strength is implicit in the yielding; *προσπαθεῖ* (dat. sg. fem. of *προσπαθής*) retains the affective quality, and *μου* explicitly identifies the voice as the speaker’s.

> 88 “O animal grazioso e benigno,  
> 89 che visitando vai per l’ aer perso  
> 90 noi che tignemmo il mondo di sanguigno;  

“O living creature, gracious and benign! that goest through the black air, visiting us who stained the earth with blood;

> 88 “Ὂν ζῶν εὐμενὲς καὶ χαρίεν, ὃς ἥκεις  
> (“Living being, kind and gracious, who come)
>
> 89 „Ὧδ’ ὀψόμενος δι’ ἀέρος ζοφώδους  
> („Hither through the murky air to visit)
>
> 90 „Τοὺς αἵματι βάψαντας ἡμᾶς τὸν Κόσμον,  
> („Us who stained the world with blood,)

- *Ὂν ζῶν εὐμενὲς καὶ χαρίεν* renders *O animal grazioso e benigno* without the vocative particle. *ὄν* is the neut. sg. pres. participle of *εἰμί* used substantivally in address (vocative identical to nominative); *ζῶν*, the agreeing contracted participle of *ζάω*, makes the sense ‘living being’ explicit. The adjectives reverse the Italian order: *εὐμενές* (neuter of *εὐμενής*) answers to *benigno*, *χαρίεν* (neuter of *χαρίεις*) to *grazioso*.
- *ὃς ἥκεις ὧδ’ ὀψόμενος* shifts from grammatical neuter to personal masculine in both relative and participle. *ἥκεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἥκω*) agrees in person with the addressee: ‘who are come hither’, rather than *che … vai*. *ὧδ’* is elided *ὧδε*. *ὀψόμενος* (nom. sg. masc., fut. mid. participle of *ὁράω*) expresses purpose after motion, ‘in order to see’, where *visitando* expresses accompanying action. *δι’ ἀέρος ζοφώδους* keeps the path through the air, interpreting *perso* as ‘gloomy’.
- *τοὺς αἵματι βάψαντας ἡμᾶς τὸν Κόσμον* interweaves *ἡμᾶς*, the object of *ὀψόμενος*, with the articular participial phrase identifying ‘us’. *τοὺς … βάψαντας* (acc. pl. masc., aor. act. participle of *βάπτω*) replaces the finite first-person relative *che tignemmo*; *τὸν Κόσμον* is the participle’s direct object. Instrumental *αἵματι* (dat. sg. of *αἷμα*) gives the substance used in the staining, ‘with blood’, rather than the resulting colour *di sanguigno*.

## Lines 91–123 — Love, Pity, and the Pain of Memory

> 91 se fosse amico il re dell' universo,  
> 92 noi pregheremmo lui per la tua pace,  
> 93 poi che hai pietà del nostro mal perverso.  

If the King of the Universe were our friend, we would pray him for thy peace; seeing that thou hast pity of our perverse misfortune.

> 91 „Εἴ ποθ’ ὁ παντὸς Ἄναξ ἦν ἡμῖν εὔνους,  
> („If the King of all were ever friendly to us,)
>
> 92 „Ἐδεόμεθ’ ἂν ὑπὲρ τῆς σῆς εἰρήνης,  
> („We would pray for your peace,)
>
> 93 „Ἀνθ’ ὧν ἐλεεῖς ἡμᾶς τῆς κακῆς τύχης.  
> („Because you pity us for our evil fortune.)

- *Εἴ ποθ’ ὁ παντὸς Ἄναξ ἦν ἡμῖν εὔνους* ‘if ever the lord of all were well disposed to us’ keeps the condition first but postpones the equivalent of *amico*. The poetic title *Ἄναξ* takes the genitive *παντός*, ‘of all’; the predicate adjective *εὔνους* (contracted from *εὔνοος*, nom. masc. sg.) takes the dative *ἡμῖν* where Italian has no expressed *a noi*. *ποθ’* adds ‘ever’: it is enclitic *ποτε*, elided, with *τ* aspirated before the rough breathing of *ὁ*.
- *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. indic. of *εἰμί*) and *ἐδεόμεθ’ ἄν* (1st pl. imperf. mid. indic. of *δέομαι*, with final *α* elided) make a present unreal condition. Greek uses past indicatives, with *ἄν* in the consequence, not an optative or a form corresponding directly to the Italian conditional *pregheremmo*. *ὑπὲρ τῆς σῆς εἰρήνης* is ‘on behalf of thy peace’; the possessive adjective agrees with its noun. Italian *lui*, the person petitioned, is not expressed.
- *ἀνθ’ ὧν* (from *ἀντὶ ὧν*, with elision and aspiration) is literally ‘in return for which things’, hence ‘inasmuch as’, for causal *poi che*. *ἐλεεῖς* (2nd sg. pres. act. indic. of *ἐλεέω*) has both a person pitied, *ἡμᾶς*, and a genitive of the ground of pity, *τῆς κακῆς τύχης*, ‘for the bad fortune’. Italian makes *nostro mal perverso* itself the object of pity; Greek transfers ‘our’ to ‘us’ and interprets *mal* as fortune.

> 94 Di quel che udire e che parlar ti piace  
> 95 noi udiremo e parleremo a vui,  
> 96 mentrechè il vento, come fa, ci tace.  

Of that which it pleases thee to hear and to speak, we will hear and speak with you, whilst the wind, as now, is silent [for us].

> 94 „Περὶ πάντων, ὧν ἐρωτῆσαι κἀκοῦσαι  
> („Concerning all that to ask and hear)
>
> 95 „Ἐπιθυμεῖς, ἐροῦμεν κἀκούσομέν σου,  
> („You wish, we will speak and we will listen to you,)
>
> 96 „Ἐφ᾽ ὅσον γε νῦν, ἠρεμοῦντος ἀνέμου.  
> („At least so long as now, while the wind is still.)

- *περὶ πάντων, ὧν … ἐπιθυμεῖς* ‘concerning all that you desire’ expands *di quel* to a plural and turns impersonal *ti piace* into personal *ἐπιθυμεῖς* (2nd sg. pres. act. indic. of *ἐπιθυμέω*). The relative *ὧν* is genitive like its antecedent *πάντων* after *περί*, rather than accusative as an object of asking and hearing would be.
- *ἐρωτῆσαι κἀκοῦσαι* are aorist active infinitives of *ἐρωτάω* and *ἀκούω*, dependent on *ἐπιθυμεῖς*. Musurus makes *parlar* specifically ‘ask’ and puts asking before hearing. He likewise reverses *udiremo e parleremo* in *ἐροῦμεν κἀκούσομέν σου*: *ἐροῦμεν* is the contracted future ‘we shall say’; *ἀκούσομεν* is 1st pl. future active of *ἀκούω*. Both *κἀκοῦσαι* and *κἀκούσομεν* show crasis of *καί* with initial *ἀ-*; the extra accent in *ἀκούσομέν σου* precedes the enclitic. The genitive singular *σου* belongs naturally to ‘listen to you’, not to ‘speak with you’: Greek does not reproduce the shift to plural *vui*.
- *ἐφ’ ὅσον γε νῦν* ‘at least for so long as now’ gives the time limit of *mentrechè*; *ἐφ’* is *ἐπί* elided and aspirated before *ὅσον*, an accusative of extent, and *γε* qualifies the limit. The genitive absolute *ἠρεμοῦντος ἀνέμου* (pres. act. participle of *ἠρεμέω* agreeing with *ἀνέμου*) means ‘while the wind is still’. *νῦν* absorbs *come fa*; the wind is at rest rather than silent, and there is no counterpart to *ci*.

> 97 Siede la terra, dove nata fui,  
> 98 su la marina dove il Po discende  
> 99 per aver pace co' seguaci sui.  

The town, where I was born, sits on the shore, where Po descends to rest with his attendant *streams*.

> 97 „Ἡ πόλις οὖν τῆς ἐμῆς γενετῆς κεῖται  
> („The city, then, of my birth lies)
>
> 98 „Παρ᾽ ἀκτὴν τῆς θαλάσσης, ἔνθ᾽ εἰρηνεύσων  
> („Upon the sea-shore, where to find peace)
>
> 99 „Πάδος ἐκχεῖται σὺν ποταμοῖς συῤῥόοις.  
> („The Po pours forth with its confluent streams.)

- *ἡ πόλις οὖν τῆς ἐμῆς γενετῆς κεῖται* ‘the city, then, of my birth lies’ puts the subject before the verb, adds connective *οὖν*, and interprets *terra* as a city. The relative clause *dove nata fui* becomes a dependent genitive with the abstract noun *γενετή*, ‘birth’, and the agreeing possessive *ἐμῆς*. *κεῖται* (3rd sg. pres. indic. of *κεῖμαι*) is stative, like *siede*, but literally ‘lies’.
- *παρ’ ἀκτὴν τῆς θαλάσσης* ‘along the shore of the sea’ unpacks *su la marina*; *παρά* with the accusative gives the city's position along the coast. *ἔνθ’* is locative *ἔνθα*, elided. *εἰρηνεύσων* (fut. act. participle, nom. masc. sg., of *εἰρηνεύω*) anticipates its subject *Πάδος*: ‘in order to be at peace’. The Greek thus places the purpose before both the river's name and its verb.
- *ἐκχεῖται* (3rd sg. pres. mid./pass. indic. of *ἐκχέω*) pictures the river ‘pouring out’, not descending. *σὺν ποταμοῖς συῤῥόοις* makes the unnamed *seguaci sui* explicitly ‘rivers flowing together’; *σύν* governs the dative plural, and there is no possessive. The spelling *-ῤῥ-* marks doubled rho in the compound *σύρροος*.

> 100 Amor, che al cor gentil ratto s' apprende,  
> 101 prese costui della bella persona  
> 102 che mi fu tolta, e il modo ancor m' offende.  

Love, which is quickly caught in gentle heart, took him with the fair body of which I was bereft; and the manner still afflicts me.

> 100 „Ἔρως ὁ νικῶν τὰς εὐγενεῖς καρδίας  
> („Love, that conquers noble hearts,)
>
> 101 „Τοῦτον κατέσχεν οὗ γεγύμνωμαι κάλλους  
> („Seized this man for the beauty of which I have been stripped)
>
> 102 „Οὕτως, ὥστ᾽ ἔτι δίδωμι μώμου δίκην·  
> („In such wise that I still pay the penalty of blame;)

- *Ἔρως ὁ νικῶν τὰς εὐγενεῖς καρδίας* ‘Love, who conquers noble hearts’ renders *che … s’apprende* by an articular present active participle of *νικάω* (nom. masc. sg.). Love is the conqueror rather than something that catches or takes hold in a heart; the singular *al cor gentil* becomes a generic accusative plural, and *ratto* disappears.
- *τοῦτον κατέσχεν* ‘seized this man’ fronts the demonstrative object of *κατέσχεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act. indic. of *κατέχω*). In *οὗ γεγύμνωμαι κάλλους*, ‘of which beauty I stand stripped’, the relative precedes its noun and both are genitive; *γεγύμνωμαι* (1st sg. perf. mid./pass. indic. of *γυμνόω*, passive in sense) takes a genitive of deprivation. Italian *bella persona* becomes abstract *κάλλος*: instead of the body being taken from her, the speaker is left divested of beauty. Its connection to ‘seized this man’ is compressed, without a preposition meaning ‘because of’.
- *οὕτως, ὥστ’ ἔτι δίδωμι μώμου δίκην* ‘in such a way that I still pay the penalty of blame’ takes up *il modo* as an adverb of manner followed by a result clause. *ὥστ’* is elided *ὥστε*; the indicative *δίδωμι* (1st sg. pres. act.) presents an actual continuing consequence. *δίκην διδόναι* means ‘pay the penalty’, with *μώμου* specifying the ground. This is an interpretation, not a lexical equivalent of *m’offende*: ‘the manner’ no longer acts on her; she pays.

> 103 Amor, che a nullo amato amar perdona,  
> 104 mi prese del costui piacer sì forte,  
> 105 che, come vedi, ancor non m' abbandona.  

Love, which to no loved one permits excuse for loving, took me so strongly with delight in him, that, as thou seest, even now it leaves me not.

> 103 „Ἔρως, ὃς ἐρώμενον ἐρᾷν βιάζει,  
> („Love, which compels the beloved to love,)
>
> 104 „Οὕτω με σφοδρῶς κατέλαβ᾽ ὑπὲρ τούτου,  
> („Overtook me so vehemently for this man,)
>
> 105 „Ὥστ᾽ οὐκέτι μ᾽, ὡς ὁρᾷς, καταλιμπάνει·  
> („That it leaves me no more, as you see;)

- *Ἔρως, ὃς ἐρώμενον ἐρᾷν βιάζει* ‘Love, who compels a beloved to love’ keeps the repeated initial *Amor* and the verbal echo of *amato amar*, but replaces the negative *a nullo … perdona* with positive compulsion. *ὅς* is the subject of *βιάζει* (3rd sg. pres. act. indic. of *βιάζω*); *ἐρώμενον* (acc. masc. sg. pres. passive participle of *ἐράω*, used substantivally) is its object and the understood subject of the contracted pres. act. infinitive *ἐρᾷν*.
- *οὕτω με σφοδρῶς κατέλαβ’ ὑπὲρ τούτου* ‘so vehemently seized me on this man's account’ separates the two adverbs by enclitic *με*. *κατέλαβ’* is elided *κατέλαβε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. indic. of *καταλαμβάνω*). The normally benefactive or relational *ὑπέρ* with genitive *τούτου* refers directly to the man; the attraction named by *del costui piacer* has no separate Greek noun.
- *ὥστ’ οὐκέτι μ’, ὡς ὁρᾷς, καταλιμπάνει* again has *ὥστε* with a finite indicative of actual result. *ὁρᾷς* (2nd sg. contracted pres. act. indic. of *ὁράω*) preserves the parenthesis *come vedi*. *μ’* is elided *με*, object of *καταλιμπάνει* (3rd sg. pres. act. indic. of *καταλιμπάνω*, an extended present beside *καταλείπω*). *οὐκέτι* strictly means ‘no longer’, not ‘not even now’: the attachment persists, but the temporal expression is not precisely *ancor non*.

> 106 Amor condusse noi ad una morte;  
> 107 Caina attende chi vita ci spense."  

Love led us to one death; Caïna waits for him who quenched our life."

> 106 „Ἔρως ἤγαγεν ἡμᾶς εἰς ταὐτὸν τέλος·  
> („Love led us to one and the same end;)
>
> 107 „Τὸν φονέ᾽ ἡμῶν ἐκδέχεται Καΐνα.”  
> („Caïna awaits our murderer.”)

- *Ἔρως ἤγαγεν ἡμᾶς εἰς ταὐτὸν τέλος* retains the third initial *Amor*. *ἤγαγεν* (3rd sg. reduplicated second aor. act. indic. of *ἄγω*) takes plural *ἡμᾶς*, not a dual, and *εἰς* with the accusative of destination. *ταὐτόν* is crasis of *τὸ αὐτό*, ‘the same’, agreeing with neuter *τέλος*: ‘to the same end’ interprets *ad una morte* without naming death.
- *τὸν φονέ’ ἡμῶν ἐκδέχεται Καΐνα* reverses the Italian order, reserving the subject *Καΐνα* for the end. *φονέ’* is elided *φονέα* (acc. sg. of *φονεύς*); ‘our murderer’, with objective genitive *ἡμῶν*, replaces the entire *chi vita ci spense*. *ἐκδέχεται* is 3rd sg. pres. mid. indic. of *ἐκδέχομαι*, ‘await’.

> 108 Queste parole da lor ci fur porte.  

These words from them were offered to us.

> 108 Τούσδ᾽ ἡμῖν οὗτοι προσεφθέγξαντο λόγους.  
> (These words they addressed to us.)

- *τούσδ’ ἡμῖν οὗτοι προσεφθέγξαντο λόγους* ‘these words these persons addressed to us’ makes passive *ci fur porte* active. *τούσδ’* (elided *τούσδε*, acc. masc. pl. of *ὅδε*) points forward to the separated *λόγους*; *οὗτοι* (nom. masc. pl. of *οὗτος*) makes *da lor* the subject, while dative *ἡμῖν* retains the recipients. *προσεφθέγξαντο* is 3rd pl. first aor. mid. indic. of *προσφθέγγομαι*, a middle verb of utterance, with the augment after the preverb.

> 109 Da che io intesi quelle anime offense,  
> 110 chinai il viso, e tanto il tenni basso,  
> 111 finchè il poeta mi disse: "Che pense?"  

After I had heard those wounded souls, I bowed my face, and held it low until the Poet said to me: "What art thou thinking of?"

> 109 Ὡς δ᾽ ἔγνων αὐτὰς τὰς ψυχὰς τετρωμένας,  
> (And when I knew those wounded souls,)
>
> 110 Κλίνας τὴν ὄψιν, οὕτως ἱστάμην κύπτων,  
> (Bowing my face, thus I stood bending down,)
>
> 111 Ἕως ὅτ᾽ εἶπεν ὁ ποιητής· “Τί σκέπτῃ;”  
> (Until the poet said: “What are you pondering?”)

- *ὡς δ’ ἔγνων αὐτὰς τὰς ψυχὰς τετρωμένας* takes *da che* temporally: ‘when I perceived the souls themselves wounded’. *δ’* is connective *δέ*; *ἔγνων* (1st sg. root aor. act. indic. of *γιγνώσκω*) interprets *intesi* as apprehension rather than specifically hearing. *αὐτάς* before article and noun is intensive rather than the ordinary demonstrative for *quelle*. *τετρωμένας* (perf. mid./pass. participle of *τιτρώσκω*, acc. fem. pl.) presents the souls as being in a wounded state.
- *κλίνας τὴν ὄψιν* ‘having lowered my face’ subordinates *chinai* as a nom. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *κλίνω*. *οὕτως ἱστάμην κύπτων* ‘thus I stood bending down’ replaces *tanto il tenni basso*: *ἱστάμην* (1st sg. imperf. mid. indic. of *ἵστημι*) and the simultaneous pres. act. participle *κύπτων* describe the speaker's sustained posture, rather than his holding his face low.
- *ἕως ὅτ’ εἶπεν ὁ ποιητής* ‘until the poet said’ joins *ἕως* to elided *ὅτε*; the aorist indicative marks the endpoint actually reached. The addressee of *mi disse* is left implicit. *τί σκέπτῃ;* has interrogative accusative *τί* and the contracted 2nd sg. pres. mid. indic. of *σκέπτομαι*, ‘what are you considering?’

> 112 Quando risposi, cominciai : "O lasso,  
> 113 quanti dolci pensier, quanto disio  
> 114 menò costoro al doloroso passo!"  

When I answered, I began: "Ah me! what sweet thoughts, what longing led them to the woful pass!"

> 112 Ἀπεκρίθην δὲ φωνήσας· “Οἴμοι τάλας!  
> (And I answered aloud: “Alas, wretched me!)
>
> 113 „Οἷοι λογισμοὶ γλυκεῖς, οἷός τε πόθος  
> („What sweet thoughts, and what desire)
>
> 114 „Ἤγαγ᾽ αὐτοὺς εἰς οὕτως ἀλγεινὸν τέρμα!”  
> („Led them to so grievous a goal!”)

- *ἀπεκρίθην δὲ φωνήσας* makes ‘I answered’ the principal verb (*ἀπεκρίθην*, 1st sg. aor. passive in form of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, active in meaning). Instead of *quando … cominciai*, *φωνήσας* (nom. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *φωνέω*) introduces the utterance as ‘speaking aloud’. *οἴμοι τάλας* renders *O lasso*: the nominative adjective applies to the speaker, ‘alas, wretched as I am!’
- *οἷοι λογισμοὶ γλυκεῖς, οἷός τε πόθος* turns quantitative *quanti … quanto* into qualitative exclamations, ‘what manner of sweet thoughts, and what longing!’ The forms of *οἷος* agree first with a masculine plural, then a masculine singular; enclitic *τε* links the subjects and accounts for the additional accent in *οἷός*.
- *ἤγαγ’ αὐτοὺς* is *ἤγαγε* elided before its object, ‘led them’. The singular verb agrees with the nearer *πόθος*, as *menò* follows *disio*. *εἰς οὕτως ἀλγεινὸν τέρμα* ‘to so grievous an end’ turns *passo* into an endpoint and adds *οὕτως* to intensify the adjective.

> 115 Poi mi rivolsi a loro, e parlai io,  
> 116 e cominciai : "Francesca, i tuoi martiri  
> 117 a lagrimar mi fanno tristo e pio.  

Then I turned again to them; and I spoke, and began: "Francesca, thy torments make me weep with grief and pity.

> 115 Ἔπειτα στραφεὶς πρὸς αὐτὰς ἀπετάθην,  
> (Then turning toward them I addressed them,)
>
> 116 Λέγων· “Φραγκίσκη, φεῦ! τὰ παθήματά σου  
> (Saying: “Francesca, alas! your sufferings)
>
> 117 „Ἔλεον ἐμποιεῖ μοι, κλαυθμοὺς καὶ λύπας.  
> („Move me to pity, to weeping, and to grief.)

- *ἔπειτα στραφεὶς πρὸς αὐτὰς ἀπετάθην, λέγων* compresses three finite Italian verbs. *στραφείς* (nom. masc. sg. aor. passive participle of *στρέφω*, intransitive ‘having turned’) precedes the main action. *πρὸς αὐτάς* ‘toward them’ refers back grammatically to feminine *ψυχάς*. *ἀπετάθην* (1st sg. aor. pass. indic. of *ἀποτείνω*) is used in the sense ‘I addressed myself [to them]’; *λέγων* (pres. act. participle of *λέγω*) introduces the accompanying words. Neither emphatic *io* nor the inception in *cominciai* is separately rendered.
- *Φραγκίσκη, φεῦ!* gives the name a Greek feminine vocative and adds an exclamation of distress. *τὰ παθήματά σου* ‘your sufferings’ replaces *i tuoi martiri* with a noun and enclitic possessive genitive; the enclitic gives *παθήματά* its additional accent.
- *ἔλεον ἐμποιεῖ μοι, κλαυθμοὺς καὶ λύπας* ‘produces in me pity, weepings and griefs’ recasts *a lagrimar mi fanno tristo e pio*. The infinitive and the adjectives become three accusative noun objects, and the person affected becomes dative *μοι*. *ἐμποιεῖ* (3rd sg. pres. act. indic. of *ἐμποιέω*) is singular by normal Greek agreement with a neuter plural subject, unlike plural *fanno*.

> 118 Ma dimmi : al tempo de' dolci sospiri,  
> 119 a che e come concedette amore,  
> 120 che conosceste i dubbiosi desiri?"  

But tell me: in the time of the sweet sighs, by what and how love granted you to know the dubious desires?"

> 118 „Ἀλλά μοι λέγ᾽ ἐν καιρῷ τῶν γλυκυθύμων  
> („But tell me, in the season of the sweet-hearted)
>
> 119 „Στεναγμῶν, εἰς τί καὶ πῶς ἆρ᾽ ὑμῖν ἔρως  
> („Sighs, by what and how to you then love)
>
> 120 „Γνῶν᾽ ἐπέτρεψε τοὺς κινδυνώδεις πόθους;”  
> („Granted to know your perilous desires?”)

- *ἀλλά μοι λέγ’* ‘but tell me’ places enclitic dative *μοι* before elided *λέγε* (2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *λέγω*). *ἐν καιρῷ τῶν γλυκυθύμων στεναγμῶν* retains the Italian genitive dependence, with *ἐν* and the dative for time. The compound adjective *γλυκύθυμος*, literally ‘sweet of spirit’, transfers a quality of feeling to the sighs themselves.
- *εἰς τί καὶ πῶς ἆρ’* closely tracks *a che e come*. *εἰς τί* is literally ‘to what [end]?’, not an instrumental ‘by what means?’; the directional preposition follows the shape of the Italian question. *πῶς* asks about manner, and *ἆρ’* is the elided interrogative particle *ἆρα*, not inferential *ἄρα*.
- *ὑμῖν ἔρως γνῶν’ ἐπέτρεψε* replaces *concedette amore, che conosceste* with the usual construction of ‘permit’: dative of the persons permitted (*ὑμῖν*, plural rather than dual) and an infinitive whose subject they supply. *ἐπέτρεψε* is 3rd sg. aor. act. indic. of *ἐπιτρέπω*; *γνῶν’* is a poetic shortening of *γνῶναι*, the root-aorist active infinitive of *γιγνώσκω*. Its object *τοὺς κινδυνώδεις πόθους* means ‘the perilous desires’: Musurus interprets *dubbiosi* as fraught with danger rather than merely uncertain.

> 121 Ed ella a me : "Nessun maggior dolore,  
> 122 che ricordarsi del tempo felice  
> 123 nella miseria : e ciò sa il tuo dottore.  

And she to me: "*There is no greater pain than* *to recall a happy time in wretchedness*; and this thy teacher knows.

> 121 Αὐτὴ δὲ πρός με· “Μείζων οὐκ ἔστι λύπη  
> (And she to me: “There is no greater grief)
>
> 122 „Ἢ τὸ διαμεμνῆσθαι καιροὺς ὀλβίους  
> („Than to remember happy times)
>
> 123 „Ἐν δυστυχίαις· τοῦτ᾽ οἶδ᾽ ὁ σὸς ἡγήτωρ.  
> („In misfortune; this your leader knows.)

- *αὐτὴ δὲ πρός με* keeps the ellipsis of *ed ella a me*: feminine nominative *αὐτή* is the speaker, *πρός* with accusative gives the addressee, and no verb of saying is supplied. *μείζων οὐκ ἔστι λύπη* puts the comparative first: ‘greater grief does not exist’. *μείζων* (nom. fem. sg. comparative of *μέγας*) agrees with *λύπη*; negative existential *οὐκ ἔστι* does the work of *nessun*.
- *ἢ τὸ διαμεμνῆσθαι καιροὺς ὀλβίους* introduces the second term of comparison with *ἤ*, ‘than’, and makes the act compared a noun by prefixing neuter singular *τό* to the infinitive. *διαμεμνῆσθαι* is the perfect middle infinitive of *διαμιμνῄσκομαι*, with the reduplication inside the compound: the perfect has the present force ‘have in memory’. *καιροὺς ὀλβίους* is its accusative object, ‘happy times’, plural for singular *del tempo felice*, with no preposition corresponding to *di*.
- *ἐν δυστυχίαις* likewise pluralizes *nella miseria*, ‘amid misfortunes’. *τοῦτ’ οἶδ’ ὁ σὸς ἡγήτωρ* ‘this your leader knows’ retains the Italian object–verb–subject order but omits *e*. *τοῦτ’* is elided neuter accusative *τοῦτο*; *οἶδ’* is elided *οἶδε*, 3rd sg. of the perfect *οἶδα* with present meaning. The poetic agent noun *ἡγήτωρ* interprets *dottore* as a guide or leader; *σός* is the agreeing attributive possessive.

## Lines 124–142 — The Reading, the Kiss, and the Fall

> 124 Ma se a conoscer la prima radice  
> 125 del nostro amor tu hai cotanto affetto,  
> 126 farò come colui che piange e dice.  

But if thou hast such desire to learn the first root of our love, I will do like one who weeps and tells.

> 124 „Ἀλλ᾽ εἰ τὴν πρώτην ἐπίστασθαι σὺ ῥίζαν  
> („But if you, to know the first root)
>
> 125 „Ἔρωτος ἡμῶν ἔφεσιν τόσην ἔχεις,  
> („Of our love, have so great a longing,)
>
> 126 „Πράξω καθώς τις ἅμα κλαίει καὶ λέγει.  
> („I will do as one who weeps and speaks together.)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ εἰ* answers to *Ma se*: *ἀλλά* loses its final vowel before *εἰ*. The condition has a present indicative, *ἔχεις* (2nd sg. of *ἔχω*), and a future indicative in the main clause, *πράξω* (1st sg. fut. act. of *πράσσω*), ‘I shall act’, for *farò*. Neither clause is put in the optative.
- As in *a conoscer … tu hai cotanto affetto*, the infinitive comes before the expression of desire on which it depends. *ἐπίστασθαι* (pres. mid. inf. of *ἐπίσταμαι*) means ‘to know’ rather than ‘to learn’. Musurus separates the accusative *τὴν πρώτην* from its noun *ῥίζαν* by inserting both the infinitive and *σύ*. The explicit nominative pronoun reproduces *tu* but stands well before its verb *ἔχεις*.
- *ἔρωτος ἡμῶν* follows *ῥίζαν* as *del nostro amor* follows *radice*: *ἔρωτος* is the gen. sg. of *ἔρως*, and the possessive genitive *ἡμῶν* replaces the adjective *nostro*. *ἔφεσιν τόσην ἔχεις*, ‘you have so great a longing’, reverses the order of verb and object in *hai cotanto affetto*; *τόσην* agrees with *ἔφεσιν* (acc. sg. fem. of *ἔφεσις*).
- *καθώς τις … κλαίει καὶ λέγει*, ‘as someone weeps and speaks’, replaces *come colui che* and its relative clause with a comparative clause whose subject is indefinite *τις*. The verbs are both 3rd sg. pres. act. indicatives; the supplied adverb *ἅμα* makes their simultaneity explicit.

> 127 Noi leggevamo un giorno per diletto  
> 128 di Lancillotto, come amor lo strinse;  

One day, for pastime, we read of Lancelot, how love constrained him;

> 127 „Συνανεγινώσκομέν ποτε πρὸς χάριν,  
> („We were reading together one day for pleasure,)
>
> 128 „Πῶς ποτ᾽ ἔρωτι Λαγκιλῶτος ἑάλω·  
> („How Lancelot was once ensnared by love;)

- *Συνανεγινώσκομεν* (1st pl. imperf. act. ind. of *συναναγινώσκω*) keeps the imperfect of *leggevamo*. The prefix *συν-* supplies ‘together’, and the augment is internal, as in *ἀνεγίνωσκον*. The stem *γινωσκ-* lacks the second gamma of Attic *γιγνωσκ-*. Enclitic *ποτε*, ‘once’, dispenses with the noun in *un giorno*; *πρὸς χάριν* uses *πρός* with the accusative to express the purpose *per diletto*.
- *πῶς ποτ᾽ … ἑάλω*, ‘how he was once captured’, gives the content of the reading; *ποτ᾽* is elided *ποτε*. Instead of introducing Lancelot by *di Lancillotto* and resuming him with the object pronoun *lo*, Musurus makes *Λαγκιλῶτος* the nominative subject within the *πῶς* clause. *ἑάλω* is the 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἁλίσκομαι*, active in form but passive in sense. Thus *amor lo strinse*, with love as subject, becomes *ἔρωτι … ἑάλω*, with love in the dative as the force by which he was taken.

> 129 soli eravamo e senza alcun sospetto.  

we were alone, and without all suspicion.

> 129 „Ἦμεν μόνοι καὶ μηδὲν ὑπονοοῦντες·  
> („We were alone and suspecting nothing;)

- *ἦμεν* is the 1st pl. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*; it precedes the predicate adjective *μόνοι* (nom. pl. masc.), reversing *soli eravamo*. Greek uses the plural, not the dual, for the pair.
- *καὶ μηδὲν ὑπονοοῦντες*, ‘and suspecting nothing’, turns *senza alcun sospetto* from a prepositional phrase into a participial expression sharing the subject of *ἦμεν*. *ὑπονοοῦντες* is the contracted pres. act. participle of *ὑπονοέω* (nom. pl. masc.); *μηδέν* is its neuter accusative object. The participle describes an ongoing state, parallel to *μόνοι*.

> 130 Per più fiate gli occhi ci sospinse  
> 131 quella lettura, e scolorocci il viso;  

Several times that reading urged our eyes to meet, and changed the colour of our faces;

> 130 „Πολλάκις ἡμῶν τὤμματα συνηντήθη,  
> („Many times our eyes met,)
>
> 131 „Ὁ χρώς τ᾽ ἐτράπη συναναγινωσκόντων·  
> („And our colour changed as we read together;)

- *πολλάκις*, ‘often’, compresses *per più fiate*. In Dante *quella lettura* is the delayed subject of both verbs; in Greek the eyes and the complexion are successively the subjects. *ἡμῶν τὤμματα* is ‘our eyes’, with the possessive genitive first and crasis of *τὰ ὄμματα* in *τὤμματα*. The neuter plural takes a singular verb, *συνηντήθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *συναντάω*). The passive form is used in the reciprocal sense ‘met’: Greek states the meeting rather than the impulse expressed by *ci sospinse*.
- *ὁ χρώς τ᾽ ἐτράπη*, ‘and the complexion changed’, has singular *χρώς*, ‘skin, complexion’, in place of *il viso*. *τ᾽* is postpositive *τε* elided before *ἐτράπη* (3rd sg. second aor. pass. ind. of *τρέπω*), here intransitive in sense. The Greek does not specify the loss of colour conveyed by *scolorocci*.
- The reading reappears not as the cause but as an accompanying circumstance in *συναναγινωσκόντων*, ‘as [we] were reading together’ (gen. pl. masc. pres. act. participle). Its understood subject is the pair already expressed by *ἡμῶν*; the genitive gives the phrase the force of a temporal genitive absolute.

> 132 ma solo un punto fu quel che ci vinse.  

but one moment alone it was that overcame us.

> 132 „Ἀπώλεσε δ᾽ ἡμᾶς ἓν μόνον χωρίον.  
> („But one single passage undid us.)

- *Ἀπώλεσε δ᾽ ἡμᾶς*, ‘but it undid us’, puts the verb first: *ἀπώλεσε* is the 3rd sg. of the transitive first aor. act. ind. of *ἀπόλλυμι*, with *ἡμᾶς* as direct object. Elided, postpositive *δέ* renders *ma*. The verb is stronger than *vinse*.
- The postponed subject *ἓν μόνον χωρίον* (nom. sg. neut.) means ‘one passage alone’: *χωρίον* interprets *punto* as a place in the text, not a moment in time. Greek keeps both the numeral and its restrictive adjective but drops the identifying construction *fu quel che* in favour of a single finite verb.

> 133 Quando leggemmo il disiato riso  
> 134 esser baciato da cotanto amante,  

When we read how the fond smile was kissed by such a lover,

> 133 „Ὅτ᾽ ἀνέγνωμεν, πῶς ὁ ποθεινὸς γέλως  
> („When we read how the longed-for smile)
>
> 134 „Ὑπ᾽ ἐραστοῦ τοιούτου κατεφιλήθη,  
> („Was kissed by such a lover,)

- *Ὅτ᾽ ἀνέγνωμεν* renders *Quando leggemmo* with elided *ὅτε* and the 1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *ἀναγιγνώσκω*. The aorist marks the particular act of reading, in contrast with the earlier imperfect.
- After *leggemmo*, Italian has *il disiato riso esser baciato*, an infinitive with its own subject. Greek substitutes a finite content clause introduced by *πῶς*, ‘how’: *ὁ ποθεινὸς γέλως* is nominative, and *κατεφιλήθη* is 3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *καταφιλέω*. *ποθεινός*, ‘longed-for’, is a verbal adjective rather than a participle; the compound verb suggests a warm or emphatic kiss.
- *ὑπ᾽ ἐραστοῦ τοιούτου* keeps the personal agent of *da cotanto amante*: elided *ὑπό* governs the genitive singular of *ἐραστής*, with agreeing *τοιούτου*. The demonstrative expresses quality, ‘of such a kind’, rather than degree; the agent precedes the passive verb instead of following the infinitive.

> 135 questi, che mai da me non fia diviso,  
> 136 la bocca mi baciò tutto tremante:  

he, who shall never be divided from me, kissed my mouth all trembling:

> 135 „Οὗτος, ὅνπερ οὐδέποτ’ ἐγκαταλείψω,  
> („He, whom I shall never abandon,)
>
> 136 „Μ’ ἐφίλησε τὸ στόμα συνάπας τρέμων.  
> („Kissed me upon the mouth, trembling all over.)

- *οὗτος*, ‘this man’, reproduces demonstrative *questi*. In *ὅνπερ οὐδέποτ’ ἐγκαταλείψω*, the relative is accusative masculine singular, the object of *ἐγκαταλείψω* (1st sg. fut. act. ind. of *ἐγκαταλείπω*); attached *-περ* gives the relative emphasis. *οὐδέποτ’* is elided *οὐδέποτε*, ‘never’. Musurus thus gives the speaker agency, ‘whom I shall never abandon’, where *che mai da me non fia diviso* has the man as subject of a future passive.
- *Μ’ ἐφίλησε τὸ στόμα* closely follows *la bocca mi baciò* in naming both person and body part, but changes their order. *Μ’* is elided accusative *με*, the person kissed; *τὸ στόμα* is an accusative specifying the part affected, ‘on the mouth’. *ἐφίλησε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *φιλέω*) is the simple verb beside the compound in 134.
- *συνάπας τρέμων* keeps the agreement of *tutto tremante* with the man: *τρέμων* is the nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *τρέμω*, simultaneous with the kiss, and the intensive adjective *συνάπας*, in the same case, means ‘all of him’, hence ‘all over’.

> 137 Galeotto fu il libro, e chi lo scrisse;  

the book, and he who wrote it, was a Galeotto;

> 137 „Γαλεῶτος ἦν ἡ βίβλος ὅ, τε γράψας.  
> („Galeotto was the book and he who wrote it.)

- *Γαλεῶτος ἦν ἡ βίβλος* preserves the marked order of *Galeotto fu il libro*: the name is a predicate nominative, the articular *ἡ βίβλος* the subject. *ἦν* is the 3rd sg. imperfect of *εἰμί*, the ordinary Greek past copula, against the Italian preterite *fu*.
- In the printed *ὅ, τε γράψας*, the construction is article plus substantival participle, with connective *τε* postpositive: ‘and the one who wrote’. *γράψας* is the nom. sg. masc. first aor. act. participle of *γράφω*; the preceding monosyllable bears an accent before enclitic *τε*. The participle replaces *chi lo scrisse*, leaving the object *lo* understood. As in Italian, the singular copula precedes the addition of the second subject.

> 138 quel giorno più non vi leggemmo avante."  

that day we read in it no farther."

> 138 „Ἔκτοτ’ οὐκ ἀνέγνωμέν τι περαιτέρω.“  
> („From that time we read no further.“)

- *Ἔκτοτ’* (elided *ἔκτοτε*, ‘from then on’) changes the time reference of *quel giorno*: it gives a starting point rather than limiting the statement to that day. *οὐκ* negates *ἀνέγνωμεν*, the same aorist as in 133; its final syllable receives an additional accent in *ἀνέγνωμέν τι* before enclitic *τι*.
- *τι* is the acc. sg. neut. indefinite pronoun, ‘anything’, under the negation. *περαιτέρω* is a comparative adverb, ‘further’, corresponding to *più … avante*; there is no separate equivalent of locative *vi*, “in it”.

> 139 Mentre che l' uno spirto questo disse,  
> 140 l' altro piangeva sì, che di pietade  

Whilst the one spirit thus spake, the other wept so,

> 139 Τῶν δύο σκιῶν ἡ μὲν ἔλεγε ταῦτα,  
> (One of the two shades was saying these words,)
>
> 140 Ἡ δ’ ἔκλαιε τοσοῦτον, ὥστ’ ἐλεήσας  
> (While the other wept so much that, moved with pity,)

- *Τῶν δύο σκιῶν*, ‘of the two shades’, is a partitive genitive set before the contrasted subjects *ἡ μὲν … ἡ δ’*, ‘the one … the other’. *δύο* is the numeral with a plural noun, not a dual construction. Feminine *ἡ* in each member takes its gender from *σκιῶν* (gen. pl. of *σκιά*), Musurus’ replacement for masculine *spirto*; *δ’* is elided *δέ*, answering to *μέν*.
- Greek coordinates the clauses instead of subordinating the first with *Mentre che*. The simultaneous actions are expressed by two imperfects, *ἔλεγε* and *ἔκλαιε* (3rd sg. act. ind. of *λέγω* and *κλαίω*): the first thus differs in aspect from *disse*, while the second matches *piangeva*. *ταῦτα*, ‘these things’, is the neuter plural object for singular *questo*. *τοσοῦτον*, an adverbial neuter accusative, gives the degree of the weeping and anticipates the result introduced by *ὥστ’*.

> 141 io venni men così com' io morisse;  

that I fainted with pity, as if I had been dying;

> 141 Ἐνόμισ’ ὄντ’ ἐμαυτὸν ἐγγὺς θανάτου,  
> (I thought myself near death,)

- The Greek result construction begins already at the end of 140: *ὥστ’ ἐλεήσας ἐνόμισ’*, ‘so that, having felt pity, I thought’. Elided *ὥστε* with a finite indicative presents an actual result. *ἐλεήσας* (nom. sg. masc. aor. act. participle of *ἐλεέω*) converts *di pietade* into a circumstantial participle with causal force, agreeing with the implicit first-person subject, not with either shade.
- *Ἐνόμισ’* is elided *ἐνόμισα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *νομίζω*). Musurus gives no direct verb for *venni men*, nor a hypothetical comparison corresponding to *così com’ io morisse*. Instead he makes nearness to death the speaker’s judgment: *ὄντ’ ἐμαυτὸν ἐγγὺς θανάτου*, ‘myself being near death’. *ὄντ’* is elided *ὄντα*, the acc. sg. masc. pres. participle of *εἰμί*, predicative to reflexive *ἐμαυτόν*. The participle is notable where an accusative and infinitive (*εἶναι*) would be a more usual complement of a verb of thinking. *ἐγγύς* governs the genitive *θανάτου*.

> 142 e caddi, come corpo morto cade.  

and fell, as a dead body falls.

> 142 Χαμαί τ’ ἔπεσον, ὡς σῶμα νεκρὸν πίπτει.  
> (And fell to the ground, as a dead body falls.)

- *Χαμαί τ’ ἔπεσον* supplies the directional adverb *χαμαί*, ‘to the ground’, before connective *τε*, elided before *ἔπεσον*. The verb is the 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *πίπτω*, answering to *caddi*.
- *ὡς σῶμα νεκρὸν πίπτει* follows *come corpo morto cade* closely: comparative *ὡς* introduces a finite clause, *νεκρόν* agrees with anarthrous *σῶμα* (nom. sg. neut.), and *πίπτει* is a generalizing present (3rd sg. act. ind.). The change from aorist *ἔπεσον* to present *πίπτει* preserves both the repetition of the verb and the tense contrast of *caddi … cade*.

## Summary — Where the Grammar Shifts and the Echo Holds

Musurus often keeps Dante’s order of emphasis while changing which action carries the finite verb. In lines 85–87, *uscir* becomes the aorist participle *λιποῦσαι* ‘having left’, while *venendo* becomes the main verb *ἦλθον* ‘they came’. Elsewhere a present participle expresses an action perceived (*προσερχομένας* ‘approaching’ after *εἶδον* ‘I saw’), and a future participle expresses purpose (*ὀψόμενος* ‘in order to see’ for *visitando*). Nor is the preference for participles uniform: in the description of the storm, *voltando e percotendo* become the finite presents *στρέφει* ‘turns’ and *πλήττει* ‘strikes’, each given the weight of a main action.

Particles make relations explicit without necessarily reproducing the Italian conjunctions. Postpositive *γάρ* ‘for’ dispenses with the reporting formula *Dico, che*; *ὅταν* with the subjunctive marks the recurrence implicit in *quando*. At the close, *ἡ μὲν … ἡ δέ* ‘the one … the other’ replaces temporal subordination with balanced clauses, while two imperfects convey simultaneity. Musurus is equally attentive to patterns he can retain: *Ἐκεῖ … Ἐκεῖ* ‘there … there’ brings both instances of *quivi* to the head of their clauses, and the three successive openings with *Amor* remain three openings with *Ἔρως*.

Some of the most consequential readings turn on a single word or a change of voice. *Τολμηράν* ‘daring’ characterizes the journey differently from *fatale* ‘fated’; *χωρίον* ‘passage’ makes *punto* a place in the text; and *ἔκτοτε* ‘from then on’ extends what *quel giorno* limits to one day. More pointedly, *non fia diviso* ‘will not be parted’ becomes *οὐδέποτ’ ἐγκαταλείψω* ‘I shall never abandon’: the Italian future passive leaves the agent unnamed, whereas the Greek makes the speaker herself answer for the bond. The final comparison is strikingly close by contrast. In *ἔπεσον … πίπτει* ‘I fell … falls’, Musurus preserves not only the repeated verb of *caddi … cade* but also its turn from a single past event to a general present.
