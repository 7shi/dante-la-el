# Inferno 13

## Lines 1–21 — The Pathless Wood and Its Harpies

> 1 Non era ancor di là Nesso arrivato,  
> 2 quando noi ci mettemmo per un bosco,  
> 3 che da nessun sentiero era segnato.  

Nessus had not yet reached the other side, when we moved into a wood, which by no path was marked.

> 1 Οὐκ ἔφθη Νέσσος ἐπανακάμψας πέραν,  
> (No sooner had Nessus returned across,)
>
> 2 Καί τινος ἐντὸς εὑρέθημεν δρυμῶνος,  
> (Than we found ourselves within a forest,)
>
> 3 Ἔνθ᾽ ὁδῶν οὔτ᾽ ἀτραπῶν ὑπῆρχεν ἴχνος·  
> (Where there was no trace of roads nor of paths;)

- *Οὐκ ἔφθη Νέσσος ἐπανακάμψας πέραν, καί…* renders *Non era ancor di là Nesso arrivato, quando…* by the Greek idiom ‘Nessus had no sooner come back across than…’. *ἔφθη* is the 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *φθάνω* ‘anticipate, be beforehand’; despite its ending it is not passive. Its supplementary participle *ἐπανακάμψας* (nom. sg. masc., aor. act. of *ἐπανακάμπτω*) agrees with *Νέσσος* and carries the main idea of motion. *πέραν* is adverbial, ‘across’; the verb specifies a **return**, where *arrivato* specifies arrival.
- *καί* introduces a second coordinate clause in place of temporal *quando*. *τινος ἐντὸς εὑρέθημεν δρυμῶνος* is literally ‘we were found inside a certain wood’: *εὑρέθημεν* (1st pl. aor. pass. ind. of *εὑρίσκω*) gives the resulting situation rather than the movement of *ci mettemmo per*. *ἐντός* governs the genitive *δρυμῶνος* (from *δρυμών*); its indefinite modifier *τινος* (from *τις*) is brought forward, away from its noun.
- *ἔνθ᾽* is the relative adverb *ἔνθα* ‘where’, elided before *ὁδῶν*, with *θ* for *τ* before the rough breathing. In place of the wood as subject of passive *era segnato*, Greek has *ὑπῆρχεν ἴχνος* ‘there existed a trace’: *ὑπῆρχεν* is the 3rd sg. imperfect active of *ὑπάρχω*, with internal augment. The genitives *ὁδῶν … ἀτραπῶν* depend on *ἴχνος*; *οὔτ᾽ … οὔτ᾽* (elided *οὔτε*) doubles the single *nessun sentiero* into ‘neither of roads nor of tracks’.

> 4 Non frondi verdi, ma di color fosco;  
> 5 non rami schietti, ma nodosi e involti;  
> 6 non pomi v' eran, ma stecchi con tosco.  

Not green the foliage, but of colour dusky; not smooth the branches, but gnarled and warped; apples none were there, but withered sticks with poison.

> 4 Οὐ φύλλα πράσιν᾽, ἀλλὰ χροιᾶς ζοφώδους·  
> (No green leaves, but of a dusky colour;)
>
> 5 Οὐ λεῖοι κλῶνες, ἀλλὰ στρεβλοὶ κὠζώδεις·  
> (No smooth boughs, but twisted and gnarled;)
>
> 6 Οὐκ ἀκρόδρυ᾽, ἀλλ᾽ ἄκανθαι φαρμακίδες.  
> (No fruits, but poisonous thorns.)

- Musurus keeps the three successive contrasts, *οὐ … ἀλλά*, without a finite verb even in the third, where Dante supplies *v’ eran*. In *φύλλα πράσιν᾽* ‘green leaves’, *πράσιν᾽* is elided *πράσινα*, neut. pl. agreeing with *φύλλα*. After *ἀλλά*, the genitive of quality *χροιᾶς ζοφώδους* ‘of a gloomy hue’ closely follows *di color fosco*; *ζοφώδους* is gen. sg. of *ζοφώδης*.
- *λεῖοι κλῶνες* ‘smooth boughs’ reverses the order of *rami schietti*. The predicate adjectives *στρεβλοὶ κὠζώδεις* ‘twisted and knotty’ likewise reverse the sense-order of *nodosi e involti*. All three adjectives are nom. pl. masc.; *κὠζώδεις* represents *καὶ ὀζώδεις* by crasis.
- *ἀκρόδρυ᾽* is elided *ἀκρόδρυα*, neut. pl. ‘tree-fruits’, for *pomi*. *ἄκανθαι φαρμακίδες* ‘poisonous thorns’ substitutes thorns for *stecchi* and a feminine nominative plural qualifier for the prepositional *con tosco*. The forms of the particles here follow their surroundings: *οὐκ* before the smooth vowel of *ἀκρόδρυ᾽*, *ἀλλ᾽* for *ἀλλά* before *ἄκανθαι*.

> 7 Non han sì aspri sterpi nè sì folti  
> 8 quelle fiere selvagge, che in odio hanno  
> 9 tra Cecina e Corneto i luoghi colti.  

No holts so rough or dense have those wild beasts, that hate the cultivated tracts, between Cecina and Corneto.

> 7 Οὐκ ἔχουσ᾽ οὕτω στρυφνὰς καὶ πυκνὰς βάτους  
> (They have not such rough and dense thickets,)
>
> 8 Ἄγριοι θῆρες, οἱ μισοῦντες τὰς μέσον  
> (The wild beasts that hate, between)
>
> 9 Κορνέτου καὶ Καικίνης ἡμέρους γαίας.  
> (Corneto and Cecina, the cultivated lands.)

- *οὐκ ἔχουσ᾽ οὕτω στρυφνὰς καὶ πυκνὰς βάτους* retains the Italian order, verb and object before the subject *ἄγριοι θῆρες* ‘wild beasts’ (without an equivalent of *quelle*). *ἔχουσ᾽* is elided *ἔχουσι*, 3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *ἔχω*, not a participle. The adjectives are acc. pl. fem. agreeing with *βάτους* ‘brambles’. The single adverb *οὕτω* ‘so’ serves both adjectives, and positive *καί* under the initial negative replaces *nè sì*.
- *οἱ μισοῦντες* ‘the ones hating’ is an attributive participial phrase in place of *che in odio hanno*: article and present active participle of *μισέω* are nom. pl. masc., in agreement with *θῆρες*. Its object is *τὰς … ἡμέρους γαίας* ‘the tame lands’: poetic *γαίας* is acc. pl. of *γαῖα*, and *ἡμέρους*, from the two-ending adjective *ἥμερος*, is feminine here.
- Musurus sets *μέσον Κορνέτου καὶ Καικίνης* between the article and *ἡμέρους γαίας*, making it unambiguously a qualification of the lands. *μέσον*, an adverbial neuter accusative used prepositionally, takes the two place-names in the genitive and renders *tra*; the names themselves appear in reverse order.

> 10 Quivi le brutte Arpíe lor nidi fanno,  
> 11 che cacciar delle Strofade i Troiani  
> 12 con tristo annunzio di futuro danno.  

Here the unseemly Harpies make their nest[s], who chased the Trojans from the Strophades with dismal note of future woe.

> 10 Ὧδ᾽ Ἅρπυιαι δυσειδεῖς γύπας ποιοῦνται,  
> (Here the hideous Harpies make their nests,)
>
> 11 Αἱ διώξασαι τοὺς Τρῶας ἐκ Στροφάδων,  
> (Who drove the Trojans out from the Strophades,)
>
> 12 Τὴν δεινὴν αὐτοῖς προφητεύσασαι μοῖραν·  
> (Prophesying to them their dreadful fate;)

- *Ὧδ᾽* is elided *ὧδε* ‘here’, for *Quivi*. *Ἅρπυιαι δυσειδεῖς* ‘Harpies ill-looking’ puts the adjective after the name; *δυσειδεῖς* is nom. pl. fem. of *δυσειδής*. In *γύπας ποιοῦνται*, *γύπας* occupies the object position of *nidi*, though it is not the ordinary Greek word for nests (and the form can also be read as acc. pl. of *γύψ* ‘vulture’). *ποιοῦνται* is the contracted 3rd pl. pres. middle of *ποιέω*: ‘make for themselves’ conveys the force of *lor* without a possessive.
- *αἱ διώξασαι* ‘the ones who drove away’ replaces finite *che cacciar* with the article and the nom. pl. fem. aor. act. participle of *διώκω*. *τοὺς Τρῶας* is its accusative object; *ἐκ Στροφάδων*, with the gen. pl. after *ἐκ*, makes the point of departure explicit.
- *τὴν δεινὴν αὐτοῖς προφητεύσασαι μοῖραν* ‘having foretold them their dreadful fate’ turns *con tristo annunzio* into another nom. pl. fem. aor. act. participle, from *προφητεύω*. The added dative *αὐτοῖς* marks the recipients. The ominous quality of *tristo* is transferred from the announcement to its object, *τὴν δεινὴν … μοῖραν*; futurity is implicit in ‘foretell’, not separately expressed as in *futuro danno*.

> 13 Ale hanno late, e colli e visi umani,  
> 14 piè con artigli, e pennuto il gran ventre;  

Wide wings they have, and necks and faces human, feet with claws, and their large belly feathered;

> 13 Εὐρέα πτερά, λαιμόν τ᾽ ὄψιν τ᾽ ἀνθρώπου  
> (Broad wings, and neck and face of a man)
>
> 14 Ἔχουσι· γαμψώνυχες, εὐρυγαστῆρες,  
> (They have; crooked-clawed, broad-bellied,)

- *εὐρέα πτερά, λαιμόν τ᾽ ὄψιν τ᾽ ἀνθρώπου ἔχουσι* postpones ‘they have’ until after all its objects, whereas *hanno* interrupts *Ale … late*. *εὐρέα* (acc. pl. neut. of *εὐρύς*) agrees with *πτερά*. The paired *τ᾽ … τ᾽* (elided enclitic *τε*) link *λαιμόν* and *ὄψιν*, both acc. sg.: Greek uses a representative singular ‘neck and face of a human being’ for plural *colli e visi umani*, with a genitive in place of the adjective.
- After *ἔχουσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *ἔχω*), the syntax shifts to nominative plural descriptions of the subject: *γαμψώνυχες* ‘hook-clawed’, *εὐρυγαστῆρες* ‘broad-bellied’. The first compound adds the curvature of the claws and dispenses with *piè*; the second takes up *gran ventre* but leaves *pennuto* untranslated.

> 15 fanno lamenti in su gli alberi strani.  

they make rueful cries on the strange trees.

> 15 Θρηνῳδοῦσ᾽ ἄνω τῶν ἀλλοκότων δένδρων.  
> (They lament above upon the strange trees.)

- *θρηνῳδοῦσ᾽* is elided *θρηνῳδοῦσι*, contracted 3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *θρηνῳδέω* ‘sing a lament’: a single verb for *fanno lamenti*. *ἄνω τῶν ἀλλοκότων δένδρων* uses *ἄνω* with the genitive, literally ‘above the strange trees’, rather than precisely ‘on’ them as in *in su gli alberi*. *ἀλλοκότων* is an attributive adjective inside the article–noun group.

> 16 Lo buon maestro: "Prima che più entre  
> 17 sappi che se' nel secondo girone,"  
> 18 mi cominciò a dire, "e sarai, mentre  
> 19 che tu verrai nell' orribil sabbione.  

The kind Master began to say to me: “Before thou goest farther, know that thou art in the second round; and shalt be, until thou comest to the horrid sand.

> 16 Ὁ δὲ Προστάτης· “Πρὶν χωρήσωμεν πρόσω,  
> (And the Master: "Before we proceed further,)
>
> 17 „Ἴσθ᾽ εἶναί σ᾽ ἔνδον τοῦ δευτέρου κυκλίσκου,”  
> (Know that you are within the second little circle,")
>
> 18 Ἤρξατο λέγων, “ ἔσεσθαί σέ τε μέχρις  
> (He began to say, "and that you shall be so until)
>
> 19 „Εἰς τοὺς φρικώδεις ἀφίκῃ ψαμαθῶνας.  
> (You come to the dreadful sands.)

- *ὁ δὲ Προστάτης* ‘and the guide’ supplies connective, postpositive *δέ*; *Προστάτης* designates one who leads or protects, rather than specifically one who teaches, and *buon* has no separate rendering. Like Dante, Musurus interrupts the speech with the postponed reporting verb *ἤρξατο* (3rd sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἄρχω*, middle ‘begin’). *λέγων* is its supplementary pres. act. participle, nom. sg. masc., in place of *a dire*; *mi* has no expressed equivalent.
- *πρὶν χωρήσωμεν πρόσω* ‘before we advance farther’ changes singular *entre* to the inclusive 1st pl. *χωρήσωμεν* (aor. act. subj. of *χωρέω*), with the adverb *πρόσω* specifying forward motion. The finite prospective *πρίν*-clause follows the shape of *prima che*; an infinitive would be more usual after *πρίν* in this affirmative setting.
- *ἴσθ᾽* is elided *ἴσθι*, the 2nd sg. imperative of *οἶδα* ‘know’ (perfect in form, present in sense). *εἶναί σ᾽* gives it an accusative-and-infinitive complement instead of finite *che se’*: *σ᾽* is elided *σε*, subject of *εἶναι*, the present infinitive of *εἰμί*. *ἔνδον* ‘within’ governs *τοῦ δευτέρου κυκλίσκου*; the diminutive *κυκλίσκος* ‘little circle’ distinguishes *girone* more narrowly than ‘circle’.
- *ἔσεσθαί σέ τε* ‘and that you will be’ resumes the construction governed by *ἴσθι* after the interruption. *ἔσεσθαι* is the future middle infinitive of *εἰμί*, with *σέ* again its accusative subject; connective *τε* links this complement to the first. As with *sarai*, the place need not be repeated.
- *μέχρις … ἀφίκῃ* construes *mentre che* as marking a limit, ‘until’, not simultaneity. *ἀφίκῃ* is 2nd sg. second aor. mid. subj. of *ἀφικνέομαι*, prospective after *μέχρις*, here without *ἄν*. *εἰς τοὺς φρικώδεις … ψαμαθῶνας* gives the verb a destination in the accusative; plural *ψαμαθῶνας* (from *ψαμαθών*, ‘sandy tract’) replaces singular *sabbione*.

> 20 Però riguarda bene, e sì vedrai  
> 21 cose che torrien fede al mio sermone."  

Therefore look well, and thou shalt see things which would take away belief from my speech.”

> 20 „Νῦν βλέπε καλῶς, καὶ τοιαῦτά τιν᾽ ὄψει,  
> (Now look well, and such things you shall see,)
>
> 21 „Οἷά σ᾽ ἀπιστεῖν ἐμοῖς ἐποίει λόγοις.”  
> (As would make you disbelieve my words.")

- *νῦν βλέπε καλῶς* ‘now look carefully’ replaces inferential *Però* with a temporal adverb. *βλέπε* is the 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *βλέπω*. In *καὶ τοιαῦτά τιν᾽ ὄψει*, *ὄψει* is the 2nd sg. future middle indicative belonging to *ὁράω* ‘see’, active in meaning. *τοιαῦτά τιν᾽* (elided *τινα*) is its neut. pl. accusative object, ‘certain things of such a kind’: where *e sì vedrai cose* modifies the seeing with *sì*, Greek qualifies the things and prepares the relative *οἷα*.
- *οἷά σ᾽ ἀπιστεῖν ἐμοῖς ἐποίει λόγοις* is literally ‘such as would make you distrust my words’. Relative *οἷα* is nom. pl. neut., subject of singular *ἐποίει*, the regular Greek agreement with a neuter plural. *ἐποίει* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ποιέω*) is causative, with *σ᾽* (elided *σε*) as the person acted upon and the subject of *ἀπιστεῖν*, pres. act. infinitive of *ἀπιστέω*. The imperfect stands for the hypothetical force of conditional *torrien*, without the *ἄν* ordinarily expected in an Attic unreal expression.
- Dante's *torrien fede al mio sermone* makes belief the thing taken away; Greek makes the hearer the one who disbelieves. *ἐμοῖς … λόγοις* is dat. pl., governed by *ἀπιστεῖν*: ‘distrust my words’, with a plural in place of *sermone*.

## Lines 22–54 — The Wounded Trunk Speaks

> 22 Io sentia da ogni parte traer guai,  
> 23 e non vedea persona che il facesse :  
> 24 per ch' io tutto smarrito m' arrestai.  

Already I heard wailings uttered on every side, and saw no one to make them : wherefore I, all bewildered, stood still.

> 22 Ἤκουον πανταχόθεν θλιβεροὺς γόους,  
> (I heard on all sides mournful wailings,)
>
> 23 Ἀλλ᾽ ἔβλεπον οὐδένα ποιοῦντα τοῦτο·  
> (But I saw no one doing this;)
>
> 24 Ὥστ᾽ ἐμβεβροντημένος ὅλος ἱστάμην.  
> (So that, wholly thunderstruck, I stood still.)

- *Ἤκουον πανταχόθεν θλιβεροὺς γόους* ‘I heard mournful cries from every side’: *ἤκουον* (1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἀκούω*, with temporal augment) answers to *sentia*; *πανταχόθεν*, with the suffix *-θεν* ‘from’, gives the direction of *da ogni parte*. The sound heard is a direct accusative object, *θλιβεροὺς γόους*, rather than the Italian infinitival expression *traer guai*. There is no separate Greek word for “Already”.
- *Ἀλλ᾽ ἔβλεπον οὐδένα ποιοῦντα τοῦτο* ‘but I saw no one doing this’: elided *ἀλλά* makes *e* expressly adversative. After the imperfect *ἔβλεπον* (1st sg. of *βλέπω*), *οὐδένα* (acc. sg. masc. of *οὐδείς*) takes the supplementary present active participle *ποιοῦντα* (from *ποιέω*), agreeing with the person perceived. This replaces the negative antecedent and relative subjunctive of *non vedea persona che il facesse*; neuter *τοῦτο* stands for *il*.
- *Ὥστ᾽ … ἱστάμην* ‘so that I stood’: elided *ὥστε* with a finite indicative marks an actual result for *per ch’*. *Ἱστάμην* is the 1st sg. imperfect middle of *ἵστημι*, presenting the posture rather than the completed arrest of motion in *m’arrestai*. *Ἐμβεβροντημένος* (nom. sg. masc. perfect passive participle of *ἐμβροντάω*) describes the resulting condition, ‘thunderstruck’; *ὅλος* agrees with the speaker, as *tutto* does, rather than being an adverb.

> 25 Io credo ch' ei credette ch' io credesse  
> 26 che tante voci uscisser tra que' bronchi  
> 27 da gente che per noi si nascondesse.  

I think he thought that I was thinking so many voices came, amongst those stumps, from people who hid themselves on our account.

> 25 Ὡς νομίζοντα μ᾽ ἐνόμιζε, νομίζω,  
> (He thought, I think, that I was thinking)
>
> 26 Τοσαύτας φωνὰς ἐξέρχεσθ᾽ ἐκ πνευμάτων  
> (So many voices came forth from spirits)
>
> 27 Ὑπὸ τοὺς κορμοὺς δι᾽ ἡμᾶς κρυπτομένων.  
> (Hidden beneath the trunks on account of us.)

- *Ὡς νομίζοντα μ᾽ ἐνόμιζε, νομίζω* retains all three occurrences of *credere* with one Greek verb, *νομίζω*, but reverses their order. The innermost thought comes first: *νομίζοντα* is a present active participle, acc. sg. masc., agreeing with elided *με*; *ὡς* presents ‘me as thinking’ as the other person's supposition. This is a compressed characterization of the object of *ἐνόμιζε*, not a finite *ὡς*-clause. The imperfect *ἐνόμιζε* (3rd sg.) replaces the preterite *credette*, and the outermost *Io credo* becomes the parenthetic present *νομίζω* at the end.
- *Τοσαύτας φωνὰς ἐξέρχεσθ᾽* is the accusative and infinitive dependent on *νομίζοντα*: *φωνάς* is the subject, *ἐξέρχεσθαι* the present middle infinitive of *ἐξέρχομαι*, for the finite subjunctive *uscisser*. Its final *-αι* is elided before *ἐκ*; the printed *-σθ᾽* retains the *θ* of the infinitive ending.
- *Ἐκ πνευμάτων … κρυπτομένων* ‘from spirits hiding’ specifies *gente* as spirits and replaces *che … si nascondesse* with a present middle participle (gen. pl. of *κρύπτω*) agreeing with *πνευμάτων* after *ἐκ*. *Ὑπὸ τοὺς κορμούς* places their concealment ‘under the trunks’, rather than placing the emergence of the voices *tra que’ bronchi*; the demonstrative disappears. The accusative after *ὑπό* is less usual for a fixed position than the dative. *Δι᾽ ἡμᾶς* (elided *διά* with accusative) reads *per noi* causally.

> 28 Però disse il maestro : “ Se tu tronchi  
> 29 qualche fraschetta d' una d' este piante,  
> 30 li pensier ch' hai si faran tutti monchi.”  

Therefore the Master said : “If thou breakest off any little shoot from one of these plants, the thoughts, which thou hast, will all become defective.”

> 28 Εἶπ᾽ οὖν ἐμοὶ Προστάτης· “Ἐὰν κλαδίσκον  
> (Therefore the Master said to me: "If a little branch)
>
> 29 „Τινὰ θραύσῃς ἐξ ἑνὸς τῶν δένδρων τούτων,  
> (You break off from one of these trees,)
>
> 30 „Αὐτίκα τοὺς σοὺς λογισμοὺς μεταλλάξεις.”  
> (At once you will change your thoughts.")

- *Εἶπ᾽ οὖν ἐμοὶ Προστάτης* ‘the guide therefore said to me’: *εἶπ᾽* is elided *εἶπε*, 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *λέγω*. Postpositive *οὖν* renders *Però* inferentially; the dative addressee *ἐμοί* is supplied. Anarthrous *Προστάτης* ‘one who stands at the head, guide’ serves as a title for *il maestro*.
- *Ἐὰν κλαδίσκον τινὰ θραύσῃς* ‘if you break some little branch’: *ἐάν* takes the aorist active subjunctive *θραύσῃς* (2nd sg. of *θραύω*) in a prospective condition. The object straddles the line ending: diminutive *κλαδίσκον* with indefinite *τινά* gives *qualche fraschetta*. *Ἐξ ἑνὸς τῶν δένδρων τούτων* distinguishes source (*ἐξ ἑνός*, ‘from one’) from the partitive genitive *τῶν δένδρων τούτων* ‘of these trees’; *ἑνός* is neuter here.
- *Αὐτίκα τοὺς σοὺς λογισμοὺς μεταλλάξεις* ‘at once you will change your thoughts’ makes the addressee the agent: *μεταλλάξεις* is 2nd sg. future active indicative of *μεταλλάσσω*. *Σούς* replaces *ch’hai* with an attributive possessive, and *αὐτίκα* adds immediacy. Neither *tutti* nor the figure of thoughts becoming *monchi* ‘maimed’ survives.

> 31 Allor porsi la mano un poco avante,  
> 32 e colsi un ramicel da un gran pruno ;  

Then I stretched my hand a little forward, and plucked a branchlet from a great thorn

> 31 Τότε τὴν χεῖρ᾽ ἐκτείνας μικρόν τι πρόσω,  
> (Then stretching out my hand a little forward,)
>
> 32 Ἀπέκοψα μεγάλης βάτου κλαδίον·  
> (I broke off a little branch from a great bramble;)

- *Τότε τὴν χεῖρ᾽ ἐκτείνας* ‘then, having extended my hand’ subordinates *porsi* to the next verb. *Ἐκτείνας* is the nom. sg. masc. aorist active participle of *ἐκτείνω*, sharing the subject of *ἀπέκοψα*; *χεῖρ᾽* is elided *χεῖρα*, acc. sg. of *χείρ*, with the article rather than a possessive. *Μικρόν τι* is an adverbial accusative of extent qualifying directional *πρόσω* ‘forward’.
- *Ἀπέκοψα μεγάλης βάτου κλαδίον* ‘I cut off a little branch of a great bramble’: *ἀπέκοψα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀποκόπτω*, augmented after the prefix) is stronger than *colsi*. *Κλαδίον* is a neuter diminutive, acc. sg.; *μεγάλης βάτου* is gen. sg., the feminine adjective marking the gender of *βάτος* despite its ending. The bare genitive relates the twig to its parent plant, where *da un gran pruno* expresses source.

> 33 e il tronco suo gridò: “Perchè mi schiante?”  

; and the trunk of it cried, “Why dost thou rend me?”

> 33 Ὁ δὲ κορμὸς ἔκραξε· “Τί με συντρίβεις;”  
> (And the trunk cried out: "Why do you crush me?")

- *Ὁ δὲ κορμὸς ἔκραξε*: postpositive *δέ* marks the change of subject; the article identifies the trunk without an equivalent of *suo*. *Ἔκραξε* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *κράζω*. *Τί με συντρίβεις;* ‘why are you crushing me?’ uses the interrogative neuter accusative *τί* adverbially and the 2nd sg. present active *συντρίβεις* (from *συντρίβω*) for *schiante*.

> 34 Da che fatto fu poi di sangue bruno,  
> 35 ricominciò a gridar: “Perchè mi scerpi?  
> 36 non hai tu spirto di pietate alcuno?  

And when it had grown dark with blood, it again began to cry : “Why dost thou tear me? hast thou no breath of pity?

> 34 Ὡς δ᾽ ἐκ ῥοῆς αἵματος κατέστη μέλας,  
> (And when from the flow of blood it had turned dark,)
>
> 35 Ἀναλαβὼν ἔκραξε· “Τί με σπαράττεις;  
> (Resuming, it cried out: "Why do you tear me?)
>
> 36 „ Οὐδὲν αἴσθημα σὺ συμπαθείας ἔχεις;  
> (Have you no feeling of pity?)

- *Ὡς δ᾽ … κατέστη μέλας* ‘and when it turned black’: temporal *ὡς* introduces the finite clause for *Da che*; *δ᾽* is elided *δέ*. *Κατέστη* is the intransitive second aorist active indicative (3rd sg. of *καθίστημι*), with predicate *μέλας* (nom. sg. masc.) agreeing with *κορμός*. Greek thus makes darkness a property of the trunk; *ἐκ ῥοῆς αἵματος* ‘from a flow of blood’ gives its cause, supplying *ῥοή* where Italian has *di sangue bruno*.
- *Ἀναλαβὼν ἔκραξε* ‘resuming, it cried out’ distributes *ricominciò a gridar* between a circumstantial participle (*ἀναλαβών*, nom. sg. masc. second aor. act. of *ἀναλαμβάνω*, used without an expressed object) and the finite aorist. *Τί με σπαράττεις;* repeats the syntax of line 33, but *σπαράττεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. of *σπαράττω*) gives *scerpi* the sense ‘rend, tear apart’.
- *Οὐδὲν αἴσθημα σὺ συμπαθείας ἔχεις;* ‘do you have no feeling of fellow-suffering?’ replaces *spirto* with *αἴσθημα*, acc. sg. neuter, qualified by the genitive *συμπαθείας*. *Οὐδέν* agrees with *αἴσθημα*: Italian *non … alcuno* becomes one negative modifier. Explicit nominative *σύ* retains *tu* although *ἔχεις* already marks the 2nd sg.

> 37 Uomini fummo, ed or sem fatti sterpi:  
> 38 ben dovrebb’ esser la tua man più pia,  
> 39 se state fossim' anime di serpi.”  

Men we were, and now are turned to trees : truly thy hand should be more merciful, had we been souls of serpents.”

> 37 „ Ἦμεν ἄνθρωποι, νῦν δ᾽ ἐσμὲν κορμοὶ δένδρων·  
> (We were men, and now we are trunks of trees;)
>
> 38 „ Μᾶλλον ἂν φιλέλεος ἡ σὴ χεὶρ εἴη,  
> (More merciful would your hand be,)
>
> 39 „ Εἴπερ εἴημέν ποτε ψυχαὶ δρακόντων.”  
> (Even if we had been souls of serpents.")

- *Ἦμεν ἄνθρωποι, νῦν δ᾽ ἐσμὲν κορμοὶ δένδρων* sets two copulas in opposition: *ἦμεν* (1st pl. imperf. of *εἰμί*) and *ἐσμέν* (1st pl. pres.). Italian fronts *Uomini*; Greek fronts the past verb instead. For *sem fatti sterpi* Greek gives a present state, not a transformation, with predicate nominative *κορμοί* and defining genitive *δένδρων* ‘trunks of trees’.
- *Μᾶλλον ἂν φιλέλεος ἡ σὴ χεὶρ εἴη* ‘your hand would be more compassionate’: *μᾶλλον* modifies predicate *φιλέλεος* (nom. sg. fem., an adjective of two terminations). Potential *ἄν* with *εἴη* (3rd sg. present optative of *εἰμί*) replaces the conditional of obligation *dovrebb’ esser*: no verb ‘ought’ and no separate *ben*. *Ἡ σὴ χείρ* retains the possessive in attributive position.
- *Εἴπερ εἴημέν ποτε ψυχαὶ δρακόντων* ‘if indeed we should ever be souls of serpents’: *εἴπερ* strengthens the condition, with concessive force in context. *Εἴημεν* is 1st pl. **present** optative of *εἰμί*; enclitic *ποτε* adds an indefinite time, not past tense. Greek uses an optative protasis with a potential apodosis, not the past counterfactual suggested by *se state fossim’*. *Ψυχαί* is predicate nominative, *δρακόντων* gen. pl. of *δράκων*.

> 40 Come d' un stizzo verde, che arso sia  
> 41 dall' un de' capi, che dall' altro geme  
> 42 e cigola per vento che va via:  

As a green brand, that is burning at one end, at the other drops, and hisses with the wind which is escaping

> 40 Ὡς δαλὸς χλωρὸς καιόμενος ἐξ ἄκρου  
> (As a green firebrand burning at one end)
>
> 41 Ἑνὸς φαίνεται στενάζων ἐκ θἀτέρου,  
> (Seems to groan from the other,)
>
> 42 Κἀκ τῆς ἀέρος διαφυγῆς συρίζει,  
> (And hisses from the escaping of the air,)

- *Ὡς δαλὸς χλωρὸς καιόμενος* ‘as a green brand burning’: *δαλός* is nominative, the subject of the comparison, where Italian begins obliquely *Come d’un stizzo verde*. The present middle/passive participle *καιόμενος* (from *καίω*) replaces the finite relative *che arso sia*, giving ongoing combustion rather than the condition of having been burned.
- *Ἐξ ἄκρου ἑνός … ἐκ θἀτέρου* ‘from one end … from the other’: *ἑνός* agrees with neuter *ἄκρου* across the line break. *Ἐξ* and *ἐκ* are the forms of the same preposition before a vowel and a consonant respectively. *Θἀτέρου* is the crasis form of *τοῦ ἑτέρου*, with aspiration from the rough breathing; ‘end’ need not be repeated.
- *Φαίνεται στενάζων* ‘seems to groan’ adds *φαίνεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid./pass. of *φαίνω*) with a supplementary nom. sg. masc. present active participle. Musurus takes *geme* in the sense ‘groans’, not in the sense rendered by “drops”. *Κἀκ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐκ*: *ἐκ τῆς ἀέρος διαφυγῆς* ‘from the escape of air’ turns *per vento che va via* into a causal prepositional phrase. *Τῆς* belongs to *διαφυγῆς*, not to *ἀέρος* (gen. sg. of *ἀήρ*), the air being what escapes. *Συρίζει* (3rd sg. pres. act. of *συρίζω*) is finite, coordinated with *φαίνεται*.

> 43 sì della scheggia rotta usciva insieme  
> 44 parole e sangue: ond' io lasciai la cima  
> 45 cadere, e stetti come l' uom che teme.  

: so from that broken splint, words and blood came forth together : whereat I let fall the top, and stood like one who is afraid.

> 43 Οὕτως ἐξήρχετ᾽ ἐκ τοῦ κλάδου συγχρόνως  
> (So came forth from the branch at the same time)
>
> 44 Αἷμα καὶ λόγοι· κἀγὼ τὸν κλάδον ῥίψας,  
> (Blood and words; and I, letting fall the branch,)
>
> 45 Ἔμειν᾽ ὥσπερ ἄνθρωπος ἐπτοημένος.  
> (Stood like a man terrified.)

- *Οὕτως ἐξήρχετ᾽ ἐκ τοῦ κλάδου συγχρόνως* answers *ὡς* with *οὕτως*, as *sì* answers *Come*. *Ἐξήρχετ᾽* is elided *ἐξήρχετο*, 3rd sg. imperfect middle of *ἐξέρχομαι*, with temporal augment after the prefix; *ἐκ τοῦ κλάδου* repeats the source already indicated by the prefix. *Κλάδου* gives simply ‘branch’, not *scheggia rotta*; *συγχρόνως* is explicitly temporal, ‘at the same time’.
- *Αἷμα καὶ λόγοι* reverses *parole e sangue*. As in Italian the verb is singular before the compound subject; in Greek it agrees readily with the nearer, singular *αἷμα*. *Κἀγώ* (crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*) gives simple coordination for consequential *ond’ io*. *Τὸν κλάδον ῥίψας* ‘having dropped the branch’ repeats *κλάδος* for *cima* and reduces *lasciai … cadere* to one aorist active participle (nom. sg. masc. of *ῥίπτω*).
- *Ἔμειν᾽* is elided *ἔμεινα*, 1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *μένω* ‘remain’, for *stetti*. *Ὥσπερ ἄνθρωπος ἐπτοημένος* replaces *come l’uom che teme* with an anarthrous noun and agreeing perfect passive participle (nom. sg. masc. of *πτοέω*): ‘like a person who has been frightened’, a resulting state rather than a present relative clause.

> 46 “ S' egli avesse potuto creder prima,”  
> 47 rispose il savio mio, “anima lesa,  
> 48 ciò ch' ha veduto pur con la mia rima,  
> 49 non averebbe in te la man distesa;  

“If he, O wounded Spirit!” my Sage replied, “could have believed before, what he has seen only in my verse, he would not have stretched forth his hand against thee

> 46 “Εἴπερ ἐπίστευ᾽ οὗτος, πρὶν ἤ σε βλάψῃ,”  
> ("If this man had believed, before he harmed you,")
>
> 47 Εἶπεν ὁ Σοφός, “ὦ ψυχὴ τετρωμένη,  
> (Said the Sage, "O wounded soul,)
>
> 48 „ Ὅ,τι ποτ᾽ ἐν τοῖς ἐμοῖς ἀνέγνω μέτροις,  
> (What once he read in my verses,)
>
> 49 „ Οὐκ ἂν ἔτειν᾽ ἐπὶ σὲ βλαβερὰν χεῖρα.  
> (He would not have stretched out against you a harmful hand.)

- *Εἴπερ ἐπίστευ᾽ οὗτος* ‘if this man indeed believed’: *ἐπίστευ᾽* is elided *ἐπίστευε*, 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *πιστεύω*. The past indicative prepares a contrary-to-fact condition but does not reproduce either the pluperfect or the explicit ability of *avesse potuto creder*. Deictic *οὗτος* stands for *egli*. *Πρὶν ἤ σε βλάψῃ* expands *prima* into ‘before he harms you’; *βλάψῃ* is 3rd sg. aor. act. subj. of *βλάπτω*. The bare subjunctive after *πρὶν ἤ* is not the ordinary Attic construction after an affirmative expression, where one would expect an infinitive.
- *Εἶπεν ὁ Σοφός* ‘said the wise one’ uses the second aorist with movable *-ν* before the article: *disse* in effect, rather than specifically *rispose*; *mio* is omitted. *Ὦ ψυχὴ τετρωμένη* marks the address explicitly with *ὦ*; *τετρωμένη* (voc. sg. fem. perfect passive participle of *τιτρώσκω*) renders *lesa* as a continuing state.
- *Ὅ,τι ποτ᾽ ἐν τοῖς ἐμοῖς ἀνέγνω μέτροις* ‘what he once read in my verses’ supplies the content of belief. *Ὅ,τι* is the neuter acc. sg. of the indefinite relative *ὅστις*, object of *ἀνέγνω* (3rd sg. root aor. act. ind. of *ἀναγιγνώσκω*); *ποτ᾽* is elided *ποτε*. Reading replaces seeing in *ciò ch’ha veduto*, and *ποτε* gives no restrictive ‘only’ for *pur*. The verb separates *τοῖς ἐμοῖς* from *μέτροις*: *con la mia rima* becomes locative *ἐν* with the dative plural.
- *Οὐκ ἂν ἔτειν᾽ ἐπὶ σὲ βλαβερὰν χεῖρα* is the unreal consequence: negative *οὐκ*, modal *ἄν*, and elided *ἔτεινε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *τείνω*), not an infinitive. The imperfect does not itself mark the completed past expressed by *non averebbe … distesa*. Directional *ἐπί* with accusative *σέ* gives *in te*; attributive *βλαβεράν* ‘harmful’ is supplied and echoes *βλάψῃ*.

> 50 ma la cosa incredibile mi fece  
> 51 indurlo ad opra, che a me stesso pesa.  

; but the incredibility of the thing made me prompt him to do what grieves myself.

> 50 „ Ἀλλ᾽ αὐτὸς ἐγώ, πρᾶγμα δύσπιστον ὅλως!  
> (But I myself—a thing wholly hard to believe!—)
>
> 51 „ Προὔτρεψ᾽ αὐτὸν εἰς τοὖργον, ὃ καὶ λυπεῖ με.  
> (Urged him on to the deed, which also grieves me.)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ αὐτὸς ἐγώ* ‘but I myself’ makes the speaker the emphatic subject, with intensive nom. sg. masc. *αὐτός*. Italian *la cosa incredibile* is the grammatical cause of *mi fece indurlo*; Greek detaches *πρᾶγμα δύσπιστον ὅλως* ‘a thing altogether hard to believe!’ as a parenthetic nominal exclamation. *Δύσπιστον* agrees with neuter *πρᾶγμα* and has passive sense here; *ὅλως* adds emphasis.
- *Προὔτρεψ᾽ αὐτὸν εἰς τοὖργον* ‘I urged him to the deed’: *προὔτρεψ᾽* is elided *προὔτρεψα*, 1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *προτρέπω*, with contraction of the prefix vowel and augment. The finite verb replaces the causative plus infinitive; *αὐτόν* is its object, *εἰς* with the accusative its goal. *Τοὖργον* is crasis of *τὸ ἔργον*. In *ὃ καὶ λυπεῖ με*, neuter relative *ὅ* is nominative subject of *λυπεῖ* (3rd sg. pres. act. of *λυπέω*); ‘which also grieves me’ gives accusative *με* instead of *a me stesso*, without repeating the intensive.

> 52 Ma dilli chi tu fosti, sì che, in vece  
> 53 d' alcuna ammenda, tua fama rinfreschi  
> 54 nel mondo su, dove tornar gli lece.”  

But tell him who thou wast ; so that, to make thee some amends, he may refresh thy fame up in the world, to which he is permitted to return.”

> 52 „ Εἰπὲ δ᾽ αὐτῷ νῦν, τίς εἶ, κἀμοιβῆς χάριν  
> (Tell him now who you are, and in return)
>
> 53 „ Ἀναμνήσει σὴν φήμην, ὅτ᾽ ἐπιστρέψει,  
> (He will refresh your fame, when he returns,)
>
> 54 „ Ὡς αὐτῷ θέμις, εἰς τὸν ἐπάνω κόσμον.”  
> (As is permitted to him, to the world above.")

- *Εἰπὲ δ᾽ αὐτῷ νῦν, τίς εἶ* ‘but tell him now who you are’: *εἰπέ* is the 2nd sg. second aorist active imperative of *λέγω*, with dative recipient *αὐτῷ* for enclitic *-lli* in *dilli*. *Νῦν* is added. The indirect question keeps the interrogative nominative *τίς*, but *εἶ* (2nd sg. present of *εἰμί*) changes *fosti* from past identity to present.
- *Κἀμοιβῆς χάριν* ‘and by way of recompense’: *κἀμοιβῆς* is crasis of *καὶ ἀμοιβῆς*; postpositive *χάριν* governs the genitive. There is no indefinite corresponding to *alcuna*. The conjunction leads into the future statement *ἀναμνήσει σὴν φήμην*, for purposive *sì che … tua fama rinfreschi*. *Ἀναμνήσει* (3rd sg. fut. act. ind. of *ἀναμιμνῄσκω*) is used with *σήν φήμην* ‘your fame’ as what he will bring back to mind, not with an expressed person whom he will remind.
- *Ὅτ᾽ ἐπιστρέψει* (elided *ὅτε* with 3rd sg. fut. act. ind. of *ἐπιστρέφω*) makes the return a definite future occasion, ‘when he returns’, instead of subordinating an infinitive to *gli lece*. *Ὡς αὐτῷ θέμις* is parenthetic, ‘as is permitted to him’: dative of the person with impersonal *θέμις* and understood *ἐστί*. *Εἰς τὸν ἐπάνω κόσμον* completes the verb of return with a destination, whereas *nel mondo su* locates the renewal of fame and *dove* introduces the return. Indeclinable *ἐπάνω* stands attributively between article and noun, ‘the world above’.

## Lines 55–87 — A Protest of Fidelity and a Plea for Remembrance

> 55 E il tronco : “Sì con dolce dir m’adeschi,  
> 56 ch’ io non posso tacere ; e voi non gravi  
> 57 perch’ io un poco a ragionar m’inveschi.  

And the trunk: “Thou so allurest me with thy sweet words, that I cannot keep silent; and let it not seem burdensome to you, if I enlarge a little in discourse.

> 55 Ὁ κορμός· “Θέλγομ’ οὕτω τοῖς σοῖς γλυκέσι  
> (The trunk: "I am so charmed by your sweet)
>
> 56 „ Λόγοις, ὥστ’ οὐ δύναμαι σιγᾷν· ἀλλά μοι  
> (Words, that I cannot keep silent; but to me)
>
> 57 „ Σύγγνωτ’, εἰ ποθῶ μικρὸν διαλεχθῆναι.  
> (Grant pardon, if I long to converse a little.)

- *Ὁ κορμός* reproduces *il tronco* as a nominative introducing direct speech without a verb of saying; the initial *E* has no Greek counterpart. *Θέλγομ’* (elided *θέλγομαι*, 1st sg. present middle/passive indicative of *θέλγω*, passive in sense) reverses the perspective of active *m’adeschi*: ‘I am charmed’, not ‘you allure me’. *Τοῖς σοῖς γλυκέσι λόγοις* is a dative plural of means; the singular addressee, no longer the subject, appears in the possessive *σοῖς*. The Italian infinitive used as a noun, *dir*, becomes the plural ‘words’.
- *Οὕτω … ὥστ’ οὐ δύναμαι σιγᾷν* answers *Sì … che io non posso tacere*. Elided *ὥστε* introduces an actual result with the indicative and *οὐ*, not an infinitive with *μή*. *Δύναμαι* is 1st sg. present middle indicative, with the complementary contracted present infinitive *σιγᾷν* from *σιγάω*.
- *Ἀλλά μοι σύγγνωτ’* ‘but pardon me’ replaces the negative wish *e voi non gravi* with a positive appeal. *Σύγγνωτ’* is elided *σύγγνωτε*, 2nd pl. root-aorist active imperative of *συγγιγνώσκω*, with the person pardoned in the dative. The plural preserves the turn from singular *m’adeschi* to *voi*. For *perch’ io … m’inveschi*, Greek has conditional *εἰ ποθῶ* ‘if I desire’ and *διαλεχθῆναι*, aorist passive in form but the infinitive of *διαλέγομαι* ‘converse’ in sense. Adverbial *μικρόν* retains *un poco*; the image in *m’inveschi* drops out.

> 58 Io son colui, che tenni ambo le chiavi  
> 59 del cor di Federico, e che le volsi  
> 60 serrando e disserrando sì soavi,  
> 61 che dal segreto suo quasi ogni uom tolsi;  

I am he, who held both keys of Frederick’s heart, and turned them, locking and unlocking so softly, that from his secrets I excluded almost every other man;

> 58 „ Εἴμ’ ἐκεῖνος, ὃς εἶχον ἄμφω τὰς κλεῖδας  
> (I am he who held both the keys)
>
> 59 „ Καρδίας Φρεδερίκου, κἀνέτως οὕτως  
> (Of Frederick's heart, and so gently)
>
> 60 „ Ἔστρεφον αὐτάς, ἀνοίγων τε καὶ κλείων,  
> (Turned them, both opening and closing,)
>
> 61 „ Ὥστ’ ἀποκρύφων αὐτοῦ πάντας ἀπεῖργον.  
> (That I kept everyone away from his secrets.)

- *Εἴμ’ ἐκεῖνος* is elided *εἰμί* with the predicate demonstrative ‘I am that man’; there is no separate pronoun for *Io*. In *ὃς εἶχον*, *ὅς* is nominative masculine singular, but the imperfect active *εἶχον* of *ἔχω* is first person, taking its person from the speaker just as *che tenni* does. The imperfect presents possession as continuing, rather than reproducing the Italian preterite.
- *Ἄμφω τὰς κλεῖδας* preserves *ambo*: *ἄμφω* is the nominative/accusative dual ‘both’, here used with an accusative **plural**, *τὰς κλεῖδας* (from *κλείς*), not a dual noun. The dependent genitives *καρδίας Φρεδερίκου* follow in the order of *del cor di Federico*, but without an article before ‘heart’.
- *Κἀνέτως* is crasis for *καὶ ἀνέτως*, ‘and easily, gently’. The adverb and *οὕτως* ‘so’ precede *ἔστρεφον* rather than following the participial phrase as *sì soavi* does. *Ἔστρεφον* (1st sg. imperfect active of *στρέφω*) again replaces a preterite, *volsi*; accented *αὐτάς* is the feminine plural object corresponding to *le*. The present active participles *ἀνοίγων τε καὶ κλείων*, nominative masculine singular agreeing with the speaker, describe the manner of turning. *Τε καί* pairs the actions, but ‘opening and closing’ reverses *serrando e disserrando*.
- *Ὥστ’ … ἀπεῖργον* is again a result clause with the indicative: *ἀπεῖργον* is 1st sg. imperfect active of *ἀπείργω*, ‘I kept away’. The verb takes the persons excluded in the accusative, *πάντας*, and that from which they are excluded in the genitive, *ἀποκρύφων αὐτοῦ* ‘from his secret things’. The substantivized neuter plural replaces singular *segreto*; *πάντας* ‘all’ leaves *quasi* unrepresented.

> 62 fede portai al glorioso ufizio,  
> 63 tanto ch’ io ne perdei lo sonno e i polsi.  

so great fidelity I bore to the glorious office, that I lost thereby both sleep and life.

> 62 „ Οὕτω τε πιστῶς τοὔνδοξον ἐξεπλήρουν  
> (And so faithfully I fulfilled the glorious)
>
> 63 „ Ὑπούργημ’, ὥστ’ ὤλεσα παλμοὺς καὶ φλέβας.  
> (Office, that I lost my pulses and veins.)

- In *οὕτω τε πιστῶς … ἐξεπλήρουν*, connective *τε* links this statement to the last. *Fede portai* becomes ‘I fulfilled faithfully’: the noun becomes the adverb *πιστῶς*, and *ἐξεπλήρουν* (1st sg. imperfect active of *ἐκπληρόω*, with internal augment and contraction) takes the office as its direct object instead of the complement of *a*.
- *Τοὔνδοξον* is crasis for *τὸ ἔνδοξον*, accusative neuter singular qualifying *ὑπούργημ’* (elided *ὑπούργημα*, ‘service, office’). *Οὕτω … ὥστ’ ὤλεσα* makes *tanto ch’* a degree-and-result construction. *Ὤλεσα* is the transitive 1st sg. aorist active indicative of *ὄλλυμι*, ‘I lost’; there is no separate equivalent of *ne*.
- *Παλμοὺς καὶ φλέβας* are two accusative plurals, literally ‘pulses and veins’. The Greek thus gives two bodily terms: *παλμούς* answers *i polsi*, but neither Greek noun renders *lo sonno*. This is a difference from the supplied Italian, not merely a different way of glossing *polsi* as “life”.

> 64 La meretrice, che mai dall’ ospizio  
> 65 di Cesare non torse gli occhi putti,  
> 66 morte comune e delle corti vizio,  
> 67 infiammò contra me gli animi tutti;  

The harlot, that never from Cæsar’s dwelling turned her adulterous eyes, common bane, and vice of courts, inflamed all minds against me;

> 64 „ Ἡ τοὺς μοιχίους ὀφθαλμοὺς τοῦ μελάθρου  
> (She who her adulterous eyes from the palace)
>
> 65 „ Καίσαρος οὔποτ’ ἀποσπάσασ’ ἑταίρα,  
> (Of Caesar never withdrew, the harlot,)
>
> 66 „ — Κοινὸς θάνατος, καὶ τῶν αὐλῶν κακότης! —  
> (—A common death, and the vice of courts!—)
>
> 67 „ Ἐξῆψε τὰς καρδίας κατ’ ἐμοῦ πάντων·  
> (Inflamed the hearts of all against me;)

- *Ἡ … ἀποσπάσασ’ ἑταίρα* postpones the noun ‘harlot’ until the end of the description. Between article and noun stands an attributive participial phrase in place of the Italian relative clause. *Ἀποσπάσασ’* is elided *ἀποσπάσασα*, nominative feminine singular aorist active participle of *ἀποσπάω*, ‘having drawn away’, negated by *οὔποτ’* (elided *οὔποτε*). The aorist participle, unlike the indicative *torse*, has no augment.
- *Τοὺς μοιχίους ὀφθαλμούς* ‘the adulterous eyes’ is the participle’s direct object, moved ahead of the verb; the article suffices for the possessor. *Τοῦ μελάθρου Καίσαρος* ‘from Caesar’s palace’ puts the place in the genitive of separation without a preposition, with the possessive genitive *Καίσαρος* following *μελάθρου*. ‘Palace’ makes *ospizio* more specific.
- *Κοινὸς θάνατος, καὶ τῶν αὐλῶν κακότης* keeps the two appositional nominatives but sets them off as an exclamation. *Κοινός* is masculine because it qualifies masculine *θάνατος*, not feminine *ἑταίρα*; *τῶν αὐλῶν* is the dependent genitive plural before *κακότης* ‘badness, vice’.
- *Ἐξῆψε* is 3rd sg. aorist active indicative of *ἐξάπτω*, ‘kindled’, with the augment after the prefix. For *gli animi tutti*, Greek has *τὰς καρδίας … πάντων* ‘the hearts of all’: the adjective quantifying *animi* becomes a substantival genitive of possessors, separated from *καρδίας* by *κατ’ ἐμοῦ* (*κατά* with the genitive, ‘against me’).

> 68 e gl’ infiammati infiammâr sì Augusto,  
> 69 che i lieti onor tornaro in tristi lutti.  

and these, [being inflamed,] so inflamed Augustus, that my joyous honours were changed to dismal sorrows.

> 68 „ Οἱ δ’ ἐξαφθέντες τὸν Αὔγουστον ἐξῆψαν·  
> (And those, inflamed, inflamed Augustus;)
>
> 69 „ Καὶ δὴ τὰς τιμὰς τὸ πένθος διεδέχθη.  
> (And so mourning succeeded to my honours.)

- *Οἱ δ’ ἐξαφθέντες* ‘and those who had been kindled’ has the article with a substantival participle, like *gl’ infiammati*. *Ἐξαφθέντες* is nominative masculine plural aorist passive of *ἐξάπτω*: the masculine picks up the people implicit in *πάντων*, not feminine *καρδίας*. *Δ’* is elided postpositive *δέ*. The 3rd pl. aorist active *ἐξῆψαν*, with *τὸν Αὔγουστον* as object, completes the same threefold verbal repetition as *infiammò … infiammati infiammâr*.
- Instead of marking degree and result as in *sì … che*, *καὶ δή* introduces the outcome in a coordinate clause, ‘and so’. *Τὰς τιμάς* is its fronted **object**, *τὸ πένθος* its singular **subject**: *διεδέχθη*, 3rd sg. aorist passive in form of *διαδέχομαι*, here means ‘succeeded, took the place of’. Thus mourning succeeds honours rather than honours turning into sorrows. The adjectives *lieti* and *tristi* are omitted, and singular *πένθος* replaces plural *lutti*.

> 70 L’ animo mio per disdegnoso gusto,  
> 71 credendo col morir fuggir disdegno,  
> 72 ingiusto fece me contra me giusto.  

My soul, in its disdainful mood, thinking to escape disdain by death, made me, *though* just, unjust against myself.

> 70 „ Ἐξ ἀγανακτήσεως τόθ’ ἡ ψυχή μου,  
> (Out of indignation then my soul,)
>
> 71 „ Τὴν ὕβριν τῷ θανάτῳ φυγεῖν ζητοῦσα,  
> (Seeking to escape outrage by death,)
>
> 72 „ Ἄδικον τέλος ἔδωκέ μοι δικαίῳ.  
> (Gave an unjust end to me, a just man.)

- *Ἐξ ἀγανακτήσεως* ‘out of indignation’ puts the motive first, using *ἐξ* with the genitive of *ἀγανάκτησις* for *per disdegnoso gusto*. *Τόθ’* is added *τότε* ‘then’, with elision and aspiration of the final consonant before rough-breathed *ἡ*. *Ἡ ψυχή μου* gives *l’ animo mio* with the enclitic possessive genitive.
- *Ζητοῦσα* (nominative feminine singular present active participle of *ζητέω*, agreeing with *ψυχή*) changes *credendo* ‘believing’ to ‘seeking’. Its complementary infinitive *φυγεῖν* is the second aorist active of *φεύγω*; *τὴν ὕβριν* ‘the insult, outrage’ is its object and *τῷ θανάτῳ* its instrumental dative. Italian *col morir*, an infinitive used as a noun, becomes a noun proper. The distinction between felt *ἀγανάκτησις* and suffered *ὕβρις* replaces the repetition in *disdegnoso … disdegno*.
- *Ἄδικον τέλος ἔδωκέ μοι δικαίῳ* ‘gave an unjust end to me, a just man’ transfers *ingiusto* from the person to the neuter object *τέλος*. *Ἔδωκε* is 3rd sg. aorist active of *δίδωμι*; its extra written accent in *ἔδωκέ μοι* is due to the enclitic. Dative *δικαίῳ* agrees with the recipient *μοι*. The opposition *ἄδικον … δικαίῳ* remains, but there is no reflexive construction corresponding to *contra me*.

> 73 Per le nuove radici d’ esto legno  
> 74 vi giuro che giammai non ruppi fede  
> 75 al mio signor, che fu d’ onor sì degno.  

By the new roots of this tree, I swear to you, never did I break faith to my lord, who was so worthy of honour.

> 73 „ Μὰ τὰς νεαρὰς ῥίζας τοῦ δένδρου τούτου,  
> (By the young roots of this tree,)
>
> 74 „ Ὄμνυμ’ ὑμῖν, οὐδέποτ’ ἔλυσα πίστιν  
> (I swear to you, never did I break faith)
>
> 75 „ Πρὸς τὸν ἀξιότιμον ἐμὸν αὐθέντην.  
> (Toward my worthy lord.)

- *Μὰ τὰς νεαρὰς ῥίζας* gives the formula of swearing by something: oath-particle *μά* with the accusative for Italian *per*. It suits the negative assertion that follows. *Τοῦ δένδρου τούτου* renders *d’ esto legno* as ‘of this tree’, with the demonstrative outside the article–noun group.
- *Ὄμνυμ’ ὑμῖν* is elided *ὄμνυμι* (1st sg. present active indicative) with a dative plural of the persons addressed. No conjunction renders *che*: the assertion follows directly. *Οὐδέποτ’ ἔλυσα πίστιν* ‘never did I dissolve faith’ uses elided *οὐδέποτε* with *ἔλυσα*, 1st sg. aorist active of *λύω*; the negative adverb suffices for Italian *giammai non*.
- *Πρὸς τὸν … ἐμὸν αὐθέντην* uses *πρός* with the accusative for the person towards whom faith was kept. The possessive adjective *ἐμόν* and *ἀξιότιμον* ‘worthy of honour’ both stand within the article–noun group. The latter adjective absorbs *che fu d’ onor sì degno*, without its separate past tense or degree word.

> 76 E se di voi alcun nel mondo riede,  
> 77 conforti la memoria mia, che giace  
> 78 ancor del colpo che invidia le diede.”  

And if any of you return to the world, strengthen the memory of me, which still lies prostrate from the blow that envy gave it.”

> 76 „ Ἤν τις δ’ ἐξ ὑμῶν εἰς κόσμον ἐπιστρέψῃ,  
> (And if either of you returns to the world,)
>
> 77 „ Ἀμυνάτω τὴν ἐμὴν μνήμην, ἔτ’ οὖσαν  
> (Let him defend my memory, still lying)
>
> 78 „ Ὑπὸ τὴν πληγήν, ἣν ἐπήνεγκε φθόνος.”  
> (Beneath the blow which envy dealt it.")

- *Ἤν τις δ’ ἐξ ὑμῶν* ‘and if anyone of you’ uses *ἤν*, a contracted alternative to *ἐάν*, with *ἐπιστρέψῃ* (3rd sg. aorist active subjunctive of *ἐπιστρέφω*) for an open future condition. *Τις* is the indefinite subject, *ἐξ ὑμῶν* partitive, and *δ’* connective. *Εἰς κόσμον* gives *nel mondo* a directional construction, without the article.
- *Ἀμυνάτω* is 3rd sg. aorist active imperative of *ἀμύνω*, answering the Italian third-person exhortation *conforti*. Musurus uses it with the thing to be defended as direct object, *τὴν ἐμὴν μνήμην*. The sense is ‘let him defend my memory’, not simply ‘strengthen’; in classical active usage the accusative of *ἀμύνω* often denotes instead the harm to be warded off.
- *Ἔτ’ οὖσαν* (elided *ἔτι* with the accusative feminine singular present participle of *εἰμί*, agreeing with *μνήμην*) replaces finite *che giace ancor*. Greek says ‘still being under the blow’, *ὑπὸ τὴν πληγήν*, rather than using a verb for lying prostrate. In *ἣν ἐπήνεγκε φθόνος*, the relative *ἥν* is the accusative object, *φθόνος* the nominative subject, and *ἐπήνεγκε* the irregular 3rd sg. aorist active of *ἐπιφέρω* ‘bring upon, inflict’; the recipient expressed by *le* is left implicit.

> 79 Un poco attese, e poi : “Da ch’ ei si tace,”  
> 80 disse il poeta a me, “non perder l’ ora;  
> 81 ma parla, e chiedi a lui se più ti piace.”  

The Poet listened a while, and then said to me: “Since he is silent, lose not the hour; but speak, and ask him, if thou wouldst know more.”

> 79 Μικρόν τι μείνας, “Ὡς σιωπῶν τυγχάνει,”  
> (Having waited a little, "Since he is silent,")
>
> 80 Ὁ Ποιητὴς εἶπέ μοι, “μὴ χρονοτρίβει,  
> (The Poet said to me, "do not waste time,)
>
> 81 „ Ἀλλ’ ὁμίλει, κ’ εἴ τί σοι φίλον ἐρώτα.”  
> (But speak with him, and ask whatever pleases you.")

- *Μικρόν τι μείνας* ‘having waited a little’ renders *Un poco attese* by a participle subordinate to *εἶπε*. *Μικρόν τι* is an adverbial accusative of extent with indefinite *τι*; *μείνας* is nominative masculine singular aorist active of *μένω*, agreeing with the delayed *ὁ Ποιητής*. The participle marks the waiting as prior, so that *e poi* needs no separate adverb.
- *Ὡς σιωπῶν τυγχάνει* turns *Da ch’ ei si tace* into causal *ὡς* with a periphrasis: *τυγχάνει* (3rd sg. present active of *τυγχάνω*) takes the supplementary present participle *σιωπῶν*, ‘he happens to be silent’. There is no expressed subject pronoun. As in Italian, *ὁ Ποιητὴς εἶπέ μοι* interrupts the direct speech; enclitic *μοι* accounts for the additional accent on *εἶπέ*.
- *Μὴ χρονοτρίβει* replaces *non perder l’ ora* with a single compound verb, ‘do not spend time idly’; *χρονοτρίβει* is 2nd sg. present active imperative of *χρονοτριβέω*, negated by *μή*. *Ἀλλ’ ὁμίλει* likewise has a present imperative (from *ὁμιλέω*), ‘but converse’, for *ma parla*.
- *Κ’ εἴ τί σοι φίλον ἐρώτα* amounts to ‘and if anything is agreeable to you, ask’. *Κ’* is a poetic shortening of *καί* before a vowel; indefinite *τι* carries an accent before enclitic *σοι*. *Φίλον* is a neuter predicate with ‘is’ understood, *σοι* the dative of the person pleased. *Ἐρώτα* is the contracted 2nd sg. present active imperative of *ἐρωτάω*. The person to be asked (*a lui*) is implicit, and *più* is not separately rendered.

> 82 Ond’ io a lui : “Domandal tu ancora  
> 83 di quel che credi che a me satisfaccia;  
> 84 ch’ io non potrei, tanta pietà m’ accora.”  

Whereat I to him: “Do thou ask him farther, respecting what thou thinkest will satisfy me; for I could not, such pity is upon my heart.”

> 82 Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν· “Σύ γε μᾶλλον πυνθάνου,  
> (And I to him: "Do you rather inquire,)
>
> 83 „ Εἴ τι νομίζεις σύμφορον ἔσεσθαί μοι·  
> (What you think will be profitable for me;)
>
> 84 „ Ἀμηχανῶ γάρ, συγκινηθεὶς ἐξ οἴκτου.”  
> (For I am at a loss, being moved by pity.")

- *Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν* ‘and I to him’ has crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ* and *πρός* with the accusative; like *Ond’ io a lui* it supplies no verb of saying. *Σύ γε* emphasizes *tu* with the restrictive particle *γε*, and *μᾶλλον* ‘rather’ interprets the request as a contrast of speakers rather than rendering *ancora* temporally. *Πυνθάνου* is 2nd sg. present middle imperative of *πυνθάνομαι*, ‘inquire’; the object represented by *-l-* in *Domandal* is not expressed.
- *Εἴ τι νομίζεις σύμφορον ἔσεσθαί μοι* is framed with ‘if anything’ rather than the Italian antecedent and relative *di quel che*: ‘if there is anything you think will be beneficial to me’. *Νομίζεις* (2nd sg. present active indicative) governs the future middle infinitive *ἔσεσθαι* of *εἰμί*, with neuter *τι* as its subject and *σύμφορον* as predicate. The future infinitive makes the prospective sense explicit. *Μοι* depends in sense on ‘beneficial’ and gives the preceding infinitive its additional accent.
- *Ἀμηχανῶ γάρ* ‘for I am at a loss’ replaces the conditional *io non potrei* by a contracted 1st sg. present active indicative of *ἀμηχανέω*; postpositive *γάρ* marks the explanation. *Συγκινηθείς* is nominative masculine singular aorist passive participle of *συγκινέω*, referring to the speaker. Where *tanta pietà m’ accora* makes pity the subject of a finite verb, Greek makes the speaker the affected person and marks the cause with *ἐξ οἴκτου*, without an equivalent for *tanta*.

> 85 Però ricominciò : “Se l’ uom ti faccia  
> 86 liberamente ciò che il tuo dir prega,  
> 87 spirito incarcerato, ancor ti piaccia  

He therefore resumed: “So may the man do freely for thee what thy words entreat him, O imprisoned spirit, please thee

> 85 Τότ’ αὐτὸς εἶπε· “Τὴν σὴν αἴτησιν οὗτος  
> (Then he himself said: "Your request this man)
>
> 86 „ Ἐκπληρώσει μὲν προθύμως· σὺ δ’, ὦ πνεῦμα  
> (Will indeed fulfil willingly; but you, O spirit)
>
> 87 „ Δέσμιον, εἴ σ’ ἀρέσκει, λέγε προσέτι,  
> (Imprisoned, if it pleases you, tell us besides,)

- *Τότ’ αὐτὸς εἶπε* ‘then he himself said’ has elided *τότε* and intensive *αὐτός*. A temporal link replaces inferential *Però*, and the simple aorist *εἶπε* (3rd sg., the aorist used with *λέγω*) does not itself express the renewed action of *ricominciò*.
- *Τὴν σὴν αἴτησιν οὗτος ἐκπληρώσει μὲν προθύμως* makes an assertion: ‘this man will indeed fulfil your request willingly’. The fronted accusative noun phrase ‘your request’ condenses *ciò che il tuo dir prega*; demonstrative *οὗτος* supplies the subject for *l’ uom*. *Ἐκπληρώσει* is 3rd sg. future active **indicative** of *ἐκπληρόω*, not a wish or a conditional form corresponding to *Se … faccia*. *Προθύμως* interprets *liberamente* as willingness.
- *Μέν … σὺ δ’* balances the assurance about *οὗτος* against the appeal to ‘you in turn’. *Ὦ πνεῦμα δέσμιον* retains the address *spirito incarcerato*: noun and adjective are vocative neuter singular, identical in form to the nominative and accusative. *Εἴ σ’ ἀρέσκει* turns *ti piaccia* into a condition with a present indicative, ‘if it pleases you’; the written *σ’* reads naturally as elided accusative *σε*, where classical *ἀρέσκω* ordinarily takes the person pleased in the dative. *Λέγε προσέτι* ‘say further’ supplies a 2nd sg. present active imperative already within line 87, with *προσέτι* taking up *ancor*; the Italian and its English rendering still leave the requested action unstated at this point.

## Lines 88–120 — How Souls Take Root and Two Fugitives Appear

> 88 di dirne come l’ anima si lega  
> 89 in questi nocchi; e dinne, se tu puoi,  
> 90 s’ alcuna mai da tai membra si spiega.”  

tell us farther, how the soul gets bound up in these knots; and tell us, if thou mayest, whether any ever frees itself from such members.”

> 88 „ Πῶς ἆρ’ ἡ ψυχὴ συνδέεται τοῖς ὄζοις  
> (How then the soul is bound to these)
>
> 89 „ Τούτοις, καὶ λέγ’ ἐπ’ ἴσης, εἰ δυνατόν σοι,  
> (Gnarled boughs, and tell us likewise, if you can,)
>
> 90 „ Εἴ τις ποτ’ ἀπηλλάχθη τῶν μελῶν τούτων.”  
> (Whether any was ever released from these limbs.")

- *Πῶς ἆρ’ ἡ ψυχὴ συνδέεται* ‘how, then, is the soul bound?’ makes a direct question of the Italian request *di dirne come*. There is no first verb of telling or pronoun for ‘us’; elided *ἆρ’* is interrogative *ἆρα*. *Συνδέεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *συνδέω*, passive in sense) renders reflexive *si lega*.
- *Τοῖς ὄζοις τούτοις* ‘to these knots’ has the dative of that to which the soul is bound, rather than a preposition for *in*. The demonstrative follows the articular noun, across the line break; *τούτοις* then stands beside the second request, rather as *in questi nocchi* does in Italian.
- *Λέγ’ ἐπ’ ἴσης* ‘tell likewise’ has the elided singular present imperative *λέγε* of *λέγω*; *ἐπ’ ἴσης* (an adverbial expression, with the genitive) links the two requests without repeating the ‘us’ of *dinne*. *Εἰ δυνατόν σοι* ‘if it is possible for you’ replaces personal *se tu puoi* with an impersonal neuter predicate, an understood copula, and a dative of the person concerned.
- The second *εἴ* means ‘whether’, introducing an indirect question. *Τις ποτ’ ἀπηλλάχθη* ‘any was ever set free’ replaces present reflexive *alcuna mai … si spiega* with a retrospective aorist passive (3rd sg. ind. of *ἀπαλλάσσω*); *ποτ’* is elided *ποτε*. The genitive of separation *τῶν μελῶν τούτων* gives ‘from these limbs’, demonstrative rather than qualitative as in *da tai membra*.

> 91 Allor soffiò lo tronco forte, e poi  
> 92 si convertì quel vento in cotal voce:  

Then the trunk blew strongly, and soon that wind was changed into these words:

> 91 Τότ’ ἐφύσησ’ ὁ κορμὸς ἰσχυρῶς, κᾆτα  
> (Then the trunk blew strongly, and thereupon)
>
> 92 Μετετράπη τὸ φύσημ’ εἰς λέξεις τάσδε.  
> (The blast was turned into these words.)

- *Τότ’ ἐφύσησ’ ὁ κορμὸς ἰσχυρῶς* closely follows *Allor soffiò lo tronco forte*, including the postverbal subject. *Τότ’* is *τότε*; *ἐφύσησ’* is elided *ἐφύσησε*, the augmented 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *φυσάω*. The adjective used adverbially in Italian becomes an explicit adverb in *-ως*.
- *Κᾆτα* is crasis of *καὶ εἶτα*, ‘and then’. *Μετετράπη* (3rd sg. second aor. pass. ind. of *μετατρέπω*, with internal augment) answers to *si convertì*. Its subject *τὸ φύσημ’* (elided *φύσημα*) picks up the verb ‘blow’ more directly than *quel vento* does. *Εἰς λέξεις τάσδε* ‘into these words’ replaces singular *in cotal voce* with a plural and a demonstrative pointing forward.

> 93 “Brevemente sarà risposto a voi.  

“Briefly shall you be answered.

> 93 “Ὑμῖν ἀποκρινοῦμαι διὰ βραχέων.  
> ("To you I will answer briefly.)

- *Ὑμῖν ἀποκρινοῦμαι διὰ βραχέων* ‘to you I shall reply in few words’ brings *a voi* to the front. The Italian impersonal passive *sarà risposto* becomes a personal 1st sg. future middle of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, with the addressees in the dative. *Διὰ βραχέων*, literally ‘through few [words]’, puts a prepositional phrase for the opening adverb *Brevemente*.

> 94 Quando si parte l’ anima feroce  
> 95 dal corpo ond’ ella stessa s’ è divelta,  
> 96 Minos la manda alla settima foce.  

When the fierce spirit quits the body, from which it has torn itself, Minos sends it to the seventh gulf.

> 94 „ Ὅταν ἐξέλθῃ θηριώδης ψυχή τις  
> (When a fierce soul departs)
>
> 95 „ Τοῦ σώματος ἑαυτῆς αὐτοχειρίᾳ,  
> (From its body by its own hand,)
>
> 96 „ Αὐτὴν εἰς κύκλον ἕβδομον πέμπει Μίνως·  
> (Minos sends it to the seventh circle;)

- *Ὅταν ἐξέλθῃ θηριώδης ψυχή τις* ‘whenever some fierce soul goes out’ marks a recurring case more explicitly than *Quando si parte l’ anima feroce*: *ὅταν* (temporal conjunction with *ἄν*) takes the subjunctive. *Ἐξέλθῃ* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. subj. of *ἐξέρχομαι*, unaugmented, the departure viewed as a whole; indefinite *τις* replaces the definite article.
- *Τοῦ σώματος* is the genitive of separation after *ἐξέλθῃ*. Instead of a relative clause for *ond’ ella stessa s’ è divelta*, Musurus has *ἑαυτῆς αὐτοχειρίᾳ* ‘by her own act’: the feminine reflexive genitive refers to the soul, and the instrumental dative of *αὐτοχειρία* expresses agency by one’s own hand. The image of tearing and the separate perfect verb disappear.
- *Αὐτὴν εἰς κύκλον ἕβδομον πέμπει Μίνως* moves the object from Italian clitic *la* to emphatic initial position and *Minos* from first to last. *Πέμπει* remains a general present. *Εἰς* with the accusative gives the destination, but *κύκλον* ‘circle’ interprets rather than reproduces *foce*.

> 97 Cade in la selva e non l’ è parte scelta;  

It falls into the wood, and no place is chosen for it;

> 97 „ Πίπτει δ᾽ ἐν τῷ δρυμῶνι, καθὼς ἂν τύχῃ.  
> (And it falls in the forest, wherever chance wills.)

- *Πίπτει δ’ ἐν τῷ δρυμῶνι* has connective *δέ* and locative *ἐν* with the dative of *δρυμών*: the forest is where it falls, rather than the goal marked by *εἰς* in the preceding line. *Πίπτει* is 3rd sg. pres. act. ind.
- *Καθὼς ἂν τύχῃ* ‘just as chance may have it’ replaces the negative passive *non l’ è parte scelta*. Neither ‘place’ nor a chooser is expressed. *Τύχῃ* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. subj. of *τυγχάνω*, not the noun *τύχη*; *ἄν* with the subjunctive makes the subordinate clause indefinite.

> 98 ma là dove fortuna la balestra,  
> 99 quivi germoglia come gran di spelta;  

but wherever fortune flings it, there it sprouts, like grain of spelt;

> 98 „ Ἀλλ᾽ ἔνθαπερ ἔῤῥιψεν αὐτὴν ἡ Μοῖρα,  
> (But wherever Fate has cast it,)
>
> 99 „ Ἐκεῖ βλαστάνει, καθὰ κόκκος ὀλύρας·  
> (There it sprouts, like a grain of spelt;)

- *Ἀλλ’ ἔνθαπερ … ἐκεῖ* preserves the correlation *ma là dove … quivi*: *-περ* stresses the exact correspondence, ‘in the very place where … there’. *Ἔῤῥιψεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ῥίπτω*) shows doubling of initial rho after the augment, in the older spelling with breathings. The aorist makes the casting anterior to the sprouting, where Italian has present *balestra*.
- *Αὐτὴν ἡ Μοῖρα* places the object before the named subject ‘Fate’, a stronger personification than *fortuna*. *Βλαστάνει* ‘it sprouts’ retains the present of *germoglia*. In *καθὰ κόκκος ὀλύρας* ‘as a grain of spelt [does]’, *καθά* introduces the comparison, nominative *κόκκος* is its subject, and *ὀλύρας* is gen. sg. of *ὄλυρα*.

> 100 surge in vermena ed in pianta silvestra;  

shoots up to a sapling, and to a savage plant;

> 100 „ Φύεται θάμνος, εἶτα δ᾽ ἄγριον δένδρον·  
> (It grows into a bush, and then a wild tree;)

- *Φύεται θάμνος, εἶτα δ’ ἄγριον δένδρον* ‘it grows as a shrub, and then as a wild tree’ replaces *surge in … ed in* with intransitive *φύεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *φύω*) and successive predicate nominatives. *Εἶτα* explicitly marks stages; *θάμνος* is a bush rather than a shoot, and *ἄγριον* agrees with neuter *δένδρον*, not with the feminine subject understood from *ψυχή*.

> 101 l’ Arpíe, pascendo poi delle sue foglie,  
> 102 fanno dolore, ed al dolor finestra.  

the Harpies, feeding then upon its leaves, give pain, and to the pain an outlet.

> 101 „ Αὐτοῦ δ᾽ Ἅρπυιαι βοσκόμεναι τὰ φύλλα  
> (And there the Harpies, feeding upon its leaves,)
>
> 102 „ Ἀλγηδόνας ποιοῦσιν, ἀλγεινά θ᾽ ἕλκη.  
> (Cause pains, and painful wounds.)

- *Αὐτοῦ δ’* ‘and there’ is naturally the locative adverb: Musurus places the feeding at the tree rather than marking its time with *poi*. *Ἅρπυιαι βοσκόμεναι* makes the Italian gerund an agreeing pres. mid. participle (nom. fem. pl. of *βόσκω*), ‘the Harpies as they feed’. *Τὰ φύλλα* is its accusative object, in place of partitive *delle sue foglie*, without an explicit possessive.
- *Ἀλγηδόνας ποιοῦσιν, ἀλγεινά θ’ ἕλκη* ‘they cause pains and painful wounds’ gives *ποιοῦσιν* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *ποιέω*) two plural accusative objects. Postpositive *τε*, elided and aspirated before the rough breathing of *ἕλκη*, links the second to the first. The echo *ἀλγηδόνας … ἀλγεινά* preserves the repetition in *dolore … al dolor*, but the dative sense ‘for the pain’ and the metaphor *finestra* give way to an adjective and the physical ‘wounds’.

> 103 Come l’ altre verrem per nostre spoglie,  
> 104 ma non però ch’ alcuna sen rivesta:  

Like the others, we shall go for our spoils, [but not to the end that any may be] clothe[d] with them again:

> 103 „ Ἕξομεν δ᾽ ἡμέτερον, ὡς ἄλλοι, σῶμα·  
> (And we shall have our body, like the others;)
>
> 104 „ Ἀλλ᾽ οὐδεὶς ἡμῶν ἐνδύσετ᾽ αὐτὸ πάλιν·  
> (Yet none of us shall put it on again;)

- *Ἕξομεν … ἡμέτερον … σῶμα* ‘we shall have our body’ states the result instead of the journey in *verrem per nostre spoglie*. *Ἕξομεν* is 1st pl. fut. act. ind. of *ἔχω*, with rough breathing in the future; singular *σῶμα* is distributive. The possessive adjective is separated from its noun by *ὡς ἄλλοι* ‘like others’, whose nominative compares the others with the implicit subject ‘we’.
- *Ἀλλ’ οὐδεὶς ἡμῶν ἐνδύσετ’ αὐτὸ πάλιν* ‘but none of us will put it on again’ makes an independent future assertion of *ma non però ch’ alcuna sen rivesta*. *Οὐδείς* takes the partitive genitive *ἡμῶν*. *Ἐνδύσετ’* is elided *ἐνδύσεται*, 3rd sg. fut. mid. ind. of *ἐνδύω*: the middle denotes putting something on oneself, while neuter *αὐτό* refers to *σῶμα*. *Πάλιν* renders the force of *ri-* separately.

> 105 chè non è giusto aver ciò ch’ uom si toglie.  

for it is not just that a man have what he takes from himself.

> 105 „ Ὃ γὰρ αὑτοῦ τις ἀφεῖλ᾽, ἔχειν οὐ θέμις.  
> (For what one took from himself, it is not lawful to possess.)

- *Ὃ γὰρ αὑτοῦ τις ἀφεῖλ’* ‘for what someone took from himself’ fronts the relative clause; neuter accusative *ὅ* is object of *ἀφεῖλ’* and the whole expression supplies the object of *ἔχειν*. Postpositive *γάρ* gives the reason. *Τις* renders generic *uom*; *αὑτοῦ* is the contracted reflexive genitive, expressing deprivation.
- *Ἀφεῖλ’* is elided *ἀφεῖλε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἀφαιρέω*), treating the taking as accomplished, unlike present *si toglie*. In *ἔχειν οὐ θέμις* ‘to have [it] is not lawful’, the present active infinitive corresponds to *aver*; the impersonal nominal predicate *οὐ θέμις* needs no expressed copula.

> 106 Qui le strascineremo, e per la mesta  
> 107 selva saranno i nostri corpi appesi,  
> 108 ciascuno al prun dell’ ombra sua molesta.”  

Hither shall we drag them, and through the mournful wood our bodies shall be suspended, each on the thorny tree of its tormented shade.”

> 106 „ Συροῦμεν δ᾽ ὧδε τὰ σώματα, κἀν τούτῳ  
> (But we shall drag our bodies hither, and in this)
>
> 107 „ Κρεμάμεν᾽ ἔσται τῷ λυπηρῷ δρυμῶνι,  
> (Mournful forest they shall be hanging,)
>
> 108 „ Ἕκαστον εἰς τῆς αὑτοῦ ψυχῆς τὸ δένδρον.”  
> (Each upon the tree of its own soul.")

- *Συροῦμεν δ’ ὧδε τὰ σώματα* gives the verb first, postpones directional *ὧδε* ‘hither’, and names ‘the bodies’ immediately instead of using the object clitic in *le strascineremo*. *Συροῦμεν* is 1st pl. contracted fut. act. ind. of *σύρω*, distinct from present *σύρομεν*.
- *Κἀν τούτῳ … τῷ λυπηρῷ δρυμῶνι* ‘and in this mournful forest’ has crasis of *καὶ ἐν*, adds a demonstrative, and uses a locative dative for *per la mesta selva*. Between demonstrative and articular noun stands *κρεμάμεν’ ἔσται*: elided *κρεμάμενα* (pres. mid./pass. participle of *κρέμαμαι*, nom. neut. pl.) describes the hanging posture; singular future *ἔσται* is regular with the neuter plural subject, the bodies.
- *Ἕκαστον* distributes that plural, ‘each one’. In *εἰς τῆς αὑτοῦ ψυχῆς τὸ δένδρον*, *εἰς* governs accusative *τὸ δένδρον*, with the dependent genitive ‘of its own soul’ interposed. The directional phrase for *al prun* suggests the point of attachment despite the static predicate. Generic ‘tree’ and ‘soul’ replace *prun* and *ombra*; *molesta* has no counterpart.

> 109 Noi eravamo ancora al tronco attesi,  
> 110 credendo ch’ altro ne volesse dire,  
> 111 quando noi fummo d’ un romor sorpresi,  

We still were listening to the trunk, thinking it would tell us more, when by a noise we were surprised;

> 109 Ὡς δ᾽ ἠκούομεν αὐτοῦ παρὰ τῇ βάτῳ,  
> (And while we were listening to him beside the bramble,)
>
> 110 Δοκοῦντες ὡς ἔμελλεν ἄλλο τι λέγειν,  
> (Supposing that he was about to say something more,)
>
> 111 Αἴφνης ἐξεπλάγημεν ἔκ τινος κρότου  
> (Suddenly we were startled by a loud noise)

- *Ὡς δ’ ἠκούομεν αὐτοῦ παρὰ τῇ βάτῳ* ‘and while we were listening to him beside the bramble’ reverses the subordination: Italian begins with a main clause, *Noi eravamo ancora al tronco attesi*. The augmented imperfect *ἠκούομεν* (1st pl. act. ind. of *ἀκούω*) conveys continued attention without an adverb for *ancora*. Genitive *αὐτοῦ* names the speaker listened to; *παρά* with the dative separately locates the listeners.
- *Δοκοῦντες* (nom. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *δοκέω*) agrees with the unexpressed ‘we’, rendering *credendo*. *Ὡς ἔμελλεν ἄλλο τι λέγειν* is its content clause: imperfect *ἔμελλεν* with present infinitive *λέγειν* gives ‘he was about to say’, rather than volition in *volesse dire*. *Ἄλλο τι* is ‘something else’; the recipient in *ne* is not expressed.
- *Αἴφνης ἐξεπλάγημεν ἔκ τινος κρότου* is now the main clause, with ‘suddenly’ instead of temporal *quando*. *Ἐξεπλάγημεν* (1st pl. second aor. pass. ind. of *ἐκπλήσσω*) has its augment after the prefix. *Ἐκ* with genitive *τινος κρότου* gives the source of the shock, ‘from a certain crash’.

> 112 similemente a colui che venire  
> 113 sente il porco e la caccia alla sua posta,  
> 114 ch’ ode le bestie e le frasche stormire.  

like one who feels the boar and chase approaching to his stand, who hears the beasts and the branches crashing.

> 112 Παραπλησίου τῷ τῶν κυνηγεσίων,  
> (Similar to that of a hunt,)
>
> 113 Ὅτ᾽ ἄγριον σῦν στῖφος κυνῶν διώκει,  
> (When a pack of hounds pursues a wild boar,)
>
> 114 Κἀκούεται πάταγος ζώων καὶ κλάδων.  
> (And a crash of beasts and boughs is heard.)

- *Παραπλησίου* (gen. masc. sg. of *παραπλήσιος*) agrees back with *κρότου*: the comparison is between noises, not between the listeners and *colui*. Its dative complement *τῷ τῶν κυνηγεσίων* means ‘to the [noise] of the hunts’, with the noun understood after the article and a dependent genitive plural. There is no equivalent of *alla sua posta*.
- *Ὅτ’ ἄγριον σῦν στῖφος κυνῶν διώκει* substitutes a temporal clause (‘when a pack of dogs pursues a wild boar’) for *che venire sente il porco e la caccia*. *Ὅτ’* is elided *ὅτε*; accusative *σῦν* (of *σῦς*) is object, and collective singular *στῖφος*, qualified by genitive plural *κυνῶν* (of *κύων*), governs singular present *διώκει*.
- *Κἀκούεται* is crasis of *καὶ ἀκούεται*, 3rd sg. pres. pass. ind.: instead of a person who hears, a *πάταγος* ‘clatter’ is heard. The Italian accusatives with infinitive, *le bestie e le frasche stormire*, become genitives of the sources of the sound, *ζώων καὶ κλάδων*.

> 115 Ed ecco duo dalla sinistra costa,  
> 116 nudi e graffiati, fuggendo sì forte,  
> 117 che della selva rompièno ogni rosta.  

And, lo! on the left hand, two *spirits*, naked and torn, fleeing so violently that they broke every fan of the wood.

> 115 Ἰδοὺ δὲ δύο πρὸς τἀριστερὸν μέρος,  
> (And behold, two on the left side,)
>
> 116 Γυμνοὶ καὶ δεδρυμμένοι, τρέχοντες σφόδρα,  
> (Naked and torn, running furiously,)
>
> 117 Ὥστε συνθλᾶσθαι τὰ πάντ᾽ ἐν τῷ δρυμῶνι·  
> (So that everything in the forest was crushed;)

- *Ἰδοὺ δὲ δύο* closely mirrors *Ed ecco duo*: presentative *ἰδού* (in origin a singular imperative) introduces the bare numeral, with no noun for “spirits”. *Πρὸς τἀριστερὸν μέρος* ‘on the left side’ has crasis of *τὸ ἀριστερόν* and *πρός* with the accusative, rather than an explicit ‘from’ for *dalla*.
- *Γυμνοὶ καὶ δεδρυμμένοι, τρέχοντες σφόδρα* uses masculine nominative plurals, not dual forms despite *δύο*. *Δεδρυμμένοι* is the reduplicated perfect mid./pass. participle of *δρύπτω*, describing the resulting scratches; present active *τρέχοντες* renders *fuggendo* as ‘running’, without its sense of flight. *Σφόδρα* supplies the intensity of *sì forte*.
- *Ὥστε συνθλᾶσθαι τὰ πάντ’ ἐν τῷ δρυμῶνι* changes a finite active result (*che … rompièno*) to *ὥστε* with a present passive infinitive (of *συνθλάω*). Elided *τὰ πάντα* is its neuter plural accusative subject: ‘so that everything in the forest was crushed’, generalizing *ogni rosta* and making *della selva* a locative phrase.

> 118 Quel dinanzi: “Ora accorri, accorri, morte!”  

The foremost: “Come now, come, O death!”

> 118 Κράζει δ᾽ ὁ πρῶτος· “ Νῦν ἔρχου, θάνατ᾽, ἔρχου.”  
> (And the first cries out: "Now come, O death, come.")

- *Κράζει δ’ ὁ πρῶτος* supplies a historical present, ‘and the first cries’, where *Quel dinanzi* has no verb. The substantivized ordinal renders position in the pair.
- *Νῦν ἔρχου, θάνατ’, ἔρχου* retains *Ora* and the repeated singular command, but places the vocative between the imperatives. *Ἔρχου* is 2nd sg. pres. mid. imperative of *ἔρχομαι*, simply ‘come’ for the more urgent *accorri*; *θάνατ’* is elided vocative *θάνατε*, without a vocative particle.

> 119 E l’ altro, a cui pareva tardar troppo,  
> 120 gridava: “Lano sì non furo accorte  

And the other, who thought himself too slow, cried: “Lano, thy legs were not so ready

> 119 Ὁ δ᾽ ἕτερος ὡς δοκῶν βραδύνειν ἄγαν,  
> (And the other, as thinking himself to be lagging too much,)
>
> 120 Ἔκραζε· “ Λάνε, τοὺς πόδας ὠκεῖς οὕτως  
> (Cried out: "Lano, feet so swift)

- *Ὁ δ’ ἕτερος* is ‘and the other [of the two]’. *Ὡς δοκῶν βραδύνειν ἄγαν* replaces relative *a cui pareva tardar troppo* with a nominative pres. act. participle of *δοκέω* and a present active infinitive whose subject is the other runner himself. *Ὡς* presents the lagging as his own impression or reason for calling; *ἄγαν* modifies *βραδύνειν*, ‘to be excessively slow’.
- *Ἔκραζε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *κράζω*) matches imperfect *gridava*, in contrast to *κράζει* in 118. *Λάνε* marks the address by a Greek singular vocative. *Τοὺς πόδας ὠκεῖς οὕτως* brings ‘the feet’ explicitly into this line, with the article rather than a possessive; *ὠκεῖς* is masc. acc. pl. of *ὠκύς*, predicative to *πόδας*, ‘feet so swift’. The quoted Greek breaks off before the governing verb or negation: *οὕτως* answers to *sì*, but one should not read the affirmative fragment as a reversal of *non furo accorte*.

## Lines 121–151 — The Hounds and the Wounded Bush

> 121 le gambe tue alle giostre del Toppo.”  

at the jousts of Toppo.”

> 121 „ Οὐκ εἶχες ἐν ταῖς Τόππου πεζομαχίαις.”  
> (You did not have in the foot-battles of Toppo.")

- *Οὐκ εἶχες* ‘you did not have’ places the negative and the verb here, where the Italian has *le gambe tue*, the object of the preceding line’s verb. *Εἶχες* is the 2nd sg. imperfect active indicative of *ἔχω*, with the irregular imperfect stem *εἰχ-*; *οὐκ* is the form of the indicative negative before a smooth-breathed vowel.
- *ἐν ταῖς Τόππου πεζομαχίαις* ‘in the foot-battles of Toppo’ interprets rather than reproduces *alle giostre del Toppo*. *Ἐν* takes the dative plural; the genitive *Τόππου* stands between the article and its noun.

> 122 E poichè forse gli fallía la lena  
> 123 di sè e d’ un cespuglio fece groppo.  

And [since] his breath perhaps [was] failing him, of himself and of a bush he made one group.

> 122 Καταληφθεὶς δὲ πιθανῶς ἀπνευστίᾳ,  
> (And overtaken, likely, by lack of breath,)
>
> 123 Προσέφυγεν εἰς θάμνον, ᾧ συνεπλέχθη.  
> (He fled for refuge into a bush, with which he entwined himself.)

- *Καταληφθεὶς δὲ πιθανῶς ἀπνευστίᾳ* ‘and being overtaken, perhaps, by breathlessness’ replaces the finite causal clause *poichè forse gli fallía la lena*. *Καταληφθείς* (nom. sg. masc., aor. pass. participle of *καταλαμβάνω*) agrees with the man rather than with his breath; the circumstance expressed by the participle supplies the cause without a conjunction meaning ‘since’. Postpositive *δέ* continues the narrative; *ἀπνευστίᾳ* is a dative of cause.
- *Προσέφυγεν εἰς θάμνον, ᾧ συνεπλέχθη* ‘he fled for refuge into a bush, with which he became entangled’ divides *di sè e d’ un cespuglio fece groppo* into a motion and its result. *Προσέφυγεν* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *προσφεύγω*, augmented after the prefix; *εἰς* takes the accusative of destination. The relative *ᾧ* has its gender and number from *θάμνον* but is dative of association with *συνεπλέχθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *συμπλέκω*). There is no separate noun for *groppo*.

> 124 Di retro a loro era la selva piena  
> 125 di nere cagne bramose e correnti,  
> 126 come veltri che uscisser di catena.  

Behind them, the wood was filled with black braches, eager and fleet, as greyhounds that have escaped the leash.

> 124 Ὡς δ᾽ ὄπισθεν αὐτῶν ἦν ὁ δρυμὼν πλήρης  
> (And when behind them the forest was full)
>
> 125 Κυνῶν μελαινῶν, λυσσωδῶν, ἁλλομένων,  
> (Of black, raging hounds, leaping)
>
> 126 Ὡς λαγοθῆραι δεσμῶν ἀπολυθέντες,  
> (Like hare-hounds loosed from their leashes,)

- Initial *Ὡς* ‘when’ makes *di retro a loro era la selva piena* subordinate to the biting in 127. *Δ᾽* is elided *δέ*; *ὄπισθεν* governs gen. pl. *αὐτῶν*. *Ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. of *εἰμί*) joins *ὁ δρυμών* to predicate *πλήρης*, which in turn governs *κυνῶν*, gen. pl. of *κύων*.
- *μελαινῶν, λυσσωδῶν, ἁλλομένων* postpones all three descriptions until after *κυνῶν*. *Μελαινῶν* (from *μέλας, μέλαινα, μέλαν*) marks the dogs as feminine, like *cagne*. *Λυσσωδῶν* ‘raging’ intensifies *bramose*; *ἁλλομένων* ‘leaping’ (gen. pl., pres. mid. participle of *ἅλλομαι*) changes *correnti* from running to bounding.
- The second *ὡς* is comparative: *λαγοθῆραι* ‘hare-hunters’ renders *veltri*. The comparison takes its own nominative plural rather than continuing the genitives. *Ἀπολυθέντες* (nom. pl. masc., aor. pass. participle of *ἀπολύω*) agrees with *λαγοθῆραι*: *che uscisser di catena*, with an imperfect subjunctive, becomes an accomplished release, with *δεσμῶν* genitive of separation, ‘loosed from bonds’.

> 127 In quel che s’ appiattò miser li denti,  
> 128 e quel dilaceraro a brano a brano;  
> 129 poi sen portar quelle membra dolenti.  

Into him, who squatted, they thrust their teeth, and rent him piece by piece; then carried off his miserable limbs.

> 127 Ἔδακον ὀδὰξ τὸν ἐκεῖ προσφυγόντα,  
> (They sank their teeth into him who had fled thither,)
>
> 128 Κατεσπάραξάν τ᾽ αὐτὸν εἰς μέρη τμήδην,  
> (And tore him limb from limb piecemeal,)
>
> 129 Μακράν τ᾽ ἔσυραν αὐτοῦ τἀλγοῦντα μέλη.  
> (And dragged far away his suffering limbs.)

- *Ἔδακον ὀδάξ* ‘they bit with their teeth’ exchanges *miser li denti* for a verb of biting reinforced by the poetic adverb *ὀδάξ*. *Ἔδακον* is 3rd pl. second aor. act. ind. of *δάκνω*. Its direct object, *τὸν ἐκεῖ προσφυγόντα* ‘the one who had fled there’, is an articular aorist active participle (acc. sg. masc. of *προσφεύγω*); it recalls 123 instead of translating the crouching or hiding of *s’ appiattò*.
- *Κατεσπάραξαν* (3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *κατασπαράσσω*) takes *αὐτόν* directly. *Εἰς μέρη* ‘into pieces’ gives the result, *τμήδην* ‘piecemeal’ the manner: two different expressions answer the repetition in *a brano a brano*.
- Postpositive *τ᾽ … τ᾽* (elided *τε*) links the successive predicates without a distinct equivalent of *poi*. *Μακράν* ‘far away’ is adverbial; *ἔσυραν* (3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *σύρω*) makes *sen portar* a dragging away. Possessive *αὐτοῦ* replaces the demonstrative in *quelle membra dolenti*; *τἀλγοῦντα* is crasis of *τὰ ἀλγοῦντα*, article and pres. act. participle of *ἀλγέω*, acc. pl. neut. with *μέλη*, literally ‘the limbs that feel pain’.

> 130 Presemi allor la mia scorta per mano,  
> 131 e menommi al cespuglio che piangea,  
> 132 per le rotture sanguinenti, invano.  

My Guide now took me by the hand, and led me to the bush, which was lamenting through its bleeding fractures, in vain.

> 130 Τότ᾽ ἐλάβετο τῆς χειρὸς ὁ Προστάτης  
> (Then the Master took me by the hand)
>
> 131 Καὶ τοῦ θάμνου πλησίον ὡδήγησέ με,  
> (And led me close to the bush,)
>
> 132 Τὰς αἱμοφύρτους πληγὰς θρηνοῦντος μάτην.  
> (Which was bewailing its blood-stained wounds in vain.)

- *Τότ᾽* (elided *τότε*) puts *allor* first. *Ἐλάβετο τῆς χειρός* ‘took hold of the hand’ uses the middle of *λαμβάνω* (3rd sg. second aor. mid. ind.) with the genitive of the part grasped. The owner is understood, not separately expressed as in *presemi*. *Ὁ Προστάτης* ‘the protector’ renders *la mia scorta* without *mia*.
- *τοῦ θάμνου πλησίον* ‘near the bush’ uses *πλησίον* after its genitive rather than *al* marking the goal. *Ὡδήγησέ με* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ὁδηγέω*, with temporal augment) now expresses the personal object of *menommi*; the extra acute on the verb precedes enclitic *με*.
- *θρηνοῦντος* (gen. sg. masc., pres. act. participle of *θρηνέω*) agrees with *θάμνου*, replacing *che piangea* and retaining its ongoing action. But *τὰς αἱμοφύρτους πληγάς* ‘the blood-stained wounds’ is what the bush laments, an accusative object, not the openings *per le rotture sanguinenti* through which it laments. *Μάτην* ‘in vain’ retains the emphatic final position of *invano*.

> 133 “O Jacomo,” dicea, “da Sant’ Andrea,  
> 134 che t’ è giovato di me fare schermo?  

“O Jacomo da Sant’ Andrea!” it cried, “what hast thou gained by making me thy screen?

> 133 “ Ἰάκωβ᾽,” ἐκραύγαζεν, “ ὁ Σαντανδρέα,  
> ("Jacomo," it cried aloud, "da Sant’ Andrea,)
>
> 134 „ Πρὸς τί παρ᾽ ἐμὲ σκέπην εὑρήσων ἦλθες ;  
> (Wherefore did you come to find shelter beside me?)

- *Ἰάκωβ᾽* is elided vocative *Ἰάκωβε*. Musurus inserts *ἐκραύγαζεν* ‘it was crying aloud’ (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *κραυγάζω*) between the two parts of the name, like *dicea* but with a stronger verb. *ὁ Σαντανδρέα* treats the byname as a foreign designation with a nominative article in apposition to the address, not as a Greek prepositional phrase for *da Sant’ Andrea*.
- *Πρὸς τί … ἦλθες;* ‘for what purpose did you come?’ asks the aim rather than the benefit expressed by *che t’ è giovato*. *Εὑρήσων* (nom. sg. masc., fut. act. participle of *εὑρίσκω*) expresses purpose with *ἦλθες* (2nd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*). *Παρ᾽ ἐμέ* (elided *παρά* + acc.) gives the direction of approach; *σκέπην* ‘shelter’ is the object of *εὑρήσων*. Thus the speaker is the person approached, no longer the material *di me* out of which a screen is made.

> 135 che colpa ho io della tua vita rea?”  

what blame have I of thy sinful life?”

> 135 „ Δι’ ἁμαρτίας τὰς σὰς ἐγὼ τί πταίω;”  
> (What blame have I for your sins?")

- *Δι’ ἁμαρτίας τὰς σάς* ‘on account of the sins that are yours’ fronts the grounds of the complaint. *Διά* takes the accusative of cause; *τὰς σάς* is a postposed articular possessive agreeing with *ἁμαρτίας*. Particular ‘sins’ replace the collective *vita rea*.
- *ἐγὼ τί πταίω;* ‘in what am I at fault?’ sets explicit *ἐγώ* against *σάς*. The verb *πταίω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.) absorbs the sense of *colpa ho*, leaving interrogative neuter *τί* as an accusative of respect rather than an adjective qualifying ‘blame’.

> 136 Quando il maestro fu sopr’ esso fermo,  
> 137 disse: “Chi fusti, che per tante punte  
> 138 soffi con sangue doloroso sermo?”  

When the Master had stopped beside it, he said: “Who wast thou, who, through so many wounds, blowest forth with blood thy dolorous speech?”

> 136 Ἐγγὺς δ’ αὐτοῦ προσελθὼν οὑμὸς Προστάτης  
> (And having drawn near to it, my Master)
>
> 137 Εἶπε· “Τίς ἦσθα, καὶ διὰ πληγῶν τόσων  
> (Said: "Who were you, and through so many wounds)
>
> 138 „ Ἀλγεινοὺς σὺν αἵματι λόγους ἐρεύγῃ;”  
> (Breathe out sorrowful words with blood?")

- *Ἐγγὺς δ’ αὐτοῦ προσελθών* ‘and having come near it’ changes *quando … fu sopr’ esso fermo* from a finite temporal clause of standing still to a participle of approach. *Ἐγγύς* governs the genitive; *προσελθών* (nom. sg. masc., second aor. act. participle of *προσέρχομαι*) agrees with the subject of *εἶπε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind., ‘said’). *Οὑμὸς Προστάτης* adds a possessive to *il maestro*: *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*.
- *Τίς ἦσθα* ‘who were you?’ uses the Attic 2nd sg. imperfect of *εἰμί* for *chi fusti*. *Καί* then coordinates a further question instead of subordinating it with relative *che*. *Διὰ πληγῶν τόσων* (gen. pl.) expresses passage ‘through so many wounds’, interpreting *punte* by its effect.
- *Ἀλγεινοὺς … λόγους* turns singular *doloroso sermo* into a plural direct object, with *σὺν αἵματι* (*σύν* + dat.) between adjective and noun. *Ἐρεύγῃ* (2nd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *ἐρεύγομαι*) is more forceful than *soffi*: literally ‘you disgorge’ the words.

> (And it said: "O souls, who the painful)
>
> 140 „ Ἰδεῖν ἐλθοῦσαι σπαραγμόν, ὅστις ἤδη  
> (Rending have come to see, which just now)
>
> 141 „ Τῶν ἐμῶν φύλλων ἐχώρισέ με, ταῦτα  
> (Severed me from my leaves, these)
>
> 142 „ Συλλέξατε νῦν παρὰ τῷδε τῷ θάμνῳ.  
> (Gather now beside this bush.)

- *Αὐτὸς δ’ εἶπεν* ‘and he said’ supplies the verb left implicit in *e quegli a noi*, but drops the addressee *a noi*. *Ὦ ψυχαί* is vocative plural; *ἐλθοῦσαι* (voc. pl. fem., second aor. act. participle of *ἔρχομαι*) agrees with it, replacing finite *che giunte siete*. The purpose infinitive *ἰδεῖν* (second aor. act. of *ὁράω*) precedes the participle, while its object *τὸν ὀδυνώδη … σπαραγμόν* encloses both. The adjective means ‘painful’, not *disonesto* ‘ignominious’.
- *ὅστις* (nom. sg. masc. of the compound relative) refers to *σπαραγμόν* and is subject of *ἐχώρισέ με* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *χωρίζω*, accented before enclitic *με*). *Ἤδη* gives temporal ‘just now’ rather than *sì* ‘thus’. Musurus reverses the objects of separation: ‘separated **me** from my leaves’, with genitive *τῶν ἐμῶν φύλλων*, against *le mie fronde … da me*.
- *ταῦτα* (acc. pl. neut.) picks up *φύλλων* in the case required by *συλλέξατε* (2nd pl. aor. act. imperative of *συλλέγω*), where *raccoglietele* has an attached pronoun. *Νῦν* ‘now’ is added. *Παρὰ τῷδε τῷ θάμνῳ* (*παρά* + dat.) specifies position ‘beside this very bush’; proximal *τῷδε* replaces the description *tristo*, and *al piè* has no distinct equivalent.

> 143 Io fui della città che nel Batista  
> 144 mutò ’l primo patrono: ond’ ei per questo  
> 145 sempre con l’ arte sua la farà trista;  

I was of the city that changed its first patron for the Baptist, on which account he with his art will always make it sorrowful;

> 143 „ Ἤμην πόλεως, ἣ τὸν πρῶτον προστάτην  
> (I was of the city that its first patron)
>
> 144 „ Μετέβαλ’ εἰς βαπτιστήν· ἀνθ’ ὧν ἐκεῖνος  
> (Changed for the Baptist; wherefore he)
>
> 145 „ ᾈεὶ μηχαναῖς ἀθλίαν καταστήσει.  
> (Will ever by his arts make it wretched.)

- *Ἤμην πόλεως* follows *io fui della città* closely, with a predicative genitive of origin and no article. *Ἤμην* is the later 1st sg. imperfect of *εἰμί* in place of Attic *ἦ*. Relative *ἥ* is feminine singular from *πόλεως* but nominative as subject of *μετέβαλ᾽* (elided *μετέβαλε*, 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *μεταβάλλω*).
- *τὸν πρῶτον προστάτην … εἰς βαπτιστήν* keeps the former patron as direct object and marks the replacement by *εἰς* + acc., closely following *mutò ’l primo patrono* and *nel Batista*. Greek puts the replacement after the verb and leaves *βαπτιστήν* anarthrous.
- *ἀνθ’ ὧν* ‘in return for which things’ refers to the preceding action as a whole: *ἀντί* is elided and its *τ* aspirated before rough-breathed gen. pl. *ὧν*. *Ἐκεῖνος* points back to the former patron like *ei*. *ᾈεί* (poetic spelling of *ἀεί*) renders *sempre*; instrumental dat. pl. *μηχαναῖς* ‘by devices’ replaces singular *con l’ arte sua*, with possession understood. *Καταστήσει* (3rd sg. fut. act. ind. of *καθίστημι*) is causative, ‘will render’; predicate acc. fem. *ἀθλίαν* describes the city, but the object itself, explicit in *la farà*, is unexpressed.

> 146 e se non fosse che in sul passo d’ Arno  
> 147 rimane ancor di lui alcuna vista,  

and were it not that at the passage of the Arno there yet remains some semblance of him,

> 146 „ Εἰ μὴ δ’ ἔμενεν ἐπὶ γεφύρας Ἄρνου  
> (And were there not remaining upon the bridge of Arno)
>
> 147 „ Ἔτι θέα τις ἐκείνου σωζομένη,  
> (Still some semblance of him preserved,)

- *Εἰ μὴ δ’ ἔμενεν* ‘and if there did not remain’ absorbs *se non fosse che … rimane* into one negative protasis. *Εἰ μή* with *ἔμενεν* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *μένω*) presents an unreal continuing condition; its consequence is deferred to 148–150. *Ἐπὶ γεφύρας Ἄρνου* uses *ἐπί* + gen. for position and interprets *passo* specifically as a bridge.
- *Ἔτι θέα τις ἐκείνου* ‘still some visible likeness of him’ retains *ancor*, *alcuna*, and *di lui*. Enclitic indefinite *τις* qualifies nominative *θέα*, the delayed subject of *ἔμενεν*; genitive *ἐκείνου* identifies the one represented. *Σωζομένη* (nom. sg. fem., pres. pass. participle of *σῴζω*) adds the state in which the likeness remains, ‘being preserved’.

> 148 quei cittadin, che poi la rifondarno  
> 149 sopra il cener che d’ Attila rimase,  
> 150 avrebber fatto lavorare indarno.  

those citizens, who afterwards rebuilt it on the ashes left by Attila, would have laboured in vain.

> 148 „ Οἱ πάλιν αὐτὴν οἰκίσαντες πολῖται  
> (The citizens who rebuilt it again)
>
> 149 „ Ἐπὶ τῶν τεφρῶν, ἃς κατέλιπ’ Ἀττίλας,  
> (Upon the ashes which Attila left behind,)
>
> 150 „ Μάτην ἂν ἀνέλαβον τοσούτους πόνους.  
> (Would have undertaken so great labours in vain.)

- *Οἱ πάλιν αὐτὴν οἰκίσαντες πολῖται* encloses the modifiers between article and noun: ‘the citizens who settled it anew’. *Οἰκίσαντες* (nom. pl. masc., aor. act. participle of *οἰκίζω*) converts *che … rifondarno* into an attributive participle; *αὐτήν* reproduces the city-object *la*. *Πάλιν* renders the repetition in *ri-* rather than the succession in *poi*; there is no separate demonstrative for *quei*.
- *Ἐπὶ τῶν τεφρῶν* retains the spatial construction with *ἐπί* + gen., but pluralizes *cener*. *Ἃς κατέλιπ’ Ἀττίλας* ‘which Attila left behind’ makes the ashes the direct object of a transitive verb, where *che d’ Attila rimase* makes the ash the subject of ‘remained’. *Ἅς* is acc. pl. fem.; *κατέλιπ’* is elided *κατέλιπε*, 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *καταλείπω*.
- *Μάτην ἂν ἀνέλαβον* places *indarno* first. *Ἄν* with *ἀνέλαβον* (3rd pl. second aor. act. ind. of *ἀναλαμβάνω*) marks the unreal past consequence, ‘would have undertaken’, corresponding to *avrebber fatto*; it is not an optative. *Τοσούτους πόνους* ‘such great labours’ changes the infinitive *lavorare* into an accusative noun phrase and adds an indication of magnitude.

> 151 Io fei giubbetto a me delle mie case.”  

I made a gibbet for myself of my own dwelling.”

> 151 „ Ἀγχόνην μοι τοὺς οἴκους ἐποιησάμην.”  
> (I made my own house a gibbet for myself.")

- *Ἀγχόνην* ‘a halter, a means of hanging’ renders *giubbetto* and stands first as predicate accusative. *Τοὺς οἴκους* retains the plural of *le mie case* but is a direct object, not a phrase with *di*: the construction is ‘I made my houses a halter’, rather than ‘I made a gibbet out of my houses’.
- *Ἐποιησάμην* (1st sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ποιέω*) marks the subject’s personal involvement where *fei* is active. Enclitic dat. *μοι* retains *a me*, ‘for myself’; possession of the houses is understood rather than repeated by a separate equivalent of *mie*.

## Summary — Echoes and Shifts of Syntax

Musurus often keeps Dante’s rhetorical pattern even when he changes the grammar that sustains it. The opening contrasts repeat *οὐ … ἀλλά* for *non … ma*, but Greek leaves the verb unexpressed even where Italian finally supplies *v’eran*. The three forms of *credere* recur as *νομίζοντα*, *ἐνόμιζε*, and *νομίζω*, in reverse order: the innermost thought comes first as a participle, and ‘I think’ closes the phrase as a parenthesis. In *ἐξῆψε … ἐξαφθέντες … ἐξῆψαν*, by contrast, both the order and the turn from active to passive and back to active follow *infiammò … infiammati infiammâr*.

The participle does much of the work of recasting Italian clauses. Article and participle replace a finite relative in *οἱ μισοῦντες*, ‘those who hate’; an aorist participle makes *porsi*, ‘I stretched out’, an action preliminary to the main verb, while the perfect participle *ἐπτοημένος*, ‘frightened’, gives a resulting state for *che teme*, ‘who fears’. A finite content clause can likewise become an accusative and infinitive, as in *εἶναί σ᾽*, ‘that you are’, for *che se’*. Musurus does not invariably prefer compression: *οὕτω … ὥστε* with a finite indicative closely follows Italian *sì … che* in marking an actual result. Elsewhere *καὶ δή*, ‘and so’, makes the result a fresh assertion, and *μέν … δέ* balances a future assurance against a request where Dante begins with a wish.

The least literal renderings can preserve a verbal echo while changing what the words say. *Ἀλγηδόνας … ἀλγεινά*, ‘pains … painful’, answers *dolore … al dolor*, but *ἕλκη*, ‘wounds’, replaces *finestra*, ‘window’: the wounds no longer expressly give pain an outlet. Similarly, *ἄδικον … δικαίῳ* keeps the opposition of *ingiusto … giusto*, yet it is an end that is unjust and its recipient who is just; the force of *me contra me* is lost. At the level of individual readings, *στενάζων*, ‘groaning’, takes *geme* as a sound rather than the dripping conveyed by Carlyle’s ‘drops’, while *παλμοὺς καὶ φλέβας*, ‘pulses and veins’, leaves *lo sonno*, ‘sleep’, altogether unrepresented.
