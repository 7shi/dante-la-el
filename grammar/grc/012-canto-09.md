# Inferno 9

## Lines 1–21 — Broken Speech and a Question About Descent

> 1 Quel color che viltà di fuor mi pinse,  
> 2 veggendo il duca mio tornare in volta,  
> 3 più tosto dentro il suo nuovo ristrinse.  

That colour which cowardice painted on my face, when I saw my Guide turn back, repressed in him more quickly his new colour.

> 1 Ἡ τὴν ὄψιν μου μεταβαλοῦσ’ ὠχρότης  
> (The paleness that changed my countenance)
>
> 2 Τὸν Προστάτην ἰδόντος παλινδρομοῦντα,  
> (When I saw my Protector turning back,)
>
> 3 Ἐβίασ’ αὐτὸν τὸ τάραγμ’ ὑποκρύψαι.  
> (Forced him to conceal his agitation.)

- *Ἡ τὴν ὄψιν μου μεταβαλοῦσ’ ὠχρότης* ‘the pallor that altered my countenance’ makes explicit what *quel color* was, but drops *viltà* as the agent that painted it. Between article and noun stands the whole attributive phrase: *μεταβαλοῦσ’* is *μεταβαλοῦσα*, nom. sg. fem. of the second aor. act. participle of *μεταβάλλω*, agreeing with *ὠχρότης* and governing *τὴν ὄψιν*. The possessive genitive *μου* belongs to *ὄψιν*; ‘my countenance’ takes the place of *mi … di fuor*.
- *Τὸν Προστάτην ἰδόντος παλινδρομοῦντα* preserves both the act of seeing and the action seen. *Ἰδόντος* (gen. sg. masc., second aor. act. participle of *ὁράω*) takes its understood subject from *μου*: ‘of me, when I saw’. Thus the participle can attach to a genitive already in the sentence rather than requiring a separate genitive absolute. *Προστάτην* ‘protector’ replaces *duca* without repeating *mio*; *παλινδρομοῦντα* (acc. sg. masc., pres. act. participle of *παλινδρομέω*) agrees with it. Greek uses a supplementary participle after a verb of perception where Italian has *tornare*.
- *Ἐβίασ’ αὐτὸν τὸ τάραγμ’ ὑποκρύψαι* ‘forced him to conceal his agitation’ abandons the second colour-image, *il suo nuovo*, and the comparison *più tosto*. *Ἐβίασ’* is elided *ἐβίασε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *βιάζω*, with the initial augment; its subject is still *ὠχρότης*. The person compelled, *αὐτόν*, is also the subject of *ὑποκρύψαι* (aor. act. inf. of *ὑποκρύπτω*); *τὸ τάραγμ’*, elided *τάραγμα*, is the infinitive’s neuter accusative object. Concealment replaces the Italian *dentro … ristrinse*.

> 4 Attento si fermò, com’ uom che ascolta;  

He stopped attentive, like one who listens:

> 4 Ἔμεν’ ἀτρεμής, ὡς ἄνθρωπος ἀκούων·  
> (He stood motionless, like a man listening;)

- *Ἔμεν’ ἀτρεμής* ‘he remained motionless’ replaces *attento si fermò*: the imperfect *ἔμενε* (3rd sg. act. ind. of *μένω*, final vowel elided) presents a continuing posture, not the act of stopping. *Ἀτρεμής* is a predicate adjective, nom. sg. masc.; it describes stillness rather than attention. *Ὡς ἄνθρωπος ἀκούων* follows *com’ uom che ascolta*, but replaces the relative clause with *ἀκούων* (nom. sg. masc., pres. act. participle of *ἀκούω*), ‘like a man listening’.

> 5 chè l’ occhio nol potea menare a lunga  
> 6 per l’ aer nero, e per la nebbia folta.  

for his eye could not lead him far, through the black air and the dense fog.

> 5 Οὐ γὰρ ἠδύνατ’ ἄγειν μακρὰν τὸ βλέμμα  
> (For he could not carry his gaze far)
>
> 6 Δι’ ἀέρος ζοφώδους πυκνῆς τ’ ἀχλύος.  
> (Through the murky air and the thick mist.)

- *Οὐ γὰρ ἠδύνατ’ ἄγειν μακρὰν τὸ βλέμμα* ‘for he could not direct his gaze far’ reverses the roles in *l’occhio nol potea menare*: the man, not his eye, is subject, and the gaze becomes the object of *ἄγειν* (pres. act. inf. of *ἄγω*). Postpositive *γάρ* supplies the causal link of *chè*. *Ἠδύνατ’* is elided *ἠδύνατο*, 3rd sg. imperf. mid. ind. of the deponent *δύναμαι*, with the Attic augment *ἠ-* found in this verb. *Μακράν* is an adverbial feminine accusative corresponding to *a lunga*.
- *Δι’ ἀέρος ζοφώδους πυκνῆς τ’ ἀχλύος* uses one *διά* with two genitives for the repeated *per*. *Ζοφώδους* (gen. sg. of *ζοφώδης*) makes *nero* ‘gloomy’; *πυκνῆς* precedes and agrees with the poetic *ἀχλύος* (gen. sg. of *ἀχλύς*) ‘mist’. The postpositive connective *τε* stands after the second phrase’s first word. Both *δι’* and *τ’* show final-vowel elision.

> 7 “Pure a noi converrà vincer la punga,”  
> 8 cominciò ei; “se non . . . tal ne s’ offerse.  

“Yet it behoves us to gain this battle,” he began; “if not . . . such help was offered to us.

> 7 “Ἀλλ’ ὅμως ἡμῖν νικᾷν δεῖ τὸν ἀγῶνα,”  
> ("Yet still we must win the struggle,")
>
> 8 Εἶπεν· “εἰ μὴ . . . τὸν Δεῖνα βοηθὸν ἔχω . . .  
> (He said; "if not . . . I have Such-a-one as helper . . .)

- *Ἀλλ’ ὅμως* ‘but nevertheless’ expands *pure*; *ἀλλ’* is elided *ἀλλά*. *Ἡμῖν νικᾷν δεῖ τὸν ἀγῶνα* closely follows *a noi converrà vincer la punga*: *ἡμῖν* is the dative of the persons concerned, echoing *a noi*, rather than the accusative subject often used with infinitival *δεῖ*. Impersonal *δεῖ* (pres. act. ind.) makes the necessity present rather than future; *νικᾷν* is the contracted pres. act. inf. of *νικάω*, with *τὸν ἀγῶνα* ‘the contest’ as object.
- *Εἶπεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*) is simply ‘he said’, not *cominciò ei*. *Εἰ μή* preserves *se non* as a conditional opening broken off before its verb. *Τὸν Δεῖνα βοηθὸν ἔχω* ‘I have so-and-so as helper’ interprets *tal* personally, as someone left unnamed: the idiom *ὁ δεῖνα*, here accusative, does not mean ‘such help’. *Βοηθόν* is a predicate accusative alongside *τὸν Δεῖνα*. The present *ἔχω* (1st sg. act. ind.) replaces *ne s’offerse*: the offer becomes possession, and the plural beneficiaries give way to a singular speaker. The Greek marks a further suspension after *ἔχω*.

> 9 Oh quanto tarda a me ch’ altri qui giunga! ”  

Oh! how long to me it seems till some one come!”

> 9 „Ὦ, πόσον ἐλθεῖν βοηθήσων βραδύνει!”  
> (Oh, how long he delays in coming to aid!")

- *Ὦ, πόσον ἐλθεῖν βοηθήσων βραδύνει* ‘oh, how long he delays to come to help’ replaces *tarda a me ch’altri qui giunga* with a personal verb. *Πόσον* is an adverbial neuter accusative of extent; *βραδύνει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *βραδύνω*) has the unnamed helper as understood subject. Neither *a me* nor *qui* has a separate equivalent, and *altri* is not expressed by a fresh indefinite.
- *Ἐλθεῖν* (second aor. act. inf. of *ἔρχομαι*) complements the verb of delay in place of *ch’ … giunga* with the subjunctive. The added *βοηθήσων* (nom. sg. masc., fut. act. participle of *βοηθέω*) agrees with the subject of *βραδύνει*: attached to the idea of coming, the future participle expresses purpose, ‘in order to help’.

> 10 Io vidi ben, sì com’ ei ricoperse  
> 11 lo cominciar con l’ altro, che poi venne,  
> 12 che fur parole alle prime diverse.  

I saw well how he covered the beginning with the other that came after, which were words differing from the first.

> 10 Ὅτι τὴν διάνοιαν ἤθελε κρύπτειν,  
> (That he wished to hide his thought,)
>
> 11 Σαφῶς διέγνων ἀπὸ λόγων ὑστέρων,  
> (I clearly perceived from his later words,)
>
> 12 Διαφερόντων τῶν πρώτων αὐτοῦ λόγων.  
> (Which differed from his first words.)

- *Ὅτι τὴν διάνοιαν ἤθελε κρύπτειν* fronts the content of the perception before the verb of perceiving: ‘that he wished to hide his thought, I clearly discerned’. *Ὅτι* introduces what was discerned, not how it was done. Where *ricoperse lo cominciar* describes a completed act upon the beginning of an utterance, *ἤθελε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἐθέλω*) with *κρύπτειν* (pres. act. inf. of *κρύπτω*) attributes an intention, and *τὴν διάνοιαν* makes its object the underlying thought.
- *Σαφῶς διέγνων* ‘I clearly discerned’ renders *io vidi ben* by a verb of recognition: *διέγνων* is 1st sg. root aor. act. ind. of *διαγιγνώσκω*, with augment after the preverb. *Ἀπὸ λόγων ὑστέρων* ‘from later words’ makes the words the evidence for the inference, rather than the means of covering the beginning (*con l’altro, che poi venne*). The relative clause of sequence becomes the adjective *ὑστέρων*.
- *Διαφερόντων* (gen. pl. masc., pres. act. participle of *διαφέρω*) agrees with *λόγων ὑστέρων*, so *che fur parole … diverse* needs no further finite verb. *Τῶν πρώτων αὐτοῦ λόγων* ‘from his first words’ is a second genitive group, this time governed by *διαφέρω* in the sense ‘differ from’; the noun implicit in *alle prime* is repeated, and *αὐτοῦ* explicitly marks the speaker as possessor.

> 13 Ma nondimen paura il suo dir dienne,  
> 14 perch’ io traeva la parola tronca  
> 15 forse a peggior sentenzia, ch’ ei non tenne.  

But not the less his language gave me fear: for perhaps I drew his broken speech to a worse meaning than he held.

> 13 Ἀλλ’ οὐχ ἧττον ὁ τρόπος οὗτος τοῦ λέγειν  
> (Yet none the less this manner of speaking)
>
> 14 Ἐφόβησέ με, τὰς κεκομμένας λέξεις  
> (Frightened me, as I interpreted the broken words)
>
> 15 Ἐννοοῦντ’ ἴσως χειρόνως ἢ προσῆκεν.  
> (Perhaps in a worse sense than was fitting.)

- *Ἀλλ’ οὐχ ἧττον* closely matches *ma nondimen*, ‘but no less’: *ἧττον* is the adverbial neuter comparative, and *οὐ* takes the form *οὐχ* before its rough breathing. *Ὁ τρόπος οὗτος τοῦ λέγειν* ‘this manner of speaking’ interprets *il suo dir* as the manner rather than the substance of the speech. The demonstrative stands outside the article–noun group; *τοῦ λέγειν* is a genitive articular pres. act. infinitive dependent on *τρόπος*.
- *Ἐφόβησέ με* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *φοβέω*) ‘frightened me’ turns the giving of fear into a transitive verb with the person affected as accusative object. Its extra final accent is occasioned by enclitic *με*. *Ἐννοοῦντ’* is elided *ἐννοοῦντα* (acc. sg. masc., contracted pres. act. participle of *ἐννοέω*), agreeing with *με*: ‘as I understood’. The participle conveys the explanatory force of *perch’ io traeva* without a causal conjunction.
- *Τὰς κεκομμένας λέξεις*, object of *ἐννοοῦντ’*, pluralizes *la parola tronca*. *Κεκομμένας* (acc. pl. fem., reduplicated perf. mid./pass. participle of *κόπτω*, passive in sense) describes the words as left cut off. *Ἴσως* preserves *forse*, but *χειρόνως ἢ προσῆκεν* ‘worse than was fitting’ replaces *a peggior sentenzia, ch’ ei non tenne*: the comparative adverb modifies the understanding itself, and impersonal *προσῆκεν* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *προσήκω*) substitutes a standard of fitness for the speaker’s intended meaning. Greek needs no negative after comparative *ἤ* ‘than’.

> 16 “In questo fondo della trista conca  
> 17 discende mai alcun del primo grado,  
> 18 che sol per pena ha la speranza cionca? ”  

“Into this bottom of the dreary shell, does any ever descend from the first degree, whose only punishment is hope cut off?”

> 16 Ἠρώτησ’ αὐτόν· “Ἐν τῷ βαράθρῳ τούτῳ  
> (I asked him: "Into this abyss)
>
> 17 „Καταβαίνει τις ἐκ τῶν τοῦ πρώτου κύκλου,  
> (Does anyone descend from among those of the first circle,)
>
> 18 „Ἔνθ’ ἐστὶ ποινὴ μόνη παῦσις ἐλπίδος;”  
> (Where the sole punishment is the cessation of hope?")

- *Ἠρώτησ’ αὐτόν* ‘I asked him’ moves the reporting clause of Italian line 19 ahead of the question. *Ἠρώτησ’* is elided *ἠρώτησα*, 1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐρωτάω*, with temporal augment. *Ἐν τῷ βαράθρῳ τούτῳ* ‘in this abyss’ compresses *in questo fondo della trista conca*: one noun replaces both bottom and containing hollow, and there is no adjective for *trista*. The demonstrative follows the articular noun. *Ἐν* with the dative specifies the place of descent rather than marking a goal with *εἰς* and the accusative.
- *Καταβαίνει τις* ‘does anyone descend?’ has the indefinite enclitic *τις*, not the interrogative *τίς* ‘who?’, with the generic present *καταβαίνει* (3rd sg. act. ind. of *καταβαίνω*); *mai* has no separate equivalent. *Ἐκ τῶν τοῦ πρώτου κύκλου* is partitive, ‘from among those of the first circle’: *τῶν* stands substantivally with the defining genitive *τοῦ πρώτου κύκλου*. *Κύκλος* interprets *grado* rather than reproducing its image of a step or degree.
- *Ἔνθ’* is *ἔνθα* ‘where’, with elision and aspiration of the final stop before the rough breathing of *ἐστὶ*’s following predicate **not** being required: *ἔνθα* already contains *θ*. The local relative turns *che sol per pena ha* into a description of the place. *Ποινὴ μόνη παῦσις ἐλπίδος* has two predicate-linked nominative groups with *ἐστί*: ‘the sole punishment is cessation of hope’. *Μόνη* agrees with *ποινή*, while *παῦσις* turns the image *la speranza cionca* into an action noun with a dependent genitive, ‘the ceasing of hope’.

> 19 Questa question fec’ io ; e quei : “Di rado  
> 20 incontra,” mi rispose, “che di nui  
> 21 faccia il cammino alcun per quale io vado.  

This question I made, and he replied to me: “Rarely it occurs that any of us makes this journey on which I go.

> 19 Ὁ δ’ ἀπεκρίθη· “Σπανιάκις συμβαίνει  
> (And he answered: "Rarely does it happen)
>
> 20 „Ἐξ ἡμῶν τινα κατερχόμενον ὧδε  
> (That any one of us, descending hither,)
>
> 21 „Φοιτᾷν εἰς τόπους, οὓς ἐγὼ διοδεύω.  
> (Visits the places through which I am passing.)

- With *questa question fec’ io* already rendered in line 16, *ὁ δ’ ἀπεκρίθη* moves directly to the answer. *Ὁ* has pronominal force in the turn-taking construction with postpositive *δέ* (here elided), ‘and he in turn’; *ἀπεκρίθη* is 3rd sg. aor. passive in form, active in sense, of *ἀποκρίνομαι*. No dative repeats the *mi* of *mi rispose*.
- *Σπανιάκις συμβαίνει* ‘rarely it happens’ brings *di rado* and *incontra* together. Impersonal *συμβαίνει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *συμβαίνω*) takes an accusative-and-infinitive clause instead of *che … alcun … faccia*. *Ἐξ ἡμῶν τινα* closely follows *di nui … alcun*: *ἐξ* is the prevocalic form of *ἐκ*, and *τινα* (acc. sg. masc. of indefinite *τις*) is the subject of the delayed infinitive *φοιτᾷν*.
- *Κατερχόμενον ὧδε* ‘descending hither’ adds a circumstantial pres. mid. participle (acc. sg. masc. of *κατέρχομαι*, agreeing with *τινα*) and a directional adverb. *Φοιτᾷν εἰς τόπους* ‘to visit places’ replaces *faccia il cammino*: the contracted pres. act. inf. of *φοιτάω* can suggest going repeatedly, and *εἰς* with the accusative gives destinations rather than a single route. *Οὓς ἐγὼ διοδεύω* ‘which I traverse’ accordingly has a direct-object relative (acc. pl. masc., referring to *τόπους*) instead of *per quale*. The explicit *ἐγώ* preserves *io*; *διοδεύω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.) incorporates the sense of going through.

## Lines 22–54 — Virgil’s Reassurance and the Furies’ Threat

> 22 Vero è che altra fiata quaggiù fui  
> 23 congiurato da quella Eriton cruda,  
> 24 che richiamava l'ombre a' corpi sui.  

It is true, that once before I was down here, conjured by that fell Erichtho, who recalled the shadows to their bodies.

> 22 „Ἀληθὲς ὅτι κατέβην ἅπαξ ὧδε,  
> (True it is that once I came down hither,)
>
> 23 „Παρακινηθεὶς ὑπ’ Ἐριχθοῦς σχετλίας,  
> (Urged on by ruthless Erichtho,)
>
> 24 „Τὰς σκιὰς ἐν σώμασιν ἐπαγαγούσης.  
> (Who brought shades back into bodies.)

- *Ἀληθὲς ὅτι* renders *Vero è che* almost word for word, except that Greek leaves *ἐστί* understood with the neuter singular predicate *ἀληθές*. *Κατέβην* (1st sg. root aor. act. ind. of *καταβαίνω*) makes *quaggiù fui* an act of descent rather than a statement of whereabouts. The direction is divided between *κατέβην* ‘I came down’ and *ὧδε* ‘hither’; *ἅπαξ* ‘once’ renders *altra fiata* without a separate word for *altra*.
- *Παρακινηθείς* (nom. sg. masc., aor. pass. participle of *παρακινέω*) agrees with the speaker: ‘having been impelled’, less specifically than *congiurato* “conjured.” The agent is *ὑπ’ Ἐριχθοῦς*, with elided *ὑπό* and the contracted genitive of *Ἐριχθώ*. Postposed *σχετλίας* (gen. sg. fem.) renders *cruda*; *quella* has no counterpart.
- *Τὰς σκιὰς … ἐπαγαγούσης* replaces the finite relative *che richiamava l’ombre* with a participle agreeing with *Ἐριχθοῦς*: *ἐπαγαγούσης* is gen. sg. fem. of the second aor. act. participle of *ἐπάγω*. The aorist presents the bringing back as a whole rather than as the characteristic activity expressed by the Italian imperfect. *Ἐν σώμασιν* (dat. pl. of *σῶμα*) describes the resulting location, where *a’ corpi sui* expresses the goal and identifies the bodies as the shades’ own.

> 25 Di poco era di me la carne nuda  
> 26 ch'ella mi fece entrar dentro a quel muro,  
> 27 per trarne un spirto del cerchio di Giuda.  

My flesh had been but short time divested of me, when she made me enter within that wall, to draw out a spirit from the Circle of Judas.

> 25 „Ἡ σὰρξ ἐξ ἐμοῦ μόλις ἀπεγυμνώθη,  
> (Scarcely had the flesh been stripped from me,)
>
> 26 „Ὅτ’ ἔσω μ’ εἰσήγαγε τοῦ τείχους τούτου,  
> (When she led me within this wall,)
>
> 27 „Ἵν’ ἐξ Ἰούδα κύκλου σκιὰν ἐκβάλω.  
> (To draw forth a shade from the circle of Judas.)

- *Ἡ σὰρξ ἐξ ἐμοῦ μόλις ἀπεγυμνώθη* ‘the flesh had scarcely been stripped from me’ retains *la carne* as subject, but turns predicative *nuda* with *era* into an event: *ἀπεγυμνώθη* is 3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ἀπογυμνόω*, with the augment after the preverb. *Ἐξ ἐμοῦ* marks separation, with the full form of the personal pronoun after the preposition; *μόλις … ὅτ’* ‘scarcely … when’ renders the short interval of *di poco … ch’*.
- *Ὅτ’ ἔσω μ’ εἰσήγαγε τοῦ τείχους τούτου* has two elisions, *ὅτε* and accusative *με*. *Εἰσήγαγε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *εἰσάγω*) ‘led me in’ replaces the causative *mi fece entrar*. *Ἔσω* governs the separated genitive *τοῦ τείχους τούτου*; the demonstrative in the ordinary position outside the article–noun group is proximal ‘this’, not distal *quel*.
- *Ἵν’ … ἐκβάλω* replaces *per trarne* with a purpose clause, elided *ἵνα* and the 1st sg. second aor. act. subj. of *ἐκβάλλω*. The subjunctive is retained after a past main verb rather than shifted to an optative. *Ἐξ Ἰούδα κύκλου* explicitly marks the source conveyed by *-ne* and *del cerchio*; *Ἰούδα* is the dependent genitive before *κύκλου*. *Σκιάν* ‘a shade’ repeats the noun of line 24 for *un spirto*.

> 28 Quell'è il più basso loco, e il più oscuro,  
> 29 e il più lontan dal ciel che tutto gira;  

That is the lowest place, and the most dark, and farthest from the Heaven, which encircles all;

> 28 „Ὁ βαθύτατος, ζοφωδέστατος τόπος  
> (That is the deepest, darkest place,)
>
> 29 „Ἀπώτατός τε τοὐρανοῦ ’στιν ἐκεῖνος.  
> (And farthest removed from heaven.)

- Greek holds the demonstrative *ἐκεῖνος* and copula *’στιν* (prodelision of the initial vowel of *ἐστίν*) until the end, reversing *Quell’è*. *Ὁ βαθύτατος, ζοφωδέστατος τόπος* groups two superlatives with one masculine singular noun: ‘the deepest, darkest place’. *Βαθύτατος* interprets *più basso* as depth; *ζοφωδέστατος* is from *ζοφώδης* ‘gloomy’.
- Postpositive *τε* links the further predicate *ἀπώτατος* ‘most remote’; *τοὐρανοῦ* is crasis for *τοῦ οὐρανοῦ*, a genitive of distance from something. Nothing renders the relative clause *che tutto gira*.

> 30 ben so il cammin : però ti fa securo.  

well do I know the way : so reassure thyself.

> 30 „Οἶδα καλῶς τὴν ὁδόν· ἥσυχος ἔσο.  
> (I know the way well; be at ease.)

- *Οἶδα* is a perfect active indicative, 1st sg., with present meaning; *καλῶς* follows it rather than preceding it like *ben*. *Τὴν ὁδόν* is its accusative object. The juxtaposition after the stop does the work of inferential *però*: *ἥσυχος ἔσο* ‘be at ease’ replaces the reflexive *ti fa securo* with a predicate adjective and *ἔσο*, 2nd sg. present imperative of *εἰμί* (beside *ἴσθι*).

> 31 Questa palude, che il gran puzzo spira,  
> 32 cinge d'intorno la città dolente,  
> 33 u' non potemo entrare omai senz'ira.”  

This marsh, which breathes the mighty stench, all round begirds the doleful city, where we cannot now enter without anger.”

> 31 „Τὸ δ’ ἀτμίζον δυσώδη τοῦτ’ ὀσμὴν ἕλος  
> (And this marsh breathing forth a foul stench)
>
> 32 „Πάντοθεν περικυκλοῖ τὴν οἰκτρὰν πόλιν,  
> (Encircles on all sides the piteous city,)
>
> 33 „Ἔνθ’ οὐκ ἔσθ’ ἡμᾶς εἰσελθεῖν ἄνευ πάλης.”  
> (Where we cannot enter without a struggle.")

- *Τὸ δ’ ἀτμίζον δυσώδη τοῦτ’ ὀσμὴν ἕλος* interlaces two groups: *τὸ … ἀτμίζον … ἕλος* ‘the marsh emitting’ and its participle’s object *δυσώδη … ὀσμήν* ‘a foul smell’. *Τοῦτ’* (elided *τοῦτο*, nom. sg. neut.) points to *ἕλος*, not to feminine *ὀσμήν*. The attributive present active participle of *ἀτμίζω* replaces *che … spira*; *δυσώδη* expresses the quality of *puzzo*, not separately the degree *gran*. Elided *δέ* supplies a transition.
- *Πάντοθεν περικυκλοῖ* ‘on all sides encircles’ retains the doubling in *cinge d’intorno*. *Περικυκλοῖ* is the contracted 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *περικυκλόω*; *τὴν οἰκτρὰν πόλιν* is ‘the pitiable city’, shifting *dolente* from suffering grief to evoking pity.
- *Ἔνθ’ οὐκ ἔσθ’ ἡμᾶς εἰσελθεῖν* renders *u’ non potemo entrare* by ‘where it is not possible for us to enter’. *Ἔνθ’* is elided *ἔνθα*; *ἔσθ’* is *ἔστι* elided and aspirated before rough-breathed *ἡμᾶς*. The accusative is the subject of *εἰσελθεῖν*, second aor. act. infinitive of *εἰσέρχομαι*. *Ἄνευ πάλης* (genitive) interprets *senz’ira* as ‘without a struggle’; *omai* is not expressed.

> 34 Ed altro disse, ma non l'ho a mente:  

And more he said, but I have it not in memory

> 34 Κἆλλά τιν’ εἶπεν, ὧνπερ οὐκ ἔχω μνήμην·  
> (And other things he said, of which I have no memory;)

- *Κἆλλά τιν’ εἶπεν* has crasis of *καὶ ἄλλα* and elision of enclitic *τινά*: ‘and certain other things he said’, with a neuter plural object for singular *altro*. *Εἶπεν* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*. Instead of the adversative *ma non l’ho*, Greek subordinates the qualification: *ὧνπερ οὐκ ἔχω μνήμην* ‘of which I have no recollection’. *Ὧνπερ* is the gen. pl. relative, reinforced by *-περ*, dependent on *μνήμην*.

> 35 perocchè l'occhio m'avea tutto tratto  
> 36 ver l'alta torre alla cima rovente,  

: for my eye had drawn me wholly to the high tower with glowing summit,

> 35 Ὁ γὰρ ὀφθαλμὸς ὅλον ἐφείλκυσέ με  
> (For my eye had drawn me wholly)
>
> 36 Πρὸς τὸν ὑψηλὸν πύργον, φλογεράν τ᾽ ἄκραν,  
> (Toward the lofty tower, and its fiery summit,)

- Postpositive *γάρ* gives the reason expressed by *perocchè*. *Ὁ … ὀφθαλμός* retains the singular subject *l’occhio*, without a possessive; *ὅλον* is accusative masculine singular agreeing predicatively with *με*, ‘the whole of me’, rather than an adverb. *Ἐφείλκυσε* (here *ἐφείλκυσέ με*, with the accent before an enclitic) is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐφέλκω*, not a pluperfect like *m’avea … tratto*.
- *Πρὸς τὸν ὑψηλὸν πύργον, φλογεράν τ’ ἄκραν* puts both accusatives under directional *πρός*. Elided *τε* coordinates ‘and [its] fiery summit’ with ‘the lofty tower’, rather than subordinating *alla cima rovente* as a description of the tower. *Φλογεράν* agrees with feminine *ἄκραν*, not with *πύργον*.

> 37 ove in un punto furon dritte ratto  
> 38 tre furie infernal di sangue tinte,  
> 39 che membra femminili aveano, ed atto;  

where all at once [had risen up] three Hellish Furies, stained with blood ; who had the limbs and attitude of women,

> 37 Ἔνθ᾽ αἴφνης εἶδον ὀρθὰς παρισταμένας  
> (Where suddenly I saw standing upright)
>
> 38 Τὰς αἱμοφύρτους ᾍδου τρεῖς Ἐριννύας,  
> (The three blood-stained Furies of Hell,)
>
> 39 Ἐχούσας γυναικείαν μορφὴν καὶ τρόπους.  
> (Having womanly form and bearing.)

- *Ἔνθ’ αἴφνης εἶδον* ‘where suddenly I saw’ gathers *in un punto … ratto* into one adverb and supplies a perceiving subject. *Εἶδον* is here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*. In place of *furon dritte* with the Furies as subject, its object is *τὰς … τρεῖς Ἐριννύας*, with predicate adjective *ὀρθάς* and supplementary participle *παρισταμένας* (acc. pl. fem., pres. mid. of *παρίστημι*): ‘I saw them standing upright’.
- *Αἱμοφύρτους* ‘blood-smeared’ renders *di sangue tinte* by a compound adjective (acc. pl. fem., with a common masculine/feminine ending). Genitive *ᾍδου* ‘of Hades’ replaces adjectival *infernal*. *Ἐχούσας* (acc. pl. fem., pres. act. participle of *ἔχω*) continues the description of the object without the finite relative *che … aveano*. *Γυναικείαν μορφήν* substitutes ‘womanly form’ for *membra femminili*; plural *τρόπους* ‘ways, bearing’ renders singular *atto*.

> 40 e con idre verdissime eran cinte;  

and were girt with greenest hydras

> 40 Ἦσαν ἐξ ὑδρῶν πρασίνων ἐζωσμέναι·  
> (They were girt with green water-snakes;)

- *Ἦσαν … ἐζωσμέναι* combines the imperfect of *εἰμί* with the nom. pl. fem. perfect middle/passive participle of *ζώννυμι*: a periphrastic pluperfect passive describing the resulting state, close to *eran cinte*. *Ἐξ ὑδρῶν πρασίνων* presents the snakes as the material of the girdle, rather than using an instrumental dative for *con*. Positive *πρασίνων* ‘green’ drops the intensification in *verdissime*.

> 41 serpentelli ceraste avean per crine,  
> 42 onde le fiere tempie eran avvinte.  

; for hair, they had little serpents and cerastes, wherewith their horrid temples were bound.

> 41 Ὄφεις κεράστας ἀντὶ πλοκάμων εἶχον,  
> (Horned serpents they had instead of tresses,)
>
> 42 Διαδέοντας τοὺς ἀγρίους κροτάφους.  
> (Binding round their fierce temples.)

- *Ὄφεις κεράστας* is one accusative group, ‘horned serpents’, not the two coordinated kinds in the English prose. *Κεράστας* (acc. pl. of *κεράστης*) specifies *ὄφεις*; the diminutive of *serpentelli* is lost. *Ἀντὶ πλοκάμων* ‘instead of locks’ takes the genitive for *per crine*. *Εἶχον* is here 3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *ἔχω*, matching *avean*.
- *Διαδέοντας* (acc. pl. masc., pres. act. participle of *διαδέω*) agrees with the snakes, which actively bind *τοὺς ἀγρίους κροτάφους*. Thus in place of instrumental relative *onde* and the passive *le fiere tempie eran avvinte*, the temples become the direct object; *ἀγρίους* preserves their epithet ‘fierce’.

> 43 E quei, che ben conobbe le meschine  
> 44 della regina dell'eterno pianto,  
> 45 “Guarda,” mi disse, “le feroci Erine.  

And he, knowing well the handmaids of the Queen of everlasting lamentation, said to me : “Mark the fierce Erynnis

> 43 Αὐτὸς δ᾽, ὃς καλῶς ἔγνω τὰς θεραπαίνας  
> (And he, who well knew the handmaids)
>
> 44 Τῆς βασιλίσσης τῶν αἰωνίων θρήνων,  
> (Of the queen of the eternal lamentations,)
>
> 45 “Ἰδέ,” λέγει μοι, “τὰς ὠμὰς Ἐριννύας·  
> ("Behold," he says to me, "the cruel Furies;)

- *Αὐτὸς δ’, ὃς καλῶς ἔγνω* follows *E quei, che ben conobbe* more closely than the English participial phrase: an emphatic pronoun ‘he’, connective *δέ*, then a finite relative. *Ἔγνω* is 3rd sg. root aor. act. ind. of *γιγνώσκω*, ‘recognized’. *Τὰς θεραπαίνας* interprets *le meschine* specifically as female attendants.
- *Τῆς βασιλίσσης τῶν αἰωνίων θρήνων* retains the chain of dependent genitives in *della regina dell’eterno pianto*, but makes the lamentations plural. *Ἰδέ* is 2nd sg. second aor. act. imperative of *ὁράω* ‘look at’, governing *τὰς ὠμὰς Ἐριννύας*. The parenthetical *λέγει μοι* uses a historical present, not the past of *mi disse*; *μοι* is the enclitic dative of the addressee.

> 46 Questa è Megera dal sinistro canto;  

! This is Megæra on the left hand

> 46 „ Μέγαιρ᾽ ἐστίν, ἣν ὁρᾷς ἐξ εὐωνύμων,  
> (That is Megæra, whom you see on the left,)

- *Μέγαιρ’ ἐστίν* (elided *Μέγαιρα*) puts the name first and dispenses with *Questa*. Greek expands the locative *dal sinistro canto* to *ἣν ὁρᾷς ἐξ εὐωνύμων* ‘whom you see on the left’: *ἥν* is the feminine singular accusative object of contracted *ὁρᾷς* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ὁράω*). *Ἐξ εὐωνύμων* is an idiomatic locative use of *ἐκ* with the substantivized plural of *εὐώνυμος* ‘left’.

> 47 quella, che piange dal destro, è Aletto;  

; she, that weeps upon the right, is Alecto

> 47 „ Ἀληκτὼ δ᾽ ἡ κλαίουσα πρὸς δεξιάν σου,  
> (And Alecto is she who weeps on your right,)

- *Ἀληκτὼ δ’ ἡ κλαίουσα* brings the predicate name forward and leaves the copula understood. The article and nom. sg. fem. present active participle of *κλαίω* give ‘the one weeping’ for *quella, che piange*. *Πρὸς δεξιάν σου* ‘on your right’ has *πρός* with a feminine accusative (understand ‘hand’ or ‘side’); enclitic possessive *σου* supplies a standpoint not specified in *dal destro*.

> 48 Tesifone è nel mezzo”; e tacque a tanto.  

; Tesiphone is in the middle” ; and therewith he was silent.

> 48 „ Τισιφόνη δ᾽ ἐν μέσῳ.” Ταῦτ᾽ εἰπὼν ἔστη.  
> (And Tisiphone is in the midst." Saying this, he stopped.)

- *Τισιφόνη δ’ ἐν μέσῳ* again links with elided *δέ* and omits the copula of *è nel mezzo*; *ἐν* takes the dative. *Ταῦτ’ εἰπὼν ἔστη* ‘having said this, he stopped’ recasts *e tacque a tanto*: *ταῦτ’* is elided neuter accusative plural *ταῦτα*, object of *εἰπών* (nom. sg. masc., second aor. act. participle of *λέγω*). Intransitive *ἔστη* (3rd sg. root aor. act. ind. of *ἵστημι*) expresses stopping, with the preceding participle specifying what ceased.

> 49 Coll'unghie si fendea ciascuna il petto;  

With her claws each was rending her breast;

> 49 Ὄνυξι διέσχιζον αὐτῶν τὰ στήθη·  
> (With their nails they tore open their breasts;)

- *Ὄνυξι* (dat. pl. of *ὄνυξ*) is instrumental, ‘with nails’, for *coll’unghie*. *Διέσχιζον* (3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *διασχίζω*, augment after the preverb) makes the action durative and plural. Instead of distributive *ciascuna*, reflexive *si*, and singular *il petto*, Greek has one plural subject understood and a plural object, *αὐτῶν τὰ στήθη* ‘their breasts’. The possessive is written as the ordinary genitive *αὐτῶν*, not the explicitly reflexive *ἑαυτῶν*.

> 50 batteansi a palme, e gridavan sì alto,  
> 51 ch'io mi strinsi al poeta per sospetto.  

they were smiting themselves with their palms, and crying so loudly, that I pressed close to the Poet for fear.

> 50 Ταῖς χερσὶν ἐτύπτοντο κράζουσαι σφόδρα,  
> (With their hands they beat themselves, shrieking aloud,)
>
> 51 Ὥστ᾽ ἠγκασάμην τὸν ποιητὴν ἐκ φόβου.  
> (So that I clung to the poet out of fear.)

- *Ταῖς χερσὶν ἐτύπτοντο* gives the instrument first (dat. pl. of *χείρ* ‘hand’, less specific than *palme*). *Ἐτύπτοντο* is 3rd pl. imperf. mid./pass. ind. of *τύπτω*, middle here in the reflexive sense of *batteansi*. *Κράζουσαι* (nom. pl. fem., pres. act. participle of *κράζω*) makes coordinate *e gridavan* an accompanying action, ‘while shrieking’.
- *Σφόδρα … ὥστ’* renders *sì alto, ch’* by intensity and result, ‘vehemently … so that’. Elided *ὥστε* with the indicative marks an actual consequence. *Ἠγκασάμην* is 1st sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἀγκάζομαι* ‘clasp in one’s arms’, with temporal augment; it takes *τὸν ποιητήν* as direct object where *mi strinsi al poeta* has a reflexive verb and a directional complement. *Ἐκ φόβου* specifies fear as the cause for *per sospetto*.

> 52 “Venga Medusa, sì il farem di smalto,”  
> 53 dicevan tutte riguardando in giuso;  

“Let Medusa come, that we may change him into stone,” they all [said], looking downwards;

> 52 “Μέδουσ᾽, εἰς λίθον μεταβάλωμεν τοῦτον,”  
> ("Medusa, let us turn him into stone,")
>
> 53 Ἐκραύγαζον, πρὸς ἡμᾶς βλέπουσαι κάτω.  
> (They shouted, looking down toward us.)

- *Μέδουσ’* is elided vocative *Μέδουσα*. Instead of the third-person summons *Venga Medusa*, Greek addresses her directly, without a verb of coming. *Μεταβάλωμεν* (1st pl. second aor. act. subj. of *μεταβάλλω*) is an independent exhortation ‘let us turn’, not the future assertion *sì il farem*. *Τοῦτον* ‘this one’ makes *il* demonstrative; fronted *εἰς λίθον* gives the resulting substance for *di smalto*.
- *Ἐκραύγαζον* (3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *κραυγάζω*) intensifies *dicevan* to ‘were shouting’; its plural does not separately express *tutte*. *Βλέπουσαι* (nom. pl. fem., pres. act. participle of *βλέπω*) matches the simultaneous gerund *riguardando*. *Κάτω* renders *in giuso*, but directional *πρὸς ἡμᾶς* adds ‘toward us’.

> 54 “mal noi vengiammo in Teseo l'assalto.”  

“badly did we avenge the assault of Theseus.”

> 54 “Τιμωρεῖν ἔδει τὴν προσβολὴν Θησέως.” —  
> ("We ought to have punished the assault of Theseus." —)

- *Τιμωρεῖν ἔδει* ‘one ought to have punished’ interprets rather than reproduces *mal noi vengiammo*. The Italian evaluates a past act with *mal*; Greek uses impersonal *ἔδει* (3rd sg. imperf. ind. of *δεῖ*) for a past obligation understood as unfulfilled. The agent *noi* is left implicit. Fronted *τιμωρεῖν* is a contracted pres. act. infinitive of *τιμωρέω*; the past force comes from *ἔδει*, not the infinitive.
- *Τὴν προσβολὴν Θησέως* is the accusative object, ‘Theseus’ assault’, with a subjective genitive (gen. sg. of *Θησεύς*). Where *in Teseo* names the person on whom vengeance was to be taken, Greek makes him the author of the assault. The active *τιμωρεῖν* with the offense as object is less idiomatic for taking vengeance than classical middle *τιμωρεῖσθαι* with the offender as object.

## Lines 55–87 — Averted Eyes and the Heavenly Messenger

> 55 “Volgiti indietro, e tien lo viso chiuso:  

"Turn thee backwards, and keep thy eyes closed:

> 55 — “Στρέψον ὀπίσω καὶ κάλυψαι τὴν ὄψιν·  
> (— "Turn back and cover your face;)

- *Στρέψον ὀπίσω* ‘turn back’ renders *Volgiti indietro*: *στρέψον* is the 2nd sg. aor. active imperative of *στρέφω*, used without an expressed object where Italian has the reflexive *-ti*. *ὀπίσω* is the adverb of direction.
- *καὶ κάλυψαι τὴν ὄψιν* ‘and cover your face’ replaces *e tien lo viso chiuso*, an injunction to maintain a state, with an injunction to perform an act. *κάλυψαι* is the 2nd sg. aor. middle imperative of *καλύπτω*: the middle marks the subject’s interest in the act, and the article with *ὄψιν* (acc. sg. of *ὄψις*, ‘sight’, ‘face’) suffices without a possessive.

> 56 chè, se il Gorgon si mostra, e tu il vedessi,  
> 57 nulla sarebbe del tornar mai suso.”  

for if the Gorgon shew herself, and thou shouldst see her, there would be no returning up again."

> 56 „Ἢν γὰρ ἡ Γοργὼ φανῇ, σὺ δ᾽ αὐτὴν ἴδῃς,  
> (For if the Gorgon appear, and you see her,)
>
> 57 „Οὐκ ἔστιν ἐλπίς σ᾽ ἐπανελθεῖν ποτ᾽ ἄνω.” —  
> (There is no hope of your ever returning above." —)

- *Ἢν γάρ* renders *chè, se*: *ἤν* is a contracted form of conditional *ἐάν* (from *εἰ ἄν*), not the imperfect of *εἰμί*; explanatory *γάρ* comes second. The condition governs two 3rd- and 2nd-person aorist subjunctives respectively: *φανῇ* (passive in form, from *φαίνω*, ‘appear’) for *si mostra*, and *ἴδῃς* (second aorist active of *ὁράω*, ‘see’) for *vedessi*. Greek thus gives both acts the same prospective construction, despite the Italian change of mood and tense.
- *ἡ Γοργώ* is a feminine nominative singular where Italian has *il Gorgon*. In *σὺ δ᾽ αὐτὴν ἴδῃς*, *δέ*, elided before *αὐτήν*, marks the change of subject; the explicit *σύ* and feminine accusative *αὐτήν* reproduce the opposition of *tu* and *il*.
- *Οὐκ ἔστιν ἐλπίς* ‘there is no hope’ supplies a noun for *nulla* and uses a present indicative, not a potential construction corresponding to *sarebbe*. *σ᾽ ἐπανελθεῖν* is *σε* elided before the second aor. active infinitive of *ἐπανέρχομαι*: the accusative is the subject of the infinitive specifying the hope. *ποτ᾽ ἄνω* ‘ever upward’ renders *mai suso*, with elision of enclitic *ποτε*.

> 58 Così disse il maestro; ed egli stessi  
> 59 mi volse, e non si tenne alle mie mani,  
> 60 che con le sue ancor non mi chiudessi.  

Thus said the Master, and he himself turned me, and trusted not to my hands, but closed me also with his own.

> 58 Ταῦτ᾽ εἶπεν ὁ Προστάτης· οὐ μόνον δ᾽ οὗτος  
> (Thus spoke my Protector; and not only did he)
>
> 59 Ἔστρεψ᾽ ἔμ᾽, ἀλλὰ παρὰ τὰς ἐμὰς χεῖρας  
> (Turn me round, but besides my own hands)
>
> 60 Καὶ ταῖς ἑαυτοῦ ᾽κάλυψέ μου τὰς ὄψεις.  
> (With his own too he covered my eyes.)

- *Ταῦτ᾽ εἶπεν ὁ Προστάτης* ‘these things the Protector said’: *ταῦτα* (neut. acc. pl. of *οὗτος*, elided) gives *εἶπεν* (3rd sg. second aor. active indicative of *λέγω*) an object in place of adverbial *Così*. *Προστάτης* ‘protector’ renders *maestro* by function rather than title.
- *οὐ μόνον δ᾽ οὗτος ἔστρεψ᾽ ἔμ᾽, ἀλλὰ … καί* ‘and he not only turned me, but also …’ reorganizes *ed egli stessi mi volse, e non si tenne*. *οὗτος* resumes the subject demonstratively rather than using intensive *αὐτός* for *stessi*. *ἔστρεψ᾽* is *ἔστρεψε*, 3rd sg. augmented aor. active indicative of *στρέφω*; its object *ἔμ᾽* is elided *ἐμέ*.
- *παρὰ τὰς ἐμὰς χεῖρας* ‘over and above my hands’ replaces the negative statement that his action did not stop at Dante’s hands. The possessive adjective *ἐμάς* agrees with the acc. pl. of *χείρ* after *παρά*. By contrast, *ταῖς ἑαυτοῦ* ‘with his own’ is instrumental dative, with *χερσί* understood and the reflexive genitive referring to the subject.
- *᾽κάλυψέ μου τὰς ὄψεις* makes a second main verb of *che … non mi chiudessi*, without carrying over its negative. *᾽κάλυψε* is the 3rd sg. aor. active indicative of *καλύπτω*, with the syllabic augment of *ἐκάλυψε* lost by prodelision after *ἑαυτοῦ*. The extra acute before enclitic *μου* belongs to the accentuation of the group. *τὰς ὄψεις* supplies a plural object, ‘my eyes’, for what was singular *τὴν ὄψιν* in 55.

> 61 O voi, che avete gl’ intelletti sani,  
> 62 mirate la dottrina, che s’ asconde  
> 63 sotto il velame degli versi strani!  

O ye, who have sane intellects, mark the doctrine, which conceals itself beneath the veil of the strange verses!

> 61 Ὑμεῖς δ᾽, οἵπερ ἔχετε σώας τὰς φρένας,  
> (And you, who have sound understandings,)
>
> 62 Ἐννοεῖτε, τίς ὑποκρύπτετ᾽ ἰδέα  
> (Consider what meaning lies hidden)
>
> 63 Ὑπὸ τὸν πέπλον τῶν παραδόξων στίχων.  
> (Beneath the veil of the wondrous verses.)

- *Ὑμεῖς δ᾽, οἵπερ ἔχετε σώας τὰς φρένας* ‘you, however, who indeed have your wits sound’: *ὑμεῖς* directly addresses the readers without a separate equivalent of *O*; elided *δέ* marks the transition. *οἵπερ* is the masc. nom. pl. relative strengthened by *-περ*; its verb *ἔχετε* is 2nd pl. pres. active indicative by agreement with the persons addressed. *σώας* (fem. acc. pl. of *σῶος*) stands predicatively outside the article and noun: *gl’ intelletti sani* becomes ‘the wits in sound condition’.
- *Ἐννοεῖτε, τίς ὑποκρύπτετ᾽ ἰδέα* ‘consider what idea lies concealed’ turns *mirate la dottrina, che s’ asconde* from an imperative with a definite object and relative clause into an indirect question. *ἐννοεῖτε* is the contracted 2nd pl. pres. active imperative of *ἐννοέω*. Interrogative *τίς* agrees with the postponed fem. nom. sg. *ἰδέα*; *ὑποκρύπτετ᾽* is *ὑποκρύπτεται*, 3rd sg. pres. middle/passive of *ὑποκρύπτω*, corresponding to reflexive *s’ asconde*.
- *ὑπὸ τὸν πέπλον* retains the spatial figure of *sotto il velame*, with accusative where a dative would be natural for rest beneath something. *τῶν παραδόξων στίχων* is a dependent genitive plural: *παραδόξων* gives *strani* the sense ‘unexpected, extraordinary’.

> 64 E già venia su per le torbid’ onde  
> 65 un fracasso d’ un suon pien di spavento,  
> 66 per cui tremavano ambedue le sponde;  

And now there came, upon the turbid waves, a crash of fearful sound, at which the shores both trembled;

> 64 Ἐφέρετ᾽ ἤδη θολῶν ἐπὶ κυμάτων  
> (Already there was borne over the turbid waves)
>
> 65 Πάταγος ἤχου πλήρους φοβεροῦ τρόμου,  
> (A crash of sound full of dreadful terror,)
>
> 66 Ὅστις ἐποίει τρέμειν ἄμφω τὰς ὄχθας,  
> (Which made both the banks tremble,)

- *Ἐφέρετ᾽ ἤδη* ‘there was already being borne’ substitutes the imperfect middle/passive *ἐφέρετο* (3rd sg. of *φέρω*, elided) for active *già venia*. As in Italian, the subject is deferred to the next line. *θολῶν ἐπὶ κυμάτων* ‘over turbid waves’ places the genitive adjective before *ἐπί* and its noun after it; no distinct word renders the directional *su*.
- *πάταγος ἤχου πλήρους φοβεροῦ τρόμου* retains the successive dependencies of *un fracasso d’ un suon pien di spavento*: *πάταγος* is the nominative subject, *ἤχου* a genitive specifying the crash, and *πλήρους* (gen. sg. of *πλήρης*) agrees with *ἤχου* and governs *τρόμου*. The additional adjective *φοβεροῦ* qualifies the terror.
- *ὅστις ἐποίει τρέμειν ἄμφω τὰς ὄχθας* ‘which made both banks tremble’: the compound relative *ὅστις* is masc. nom. sg., resuming *πάταγος*. Instrumental or causal *per cui* becomes the subject of causative *ἐποίει* (contracted 3rd sg. imperf. active indicative of *ποιέω*). The banks are now the accusative subject of pres. active infinitive *τρέμειν*, not the subject of a finite imperfect. *ἄμφω* is the nom./acc. dual ‘both’, used here with the plural *τὰς ὄχθας*.

> 67 non altrimenti fatto che d’ un vento  
> 68 impetuoso per gli avversi ardori,  
> 69 che fier la selva senza alcun rattento;  

a sound as of a wind, impetuous for the adverse heats, which smites the forest without any stay;

> 67 Παρόμοιος ἀνέμῳ σφόδρα βιαίῳ,  
> (Like unto a wind exceeding violent,)
>
> 68 Ὃς ὁρμώμενος δι᾽ ἀντίθετα θάλπη  
> (Which, rushing forth through opposing heats,)
>
> 69 Σείει δρυμῶνας, καὶ χωρὶς ἀναπαύλης  
> (Shakes the forests, and without pause)

- *Παρόμοιος ἀνέμῳ σφόδρα βιαίῳ* ‘like a very violent wind’ replaces *non altrimenti fatto che d’ un vento impetuoso* with a positive comparison. Nominative *παρόμοιος* describes *πάταγος* and takes a dative of comparison; *βιαίῳ* agrees with *ἀνέμῳ*, and adverbial *σφόδρα* intensifies it.
- *ὃς ὁρμώμενος δι᾽ ἀντίθετα θάλπη σείει δρυμῶνας* ‘which, rushing on account of opposing heats, shakes woods’: *ὅς* is nominative as subject of *σείει*, though its antecedent *ἀνέμῳ* was dative. *ὁρμώμενος* is the contracted pres. middle/passive participle of *ὁρμάω*, masc. nom. sg.; it supplies the motion or impulse to which the heats are related. Elided *διά* with accusative *ἀντίθετα θάλπη* (neut. pl. of *θάλπος*) naturally expresses cause, as does *per gli avversi ardori*, rather than passage through a medium.
- *σείει* (3rd sg. pres. active indicative of *σείω*) gives ‘shakes’ for *fier* ‘strikes’; plural *δρυμῶνας* replaces singular *la selva*. *καὶ χωρὶς ἀναπαύλης* ‘and without a pause’, with *χωρίς* governing the genitive, carries *senza alcun rattento* forward to the verbs of the next line rather than attaching it directly to *σείει*.

> 70 li rami schianta, abbatte, e porta fuori;  

shatters off the boughs, beats down, and sweeps away;

> 70 Ἀποσπᾷ, θραύει, σφενδονίζει τοὺς κλάδους·  
> (Tears off, shatters, and hurls the branches;)

- *Ἀποσπᾷ, θραύει, σφενδονίζει τοὺς κλάδους* postpones the common accusative object where *li rami schianta, abbatte, e porta fuori* puts it first. The three verbs are asyndetic 3rd sg. present active indicatives: contracted *ἀποσπᾷ* (from *ἀποσπάω*) ‘wrenches off’, *θραύει* ‘breaks’, *σφενδονίζει* ‘slings away’. The last makes a single verb of *porta fuori* and specifies the manner of removal.

> 71 dinanzi polveroso va superbo,  
> 72 e fa fuggir le fiere e li pastori.  

dusty in front, it goes superb, and makes the wild beasts and the shepherds flee.

> 71 Αὐθαδῶς βαίνει κονιορτὸν προπέμπων,  
> (Proudly advances, sending dust before it,)
>
> 72 Καὶ φεύγειν ποιεῖ καὶ θῆρας καὶ ποιμένας.  
> (And puts to flight both beasts and shepherds.)

- *Αὐθαδῶς βαίνει κονιορτὸν προπέμπων* ‘self-willedly it advances, sending dust ahead’: *superbo* becomes an adverb, now first and modifying *βαίνει* (3rd sg. pres. active indicative of *βαίνω*). *dinanzi polveroso* becomes a participial clause; the direction is in the prefix of *προπέμπων* (pres. active participle, masc. nom. sg., agreeing with the wind), and the dust is its accusative object.
- *καὶ φεύγειν ποιεῖ καὶ θῆρας καὶ ποιμένας* closely follows causative *e fa fuggir*, but puts the pres. active infinitive *φεύγειν* before contracted *ποιεῖ*. The paired *καί … καί* ‘both … and’ connects the two accusative subjects of the infinitive, *θῆρας* (from *θήρ*) and *ποιμένας* (from *ποιμήν*); neither retains the Italian article.

> 73 Gli occhi mi sciolse, e disse: “Or drizza il nerbo  
> 74 del viso su per quella schiuma antica,  
> 75 per indi ove quel fummo è più acerbo.”  

He loosed my eyes, and said: "Now turn thy nerve of vision on that ancient foam, there where the smoke is harshest."

> 73 Ἐκκαλύψας μοι τὰς ὄψεις εἶπε· “Τεῖνον  
> (Uncovering my eyes, he said: "Direct)
>
> 74 „ Τὸ βλέμμα πρὸς τὸν ἀφρὸν ἀρχαῖον τόνδε,  
> (Your gaze toward this ancient foam,)
>
> 75 „Ἔνθα καθορᾷς τὴν ἄχνην παχυτέραν.”  
> (Where you behold the mist thicker.")

- *Ἐκκαλύψας μοι τὰς ὄψεις εἶπε* ‘having uncovered my eyes, he said’: the first of the coordinated finite verbs *sciolse, e disse* becomes an anterior aor. active participle (masc. nom. sg. of *ἐκκαλύπτω*) sharing the subject of *εἶπε*. The compound reverses the *καλύπτω* of 60; *μοι* is a dative of the person affected, where 60 used possessive *μου*.
- *Τεῖνον τὸ βλέμμα πρὸς τὸν ἀφρὸν ἀρχαῖον τόνδε* ‘stretch your gaze toward this ancient foam’: *τεῖνον*, 2nd sg. aor. active imperative of *τείνω*, preserves the notion of tension in *drizza il nerbo*, but *τὸ βλέμμα* ‘the gaze’ replaces *il nerbo del viso* as object. *πρός* with accusative gives the direction; postposed *τόνδε* (acc. sg. masc. of *ὅδε*) makes distal *quella* proximal. *Or* has no separate equivalent.
- *ἔνθα καθορᾷς τὴν ἄχνην παχυτέραν* ‘where you discern the mist thicker’ replaces *per indi ove quel fummo è più acerbo*. Relative adverb *ἔνθα* suffices for the local expressions; contracted *καθορᾷς* (2nd sg. pres. active indicative of *καθοράω*) introduces the viewer as subject. *παχυτέραν*, comparative of *παχύς*, is predicative to the accusative object: the mist is perceived as thicker, rather than the smoke being described as harsher.

> 76 Come le rane innanzi alla nimica  
> 77 biscia per l’ acqua si dileguan tutte,  
> 78 fin ch’ alla terra ciascuna s’ abbica;  

As frogs, before their enemy the serpent, run all asunder through the water, till each squats upon the bottom:

> 76 Ὡς οἱ βάτραχοι συναντηθέντες ὄφει  
> (As frogs, when met by a hostile)
>
> 77 Ἐχθρῷ φεύγουσιν ἅπαντες δι’ ὑδάτων,  
> (Serpent, all flee through the waters,)
>
> 78 Μέχρις ὑπὸ τὴν ἰλὺν κατορυχθῶσι,  
> (Until they bury themselves beneath the mud,)

- *Ὡς οἱ βάτραχοι συναντηθέντες ὄφει ἐχθρῷ* ‘as the frogs, having encountered a hostile snake’: *ὡς* opens the comparison, while *innanzi alla nimica biscia* becomes a prior encounter. *συναντηθέντες* is an aor. passive participle of *συναντάω*, masc. nom. pl., used with the sense ‘having met’; the ordinary active verb of meeting takes the dative, retained in *ὄφει* (dat. sg. of *ὄφις*) with agreeing *ἐχθρῷ*.
- *φεύγουσιν ἅπαντες δι’ ὑδάτων* ‘all flee through waters’: *φεύγουσιν* (3rd pl. pres. active indicative of *φεύγω*) gives flight rather than reflexive *si dileguan*. Nominative *ἅπαντες* agrees with masculine *βάτραχοι*, against feminine *tutte*; elided *διά* with the genitive plural expresses passage through, unlike the accusative construction in 68.
- *Μέχρις ὑπὸ τὴν ἰλὺν κατορυχθῶσι* ‘until they are buried beneath the mud’: *μέχρις* introduces a prospective limit with the subjunctive, without *ἄν*. *κατορυχθῶσι* is 3rd pl. aor. passive subjunctive of *κατορύσσω* (Attic *κατορύττω*). The plural and passive replace distributive *ciascuna s’ abbica*; *ὑπό* with accusative marks movement under *ἰλύς* ‘mud’, a more specific interpretation than *alla terra*.

> 79 vid’ io più di mille anime distrutte  
> 80 fuggir così dinanzi ad un, che al passo  
> 81 passava Stige colle piante asciutte.  

so I saw more than a thousand ruined spirits flee before one, who passed the Stygian ferry with soles unwet.

> 79 Μυρίας ἐγὼ ψυχὰς εἶδον ἐνόχους  
> (Countless guilty souls I saw)
>
> 80 Οὕτω φευγούσας ἔναντί τινος, ὅστις  
> (Thus fleeing before one who)
>
> 81 Διέβαινε τὴν Στύγα ποσὶν ἀβρόχοις.  
> (Was crossing the Styx with unwet feet.)

- *Μυρίας ἐγὼ ψυχὰς εἶδον ἐνόχους* places the number first: *μυρίας* (fem. acc. pl. of *μυρίος*) means ‘countless’, not specifically *più di mille*. *ἐνόχους* (fem. acc. pl. of the two-termination *ἔνοχος*) interprets *distrutte* as ‘guilty’ rather than ‘ruined’. Emphatic *ἐγώ* retains the expressed pronoun of *vid’ io*; *εἶδον* is 1st sg. second aor. active indicative of *ὁράω*.
- *οὕτω φευγούσας ἔναντί τινος* ‘fleeing thus before someone’ answers *ὡς* with *οὕτω*, as *così* answers *Come*. After the verb of perception the supplementary pres. active participle *φευγούσας* agrees with *ψυχάς*, in place of Italian infinitive *fuggir*. *ἔναντι* takes the genitive of indefinite *τις* for *dinanzi ad un*; its final acute in *ἔναντί* is due to enclitic *τινος*.
- *ὅστις διέβαινε τὴν Στύγα ποσὶν ἀβρόχοις*: the compound relative resumes the indefinite person; *διέβαινε* (3rd sg. imperf. active indicative of *διαβαίνω*, with internal augment) takes the river itself as accusative object, as in *passava Stige*. Instrumental *ποσίν* (dat. pl. of *πούς*) and agreeing *ἀβρόχοις* render *colle piante asciutte* as ‘with unwetted feet’; there is no separate expression for *al passo*.

> 82 Dal volto rimovea quell’ aer grasso,  
> 83 menando la sinistra innanzi spesso;  
> 84 e sol di quell’ angoscia parea lasso.  

He waved that gross air from his countenance, often moving his left *hand* before *him*; and only of that trouble seemed he weary.

> 82 Ἡ δ’ ὄψις αὐτοῦ τὴν ἀχλὺν ἀπεσόβει,  
> (And his countenance drove away the mist,)
>
> 83 Τὴν ἀριστερὰν προεκτείνοντος χεῖρα,  
> (As he stretched forward his left hand,)
>
> 84 Ὡς ἂν εἰ μὴ κάματον ᾐσθάνετ’ ἄλλον.  
> (As though he felt no other weariness.)

- *Ἡ δ’ ὄψις αὐτοῦ τὴν ἀχλὺν ἀπεσόβει* ‘and his face drove off the haze’ alters the agency: Italian *dal volto* indicates what the air is removed **from**, whereas Greek makes *ἡ ὄψις* nominative subject. *αὐτοῦ* is possessive; *τὴν ἀχλύν* condenses *quell’ aer grasso* into a noun for haze. *ἀπεσόβει* is the internally augmented, contracted 3rd sg. imperf. active indicative of *ἀποσοβέω*, retaining the sustained action of *rimovea*.
- *τὴν ἀριστερὰν προεκτείνοντος χεῖρα* ‘as he stretched his left hand forward’: *προεκτείνοντος* is a pres. active participle, masc. gen. sg. of *προεκτείνω*, referring to the possessor *αὐτοῦ*, not agreeing with the new subject *ὄψις*. The genitive participial phrase thus gives the attendant action of *menando* despite the change of grammatical subject. *χεῖρα* supplies the noun implicit in *la sinistra*; the prefix *προ-* renders *innanzi*, but *spesso* has no explicit equivalent.
- *ὡς ἂν εἰ μὴ κάματον ᾐσθάνετ’ ἄλλον* ‘as if he felt no other fatigue’ recasts *e sol di quell’ angoscia parea lasso* as a hypothetical comparison. *ὡς ἂν εἰ* introduces the supposition, with conditional negative *μή* and an imperfect indicative, not an optative. *ᾐσθάνετ᾽* is elided *ᾐσθάνετο*, 3rd sg. imperf. middle of deponent *αἰσθάνομαι*, with temporal augment. *κάματον … ἄλλον* is its accusative object; exclusion of any *other* fatigue replaces *sol di quell’ angoscia*.

> 85 Ben m’ accorsi ch’ egli era del ciel messo,  
> 86 e volsimi al maestro; e quei fe’ segno,  
> 87 ch’ io stessi cheto, ed inchinassi ad esso.  

Well did I perceive that he was a Messenger of Heaven; and I turned to the Master; and he made a sign that I should stand quiet, and bow down to him.

> 85 Εὖ δὲ γνοὺς ὅτι θεόθεν ἀπεστάλη,  
> (And well perceiving that he was sent from God,)
>
> 86 Εἶδον πρὸς τὸν Προστάτην· ὁ δ’ ἔνευσέ μοι  
> (I looked toward my Protector; and he signed to me)
>
> 87 Ἀτρέμας ἔχειν, κἀκεῖνον προσκυνῆσαι.  
> (To keep still, and to bow down before him.)

- *Εὖ δὲ γνοὺς ὅτι θεόθεν ἀπεστάλη* ‘and having well perceived that he had been sent from God’: *εὖ* renders *Ben*, but finite *m’ accorsi* becomes *γνούς*, masc. nom. sg. second (root) aor. active participle of *γιγνώσκω*, subordinate to *εἶδον* in 86. Declarative *ὅτι* retains *che*; *θεόθεν* uses the adverbial suffix *-θεν* of origin, and *ἀπεστάλη* (3rd sg. second aor. passive indicative of *ἀποστέλλω*) translates *era del ciel messo* by an act of sending rather than a title.
- *Εἶδον πρὸς τὸν Προστάτην* ‘I looked toward the Protector’ uses the same aorist of *ὁράω* as in 79, now with directional *πρός* and accusative instead of a direct object: looking takes the place of physically turning in *volsimi al maestro*. *ὁ δ’ ἔνευσέ μοι* ‘and he nodded to me’ uses pronominal *ὁ* with elided *δέ* for the change of subject in *e quei*. *ἔνευσε* is 3rd sg. aor. active indicative of *νεύω*; enclitic dative *μοι* names the recipient of the sign and accounts for the extra acute on the verb.
- *Ἀτρέμας ἔχειν, κἀκεῖνον προσκυνῆσαι* gives the content of the sign in two infinitives whose understood subject is the recipient *μοι*. Greek thus dispenses with *ch’ io* and the two imperfect subjunctives. Adverbial *ἀτρέμας* ‘motionless’ with pres. active *ἔχειν* describes a continuing state for *stessi cheto*; contracted aor. active *προσκυνῆσαι* (from *προσκυνέω*) presents *inchinassi* as a single act. *κἀκεῖνον* is crasis of *καὶ ἐκεῖνον*: ‘and [bow to] that one’, with an accusative object of the Greek verb in place of *ad esso*.

## Lines 88–120 — The Gate Opened and the Glowing Tombs

> 88 Ahi quanto mi parea pien di disdegno!  

Ah, how full he seemed to me of indignation!

> 88 Ὦ, πόσον ἐδόκει μοι μηνιθμοῦ πλήρης !  
> (Oh, how full of wrath he seemed to me!)

- *Ὦ, πόσον* distinguishes the cry *Ahi* from the exclamation of degree *quanto*. *πόσον* is the neuter accusative singular of *πόσος* used adverbially: ‘how very full’, not ‘of how much wrath’.
- *ἐδόκει μοι* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *δοκέω*, with the dative of the person judging) closely matches *mi parea*. The predicate *πλήρης* is nominative masculine singular, with ‘to be’ understood; *μηνιθμοῦ* (gen. sg. of the poetic noun *μηνιθμός*, ‘wrath’) depends on it. Where Dante has *pien di disdegno*, Musurus ends with the adjective, placing its complement first.

> 89 Venne alla porta, e con una verghetta  
> 90 l' aperse, chè non ebbe alcun ritegno.  

He reached the gate, and with a wand opened it : for there was no resistance.

> 89 Ἐλθὼν δὲ πρὸς τὴν πύλην μικρᾷ ῥαπίδι  
> (And coming to the gate, with a little wand)
>
> 90 Ἀνέῳξ’ αὐτὴν εὐθὺς ἀνεμποδίστως.  
> (He opened it straightway without hindrance.)

- *Ἐλθὼν δὲ πρὸς τὴν πύλην* turns the finite *Venne alla porta* into a preliminary action: ‘having come to the gate’. *ἐλθών* is the nominative masculine singular of the second aorist active participle of *ἔρχομαι*; postpositive *δέ* connects the action to the preceding line. *πρός* with the accusative marks the goal.
- *μικρᾷ ῥαπίδι* is an instrumental dative (fem. sg.; noun *ῥαπίς*, ‘rod’); the adjective makes the diminutive force of *verghetta* explicit. *ἀνέῳξ’* is elided *ἀνέῳξε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀνοίγω*, with the augment within the compound and lengthening of the initial vowel of its second member. *αὐτήν* takes up feminine *πύλην*, as *l’* takes up *porta*.
- *εὐθὺς ἀνεμποδίστως* ‘at once, without hindrance’ supplies the immediacy and compresses *chè non ebbe alcun ritegno* into an adverb of manner. There is no separate assertion of cause in Greek.

> 91 "O cacciati del ciel, gente dispetta,”  
> 92 cominciò egli in su l’ orribil soglia,  
> 93 "ond’ esta oltracotanza in voi s’ alletta?  

“O outcasts of Heaven! race despised!” began he, upon the horrid threshold, “why dwells this insolence in you?

> 91 “Ὦ φαῦλον γένος διωχθὲν οὐρανόθεν,”  
> ("O vile race driven out from heaven,")
>
> 92 Ἤρξατο λέγων πρὸ τοῦ φρικτοῦ θυρῶνος,  
> (He began speaking before the dreadful threshold,)
>
> 93 “Πόθεν ὑμῖν οἴησις ἐπῆλθε τόση ;  
> ("Whence came so great arrogance upon you?)

- *Ὦ φαῦλον γένος διωχθὲν οὐρανόθεν* makes one vocative group out of *O cacciati del ciel, gente dispetta*. *φαῦλον* ‘base’ expresses a judgment of worth rather than the passive sense of *dispetta*. *διωχθέν* (aor. pass. participle of *διώκω*) agrees with the collective *γένος* in the neuter singular, instead of addressing the outcasts in the plural. *οὐρανόθεν*, with the directional suffix *-θεν*, means ‘from heaven’.
- *ἤρξατο λέγων* renders *cominciò egli* by ‘he began speaking’: *ἤρξατο* is 3rd sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἄρχω*, in the middle sense ‘begin’; *λέγων* is a supplementary present active participle agreeing with the implicit subject. *πρὸ τοῦ φρικτοῦ θυρῶνος* places him ‘before the dreadful doorway’ (*πρό* + gen.; *θυρών*, ‘doorway, entrance’), not precisely *in su* the threshold.
- *πόθεν ὑμῖν οἴησις ἐπῆλθε τόση;* ‘whence did such great conceit come upon you?’ takes *ond’* in its directional sense. In place of the present *s’ alletta* ‘lodges’, Musurus uses *ἐπῆλθε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἐπέρχομαι*) with a personal dative. *οἴησις*, literally ‘opinion’, here ‘self-conceit’, is the subject; the delayed feminine nominative *τόση* gives degree in place of the pointing *esta*.

> 94 Perchè ricalcitrate a quella voglia,  
> 95 a cui non puote il fin mai esser mozzo,  
> 96 e che più volte v’ ha cresciuta doglia?  

Why spurn ye at that Will, whose object never can be frustrated, and which often has increased your pain?

> 94 „Πῶς λακτίζετε πρὸς τὴν βουλὴν ἐκείνην,  
> (Why do you kick against that will,)
>
> 95 „Ἧσπερ ὁ σκοπὸς οὔποτε ματαιοῦται,  
> (Whose purpose is never frustrated,)
>
> 96 „Ἀλλ᾿ ἣ βασάνους ηὔξησ᾿ ὑμῶν πολλάκις ;  
> (But which has many times increased your torments?)

- *πῶς λακτίζετε πρὸς τὴν βουλὴν ἐκείνην* ‘how do you kick against that resolve?’ retains the physical verb of *ricalcitrate* (2nd pl. pres. act. ind. of *λακτίζω*), but asks *πῶς* ‘how?’ rather than *Perchè*. *πρός* + accusative gives the direction of the kicks. The demonstrative follows the articular noun in the regular Greek order.
- *ἧσπερ ὁ σκοπός* ‘whose very purpose’ replaces *a cui ... il fin* with a possessive genitive. *ἧσπερ* is feminine singular referring to *βουλήν*, with intensive *-περ*. *οὔποτε ματαιοῦται* ‘is never brought to nothing’ is 3rd sg. pres. pass. ind. of contracted *ματαιόω*: a statement of what never happens replaces the modal *non puote ... mai esser mozzo*.
- *ἀλλ’ ἣ* (elided *ἀλλά*) substitutes contrast for *e che* and resumes the antecedent with a nominative relative, now the subject. *βασάνους ηὔξησ’ ὑμῶν πολλάκις* ‘has often increased your torments’ makes *doglia* plural and *v’* a possessive genitive. *ηὔξησ’* is elided *ηὔξησε*, 3rd sg. augmented aor. act. ind. of *αὐξάνω*, for the Italian compound past; *πολλάκις* gives *più volte* in one adverb.

> 97 Che giova nelle Fata dar di cozzo?  

What profits it to butt against the Fates?

> 97 „Πρὸς τί προσκρούειν ὑμᾶς τῷ πεπρωμένῳ ;  
> (Why do you dash yourselves against what is fated?)

- *πρὸς τί* ‘to what end?’ conveys *Che giova* without a finite verb of profiting. *προσκρούειν* (pres. act. inf. of *προσκρούω*, ‘strike against’) retains the collision of *dar di cozzo*, with *ὑμᾶς* supplying its accusative subject: ‘to what end [is] your striking against ...?’
- *τῷ πεπρωμένῳ* is the dative governed by the compound verb. The substantivized neuter singular perfect passive participle of *πόρω*, ‘that which has been allotted’, replaces plural *le Fata*.

> 98 Cerbero vostro, se ben vi ricorda,  
> 99 ne porta ancor pelato il mento e il gozzo.”  

Your Cerberus, if ye remember, still bears his chin and his throat peeled for doing so.”

> 98 „Κέρβερος ὑμῶν, ὡς ὑμᾶς δεῖ μεμνῆσθαι,  
> (Your Cerberus, as you ought to remember,)
>
> 99 „Ἄτριχον ἔτι λαιμὸν καὶ πώγων᾿ ἔχει.“  
> (Still has his throat and chin stripped of hair.")

- *Κέρβερος ὑμῶν* reproduces the order of *Cerbero vostro* with a possessive genitive. *ὡς ὑμᾶς δεῖ μεμνῆσθαι* ‘as you ought to remember’ makes *se ben vi ricorda* an assertion of obligation rather than a condition. Impersonal present *δεῖ* takes the infinitive with accusative subject *ὑμᾶς*; *μεμνῆσθαι* is the perfect middle infinitive of *μιμνῄσκω*, with the present force ‘have in mind’.
- *ἄτριχον ἔτι λαιμὸν καὶ πώγων’ ἔχει* puts *pelato* first as the adjective *ἄτριχον* ‘hairless’ (*ἄτριχος*, acc. masc. sg.), and ends with *ἔχει* ‘has’ for *porta*. The predicate adjective is singular with the coordinated singular objects. *λαιμόν* ‘throat’ and *πώγων’* (elided accusative *πώγωνα*, from *πώγων*, ‘beard, chin’) reverse *il mento e il gozzo*. *ἔτι* represents *ancor*; there is no equivalent of the backward-pointing *ne*.

> 100 Poi si rivolse per la strada lorda,  
> 101 e non fe’ motto a noi ; ma fe’ sembiante  
> 102 d’ uomo, cui altra cura stringa e morda,  
> 103 che quella di colui che gli è davante.  

Then he returned by the filthy way, and spake no word to us; but looked like one whom other care urges and incites than that of those who stand before him.

> 100 Ἔπειτ᾿ ἐστράφη διὰ τῆς βορβορώδους  
> (Then he turned back along the miry)
>
> 101 Ὁδοῦ, μηδὲν προσειπὼν ἡμῖν, ὁμοῖος  
> (Way, addressing not a word to us, like)
>
> 102 Ἀνδρὶ τὸν νοῦν ἔχοντι πάμπαν πρὸς ἄλλα  
> (A man whose mind is wholly turned to other things)
>
> 103 Ἢ τοὺς ἔμπροσθεν αὑτοῦ παρεστηκότας.  
> (Than those standing before him.)

- *ἔπειτ’ ἐστράφη* ‘then he turned’ has elided *ἔπειτα* and the 3rd sg. second aor. pass. ind. of *στρέφω*, used intransitively for reflexive *si rivolse*. *διὰ τῆς βορβορώδους ὁδοῦ* specifies passage ‘along the miry road’ by *διά* + genitive; the noun is carried over into line 101.
- *μηδὲν προσειπὼν ἡμῖν* ‘saying nothing to us’ subordinates *e non fe’ motto a noi* to *ἐστράφη*. *προσειπών* is a nominative masculine singular second aorist active participle (from *προσεῖπον*, ‘address’), with *μηδέν* as neuter accusative object and the persons addressed in the dative.
- *ὁμοῖος ἀνδρί* ‘like a man’ is predicative, without another finite verb or an adversative corresponding to *ma*. *ἔχοντι* (pres. act. participle of *ἔχω*, dat. masc. sg.) qualifies *ἀνδρί*. The idiom *τὸν νοῦν ἔχειν πρός* ‘have one’s mind directed toward’ replaces a care that actively *stringa e morda* with the man’s own state of attention; intensive *πάμπαν* ‘wholly’ is supplied.
- *πρὸς ἄλλα ἢ τοὺς ... παρεστηκότας* ‘toward things other than those standing ...’ shifts from a comparison of cares to a comparison of objects of attention. Comparative *ἤ* follows *ἄλλα*; *τοὺς ... παρεστηκότας* (acc. masc. pl. perfect act. participle of *παρίστημι*, substantivized) gives the resulting state ‘those who stand there’ for singular *colui che ... è*. *ἔμπροσθεν* governs the genitive *αὑτοῦ*, the contracted reflexive *ἑαυτοῦ*, ‘in front of himself’.

> 104 E noi movemmo i piedi in ver la terra,  
> 105 sicuri appresso le parole sante.  

And we moved our feet towards the city, secure after the sacred words.

> 104 Ἐτρέψαμεν δὲ τὸ βῆμα πρὸς τὴν χώραν,  
> (And we turned our steps toward the region,)
>
> 105 Ἀσφαλισθέντες ἐκ τῶν ἁγίων λόγων·  
> (Made secure by the holy words;)

- *Ἐτρέψαμεν δὲ τὸ βῆμα* ‘and we turned our step’ replaces *movemmo i piedi* with a singular noun for the movement. *ἐτρέψαμεν* is 1st pl. aor. act. ind. of *τρέπω*: the ending does the work of *noi*. *πρὸς τὴν χώραν* gives *in ver la terra* as motion toward a place or region, without an explicit word for “city”.
- *ἀσφαλισθέντες* (nom. masc. pl. aor. pass. participle of *ἀσφαλίζω*) makes *sicuri* the result of an action, ‘having been reassured’. *ἐκ τῶν ἁγίων λόγων* expresses the source of that assurance by *ἐκ* + genitive, rather than the temporal succession of *appresso*.

> 106 Dentro v’ entrammo senza alcuna guerra ;  

We entered into it without any strife;

> 106 Ἔσω τότ᾿ εἰσήλθομεν ἄνευ πολέμου.  
> (Then we entered within without strife.)

- *ἔσω τότ’ εἰσήλθομεν* ‘inward then we went in’ matches the doubling of direction in *Dentro v’ entrammo* by an adverb and the prefix of the verb, without a separate locative pronoun. *τότ’* is elided *τότε*, an added temporal link; *εἰσήλθομεν* is 1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *εἰσέρχομαι*.
- *ἄνευ πολέμου* uses *ἄνευ* with the genitive of *πόλεμος* ‘war, fighting’; the anarthrous noun suffices for *alcuna guerra* under the negative sense of the preposition.

> 107 ed io, ch’ avea di riguardar disio  
> 108 la condizion che tal fortezza serra,  
> 109 com’ io fui dentro, l’ occhio intorno invio ;  

and I, who was desirous to behold the condition which such a fortress encloses, as soon as I was in, sent my eyes around;

> 107 Ἐγὼ δὲ θεάσασθαι ποθῶν τὴν ἔνδον  
> (And I, longing to behold the state)
>
> 108 Τοῦ τείχους κατάστασιν, τίς ποτ᾿ ἂν εἴη,  
> (Within the wall, whatever it might be,)
>
> 109 Ἅμ᾿ ὡς εἰσέβην, τὸ βλέμμ᾿ ἔτεινα πέριξ.  
> (As soon as I entered, cast my gaze around.)

- *ἐγὼ δὲ ... ποθῶν* keeps the expressed *io* and replaces *ch’ avea ... disio* with a circumstantial present active participle (nom. masc. sg. of contracted *ποθέω*). Its complement *θεάσασθαι* is the aorist middle infinitive of deponent *θεάομαι*, ‘to take a look at’, for *di riguardar*.
- *τὴν ἔνδον τοῦ τείχους κατάστασιν* ‘the state of things inside the wall’ encloses the locative expression between article and noun. *ἔνδον* with the genitive *τοῦ τείχους* replaces the active relative *che tal fortezza serra*; there is no equivalent of *tal*. *τίς ποτ’ ἂν εἴη* adds an indirect question in apposition: ‘what it might be’. Interrogative *τίς* is feminine singular with *κατάστασιν* as its referent; elided *ποτε* lends emphasis, and *ἄν* with *εἴη* (3rd sg. pres. opt. of *εἰμί*) expresses potentiality.
- *ἅμ’ ὡς εἰσέβην* ‘just as soon as I stepped in’ reinforces temporal *ὡς* with elided *ἅμα*; *εἰσέβην* is 1st sg. root aor. act. ind. of *εἰσβαίνω*. *τὸ βλέμμ’ ἔτεινα πέριξ* ‘I directed my gaze round about’ substitutes elided *βλέμμα* ‘gaze’ for *l’ occhio*, and augmented *ἔτεινα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *τείνω*) for the vivid present *invio*. Adverbial *πέριξ* renders *intorno*.

> 110 e veggio ad ogni man grande campagna  
> 111 piena di duolo e di tormento rio.  

and saw, on either hand, a spacious plain full of sorrow and of evil torment.

> 110 Εἶδον πάντοσε μεγάλην πεδιάδα,  
> (I saw on every side a great plain,)
>
> 111 Πλήρη βασάνων καὶ δεινῶν ἀχθηδόνων.  
> (Full of torments and dreadful woes.)

- *εἶδον* (here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*) continues the past narrative without a connective for *e*; Dante continues in the present, *veggio*. *πάντοσε* properly gives direction, ‘in every direction’, for *ad ogni man*. *μεγάλην πεδιάδα* (acc. fem. sg. of *μέγας* and *πεδιάς*) gives *grande campagna* as a large expanse of level ground.
- *πλήρη* (acc. fem. sg. of *πλήρης*) agrees with *πεδιάδα* and takes genitives of contents. *βασάνων καὶ δεινῶν ἀχθηδόνων* ‘of torments and dreadful distresses’ pluralizes and reverses *di duolo e di tormento rio*: *δεινῶν* now qualifies the distresses (*ἀχθηδών*), not the torments.

> 112 Sì come ad Arli, ove il Rodano stagna,  

As at Arles, where the Rhone stagnates,

> 112 Ὡς ἐν Ἄρλαις ἔνθ᾿ ὁ Ῥοδανὸς λιμνάζει,  
> (As at Arles, where the Rhone forms marshes,)

- *ὡς ἐν Ἄρλαις* begins the comparison with *ὡς* alone for *Sì come*, local *ἐν* taking the dative of a plural place name. *ἔνθ’* is elided *ἔνθα* ‘where’, introducing the local clause. *ὁ Ῥοδανὸς λιμνάζει* closely follows *il Rodano stagna*: *λιμνάζει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *λιμνάζω*, from *λίμνη*, ‘lake, pool’) means ‘forms pools, lies like a lake’.

> 113 sì com’ a Pola presso del Quarnaro,  
> 114 che Italia chiude e i suoi termini bagna,  

as at Pola near the Quarnaro _gulf_, which shuts up Italy and bathes its confines,

> 113 Ὡς ἐν Πόλᾳ πλησίον κόλπου Φλανώνης,  
> (As at Pola, near the Gulf of Flanona,)
>
> 114 Ὃς Ἰταλίας κλείων ὅρια λούει,  
> (Which, enclosing Italy, washes its borders,)

- *ὡς ἐν Πόλᾳ* repeats the comparative opening, now with a singular dative. *πλησίον* ‘near’ takes the genitive *κόλπου* ‘of a gulf’, making the geographical noun explicit where Dante gives *del Quarnaro*. *Φλανώνης* is itself a genitive, ‘of Flanona’: Musurus identifies the gulf by a different name.
- *ὃς* is nominative masculine singular, referring to *κόλπου* but taking its case as subject of *λούει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *λούω*, ‘washes’). *κλείων* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *κλείω*) subordinates the first of Dante’s two coordinated verbs: ‘enclosing ... washes’. *Ἰταλίας* is genitive, not the accusative object one would expect from *Italia chiude*; it belongs with *ὅρια* ‘boundaries’. The compressed Greek thus brings ‘Italy’s boundaries’ into relation with both enclosing and washing, rather than distinguishing *Italia* from *i suoi termini*.

> 115 fanno i sepolcri tutto il loco varo :  

the sepulchres make all the place uneven

> 115 Οἱ τάφοι τὴν γῆν ἀνώμαλον ποιοῦσι,  
> (The tombs make the ground uneven,)

- *οἱ τάφοι τὴν γῆν ἀνώμαλον ποιοῦσι* supplies the main clause after the paired local expressions. Greek leads with the subject, where Italian leads with *fanno*. *ποιοῦσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of contracted *ποιέω*) takes an object *τὴν γῆν* ‘the ground’ and a predicate accusative *ἀνώμαλον* (fem. sg. of the two-ending adjective *ἀνώμαλος*). This interprets *varo* specifically as unevenness of surface; there is no separate *tutto*.

> 116 così facevan quivi d’ ogni parte,  
> 117 salvo che il modo v’ era più amaro :  

: so did they here on every side, only the manner here was bitterer

> 116 Τοιαύτη τις ἦν ἡ πεδιὰς ἐκείνη,  
> (Such in manner was that plain,)
>
> 117 Πλὴν ὅτι πικροτέραν παρεῖχε θέαν.  
> (Save that it presented a more bitter sight.)

- *τοιαύτη τις ἦν ἡ πεδιὰς ἐκείνη* ‘of some such kind was that plain’ answers *ὡς* with qualitative *τοιαύτη*. Enclitic indefinite *τις* qualifies the likeness. *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*) retains the imperfect of *facevan*, but instead of repeating what the tombs did, Musurus predicates a quality of *ἡ πεδιάς*. Postnominal *ἐκείνη* points back to the plain; *quivi d’ ogni parte* is not separately rendered.
- *πλὴν ὅτι* ‘except that’ closely follows *salvo che*. *πικροτέραν παρεῖχε θέαν* ‘it presented a more bitter sight’ changes *il modo v’ era più amaro* into a transitive clause with the plain still the subject. *παρεῖχε* is 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *παρέχω*; comparative *πικροτέραν* (from *πικρός*) agrees with feminine accusative *θέαν*, ‘sight, spectacle’.

> 118 chè tra gli avelli fiamme erano sparte,  
> 119 per le quali eran sì del tutto accesi,  
> 120 che ferro più non chieda verun’ arte.  

: for amongst the tombs were scattered flames, whereby they were made all over so glowing-hot, that iron more _hot_ no craft requires.

> 118 Διὰ τῶν τάφων ἐσκεδάζοντο φλόγες,  
> (Among the tombs flames were scattered,)
>
> 119 Κατακαίουσαι τόσον, ὥστε σιδήρῳ  
> (Burning so fiercely that for iron)
>
> 120 Οὐκ ἔστι τέχνη διδοῦσα θέρμην ἴσην.  
> (There is no art that gives equal heat.)

- *διὰ τῶν τάφων* ‘throughout the tombs’ uses *διά* + genitive for *tra gli avelli*. There is no conjunction for explanatory *chè*. *ἐσκεδάζοντο* (3rd pl. imperf. mid./pass. ind. of *σκεδάζω*, passive here) presents the flames as dispersed; as in *erano sparte*, the plural subject *φλόγες* follows the verb.
- *κατακαίουσαι* (nom. fem. pl. pres. act. participle of *κατακαίω*) agrees with *φλόγες*: ‘burning [the tombs] intensely’. The flames remain the grammatical subject instead of becoming the instrument *per le quali* of the passive *eran ... accesi*. Adverbial neuter accusative *τόσον* ‘so much’ marks degree and prepares *ὥστε*; *del tutto* has no separate equivalent.
- *ὥστε σιδήρῳ οὐκ ἔστι τέχνη διδοῦσα θέρμην ἴσην* ‘so that there is no craft giving iron equal heat’ has a finite indicative after *ὥστε*, presenting the result as a fact. *οὐκ ἔστι* is existential; *διδοῦσα* (nom. fem. sg. pres. act. participle of *δίδωμι*) qualifies *τέχνη*, with accusative thing given *θέρμην ἴσην* and dative recipient *σιδήρῳ*, brought forward before the negative. Musurus thus changes the comparison itself: where *ferro più non chieda verun’ arte* denies that any craft **requires** iron hotter than this, the Greek denies that any craft **gives** iron even equal heat.

## Lines 121–133 — The Tombs of the Heresiarchs

> 121 Tutti gli lor coperchi eran sospesi,  

Their covers were all raised up;

> 121 Τὰ πώματ’ αὐτῶν ἦν πάντ’ ᾐωρημένα·  
> (Their lids were all raised up;)

- *Τὰ πώματ’ αὐτῶν* ‘their lids’ puts the possessive after the noun: *πώματ’* is *πώματα* (nom. neut. pl. of *πῶμα*) with the final vowel elided before *αὐτῶν*, the genitive plural of *αὐτός* rendering *lor*.
- Italian opens with *Tutti*; Greek holds *πάντ’* (elided *πάντα*, nom. neut. pl. of *πᾶς*) back until after the verb. Both *πάντ’* and *ᾐωρημένα* (nom. neut. pl., perfect middle/passive participle of *αἰωρέω*, passive in sense) agree with *πώματα*. Where *eran* is plural, *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*) is regularly singular with a neuter plural subject. With the perfect participle it expresses the resulting state in past time: ‘were hanging aloft’.

> 122 e fuor n’ uscivan sì duri lamenti,  
> 123 che ben parean di miseri e d’ offesi.  

and out of them proceeded moans so grievous, that they seemed indeed *the moans of spirits sad and wounded*.

> 122 Ἐξήρχοντο δὲ κλαυθμοὶ γοώδεις οὕτως,  
> (And thence came forth such mournful wailings,)
>
> 123 Ὥστ’ ἦν βέβαιον δυστήνους ἐκεῖ πάσχειν.  
> (That it was certain wretched beings suffered there.)

- *Ἐξήρχοντο δέ* renders *e fuor n’ uscivan*: connective *δέ* is postpositive, and *ἐξήρχοντο* (3rd pl. imperf. mid. ind. of the deponent *ἐξέρχομαι*, with temporal augment after the prefix) conveys continued emergence. The prefix gives the outward direction; there is no separate pronoun for *n’* ‘from them’.
- *κλαυθμοὶ γοώδεις* ‘mournful wailings’ is the postverbal subject (both words nom. masc. pl.). *κλαυθμός* suggests weeping, and *γοώδης* lamentation, rather than the hardness of *duri*. Italian *sì* precedes the adjective; Greek *οὕτως* follows it and anticipates *Ὥστ’* (elided *ὥστε*) ‘so that’.
- *ἦν βέβαιον* ‘it was certain’ is impersonal, with the predicate adjective in the neuter singular. The indicative *ἦν* presents the result as a fact. Instead of *parean* ‘they seemed’, with the laments still the subject, Musurus makes a statement about what could be inferred with certainty. *δυστήνους* (acc. masc. pl. of *δύστηνος*, used substantivally) is the subject of *πάσχειν* (pres. act. inf. of *πάσχω*): ‘that wretched beings were suffering there’. Thus *di miseri e d’ offesi* becomes a whole infinitival clause: the wretched are no longer possessors of the laments, their hurt becomes a verb, and *ἐκεῖ* ‘there’ is supplied.

> 124 Ed io : “ Maestro, quai son quelle genti,  
> 125 che seppellite dentro da quell’ arche  
> 126 si fan sentir coi sospiri dolenti?”  

And I: "Master, what are these people who, buried within those chests, make themselves heard by their painful sighs?"

> 124 Κἀγώ· “Προστάτα, τίνες αἱ ψυχαὶ ᾽κεῖναι,  
> (And I: "Protector, who are those souls,)
>
> 125 „ Αἵπερ ἐντὸς μνημάτων ἐντεθαμμέναι  
> (Who, buried within sepulchres,)
>
> 126 „ Ἀναπέμπουσι στεναγμοὺς ὀδυνώδεις;”  
> (Send up painful groans?")

- *Κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*, reproducing the verbless *Ed io*. *Προστάτα* (voc. sg. of *προστάτης*) means ‘protector’, not literally *Maestro* ‘master’. *τίνες αἱ ψυχαὶ ᾽κεῖναι* ‘who [are] those souls?’ leaves the copula understood: *τίνες* is the nominative plural interrogative predicate, and *αἱ ψυχαί* (nom. fem. pl. of *ψυχή*) substitutes ‘souls’ for *genti*. *᾽κεῖναι*, by prodelision for *ἐκεῖναι*, agrees with *ψυχαί* and occupies the usual demonstrative position outside the article–noun group.
- *Αἵπερ* (nom. fem. pl. of *ὅσπερ*) resumes *ψυχαί* as subject; the strengthening particle *-περ* makes the identification more pointed than simple *che*. *ἐντός* governs the genitive plural *μνημάτων* (from *μνῆμα*), ‘within tombs’: *arche* is interpreted by function, and *quell’* has no separate equivalent. *ἐντεθαμμέναι* (nom. fem. pl., perfect passive participle of *ἐνθάπτω*) comes after the place phrase, unlike *seppellite*. It agrees with the relative subject and describes its existing state; the perfect reduplication is internal, after *ἐν-*.
- *Ἀναπέμπουσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *ἀναπέμπω*) ‘send up’ replaces the reflexive causative *si fan sentir*. Greek says nothing separately about being heard: what Italian expresses as the means, *coi sospiri dolenti*, becomes the direct object *στεναγμοὺς ὀδυνώδεις* ‘painful groans’ (acc. masc. pl.).

> 127 Ed egli a me: “ Qui son gli eresiarche  
> 128 co’ lor seguaci d’ ogni setta, e molto  

And he to me: "[Here] are the Arch-heretics with their followers of every sect;

> 127 Ὁ δ’ ὑπέλαβεν· “Εἰσὶν αἱρεσιάρχαι  
> (And he replied: "They are the heresiarchs)
>
> 128 „ Σὺν τοῖς ὀπαδοῖς αἱρέσεως ἑκάστης.  
> (With the followers of every heresy.)

- *Ὁ δ’ ὑπέλαβεν* ‘and he replied’ uses the article pronominally in the narrative idiom *ὁ δέ*, with *δέ* elided. *ὑπέλαβεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὑπολαμβάνω*, augmented after the prefix) supplies the verb of answering that *Ed egli a me* leaves implicit; conversely *a me* is not expressed.
- *Εἰσὶν αἱρεσιάρχαι* ‘they are heresiarchs’ answers the question of identity with *εἰσίν* (3rd pl. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*) and an anarthrous predicate nominative (masc. pl. of *αἱρεσιάρχης*). There is no locative corresponding to *Qui*.
- *Σὺν τοῖς ὀπαδοῖς* ‘with the followers’ uses *σύν* with the associative dative (masc. pl. of *ὀπαδός*); the article, without a possessive, stands against *co’ lor*. *αἱρέσεως ἑκάστης* ‘of each sect’ depends on ‘followers’: *αἱρέσεως* is gen. sg. of *αἵρεσις*, with the distributive adjective *ἑκάστης* following its noun, in reverse order to *d’ ogni setta*.

> 129 più che non credi, son le tombe carche.  

and much more, than thou thinkest, the tombs are laden.

> 129 „ Πλέον ἢ φρονεῖς πλήρεις εἰσὶν οἱ τύμβοι.  
> (More than you suppose are the tombs filled.)

- *Πλέον ἢ φρονεῖς* ‘more than you suppose’ keeps the comparison before the main predication. *πλέον* is the neuter singular comparative of *πολύς* used adverbially with *πλήρεις*; *ἤ* introduces the comparison, and *φρονεῖς* is the contracted 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *φρονέω*. Neither the connective *e* nor the intensifier *molto* has a separate equivalent.
- *πλήρεις εἰσὶν οἱ τύμβοι* ‘full are the tombs’ puts the predicate first and the articular subject last, against *son le tombe carche*. *πλήρεις* (nom. masc. pl. of *πλήρης*) expresses fullness by an adjective rather than the image of a load; it agrees with *οἱ τύμβοι* (nom. pl. of *τύμβος*).

> 130 Simile qui con simile è sepolto;  

Like with like is buried here;

> 130 „ Ὧδ’ ὅμοιος ὁμοίῳ σύναμα κεῖται·  
> (Here like lies together with like;)

- *Ὧδ’* is elided *ὧδε* ‘here’, moved to the fore from Italian *qui*. *ὅμοιος ὁμοίῳ* reproduces *Simile … con simile* by repeating one adjective substantivally: the first form is nom. masc. sg., the generic subject ‘one who is like’; the second is dat. masc. sg., ‘with one like him’. The unprepositioned dative suits both comparison with *ὅμοιος* and association with *σύναμα* ‘together’.
- *κεῖται* (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *κεῖμαι*) ‘lies’ describes position or state without the passive burial construction of *è sepolto*.

> 131 e i monimenti son più, e men caldi.”  

and the monuments are more and less hot."

> 131 „ Τὰ μνήματα δ’ οὐκ ἔστιν ἔμπυρ’ ὁμοίως.”  
> (And the sepulchres are not equally on fire.")

- *Τὰ μνήματα δ’* ‘and the tombs’ renders *e i monimenti*, with connective *δέ* elided before *οὐκ*. *ἔστιν* (3rd sg. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*) is singular with the neuter plural subject, as in line 121; its predicate *ἔμπυρ’* is elided *ἔμπυρα* (nom. neut. pl. of *ἔμπυρος*) ‘on fire’.
- *οὐκ … ὁμοίως* ‘not … equally’ gives the implication of *più, e men* rather than translating the two degrees of comparison. The final adverb qualifies the predication: it is the uniformity, not the presence, of fire that is denied. *ἔμπυρα* itself is stronger than *caldi* ‘hot’.

> 132 E poi ch’ alla man destra si fu volto,  
> 133 passammo tra i martiri e gli alti spaldi.  

Then, after turning to the right hand, we passed between the tortures and the high battlements.

> 132 Εἶτα πρὸς τὴν δεξιὰν χεῖρα τραπέντες  
> (Then, turning toward the right hand,)
>
> 133 Ἔβημεν μέσον τειχῶν καὶ μαρτυρίων.  
> (We walked between the walls and the *martyria*.)

- *Εἶτα* ‘then’ marks succession; the temporal clause *poi ch’ … si fu volto* becomes a circumstantial participle, *τραπέντες* (nom. masc. pl., second aor. pass. participle of *τρέπω*, intransitive in sense, ‘having turned’). The aorist presents the turn as prior to the walking. Unlike singular *si fu volto*, the plural participle shares the subject of the main verb. *πρὸς τὴν δεξιὰν χεῖρα* keeps the explicit ‘hand’ of *alla man destra*, with directional *πρός* governing the accusative (*χεῖρα*, acc. sg. of *χείρ*, qualified by fem. *δεξιάν*).
- *Ἔβημεν* (1st pl. root aor. act. ind. of *βαίνω*) ‘we went’ renders *passammo*. *μέσον* is an adverbial neuter accusative used prepositionally with the genitives *τειχῶν* (from *τεῖχος*) and *μαρτυρίων* (from *μαρτύριον*), ‘between walls and torments’. Greek reverses the order of the two terms, reduces *gli alti spaldi* to ‘walls’ without the adjective, and uses neither article. *μαρτυρίων* follows *martiri* closely in word family: the intended sense is ‘sufferings’, rather than the ordinary classical ‘testimonies’ or ‘proofs’.

## Summary — From Italian Clauses to Greek Participles

Musurus often keeps Dante’s order of perceptions and actions while changing which of them receives a finite verb. After a verb of seeing, *παλινδρομοῦντα* ‘turning back’ and *φευγούσας* ‘fleeing’ agree with the person or persons seen, where Italian uses the infinitives *tornare* and *fuggir*. Elsewhere the participle marks a distinction that Italian coordination leaves implicit: present *κράζουσαι* ‘while shrieking’ makes *e gridavan* an accompanying action, whereas aorist *ἐκκαλύψας* ‘having uncovered’ makes *sciolse* preliminary to *disse*. Musurus does not invariably prefer subordination, however: in the description of the wind he retains three successive finite verbs but moves their shared object, *τοὺς κλάδους* ‘the branches’, from first place to last.

The particles show both closeness and freedom. Postpositive *γάρ* preserves the explanatory force of *chè*, and *οὕτω* answers *ὡς* much as *così* answers *Come*. The recurrent *ὁ δέ* ‘and he in turn’ marks a change of speaker economically. Yet a clause can disappear altogether: *ἀνεμποδίστως* ‘without hindrance’ conveys what *chè non ebbe alcun ritegno* says without retaining its causal syntax. Conversely, *ἵνα* with the aorist subjunctive *ἐκβάλω* ‘that I may bring out’ makes a finite purpose clause of *per trarne* and identifies the agent by the verb ending.

Some of the most consequential choices concern what a speaker knows or means. In the opening, *ὠχρότης* ‘pallor’ specifies the first *color*, while *τάραγμα* ‘agitation’ names the feeling behind the second: what Italian conveys through two changes of colour, Greek divides between appearance and emotion. *Εἰ μή* ‘if not’ preserves the broken conditional, but *τὸν Δεῖνα* ‘so-and-so’ reads *tal* as an unnamed person, and *ἔχω* ‘I have’ replaces the offer with a present assurance. Even a close verbal parallel can alter the claim: in the comparison with heated iron, Dante denies that any craft *requires* greater heat, whereas Musurus’ *διδοῦσα θέρμην ἴσην* ‘giving equal heat’ denies that any craft even *produces* its equal.
