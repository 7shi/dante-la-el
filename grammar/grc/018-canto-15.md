# Inferno 15

## Lines 1–24 — Along the Bank and Recognised

> 1 Ora cen porta l' un de' duri margini,  

Now one of the hard margins bears us on,

> 1 Νῦν δὲ βαίνομεν ἐφ᾽ ἑνὸς τῶν λιθίνων  
> (And now we walk along one of the stony)

- *Νῦν δὲ βαίνομεν* ‘now we walk’ reverses the construction of *Ora cen porta*: instead of the margin carrying ‘us’, the travellers are the subject of *βαίνομεν* (1st pl. pres. act. ind. of *βαίνω*). Postpositive *δέ* marks the transition; the Italian pronominal *ne* has no separate equivalent.
- *ἐφ᾽ ἑνὸς τῶν λιθίνων χειλῶν* ‘on one of the stony lips’ runs into line 2. *Ἐπί* with the genitive gives the surface on which they walk; its final vowel is elided and *π* becomes *φ* before the rough breathing of *ἑνός*. The numeral (gen. sg. neut. of *εἷς*) takes a partitive genitive, as does *l’un de’ duri margini*. *Χειλῶν* is the contracted gen. pl. of neuter *χεῖλος* ‘lip, edge’; *λιθίνων* specifies material rather than simply hardness.

> 2 e il fummo del ruscel di sopra aduggia  
> 3 sì che dal foco salva l' acqua e gli argini.  

and the smoke of the rivulet makes shade above, so that from the fire it shelters the water and the banks.

> 2 Χειλῶν· ὁ δ᾽ ἀτμὸς τοῦ ῥύακος ἐπάνω  
> (Margins; and the vapour of the stream above)
>
> 3 Σκεπάζει πυκνούμενος ὕδωρ τε κὤχθην.  
> (Thickening shelters both water and bank.)

- *ὁ δ᾽ ἀτμὸς τοῦ ῥύακος* ‘and the vapour of the stream’ gives *fummo* as vapour rather than smoke. Elided *δέ* stands between article and noun; *τοῦ ῥύακος* (gen. sg. of *ῥύαξ*) depends on *ἀτμός*. Adverbial *ἐπάνω* answers to *di sopra*.
- *Σκεπάζει πυκνούμενος* ‘it covers as it thickens’ replaces both *aduggia* and the result clause *sì che … salva* with one finite verb (*σκεπάζει*, 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *σκεπάζω*) and a circumstantial participle. *Πυκνούμενος* (nom. sg. masc. pres. mid./pass. participle of *πυκνόω*) agrees with the vapour; its thickening is supplied, while neither shade nor *dal foco* is named.
- *ὕδωρ τε κὤχθην* ‘both water and bank’ gives the two accusative objects of *σκεπάζει*. Postpositive *τε* is paired with *καί*, here in crasis with *ὄχθην* (acc. sg. of *ὄχθη*): *κὤχθην*. The singular replaces plural *gli argini*.

> 4 Quale i Fiamminghi tra Guizzante e Bruggia,  
> 5 temendo il fiotto che ver lor s' avventa,  
> 6 fanno lo schermo perchè il mar si fuggia;  

As the Flemings between Bruges and [Wissant], dreading the flood that rushes towards them, make their bulwark to repel the sea;

> 4 Ὡς Φλανδρεῖς Βρυγῶν μεταξὺ καὶ Κασσάνδας  
> (As the Flemings between Bruges and Cassanda,)
>
> 5 Δεδιότες χειμῶνος κῦμα πλημμύρας,  
> (Fearing the winter's wave of the flood-tide,)
>
> 6 Ἀπείργουσ᾽ ὠκεανὸν διὰ χωμάτων·  
> (Keep off the ocean by means of embankments;)

- *Ὡς Φλανδρεῖς* introduces the comparison of *Quale i Fiamminghi*; *Φλανδρεῖς* is nominative plural, subject of *ἀπείργουσι*. In *Βρυγῶν μεταξὺ καὶ Κασσάνδας*, *μεταξύ* ‘between’ governs both genitives but is placed after the first. Musurus reverses *tra Guizzante e Bruggia* and gives *Κασσάνδας* for *Guizzante*, a different identification from the English “[Wissant]”.
- *Δεδιότες* (nom. pl. masc. perfect act. participle of *δείδω*) has present force, ‘being in fear’, and, like *temendo*, gives the motive. Its object is *κῦμα* ‘wave’, qualified by the genitives *χειμῶνος* ‘of winter’ or ‘of storm’ and *πλημμύρας* ‘of the flood-tide’. The relative clause *che ver lor s’avventa* disappears: Greek characterises the wave without saying that it rushes at them.
- *Ἀπείργουσ᾽ ὠκεανὸν διὰ χωμάτων* ‘they keep off ocean by means of earthworks’ makes the purpose of the Italian bulwark the main assertion. *Ἀπείργουσ᾽* is elided *ἀπείργουσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *ἀπείργω*), with *ὠκεανόν* as object. *Fanno lo schermo* becomes an expression of means, *διά* with gen. pl. *χωμάτων* (from *χῶμα*); *il mar*, subject of subjunctive *si fuggia*, becomes the thing kept off.

> 7 e quale i Padovan lungo la Brenta,  
> 8 per difender lor ville e lor castelli,  
> 9 anzi che Chiarentana il caldo senta :  

and as the Paduans, along the Brenta, to defend their villages and [castles] ere Chiarentana feels the heat:

> 7 Καθὼς καὶ Παταυῗνοι παρὰ τὴν Βρένταν  
> (Just as the Paduans also along the Brenta,)
>
> 8 Εἰς προφύλαξιν τῆς πόλεως καὶ πύργων,  
> (For the protection of their city and towers,)
>
> 9 Πρὶν αἴσθηται τὸν καύσων᾽ ἡ Κλαρεντάνα·  
> (Before Clarentana feels the scorching heat;)

- *Καθὼς καὶ Παταυῗνοι* ‘just as the Paduans too’ resumes *ὡς Φλανδρεῖς*. As in *e quale i Padovan*, the nominative plural has no new finite verb: the defensive action is understood from the first comparison. *Παρὰ τὴν Βρένταν* uses *παρά* with the accusative for extension ‘along’ the river.
- *Εἰς προφύλαξιν* ‘for protection’ replaces *per difender* with *εἰς* and an accusative action noun (*προφύλαξις*). Its objective genitives *τῆς πόλεως καὶ πύργων* name what is protected: one ‘city’ (gen. sg. of *πόλις*) for *ville*, and ‘towers’ (gen. pl. of *πύργος*) for *castelli*. Neither instance of possessive *lor* is expressed.
- *Πρὶν αἴσθηται* retains a finite temporal clause like *anzi che … senta*. *Αἴσθηται* is 3rd sg. second aor. mid. subj. of deponent *αἰσθάνομαι*, the perception viewed as an anticipated event. *Πρίν* here takes the bare subjunctive; after an affirmative main assertion classical Attic would more naturally use *πρίν* with an infinitive. *Τὸν καύσων᾽* (elided acc. sg. *καύσωνα*, from *καύσων*) intensifies *il caldo* to ‘scorching heat’; the object precedes the articular subject *ἡ Κλαρεντάνα*, reversing the Italian order.

> 10 a tale imagine eran fatti quelli,  
> 11 tutto che nè sì alti nè sì grossi,  
> 12 qual che si fosse, lo maestro felli.  

in like fashion those banks were formed, though not so high nor so large, the master, whoever it might be, made them.

> 10 Παρόμοι᾽ ἦν τὰ χείλη τοῦ ποταμίου,  
> (Similar were the margins of the little river,)
>
> 11 Ἀλλ᾽ οὐχ ὁμοίου πάχους οὔτε μὴν ὕψους·  
> (Yet not of like thickness nor indeed height;)
>
> 12 Ταῦτ᾽ ἔκτισ᾽ ὁ Κύριος, ὅστις ἂν εἴη.  
> (The Lord built these, whoever he may be.)

- *Παρόμοι᾽ ἦν τὰ χείλη τοῦ ποταμίου* ‘similar were the lips of the little river’ answers the two comparisons with a predicate adjective, not the passive *eran fatti*. *Παρόμοι᾽* is elided *παρόμοια* (nom. pl. neut. of *παρόμοιος*); *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. of *εἰμί*) has the regular singular agreement with a neuter plural subject. The explicit genitive *τοῦ ποταμίου* replaces the reference back by *quelli*.
- *Ἀλλ᾽ οὐχ ὁμοίου πάχους οὔτε μὴν ὕψους* ‘but not of like thickness, nor indeed of height’ turns concessive *tutto che* into adversative *ἀλλά*, elided before *οὐχ*. The negative takes its aspirated form before rough-breathed *ὁμοίου*. Predicate genitives of quality, *πάχους* and *ὕψους* (from *πάχος*, *ὕψος*), replace the adjectives *alti* and *grossi* in reverse order; *ὁμοίου* supplies the comparison for both. *Μήν* reinforces the second negative.
- *Ταῦτ᾽ ἔκτισ᾽ ὁ Κύριος* ‘these the Lord built’ puts the object first: *ταῦτ᾽* is elided acc. pl. neut. *ταῦτα*, answering to the pronoun in *felli* ‘made them’. *Ἔκτισ᾽* is elided *ἔκτισε* (3rd sg. first aor. act. ind. of *κτίζω*, with syllabic augment). *Ὁ Κύριος* gives *lo maestro* the title ‘the Lord’ rather than a craftsman’s designation.
- *ὅστις ἂν εἴη* ‘whoever he might be’ moves *qual che si fosse* after the subject to which it refers. *Ὅστις* is the indefinite relative (nom. sg. masc.); *ἄν* with *εἴη* (3rd sg. pres. opt. of *εἰμί*) is potential, not a past indicative despite the preceding narrative aorist.

> 13 Già eravam dalla selva rimossi  
> 14 tanto, ch' io non avrei visto dov' era,  
> 15 perch' io indietro rivolto mi fossi,  

Already we were so far removed from the wood, that I should not have seen where it was, had I turned back,

> 13 Ἤδη τοῦ δρυμοῦ μακρὰν τοσοῦτον ἦμεν,  
> (Already we were so far from the wood,)
>
> 14 Ὥστ᾽ ἰδεῖν αὐτόν, ἔνθ᾽ ἦν, οὐκ ἂν δυναίμην,  
> (That I could not have seen it, where it was,)
>
> 15 Εἰ τὰ βλέμματ᾽ ὄπισθεν ἔμελλον τρέπειν,  
> (If I were to turn my glances backward,)

- *Ἤδη τοῦ δρυμοῦ μακρὰν τοσοῦτον ἦμεν* replaces *eravam dalla selva rimossi tanto* with a statement of distance. Adverbial *μακράν* ‘far away’ takes the genitive of separation *τοῦ δρυμοῦ*; adverbial acc. neut. *τοσοῦτον* marks degree and anticipates the result clause. *Ἦμεν* is 1st pl. imperf. of *εἰμί*.
- *Ὥστ᾽ ἰδεῖν αὐτόν, ἔνθ᾽ ἦν, οὐκ ἂν δυναίμην* ‘so that I could not see it, where it was’ has a finite result clause after elided *ὥστε*: *ἰδεῖν* (second aor. act. inf. of *ὁράω*) depends on *δυναίμην*, not directly on *ὥστε*. Greek adds the direct object *αὐτόν* (the masculine *δρυμός*) alongside the Italian-like local clause *ἔνθ᾽ ἦν* ‘where it was’ (*ἔνθα* elided before *ἦν*).
- *Οὐκ ἂν δυναίμην* is a negated potential optative (1st sg. pres. mid. opt. of deponent *δύναμαι*). Rather than mark past unreality as in *non avrei visto … perch’io … rivolto mi fossi*, Musurus frames an imagined possibility: *εἰ … ἔμελλον τρέπειν* ‘if I were going to turn’. *Ἔμελλον* is 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *μέλλω* with the pres. act. inf. of *τρέπω*; the protasis is indicative, not the optative usual in a uniformly less-vivid condition. Elided *τὰ βλέμματ᾽* (*βλέμματα*, acc. pl. neut.) supplies ‘my glances’ as object in place of reflexive *mi*; *ὄπισθεν* gives their direction.

> 16 quando incontrammo d' anime una schiera,  
> 17 che venia lungo l' argine; e ciascuna  

when we met a troop of spirits, who were coming alongside the bank;

> 16 Ὅτ᾽ αἴφνης ψυχῶν ἀπηντήσαμεν πλῆθος  
> (When suddenly we met a multitude of souls)
>
> 17 Παρὰ τὴν κρηπῖδ᾽ ἐρχόμενον, καὶ βλέπον  
> (Coming alongside the bank, and looking)

- *Ὅτ᾽ αἴφνης* is elided *ὅτε* ‘when’ with an added ‘suddenly’. *Ψυχῶν ἀπηντήσαμεν πλῆθος* separates the genitive of the members, *ψυχῶν*, from its head *πλῆθος*, much as *d’anime* precedes *una schiera*. The neuter collective means ‘multitude’, without the suggestion of an ordered troop.
- *Ἀπηντήσαμεν* (1st pl. first aor. act. ind. of *ἀπαντάω*, with internal temporal augment) treats *πλῆθος* as its accusative object, like transitive *incontrammo*; the usual classical construction for meeting someone is *ἀπαντάω* with the dative.
- *Παρὰ τὴν κρηπῖδ᾽ ἐρχόμενον* ‘coming alongside the embankment’ replaces *che venia* with a pres. mid. participle of deponent *ἔρχομαι*, acc. sg. neut. agreeing with *πλῆθος*. *Κρηπῖδ᾽* is elided *κρηπῖδα* (acc. sg. of *κρηπίς*). Greek already begins the next action in line 17 with *καὶ βλέπον*, a second participle in the same construction.

> 18 ci riguardava, come suol da sera  
> 19 guardar l' un l' altro sotto nuova luna;  

and each looked at us, as in the evening men are wont to look at one another under a new moon;

> 18 Ἅπαν πρὸς ἡμᾶς, ὥσπερ εἴωθε νύκτωρ  
> (All toward us, as men are wont by night)
>
> 19 Βλέπεσθ᾽ ἀλλήλους ὑπὸ νέαν σελήνην.  
> (To look at one another under a new moon.)

- *βλέπον ἅπαν πρὸς ἡμᾶς* ‘the whole of it looking towards us’ keeps *πλῆθος* as the centre of the construction. *Βλέπον* (pres. act. participle of *βλέπω*) and emphatic *ἅπαν* (from *ἅπας*) are acc. sg. neut.; the latter belongs with *πλῆθος*, not *ἡμᾶς*. Thus distributive *ciascuna* with finite imperfect *ci riguardava* becomes collective and participial, with *πρός* plus accusative indicating the direction of the gaze.
- *ὥσπερ εἴωθε νύκτωρ* ‘as is customary by night’ retains generic singular *suol*. *Εἴωθε* (3rd sg. perfect act. ind. of *ἔθω*) has present habitual meaning; adverbial *νύκτωρ* shifts *da sera* to night.
- *Βλέπεσθ᾽ ἀλλήλους* gives the reciprocal action of *guardar l’un l’altro*: *βλέπεσθ᾽* is *βλέπεσθαι* (pres. mid./pass. inf. of *βλέπω*) with poetic elision of *-αι*, construed here as middle with the reciprocal acc. pl. masc. *ἀλλήλους*. The people looking are understood, not named by a plural subject of *εἴωθε*. *Ὑπὸ νέαν σελήνην* closely follows *sotto nuova luna*, using *ὑπό* with the accusative for the circumstances of the looking.

> 20 e sì ver noi aguzzavan le ciglia,  
> 21 come vecchio sartor fa nella cruna.  

and towards us sharpened their vision, as an aged tailor does at the eye of his needle.

> 20 Ὄμμασι δ᾽ ἡμᾶς ἑώρων ἀτενέσιν,  
> (And they gazed upon us with intent eyes,)
>
> 21 Ὥσπερ βελόνην διείρων γέρων ῥάπτης.  
> (Like an old tailor threading a needle.)

- *Ὄμμασι δ᾽ ἡμᾶς ἑώρων ἀτενέσιν* ‘and they were looking at us with intent eyes’ replaces *aguzzavan le ciglia* with the effect of that action. *Ὄμμασι* (dat. pl. of *ὄμμα*) is instrumental; its adjective *ἀτενέσιν* (dat. pl. of *ἀτενής*, with movable *ν*) is held back to the end. *Δ᾽* is elided postpositive *δέ*. Italian *ver noi* becomes direct object *ἡμᾶς*.
- *Ἑώρων* (3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *ὁράω*, with the characteristic augmented stem *ἑωρ-* and contracted ending) shifts from grammatical agreement with singular *πλῆθος* to the plural persons making it up.
- *Ὥσπερ βελόνην διείρων γέρων ῥάπτης* ‘like an old tailor threading a needle’ leaves the verb of looking understood. Instead of a finite substitute verb (*fa*) and the point looked at (*nella cruna*), Musurus supplies the occupation of the hands: *διείρων* (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *διείρω*) agrees with *γέρων ῥάπτης* and governs acc. sg. *βελόνην*. The needle is named, its eye is not.

> 22 Così adocchiato da cotal famiglia,  
> 23 fui conosciuto da un, che mi prese  
> 24 per lo lembo e gridò: "Qual maraviglia!"  

Thus eyed by that family, I was recognised by one who took me by the skirt, and said: “What a wonder!”

> 22 Ἐκ δὲ τῶν ψυχῶν τῶν προσβλεπουσῶν μ᾽ οὕτω  
> (And from among the souls thus gazing upon me)
>
> 23 Μία μ᾽ ἀνεγνώρισεν, ἣ λαβομένη  
> (One recognized me, who, taking hold)
>
> 24 Ἐμῶν κρασπέδων ἔκραξεν· “Οἷον θαῦμα!”  
> (Of my hem, cried out: “What a marvel!”)

- *Ἐκ δὲ τῶν ψυχῶν τῶν προσβλεπουσῶν μ᾽ οὕτω μία* ‘and from among the souls thus looking at me, one’ changes the perspective of both Italian passives. *Ἐκ* with the genitive is partitive, not an expression of agency; *ψυχῶν* resumes the earlier noun instead of rendering *famiglia*. The repeated article puts *προσβλεπουσῶν* (gen. pl. fem. pres. act. participle of *προσβλέπω*) in attributive position. Elided *μ᾽* (*με*) is its object; *οὕτω* renders *così*.
- *Μία μ᾽ ἀνεγνώρισεν* ‘one recognised me’ makes Italian *da un* the active subject. *Μία* is the feminine nominative of *εἷς* because the understood noun is *ψυχή*, hence also feminine relative *ἣ*; the gender does not translate masculine *un* directly. *Ἀνεγνώρισεν* is 3rd sg. first aor. act. ind. of *ἀναγνωρίζω*, with augment after the preverb and movable *ν*.
- *ἣ λαβομένη ἐμῶν κρασπέδων ἔκραξεν* subordinates the first of the coordinated acts *mi prese … e gridò*: *λαβομένη* (nom. sg. fem. second aor. mid. participle of *λαμβάνω*, without augment) means ‘having taken hold’. The middle in this sense takes a genitive of the thing grasped: *ἐμῶν κρασπέδων* ‘of my hems’, with a possessive adjective and plural noun for *mi … per lo lembo*.
- *Ἔκραξεν* (3rd sg. first aor. act. ind. of *κράζω*) retains the force of *gridò*, rather than the English “said”. *Οἷον θαῦμα* is a verbless exclamation like *Qual maraviglia*: exclamatory *οἷον* agrees with the neuter singular *θαῦμα*, ‘what a marvel!’

## Lines 25–57 — Recognition and Conversation With Ser Brunetto

> 25 Ed io, quando il suo braccio a me distese,  
> 26 ficcai gli occhi per lo cotto aspetto  

And I, when he stretched out his arm to me, fixed my eyes on his baked aspect,

> 25 Ἐγὼ δ᾽, ἐπεί μοι τὴν χεῖρ᾽ ἔτεινεν, εἶδον  
> (And I, when he stretched out his hand to me, looked)
>
> 26 Ἀτενῶς αὐτῆς τὴν πυρίφλεκτον ὄψιν·  
> (Steadfastly upon his fire-scorched visage;)

- *Ἐγὼ δ᾽, ἐπεί μοι … ἔτεινεν* follows the order of *Ed io, quando … a me distese*, except that the enclitic dative *μοι* comes early in the temporal clause. *ἔτεινεν* is the augmented, contracted liquid aorist of *τείνω* (3rd sg. act. ind.). *τὴν χεῖρ᾽*, elided for *τὴν χεῖρα*, gives ‘the hand’ for *il suo braccio*; the article suffices for the possessor of the body part.
- *εἶδον ἀτενῶς* ‘I looked intently’ distributes *ficcai gli occhi* between a verb of seeing (*εἶδον*, 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*) and an adverb. Instead of the eyes being the object, the object is what they see, *τὴν πυρίφλεκτον ὄψιν*. The compound adjective ‘burnt by fire’ makes *cotto* explicit; its two-termination form in *-ον* is feminine accusative singular here.
- The preceding possessive genitive *αὐτῆς* is feminine, not masculine as one would expect if its grammatical antecedent were Brunetto. The English gloss “his” conveys the referent, not the gender of the Greek; the feminine recurs in lines 28–29.

> 27 sì che il viso abbruciato non difese  
> 28 la conoscenza sua al mio intelletto;  

so that the scorching of his visage hindered not my mind from knowing him;

> 27 Τὸ δ᾽ αἰθαλόεν σχῆμ᾽ οὐκ ἐκώλυσέ με,  
> (And the sooty figure did not hinder me)
>
> 28 Ὅπως ἐκ μνήμης αὐτὴν ἀναγνωρίσω.  
> (From recognizing him from memory.)

- Italian *sì che* marks a result; Greek starts a coordinated clause, *τὸ δ᾽ αἰθαλόεν σχῆμ᾽* ‘and the smoke-blackened form’. *αἰθαλόεν* is the poetic neuter singular of *αἰθαλόεις*, agreeing with *σχῆμα* (elided before *οὐκ*). It is this visible form, rather than specifically *il viso abbruciato*, that is the subject of *ἐκώλυσέ με* ‘prevented me’ (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *κωλύω*, with an additional accent before enclitic *με*).
- *la conoscenza sua al mio intelletto* becomes an act of recognition by the speaker: *ὅπως ἐκ μνήμης αὐτὴν ἀναγνωρίσω* ‘that I should recognize her from memory’. *ἐκ μνήμης* supplies the source of recognition in place of naming the intellect; *αὐτήν* is its feminine accusative object. After *ὅπως*, *ἀναγνωρίσω* is naturally read as 1st sg. aor. act. subj. of *ἀναγνωρίζω*. The finite clause is unusual after *κωλύω*: ordinary Attic would use an infinitive for the act not prevented.

> 29 e chinando la mia alla sua faccia,  
> 30 risposi: “Siete voi qui, ser Brunetto?”  

and bending my face to his, I answered: “Are you here, Ser Brunetto?”

> 29 Πρὸς δ᾽ αὐτῆς τὸ πρόσωπον τὴν χεῖρα κλίνας  
> (And inclining my hand toward his face,)
>
> 30 Ἀπεκρινάμην· “Ὧδ᾽ εἶ σύ, Κὺρ Βρουνέττε;”  
> (I answered: “Are you here, Ser Brunetto?”)

- *πρὸς δ᾽ αὐτῆς τὸ πρόσωπον τὴν χεῖρα κλίνας* puts the goal (*πρός* + acc.) first. *αὐτῆς* is again a possessive genitive, dependent on *πρόσωπον*. Crucially, the object of *κλίνας* (nom. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *κλίνω*) is *τὴν χεῖρα* ‘my hand’. Musurus takes *la mia* as a hand, whereas Carlyle supplies “my face” from *alla sua faccia*.
- *ἀπεκρινάμην* is 1st sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, ‘I answered’. *ὧδ᾽ εἶ σύ* ‘here are you?’ fronts the elided locative adverb *ὧδε* and stresses the pronoun; *εἶ* is singular, unlike the courteous plural *siete*. *Κὺρ Βρουνέττε* retains the honorific by using the later Greek title *κύρ* with the vocative of *Βρουνέττος*.

> 31 E quegli: “O figliuol mio, non ti dispiaccia  
> 32 se Brunetto Latini un poco teco  
> 33 ritorna indietro, e lascia andar la traccia.”  

And he: “O my son! let it not displease thee, if Brunetto Latini turn back with thee a little, and let go his train.”

> 31 Ὁ δέ· “Τέκνον μου, σὺν σοὶ μὴ δυσφορήσῃς  
> (And he: “My son, be not displeased if with you)
>
> 32 „Ἐὰν Βρουνέττος Λατίνης ἐπιστρέψῃ  
> („Brunetto Latini turns)
>
> 33 „Μικρὸν ὀπίσω, τὴν ῥύμην λιπὼν τούτων.”  
> („A little way back, leaving the file of these.”)

- *ὁ δέ* ‘and he’ leaves the verb of speaking unexpressed, as does *E quegli*. *τέκνον μου* has the neuter vocative and an enclitic possessive genitive. *μὴ δυσφορήσῃς* ‘do not take it ill’ is a personal prohibition (2nd sg. aor. act. subj. of *δυσφορέω*), replacing impersonal *non ti dispiaccia*.
- *σὺν σοί* ‘with you’, corresponding to *teco*, is brought forward even before the prohibition, though it belongs in sense with *ἐπιστρέψῃ*. *ἐὰν … ἐπιστρέψῃ* uses the conditional conjunction with the 3rd sg. aor. act. subj. of *ἐπιστρέφω* for the envisaged turning back; *Βρουνέττος Λατίνης* is its nominative subject. *μικρὸν ὀπίσω* ‘a little way back’ combines an adverbial accusative of extent with a directional adverb.
- *τὴν ῥύμην λιπὼν τούτων* ‘leaving the file of these’ subordinates *e lascia andar la traccia* as a participle: *λιπών* is nom. masc. sg., second aor. act. of *λείπω*, agreeing with Brunetto. *τούτων* is a demonstrative genitive plural identifying those who make up the file; there is no separate equivalent of *andar*.

> 34 Io dissi lui: “Quanto posso ven preco;  

I said: “With all my power I do beseech it of you;

> 34 Ἐγὼ δέ· “Τό γ᾽ ἐπ᾽ ἐμοί, πάνυ προθύμως·  
> (And I: “For my part, most willingly;)

- *ἐγὼ δέ* ‘and I’ signals the change of speaker without translating *dissi lui* by a verb and indirect object. *τό γ᾽ ἐπ᾽ ἐμοί* ‘as far at least as rests with me’ is an idiomatic expression of limitation: neuter *τό*, restrictive *γε* elided before *ἐπί*, and *ἐπί* (itself elided) with the dative. *πάνυ προθύμως* ‘very willingly’ completes a verbless answer. Thus *Quanto posso* survives as a qualification, but *ven preco*, an actual entreaty addressed to Brunetto, becomes an expression of readiness.

> 35 e se volete che con voi m' asseggia,  
> 36 faròl, se piace a costui, chè vo seco.”  

and if you wish me to sit down with you, I will do so, if it pleases him there, for I go with him.”

> 35 „ Εἰ δὲ παρὰ σὲ βούλει με συγκαθῖσαι,  
> („ And if you wish me to sit down beside you,)
>
> 36 „ Ἔσετ᾿, ἂν ἐμὸν ἀρέσκῃ συνοδίτην.”  
> („ It shall be so, if it pleases my fellow-traveller.”)

- *εἰ δὲ παρὰ σὲ βούλει με συγκαθῖσαι* retains *se volete che …*, but uses singular *βούλει* (2nd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *βούλομαι*) and an accusative subject *με* with the aorist active infinitive *συγκαθῖσαι* of *συγκαθίζω*. The compound already conveys sitting together; *παρὰ σέ* specifies taking a place beside the addressee.
- *ἔσετ᾿* (for *ἔσεται*, 3rd sg. fut. of *εἰμί*) means ‘it shall be so’, not literally *faròl* ‘I shall do it’. *ἂν … ἀρέσκῃ* has conditional *ἄν* in the sense of *ἐάν* and the 3rd sg. pres. act. subj. of *ἀρέσκω*. *ἐμὸν … συνοδίτην* ‘my fellow traveller’ compresses *a costui, chè vo seco* into a description. Its accusative is striking: classical *ἀρέσκω* in the sense ‘be pleasing to’ normally takes the person in the dative.

> 37 “O figliuol,” disse, “qual di questa greggia  
> 38 s’ arresta punto, giace poi cent’ anni  
> 39 senza arrostarsi quando il foco il feggia.  

“O my son,” he said, “whoever of this flock stops one instant, lies a hundred years thereafter without fanning himself when the fire strikes him.

> 37 —“Ὦ τέκνον,” εἶπεν, “ἤν τις τῶν ψυχῶν τούτων  
> (—“O son,” he said, “if any of these souls)
>
> 38 „ Στήσητ᾿ ἀκαρές, ἑκατὸν ἔτη μένει,  
> („ Halts for a single instant, he remains a hundred years,)
>
> 39 „ Ἀμύνασθαι τοῦ πυρὸς οὐ δυναμένη.  
> („ Unable to ward off the fire.)

- *ὦ τέκνον … ἤν τις τῶν ψυχῶν τούτων* turns *qual di questa greggia* into ‘if any one of these souls’. *ἤν* is a contracted form of conditional *ἐάν*; indefinite *τις* takes the partitive genitive *τῶν ψυχῶν τούτων*. The collective metaphor of *greggia* is not used here.
- *στήσητ᾿ ἀκαρές* ‘should stop for an instant’ has elided *στήσηται*, 3rd sg. aor. mid. subj. of *ἵστημι*, followed by an adverbial neuter accusative meaning ‘a tiny space [of time]’. *ἑκατὸν ἔτη μένει* uses another accusative of duration, with uncontracted stem visible in the singular *ἔτος* but contracted plural *ἔτη*. Present *μένει* ‘remains’ replaces *giace poi*: neither the posture nor ‘thereafter’ is explicit.
- *ἀμύνασθαι τοῦ πυρὸς οὐ δυναμένη* ‘being unable to defend herself against the fire’ gives the consequence as incapacity, not as *senza* followed by an infinitive and *quando* followed by a finite verb. *δυναμένη* (nom. fem. sg. pres. mid. participle of *δύναμαι*) agrees with the individual understood as a *ψυχή*. It governs the aor. mid. infinitive *ἀμύνασθαι* of *ἀμύνω*. The middle expresses self-defence; *τοῦ πυρός* names the danger in the genitive, rather than making the fire the subject of ‘strikes’.

> 40 Però va oltre; io ti verrò a’ panni,  
> 41 e poi rigiugnerò la mia masnada,  
> 42 che va piangendo i suoi eterni danni.”  

Therefore go on; I will follow at thy skirts; and then will I rejoin my band, that go lamenting their eternal losses.”

> 40 „ Ἀλλ᾿ ἄγ᾿, ἀρτηθήσομαι τῶν σῶν κρασπέδων,  
> („ But come then, I will cling to your hem,)
>
> 41 „ Ἕως συμμιχθῶ τῇ ᾽μῇ πάλιν ἀγέλῃ,  
> („ Until I rejoin my band again,)
>
> 42 „ Ὀδυρομένῃ κολάσεις αἰωνίους.”  
> („ Which laments its eternal torments.”)

- *ἀλλ᾿ ἄγ᾿* ‘but come!’ has two elisions, of *ἀλλά* and of the imperative *ἄγε* (2nd sg. pres. act. of *ἄγω*), here an exhortation rather than a literal equivalent of *va oltre*. *ἀρτηθήσομαι τῶν σῶν κρασπέδων* ‘I shall hang from your hems’ makes *ti verrò a’ panni* more physical. *ἀρτηθήσομαι* is 1st sg. fut. pass. of *ἀρτάω*; the genitive gives that from which one hangs.
- *ἕως συμμιχθῶ* ‘until I mingle again’ replaces the coordinate future *e poi rigiugnerò* with a prospective temporal clause. *συμμιχθῶ* is 1st sg. aor. pass. subj. of *συμμίγνυμι*, intransitive in sense; Musurus uses *ἕως* without the *ἄν* usual in Attic with this prospective subjunctive. *τῇ ᾽μῇ πάλιν ἀγέλῃ* gives the group rejoined in the associative dative; *᾽μῇ* is *ἐμῇ* with initial vowel lost after the article, and *πάλιν* supplies the ‘again’ of *rigiugnerò*.
- *ὀδυρομένῃ* is dat. fem. sg. pres. mid. participle of *ὀδύρομαι*, agreeing with *ἀγέλῃ* ‘herd, band’. It replaces the relative clause *che va piangendo* without a verb of motion. Its object *κολάσεις αἰωνίους* ‘eternal punishments’ interprets *eterni danni*; *αἰωνίους* is feminine accusative plural here, and *suoi* is left implicit.

> 43 Io non osava scender della strada  
> 44 per andar par di lui; ma il capo chino  
> 45 tenea, come uom che reverente vada.  

I durst not descend from the road to go level with him; but kept my head bent down, like one who walks in reverence.

> 43 Ἀλλ᾿ οὐκ ἐτόλμων καταβῆναι τοῦ χείλους,  
> (Yet I dared not step down from the bank,)
>
> 44 Ἐγγύς τ᾿ ἰέναι· τὴν κεφαλὴν δὲ κλίνας  
> (And go near him; but bowing my head)
>
> 45 Ἔστην, ὡς ἀνήρ, ὃς μετ᾿ αἰδοῦς βαδίζει.  
> (I stood, like a man who walks with reverence.)

- *ἀλλ᾿ οὐκ ἐτόλμων* ‘but I did not dare’ keeps the imperfect of *non osava*: *ἐτόλμων* is the augmented, contracted 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *τολμάω*. Its infinitives are *καταβῆναι* (second aor. act. of *καταβαίνω*) and *ἰέναι* (pres. of *εἶμι*), linked by elided *τε* in *ἐγγύς τ᾿ ἰέναι*. The second is coordinated rather than marked as the purpose of the first. The separative genitive *τοῦ χείλους* gives ‘from the edge’ for *della strada*; *ἐγγύς* gives proximity, not explicitly going abreast as in *par di lui*.
- *τὴν κεφαλὴν δὲ κλίνας ἔστην* is literally ‘but, having bowed my head, I stood’. Italian *il capo chino tenea* makes holding the continuing action; Greek distinguishes the prior act (*κλίνας*, as in line 29) from *ἔστην*, 1st sg. intransitive second aor. act. ind. of *ἵστημι*.
- *ὡς ἀνήρ, ὃς μετ᾿ αἰδοῦς βαδίζει* closely follows *come uom che reverente vada*. The indefinite comparison with Italian subjunctive becomes a relative clause with indicative *βαδίζει* ‘walks’; the predicative adjective *reverente* becomes *μετά* with the genitive of the abstract noun *αἰδώς*, ‘with reverence’.

> 46 Ei cominciò: “Qual fortuna o destino  
> 47 anzi l’ ultimo dì quaggiù ti mena?  

He began: “What chance, or destiny, brings thee, ere thy last day, down here?

> 46 Εἶπε δὲ τότε· “Τί συμβὰν ἢ τίς τύχη  
> (Then he said: “What chance or what fortune)
>
> 47 „ Πρὶν ἐσχάτης ἡμέρας ἤγαγέ σ᾿ ὧδε;  
> („ Before your last day has brought you hither?)

- *εἶπε δὲ τότε* ‘and then he said’ substitutes the act of speaking for *cominciò*. *τί συμβὰν ἢ τίς τύχη* distinguishes the neuter substantival participle *συμβάν* ‘happening’ (nom. sg. second aor. act. of *συμβαίνω*) from the feminine noun *τύχη* ‘fortune’. Each has its own interrogative adjective, unlike *Qual fortuna o destino*; the Greek pair contrasts an occurrence with fortune rather than explicitly naming destiny.
- *πρὶν ἐσχάτης ἡμέρας* ‘before the last day’ uses *πρίν* prepositionally with the genitive, closely tracking Italian *anzi*; classical prose more commonly uses *πρό* for this nominal construction. *ἤγαγέ σ᾿ ὧδε* ‘brought you hither’ changes present *ti mena* to the augmented reduplicated second aorist *ἤγαγε* (3rd sg. act. ind. of *ἄγω*), accented again before elided enclitic *σε*. *ὧδε* gives the destination but not the downward direction of *quaggiù*.

> 48 e chi è questi che mostra il cammino?”  

and who is this that shews the way?”

> 48 „ Τίς δ᾿ ἔστ᾿ ἐκεῖνος ὁ τὴν ὁδὸν δεικνύων;”  
> („ And who is he yonder who shows the way?”)

- *τίς δ᾿ ἔστ᾿ ἐκεῖνος* ‘and who is that one?’ uses the more distant demonstrative for *questi*; *δ᾿* and *ἔστ᾿* represent *δέ* and *ἐστί*. *ὁ τὴν ὁδὸν δεικνύων* turns *che mostra il cammino* into an articular participial phrase in apposition to the demonstrative. *δεικνύων* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle, from the thematic variant *δεικνύω* of *δείκνυμι*) encloses its accusative object between article and participle.

> 49 “Lassù di sopra in la vita serena,”  
> 50 rispos’ io lui, “mi smarri’ in una valle,  
> 51 avanti che l’ età mia fosse piena.  

“There above, up in the clear life, I lost myself,” replied I, “in a valley, before my age was full.

> 49 —“Ἄνω ᾽κεῖθ᾿ ἐν τῇ ζωῇ τῇ γαληναίᾳ,”  
> (—“Up yonder in the serene life,”)
>
> 50 Τότ᾿ εἶπον, “ἐντὸς κοιλάδος ἐπλανήθην,  
> (Then I said, “I went astray within a valley,)
>
> 51 „ Πρὶν εἶναί με τέλειον τὴν ἡλικίαν.  
> („ Before I had reached the fullness of my years.)

- *ἄνω ᾽κεῖθ᾿* answers the doubled *Lassù di sopra* with two adverbs: *ἄνω* ‘above’ and poetic *ἐκεῖθι* ‘there’, the latter losing its initial vowel after *ἄνω* and its final vowel before *ἐν*. *ἐν τῇ ζωῇ τῇ γαληναίᾳ* closely renders *in la vita serena*: the repeated article puts the adjective in the second attributive position, ‘in the life that is serene’.
- *τότ᾿ εἶπον* ‘then I said’ replaces *rispos’ io lui*, supplying a temporal adverb (*τότε*, elided) but leaving person to the ending understood in *εἶπον* (here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*) and omitting the addressee. *ἐντὸς κοιλάδος* ‘within a valley’ precedes the verb and uses the genitive after *ἐντός*. *ἐπλανήθην* (1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *πλανάω*) gives the intransitive sense ‘went astray’ of reflexive *mi smarri’*.
- *πρὶν εἶναί με τέλειον τὴν ἡλικίαν* makes the person, not *l’età mia*, the subject: ‘before I was full-grown in respect of age’. *πρίν* takes the present infinitive *εἶναι* with accusative subject *με* and agreeing predicate *τέλειον*; *τὴν ἡλικίαν* is an accusative of respect. The extra accent on *εἶναί* is due to the enclitic *με*.

> 52 Pur ier mattina le volsi le spalle;  

Only yester morn I turned my back to it;

> 52 „ Χθὲς δ᾿ ἔτρεψ᾿ αὐτῇ πρωῒ μόνον τὰ νῶτα·  
> („ And only yesterday morning I turned my back upon it;)

- *χθὲς δ᾿ … πρωῒ μόνον* separates the elements of *Pur ier mattina*: *χθές* ‘yesterday’, *πρωΐ* ‘in the morning’, and adverbial *μόνον* ‘only’. *ἔτρεψ᾿ αὐτῇ … τὰ νῶτα* keeps the Italian idiom and its cases of participation: *ἔτρεψ᾿* is elided *ἔτρεψα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *τρέπω*), feminine dative *αὐτῇ* answers to *le* and refers to *κοιλάς*, and neuter plural *τὰ νῶτα* ‘the back’ answers to plural *le spalle*.

> 53 questi m’ apparve, tornand’ io in quella,  
> 54 e riducemi a ca per questo calle.”  

he appeared to me, as I was returning into it, and guides me home again by this path.”

> 53 „ Οὗτος δὲ παρέστη μοι, καί μ᾿ οἴκαδ᾿ ἄξων  
> („ And this one appeared to me, and to bring me homeward)
>
> 54 „ Ὁδηγεῖ με νῦν διὰ τῆς ὁδοῦ ταύτης.”—  
> („ Guides me now along this road.”—)

- *οὗτος δὲ παρέστη μοι* ‘and this one came to stand beside me’ retains demonstrative *questi* and dative *m’*, but uses the intransitive second aorist *παρέστη* (3rd sg. act. ind. of *παρίστημι*, with augment after the prefix) for *apparve*. Italian *tornand’ io in quella*, with the speaker as subject of the gerund, has no equivalent.
- Instead *καί μ᾿ οἴκαδ᾿ ἄξων* introduces a purpose belonging to *οὗτος*: ‘and, intending to take me home’. *ἄξων* is the nom. masc. sg. fut. act. participle of *ἄγω*, with object *μ᾿* (*με*); *οἴκαδ᾿* is elided *οἴκαδε*, whose suffix already marks motion homeward. Thus *riducemi a ca* is spread across the participle and *ὁδηγεῖ με νῦν* ‘he now guides me’ (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ὁδηγέω*). *νῦν* is supplied; *διὰ τῆς ὁδοῦ ταύτης*, with *διά* + genitive for the route, follows *per questo calle*.

> 55 Ed egli a me: “Se tu segui tua stella,  
> 56 non puoi fallire al glorioso porto,  
> 57 se ben m’ accorsi nella vita bella;  

And he to me: “If thou follow thy star, thou canst not fail of glorious haven, if I discerned rightly in the fair life;

> 55 Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπε· “Τῷ σῷ παρακολουθῶν ἄστρῳ  
> (And he said: “Following your star,)
>
> 56 „ Ἀσφαλῶς εἰς ἔνδοξον λιμέν᾽ ἀφίξῃ,  
> („ You shall surely reach a glorious harbour,)
>
> 57 „ Εἰ καλῶς ἔγνων ἐν τῷ καλλίμῳ βίῳ.  
> („ If I discerned rightly in the fair life.)

- *ὁ δ᾽ εἶπε* ‘and he said’ supplies the verb missing in *Ed egli a me* and omits the recipient. *τῷ σῷ παρακολουθῶν ἄστρῳ* ‘following your star’ renders *Se tu segui tua stella* by a circumstantial participle with conditional force, not a finite *if*-clause. *παρακολουθῶν* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *παρακολουθέω*) agrees with the unexpressed ‘you’; the verb governs the dative, and the participle comes between the possessive and its noun.
- *ἀσφαλῶς … ἀφίξῃ* ‘you will surely arrive’ reverses the negative formulation *non puoi fallire*: an adverb of certainty and an affirmative future replace the modal verb and infinitive. *ἀφίξῃ* is 2nd sg. fut. mid. ind. of *ἀφικνέομαι*, with *εἰς* + accusative for the destination; *λιμέν᾽* is elided *λιμένα*, acc. sg. of *λιμήν*, qualified by *ἔνδοξον*.
- The second Italian *se*-clause, unlike the first, remains finite: *εἰ καλῶς ἔγνων* ‘if I judged rightly’, with *εἰ* and the indicative referring to a past judgment. *ἔγνων* is 1st sg. root aor. act. ind. of *γιγνώσκω*; no reflexive pronoun is needed for *m’accorsi*. *ἐν τῷ καλλίμῳ βίῳ* closely follows *nella vita bella*, choosing *βίος* rather than the *ζωή* of line 49 and the poetic adjective *κάλλιμος* ‘fair’ in attributive position.

## Lines 58–90 — Enmity Foretold and Gratitude Avowed

> 58 e s' io non fossi sì per tempo morto,  
> 59 veggendo il cielo a te così benigno,  
> 60 dato t' avrei all' opera conforto.  

and if I had not died so early, seeing heaven so kind to thee, I would have cheered thee in the work.

> 58 „ Εἰ δ᾽ ὁ θάνατος μή μ᾽ ἀνήρπαζ᾽ ἀώρως,  
> („ And if death had not snatched me away untimely,)
>
> 59 „ Βλέπων τὸ θεῖον οὕτως εὐμενὲς ὄν σοι,  
> („ Seeing heaven thus favourable to you,)
>
> 60 „ Ἐδίδων ἄν σοι προτροπὴν ἐν τοῖς ἔργοις.  
> („ I would have given you encouragement in your works.)

- *Εἰ δ᾽ ὁ θάνατος μή μ᾽ ἀνήρπαζ᾽ ἀώρως* ‘and if death had not snatched me away untimely’ makes *ὁ θάνατος* the subject and the speaker its object, in place of *s’ io non fossi … morto*. *δ᾽* is postpositive *δέ*, *μ᾽* is accusative *με*, and *ἀνήρπαζ᾽* is elided *ἀνήρπαζε*, 3rd sg. imperfect active indicative of *ἀναρπάζω*, with the augment after the preverb. *ἀώρως* expresses prematurity without a separate word for *sì*.
- *Βλέπων* (nom. masc. sg. present active participle of *βλέπω*) renders *veggendo*: its subject is the speaker of the main clause, not *ὁ θάνατος*. *τὸ θεῖον* ‘the divine’ replaces *il cielo*; *οὕτως εὐμενὲς ὄν σοι* ‘being thus favourable to you’ has the predicate adjective and the participle of *εἰμί* both neuter accusative singular, agreeing with the object of perception. *σοι* goes with *εὐμενές*.
- *Ἐδίδων ἄν σοι προτροπήν* ‘I would give you encouragement’ retains the verb of giving and the abstract noun of *dato t’ avrei … conforto*. *ἐδίδων* is 1st sg. imperfect active indicative of *δίδωμι*, in *-ων* rather than Attic *-ουν*. Greek marks the unreal condition by *εἰ* with a past indicative, negated by *μή*, and a past indicative with *ἄν* in the result; the two imperfects do not reproduce the Italian compound perfect forms. *ἐν τοῖς ἔργοις* ‘in the works’ pluralizes *all’ opera*.

> 61 Ma quell' ingrato popolo maligno,  
> 62 che discese di Fiesole ab antico  
> 63 e tiene ancor del monte e del macigno,  

But that ungrateful, malignant people, who of old came down from Fiesole, and still savours of the mountain and the rock,

> 61 „ Ἀλλ᾽ ὁ κακόνους κἀγνώμων λαὸς κεῖνος,  
> („ But that malicious and ungrateful people,)
>
> 62 „ Ὃς κατάγετ᾽ ἀρχῆθεν ἐκ Φαισουλάνων,  
> („ Which descends from of old from Fiesole,)
>
> 63 „ Καὶ μετέχει τοῦ τ᾽ ὄρους καὶ πέτρας ἔτι,  
> („ And still partakes of the mountain and the rock,)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ ὁ κακόνους κἀγνώμων λαὸς κεῖνος* reverses the adjectives of *quell’ ingrato popolo maligno*. *ἀλλ᾽* is elided *ἀλλά*; *κακόνους* is a contracted adjective ‘ill-disposed’, and *κἀγνώμων* is crasis for *καὶ ἀγνώμων*. Both stand between article and noun; the demonstrative *κεῖνος*, the poetic short form of *ἐκεῖνος*, stands outside that group, at the end.
- *Ὃς κατάγετ᾽ ἀρχῆθεν ἐκ Φαισουλάνων* ‘which traces its descent from the Fiesolans from the beginning’ turns *discese* from a past movement into present descent. *ὅς* is nominative masculine singular after *λαός*; *κατάγετ᾽* is poetic elision of the ending *-αι* in *κατάγεται*, 3rd sg. present middle/passive indicative of *κατάγω*. *ἀρχῆθεν* has the adverbial suffix of source *-θεν*; *ἐκ* with the genitive plural names the people rather than the place *Fiesole*.
- *μετέχει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *μετέχω*) renders *tiene … del* by ‘has a share in’, governing both genitives. In *τοῦ τ᾽ ὄρους καὶ πέτρας*, *τ᾽* is elided connective *τε*, paired with *καί*; *πέτρας* ‘rock’ has no repeated article. *ἔτι* shifts *ancor* to the end.

> 64 ti si farà, per tuo ben far, nimico:  

will make itself an enemy to thee for thy good deeds;

> 64 „ Ἐχθρός, ὡς ἀγαθουργῷ, γενήσεταί σοι.  
> („ Will become your enemy, as to a doer of good.)

- *Ἐχθρός … γενήσεταί σοι* ‘will become an enemy to you’ resumes the singular *λαός*. The predicate nominative comes first, in place of the final *nimico*; *γενήσεται* is 3rd sg. future middle indicative of *γίγνομαι*, not a reflexive verb of making. The extra accent in *γενήσεταί* is due to enclitic *σοι*.
- *ὡς ἀγαθουργῷ* ‘as to a doer of good’ recasts *per tuo ben far*: the adjective used substantivally is dative masculine singular in apposition to *σοι*. *ὡς* presents the capacity in which the addressee incurs enmity, rather than directly introducing a cause.

> 65 ed è ragion: chè tra li lazzi sorbi  
> 66 si disconvien fruttare al dolce fico.  

and there is cause : for amongst the tart sorbtrees, it befits not the sweet fig to fructify.

> 65 „ Τοῦτο δὲ θέμις· ἐν ὄαις γὰρ ἀγρίαις  
> („ And this is fitting: for among wild sorb-trees)
>
> 66 „ Γλυκεῖαν συκῆν καρποφορεῖν οὐκ ἔνι.  
> („ It is not possible for the sweet fig-tree to bear fruit.)

- *Τοῦτο δὲ θέμις* ‘and this is fitting’ supplies demonstrative *τοῦτο* and leaves *ἐστί* understood with predicative *θέμις* ‘what is ordained or right’. Thus *è ragion* is rendered as a judgement of fitness. Postpositive *γάρ* gives the force of explanatory *chè*.
- *ἐν ὄαις … ἀγρίαις* ‘among wild sorb-trees’ uses *ἐν* with the dative plural of *ὄα* and renders *lazzi* by ‘wild’, not by an adjective of taste. *γλυκεῖαν συκῆν* ‘a sweet fig-tree’ is the accusative subject of *καρποφορεῖν*, present active infinitive of *καρποφορέω*. The final impersonal *οὐκ ἔνι* ‘it is not possible’ (*ἔνι* for *ἔνεστι*) changes *si disconvien* from impropriety to impossibility.

> 67 Vecchia fama nel mondo li chiama orbi,  
> 68 gente avara, invidiosa e superba:  

Old report on earth proclaims them blind, a people avaricious, envious, and proud

> 67 „ Αὐτοὺς καλεῖ τοῦ κόσμου παλαιὰ φήμη  
> („ Old report in the world calls them)
>
> 68 „ Τυφλούς, φειδωλούς, φθονερούς, ἀλαζόνας.  
> („ Blind, miserly, envious, arrogant.)

- *Αὐτοὺς καλεῖ τοῦ κόσμου παλαιὰ φήμη* begins with the object ‘them’ and puts the subject *παλαιὰ φήμη* ‘old report’ last. *τοῦ κόσμου* ‘of the world’ attaches to *φήμη* in place of locative *nel mondo*. *καλεῖ* is the contracted 3rd sg. present active indicative of *καλέω*.
- *Τυφλούς, φειδωλούς, φθονερούς, ἀλαζόνας* are four masculine accusative plurals predicated of *αὐτούς* after ‘calls’. Musurus omits *gente* and the final conjunction, making the descriptions grammatically parallel. *φειδωλούς* means ‘sparing, stingy’; *ἀλαζόνας*, from *ἀλαζών*, is more specifically ‘boasters’.

> 69 da' lor costumi fa che tu ti forbi.  

: look that thou cleanse thyself of their customs.

> 69 „ Φυλάσσου μὴ μολυνθῇς τοῖς τούτων τρόποις.  
> („ Beware lest you be polluted by their ways.)

- *Φυλάσσου μὴ μολυνθῇς* ‘guard yourself lest you be stained’ reverses the perspective of *fa che tu ti forbi*. *φυλάσσου* is 2nd sg. present middle imperative of *φυλάσσω* (with *-σσ-* rather than Attic *-ττ-*); *μή* after the verb of precaution introduces the event to be avoided. *μολυνθῇς* is 2nd sg. aorist passive subjunctive of *μολύνω*, not a reflexive verb of cleansing.
- *τοῖς τούτων τρόποις* ‘by the ways of these people’ is an instrumental dative, in place of separative *da’ lor costumi*. The possessive genitive plural *τούτων* is enclosed between article and noun.

> 70 La tua fortuna tanto onor ti serba,  
> 71 che l' una parte e l' altra avranno fame  
> 72 di te; ma lungi fia dal becco l' erba.  

Thy fortune reserves such honour for thee, that both parties will have a hunger of thee; but far from the goat shall be the grass.

> 70 „ Ταμιεύει σοι τόσην τιμὴν ἡ τύχη,  
> („ Fortune stores up so great honour for you,)
>
> 71 „ Ὥστε σε ποθήσουσι μερίδες ἄμφω·  
> („ That both parties shall hunger after you;)
>
> 72 „ Ἀλλὰ τοῦ ῥύγχους μακρὰν ἔστω τὸ χόρτον·  
> („ But far from the snout let the grass be;)

- *Ταμιεύει σοι τόσην τιμὴν ἡ τύχη* ‘fortune stores up so much honour for you’ postpones the subject and omits possessive *tua*. *ταμιεύει* is 3rd sg. present active indicative of *ταμιεύω*; *σοι* is dative of advantage. Feminine accusative *τόσην* agrees with *τιμήν* and anticipates the degree-and-result construction *τόσην … ὥστε*.
- *Ὥστε σε ποθήσουσι μερίδες ἄμφω* expresses the predicted result by *ὥστε* with the indicative. *ποθήσουσι* (3rd pl. future active of *ποθέω*) ‘will long for’ takes direct object *σε*, replacing *avranno fame di te*. *μερίδες* is nominative plural of *μερίς*, with postposed *ἄμφω* ‘both’: the latter is dual in form, but the noun and verb are plural.
- *Ἀλλὰ τοῦ ῥύγχους μακρὰν ἔστω τὸ χόρτον* ‘but let the grass be far from the snout’ takes *becco* as a projecting mouth rather than the animal named in the prose translation. *τοῦ ῥύγχους* is the contracted genitive singular of neuter *ῥύγχος*, with adverbial *μακράν*. *ἔστω*, 3rd sg. present imperative of *εἰμί*, turns future *fia* into an injunction. The text treats *τὸ χόρτον* as a neuter nominative subject, unlike usual classical masculine *ὁ χόρτος*.

> 73 Faccian le bestie Fiesolane strame  
> 74 di lor medesme, e non tocchin la pianta,  
> 75 s' alcuna surge ancora in lor letame,  

Let the beasts of Fiesole make litter of themselves, and not touch the plant, if any yet springs up amid their rankness,

> 73 „ Βοσκὴν εὑρέτω τὰ Φαισουλάνων κτήνη  
> („ Let the beasts of Fiesole find pasture)
>
> 74 „ Αὔτ᾽ ἀφ᾽ ἑαυτῶν· μὴ τοῦ φυτοῦ ψαυέσθω,  
> („ Of themselves; let the plant not be touched,)
>
> 75 „Εἴ τί γ᾽ ἔτ᾽ ἐν ταῖς αὐτῶν φύει κοπρίαις,  
> („If any still grows in their dunghill,)

- *Βοσκὴν εὑρέτω τὰ Φαισουλάνων κτήνη* ‘let the beasts of the Fiesolans find pasture’ interprets *strame* as food and substitutes finding for making. *βοσκήν* is the fronted object; *εὑρέτω* is 3rd sg. second aorist active imperative of *εὑρίσκω*, singular with neuter plural subject *τὰ … κτήνη*. Genitive plural *Φαισουλάνων* replaces the Italian adjective.
- *Αὔτ᾽ ἀφ᾽ ἑαυτῶν* ‘themselves from themselves’ expands *di lor medesme*. *αὔτ᾽* is elided intensive *αὐτά*, agreeing with *κτήνη*; *ἀφ᾽* is *ἀπό* elided and aspirated before rough-breathed *ἑαυτῶν*, the reflexive genitive plural.
- *μὴ τοῦ φυτοῦ ψαυέσθω* ‘let them not touch the plant’ retains the beasts as subject: *ψαυέσθω* is a 3rd sg. present middle imperative of *ψαύω*, again singular with a neuter plural. *μή* negates the command; genitive *τοῦ φυτοῦ* is the complement of touching, not the subject of a passive.
- *Εἴ τί γ᾽ ἔτ᾽ … φύει* ‘if indeed any still grows’ has indefinite neuter singular *τι* referring to *φυτόν*, for feminine *alcuna* referring to *pianta*. *γ᾽* is elided restrictive or emphatic *γε*, *ἔτ᾽* elided *ἔτι*. *φύει* is 3rd sg. present active indicative of *φύω*, used intransitively for *surge*. *ἐν ταῖς αὐτῶν … κοπρίαις* uses a possessive genitive and a concrete dative plural ‘in their dung-heaps’ for singular *in lor letame*.

> 76 in cui riviva la sementa santa  
> 77 di quei Roman, che vi rimaser, quando  
> 78 fu fatto il nido di malizia tanta.”  

in which the holy seed revives of those Romans who remained there, when the nest of so much malice [was made]."

> 76 „Ἐν ᾧ τὸ σεπτὸν ἀναζησάτω σπέρμα  
> („In which let the sacred seed revive)
>
> 77 „Τῶν ἐκεῖ που μεινάντων Ῥωμαίων, ὅτε  
> („Of the Romans who remained thereabout, when)
>
> 78 „Ὁ κακίας φωλεὸς οὗτος ἠγέρθη.”—  
> („This den of wickedness was raised.”—)

- *Ἐν ᾧ* ‘in which’ has a neuter dative singular relative referring to the plant. *τὸ σεπτὸν … σπέρμα* ‘the revered seed’ encloses *ἀναζησάτω*, 3rd sg. aorist active imperative of *ἀναζάω*. The Italian relative subjunctive *riviva* thus becomes an express wish or command, ‘let … come to life again’.
- *Τῶν ἐκεῖ που μεινάντων Ῥωμαίων* ‘of the Romans who stayed somewhere there’ depends on *σπέρμα*. Instead of *di quei Roman, che vi rimaser*, Musurus uses article and attributive participle without a demonstrative or finite relative clause. *μεινάντων* is genitive masculine plural of the aorist active participle of *μένω*; enclitic *που* qualifies *ἐκεῖ*.
- *ὅτε … ἠγέρθη* ‘when … was raised’ keeps the temporal clause and the passive: *ἠγέρθη* is 3rd sg. aorist passive indicative of *ἐγείρω*, with temporal augment. *ὁ κακίας φωλεὸς οὗτος* is ‘this den of wickedness’: *κακίας* is a dependent genitive, *φωλεός* a lair rather than a nest, and postnominal *οὗτος* points to the den instead of measuring the malice as *tanta* does.

> 79 “Se fosse tutto pieno il mio dimando,”  
> 80 risposi lui “voi non sareste ancora  
> 81 dell' umana natura posto in bando:  

“Were my desire all fulfilled,” I answered him, “you had not yet been banished from human nature

> 79 —“Εἴπερ ποτ᾽ εἰσηκούεθ᾽ ἡ δέησίς μου,”  
> (—“If ever my prayer had been heard,”)
>
> 80 Ἀπεκρινάμην, “οὐκ ἂν ἔξορος εἴης  
> (I answered, “you would not be banished)
>
> 81 „Ἐκ τῆς φύσεως τῆς ἀνθρωπίνης ἔτι·  
> („From human nature yet;)

- *Εἴπερ ποτ᾽ εἰσηκούεθ᾽ ἡ δέησίς μου* ‘if indeed my petition were ever heard’ renders fulfilment as a hearing. *εἴπερ* strengthens *εἰ*; *ποτ᾽* is elided indefinite *ποτε* ‘ever’, not a rendering of the degree word *tutto*. *εἰσηκούεθ᾽* stands for *εἰσηκούετο*, 3rd sg. imperfect passive indicative of *εἰσακούω*, augmented after *εἰσ-*; the final vowel is elided and *τ* becomes *θ* before *ἡ*. Nominative *ἡ δέησις* is the subject, *μου* its possessive genitive.
- *Ἀπεκρινάμην* ‘I answered’ is 1st sg. aorist middle indicative of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, without a dative answering to *lui*. *οὐκ ἂν ἔξορος εἴης* ‘you would not be an exile’ replaces *sareste … posto in bando* with a predicate adjective and *εἴης*, 2nd sg. present optative of *εἰμί*. The singular replaces polite plural *voi*. This potential optative with *ἄν*, following an imperfect indicative condition, differs from the regular Attic unreal result with *ἄν* and a past indicative.
- *ἐκ τῆς φύσεως τῆς ἀνθρωπίνης* makes the separation explicit by *ἐκ* with the genitive; the repeated article puts the adjective in the second attributive position. Final *ἔτι*, read with *οὐκ*, gives *non … ancora* ‘not yet’.

> 82 chè in la mente m' è fitta, ed or mi accora,  
> 83 la cara e buona imagine paterna  
> 84 di voi, quando nel mondo ad ora ad ora  
> 85 m' insegnavate come l' uom s' eterna;  

: for in my memory is fixed, and now goes to my heart, the dear [and] kind, paternal image of you, when in the world, hour by hour, you taught me how man makes himself eternal;

> 82 „Ἐν μνήμῃ γάρ ἐστί μοι κεχαραγμένη,  
> („For in my memory is graven,)
>
> 83 „Καὶ θέλγει μ᾽ ἡ σὴ πατρική τε καὶ φίλη  
> („And charms me, your fatherly and dear)
>
> 84 „Εἰκών, ὅτε διδάσκων ἦς μ᾽ ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ  
> („Image, when you were teaching me in the world)
>
> 85 „Ἔναγχος, πῶς ἄνθρωπος ἀθανατοῦται.  
> („Lately, how man becomes immortal.)

- *Ἐν μνήμῃ γάρ ἐστί μοι κεχαραγμένη* ‘for it stands engraved in memory for me’ retains explanatory *chè* as postpositive *γάρ*. *μοι* is dative of the person concerned. *κεχαραγμένη*, the reduplicated perfect middle/passive participle of *χαράσσω*, is nominative feminine singular in anticipation of *εἰκών*; with *ἐστί* it expresses the abiding result of engraving, for *è fitta*.
- *Καὶ θέλγει μ᾽ ἡ σὴ πατρική τε καὶ φίλη εἰκών* ‘and your fatherly and dear image charms me’ changes the emotional verb *mi accora* to *θέλγει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *θέλγω*), with elided accusative *μ᾽*. There is no separate *or* ‘now’. Attributive *σή* replaces *di voi*; *τε καί* links *πατρική* and *φίλη*, both nominative feminine singular, with no distinct adjective for *buona*. The subject noun itself is delayed until line 84.
- *ὅτε διδάσκων ἦς μ᾽ ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ* ‘when you were teaching me in the world’ uses a periphrastic imperfect for *m’ insegnavate*: *διδάσκων* is a nominative masculine singular present active participle, *ἦς* the 2nd sg. imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*. *ἔναγχος* ‘lately’ locates the teaching in the recent past instead of expressing the recurrence of *ad ora ad ora*.
- *πῶς ἄνθρωπος ἀθανατοῦται* retains the indirect question after ‘teach’. Anarthrous *ἄνθρωπος* is generic; *ἀθανατοῦται* is the contracted 3rd sg. present middle/passive indicative of *ἀθανατόω* ‘make immortal’. It allows ‘becomes immortal’ without a separate reflexive pronoun for *s’ eterna*.

> 86 e quant' io l' abbia in grado, mentre io vivo  
> 87 convien che nella mia lingua si scerna.  

and whilst I live, beseems my tongue should shew what gratitude I have for it.

> 86 „Ὅτι δ᾽ ἔσομ᾽ εὐγνώμων ἐφ᾽ ὅσον ζήσω,  
> („And that I shall be grateful as long as I live,)
>
> 87 „Ἡ γλῶσσά μου τοῖς πᾶσι διατρανώσει.  
> („My tongue shall make clear to all.)

- *Ὅτι δ᾽ ἔσομ᾽ εὐγνώμων* ‘and that I shall be grateful’ turns *quant’ io l’ abbia in grado* from a question of degree into a statement of fact, fronted as the object of *διατρανώσει*. *δ᾽* is elided *δέ*; *ἔσομ᾽* is poetic elision of *ἔσομαι*, 1st sg. future middle indicative of *εἰμί*. Predicate *εὐγνώμων* has no expressed complement corresponding to *l’*.
- *ἐφ᾽ ὅσον ζήσω* ‘for as long as I shall live’ has *ἐπί* elided and aspirated before *ὅσον*, accusative of extent with ‘time’ understood. *ζήσω* is 1st sg. future active indicative of *ζάω*, in place of present *vivo*.
- *Ἡ γλῶσσά μου τοῖς πᾶσι διατρανώσει* ‘my tongue will make it clear to all’ makes the tongue the nominative agent instead of the medium *nella mia lingua*. *διατρανώσει*, 3rd sg. future active indicative of *διατρανόω*, replaces the impersonal necessity and subjunctive of *convien che … si scerna* with a prediction. The dative plural audience *τοῖς πᾶσι* is supplied.

> 88 Ciò che narrate di mio corso scrivo,  
> 89 e serbolo a chiosar con altro testo  
> 90 a donna che saprà, se a lei arrivo.  

That which you relate about my course, I write; and keep it, with another text, for a Lady to comment, who will be able if I get to her.

> 88 „Ὃ περὶ τύχης τῆς ἐμῆς λέγεις, γράψας  
> („What you tell of my fortune, having written it down,)
>
> 89 „Δείξω σὺν ἄλλαις προῤῥήσεσι Δεσποίνῃ  
> („I will show with other prophecies to a Lady)
>
> 90 „Σοφῇ πρὸς ἐξήγησιν, ἢν αὐτὴν ἴδω·  
> („Wise to expound it, if I see her;)

- *Ὃ περὶ τύχης τῆς ἐμῆς λέγεις* ‘what you say about my fortune’ uses a free relative: *ὅ* is neuter accusative singular, object of *λέγεις*, and the whole clause supplies what is written down. *λέγεις* is 2nd sg. present active indicative for polite plural *narrate*. *περί* with the genitive marks the topic; *τύχη* interprets *corso* as fortune, and *τῆς ἐμῆς* is the postposed articular possessive.
- *γράψας δείξω* ‘having written it down, I shall show it’ subordinates present *scrivo* as a nominative masculine singular aorist active participle of *γράφω*, denoting action prior to *δείξω*, 1st sg. future active indicative of *δείκνυμι*. Showing the writing replaces keeping it (*serbolo*); its object need not be repeated.
- *σὺν ἄλλαις προῤῥήσεσι* ‘with other predictions’ interprets singular *altro testo* as plural utterances about the future. *σύν* governs the dative plural of *πρόρρησις*; the spelling *ῤῥ* marks the doubled rho at the compound boundary. *Δεσποίνῃ σοφῇ* ‘to a wise lady’ is the dative recipient of *δείξω*; the adjective takes the place of relative *che saprà*.
- *πρὸς ἐξήγησιν* ‘for interpretation’ uses *πρός* with an accusative action noun for the purpose expressed by *a chiosar*. *ἢν αὐτὴν ἴδω* ‘if I see her’ replaces reaching her with seeing her: *ἤν* is a contracted form of *ἐάν* (*εἰ* with *ἄν*), introducing an open future condition with *ἴδω*, 1st sg. second aorist active subjunctive of *ὁράω*. The person who was the goal of *a lei arrivo* is now the direct object *αὐτήν*.

## Lines 91–124 — Fortune, Brunetto’s Companions, and His Farewell

> 91 Tanto vogl'io che vi sia manifesto,  
> 92 pur che mia coscienza non mi garra,  
> 93 che alla fortuna, come vuol, son presto.  

Thus much I would have you know; so conscience chide me not, I am prepared for Fortune as she wills.

> 91 „Τοσοῦτο μόνον γνωστὸν εἶναί σοι θέλω·  
> („This much alone I wish to be known to you:)
>
> 92 „Ἐφ᾽ ὅσον τὸ συνειδὸς οὐδέν μ᾽ ἐλέγχει,  
> („So long as my conscience reproaches me with nothing,)
>
> 93 „Πρὸς πᾶν θέλημα τῆς Τύχης εἶμ᾽ ἑτοῖμος.  
> („For every will of Fortune I am ready.)

- *Τοσοῦτο μόνον γνωστὸν εἶναί σοι θέλω* ‘this much alone I wish to be known to you’ keeps the emphatic *Tanto* first, adds *μόνον* ‘alone’, and puts the verb of wishing last. For *vogl’io che vi sia manifesto*, Greek has *θέλω* with an accusative and infinitive: *τοσοῦτο* is the neuter singular subject of *εἶναι*, *γνωστόν* its predicate adjective, and *σοι* the dative of the person to whom the matter is known. There is no separate pronoun for *io*.
- *Ἐφ᾽ ὅσον* (*ἐπί* with elision and aspiration before the rough breathing) ‘insofar as, so long as’ renders the proviso *pur che*. Instead of the Italian subjunctive *garra*, *ἐλέγχει* is present active indicative, 3rd singular of *ἐλέγχω*. *Τὸ συνειδός* ‘that which is conscious’, hence ‘conscience’, is the substantivized neuter singular perfect active participle of *σύνοιδα*. The possessive *mia* disappears; *μ᾽* is elided *με*, the person reproached, and *οὐδέν* specifies the charge negatively, ‘in nothing’.
- Greek resumes with an independent assertion where Italian has the explanatory *che … son presto*. *Πρὸς πᾶν θέλημα τῆς Τύχης* ‘for every wish of Fortune’ turns *alla fortuna, come vuol* into a single prepositional phrase: *πρός* takes the accusative, and Fortune becomes the genitive dependent on *θέλημα*. *Εἶμ᾽ ἑτοῖμος* is *εἰμί* with its final vowel elided, followed by a masculine singular predicate adjective.

> 94 Non è nuova agli orecchi miei tale arra:  
> 95 però giri fortuna la sua rota,  
> 96 come le piace, e il villan la sua marra."  

Not new to my ears is such earnest: therefore, let Fortune turn her wheel as pleases her, and the boor his mattock."

> 94 „Οὐ καινὴ τοῖς ὠσί μου πρόῤῥησις αὕτη·  
> („Not new to my ears is this prophecy;)
>
> 95 „ Ἡ Τύχη τὸν ἑαυτῆς τροχὸν στρεφέτω,  
> („ Let Fortune turn her wheel,)
>
> 96 „ Ὡς ἂν ἀρέσκῃ, κἀγρότης τὴν σκαπάνην.”  
> („ As it may please her, and the peasant his mattock.”)

- *Οὐ καινὴ τοῖς ὠσί μου πρόῤῥησις αὕτη* ‘not new to my ears [is] this prediction’ follows the Italian predicate-first order, but omits the copula. *Τοῖς ὠσί* is the irregular dative plural of *οὖς*, with possessive *μου*. *Αὕτη* points to this particular utterance rather than qualifying it as *tale*; *πρόῤῥησις* interprets *arra* ‘pledge, earnest’ as a foretelling. The two breathings on the doubled rho belong to an older spelling convention.
- *Ἡ Τύχη τὸν ἑαυτῆς τροχὸν στρεφέτω* puts subject and object before the verb and leaves *però* untranslated. *Στρεφέτω* is the 3rd singular present active imperative of *στρέφω*, answering to jussive *giri*; the feminine reflexive genitive *ἑαυτῆς* explicitly makes the wheel the subject’s own.
- *Ὡς ἂν ἀρέσκῃ* ‘however it may please’ uses modal *ἄν* with the 3rd singular present active subjunctive of *ἀρέσκω* for indefinite manner; the equivalent of *le* is understood. *Κἀγρότης* is crasis of *καί* and *ἀγρότης*. The nominative introduces a second subject for the understood imperative, with *τὴν σκαπάνην* as its accusative object; Italian *sua* is not repeated in Greek.

> 97 Lo mio maestro allora in sulla gota  
> 98 destra si volse indietro, e riguardommi;  
> 99 poi disse: "Bene ascolta chi la nota."  

Thereupon my Master turned backward on his right, and looked at me, then said: "He listens well who notes it."

> 97 Οὑμὸς Προστάτης πρὸς τὰ δεξιὰ τότε  
> (My Protector then toward the right)
>
> 98 Στραφεὶς ὀπίσω προσέβλεψέ με, κᾆτα  
> (Turning backward looked upon me, and then)
>
> 99 Εἶπεν· “ Ἀκούει καλῶς ὁ μνημονεύων.”  
> (Said: “He listens well who remembers it.”)

- *Οὑμὸς Προστάτης* ‘my protector’ has crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*; *Προστάτης* gives *maestro* the sense of one who stands before or guards another. *Πρὸς τὰ δεξιά* ‘towards the right’ uses the substantivized neuter plural for direction, dropping the concrete *gota*. *Τότε* retains *allora*, but follows the directional phrase.
- *Στραφεὶς ὀπίσω προσέβλεψέ με* subordinates the first of the coordinated Italian actions: *στραφείς*, nominative masculine singular of the aorist passive participle of *στρέφω*, has intransitive force, ‘having turned’. *Προσέβλεψε* is the 3rd singular aorist active indicative of *προσβλέπω*, with the augment after the prefix; enclitic *με* accounts for the additional accent in *προσέβλεψέ με*.
- *Κᾆτα* is crasis of *καὶ εἶτα*, ‘and then’; *εἶπεν* is the 3rd singular second aorist active indicative corresponding to *λέγω*. *Ἀκούει καλῶς* reverses *Bene ascolta*, while *ὁ μνημονεύων*, an articular present active participle, nominative masculine singular of *μνημονεύω*, replaces the free relative *chi la nota*. It means ‘the one who remembers’, interpreting noting as retaining in memory; there is no expressed object for *la*.

> 100 Nè per tanto di men parlando vommi  
> 101 con ser Brunetto, e dimando chi sono  
> 102 li suoi compagni più noti e più sommi.  

Not the less I go on speaking with Ser Brunetto, and ask who are the most noted and highest of his companions.

> 100 Οὐχ ἧττον ὅμως ὁμιλῶν διετέλουν  
> (Nevertheless I continued conversing)
>
> 101 Μετὰ τοῦ Κὺρ Βρουνέτου, καὐτὸν ἠρώτων,  
> (With Ser Brunetto, and asked him)
>
> 102 Τίνες πρώτιστοι τῶν συμπασχόντων ἦσαν.  
> (Who were the foremost of his fellow-sufferers.)

- *Οὐχ ἧττον ὅμως* ‘nonetheless, however’ concentrates *Nè per tanto di men* into a negated comparative adverb reinforced by *ὅμως*. *Ὁμιλῶν διετέλουν* replaces *parlando vommi*: *διατελέω* with a supplementary present participle means ‘continue doing’, without a verb of motion. *Ὁμιλῶν* is nominative masculine singular; *διετέλουν* is here 1st singular imperfect active indicative, with internal augment. The Greek changes the narrative present to a durative past.
- *Μετὰ τοῦ Κὺρ Βρουνέτου* retains the construction ‘with’ a person, using *μετά* with the genitive. *Κύρ* is a later abbreviated honorific corresponding to *ser*, left indeclinable between the article and the inflected name. *Καὐτόν* is crasis of *καὶ αὐτόν*: Greek supplies the person questioned as an accusative object. *Ἠρώτων* is here 1st singular imperfect active indicative of *ἐρωτάω*, in the same tense as *διετέλουν*.
- *Τίνες πρώτιστοι τῶν συμπασχόντων ἦσαν* retains the interrogative in the indirect question but uses imperfect *ἦσαν* ‘were’ after the past reporting verb, against *chi sono*. Predicate *πρώτιστοι* ‘foremost’ does duty for both *più noti* and *più sommi*. The partitive genitive *τῶν συμπασχόντων*, an articular present active participle of *συμπάσχω*, defines the companions as ‘those suffering together’; *suoi* is left implicit.

> 103 Ed egli a me: "Saper d'alcuno è buono;  
> 104 degli altri fia laudabile tacerci,  
> 105 chè il tempo saria corto a tanto suono.  

And he to me: "It is good to know of some; of the rest it will be laudable that we keep silence, as the time would be too short for so much talk.

> 103 Ὁ δ’ εἶπε· “ Καλὸν εἰδέναι τινὰς τούτων,  
> (And he said: “It is good to know some of these,)
>
> 104 „ Βέλτιον δὲ τῇ σιγῇ παρελθεῖν ἄλλους·  
> („ But better to pass over the others in silence;)
>
> 105 „ Μακρὸν γὰρ ἂν εἴη μοι λέγειν σοι πάντας.  
> („ For it would be too long for me to tell you all.)

- *Ὁ δ’ εἶπε* ‘and he said’ uses pronominal *ὁ* with postpositive, elided *δέ*. Greek supplies the verb omitted in *Ed egli a me*, but drops the recipient. *Καλὸν εἰδέναι τινὰς τούτων* has an understood copula: the infinitive is its subject and neuter *καλόν* its predicate. *Εἰδέναι* is the perfect active infinitive of *οἶδα*, present in meaning; indefinite *τινάς* is its accusative plural object, with partitive genitive *τούτων*. Thus *saper d’alcuno* becomes ‘to know some of these’.
- *Βέλτιον δὲ τῇ σιγῇ παρελθεῖν ἄλλους* continues the verbless construction, with comparative *βέλτιον* ‘better’ instead of future *fia laudabile*. *Παρελθεῖν*, second aorist active infinitive of *παρέρχομαι*, takes *ἄλλους* as the people passed over and *τῇ σιγῇ* as a dative of manner. ‘To pass others over in silence’ replaces *degli altri … tacerci*, without the first-person reference.
- *Μακρὸν γὰρ ἂν εἴη μοι λέγειν σοι πάντας* reverses the formulation: not ‘the time would be short’, but ‘to recount them all to you would be long for me’. Postpositive *γάρ* marks the reason; *ἄν* with *εἴη*, 3rd singular present optative of *εἰμί*, expresses potentiality. *Μακρόν* is neuter because the subject is the infinitive phrase. *Μοι* gives the speaker’s standpoint, *σοι* the addressee, and masculine accusative *πάντας* the persons to be recounted.

> 106 In somma sappi, che tutti fur cherci  
> 107 e letterati grandi e di gran fama,  
> 108 d'un peccato medesmo al mondo lerci.  

In brief, know that all were clerks, and great scholars, and of great renown; by one same crime on earth defiled.

> 106 „ Ἴσθι συνόλως, ὅτι κληρικοὶ πάντες  
> („ Know in brief, that clerics all)
>
> 107 „ Ἦσαν καὶ φιλόλογοι μεγάλης φήμης,  
> („ Were, and men of letters of great renown,)
>
> 108 „ Ὁμοίῳ μύσει μολυνθέντες ἐν κόσμῳ.  
> („ Defiled by the same abomination in the world.)

- *Ἴσθι συνόλως, ὅτι* moves *sappi* before *In somma*. *Ἴσθι* is here the 2nd singular perfect active imperative of *οἶδα*, ‘know’, not the identically spelled imperative ‘be’. *Ὅτι* introduces the statement to be known.
- *Κληρικοὶ πάντες ἦσαν καὶ φιλόλογοι μεγάλης φήμης* puts predicate *κληρικοί* before subject *πάντες* and delays *ἦσαν* to the next line. *Φιλόλογοι* renders *letterati* as ‘men of letters’; the genitive of quality *μεγάλης φήμης* keeps *di gran fama*, but there is no separate adjective for *grandi* qualifying the scholars themselves.
- *Ὁμοίῳ μύσει μολυνθέντες ἐν κόσμῳ* turns adjectival *lerci* into a participle agreeing with *πάντες*: *μολυνθέντες* is nominative masculine plural, aorist passive of *μολύνω*, ‘having been defiled’. *Μύσει*, dative singular of neuter *μύσος*, gives the cause or means of defilement. *Ὁμοίῳ* means ‘like, of the same kind’, rather than stressing numerical identity as *d’un peccato medesmo* does. *Ἐν κόσμῳ* retains the locative force of *al mondo*.

> 109 Priscian sen va con quella turba grama,  

Priscian goes with that wretched crowd,

> 109 „ Πρισκιανὸς οἴχεται μετὰ τῶν ἄλλων,  
> („ Priscian goes along with the rest,)

- *Πρισκιανὸς οἴχεται* uses the present middle indicative, 3rd singular of *οἴχομαι*, ‘go away, be gone’, for *sen va*. *Μετὰ τῶν ἄλλων* ‘with the others’ replaces the collective *con quella turba*: neither the demonstrative nor the epithet *grama* is retained.

> 110 e Francesco d'Accorso; anco vedervi,  
> 111 s'avessi avuto di tal tigna brama,  

and Francesco d'Accorso; also, if thou hadst had any longing for such scurf,

> 110 „ Καὶ Φραγκίσκος Δακόρσος· εἰ δέ γε θέαν  
> („ And Francesco d'Accorso; and if a sight)
>
> 111 „ Τοιαύτης λέπρας ἐπόθεις, ἐξῆν ἄν σοι  
> („ Of such leprosy you desired, you might have)

- *Καὶ Φραγκίσκος Δακόρσος* adds a second nominative subject to *οἴχεται*. The Italian *d’* is incorporated into *Δακόρσος*, which has a Greek nominative ending rather than a separate construction of descent or possession.
- *Εἰ δέ γε θέαν τοιαύτης λέπρας ἐπόθεις* puts the condition before the infinitive of seeing, whereas Italian anticipates it with *anco vedervi*. Postpositive *δέ* marks the transition and *γε* lends emphasis. *Ἐπόθεις*, 2nd singular imperfect active indicative of *ποθέω*, makes *avessi avuto … brama* a simple past verb of desire. Its accusative object *θέαν* ‘a sight’ is supplied, and *τοιαύτης λέπρας* depends on that noun: ‘a sight of such leprosy’, rather than a longing for the *tigna* itself.
- The consequence already begins in 111: *ἐξῆν ἄν σοι* ‘it would have been possible for you’. Impersonal *ἐξῆν* is the imperfect of *ἔξεστι*; the past indicative with *ἄν* answers the past indicative in the unreal condition. *Σοι* names the person for whom the opportunity existed; the following infinitive specifies the act.

> 112 colui potei che dal servo de' servi  
> 113 fu trasmutato d'Arno in Bacchiglione,  
> 114 dove lasciò li mal protesi nervi.  

thou mightest have seen him there, who by the Servant of servants was translated from the Arno to the Bacchiglione, where he left his ill-strained nerves.

> 112 „ Ἰδεῖν ἐκεῖνον, ὃν ὁ δοῦλος τῶν δούλων  
> („ Seen him whom the servant of the servants)
>
> 113 „ Εἰς Τογισόνον μετήνεγκεν ἐξ Ἄρνου,  
> („ Transferred to the Togisonus from the Arno,)
>
> 114 „ Ἔνθ’ αὐτοῦ κατέλιπεν ἔκλυτα νεῦρα.  
> („ Where he left his unstrung sinews.)

- *Ἰδεῖν ἐκεῖνον* completes *ἐξῆν ἄν σοι*. *Ἰδεῖν* is the second aorist active infinitive belonging to *ὁράω*; its demonstrative accusative object answers to *colui*. Greek thus brings *veder-* and *colui* together, without a separate locative for *-vi*.
- *Ὃν ὁ δοῦλος τῶν δούλων … μετήνεγκεν* changes *che dal servo de’ servi fu trasmutato* from passive to active. The relative *ὅν* is accusative object, the former agent is nominative subject, and *τῶν δούλων* is genitive plural dependent on *δοῦλος*. *Μετήνεγκεν* is 3rd singular aorist active indicative of *μεταφέρω*, with internal augment and the irregular aorist stem of *φέρω*.
- *Εἰς Τογισόνον … ἐξ Ἄρνου* reverses the Italian sequence of source and destination, placing *εἰς* with the accusative before the verb and *ἐξ* with the genitive after it. *Τογισόνον* substitutes an ancient river name for *Bacchiglione*.
- *Ἔνθ’* is *ἔνθα* ‘where’, elided before *αὐτοῦ*. The genitive *αὐτοῦ* gives the possessor of *νεῦρα*. *Κατέλιπεν* is 3rd singular second aorist active indicative of *καταλείπω*, with internal augment. *Ἔκλυτα νεῦρα* is neuter accusative plural: the verbal adjective means ‘loosened, unstrung’, interpreting rather than reproducing *mal protesi*.

> 115 Di più direi; ma il venir e il sermone  
> 116 più lungo esser non può: però ch'io veggio  
> 117 là surger nuovo fummo dal sabbione.  

I would say more, but my going and my speech must not be longer: for there I see new smoke arising from the great sand.

> 115 „ Πλείον᾽ ἂν εἶπον· ἀλλ᾽ οὐ δύναμαι λέγειν  
> („ More would I say; but I cannot speak)
>
> 116 „ Ἢ μένειν μακρότερον· νέον γὰρ βλέπω  
> („ Or stay longer; for I see a new)
>
> 117 „ Ἀτμὸν ἀνυψούμενον ἐκ ψαμαθῶνος.  
> („ Vapour rising up from the sandy plain.)

- *Πλείον᾽ ἂν εἶπον* ‘I would have said more’ has elided *πλείονα*, neuter accusative plural used substantivally, and *ἄν* with the 1st singular second aorist active indicative *εἶπον*. The past unreal form differs from the Italian present conditional *direi*.
- *Ἀλλ᾽ οὐ δύναμαι λέγειν ἢ μένειν μακρότερον* turns *il venir e il sermone più lungo esser non può* into a personal construction. *Δύναμαι* is 1st singular present middle indicative, ‘I am able’, governing two present active infinitives. Speaking now comes first, staying replaces coming, and *ἤ* ‘or’ coordinates the alternatives under negation. *Μακρότερον* is a neuter comparative used adverbially of duration, not an adjective agreeing with either activity.
- *Νέον γὰρ βλέπω ἀτμὸν ἀνυψούμενον ἐκ ψαμαθῶνος* places *νέον* before causal *γάρ* and the verb, separating the adjective from *ἀτμόν*. For *veggio … surger nuovo fummo*, Greek uses a verb of perception with an accusative and a supplementary participle: *ἀνυψούμενον*, present middle/passive participle of *ἀνυψόω*, agrees with masculine *ἀτμόν*, ‘vapour rising’. *Ἐκ ψαμαθῶνος* gives the source, using the genitive of poetic *ψαμαθών* ‘sandy tract’; *là* is not separately rendered.

> 118 Gente vien con la quale esser non deggio;  

People are coming with whom I may not be;

> 118 „ Ἔρχετ᾽ ἀγέλη, μεθ᾽ ἧς μ᾽ οὐ δεῖ συνεῖναι·  
> („ A troop is coming, with which I must not be;)

- *Ἔρχετ᾽ ἀγέλη* puts the verb first: *ἔρχετ᾽* is elided *ἔρχεται*, 3rd singular present middle indicative of *ἔρχομαι*. The feminine collective *ἀγέλη* ‘troop, herd’ preserves the grammatical singular of *gente*.
- *Μεθ᾽ ἧς* is *μετά* with elision and aspiration before the feminine genitive singular relative, ‘with which’. *Μ᾽ οὐ δεῖ συνεῖναι* replaces personal *esser non deggio* with impersonal *δεῖ*: elided *με* is the accusative subject of *συνεῖναι*, the present infinitive of *σύνειμι* ‘be together’. The compound reinforces the association already expressed by *μεθ᾽ ἧς*.

> 119 sieti raccomandato il mio 'Tesoro,'  
> 120 nel quale io vivo ancora; e più non cheggio."  

let my 'Treasure,' in which I still live, be commended to thee; and more I ask not."

> 119 „ Τὸν ἐμὸν συνιστῶ σοι Θησαυρόν, ἔνθα  
> („ To you I commend my Treasure, wherein)
>
> 120 „ Ἔτι ζῶν εἰμι, κοὐδὲν ἄλλ᾽ ἐξαιτοῦμαι.”  
> („ I am still alive, and I ask nothing else.”)

- *Τὸν ἐμὸν συνιστῶ σοι Θησαυρόν* makes passive *sieti raccomandato* a direct first-person act, ‘I commend’. *Συνιστῶ* is a contracted 1st singular present active indicative; *σοι* is its dative recipient. The possessive is attributive, but *τὸν ἐμόν* is separated from its masculine accusative noun *Θησαυρόν* by verb and pronoun.
- Relative adverb *ἔνθα* ‘where’ replaces *nel quale*. *Ἔτι ζῶν εἰμι* expands *vivo ancora* into present active participle *ζῶν* (nominative masculine singular of *ζάω*) with *εἰμι*, ‘I am still living’.
- *Κοὐδὲν ἄλλ᾽* is *καὶ οὐδὲν ἄλλο*, with crasis at the beginning and elision at the end. The neuter accusative phrase ‘nothing else’ replaces comparative *più* under negation; *ἐξαιτοῦμαι*, 1st singular contracted present middle indicative of *ἐξαιτέομαι*, gives *cheggio* the sense ‘ask for oneself’.

> 121 Poi si rivolse, e parve di coloro  
> 122 che corrono a Verona il drappo verde  
> 123 per la campagna; e parve di costoro  
> 124 quegli che vince e non colui che perde.  

Then he turned back, and seemed like one of those who run for the green cloth at Verona through the open field; and of them seemed he who gains, not he who loses.

> 121 Εἶτ᾽ ἐτράπετο κᾤχετο καθὰ ᾽κεῖνοι,  
> (Then he turned and sped away like those)
>
> 122 Οἳ δι᾽ ὕφασμα πράσινον ἐν Βηρώνῃ  
> (Who for a green cloth at Verona)
>
> 123 Στάδιον τρέχουσ᾽· ἐδόκει δ᾽ ἐν τῷ δρόμῳ  
> (Run the race-course; and in the running he seemed)
>
> 124 Εἶναι νικητής, ἀλλ᾽ οὐ νενικημένος.  
> (To be the winner, and not the vanquished.)

- *Εἶτ᾽ ἐτράπετο κᾤχετο* ‘then he turned and went off’ begins with elided *εἶτα*. *Ἐτράπετο* is the 3rd singular second aorist middle indicative of *τρέπω*, corresponding to reflexive *si rivolse*; *κᾤχετο* is crasis of *καὶ ᾤχετο*, the latter the imperfect of *οἴχομαι*. Greek supplies a verb of departure for the first *parve*, reserving the actual verb of seeming for line 123.
- *Καθὰ ᾽κεῖνοι, οἳ … τρέχουσ᾽* ‘just as those who run’ replaces *di coloro che* with a direct comparison. *᾽Κεῖνοι* shows prodelision, loss of the initial vowel of *ἐκεῖνοι* after *καθά*. Relative *οἵ* is nominative masculine plural, subject of *τρέχουσ᾽*, elided *τρέχουσι*, 3rd plural present active indicative of *τρέχω*.
- *Δι᾽ ὕφασμα πράσινον* takes *διά* with the accusative in the sense ‘for the sake of’, making explicit what the runners run for in *corrono … il drappo verde*. *Ἐν Βηρώνῃ* renders the place with *ἐν* and a Hellenized dative singular. *Στάδιον* is an accusative of course or extent with *τρέχουσι*: Greek substitutes a racecourse for the Italian route *per la campagna*.
- *Ἐδόκει δ᾽ ἐν τῷ δρόμῳ εἶναι νικητής* changes the second *parve* to the imperfect active indicative of *δοκέω* with complementary *εἶναι*. ‘In the race’ replaces partitive *di costoro*. *Νικητής* is a predicate nominative because the runner is also the understood subject of the infinitive. Against the parallel active clauses *quegli che vince e non colui che perde*, Greek sets an agent noun against *οὐ νενικημένος*, the negated perfect middle/passive participle of *νικάω*, nominative masculine singular: ‘a victor, but not one who has been defeated’.

## Summary — From Clause to Participle

Musurus often preserves Dante’s order of ideas while changing which action receives a finite verb. In lines 97–98, for example, *στραφείς* ‘having turned’ subordinates *si volse* to the act of looking: the sequence is the same, but only the second verb remains finite. Participles can also carry the force of a whole clause. At line 55 *παρακολουθῶν* ‘following’ stands for the condition *se tu segui*, yet two lines later the second *se* is closely matched by *εἰ* with the indicative. Nor does the compression always run in one direction: *m’insegnavate* expands into *διδάσκων ἦς* ‘you were teaching’, a present participle with the imperfect of *εἰμί*.

The particles often show just how closely the argument is followed. Explanatory *chè* regularly becomes postpositive *γάρ*, and the progression from *tanto* to *che* has a close counterpart in *τόσην* ‘so great’ followed by *ὥστε* with a future indicative. Elsewhere a particle changes the relation: adversative *ἀλλά* ‘but’ replaces concessive *tutto che*, while *δέ* simply coordinates where *sì che* marks a result. The case constructions can be equally economical. Where *περὶ τύχης* ‘about fortune’ follows the topical sense of *di mio corso*, the instrumental dative *τοῖς τούτων τρόποις* ‘by these people’s ways’ accompanies a reversal: instead of cleansing oneself of their customs, one guards against being stained by them.

Several of the most revealing choices concern words that Dante leaves to be supplied. The feminine forms *αὐτῆς* and *αὐτήν* in lines 26–29 look back to *ψυχή* ‘soul’, rather than taking their gender from Brunetto. In the same passage, Musurus supplies *τὴν χεῖρα* ‘the hand’ for *la mia*, where Carlyle understands ‘my face’. Elsewhere he makes an implication explicit: *altro testo* becomes *ἄλλαις προῤῥήσεσι* ‘other predictions’, preserving the reference but losing the image of a second text to be glossed. The closing contrast shows the distinction in miniature. Musurus keeps Dante’s antithesis, but the parallel verbs *vince* and *perde* give way to *νικητής* ‘victor’ and *νενικημένος* ‘one who has been defeated’: an agent noun on one side, a participle expressing the result on the other.
