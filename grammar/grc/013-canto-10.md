# Inferno 10

## Lines 1–21 — The Open Tombs and the Unspoken Wish

> 1 Ora sen va per un secreto calle,  
> 2 tra il muro della terra e li martiri,  
> 3 lo mio maestro, ed io dopo le spalle.  

Now by a [secret] path, between the city-wall and the torments, my Master goes on, and I behind him.

> 1 Νῦν διὰ στενοῦ προβαίνει μονοπάτου  
> (Now along a narrow path advances)
>
> 2 Μέσον τοῦ τείχους τῆς γῆς καὶ μαρτυρίων  
> (Between the wall of the land and the torments)
>
> 3 Οὑμὸς Προστάτης· ἐγὼ δὲ κατὰ νῶτα·  
> (My Master; and I behind his back:)

- *Νῦν* answers to *Ora*, and *προβαίνει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *προβαίνω*) gives *sen va* as ‘goes forward’, without separate equivalents for the Italian pronominal particles. *διὰ στενοῦ … μονοπάτου* has *διά* with the genitive for the route traversed. The verb comes between adjective and noun; *στενοῦ* means ‘narrow’, not *secreto* ‘secret, secluded’.
- *Μέσον* is used adverbially with two genitives, *τοῦ τείχους … καὶ μαρτυρίων*, ‘between the wall … and torments’. Within the first genitive phrase, *τῆς γῆς* depends on *τείχους* (gen. sg. of neuter *τεῖχος*): Greek takes over the wording of *muro della terra*, but *γῆ* says ‘land’ rather than ‘city’. The anarthrous gen. pl. *μαρτυρίων*, from *μαρτύριον*, corresponds to *li martiri* in the sense ‘sufferings’.
- As in Italian, the subject is delayed until line 3. *Οὑμὸς* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, ‘my’; *Προστάτης* is a protector or leader rather than literally a teacher (*maestro*). *ἐγὼ δέ* balances the two subjects, ‘and I in turn’, with the verb of motion understood. *κατὰ νῶτα* (*κατά* with the accusative plural) is idiomatic ‘behind, at the back’: like *dopo le spalle*, it need not express the possessor.

> 4 “O virtù somma, che per gli empi giri  
> 5 mi volvi,” cominciai, “come a te piace;  

“O Virtue supreme! who through the impious circles thus wheelest me, as it pleases thee,” I began;

> 4 “Ὦ πρωτεύων ἀρετῇ, σύ μ᾽ ἄγεις,” εἶπον,  
> (“O foremost in virtue, you lead me,” I said,)
>
> 5 “Διὰ δυσσεβῶν, ὡς ἀρέσκει σοι, κύκλων.  
> (“Through impious circles, as pleases you.)

- *Ὦ πρωτεύων ἀρετῇ* changes the abstract address *O virtù somma* into a personal one, ‘O you who rank first in virtue’. *πρωτεύων* is the present active participle of *πρωτεύω*, masculine singular, used in address; *ἀρετῇ* is a dative of respect. Instead of continuing with relative *che*, Greek makes an independent assertion with emphatic nominative *σύ*: *σύ μ᾽ ἄγεις*, ‘you lead me’. *μ᾽* is elided accusative *με*; *ἄγεις* (2nd sg. present active indicative of *ἄγω*) drops the suggestion of turning in *mi volvi*.
- *εἶπον* (1st sg. second aorist active indicative, the suppletive aorist of *λέγω*) replaces *cominciai* ‘I began’ with ‘I said’. The reporting verb now interrupts the Greek between *ἄγεις* and its route phrase, *διὰ δυσσεβῶν … κύκλων*, ‘through impious circles’. Within that genitive phrase *ὡς ἀρέσκει σοι* reproduces *come a te piace*: *ἀρέσκει* is impersonal, with dative *σοι* of the person pleased.

> 6 parlami, e soddisfammi a’ miei desiri.  

“speak to me, and satisfy my wishes.

> 6 „ Λέγε μοι, καὶ τοὺς ἐμοὺς πλήρωσον πόθους.  
> („ Tell me, and fulfil my desires.)

- *Λέγε μοι* renders *parlami* with the 2nd sg. present active imperative of *λέγω* and an enclitic dative. *πλήρωσον*, by contrast, is the 2nd sg. aorist active imperative of *πληρόω*: after the invitation to speak, the fulfilment is asked for as a whole. Where *soddisfammi a’ miei desiri* marks both the person and the wishes, Greek makes *τοὺς ἐμοὺς … πόθους* the direct object, ‘fulfil my desires’; the possessive adjective between article and noun suffices to mark the person.

> 7 La gente, che per li sepolcri giace,  
> 8 potrebbesi veder? già son levati  
> 9 tutti i coperchi, e nessun guardia face.”  

Might those people, who lie within the sepulchres, be seen? the covers all are raised, and none keeps guard.”

> 7 „ Ἰδεῖν τοὺς ἐν τοῖς τάφοις τούτοις κειμένους  
> („ To see those who lie in these tombs)
>
> 8 „ Δύναταί τις; ἤδη γὰρ πάντ᾽ ἀπηρμένα  
> („ Can one? for already all lifted off)
>
> 9 „ Τὰ πώματ᾽ ἐστίν, οὐδεὶς δ᾽ αὐτοὺς φυλάσσει.”  
> („ Are the lids, and no one guards them.”)

- *Ἰδεῖν τοὺς ἐν τοῖς τάφοις τούτοις κειμένους δύναταί τις;* turns *La gente … potrebbesi veder?* into ‘Can someone see those lying in these tombs?’ The Italian conditional with *si* gives way to *δύναται* (3rd sg. present middle indicative of *δύναμαι*) with indefinite nominative *τις* and a fronted active infinitive, *ἰδεῖν* (second aorist of *ὁράω*). The extra accent in *δύναταί* is due to the following enclitic; there is no potential optative.
- Collective *la gente* and relative *che … giace* become a single substantival participial phrase: *τοὺς … κειμένους*, accusative masculine plural of the present middle-form participle of *κεῖμαι*, ‘those who lie’. *ἐν* takes the dative for position, and *τοῖς τάφοις τούτοις* adds a demonstrative to *li sepolcri*: *τούτοις* stands outside the article–noun group, the normal position for ‘these’.
- *ἤδη γάρ* makes the connection after the question explicitly explanatory, ‘for already’; *già* has no corresponding conjunction. *πάντ᾽ ἀπηρμένα τὰ πώματ᾽ ἐστίν* has elision of final *-α* in both *πάντα* and *πώματα*. *ἀπηρμένα* is the neuter plural perfect middle-passive participle of *ἀπαίρω*, ‘lift off’; with *ἐστίν* it expresses the resulting state, as does *son levati*. The singular verb with the neuter plural subject *τὰ πώματα* is regular Greek.
- *οὐδεὶς δ᾽ αὐτοὺς φυλάσσει* replaces *e nessun guardia face* with a transitive verb, ‘and no one guards them’. *δ᾽* is elided postpositive *δέ*. The supplied object *αὐτούς* is masculine plural: it can point back to the occupants or the tombs, not to neuter *πώματα*.

> 10 Ed egli a me: “Tutti saran serrati,  
> 11 quando di Josaffàt qui torneranno  
> 12 coi corpi, che lassù hanno lasciati.  

And he to me: “All shall be closed up, when, from Jehosaphat, they return here with the bodies which they have left above.

> 10 Ὁ δ᾽ ἔφη· “Πάντες ἔσοντ᾽ ἐγκεκλεισμένοι,  
> (And he said: “All shall be shut in,)
>
> 11 „ Ὅταν ἐξ Ἰωσαφὰτ ἔλθωσιν ὧδε  
> („ When from Jehosaphat they come hither)
>
> 12 „ Ἐν τοῖς σώμασιν, ἃ κατέλιπον ἄνω.  
> („ In the bodies which they left above.)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ ἔφη* supplies a verb for *Ed egli a me* but omits the addressee: *ὁ δέ* is the pronominal use of the article, ‘and he’; *ἔφη* is the augmented 3rd sg. imperfect active indicative of *φημί*, idiomatically ‘said’. *Πάντες ἔσοντ᾽ ἐγκεκλεισμένοι* renders *Tutti saran serrati* with elided *ἔσονται* (3rd pl. future of *εἰμί*) and the perfect middle-passive participle of *ἐγκλείω*, ‘shut in’. This periphrastic future perfect stresses the state that will obtain. *πάντες* and the participle are masculine plural; the Greek admits ‘all [the occupants] will be shut in’, not ‘all the lids’, which would require neuters.
- *Ὅταν … ἔλθωσιν* renders future indicative *quando … torneranno* by the regular prospective temporal construction, *ὅταν* (*ὅτε* with *ἄν*) and the subjunctive. *ἔλθωσιν* is 3rd pl. second aorist active subjunctive of *ἔρχομαι*: ‘when they come’, without an explicit ‘back’. *ἐξ* marks the point of departure before the indeclinable name *Ἰωσαφάτ*; directional *ὧδε*, ‘hither’, gives *qui*.
- *Ἐν τοῖς σώμασιν* interprets *coi corpi* as ‘in the bodies’ rather than ‘together with the bodies’. *ἃ* is neuter plural like its antecedent *σώμασιν*, but accusative as the object of *κατέλιπον*. The latter is here 3rd pl. second aorist active indicative of *καταλείπω*, with the augment after the preverb: Greek uses a simple past for *hanno lasciati*. *ἄνω* gives *lassù* as ‘above’.

> 13 Suo cimitero da questa parte hanno  
> 14 con Epicuro tutti i suoi seguaci,  
> 15 che l’ anima col corpo morta fanno.  

In this part are entombed with Epicurus all his followers, who make the soul die with the body.

> 13 „ Ἐντεῦθέν ἐστιν ὁ νεκρὼν Ἐπικούρου  
> („ On this side is the burial-place of Epicurus)
>
> 14 „ Καὶ τῶν ὀπαδῶν αὐτοῦ τῶν δοξαζόντων,  
> („ And of his followers who hold the doctrine,)
>
> 15 „Ὅτι σὺν τῷ σώματι χἠ ψυχὴ θνήσκει.  
> („That with the body the soul also dies.)

- *Ἐντεῦθέν ἐστιν ὁ νεκρὼν Ἐπικούρου* recasts the possessive *Suo cimitero … hanno* as ‘on this side is the burial place of Epicurus’. *ἐντεῦθεν*, ordinarily ‘from here’ or ‘on this side’, serves for static *da questa parte*; its extra accent is due to enclitic *ἐστιν*. *ὁ νεκρών* is a nominative singular noun, not a genitive plural of *νεκρός*.
- *Ἐπικούρου καὶ τῶν ὀπαδῶν αὐτοῦ* coordinates two genitives dependent on *νεκρών*, ‘of Epicurus and of his followers’. Thus *con Epicuro* no longer modifies what the followers do, and *tutti* has no separate equivalent. The repeated article in *τῶν ὀπαδῶν αὐτοῦ τῶν δοξαζόντων* attaches an attributive participle to ‘followers’: *δοξαζόντων* is gen. masc. pl. present active of *δοξάζω*, here ‘hold as an opinion’.
- *Ὅτι … θνήσκει* states the content of that opinion instead of reproducing *l’anima col corpo morta fanno*, literally ‘make the soul dead with the body’. *θήνῃσκει* is not the spelling used: *θνήσκει* is 3rd sg. present active indicative of *θνήσκω*, with *ψυχή* as subject. *σὺν τῷ σώματι* uses the associative dative; *χἠ* is crasis of *καὶ ἡ*, with aspiration of the consonant before the rough breathing: ‘together with the body the soul **also** dies’.

> 16 Però alla dimanda che mi faci  
> 17 quinci entro soddisfatto sarai tosto,  
> 18 e al disio ancor, che tu mi taci.”  

Therefore to the question, which thou askest me, thou shalt soon have satisfaction here within; and also to the wish which thou holdest from me.”

> 16 „Ὡς πρὸς δ᾽ ὅ, τι πυνθάνῃ, σαφηνιεῖ σοι  
> („And as for what you ask, shall make clear to you)
>
> 17 „Ἓν τῶν μνημάτων τούτων αὐτίκα μάλα,  
> („One of these tombs immediately,)
>
> 18 „Καί σου τὸν πόθον, ὃν μοι κρύπτεις, πληρώσει.”  
> („And your desire, which you hide from me, it will fulfil.”)

- *Ὡς πρὸς δ᾽ ὅ, τι πυνθάνῃ* means ‘and as regards what you ask’. Postpositive, elided *δ᾽* marks a transition rather than the explicit inference of *Però*. *πρός* governs the whole free relative in place of *alla dimanda che mi faci*; *ὅ, τι* (neuter singular of indefinite relative *ὅστις*) is the accusative object within it, distinct from conjunction *ὅτι* in line 15. *πυνθάνῃ* is here 2nd sg. present middle indicative of *πυνθάνομαι*, ‘inquire’; the person asked is not expressed.
- *σαφηνιεῖ σοι ἓν τῶν μνημάτων τούτων* replaces *quinci entro soddisfatto sarai*: not ‘you will be satisfied here within’, but ‘one of these tombs will make [it] clear to you’. *σαφηνιεῖ* is the contracted future active indicative, 3rd sg., of *σαφηνίζω*; *σοι* is dative of the recipient. The postponed subject is neuter nominative *ἕν* with a partitive genitive, *τῶν μνημάτων τούτων*. Musurus thus interprets the local phrase by supplying a particular grammatical agent. *αὐτίκα μάλα* is the emphatic idiom ‘at once’, stronger than *tosto*.
- The same subject carries over to *πληρώσει* (3rd sg. future active indicative of *πληρόω*), which resumes the verb of line 6. Where Italian extends *soddisfatto sarai* to *al disio ancor*, Greek supplies this second finite verb with direct object *τὸν πόθον*; *σου* is a possessive genitive placed before the article–noun group. In *ὃν μοι κρύπτεις*, ‘which you conceal from me’, accusative *ὅν* refers to *πόθον*, and the dative of the person follows *che tu mi taci* closely. *κρύπτεις* makes concealment explicit where *taci* speaks of silence.

> 19 Ed io: “Buon Duca, non tegno nascosto  
> 20 a te mio cor, se non per dicer poco;  
> 21 e tu m’ hai non pur mo a ciò disposto.”  

And I : “Kind Guide, I do not keep my heart concealed from thee, except for brevity of speech, to which thou hast ere now disposed me.”

> 19 Κἀγώ· “Προστάτα, τὴν ἐμὴν οὔ σοι κρύπτω  
> (And I: “Master, I do not hide from you)
>
> 20 „Καρδίαν, εἰ μὴ θέλων λέγειν ὀλίγα.  
> („My heart, except through wishing to speak little.)
>
> 21 „Σὺ γὰρ οὐχ ἅπαξ ἐδίδαξάς με τοῦτο.”  
> („For not once only have you taught me this.”)

- *Κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*, matching verbless *Ed io*. *Προστάτα*, the vocative singular of *προστάτης*, repeats the designation of line 3 for *Duca*; *Buon* drops out. *τὴν ἐμὴν … καρδίαν*, feminine accusative singular, places the possessive adjective before the verb and delays the noun until the next line, unlike the contiguous *mio cor*.
- *οὔ σοι κρύπτω* directly echoes *μοι κρύπτεις*, now with the persons reversed: ‘I do not hide [it] from you’. *κρύπτω* is 1st sg. present active indicative; Italian *non tegno nascosto* uses a verb plus a predicative participle. The accent on *οὔ* is occasioned by enclitic *σοι*. *εἰ μή* retains the exception *se non*, but *θέλων* (nom. masc. sg. present active participle of *θέλω*, agreeing with the speaker) supplies a motive: ‘except through wishing to say little’. *λέγειν* is its present active infinitive, with neuter acc. pl. *ὀλίγα* as object, ‘few things’.
- *Σὺ γάρ* replaces *e tu* with an explicit reason, ‘for you’; the pronoun is emphatic, since the verb already marks the person. *οὐχ ἅπαξ* interprets *non pur mo* (‘not just now’) in terms of frequency, ‘not [just] once’; *οὐχ* is the form of the negative before a rough-breathed vowel. *ἐδίδαξάς με τοῦτο* replaces *m’hai … a ciò disposto* with ‘you taught me this’: *ἐδίδαξας* is 2nd sg. first aorist active indicative of *διδάσκω*, with initial augment and an extra accent before enclitic *με*. The two accusatives are the person taught and the thing taught.

## Lines 22–54 — Farinata’s Challenge and Dante’s Reply

> 22 “O Tosco, che per la città del foco  
> 23 vivo ten vai così parlando onesto,  
> 24 piacciati di ristare in questo loco.  

“O Tuscan! who through the city of fire goest alive, speaking thus decorously; may it please thee to stop in this place.

> 22 —“Ὦ Τοῦσκε, τὴν τοῦ πυρὸς διιὼν χώραν  
> (—“O Tuscan, who passing through the region of fire)
>
> 23 „Ζωὸς καὶ λέγων οὕτω σώφρονας λόγους,  
> („Alive and speaking such modest words,)
>
> 24 „Ὤφελες ἐν τῷ τόπῳ τῷδ᾽ ἀναμεῖναι.  
> („May you be pleased to tarry in this place.)

- *Ὦ Τοῦσκε* keeps the direct address of *O Tosco*, with *Τοῦσκε* the masculine vocative singular of *Τοῦσκος*. The Greek has no relative corresponding to *che*: *διιών* (present active participle of *δίειμι*, ‘go through’) and *λέγων* (present active participle of *λέγω*) are nominative masculine singular, referring to the understood ‘you’ of the main verb. They turn *ten vai* and *parlando* into coordinated descriptions of the addressee.
- *τὴν τοῦ πυρὸς διιὼν χώραν* gives ‘passing through the region of fire’. *διιών* takes an accusative of the space traversed; its position separates *χώραν* from its article and the attributive genitive *τοῦ πυρός*. *χώρα* is less specifically urban than *città*. *Ζωός*, the poetic uncontracted counterpart of *ζῶς*, is nominative masculine singular and predicative, like *vivo*.
- *λέγων οὕτω σώφρονας λόγους* gives *parlando onesto* an expressed object: ‘speaking such temperate words’. *σώφρονας* (accusative masculine plural of *σώφρων*) qualifies *λόγους*, not the speaker; *οὕτω* conveys the degree expressed by *così*.
- *Ὤφελες … ἀναμεῖναι* is literally ‘would that you would stay’: *ὤφελες* is the second singular of the second aorist active indicative of *ὀφείλω*, used in a wish with the aorist active infinitive of *ἀναμένω*. The Italian *piacciati di ristare* appeals to the hearer’s pleasure; the Greek voices the speaker’s wish. *ἐν τῷ τόπῳ τῷδ᾽* retains the static locative, with *τῷδ᾽* elided from the demonstrative *τῷδε* before *ἀναμεῖναι*.

> 25 La tua loquela ti fa manifesto  
> 26 di quella nobil patria natio,  
> 27 alla qual forse fui troppo molesto.”  

Thy speech clearly shews thee a native of that noble country, which perhaps I vexed too much.”

> 25 „Ἡ λαλιά σου ποιεῖ σε δῆλον ὄντα  
> („Your speech makes you manifestly)
>
> 26 „Πολίτην τῆς εὐγενοῦς κείνης πατρίδος,  
> („A citizen of that noble fatherland,)
>
> 27 „ᾟ λίαν ὀλέθριος ἴσως ὑπῆρξα.” —  
> („To which perhaps I was too destructive.”—)

- *Ἡ λαλιά σου* renders *La tua loquela* with a noun for manner of speech and a postposed possessive genitive. *ποιεῖ σε δῆλον ὄντα πολίτην* follows *ti fa manifesto … natio* quite closely: *ποιεῖ* (third singular present active indicative of *ποιέω*) has *σε* as object and *δῆλον* as its predicate, while *ὄντα* (accusative masculine singular present participle of *εἰμί*) agrees with *σε* and takes the predicate noun *πολίτην*. The sense is ‘makes it plain that you are a citizen’; *πολίτην* denotes civic belonging rather than expressly saying ‘born there’.
- *τῆς εὐγενοῦς κείνης πατρίδος* depends on *πολίτην*. *εὐγενοῦς* is the genitive feminine singular of *εὐγενής*; *κείνης* is the poetic short form of *ἐκείνης*, corresponding to *quella*.
- *ᾟ* is the dative feminine singular relative, referring to *πατρίδος* but taking its case from its own clause: ‘destructive **to** which’. *λίαν ὀλέθριος* makes *troppo molesto* stronger, ‘excessively destructive’. *ἴσως* retains *forse*; *ὑπῆρξα*, first singular aorist active indicative of *ὑπάρχω*, serves as a copula with the nominative predicate *ὀλέθριος*.

> 28 Subitamente questo suono uscio  
> 29 d’ una dell’ arche: però m’ accostai,  
> 30 temendo, un poco più al duca mio.  

Suddenly this sound issued from one of the chests : whereat in fear I drew a little closer to my Guide.

> 28 Τὴν φωνὴν ταύτην ἐξερχομένην αἴφνης  
> (This voice coming forth suddenly)
>
> 29 Ἤκουσ᾽ ἐξ ἑνὸς τῶν τάφων, καὶ δὴ μᾶλλον  
> (I heard from one of the tombs, and indeed closer)
>
> 30 Φοβηθεὶς προσήγγισα τοὐμοῦ Προστάτου.  
> (In fear I drew near to my Master.)

- *Τὴν φωνὴν ταύτην ἐξερχομένην αἴφνης Ἤκουσ᾽* changes the subject of *questo suono uscio* into the object of an added verb of perception: ‘this voice I heard suddenly coming out’. *Ἤκουσ᾽* is elided *ἤκουσα*, first singular aorist active indicative of *ἀκούω*; *ἐξερχομένην*, accusative feminine singular present middle participle of *ἐξέρχομαι*, agrees with *φωνήν* and presents the movement as it was heard.
- *ἐξ ἑνὸς τῶν τάφων* distinguishes source (*ἐξ* with the genitive masculine singular of *εἷς*) from the partitive genitive *τῶν τάφων*. *τάφων* makes *arche* explicitly ‘tombs’. *καὶ δή* links the reaction rather than expressly marking the inference in *però*.
- *Φοβηθείς*, nominative masculine singular aorist passive participle of *φοβέω*, is intransitive in sense, ‘having taken fright’, where *temendo* represents the fear as accompanying the movement. *μᾶλλον … προσήγγισα* gives ‘I drew nearer’, without a separate *un poco*. *προσήγγισα* is the internally augmented first singular aorist active indicative of *προσεγγίζω*. The goal appears in the genitive *τοὐμοῦ Προστάτου*, a less usual construction than a dative with a verb of approach; *τοὐμοῦ* is crasis of *τοῦ ἐμοῦ*. *Προστάτης* gives *duca* the sense ‘protector’.

> 31 Ed ei mi disse: “Volgiti; che fai?  

And he said to me : “ Turn thee round ; what art thou doing?

> 31 Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπέ μοι· “Στράφητι· τί πράττεις ἤδη;  
> (And he said to me: “Turn round; what are you doing now?)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπέ μοι* uses pronominal *ὁ* with postpositive *δέ* (elided) for *Ed ei*. *εἶπε* is third singular second aorist active indicative, serving as the aorist of *λέγω*; the added accent in *εἶπέ* is due to enclitic *μοι*, dative of the person addressed.
- *Στράφητι* is the second singular second aorist passive imperative of *στρέφω*, intransitive ‘turn round’, for reflexive *Volgiti*. *τί πράττεις* keeps the direct question, with the interrogative as object of the second singular present active indicative of *πράττω*. *ἤδη* supplies ‘now’.

> 32 vedi là Farinata, che s’ è dritto;  
> 33 dalla cintola in su tutto il vedrai.”  

lo there Farinata ! who has raised himself erect ; from the girdle upwards thou shalt see him all.”

> 32 „Ἴδ᾽ ἐκεῖ Φαρινάτην ἀνορθωθέντα·  
> („Look there at Farinata risen upright:)
>
> 33 „Ἐκ ζώνης ἄνω νῦν ὅλον αὐτὸν ὄψει.”  
> („From the girdle upward now you shall see him all.”)

- *Ἴδ᾽* is elided *ἴδε*, second singular second aorist active imperative of the verb ‘see’. *ἐκεῖ Φαρινάτην ἀνορθωθέντα* renders *là Farinata, che s’ è dritto* by an accusative name and agreeing participle rather than a relative clause. *ἀνορθωθέντα* (aorist passive participle of *ἀνορθόω*) gives the resulting posture, ‘raised erect’, without the Italian reflexive.
- *Ἐκ ζώνης ἄνω* reproduces the starting point and direction of *dalla cintola in su*: ‘from girdle upwards’. *νῦν* is supplied. *ὅλον αὐτὸν ὄψει* keeps the emphasis of *tutto il vedrai*, with *ὅλον* predicative, ‘him in his entirety’. *ὄψει* is second singular future middle indicative of *ὁράω*, active in meaning.

> 34 Io avea già il mio viso nel suo fitto;  

Already I had fixed my look on his

> 34 Ταῖς τούτου δὴ τὰς ἐμὰς προσήλωσ᾽ ὄψεις·  
> (Upon his, then, I riveted my eyes;)

- *Ταῖς τούτου … τὰς ἐμὰς … ὄψεις* means ‘upon his [eyes] my own eyes’: the dative plural article stands with an understood *ὄψεσι*, recoverable from the expressed accusative plural *ὄψεις*. *τούτου* is a possessive genitive, literally ‘of this man’, whereas *ἐμάς* is an agreeing possessive adjective. The Greek makes explicit and plural the visual organs suggested by *il mio viso nel suo* and puts the point of fixation first.
- *προσήλωσ᾽* is elided *προσήλωσα*, first singular aorist active indicative of *προσηλόω*, literally ‘nail to’, with accusative of the thing fixed and dative of that to which it is fixed. The aorist replaces the pluperfect *avea … fitto*; connective or emphatic *δή* does not mark anteriority as explicitly as *già*.

> 35 ed ei s’ ergea col petto e colla fronte,  
> 36 come avesse lo inferno in gran dispitto;  

; and he rose upright with breast and countenance, as if he entertained great scorn of Hell

> 35 Ὁ δ᾽ ἀνηγέρθη τῷ στήθει καὶ μετώπῳ,  
> (And he rose up with his chest and brow,)
>
> 36 Ὡς ἂν εἰ περιφρονῶν πολὺ τὸν ᾍδην.  
> (As if greatly scorning Hell.)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ ἀνηγέρθη* renders imperfect reflexive *ed ei s’ ergea* by the third singular aorist passive indicative of *ἀνεγείρω*, ‘he rose up’. *τῷ στήθει καὶ μετώπῳ* uses datives of respect, ‘upright in chest and brow’; *μετώπῳ* specifically retains the ‘forehead’ of *fronte*.
- *Ὡς ἂν εἰ* introduces the hypothetical comparison in *come avesse*. Instead of completing it with a finite verb, Musurus gives *περιφρονῶν*, nominative masculine singular present active participle of *περιφρονέω*, agreeing with *ὁ*. The wording combines the force of ‘as if’ with that of *ὡς* and a participle, ‘as one scorning’. *πολύ* is adverbial, transferring *gran* from the noun *dispitto* to the act of scorning; *τὸν ᾍδην* is its accusative object.

> 37 e le animose man del duca e pronte  
> 38 mi pinser tra le sepolture a lui,  
> 39 dicendo: “Le parole tue sien conte.”  

; and the bold and ready hands of my Guide pushed me amongst the sepultures to him, saying : “ Let thy words be numbered.”

> 37 Εὐθὺς δ᾽ αἱ τοῦ Προστάτου πρόθυμοι χεῖρες  
> (And straightway the Master's ready hands)
>
> 38 Ὤθησάν με πρὸς αὐτὸν διὰ τῶν τάφων,  
> (Pushed me toward him among the tombs,)
>
> 39 Λέγοντος· “Σαφεῖς ἔστωσαν οἱ σοὶ λόγοι.”  
> (Saying: “Let your words be clear.”)

- *Εὐθὺς δ᾽* adds ‘and straightway’. *αἱ τοῦ Προστάτου πρόθυμοι χεῖρες* retains the hands as grammatical subject, with the possessor inside the article–noun group. *πρόθυμοι* (‘eager, ready’, nominative feminine plural of the two-ending adjective *πρόθυμος*) does duty for *animose … e pronte*; *mio* is not separately rendered.
- *Ὤθησάν με* is third plural aorist active indicative of *ὠθέω* with an enclitic accusative pronoun. The Greek puts the destination *πρὸς αὐτόν* before the route *διὰ τῶν τάφων* (‘through the tombs’), reversing *tra le sepolture a lui*.
- *Λέγοντος*, genitive masculine singular present active participle of *λέγω*, picks up the genitive *τοῦ Προστάτου*: it is the possessor, not the nominative *χεῖρες*, who speaks. This case agreement resolves the loose attachment of *dicendo*. *Σαφεῖς ἔστωσαν οἱ σοὶ λόγοι* puts the predicate adjective first: ‘clear let your words be’. *ἔστωσαν* is the third plural present imperative of *εἰμί* for jussive *sien*. Musurus thus interprets *conte* as a direction to speak distinctly, not literally to count words.

> 40 Com’ io al piè della sua tomba fui,  
> 41 guardommi un poco, e poi quasi sdegnoso  
> 42 mi dimandò: “Chi fur li maggior tui?”  

[When] I was at the foot of his tomb, he looked at me a little ; and then, almost contemptuously, he asked me : “ Who were thy ancestors?”

> 40 Ἐπεὶ δ᾽ ἀφίγμην πρὸς τοὺς πόδας τοῦ τάφου,  
> (And when I had come to the foot of the tomb,)
>
> 41 Εἶδέ με μικρόν, καὶ σχεδὸν ὑπερόπτως  
> (He looked at me a little, and almost disdainfully)
>
> 42 Ἠρώτα· “Τίνες ἦσαν οἱ σοὶ γενάρχαι;”  
> (Asked: “Who were your ancestors?”)

- *Ἐπεὶ δ᾽ ἀφίγμην* takes *Com’ … fui* temporally, ‘when I had arrived’. *ἀφίγμην* is first singular pluperfect middle indicative of *ἀφικνέομαι* (perfect *ἀφῖγμαι*), not its aorist *ἀφικόμην*. *πρὸς τοὺς πόδας τοῦ τάφου* substitutes a directional accusative and plural ‘feet’ for *al piè*; the possessive *sua* is unexpressed.
- *Εἶδέ με μικρόν* gives ‘he looked at me a little’, with *εἶδε* the third singular second aorist active indicative of ‘see’, and *μικρόν* an adverbial neuter accusative. *καὶ σχεδὸν ὑπερόπτως* makes predicative *quasi sdegnoso* adverbial, ‘and almost disdainfully’; *poi* has no distinct equivalent.
- *Ἠρώτα* is third singular imperfect active indicative of *ἐρωτάω*, contracted from *ἠρώταε*, for the single past *dimandò*; the addressee is not expressed again. *Τίνες ἦσαν οἱ σοὶ γενάρχαι;* keeps the interrogative predicate *τίνες*, plural imperfect *ἦσαν*, and definite subject, with masculine nominative plural *γενάρχαι* (‘founders of a line’) for substantival *maggior*.

> 43 Io, ch’ era d’ ubbedir disideroso,  
> 44 non gliel celai, ma tutto gliel’ apersi:  

I, being desirous to obey, concealed it not ; but opened the whole to him

> 43 Ἐγὼ δὲ ποθῶν εὐπείθειαν δεικνύναι  
> (And I, eager to show obedience,)
>
> 44 Οὐδὲν ὑπέκρυψ᾽, ἀλλ᾽ εἶπον αὐτῷ πάντα.  
> (Concealed nothing, but told him all.)

- *Ἐγὼ δὲ ποθῶν* replaces *Io, ch’ era … disideroso* with a nominative masculine singular present active participle of *ποθέω* attached to emphatic *ἐγώ*. *εὐπείθειαν δεικνύναι* expands *d’ ubbedir* into ‘to show obedience’: an abstract accusative noun is the object of the complementary present active infinitive of *δείκνυμι*.
- *Οὐδὲν ὑπέκρυψ᾽* (‘I concealed nothing’) makes the negative the object instead of reproducing the pronouns of *non gliel celai*. *ὑπέκρυψ᾽* is elided *ὑπέκρυψα*, first singular aorist active indicative of *ὑποκρύπτω*. *ἀλλ᾽ εἶπον αὐτῷ πάντα* abandons the metaphor of *tutto gliel’ apersi*: ‘but I told him everything’, with second aorist active *εἶπον*, dative recipient, and neuter plural object.

> 45 ond’ ei levò le ciglia un poco in soso;  

: whereupon he raised his brows a little

> 45 Ὁ δ᾽ ὑψώσας τὰς ὄψεις ὀλίγον ἄνω,  
> (And he, lifting his eyes a little upward,)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ ὑψώσας* begins a construction completed by *Εἶπεν* in 46: ‘and he, having raised …, said’. *ὑψώσας* is nominative masculine singular aorist active participle of *ὑψόω*. The Italian coordinates successive finite verbs (*levò … poi disse*); Greek subordinates the prior action, with *δέ* less explicitly consequential than *ond’*.
- *τὰς ὄψεις* gives ‘his eyes’ or ‘his gaze’ rather than *le ciglia*. Adverbial *ὀλίγον* supplies the measure and *ἄνω* the direction of *un poco in soso*.

> 46 poi disse: “Fieramente furo avversi  
> 47 a me ed a’ miei primi, ed a mia parte,  
> 48 sì che per due fiate gli dispersi.”  

; then he said : “ Fiercely adverse were they to me, and to my progenitors, and to my party ; so that twice I scattered them.”

> 46 Εἶπεν· “Ἐχθροὶ τρομεροὶ βεβαίως ἦσαν  
> (Said: “Formidable enemies indeed were they)
>
> 47 „Ἐμοί, μερίδι τ᾽ ἐμῇ κἀμοῖς γενάρχαις·  
> („To me, to my party, and to my ancestors;)
>
> 48 „Ἀλλὰ δὶς αὐτοὺς διεσκέδασα πάντας.”  
> („But twice I scattered them all.”)

- *Εἶπεν* is third singular second aorist active indicative, with movable *-ν*. *Ἐχθροὶ τρομεροὶ βεβαίως ἦσαν* turns *Fieramente furo avversi* into ‘formidable enemies indeed they were’: *τρομεροί* qualifies the predicate *ἐχθροί*, while *βεβαίως* adds an assurance not separately expressed in Italian. The copula is imperfect, not a Greek aorist.
- *Ἐμοί, μερίδι τ᾽ ἐμῇ κἀμοῖς γενάρχαις* retains three datives dependent on ‘enemies’, but interchanges party and ancestors. *μερίδι* (dative singular of *μερίς*, ‘portion, faction’) is qualified by *ἐμῇ*. *τ᾽* is elided enclitic *τε*, paired with *καί* in *κἀμοῖς*, crasis for *καὶ ἐμοῖς*. Both *ἐμοῖς* and *γενάρχαις* are dative masculine plural.
- *Ἀλλὰ δὶς αὐτοὺς διεσκέδασα πάντας* gives an adversative independent clause, ‘but twice I scattered them all’, instead of the consequence introduced by *sì che*. *δίς* condenses *per due fiate*; *διεσκέδασα* is the internally augmented first singular aorist active indicative of *διασκεδάννυμι*. *πάντας*, agreeing with *αὐτούς*, adds explicit totality.

> 49 “S’ ei fur cacciati, ei tornar d’ ogni parte,”  
> 50 risposi io lui, “l’ una e l’ altra fiata;  
> 51 ma i vostri non appreser ben quell’ arte.”  

“ If they were driven forth, they returned from every quarter, both times,” I answered him ; “ but yours have not rightly learnt that art.”

> 49 — “Εἰ δὶς ἐδιώχθησαν, δὶς ἐπανῆλθον,”  
> (—“If twice they were driven out, twice they returned,”)
>
> 50 Ἀπεκρίθην, “πάντοθεν συναγερθέντες·  
> (I answered, “gathered together from every quarter;)
>
> 51 „Ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἔμαθον οἱ σοὶ τὴν τέχνην κείνην.” —  
> („But yours did not learn that art.”—)

- *Εἰ δὶς ἐδιώχθησαν, δὶς ἐπανῆλθον* balances protasis and apodosis by moving the force of *l’ una e l’ altra fiata* into a repeated *δίς*. *εἰ* with the indicative takes the past event as the premise of the reply, not as counterfactual. *ἐδιώχθησαν* is third plural aorist passive indicative of *διώκω*; *ἐπανῆλθον* is third plural second aorist active indicative of *ἐπανέρχομαι*.
- *Ἀπεκρίθην*, first singular aorist in passive form of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, has active sense, ‘I answered’; *lui* is left implicit. After the interruption, *πάντοθεν συναγερθέντες* resumes the subject of *ἐπανῆλθον*: ‘having gathered together from every side’. *πάντοθεν* contains the source sense of *d’ ogni parte*; *συναγερθέντες*, nominative masculine plural aorist passive participle of *συναγείρω*, adds a gathering where the Italian qualifies the return directly.
- *Ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἔμαθον οἱ σοὶ τὴν τέχνην κείνην* retains the substantival possessive *i vostri* as *οἱ σοί*, ‘yours’, following the third plural second aorist active indicative of *μανθάνω*. The negation falls on learning itself; there is no adverb for *ben*. Demonstrative *κείνην* (for *ἐκείνην*) stands after the articular noun in the normal demonstrative position.

> 52 Allor surse alla vista scoperchiata  
> 53 un’ ombra lungo questa infino al mento;  
> 54 credo che s’ era in ginocchie levata.  

Then, beside him, there rose a shadow, visible to the chin ; it had raised itself, I think, upon its knees.

> 52 Τότ᾽ ἠγέρθη πρὸς τἄκρα τἀνοικτοῦ τάφου  
> (Then there rose at the rim of the open tomb)
>
> 53 Σκιὰ τῆς ἄλλης ἐγγὺς μέχρις ὑπήνης,  
> (A shade beside the other, as far as the chin,)
>
> 54 Ἀναστᾶσ᾽, ὡς ᾠόμην, ἐπὶ γονάτων.  
> (Having risen, as I thought, upon its knees.)

- *Τότ᾽ ἠγέρθη* retains the initial time adverb and delays the subject until 53, like *Allor surse … un’ ombra*. *Τότ᾽* is elided *τότε*; *ἠγέρθη* is third singular aorist passive indicative of *ἐγείρω*, intransitive in sense. *πρὸς τἄκρα τἀνοικτοῦ τάφου* takes *alla vista scoperchiata* spatially, ‘at the edges of the open tomb’. *τἄκρα* combines *τὰ ἄκρα* by crasis; *τἀνοικτοῦ* likewise fuses article and adjective, with *ἀνοικτοῦ* genitive masculine singular agreeing with the supplied *τάφου*.
- *Σκιὰ τῆς ἄλλης ἐγγύς* renders *un’ ombra lungo questa* by an anarthrous subject and a genitive governed by postposed *ἐγγύς*: ‘a shade near the other [shade]’. The feminine *ἄλλης* picks up *σκιά* rather than reproducing the demonstrative *questa*. *μέχρις ὑπήνης* marks a limit with the genitive; *ὑπήνη* denotes the beard, thus locating the limit of *infino al mento* by a neighboring feature.
- *Ἀναστᾶσ᾽* is elided *ἀναστᾶσα*, nominative feminine singular second aorist active participle of *ἀνίστημι*, intransitive and agreeing with *σκιά*: ‘having risen’. *ἐπὶ γονάτων* gives the supporting posture with *ἐπί* and the genitive plural of *γόνυ*. Italian makes *credo* the present main verb and subordinates the pluperfect *s’ era … levata* to it; Greek attaches the rising to the shade as a participle and inserts the judgment parenthetically as *ὡς ᾠόμην*, ‘as I thought’, with the augmented first singular imperfect middle indicative of *οἴομαι*.

## Lines 55–87 — A Father’s Question and Farinata’s Reply

> 55 D' intorno mi guardò, come talento  
> 56 avesse di veder s' altri era meco;  

It looked around me, as if it had a wish to see whether some one were with me;

> 55 Περιέβλεψε πέριξ, ὡς βουλομένη  
> (It looked round about, as if wishing)
>
> 56 Ἰδεῖν, εἰ τυχὸν σὺν ἐμοί τις ἦν ἄλλος.  
> (To see if perchance another was with me.)

- *Περιέβλεψε πέριξ* ‘it looked round about’ renders *D’ intorno mi guardò*. The aorist active indicative *περιέβλεψε* (3rd sg. of *περιβλέπω*, with the augment after the preverb) already expresses looking round; the adverb *πέριξ* reinforces it. There is no pronoun corresponding to *mi* in this first clause.
- *ὡς βουλομένη ἰδεῖν* ‘as if wishing to see’ replaces *come talento avesse di veder*: instead of a noun and an imperfect subjunctive, Greek has *ὡς* with a present middle participle of *βούλομαι*, presenting the wish as an inferred intention. *Βουλομένη* is nominative feminine singular, agreeing with the figure described, not a neuter corresponding to English “It.” Its complement *ἰδεῖν* is the second aorist active infinitive of *ὁράω*.
- *εἰ τυχὸν σὺν ἐμοί τις ἦν ἄλλος* is the indirect question dependent on *ἰδεῖν*. *Τυχόν*, originally the neuter singular second aorist participle of *τυγχάνω*, is adverbial, ‘perchance’. *Σὺν ἐμοί* ‘with me’ precedes the discontinuous subject *τις … ἄλλος* ‘someone else’ (*altri*); *ἦν*, the imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*, retains the indicative of *era* rather than shifting to an oblique optative.

> 57 ma poi che il sospicar fu tutto spento,  
> 58 piangendo disse : “Se per questo cieco  
> 59 carcere vai per altezza d' ingegno,  
> 60 mio figlio ov' è, e perchè non è teco?”  

but when all its expectation was quenched, it said, weeping: “If through this blind prison thou goest by height of genius, where is my son and why is he not with thee?”

> 57 Ἀλλ᾿ ὅτε πᾶσαν ἀπώλεσεν ἐλπίδα,  
> (But when it had lost all hope,)
>
> 58 Κλαίουσ᾿ εἶπεν· “Εἰ σὺ νῦν διέρχῃ ταύτην  
> (Weeping it said: “If you now pass through this)
>
> 59 „Τὴν ζοφώδη φυλακὴν τῇ σῇ σοφίᾳ,  
> („Gloomy prison by your wisdom,)
>
> 60 „Ποῦ ᾿στιν υἱός μου; καὶ σὺν σοὶ πῶς οὐκ ἔστι;”  
> („Where is my son? and why is he not with you?”)

- *Ἀλλ᾿ ὅτε πᾶσαν ἀπώλεσεν ἐλπίδα* ‘but when it lost all hope’ keeps the temporal clause of *ma poi che*, but makes the figure the subject of active *ἀπώλεσεν* (3rd sg. first aorist of *ἀπόλλυμι*). The substantivized infinitive *il sospicar*, subject of *fu tutto spento*, becomes the accusative object *πᾶσαν … ἐλπίδα*. *Ἀλλ᾿* is elided *ἀλλά*.
- *Κλαίουσ᾿ εἶπεν* closely follows *piangendo disse*: *κλαίουσ᾿* is elided *κλαίουσα*, present active participle of *κλαίω*, nominative feminine singular like *βουλομένη*; the weeping accompanies the act of speaking. *Εἶπεν* is the suppletive second aorist active indicative of *λέγω*, 3rd sg.
- *Εἰ σὺ νῦν διέρχῃ ταύτην τὴν ζοφώδη φυλακήν* puts an explicit *σύ* and an added *νῦν* ‘now’ before the verb. *Διέρχῃ* is 2nd sg. present middle indicative of *διέρχομαι*: the verb itself expresses passage through, with an accusative of the place traversed, in place of *per … vai*. *Ζοφώδη* (acc. fem. sg. of *ζοφώδης*) gives ‘gloomy’ for *cieco*. The instrumental dative *τῇ σῇ σοφίᾳ* ‘by your wisdom’ makes the possessor explicit but drops the image of height in *per altezza d’ ingegno*.
- The consequence of the *εἰ* clause takes the form of questions. *Ποῦ ᾿στιν υἱός μου;* places the interrogative first, unlike *mio figlio ov’ è*; *᾿στιν* loses the initial vowel of *ἐστίν* by prodelision. *Καὶ σὺν σοὶ πῶς οὐκ ἔστι;* fronts ‘with you’ and uses *πῶς* ‘how’ in the sense ‘how is it that …?’ for *perchè*.

> 61 Ed io a lui : “Da me stesso non vegno:  
> 62 colui, che attende là, per qui mi mena,  
> 63 forse cui Guido vostro ebbe a disdegno.”  

And I to him: “Of myself I come not: he, that waits yonder, leads me through this place; whom perhaps thy Guido held in disdain.”

> 61 Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν· “Οὐκ ἀπ᾿ ἐμαυτοῦ δεῦρο  
> (And I to him: “Not of myself hither)
>
> 62 „Ἦλθον· ἤγαγέ μ᾿ ὁ ᾿κεῖσε περιμένων,  
> („I came; he who waits yonder brought me,)
>
> 63 „Ὃν τάχ᾿ οὐκ εἶχεν ἐν τιμῇ σὸς Γουΐδος.”  
> („Whom perhaps your Guido held not in honour.”)

- *Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν* reproduces the verbless *Ed io a lui*: *κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*, and *πρός* takes the accusative of the person addressed. The masculine *αὐτόν* now designates the speaker previously described by feminine participles.
- *Οὐκ ἀπ᾿ ἐμαυτοῦ δεῦρο ἦλθον* ‘I did not come here of myself’ keeps the reflexive (*ἀπό* with the genitive), adds the directional adverb *δεῦρο*, and changes present *vegno* to *ἦλθον*, 1st sg. second aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι*. Likewise *ἤγαγέ μ᾿* ‘brought me’ replaces present *mi mena* with the reduplicated second aorist of *ἄγω* (3rd sg.); *μ᾿* is elided enclitic *με*. There is no separate expression for *per qui*.
- *ὁ ᾿κεῖσε περιμένων* ‘the one waiting yonder’ follows the verb as its subject. The article substantivizes the nominative masculine singular present active participle of *περιμένω*, replacing *colui, che attende*. *᾿Κεῖσε* is prodelided *ἐκεῖσε*, normally directional ‘thither’, here used of where he waits (*là*).
- *Ὃν τάχ᾿ οὐκ εἶχεν ἐν τιμῇ σὸς Γουΐδος* takes *cui* as referring to the waiting guide: *ὅν* is the accusative object of *εἶχεν* (3rd sg. imperfect active indicative of *ἔχω*). *Τάχ᾿* is elided *τάχα* ‘perhaps’; *οὐκ … ἐν τιμῇ* ‘not in honour’ understates *a disdegno*. The subject is postponed, and singular familiar *σός* replaces *vostro*. The imperfect, unlike the Italian preterite *ebbe*, describes an attitude as continuing in the past.

> 64 Le sue parole, e il modo della pena  
> 65 m' avevan di costui già letto il nome:  
> 66 però fu la risposta così piena.  

Already his words and the manner of his punishment had read his name to me: hence my answer was so full.

> 64 Ἥ τε λαλιὰ καὶ τὸ τοῦ πάθους ὕφος  
> (Both his speech and the manner of his suffering)
>
> 65 Αὐτοῦ τοὔνομα γνωστὸν κατέστησάν μοι·  
> (Made his name known to me;)
>
> 66 Διόπερ αὐτῷ σαφῶς ἀπεκρινάμην.  
> (Therefore I answered him clearly.)

- *Ἥ τε λαλιὰ καὶ τὸ τοῦ πάθους ὕφος* ‘both the speech and the character of the suffering’ renders the two subjects of *m’ avevan … letto*. *Ἥ* is the feminine article accented before enclitic *τε*, not a relative pronoun; *τε … καί* couples two singular nouns. *Λαλιά* collects plural *parole* into ‘speech’; the genitive *τοῦ πάθους* is enclosed between the article and *ὕφος*, which renders *modo*.
- *Αὐτοῦ τοὔνομα γνωστὸν κατέστησάν μοι* ‘they made his name known to me’ removes the metaphor of reading. *Αὐτοῦ* is the possessive genitive corresponding to *di costui*; *τοὔνομα* is crasis of *τὸ ὄνομα*, accusative object, with predicate adjective *γνωστόν*. *Κατέστησαν* is the transitive first aorist active indicative of *καθίστημι*, 3rd pl. with the paired subjects, not the intransitive second aorist *κατέστησαν* ‘they stood’. The dative *μοι* marks to whom the name became known; neither the pluperfect nor *già* is separately reproduced.
- *Διόπερ* ‘for which very reason’ answers to *però*. *Αὐτῷ σαφῶς ἀπεκρινάμην* ‘I answered him clearly’ replaces *fu la risposta così piena*: the answer becomes the middle verb *ἀπεκρινάμην* (1st sg. aorist of *ἀποκρίνομαι*), with dative addressee, and fullness becomes the adverb of clarity *σαφῶς*.

> 67 Di subito drizzato gridò : “Come  
> 68 dicesti : egli ebbe? non viv' egli ancora?  
> 69 non fiere gli occhi suoi lo dolce lome?”  

Rising instantly erect, he cried: “How saidst thou: he had? lives he not still? does not the sweet light strike his eyes?”

> 67 Αἴφνης ὀρθωθεὶς ἀντέκραξε· “Πῶς εἶπας,  
> (Suddenly rising upright he cried out in answer: “How did you say,)
>
> 68 „Εἶχεν; ἐν τοῖς ζῶσι νῦν οὐκ ἔστιν ἔτι;  
> („Held? Is he now among the living no more?)
>
> 69 „Τὰς ὄψεις τ᾽ αὐτοῦ γλυκὺ φῶς οὐ φαιδρύνει;...”  
> („And does the sweet light not gladden his eyes?...”)

- *Αἴφνης ὀρθωθεὶς ἀντέκραξε* follows the sequence of *Di subito drizzato gridò*. *Ὀρθωθείς*, nominative masculine singular aorist passive participle of *ὀρθόω*, means ‘having straightened up’; *ἀντέκραξε*, 3rd sg. aorist active indicative of *ἀντικράζω*, adds the sense of crying **in reply**.
- *Πῶς εἶπας, Εἶχεν;* repeats Musurus’ own *εἶχεν* for *ebbe*, omitting *egli*, since the verb marks the person. *Εἶπας* is the 2nd sg. aorist active indicative with the analogical ending *-ας*, beside Attic *εἶπες*.
- *ἐν τοῖς ζῶσι νῦν οὐκ ἔστιν ἔτι;* ‘is he now no longer among the living?’ recasts *non viv’ egli ancora?* with a locative phrase. *Τοῖς ζῶσι* is a substantivized present active participle of *ζάω*, dative masculine plural after *ἐν*; *οὐκ … ἔτι* means ‘no longer’, and *νῦν* is supplied.
- *Τὰς ὄψεις τ᾽ αὐτοῦ γλυκὺ φῶς οὐ φαιδρύνει;* fronts the accusative object ‘his eyes’ before the neuter singular subject ‘sweet light’. Postpositive *τ᾽* (elided *τε*) links the questions. *Φαιδρύνει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *φαιδρύνω*) gives ‘gladdens’ or ‘brightens’ rather than the physical ‘strikes’ of *fiere*.

> 70 Quando s' accorse d' alcuna dimora  
> 71 ch' io faceva dinanzi alla risposta,  
> 72 supin ricadde, e più non parve fuora.  

When he perceived that I made some delay in answering, supine he fell again, and shewed himself no more.

> 70 Βραδύτητα δέ τινα παρατηρήσας  
> (And observing a certain slowness)
>
> 71 Εἰς ἣν ἐπόθει θερμῶς ἀπόκρισίν μου,  
> (In my answer which he ardently desired,)
>
> 72 Ὑπτίως ἔπεσ᾿, οὐκέτι φανεὶς ἔξω.  
> (He fell backward, appearing outside no more.)

- *Βραδύτητα δέ τινα παρατηρήσας* ‘but on noticing a certain slowness’ replaces finite *Quando s’ accorse* with a circumstantial aorist active participle of *παρατηρέω*, nominative masculine singular, anterior to the fall. Accusative *βραδύτητά … τινα* is its direct object, rather than a complement introduced by *di*.
- *Εἰς ἣν ἐπόθει θερμῶς ἀπόκρισίν μου* attaches the slowness to ‘the answer of mine which he ardently desired’. The order interweaves two constructions: *εἰς … ἀπόκρισίν μου* specifies what the delay concerned, while relative *ἥν* is understood as the object of *ἐπόθει* (3rd sg. imperfect active indicative of *ποθέω*), with its antecedent *ἀπόκρισιν* postponed. The Italian relative *ch’ io faceva* qualifies the delay; the Greek relative qualifies the answer, adding both the desire and *θερμῶς* ‘ardently’.
- *Ὑπτίως ἔπεσ᾿* renders adjectival *supin* by an adverb ‘backward’; *ἔπεσ᾿* is elided *ἔπεσε*, 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *πίπτω*, without a separate ‘again’ for *ri-*. *Οὐκέτι φανεὶς ἔξω* turns coordinated *e più non parve fuora* into a participial phrase: *φανείς* is nominative masculine singular, second aorist passive of *φαίνω*, intransitive ‘appearing’.

> 73 Ma quell' altro magnanimo, a cui posta  
> 74 restato m' era, non mutò aspetto,  
> 75 nè mosse collo, nè piegò sua costa.  

But that other, magnanimous, at whose desire I had stopped, changed not his aspect, nor moved his neck, nor bent his side.

> 73 Ὁ δ’ ἄλλος μεγάθυμος, δι’ ὃν ἐστάθην  
> (But the other great-souled one, for whose sake I had stopped)
>
> 74 Πρῶτον, οὐδαμῶς μετέβαλε τὴν ὄψιν,  
> (At first, in no wise changed his countenance,)
>
> 75 Οὔτε λαιμὸν κινήσας, ἢ πλευρὰν κάμψας·  
> (Neither moving his neck nor bending his side;)

- *Ὁ δ’ ἄλλος μεγάθυμος* ‘but the other great-hearted one’ uses postpositive *δέ* (elided) for initial *Ma*, and the poetic compound *μεγάθυμος* for *magnanimo*. *Δι’ ὃν ἐστάθην πρῶτον* ‘on whose account I stopped in the first place’ simplifies *a cui posta*: *διά* with the accusative gives the reason, without a noun for ‘desire’. *Ἐστάθην* is 1st sg. aorist passive indicative of *ἵστημι*, intransitive here, not a pluperfect like *restato m’ era*; adverbial *πρῶτον* is supplied.
- *Οὐδαμῶς μετέβαλε τὴν ὄψιν* strengthens *non* to ‘in no way’. *Μετέβαλε* is 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *μεταβάλλω*, with internal augment; *τὴν ὄψιν* is its accusative object, ‘his countenance’, the possessor understood.
- *Οὔτε λαιμὸν κινήσας, ἢ πλευρὰν κάμψας* subordinates the two further finite verbs *mosse … piegò* as nominative masculine singular aorist active participles of *κινέω* and *κάμπτω*. Neither body part has an explicit possessive, even for *sua costa*. The negative carries over the disjunction ‘or bending’; the connectors are *οὔτε … ἤ*, not the usual balanced *οὔτε … οὔτε*.

> 76 “E se,” continuando al primo detto,  
> 77 “egli han quell' arte,” disse, “male appresa,  
> 78 ciò mi tormenta più che questo letto.  

“And if,” continuing his former words, he said, “they have learnt that art badly, it more torments me than this bed.

> 76 “Ἀλλ’ εἰ,” τὸν πρῶτον εἶπ’ ἀναλαβὼν λόγον,  
> (“But if,” he said, resuming his former speech,)
>
> 77 “Ἐκεῖνοι κακῶς ἐξέμαθον τὴν τέχνην,  
> (“Those men learned that art ill,)
>
> 78 „Τοῦτ’ ἀνιᾷ με πλέον τοῦ τάφου τοῦδε.  
> („This grieves me more than this tomb.)

- *Ἀλλ’ εἰ* ‘but if’ is more adversative than *E se*. As in Italian, the condition is interrupted, but Musurus puts *εἶπ’* (elided *εἶπε*, ‘he said’) inside *τὸν πρῶτον … λόγον*, the object of *ἀναλαβών*. This nominative masculine singular second aorist active participle of *ἀναλαμβάνω* means ‘having taken up again’, rather than the simultaneous ‘continuing’ of *continuando*.
- *Ἐκεῖνοι κακῶς ἐξέμαθον τὴν τέχνην* uses the demonstrative ‘those men’ for *egli* and no demonstrative for *quell’ arte*. *Ἐξέμαθον* is here 3rd pl. second aorist active indicative of *ἐκμανθάνω* ‘learn thoroughly’; adverb *κακῶς* qualifies the learning. The aorist replaces the Italian compound perfect *han … appresa*.
- *Τοῦτ’ ἀνιᾷ με πλέον τοῦ τάφου τοῦδε* retains *ciò* as the neuter subject *τοῦτ’* (elided *τοῦτο*). *Ἀνιᾷ*, contracted 3rd sg. present active indicative of *ἀνιάω*, is ‘distresses’; adverbial *πλέον* takes the genitive of comparison *τοῦ τάφου τοῦδε*. The demonstrative *τοῦδε* keeps the immediacy of *questo*, but ‘tomb’ replaces ‘bed’.

> 79 Ma non cinquanta volte fia raccesa  
> 80 la faccia della donna, che qui regge,  
> 81 che tu saprai quanto quell' arte pesa.  

But the face of the Queen, who reigns here, shall not be fifty times rekindled ere thou shalt know the hardness of that art.

> 79 „Πλὴν οὐ πεντηκοντάκις ἐξαναλάμψει  
> („Yet not fifty times shall rekindle)
>
> 80 „Ὄψιν ἡ τῇδε κυβερνῶσα Κυρία,  
> („Her face the Lady who rules here,)
>
> 81 „Καὶ γνώσῃ, τί πότ’ ἐστιν ἡ τέχνη ’κείνη.  
> („And you shall know what that art truly is.)

- *Πλὴν οὐ πεντηκοντάκις* ‘yet not fifty times’ condenses *cinquanta volte* into a multiplicative adverb. *Ἐξαναλάμψει* (3rd sg. future active indicative of *ἐξαναλάμπω*) is used transitively: ‘the Lady … will make [her] face shine out again’. Thus *ὄψιν* is accusative object, not the subject of a passive as *la faccia* is with *fia raccesa*.
- *ἡ τῇδε κυβερνῶσα Κυρία* is the postponed subject. Between article and noun stands the attributive present active participle *κυβερνῶσα* (nom. fem. sg. of *κυβερνάω*) with locative adverb *τῇδε* ‘here’, replacing the finite relative *che qui regge*.
- *Καὶ γνώσῃ* ‘and you will know’ uses coordination where Italian *che* after the negated prediction has the force “ere.” *Γνώσῃ* is 2nd sg. future middle indicative of *γιγνώσκω*. Its indirect question *τί πότ’ ἐστιν ἡ τέχνη ’κείνη* means ‘what that art really is’: interrogative *τί* is predicative, and enclitic *ποτε*, elided, lends emphasis. The prodelided demonstrative *’κείνη* follows the articular noun. The metaphor and the degree expressed in *quanto … pesa* both disappear.

> 82 E se tu mai nel dolce mondo regge,  
> 83 dimmi, perchè quel popolo è sì empio  
> 84 incontro a' miei in ciascuna sua legge?”  

And so mayest thou once return to the sweet world, tell me why that people is so fierce against my kindred in all its laws?”

> 82 „Εἴθε ποτ’ εἰς τὸν γλυκὺν ἀνέλθοις κόσμον,  
> („So may you one day ascend to the sweet world,)
>
> 83 „Εἰπέ μοι, πῶς ὁ λαὸς ἐκεῖνος οὕτω  
> („Tell me, why that people lays down)
>
> 84 „Σκληροὺς κατὰ τῶν ἐμῶν τίθησι νόμους.”  
> („Laws so harsh against my own?”)

- *Εἴθε ποτ’ … ἀνέλθοις* makes the wish implicit in *E se tu mai … regge* independent and explicit. *Εἴθε* introduces a wish with the optative alone, without *ἄν*; *ἀνέλθοις* is 2nd sg. second aorist active optative of *ἀνέρχομαι*. Elided *ποτε* means ‘one day’ here. *Εἰς τὸν γλυκὺν … κόσμον* gives a destination in the accusative, and the verb specifies ascending rather than returning.
- *Εἰπέ μοι* is the 2nd sg. second aorist active imperative of *λέγω* with enclitic dative ‘to me’. *Πῶς* introduces the question for *perchè*; *ὁ λαὸς ἐκεῖνος* ‘that people’ is nominative singular subject of *τίθησι*, 3rd sg. present active indicative of *τίθημι*.
- *Οὕτω σκληροὺς … τίθησι νόμους* ‘lays down laws so harsh’ transfers the Italian predicate *sì empio* from the people to the laws. *Σκληρούς* agrees with the delayed accusative plural *νόμους*, now the direct object in the idiom ‘lay down laws’; neither *ciascuna* nor *sua* is separately translated. *Κατὰ τῶν ἐμῶν* ‘against my own’ uses *κατά* with the genitive and a substantivized possessive adjective for *incontro a’ miei*.

> 85 Ond' io a lui : “Lo strazio e il grande scempio,  
> 86 che fece l' Arbia colorata in rosso,  
> 87 tale orazion fa far nel nostro tempio.”  

Whereat I to him: “The havoc, and the great slaughter, which dyed the Arbia red, causes such orations in our temple.”

> 85 Κἀγώ· “Σφαγαὶ καὶ ῥοαὶ πολλῶν αἱμάτων,  
> (And I: “The slaughters and streams of much blood,)
>
> 86 „Αἱ διαφοινίξασαι τὴν γῆν Ἀρβίας,  
> („Which dyed crimson the land of Arbia,)
>
> 87 „Τοιάνδ’ ἤνεγκαν ἐν ἐκκλησίᾳ ψῆφον.”  
> („Brought forth such a decree in church.”)

- *Κἀγώ* ‘and I’ is again crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*; this time neither the responsive force of *Ond’* nor *a lui* has a separate equivalent. *Σφαγαὶ καὶ ῥοαὶ πολλῶν αἱμάτων* ‘slaughters and streams of much blood’ replaces *Lo strazio e il grande scempio* with two nominative feminine plurals. *Πολλῶν αἱμάτων*, dependent on ‘streams’, is itself plural, literally ‘of many bloods’.
- *Αἱ διαφοινίξασαι* ‘which dyed crimson’ is an attributive participle with a repeated article, nominative feminine plural referring back to the paired subjects. The aorist active participle of *διαφοινίσσω* concentrates *fece … colorata in rosso* into one verb. Musurus adds *τὴν γῆν* as its object and makes the name a dependent genitive, *Ἀρβίας*: it is ‘the land of Arbia’, not *l’ Arbia* itself, that is coloured.
- *Τοιάνδ’ ἤνεγκαν ἐν ἐκκλησίᾳ ψῆφον* ‘brought forth such a decree in assembly’ separates *τοιάνδ’* (elided *τοιάνδε*, acc. fem. sg. of *τοιόσδε*) from its noun *ψῆφον*. *Ἤνεγκαν*, 3rd pl. aorist active indicative of *φέρω*, agrees with the plural subjects and replaces the present causative *fa far*. Musurus takes *orazion* in the sense of a formal resolution rather than a speech or prayer. *Ἐν ἐκκλησίᾳ* admits the senses ‘in assembly’ and ‘in church’; unlike *nel nostro tempio*, it has no possessive and does not expressly name a temple.

## Lines 88–120 — Defending Florence and Seeing the Future

> 88 Poi ch’ ebbe sospirando il capo scosso,  

And sighing, he shook his head; then said:

> 88 Κεφαλὴν σείσας, ὑποστενάζων εἶπεν·  
> (Shaking his head, sighing softly he said:)

- *Κεφαλὴν σείσας* ‘having shaken his head’ turns *Poi ch’ ebbe … il capo scosso* into an aorist participial phrase. *Σείσας* (nom. sg. masc., aor. act. of *σείω*) has the same subject as *εἶπεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*); its action precedes the speaking without a separate word for *poi*. The initial accusative *κεφαλήν* is its object, without article or possessive.
- *ὑποστενάζων* (nom. sg. masc., pres. act. participle of *ὑποστενάζω*) renders the gerund *sospirando*. Unlike *σείσας*, it presents an action in progress; its position next to *εἶπεν* associates the sighing with the utterance. Musurus brings *disse* forward from line 89.

> 89 “A ciò non fui io sol,” disse, “nè certo  
> 90 senza cagion sarei con gli altri mosso;  

"In that I was not single; nor without cause, assuredly, should I have stirred with the others;

> 89 “Οὐκ ἤμην μόνος, οὔτ’ ἂν βεβαίως ἄνευ  
> (“I was not alone, nor would I surely without)
>
> 90 „Αἰτίας σὺν τοῖς ἄλλοις συνεπορεύθην.  
> („Cause have marched along with the others.)

- *Οὐκ ἤμην μόνος* ‘I was not alone’ omits *A ciò* and the explicit *io*. *Ἤμην* is the 1st sg. imperfect of *εἰμί*, a later form beside Attic *ἦ*; *μόνος* is predicate nominative. Musurus reserves the emphatic pronoun for the contrasting assertion in line 91.
- *οὔτ’ ἂν … συνεπορεύθην* renders the past conditional *nè … sarei … mosso*: *οὔτ’* is elided *οὔτε*, and modal *ἄν* goes with a past indicative, not an optative. *Συνεπορεύθην* (1st sg. aor. pass. ind. in form, of *συμπορεύομαι*) means ‘I would have gone along’; the augment comes after the preverb. The idea of accompaniment in the compound is made explicit again by *σὺν τοῖς ἄλλοις* (*σύν* with dative).
- *βεβαίως* answers to *certo*; *ἄνευ* governs the genitive *αἰτίας* across the line break, whereas *senza* marks no case distinction.

> 91 ma fu’ io sol colà, dove sofferto  
> 92 fu per ciascuno di torre via Fiorenza,  
> 93 colui che la difesi a viso aperto.”  

but I was single there, where all consented to extirpate Florence, I alone with open face defended her."

> 91 „Ἀλλ’ ἐγὼ μόνος, ὅτ’ εἶδον ἐγνωκότας  
> („But I alone, when I saw resolved)
>
> 92 „Πάντας κατεδαφίσαι τὴν Φλωρεντίαν,  
> („All to raze Florence to the ground,)
>
> 93 „Ἤμυν᾽ αὐτῇ τότ᾽ ἀποκεκαλυμμένως.”  
> („Defended her then openly.”)

- *Ἀλλ’ ἐγὼ μόνος … ἤμυν’* ‘but I alone … defended’ gives *ma fu’ io sol … colui che la difesi* a single finite verb. *Ἀλλ’* is elided *ἀλλά*; *μόνος* agrees with emphatic *ἐγώ*. There is no equivalent of the identifying relative *colui che*.
- The local *colà, dove* becomes temporal *ὅτ’ εἶδον … τότε* ‘when I saw … then’ (*ὅτ’* = *ὅτε*). The impersonal passive *sofferto fu per ciascuno* becomes a personal perception: *εἶδον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*) takes *πάντας* as object and *ἐγνωκότας* (acc. pl. masc., perf. act. participle of *γιγνώσκω*) as its supplementary participle, ‘saw them all resolved’. The perfect presents their decision as already made.
- *κατεδαφίσαι* (aor. act. infinitive of *κατεδαφίζω*) completes *ἐγνωκότας* and makes *torre via* specifically ‘raze to the ground’; *τὴν Φλωρεντίαν* is its accusative object. *Ἤμυν’* is elided *ἤμυνα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀμύνω*); the person or place defended takes the dative *αὐτῇ*, unlike direct-object *la*. *τότ’* is elided *τότε*. The adverb *ἀποκεκαλυμμένως*, formed on the perfect middle-passive participle of *ἀποκαλύπτω*, gives ‘openly’, literally ‘in an unveiled manner’, without naming the face in *a viso aperto*.

> 94 “Deh se riposi mai vostra semenza,”  
> 95 pregai io lui, “solvetemi quel nodo,  
> 96 che qui ha inviluppata mia sentenza.  

"Ah! so may thy seed sometime have rest," I prayed him, "solve the knot which has here involved my judgment.

> 94 — “Εἴθ᾽ ἄνεσιν εὕροι σὸν σπέρμα,” τότ᾽ εἶπον  
> (—“So may your seed find rest,” then I said)
>
> 95 Ἐκλιπαρῶν, “λῦσόν μοι σύναμμα τόδε  
> (Beseeching, “untie for me this knot)
>
> 96 „ Τὸ συμπλέκον ἐνταῦθα τὴν ἐμὴν φρένα.  
> („ Which here entangles my mind.)

- *Εἴθ’ ἄνεσιν εὕροι σὸν σπέρμα* ‘would that your seed might find relief’ renders *Deh se riposi mai vostra semenza*. *Εἴθ’* is *εἴθε* elided before a rough breathing, with aspiration of the final consonant. *Εὕροι* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. optative of *εὑρίσκω*: a wish with *εἴθε*, without potential *ἄν*. The verb *riposi* becomes verb plus accusative object *ἄνεσιν*. The subject *σὸν σπέρμα* is neuter singular; *σόν* substitutes singular address for courteous *vostra*. *Mai* has no separate equivalent.
- *τότ’ εἶπον ἐκλιπαρῶν* ‘then I said, entreating’ makes the entreaty of *pregai io lui* subsidiary to a supplied verb of speaking: *εἶπον* is 1st sg. second aor. act. ind., *ἐκλιπαρῶν* a contracted nom. sg. masc. present active participle of *ἐκλιπαρέω*. The object *lui* is left implicit.
- *λῦσόν μοι σύναμμα τόδε* has a singular aorist active imperative of *λύω* for *solvetemi*; enclitic *μοι* is the dative of the person for whom the knot is to be undone and accounts for the additional accent on *λῦσόν*. *Τόδε* makes *quel nodo* ‘this knot’. *Τὸ συμπλέκον … τὴν ἐμὴν φρένα* replaces the finite relative with an articular present active participle of *συμπλέκω* (acc. sg. neut., agreeing with *σύναμμα*). The Italian result *ha inviluppata* becomes an ongoing entanglement; *ἐνταῦθα* keeps *qui*, and *φρένα* (acc. sg. of *φρήν*) makes *sentenza* the mind itself.

> 97 E’ par che voi veggiate, se ben odo,  
> 98 dinanzi quel, che il tempo seco adduce,  
> 99 e nel presente tenete altro modo.”  

It seems that you see beforehand what time brings with it, if I rightly hear; and have a different manner with the present."

> 97 „ Ἔοικεν, ὡς λέγουσιν, ὑμᾶς προβλέπειν  
> („ It seems, as they say, that you foresee)
>
> 98 „ Ἅπερ ὁ χρόνος σὺν ἑαυτῷ παράγει,  
> („ What time brings forward with itself,)
>
> 99 „ Εἶναι τ᾽ ἀγνῶτας τῶν παρόντων πραγμάτων.” —  
> („ And are ignorant of present things.”—)

- Impersonal *ἔοικεν* (3rd sg. perf. act. ind. of *ἔοικα*, with present sense) takes accusative and infinitive for *E’ par che voi veggiate*: *ὑμᾶς* is the plural subject of *προβλέπειν* and *εἶναι*. The prefix of *προβλέπειν* incorporates *dinanzi*. *ὡς λέγουσιν* ‘as they say’, with an indefinite 3rd pl. subject, replaces the speaker’s qualification *se ben odo*: report rather than a condition about hearing correctly.
- *ἅπερ* (acc. pl. neut. of the strengthened relative *ὅσπερ*) introduces the object of foresight without a separate antecedent for *quel*: ‘the very things which time brings’. *ὁ χρόνος* is subject of *παράγει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *παράγω*); *σὺν ἑαυτῷ* reproduces *seco* with a reflexive dative.
- *εἶναι τ’ ἀγνῶτας* ‘and to be ignorant’ interprets rather than reproduces *tenete altro modo*. Postpositive *τ’* (*τε*) coordinates the infinitives; predicate *ἀγνῶτας* is accusative plural in agreement with *ὑμᾶς*, and the genitive *τῶν παρόντων πραγμάτων* specifies what is unknown. *Παρόντων* is the pres. participle of *πάρειμι*, here attributive to the supplied noun *πραγμάτων*: *nel presente* becomes ‘of present things’.

> 100 “Noi veggiam come quei, che ha mala luce,  
> 101 le cose,” disse, “che ne son lontano;  
> 102 cotanto ancor ne splende il sommo Duce:  

"Like one who has imperfect vision, we see the things," he said, "which are remote from us; so much light the Supreme Ruler still gives to us;

> 100 — “Βλέπομεν,” εἶπε, “τὰ κείμενα μακρόθεν,  
> (—“We see,” he said, “the things that lie afar off,)
>
> 101 „ Ὥσπερ ὁ τὴν ὅρασιν ἔχων ἀμβλεῖαν·  
> („ Like one whose eyesight is dim;)
>
> 102 „ Τόσον ἔτι φῶς Θεὸς ἡμῖν παρέχει.  
> („ So much light still God grants to us.)

- *Βλέπομεν, εἶπε, τὰ κείμενα μακρόθεν* puts verb, reporting clause, and object before the comparison, instead of inserting *come quei …* between *veggiam* and *le cose*. The subject *noi* is expressed by the 1st pl. ending alone. *Τὰ κείμενα* (acc. pl. neut., articular pres. middle participle of the stative *κεῖμαι*) means ‘the things that lie’; *μακρόθεν*, literally ‘from afar’, marks remoteness without an explicit counterpart to *ne* ‘from us’.
- *ὥσπερ ὁ τὴν ὅρασιν ἔχων ἀμβλεῖαν* ‘just like one who has his sight dull’ makes *quei, che ha* an articular nom. sg. masc. participle (*ἔχων*, pres. act. of *ἔχω*). *Ἀμβλεῖαν* (acc. sg. fem. of *ἀμβλύς*) is predicative to *τὴν ὅρασιν*, not inside its article group. *Mala luce* thus denotes defective eyesight, distinguished from *φῶς* in the next line.
- *Τόσον ἔτι φῶς Θεὸς ἡμῖν παρέχει* ‘so much light God still supplies us’ turns *cotanto … ne splende* into a transitive construction. *Τόσον* qualifies the accusative *φῶς*, *Θεός* replaces the title *il sommo Duce* as nominative subject, and *ἡμῖν* is the recipient dative with *παρέχει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *παρέχω*).

> 103 quando s’ appressano, o son, tutto è vano  
> 104 nostro intelletto; e, s’ altri nol ci apporta,  
> 105 nulla sapem di vostro stato umano.  

when they draw nigh, or are, our intellect is altogether void; and except what others bring us, we know nothing of your human state.

> 103 „ Ἀλλ᾽ ὡς πρὸς τἀγγὺς καὶ τὰ τετελεσμένα  
> („ But as for what is near and accomplished,)
>
> 104 „ Ἐκλείπει τὸ φῶς ἡμῖν· ἂν δ᾽ ἀλλαχόθεν  
> („ Light fails us; and if from elsewhere)
>
> 105 „ Μηδὲν μάθωμεν, ἀγνοοῦμεν τἀν κόσμῳ.  
> („ We learn nothing, we are ignorant of things in the world.)

- *Ἀλλ’ ὡς πρὸς τἀγγὺς καὶ τὰ τετελεσμένα* ‘but as regards things near and things accomplished’ replaces the temporal clause *quando s’appressano, o son* by *ὡς πρός* with accusatives. *Τἀγγύς* is crasis of *τὰ ἐγγύς*, the article substantivizing an adverb. *Τὰ τετελεσμένα* is an articular perf. middle-passive participle of *τελέω* (acc. pl. neut.): *son* is read as ‘have come to pass’, and ‘and’ replaces ‘or’.
- *ἐκλείπει τὸ φῶς ἡμῖν* ‘the light fails us’ continues the preceding Greek image rather than naming *nostro intelletto*. *Τὸ φῶς* is nominative subject of *ἐκλείπει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἐκλείπω*); the possessors become affected persons in the dative. *Tutto* has no distinct equivalent.
- *ἂν δ’ ἀλλαχόθεν μηδὲν μάθωμεν* ‘and if we learn nothing from elsewhere’ shifts the subject from the bringer in *s’altri nol ci apporta* to the learners. Here *ἄν* functions as conditional *ἐάν* with *μάθωμεν* (1st pl. second aor. act. subj. of *μανθάνω*), not as the modal particle of line 89; *δ’* is elided *δέ*. *Μηδέν* is its negative object, *ἀλλαχόθεν* an adverb of source. In the main clause the negative sense of *nulla sapem* is carried by *ἀγνοοῦμεν* (contracted 1st pl. pres. act. ind. of *ἀγνοέω*). Its object *τἀν κόσμῳ* (*τὰ ἐν κόσμῳ* in crasis) broadens *vostro stato umano* to ‘things in the world’.

> 106 Però comprender puoi che tutta morta  
> 107 fia nostra conoscenza da quel punto,  
> 108 che del futuro fia chiusa la porta.”  

Therefore thou mayest understand that all our knowledge shall be dead, from that moment when the portal of the Future shall be closed."

> 106 „ Δύνασ᾽ ἐννοεῖν νῦν, ὅτι νεκρὰ πᾶσα  
> („ You can now understand that wholly dead)
>
> 107 „ Ἔσεται παντάπασιν ἡμῶν ἡ γνῶσις,  
> („ Shall our knowledge altogether be,)
>
> 108 „ Ὁπόταν κλεισθῇ τοῦ μέλλοντος ἡ θύρα.” —  
> („ When the door of the future is closed.”—)

- *Δύνασ’ ἐννοεῖν νῦν* ‘you can understand now’ resumes singular address in *comprender puoi*. *Δύνασ’* stands for *δύνασαι* (2nd sg. pres. middle ind. of *δύναμαι*), with poetic elision of final *-αι*; *ἐννοεῖν* is its complementary pres. act. infinitive. Temporal *νῦν* takes the place of inferential *però*.
- *ὅτι νεκρὰ πᾶσα ἔσεται παντάπασιν ἡμῶν ἡ γνῶσις* keeps the finite *che* clause and postpones the subject as in Italian. *Ἔσεται* (3rd sg. fut. ind. of *εἰμί*, middle in form) links predicate *νεκρά* to *ἡ γνῶσις*; *πᾶσα* agrees with that subject (nom. sg. fem.). The adverb *παντάπασιν* ‘altogether’ reinforces *πᾶσα* beyond the single *tutta*. Possessive *ἡμῶν* precedes the article and noun.
- *ὁπόταν κλεισθῇ* ‘when it is shut’ compresses *da quel punto, che*: there is no separate expression of the starting point. *Ὁπόταν* (*ὁπότε* with *ἄν*) introduces a prospective temporal clause with *κλεισθῇ* (3rd sg. aor. pass. subj. of *κλείω*), not a future indicative like *fia*. In *τοῦ μέλλοντος ἡ θύρα*, the possessive genitive is a substantivized pres. active participle of *μέλλω*, neuter singular, ‘of what is to come’.

> 109 Allor, come di mia colpa compunto,  
> 110 dissi: “Or direte dunque a quel caduto,  
> 111 che il suo nato è co’ vivi ancor congiunto.  

Then, as compunctious for my fault, I said: "Now will you therefore tell that fallen one, that his child is still joined to the living.

> 109 Τότε μετανοήσας τὸ πραχθὲν σφάλμα  
> (Then repenting of the fault I had committed,)
>
> 110 Εἶπον· “Ἐρεῖς νῦν ἐκείνῳ τῷ πεσόντι,  
> (I said: “Tell now that fallen one)
>
> 111 „ Ὡς ἔτ᾽ ἔστ᾽ αὐτοῦ τὸ τέκνον ἐν τοῖς ζῶσιν·  
> („ That his son is still among the living;)

- *Τότε μετανοήσας τὸ πραχθὲν σφάλμα εἶπον* makes *come di mia colpa compunto* an unqualified active participial phrase, ‘having repented of the fault committed’. *Μετανοήσας* (nom. sg. masc., aor. act. participle of *μετανοέω*) agrees with the speaker of *εἶπον*. *Τὸ … σφάλμα* is accusative, the matter regretted; attributive *πραχθέν* (acc. sg. neut., aor. pass. participle of *πράσσω*) supplies ‘committed’, while the agent implicit in *mia* is not expressed.
- *Ἐρεῖς νῦν* retains the directive future of *Or direte*, but in the 2nd sg. (fut. act. ind. of *λέγω*); *dunque* is not separately rendered. *Ἐκείνῳ τῷ πεσόντι* ‘to that one who fell’ is a recipient dative. The demonstrative stands outside the article group; *πεσόντι* is the dat. sg. masc. second aor. act. participle of *πίπτω*, presenting the fall as an event.
- *ὡς ἔτ’ ἔστ’ αὐτοῦ τὸ τέκνον ἐν τοῖς ζῶσιν* uses declarative *ὡς* after a verb of saying. *Ἔτ’* is elided *ἔτι*, *ἔστ’* elided *ἔστι*; *αὐτοῦ* is possessive genitive with neuter *τὸ τέκνον* ‘his child’ for *il suo nato*. *Ἐν τοῖς ζῶσιν* ‘among the living’ is *ἐν* with an articular dat. pl. masc. pres. act. participle of *ζάω*: location replaces *è … congiunto*.

> 112 E s’ io fui dianzi alla risposta muto,  
> 113 fat’ ei saper che il fei, perchè pensava  
> 114 già nell’ error che m’ avete soluto.”  

And if I was mute before, at the response, let him know, it was because my thoughts already were in that error which you have resolved for me."

> 112 „ Εἰ δ᾽ ἀποκριθῆν᾽ αὐτῷ βραδὺς πρὶν ἤμην,  
> („ And if I was slow to answer him before,)
>
> 113 „ Φράσον, ὅτι τοῦτ᾽ ἐγένετο, διότι  
> („ Say that this happened because)
>
> 114 „ Ἐμελέτων ἣν ἔλυσας ἀπορίαν.”  
> („ I was pondering the perplexity which you have resolved.”)

- *Εἰ δ’ … βραδὺς πρὶν ἤμην* ‘and if I was slow before’ keeps *se* with an indicative, but interprets *muto* as delay, not silence. *Πρίν* is adverbial like *dianzi*, not a conjunction. *Ἀποκριθῆν’* is *ἀποκριθῆναι* with poetic elision of *-αι* (aor. pass. infinitive of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, active in sense). It specifies in what the speaker was slow; *αὐτῷ* is the person answered. Thus *alla risposta* becomes ‘to answer him’.
- *Φράσον* (2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *φράζω*) replaces causative *fat’ ei saper* with ‘tell’; no recipient is repeated. *ὅτι τοῦτ’ ἐγένετο, διότι* distinguishes reported content (*ὅτι*) from its cause (*διότι*). *Τοῦτ’* is elided *τοῦτο*, subject of *ἐγένετο* (3rd sg. second aor. middle ind. of *γίγνομαι*): ‘this happened’ makes *il fei* impersonal in sense rather than an act expressly attributed to the speaker.
- *ἐμελέτων ἣν ἔλυσας ἀπορίαν* ‘I was pondering the difficulty you solved’ has an augmented, contracted 1st sg. imperfect of *μελετάω*, preserving the duration of *pensava*. *Ἀπορίαν* is the matter pondered, not an *error* in which the speaker was thinking. The noun is drawn into the relative group after *ἣν ἔλυσας*; *ἥν* is acc. sg. fem., object of *ἔλυσας* (2nd sg. aor. act. ind. of *λύω*). The simple aorist replaces *avete soluto*, with neither *già* nor beneficiary *m’* expressed.

> 115 E già il Maestro mio mi richiamava:  
> 116 per ch’ io pregai lo spirito più avaccio,  
> 117 che mi dicesse, chi con lui si stava.  

And now my Master was recalling me: wherefore I, in more haste, besought the spirit to tell me who was with him.

> 115 Ἐπειδὴ δ᾽ οὑμὸς Προστάτης μ᾽ ἀνεκάλει,  
> (And since my Master was calling me back,)
>
> 116 Ἠρώτησα τὴν σκιὰν κατεσπευσμένως,  
> (I asked the shade hastily)
>
> 117 Τίνες μετ᾽ αὐτῆς συνέκειντ᾽ ἐν τῷ τάφῳ.  
> (Who lay together with him in the tomb.)

- *Ἐπειδὴ δ’ οὑμὸς Προστάτης μ’ ἀνεκάλει* ‘and since my protector was calling me back’ makes the first Italian main clause causal, so that no connective corresponding to *per ch’* is needed in the next. *Οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, article and possessive before *Προστάτης* ‘protector’ for *Maestro*. *Μ’* is elided accusative *με*; *ἀνεκάλει* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἀνακαλέω*) has internal augment and a contracted ending. It keeps the ongoing *richiamava*, but *già* is omitted.
- *Ἠρώτησα τὴν σκιὰν κατεσπευσμένως* ‘I asked the shade hastily’ replaces *pregai lo spirito … che mi dicesse* by a single verb of questioning. *Ἠρώτησα* is the temporally augmented 1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐρωτάω*, with accusative of the person asked. *Κατεσπευσμένως*, an adverb formed on the perfect middle-passive participle of *κατασπεύδω*, does not retain the comparison of *più avaccio*.
- *τίνες μετ’ αὐτῆς συνέκειντ’ ἐν τῷ τάφῳ* is the indirect question itself. *Τίνες* (nom. pl. of interrogative *τίς*) makes *chi* explicitly plural. *Μετ’* is *μετά* with a genitive of accompaniment; feminine *αὐτῆς* refers grammatically to *σκιάν*, despite masculine *lui*. *Συνέκειντ’* is elided *συνέκειντο* (3rd pl. imperf. middle ind. of *σύγκειμαι*), ‘were lying together’, more specific than *si stava*. *Ἐν τῷ τάφῳ* supplies a location absent from the Italian question.

> 118 Dissemi: “Qui con più di mille giaccio;  
> 119 qua entro è lo secondo Federico,  
> 120 e il Cardinale, e degli altri mi taccio.”  

He said to me: "With more than a thousand lie I here; the second Frederick is here within, and the Cardinal; and of the rest I speak not."

> 118 Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπε· “Σὺν χιλίοις καὶ πλέον κεῖμαι  
> (And he said: “With a thousand and more I lie)
>
> 119 „Ὧδ᾽, ἐν οἷς Φρεδερίκος ὁ δεύτερός τε  
> („Here, among whom are both Frederick the Second)
>
> 120 „ Χὠ Καρδινάλις· τοὺς ἄλλους παραλείπω.”  
> („ And the Cardinal; of the rest I say nothing.”)

- *Ὁ δ’ εἶπε* ‘and he said’ uses the article pronominally in the turn-taking construction *ὁ δέ*; there is no dative for *-mi* in *Dissemi*. *Σὺν χιλίοις καὶ πλέον κεῖμαι ὧδ’* puts the companions first and postpones *qui* to the next verse. *Χιλίοις* is dat. pl. after *σύν*; *πλέον* (neut. sg. comparative of *πολύς*, used adverbially) gives ‘a thousand and more’ rather than the comparative construction *più di mille*. *Κεῖμαι* (1st sg. pres. middle ind.) renders *giaccio*; *ὧδ’* is elided *ὧδε* ‘here’.
- *ἐν οἷς Φρεδερίκος ὁ δεύτερός τε χὠ Καρδινάλις* changes *qua entro è* to ‘among whom [are]’: *οἷς* is a dat. pl. masc. relative referring to the companions, and the copula is understood. *Ὁ δεύτερος* follows the name as an articular epithet instead of preceding it as *lo secondo* does. Enclitic *τε* gives *δεύτερός* its additional accent and pairs with the *καί* contained in *χὠ* (crasis of *καὶ ὁ*, with aspiration), linking the two nominatives.
- *τοὺς ἄλλους παραλείπω* ‘I pass over the others’ gives *degli altri mi taccio* a transitive, non-reflexive construction: the remaining persons are the fronted accusative object of *παραλείπω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.), not a topic introduced by a preposition.

## Lines 121–136 — The Sage’s Counsel and the Path to the Valley

> 121 Indi s’ascose; ed io in ver l’ antico  
> 122 poeta volsi i passi, ripensando  
> 123 a quel parlar che mi parea nemico.  

Therewith he hid himself; and I towards the ancient Poet turned my steps, revolving that saying which seemed hostile to me.

> 121 Ἔπειτα κατεκλίθη· κἀγὼ τὸ βῆμα  
> (Then he lay down; and I my step)
>
> 122 Πρὸς τὸν ποιητὴν ἔτρεψα κἀνεπόλουν  
> (Turned toward the poet, and pondered again)
>
> 123 Τοὺς ἀπειλητικούς μοι δοκοῦντας λόγους.  
> (The words that seemed threatening to me.)

- *Ἔπειτα κατεκλίθη* ‘then he lay down’ takes *indi* temporally and renders *s’ascose* by the movement rather than its result. *Κατεκλίθη* is the 3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *κατακλίνω*, with the augment after the preverb; the Greek passive has intransitive force where the Italian uses a reflexive.
- *κἀγὼ τὸ βῆμα πρὸς τὸν ποιητὴν ἔτρεψα* ‘and I turned my step toward the poet’ retains the explicit subject of *ed io*: *κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*. The singular *τὸ βῆμα* replaces the plural *i passi* and precedes both the directional phrase (*πρός* + acc.) and the verb *ἔτρεψα* (1st sg. sigmatic aor. act. ind. of *τρέπω*). *Antico* has no expressed equivalent.
- *κἀνεπόλουν* ‘and I was turning over in my mind’ is crasis of *καὶ ἀνεπόλουν*. Instead of subordinating the reflection by the gerund *ripensando*, Musurus coordinates a second finite verb; its imperfect (1st sg. act. ind. of *ἀναπολέω*) sets the continuing reflection against the aorist *ἔτρεψα*.
- *τοὺς ἀπειλητικούς μοι δοκοῦντας λόγους* ‘the words that seemed threatening to me’ makes *quel parlar* a plural direct object, with the article rather than a demonstrative. The relative *che mi parea nemico* becomes an attributive participial phrase between article and noun: *δοκοῦντας* (pres. act. ptcp. of *δοκέω*) agrees with *λόγους* in the masc. acc. pl.; *ἀπειλητικούς* is its predicate adjective and *μοι* the dative of the person judging. ‘Threatening’ interprets rather than literally reproduces *nemico*.

> 124 Egli si mosse; e poi così andando  
> 125 mi disse: “Perchè sei tu sì smarrito?”  
> 126 Ed io li soddisfeci al suo dimando.  

He moved on; and then, as we were going, he said to me: “Why art thou so bewildered?” And I satisfied him in his question.

> 124 Ὁ δ᾽ ἐκινήθη, καί μοι καθ᾽ ὁδὸν εἶπε.  
> (And he moved on, and along the way said to me:)
>
> 125 “Τί δοκεῖς οὕτω περιαλγής ; ” Ἐπεί τε  
> (“Why do you seem so sorely distressed?” And when)
>
> 126 Πρὸς τοὐρώτημα δεόντως ἀπεκρίθην,  
> (I had duly answered his question,)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ ἐκινήθη* ‘and he moved’ uses pronominal *ὁ* with postpositive *δέ* (elided) to mark the change of subject. *Ἐκινήθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *κινέω*) is intransitive in sense, answering to reflexive *si mosse*. In *καί μοι καθ᾽ ὁδὸν εἶπε* ‘and on the way he said to me’, *καθ᾽ ὁδόν* replaces *così andando* with an adverbial phrase; *κατά* loses its final vowel and its *τ* becomes *θ* before the rough breathing. *Poi* is not separately rendered. *Μοι* is the recipient of *εἶπε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*).
- *Τί δοκεῖς οὕτω περιαλγής;* ‘why do you seem so distressed?’ substitutes *δοκεῖς* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *δοκέω*) for *sei* and leaves *tu* implicit. Interrogative *τί* is an adverbial accusative, ‘why?’; *οὕτω* marks degree like *sì*. *Περιαλγής* (nom. sg.) is predicated of the subject: the choice stresses distress rather than the disorientation of *smarrito*.
- *Ἐπεί τε πρὸς τοὐρώτημα δεόντως ἀπεκρίθην* ‘and when I had duly answered the question’ makes the independent *Ed io li soddisfeci al suo dimando* a temporal clause whose main verb comes in line 128. Postpositive *τε* links this construction to the preceding narrative; *ἐπεί* with the aorist indicative marks a completed occasion. *Ἀπεκρίθην* is the 1st sg. aorist passive form of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, active in meaning. *Δεόντως* ‘as was fitting’ conveys the adequacy implied in *soddisfeci*; *πρός* + acc. specifies what the reply answers. *Τοὐρώτημα* is crasis of *τὸ ἐρώτημα*; neither *li* nor possessive *suo* is separately expressed.

> 127 “La mente tua conservi quel che udito  
> 128 hai contra te,” mi comandò quel saggio,  
> 129 “ed ora attendi qui”; e drizzò il dito.  

“Let thy memory retain what thou hast heard against thee,” that Sage exhorted me; “and now mark here”; and he raised his finger.

> 127 “Ἐν μνήμῃ τήρει τὰ κατὰ σοῦ ῥηθέντα,  
> (“Keep in memory what was spoken against you,)
>
> 128 „ Ὡς ἤκουσας,” ὁ Σοφὸς διέταξέ μοι.  
> („ As you heard it,” the Sage commanded me.)
>
> 129 “Νῦν ὡδὶ βλέπε . . . ” Καὶ τὸν δάκτυλον τείνας,  
> (“Now look here...” And stretching forth his finger,)

- *Ἐν μνήμῃ τήρει* ‘keep in memory’ makes the addressee, not *la mente tua*, the subject. *Τήρει* is the contracted 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *τηρέω*, enjoining continued retention; *ἐν* + dat. makes memory the place of retention, without an expressed possessive.
- *τὰ κατὰ σοῦ ῥηθέντα, ὡς ἤκουσας* ‘the things said against you, as you heard them’ redistributes *quel che udito hai contra te*. The object of *τήρει* is the substantivized neut. acc. pl. aor. pass. participle *ῥηθέντα* (from *ἐρρήθην*, the passive aorist used with *λέγω*). *Κατά* + gen. marks the person spoken against. Hearing is now in a separate clause of manner, *ὡς ἤκουσας*, with the 2nd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀκούω* in place of the Italian compound perfect.
- *ὁ Σοφὸς διέταξέ μοι* ‘the sage commanded me’ supplies the main clause after *ἐπεί*. The substantivized adjective has the definite article rather than the demonstrative of *quel saggio*. *Διέταξε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *διατάσσω*, with internal augment) takes the person commanded in the dative; its additional final accent in *διέταξέ μοι* is due to enclitic *μοι*.
- *Νῦν ὡδὶ βλέπε* ‘now look right here’ renders *ed ora attendi qui* without an initial conjunction. *Βλέπε* (2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *βλέπω*) makes the appeal to attention visual; *ὡδί* is the emphatic deictic form of *ὧδε*, with demonstrative *-ί*. *Καὶ τὸν δάκτυλον τείνας* ‘and having extended his finger’ changes finite *drizzò* into a participle: *τείνας* (nom. masc. sg. aor. act. of *τείνω*) agrees with the subject of the following *εἶπεν*. The article with *δάκτυλον* suffices for the understood possessor.

> 130 “Quando sarai dinanzi al dolce raggio  
> 131 di quella, il cui bell’ occhio tutto vede,  
> 132 da lei saprai di tua vita il viaggio.”  

“When thou shalt stand before the sweet ray of that Lady, whose bright eye seeth all, from her shalt thou know the journey of thy life.”

> 130 Εἶπεν· “Ὅτ᾽ ὄψει τὴν γλυκεῖαν ἀκτῖνα  
> (He said: “When you shall see the sweet ray)
>
> 131 „ Ἐκείνης, ἧς ὀφθαλμὸς καθορᾷ πάντα,  
> („ Of her whose eye beholds all things,)
>
> 132 „ Παρ᾽ αὐτῆς γνώσῃ τὴν βίου σου πορείαν.”  
> („ From her you shall learn the course of your life.”)

- *Εἶπεν* ‘he said’ is supplied to carry the participle *τείνας* and introduce the speech; it is the same aorist as *εἶπε* in line 124, here with movable *ν*. *Ὅτ᾽ ὄψει τὴν γλυκεῖαν ἀκτῖνα* ‘when you shall see the sweet ray’ turns *sarai dinanzi al dolce raggio* from a statement of position into one of perception. *Ὅτ᾽* is elided *ὅτε*; the future indicative follows the definite future wording of the Italian rather than using the common prospective *ὅταν* with the subjunctive. *Ὄψει* is the 2nd sg. fut. mid. ind. used as the future of *ὁράω*, active in sense. *Γλυκεῖαν* (fem. acc. sg. of *γλυκύς*) agrees with the direct object *ἀκτῖνα* (from *ἀκτίς*).
- *Ἐκείνης, ἧς ὀφθαλμὸς καθορᾷ πάντα* ‘of her whose eye sees all things’ keeps the feminine demonstrative of *di quella* without supplying a noun. *Ἐκείνης* depends on *ἀκτῖνα*; the relative *ἧς*, likewise fem. gen. sg., is possessive with *ὀφθαλμός*. *Bell’* is omitted. *Καθορᾷ* (contracted 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *καθοράω*) suggests clear or full perception; singular *tutto* becomes substantival neut. acc. pl. *πάντα*.
- *Παρ᾽ αὐτῆς γνώσῃ* ‘from her you will learn’ retains the emphatic initial *da lei*: elided *παρά* takes the genitive of personal source. *Γνώσῃ* is the 2nd sg. fut. mid. ind. of *γιγνώσκω*, active in meaning. In *τὴν βίου σου πορείαν* ‘the course of your life’, *πορείαν* renders *il viaggio*; the dependent genitive *βίου*, with its possessive enclitic *σου*, stands between article and noun, preserving the precedence of *di tua vita* over *il viaggio*.

> 133 Appresso volse a man sinistra il piede;  

Then to the sinister hand he turned his feet;

> 133 Ἐπ᾽ ἀριστερὰ τότ᾽ ἔτρεψε τὸν πόδα·  
> (To the left then he turned his foot;)

- *Ἐπ᾽ ἀριστερὰ τότ᾽ ἔτρεψε τὸν πόδα* ‘to the left then he turned his foot’ fronts the direction, moving the temporal *τότε* from the initial position of *Appresso*. Both *ἐπί* and *τότε* are elided before vowels. *Ἐπ᾽ ἀριστερά* is a directional expression with the neut. acc. pl. of *ἀριστερός* used substantivally; no word for *man* is needed. *Ἔτρεψε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *τρέπω*) takes *τὸν πόδα* (acc. sg. of *πούς*), retaining the singular of *il piede*.

> 134 lasciammo il muro, e gimmo in ver lo mezzo  
> 135 per un sentier, che ad una valle fiede,  
> 136 che in fin lassù facea spiacer suo lezzo.  

we left the wall, and went towards the middle, by a path that strikes into a valley, which even up there annoyed us with its fetor.

> 134 Καὶ τὸ τεῖχος λιπόντες ἔβημεν μέσην  
> (And leaving the wall we went into the middle)
>
> 135 Εἰς μονόπατον ἄγοντα πρὸς κοιλάδα,  
> (Into a path leading to a valley,)
>
> 136 Ἧς ἱκνεῖτ᾽ ἄχρις ἡμῶν ἡ δυσωδία.  
> (Whose stench reached even up to us.)

- *Καὶ τὸ τεῖχος λιπόντες ἔβημεν* ‘and having left the wall we went’ subordinates the first of the coordinated verbs *lasciammo … e gimmo*. *Λιπόντες* is the masc. nom. pl. second aor. act. participle of *λείπω*, agreeing with the understood subject of *ἔβημεν*; it has the unaugmented participial stem *λιπ-*. *Ἔβημεν* is the 1st pl. root aor. act. ind. of *βαίνω*.
- *μέσην* answers to *in ver lo mezzo*, but its form is fem. acc. sg., not the substantivized neuter *τὸ μέσον*. It suggests an understood feminine such as *ὁδόν* ‘way’; it does not agree with *μονόπατον* as construed in the next line. *Εἰς μονόπατον* ‘onto a path’ expresses entry with *εἰς* + acc., rather than traversal as in *per un sentier*.
- *ἄγοντα πρὸς κοιλάδα* ‘leading toward a valley’ replaces the finite relative *che ad una valle fiede* with a pres. act. participle of *ἄγω*. *Ἄγοντα* is masc. acc. sg. in agreement with *μονόπατον*; here *ἄγω* is used of where a path leads, without the image of striking in *fiede*. *Πρός* + acc. gives its direction; *κοιλάδα* is the acc. sg. of *κοιλάς*.
- *Ἧς ἱκνεῖτ᾽ ἄχρις ἡμῶν ἡ δυσωδία* ‘whose stench reached as far as us’ shifts the subject from the valley to its smell. The relative *ἧς* (fem. gen. sg., referring to *κοιλάδα*) depends on the delayed subject *ἡ δυσωδία*, so that possession expressed by Italian *suo* now furnishes the relative link. *Ἱκνεῖτ᾽* is elided *ἱκνεῖτο*, 3rd sg. imperf. mid. ind. of poetic *ἱκνέομαι* ‘reach’. The imperfect corresponds to *facea*, but the construction of causing displeasure disappears: *ἄχρις* + gen. marks the limit reached, and the personal *ἡμῶν* ‘us’ replaces the spatial reference of *fin lassù*.

## Summary — From Relative Clause to Participle

Musurus often keeps Dante’s order of presentation while changing the construction that carries it. Both versions delay the subject of the opening sentence, but elsewhere an Italian relative clause becomes an article and participle: *colui, che attende* becomes *ὁ … περιμένων*, ‘the one waiting’, and *quel che udito hai contra te* is recast around *τὰ κατὰ σοῦ ῥηθέντα*, ‘the things said against you’. Participles also distinguish actions that Italian coordinates. Where *levò … poi disse* gives two successive finite verbs, *ὑψώσας … εἶπεν*, ‘having raised … he said’, makes the first action preliminary. Conversely, the gerund *ripensando* becomes a coordinated imperfect, *κἀνεπόλουν*, ‘and I was pondering’: the reflection continues while the preceding aorist marks the turn.

Small particles often change the relation between clauses. The recurrent *ὁ δέ* marks a change of speaker, and the compact *κἀγώ*, ‘and I’, closely matches *ed io*. Elsewhere Musurus makes a connection more explicit, as when *Σὺ γάρ*, ‘for you’, turns *e tu* into a reason; he can also weaken one, using *δέ* for inferential *però*, or change its force altogether, answering consecutive *sì che* with adversative *ἀλλά*, ‘but’. His treatment of future time is similarly flexible: *quando … torneranno* becomes prospective *ὅταν* with an aorist subjunctive, whereas the later *quando sarai* retains the future indicative after *ὅτε*, though being in someone’s presence becomes seeing her.

The most revealing departures often lie in a single word. *Στενοῦ*, ‘narrow’, gives the path a dimension rather than the seclusion of *secreto*; *σαφεῖς*, ‘clear’, reads *conte* as an instruction about intelligibility. At times a lexical choice reshapes the syntax as well: *quinci entro*, ‘in here’, gives way to *ἓν τῶν μνημάτων τούτων*, ‘one of these tombs’, as the subject of two future verbs. Yet Musurus is attentive to verbal echoes even when he does not translate literally. His imperfect *εἶχεν*, ‘held’, differs in aspect from *ebbe*, but its exact repetition in the ensuing question preserves the Italian insistence on the past tense itself.
