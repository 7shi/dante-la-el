# Inferno 20

## Lines 1–18 — Faces Turned Backward

> 1 Di nuova pena mi convien far versi,  
> 2 e dar materia al ventesimo canto  
> 3 della prima canzon, ch' è de' sommersi.  

Of new punishment behooves me to make verses, and give matter for the twentieth canto of the first canzone, which concerns the sunken.

> 1 Νέας στιχουργεῖν προσήκει μοι βασάνους,  
> (Of new torments it behoves me to make verses,)
>
> 2 Καὶ πρὸς ᾠδὴν εἰκοστὴν τοῦ τῶν ἐν ᾍδου  
> (And for the twentieth canto of the one about those in Hell,)
>
> 3 Πρώτου μου μέλους ὕλην παρασκευάσαι.  
> (My first song, to prepare the matter.)

- *Νέας στιχουργεῖν προσήκει μοι βασάνους* ‘it befits me to versify new torments’ keeps *νέας* in the emphatic opening position of *nuova*, but postpones its noun to the end. Italian *di nuova pena* gives the subject matter of *far versi*; Greek makes it the direct object of *στιχουργεῖν* (contracted pres. act. inf. of *στιχουργέω*), with both adjective and noun in the feminine accusative plural instead of the singular.
- *προσήκει μοι* closely answers to *mi convien*: impersonal *προσήκει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *προσήκω*) takes the dative of the person concerned and two coordinated infinitives. The second, *παρασκευάσαι* (aor. act. inf. of *παρασκευάζω*), presents the provision of the material as a whole, beside present *στιχουργεῖν*. *ὕλην παρασκευάσαι* is ‘to prepare material’ rather than *dar materia* ‘to give material’.
- *πρὸς ᾠδὴν εἰκοστήν* ‘for a twentieth ode’ renders *al ventesimo canto* by *πρός* with the accusative of purpose; *ᾠδή* and *μέλος* distinguish *canto* and *canzon*. In *τοῦ τῶν ἐν ᾍδου πρώτου μου μέλους*, *τοῦ* belongs to *πρώτου … μέλους* (gen. sg. of neuter *μέλος*), and enclitic *μου* supplies a possessive not expressed in Italian. Inside this group, *τῶν ἐν ᾍδου* ‘of those in Hades’ replaces the relative clause *ch’ è de’ sommersi*. The plural article makes the prepositional phrase substantival; *ἐν ᾍδου* is the idiom ‘in the house of Hades’, with a word for ‘house’ understood, not *ἐν* directly governing a genitive.

> 4 Io era già disposto tutto quanto  
> 5 a riguardar nello scoperto fondo,  
> 6 che si bagnava d' angoscioso pianto ;  

I now was all prepared to look into the depth discovered to me, which was bathed with tears of anguish;

> 4 Ὅλος ἤδη προσέχων εἰς τὸν φανέντα  
> (Already wholly intent, into the disclosed)
>
> 5 Βυθὸν ἔβλεπον, ὃς ἐπικεκλυσμένος  
> (Depth I looked, which inundated)
>
> 6 Ἦν πλημμυρίσι δακρύων πολυστόνων.  
> (Was with floods of mournful tears.)

- *Ὅλος ἤδη προσέχων* ‘already wholly intent’ recasts *Io era già disposto tutto quanto*. The emphatic *tutto quanto* becomes the single predicative *ὅλος* (nom. sg. masc.), and *disposto* becomes *προσέχων* (pres. act. participle of *προσέχω*, ‘give heed’), simultaneous with *ἔβλεπον*. Where Italian has a finite expression of readiness followed by *a riguardar*, Greek makes the looking itself finite: *ἔβλεπον* is the augmented 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *βλέπω*, ‘I was looking’.
- *εἰς τὸν φανέντα βυθόν* ‘into the depth that had come into view’ gives the direction of the gaze. Attributive *φανέντα* (acc. sg. masc., second aor. pass. participle of *φαίνω*) renders *scoperto* with an intransitive sense, ‘having appeared’; there is no expressed equivalent of “to me”.
- *ὃς ἐπικεκλυσμένος ἦν* ‘which was in a flooded state’ replaces reflexive *che si bagnava*. Masculine nominative *ὅς* resumes accusative *βυθόν* but takes its case as subject of the relative clause. *ἐπικεκλυσμένος* (nom. sg. masc., reduplicated perf. mid./pass. participle of *ἐπικλύζω*) with *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. of *εἰμί*) is a periphrastic pluperfect passive, stressing the resulting state. *πλημμυρίσι* (dat. pl. of *πλημμυρίς*) supplies ‘with floods’; dependent *δακρύων πολυστόνων* ‘of much-lamenting tears’ expands singular *d’ angoscioso pianto*.

> 7 e vidi gente per lo vallon tondo  
> 8 venir tacendo e lagrimando, al passo  
> 9 che fan le letaníe in questo mondo.  

and through the circular valley I saw a people coming silent and weeping, at the pace which the Litanies make in this world.

> 7 Ψυχὰς δ᾽ εἰς τὸν στρογγύλον εἶδον αὐλῶνα  
> (And souls into the round valley I saw)
>
> 8 Ἐρχομένας ἐν σιγῇ καὶ κλαυθμοῖς βάδην,  
> (Coming in silence and weeping at a slow pace,)
>
> 9 Ὡς ποιοῦσ᾽ ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ τὰς λιτανείας.  
> (As they make the litanies in the world.)

- *Ψυχὰς δ᾽ … εἶδον* ‘and souls I saw’ puts the object first, replacing collective singular *gente* with feminine plural *ψυχάς*. Postpositive *δέ*, elided before *εἰς*, continues the description like *e*. *εἶδον*, the suppletive 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*, marks the perception against the preceding imperfect *ἔβλεπον*.
- *εἰς τὸν στρογγύλον … αὐλῶνα* (acc. sg. of *αὐλών*) gives a direction, ‘into the round valley’, rather than the route *per lo vallon tondo*. *ἐρχομένας* (acc. pl. fem., pres. middle participle of *ἔρχομαι*) agrees with *ψυχάς* as the supplementary participle after a verb of perception: ‘I saw them coming’, where Italian has *vidi … venir*.
- *ἐν σιγῇ καὶ κλαυθμοῖς* ‘in silence and in lamentations’ substitutes nouns in the dative of manner under *ἐν* for the two gerunds *tacendo e lagrimando*. Adverbial *βάδην* ‘at a walk’ replaces *al passo*; instead of attaching a relative to *passo*, *ὡς* introduces a comparison of manner.
- *ὡς ποιοῦσ᾽ ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ τὰς λιτανείας* is ‘as they conduct the litanies in the world’. *ποιοῦσ᾽* is elided *ποιοῦσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *ποιέω*), with an indefinite human subject and accusative object *τὰς λιτανείας*. Thus, unlike the English “which the Litanies make”, Greek takes the litanies as what people perform, not as what sets the pace. *ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ* leaves *questo* unexpressed.

> 10 Come il viso mi scese in lor più basso,  
> 11 mirabilmente apparve esser travolto  
> 12 ciascun tra il mento e'l principio del casso :  

When my sight descended lower on them, each seemed wondrously distorted, [between] the chin [and] the commencement of the chest:

> 10 Τοὺς ὀφθαλμοὺς δὲ πρὸς αὐτὰς τείναντί μοι  
> (And as I directed my eyes toward them)
>
> 11 Ἐδόκει παραδόξως ἔχουσ᾽ ἑκάστη  
> (Each seemed strangely to have)
>
> 12 Τὸν πώγων᾽ ἐστραμμένον ἀπ᾽ ἄκρας στήθους,  
> (Its chin turned away from the top of the chest,)

- *τοὺς ὀφθαλμοὺς … πρὸς αὐτὰς τείναντί μοι* ‘to me as I directed my eyes towards them’ replaces the temporal clause *Come il viso mi scese*. The gaze is no longer the subject of a downward movement: the eyes are the direct object of *τείναντι* (dat. sg. masc., first aor. act. participle of *τείνω*). The participle agrees with enclitic *μοι*, the perceiver to whom each figure seemed so; it is not an absolute construction. *πρὸς αὐτάς* refers to feminine *ψυχάς*; *più basso* has no separate equivalent. Postpositive *δέ* marks the transition.
- *ἐδόκει παραδόξως ἔχουσ᾽ ἑκάστη* gives ‘each seemed, strangely, to have’. *ἑκάστη* (nom. sg. fem.) is the subject of augmented *ἐδόκει* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *δοκέω*), replacing *apparve … ciascun*. *ἔχουσ᾽* is elided *ἔχουσα* (nom. sg. fem., pres. act. participle of *ἔχω*), not an infinitive. It describes the subject as she appeared; the more usual Classical complement for ‘seemed to have’ would be the infinitive *ἔχειν*. *παραδόξως* renders *mirabilmente* as ‘contrary to expectation’.
- *τὸν πώγων᾽ ἐστραμμένον ἀπ᾽ ἄκρας στήθους* ‘the chin turned away from the top of the chest’ changes what is twisted: not *ciascun* at a point *tra* chin and chest, but the chin itself, held turned away *from* the chest. *πώγων᾽* is elided *πώγωνα* (acc. sg. of *πώγων*, ‘beard’, here ‘chin’); *ἐστραμμένον* (acc. sg. masc., perf. mid./pass. participle of *στρέφω*) predicates a resulting position of this object of *ἔχουσα*. *ἀπ᾽* is elided *ἀπό*, governing *ἄκρας* ‘summit, upper end’, with dependent *στήθους* (gen. sg. of neuter *στῆθος*).

> 13 chè dalle reni era tornato il volto,  
> 14 ed indietro venir gli convenia,  
> 15 perchè il veder dinanzi era lor tolto.  

[for] the face was turned towards the loins; and they had to come backward, for to look before them was denied.

> 13 Καὶ πρὸς τοὺς νεφροὺς τὴν ὄψιν τετραμμένην,  
> (And its face turned toward the loins,)
>
> 14 Ἀνὰ πόδα βαδίζειν βιαζομένη,  
> (Compelled to walk backwards,)
>
> 15 Ὡς εἰς τοὔμπροσθεν οὐ δυναμένη βλέπειν.  
> (As being unable to look forward.)

- *καὶ πρὸς τοὺς νεφροὺς τὴν ὄψιν τετραμμένην* ‘and the face turned towards the kidneys’ gives a second object and predicate to *ἔχουσα*. Thus *καί* coordinates where *chè* explains. *πρός* with the accusative makes *dalle reni* explicitly directional, towards rather than away from the loins. *τετραμμένην* (acc. sg. fem., reduplicated perf. mid./pass. participle of *τρέπω*) agrees with *ὄψιν* ‘face’ and retains the resultant state of *era tornato il volto* without a finite verb.
- *ἀνὰ πόδα βαδίζειν βιαζομένη* ‘being compelled to walk backwards’ replaces impersonal *gli convenia* and its dative with a personal passive. *βιαζομένη* (nom. sg. fem., pres. mid./pass. participle of *βιάζω*, passive in sense) resumes *ἑκάστη*, not *ὄψιν*. It governs *βαδίζειν* (pres. act. inf. of *βαδίζω*), ‘walk’ rather than *venir*; *ἀνὰ πόδα*, with accusative singular of *πούς*, is an adverbial idiom for movement backwards.
- *ὡς εἰς τοὔμπροσθεν οὐ δυναμένη βλέπειν* ‘as being unable to look forwards’ turns causal *perchè* with a finite passive into *ὡς* with a causal participle, giving the reason as apprehended. *δυναμένη* (nom. sg. fem., pres. middle participle of *δύναμαι*) again agrees with *ἑκάστη* and governs present active *βλέπειν*. In place of *il veder … era lor tolto*, the person is unable, rather than the faculty being taken from her. *τοὔμπροσθεν* is crasis of *τὸ ἔμπροσθεν*: the article substantivizes the adverb, and *εἰς* gives ‘towards what is in front’.

> 16 Forse per forza già di parlasìa  
> 17 si travolse così alcun del tutto ;  

Perhaps by force of palsy some have been thus quite distorted;

> 16 Ἐνδέχετ᾿ ἐξ αἰτίας παραλυσίας  
> (It may be that because of palsy)
>
> 17 Ἐξ αὐτῶν τινας οὕτως ἐξαρθρωθῆναι·  
> (Some of them were thus dislocated;)

- *Ἐνδέχετ᾿ ἐξ αἰτίας παραλυσίας* ‘it is possible that, by reason of paralysis’ converts *Forse* into an impersonal verb, *ἐνδέχετ᾿* for *ἐνδέχεται* (3rd sg. pres. middle ind. of *ἐνδέχομαι*, with final *-αι* elided). *ἐξ αἰτίας* with dependent genitive *παραλυσίας* gives cause rather than the force of *per forza … di parlasìa*. Italian *già* has no explicit equivalent.
- *ἐξ αὐτῶν τινας … ἐξαρθρωθῆναι* is an accusative-and-infinitive clause dependent on *ἐνδέχεται*. *τινας* (acc. pl. of indefinite *τις*) is the subject of *ἐξαρθρωθῆναι* (aor. pass. inf. of *ἐξαρθρόω*, ‘put out of joint’). In place of indefinite singular *alcun*, the plural and the added partitive *ἐξ αὐτῶν* give ‘some of them’, referring back to the previously mentioned figures. The passive infinitive presents *si travolse* as a possible completed occurrence; *οὕτως* retains *così*, but there is no distinct adverb for *del tutto*.

> 18 ma io nol vidi, nè credo che sia.  

but I have not seen, nor do believe it to be so.

> 18 Ἀλλ’ ἐγὼ τοῦτ’ οὐκ εἶδον, οὔτε νομίζω.  
> (But I did not see this, nor do I believe it.)

- *Ἀλλ’ ἐγὼ τοῦτ’ οὐκ εἶδον* closely follows *ma io nol vidi*: *ἀλλ’* is elided *ἀλλά*; explicit *ἐγώ* preserves *io*; and elided *τοῦτ’* for *τοῦτο* (neut. acc. sg. of *οὗτος*) makes the object in *nol* (‘not it’) a demonstrative, ‘this’. *οὐκ* is the form of *οὐ* before a smooth vowel; *εἶδον* is aorist, like *vidi*, rather than a Greek perfect corresponding to English “have not seen”.
- *οὔτε νομίζω* ‘nor do I think so’ coordinates a second negated verb with *οὐκ εἶδον*. *νομίζω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.) preserves the tense of *credo* but leaves its complement to be understood from *τοῦτο* and the preceding possibility; there is no Greek subordinate clause corresponding to *che sia*.

## Lines 19–51 — Pity and the Backward Gaze

> 19 Se Dio ti lasci, lettor, prender frutto  
> 20 di tua lezione, or pensa per te stesso  
> 21 com' io potea tener lo viso asciutto,  

Reader, so God grant thee to take profit of thy reading, now think for thyself how I could keep my visage dry,

> 19 Εἴθ’ ὁ Θεός σοι παράσχοιτ’, ἀναγνῶστα,  
> (May God grant you, O reader,)
>
> 20 Ἐκ τῆς ἀναγνώσεως καρπόν, καὶ σκόπει,  
> (Fruit from your reading, and consider)
>
> 21 Εἰ στεγνοῖς ἠδυνάμην ὀφθαλμοῖς μένειν,  
> (Whether I could remain with dry eyes,)

- *Εἴθ’ ὁ Θεός σοι παράσχοιτ’, ἀναγνῶστα* makes the wish in *Se Dio ti lasci* independent: *εἴθε* with the optative means ‘may it be that’, without a conditional conclusion. *Εἴθ’* loses its final vowel, and its *τ* is aspirated before the rough breathing of *ὁ*. *παράσχοιτ’* is elided *παράσχοιτο*, 3rd sg. second aor. mid. opt. of *παρέχω*, ‘grant’; *σοι* is the enclitic dative of the recipient. The address *lettor* becomes *ἀναγνῶστα*, voc. sg. of *ἀναγνώστης*.
- *ἐκ τῆς ἀναγνώσεως καρπόν* ‘fruit from the reading’ preserves *frutto* as a noun but makes it the direct object of ‘grant’: there is no separate infinitive for *prender*. *ἐκ* with the genitive marks the source; the article, rather than an explicit possessive answering to *tua*, determines *ἀναγνώσεως*.
- *καὶ σκόπει* ‘and consider’ coordinates the wish and the command rather than translating *or* temporally. *σκόπει* is the contracted 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *σκοπέω*; *per te stesso* has no separate equivalent. Its complement is an indirect question introduced by *εἰ* ‘whether’, not an adverb of manner corresponding to *com’*.
- *στεγνοῖς ἠδυνάμην ὀφθαλμοῖς μένειν* ‘I was able to remain with dry eyes’ replaces *tener lo viso asciutto*: the eyes, not the face, are specified, and their condition is expressed by a dative of attendant circumstance. *ἠδυνάμην* (1st sg. imperf. mid. ind. of *δύναμαι*, with the variant augment *ἠ-*) separates the agreeing adjective and noun and takes the pres. act. infinitive *μένειν*.

> 22 quando la nostra imagine da presso  
> 23 vidi sì torta, che il pianto degli occhi  
> 24 le natiche bagnava per lo fesso.  

when near at hand I saw our image so contorted, that the weeping of the eyes bathed the hinder parts at their division?

> 22 Τὴν εἰκόν’ ἡμῶν ἐκ τοῦ σύνεγγυς βλέπων  
> (Seeing our likeness close at hand)
>
> 23 Οὕτως ἐξηρθρωμένην, ὥστε τὸ δάκρυ  
> (So dislocated, that the tear)
>
> 24 Ῥέειν ἐπὶ τοὺς γλουτοὺς ἐκ τῶν ὀμμάτων.  
> (Flowed down upon the buttocks from the eyes.)

- *τὴν εἰκόν’ ἡμῶν … βλέπων* replaces the finite temporal clause *quando … vidi* with a circumstantial pres. act. participle, nom. masc. sg., agreeing with the subject of *ἠδυνάμην*: ‘while looking at our likeness’. *εἰκόν’* is elided *εἰκόνα*, acc. sg. of *εἰκών*; *ἡμῶν* supplies the possessive. *ἐκ τοῦ σύνεγγυς* ‘from close by’ renders *da presso* with a prepositional phrase formed from an articular adverb.
- *οὕτως ἐξηρθρωμένην* ‘so dislocated’ is predicative to *εἰκόνα*. The perf. pass. participle of *ἐξαρθρόω*, acc. fem. sg., specifies the resulting condition more narrowly than *torta*. *οὕτως … ὥστε* answers to *sì … che*, but the Greek uses a result infinitive rather than a finite verb.
- *τὸ δάκρυ ῥέειν* ‘for the tear to flow’ has *τὸ δάκρυ* as the accusative subject of the pres. act. infinitive of *ῥέω*, here uncontracted. The singular tear replaces *il pianto*; instead of transitive *bagnava* with *le natiche* as object, *ἐπὶ τοὺς γλουτούς* gives the direction of flow and *ἐκ τῶν ὀμμάτων* its source. *Per lo fesso* has no equivalent.

> 25 Certo i' piangea, poggiato ad un de' rocchi  
> 26 del duro scoglio, sì che la mia scorta  
> 27 mi disse: " Ancor se' tu degli altri sciocchi?  

Certainly I wept, leaning on one of the rocks of the hard cliff, so that my Escort said to me: "Art thou, too, like the other fools?

> 25 Ἔκλαιον δ’ ἠρεισμένος ἐπὶ γωνίαν  
> (And I was weeping, leaning against a corner)
>
> 26 Τοῦ σκληροῦ κρημνοῦ· τότε δ’ οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ  
> (Of the hard crag; and then my Guide)
>
> 27 Εἶπεν· “Ἔτι σὺ τῶν ἀφρόνων εἶς ἄλλων ;  
> (Said: “Are you still one of the other fools?)

- *ἔκλαιον δ’* ‘and I was weeping’ retains the imperfect of *piangea* (*ἔκλαιον*, 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *κλαίω*); connective *δέ*, elided before the next vowel, does not reproduce the asseveration *Certo*. *ἠρεισμένος* is the nom. masc. sg. perf. mid.-pass. participle of *ἐρείδω*: ‘having braced myself’, hence ‘leaning’. *ἐπὶ γωνίαν τοῦ σκληροῦ κρημνοῦ* gives the point of support as ‘against a corner of the hard cliff’, dropping the partitive *un de’ rocchi*.
- *τότε δ’ οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ εἶπεν* ‘and then my guide said’ substitutes temporal succession for consecutive *sì che* and omits *mi*. *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, with the rough breathing of the article; *ἡγήτωρ* is a masc. agent noun in place of fem. *scorta*. *εἶπεν* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of the verb ‘say’.
- *ἔτι σὺ τῶν ἀφρόνων εἶς ἄλλων;* puts *Ancor* in temporal terms, ‘still’, rather than Carlyle’s “too”. The construction calls for ‘one of the other fools’, with *τῶν ἀφρόνων … ἄλλων* gen. pl. and the copula understood. The printed *εἶς* is thus functioning as the numeral, normally written *εἷς* with rough breathing, not as the copula *εἶ* ‘you are’.

> 28 Qui vive la pietà quando è ben morta.  

Here pity lives when it is altogether dead.

> 28 „Ὧδ’ ἔλεος ζῇ, καίτοι τεθνηκὼς ἤδη.  
> („Here pity lives, even though already dead.)

- *ὧδ’* is elided *ὧδε*, ‘here’; *ἔλεος ζῇ* follows *vive la pietà* with *ἔλεος* in the masculine, as the concord of *τεθνηκώς* shows. *ζῇ* is 3rd sg. contracted pres. act. ind. of *ζάω*.
- *καίτοι τεθνηκὼς ἤδη* ‘although already dead’ changes both the relation and the qualification: temporal *quando* becomes concessive *καίτοι* with a participle, intensive *ben* temporal *ἤδη*. *τεθνηκώς* is the nom. masc. sg. perf. act. participle of *θνῄσκω*, expressing the state consequent on dying without a copula.

> 29 Chi è più scellerato che colui  
> 30 che al giudicio divin compassion porta?  

Who more impious than he that sorrows at God's judgment?

> 29 „Ἔστι τις μᾶλλον ἐπίψογος ἐκείνου,  
> („Is anyone more blameworthy than he)
>
> 30 „Ὃς οὓς Θεὸς ἐδίκασ’ αὐτὸς οἰκτείρει ;  
> („Who himself pities those whom God has judged?)

- *ἔστι τις μᾶλλον ἐπίψογος ἐκείνου;* ‘is there anyone more deserving of blame than that man?’ recasts *Chi è più scellerato* as a yes-or-no question with indefinite *τις*, not interrogative *τίς*. *μᾶλλον* makes the adjective comparative in sense; *ἐκείνου*, gen. masc. sg. of *ἐκεῖνος*, is the genitive of comparison answering to *che colui*.
- *ὃς οὓς Θεὸς ἐδίκασ’ αὐτὸς οἰκτείρει* gives *compassion porta* a personal object not expressed in the Italian: ‘who himself pities those whom God judged’. Nom. sg. *ὅς* resumes *ἐκείνου* as subject of *οἰκτείρει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *οἰκτείρω*); acc. masc. pl. *οὕς* is object of *ἐδίκασ’* (elided *ἐδίκασε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *δικάζω*). The latter relative clause, with its antecedent unexpressed, supplies the object of ‘pities’. *Αὐτός* emphasizes the human subject in contrast to *Θεός*: the noun phrase *al giudicio divin* has become a clause with God as agent.

> 31 Drizza la testa, drizza, e vedi a cui  
> 32 s' aperse agli occhi de' Teban la terra,  
> 33 per ch' ei gridavan tutti: ' Dove rui,  
> 34 Anfiarao? perchè lasci la guerra? '  

Raise up thy head, raise up, and see him for whom the earth opened herself before the eyes of the Thebans, whereat they all cried, 'Whither rushest thou, Amphiaräus? Why leavest thou the war?'

> 31 „Ἆρον, ἆρον κεφαλήν· ἐκεῖνον βλέπε,  
> („Lift up, lift up your head; behold him)
>
> 32 „Ὃν κατέπιεν ἡ γῆ πρὸ τῶν ὀμμάτων  
> („Whom the earth swallowed up before the eyes)
>
> 33 „Τῶν Θηβαίων κραζόντων· ‘“Ποῦ δὴ σὺ πίπτεις,  
> („Of the Thebans crying out: ‘“Where then are you falling,)
>
> 34 „Ἀμφιάραε ; τί τὸν πόλεμον φεύγεις ;”’ —  
> („Amphiaräus? Why do you flee the war?”’ —)

- *ἆρον, ἆρον κεφαλήν* brings the repeated verbs together before the bare accusative ‘head’. *ἆρον* is the 2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *αἴρω*, ‘lift’, rather than literally ‘straighten’. *ἐκεῖνον βλέπε* follows without *e*; the demonstrative supplies an explicit antecedent and the imperative of *βλέπω* is present, ‘look at him’.
- *ὃν κατέπιεν ἡ γῆ* ‘whom the earth swallowed’ changes *a cui s’aperse … la terra* from a reflexive verb with an oblique relative to a transitive verb with an accusative relative. *κατέπιεν* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *καταπίνω*, with internal augment and movable *ν*.
- *πρὸ τῶν ὀμμάτων τῶν Θηβαίων κραζόντων* attaches ‘of the Thebans’ to ‘before the eyes’; *κραζόντων*, gen. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *κράζω*, agrees with that dependent genitive. Their cry is simultaneous rather than introduced by the separate consequential *per ch’*; *tutti* is omitted.
- *ποῦ δὴ σὺ πίπτεις, Ἀμφιάραε;* uses *ποῦ* ‘where’ with a verb of motion rather than specifically directional *ποῖ*. *δή* urges the question, ‘where, then’; *σύ* is explicit, and *πίπτεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind.) interprets *rui* as ‘fall’. *Ἀμφιάραε* is the vocative. In *τί τὸν πόλεμον φεύγεις;* adverbial *τί* means ‘why’, and transitive *φεύγεις* gives *lasci* the stronger sense ‘flee’.

> 35 e non restò di ruinare a valle  
> 36 fino a Minòs, che ciascheduno afferra.  

And he ceased not rushing headlong down to Minos, who lays hold on every sinner.

> 35 “Εἰς βυθὸν ἀπὸ βυθοῦ κατεκυλίσθη  
> (“Down from depth to depth he rolled)
>
> 36 „ Ἄχρι Μίνωος τοῦ δραττομένου πάντων.  
> („ As far as Minos, who lays hold of all.)

- *εἰς βυθὸν ἀπὸ βυθοῦ κατεκυλίσθη* ‘into a depth from a depth he rolled down’ omits the coordination and converts negative durative *non restò di ruinare* into a single bounded action. Goal (*εἰς* + acc.) precedes source (*ἀπό* + gen.); the repeated *βυθός* expands *a valle*. *κατεκυλίσθη* is 3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *κατακυλίω*, with internal augment and intransitive sense.
- *ἄχρι Μίνωος* ‘as far as Minos’ uses *ἄχρι* as a preposition governing the genitive, here the uncontracted form of *Μίνως*. *τοῦ δραττομένου πάντων* stands in apposition to *Μίνωος*: the articular pres. mid. participle of *δράττομαι*, gen. masc. sg., replaces finite *che … afferra*. The verb governs the genitive *πάντων*, ‘of all’, collective in place of distributive *ciascheduno*; Greek supplies no noun for “sinner”.

> 37 Mira che ha fatto petto delle spalle:  
> 38 perchè volle veder troppo davante,  
> 39 di retro guarda e fa ritroso calle.  

Mark how he has made a breast of his shoulders: because he wished to see too far before him, he now looks behind and goes backward.

> 37 „ Σκόπει, πῶς ἐποίησατ᾽ ἐξ ὤμων στῆθος·  
> („ Look how he has made a breast out of his shoulders;)
>
> 38 „ Θέλων γὰρ βλέπειν λίαν πρόσω, νῦν βλέπει  
> („ For wishing to see too far ahead, now he sees)
>
> 39 „ Ἐξόπισθεν, βαδίζων ἀναστροφάδην.  
> („ Behind him, walking backwards.)

- *σκόπει, πῶς ἐποίησατ’ ἐξ ὤμων στῆθος* makes *che ha fatto* explicitly ‘how he made’. *ἐποίησατ’* is elided *ἐποιήσατο*, 3rd sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ποιέω*, ‘made for himself’, in place of the Italian compound perfect. *ἐξ ὤμων* (gen. pl.) identifies what the breast is made out of; Musurus places it before the result, acc. sg. *στῆθος*, reversing *petto delle spalle*.
- *θέλων γὰρ βλέπειν λίαν πρόσω* gives the explanation with postpositive *γάρ* and a circumstantial participle: *θέλων*, nom. masc. sg. pres. act. of *θέλω*, replaces finite past *volle*, while pres. act. infinitive *βλέπειν* answers to *veder*. *λίαν πρόσω* is ‘too far forward’.
- *νῦν βλέπει ἐξόπισθεν* supplies ‘now’ and repeats the same verb of sight where Italian varies *veder* and *guarda*. *βαδίζων ἀναστροφάδην* ‘walking backwards’ subordinates the second action rather than coordinating it with *e*: the pres. act. participle of *βαδίζω* takes an adverb of manner in *-δην*, with no separate noun for *calle*.

> 40 Vedi Tiresia, che mutò sembiante,  
> 41 quando di maschio femmina divenne,  
> 42 cangiandosi le membra tutte quante;  

Behold Tiresias who changed his aspect, when of male he was made woman, all his limbs transforming;

> 40 „ Ἰδὲ τὸν Τειρεσίαν, ὃς μετεπλάσθη,  
> („ Behold Tiresias, who was transformed,)
>
> 41 „ Ἐξ ἀνδρὸς γυνὴ γενόμενος, καὶ πάντων  
> („ Becoming a woman from a man, and all)
>
> 42 „ Ὁμοῦ τῶν μελῶν αὐτοῦ μεταλλαχθέντων,  
> („ His limbs together being changed,)

- *ἰδὲ τὸν Τειρεσίαν* uses the 2nd sg. second aor. act. imperative *ἰδέ* (‘see’, from the aorist *εἶδον*) with the personal name in the accusative and with the article. *ὃς μετεπλάσθη* ‘who was reshaped’ renders active *mutò sembiante* by the 3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *μεταπλάσσω*, internally augmented: the object *sembiante* disappears into the verb.
- *ἐξ ἀνδρὸς γυνὴ γενόμενος* ‘having become a woman from a man’ replaces *quando … divenne* by an aor. mid. participle of *γίγνομαι*. *γενόμενος* agrees in the nom. masc. sg. with *ὅς*, not with its predicate noun *γυνή*; *ἐξ ἀνδρός* marks the initial state.
- *καὶ πάντων ὁμοῦ τῶν μελῶν αὐτοῦ μεταλλαχθέντων* gives *cangiandosi le membra tutte quante* a genitive absolute: *μεταλλαχθέντων*, aor. pass. participle of *μεταλλάσσω*, agrees with gen. neut. pl. *τῶν μελῶν* ‘the limbs’. *πάντων* and adverbial *ὁμοῦ* ‘together’ reinforce the totality; *αὐτοῦ* makes the possessor explicit.

> 43 e prima poi ribatter gli convenne  
> 44 li due serpenti avvolti con la verga,  
> 45 che riavesse le maschili penne.  

and afterwards he had again to strike the two involved serpents with his rod, before he could resume his manly plumes.

> 43 „ Ὥστ᾽ ἐδέησεν αὐτὸν πάλιν τῇ ῥάβδῳ  
> („ So that he had need again with his rod)
>
> 44 „ Τύψαι τοὺς δύο συμπεπλεγμένους ὄφεις,  
> („ To strike the two entwined serpents,)
>
> 45 „ Πρὶν τὴν ἀνδρικὴν ἐπαναλάβῃ φύσιν.  
> („ Before he could recover his manly nature.)

- *ὥστ’ ἐδέησεν* ‘so that it became necessary’ makes the sequel a result rather than simply adding it with *e … poi*. Unlike the result infinitive in lines 23–24, elided *ὥστε* here introduces a finite indicative. *ἐδέησεν* is the impersonal 3rd sg. aor. act. of *δέω* in the sense of *δεῖ*. Musurus gives *gli convenne* as *ἐδέησεν αὐτὸν … τύψαι*: the person obliged is accusative subject of the infinitive.
- *πάλιν τῇ ῥάβδῳ τύψαι* distributes *ribatter* between ‘again’ and *τύψαι*, aor. act. infinitive of *τύπτω*. *τῇ ῥάβδῳ* is instrumental dative. *τοὺς δύο συμπεπλεγμένους ὄφεις* renders *li due serpenti avvolti*: with *δύο* the article, participle, and noun are plural, not dual. *συμπεπλεγμένους*, acc. masc. pl. perf. mid.-pass. participle of *συμπλέκω*, describes the resulting state, ‘entwined’.
- *πρὶν … ἐπαναλάβῃ* combines the separated *prima … che* into ‘before’. *ἐπαναλάβῃ* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. subj. of *ἐπαναλαμβάνω*, unaugmented. Bare *πρίν* with this subjunctive closely reflects *che riavesse*; after an affirmative main clause classical Attic would more naturally use *πρίν* with an infinitive. *τὴν ἀνδρικὴν … φύσιν* ‘his masculine nature’ interprets rather than reproduces *le maschili penne*.

> 46 Aronta è quel che al ventre gli s' atterga,  
> 47 che nei monti di Luni, dove ronca  
> 48 lo Carrarese che di sotto alberga,  

That is Aruns coming back before him, who in the mountains of Luni, where hoes the Carrarese that dwells beneath,

> 46 „ Ὅδ᾽ ἔστ᾽ Ἀρόντας, ἐπ᾽ αὐτοῦ τὴν γαστέρα  
> („ This is Aruns, against his belly)
>
> 47 „ Τὰ νῶτ᾽ ἐρείδων, ὃς ἐν ὄρεσι Λούνης,  
> („ Pressing his back, who in the mountains of Luni,)
>
> 48 „ Ἔνθ᾽ ὁ Καῤῥαρεὺς οἰκεῖ τὴν κάτω χώραν  
> („ Where the Carrarese dwells in the lower region)

- *ὅδ’ ἔστ’ Ἀρόντας* ‘this one is Aruns’ reverses *Aronta è quel* and uses the near demonstrative. *ὅδ’* and *ἔστ’* are successive elisions of *ὅδε* and *ἔστι*; *Ἀρόντας* is nominative. *ἐπ’ αὐτοῦ τὴν γαστέρα τὰ νῶτ’ ἐρείδων* takes *al ventre gli s’atterga* physically as ‘pressing his back against the other’s belly’. *ἐπ’* governs acc. *τὴν γαστέρα*; the intervening *αὐτοῦ* is possessive. *τὰ νῶτ’* (elided *νῶτα*, neut. acc. pl.) is the object of *ἐρείδων*, nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *ἐρείδω*, in place of the Italian relative clause.
- *ὃς ἐν ὄρεσι Λούνης* retains the next relative *che*; its verb is delayed until line 49. *ὄρεσι* is dat. pl. of the neuter s-stem *ὄρος* after locative *ἐν*; *Λούνης* is a dependent gen. sg.
- *ἔνθ’ ὁ Καῤῥαρεὺς οἰκεῖ τὴν κάτω χώραν* ‘where the Carrarese inhabits the land below’ promotes *alberga* from the relative clause to the principal verb of the local clause. *ἔνθ’* is *ἔνθα* elided before *ὁ*. *Καῤῥαρεύς* is a nominative ethnic in *-εύς*, with the traditional smooth and rough breathings on doubled rho. Contracted *οἰκεῖ* takes an accusative of the place inhabited; adverb *κάτω* in attributive position qualifies the supplied *χώραν*.

> 49 ebbe tra i bianchi marmi la spelonca  
> 50 per sua dimora; onde a guardar le stelle  
> 51 e il mar non gli era la veduta tronca.  

amongst [the] white marbles had the cave for his abode; from which he could observe the stars and the sea with unobstructed view.

> 49 „ Γεωργῶν, ἄντρον ἔσχ᾽ ἐκ λευκῶν μαρμάρων  
> („ Tilling the soil, had a cave of white marbles)
>
> 50 „ Πρὸς κατοικίαν, ὅθεν θεωρεῖν εἶχεν  
> („ For his dwelling, whence he was able to behold)
>
> 51 „ Ἀστέρας καὶ θάλασσαν ἀνεμποδίστως.  
> („ The stars and the sea unhindered.)

- *γεωργῶν* completes the preceding local clause: nom. masc. sg. contracted pres. act. participle of *γεωργέω*, ‘tilling’, agrees with *ὁ Καῤῥαρεύς*. Thus finite *ronca* becomes an attendant activity, and its specific sense is generalized.
- *ἄντρον ἔσχ’ ἐκ λευκῶν μαρμάρων πρὸς κατοικίαν* resumes *ὅς*: *ἔσχ’* is elided *ἔσχε*, 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἔχω*, answering to *ebbe*. Bare *ἄντρον* is its object. *ἐκ* with the genitive suggests the cave’s material, ‘of white marbles’, instead of its situation *tra i bianchi marmi*. *πρὸς κατοικίαν*, with accusative of purpose, renders *per sua dimora* without an explicit possessive.
- *ὅθεν θεωρεῖν εἶχεν* keeps the relative adverb *onde*, ‘whence’, but takes the positive route to *non gli era la veduta tronca*: *εἶχεν* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἔχω*) with the pres. act. infinitive *θεωρεῖν* means ‘he was able to observe’. *ἀστέρας καὶ θάλασσαν* are its two anarthrous accusative objects. Final *ἀνεμποδίστως* ‘unhindered’ expresses by a privative adverb modifying the act of seeing what Italian predicates, with a negated adjective, of *la veduta*.

## Lines 52–84 — Manto and the Waters of Benacus

> 52 E quella che ricopre le mammelle,  
> 53 che tu non vedi, con le trecce sciolte,  
> 54 e ha di là ogni pilosa pelle,  

And she that covers her bosom, which thou seest not, with her flowing tresses, and has all her hair[y skin] on the other side,

> 52 „ Ἐκείνη δ᾽, ἣ πλοκάμοις περικαλύπτει  
> („ And she who with tresses covers over)
>
> 53 „ Λελυμένοις τοὺς μαστούς, ὅπερ οὐ βλέπεις,  
> („ Unbound her breasts, which you do not see,)
>
> 54 „ Ἀνάστροφόν τ᾽ ἔχει πᾶν τριχῶδες δέρμα,  
> („ And has all her hairy skin turned backwards,)

- *Ἐκείνη δ᾽, ἣ … περικαλύπτει* ‘and that woman who covers over’ keeps the demonstrative and relative of *E quella che*. Both pronouns are feminine nominative singular, but *ἐκείνη* is the subject of the still deferred *ἦν* (55), *ἣ* of the present active *περικαλύπτει*. The connective *δέ* is postpositive and elided before the relative.
- *πλοκάμοις … λελυμένοις* ‘with tresses let loose’ brings the instrument before the object *τοὺς μαστούς* ‘the breasts’, reversing *le mammelle … con le trecce sciolte*. The dative does the work of *con*; *λελυμένοις* (perfect middle-passive participle of *λύω*, dative masculine plural) expresses the resulting state, and is separated from its noun by the verb.
- *ὅπερ οὐ βλέπεις* ‘which very thing you do not see’ follows the object, but *ὅπερ* is neuter singular, not masculine plural in agreement with *μαστούς*: it takes up what has just been described collectively. It is the accusative object of *βλέπεις* (present active indicative, second singular); Greek needs no separate *tu*.
- *ἀνάστροφόν τ᾽ ἔχει πᾶν τριχῶδες δέρμα* ‘and has the whole hairy skin turned about’ substitutes the predicative adjective *ἀνάστροφον* for the local *di là*. It agrees with the accusative object *δέρμα*, as do *πᾶν* and the two-termination adjective *τριχῶδες* (from *τριχώδης*). Enclitic *τε*, elided before *ἔχει*, adds a second predicate to the relative clause; the extra acute on *ἀνάστροφόν* is due to the enclitic.

> 55 Manto fu, che cercò per terre molte,  
> 56 poscia si pose là dove nacqu’ io:  

was Manto, who searched through many lands, then settled there where I was born:

> 55 „Ἦν Μαντώ, διὰ πολλῶν πλανητευθεῖσα  
> („Was Manto, who wandered through many)
>
> 56 „ Τόπων, ἣ τέλος ἦλθεν ἔνθ᾽ ἐγεννήθην·  
> („ Places, and at last came where I was born;)

- *ἦν Μαντώ* retains the identification but reverses *Manto fu*: *ἦν* is the third singular imperfect of *εἰμί*, with the name in the predicate nominative. For *che cercò per terre molte* Musurus uses *διὰ πολλῶν πλανητευθεῖσα τόπων* ‘having wandered through many places’. The feminine nominative singular participle, aorist passive in form from *πλανητεύω*, has intransitive sense; *διά* governs the genitive *πολλῶν … τόπων*, divided by the participle.
- *ἣ τέλος ἦλθεν* ‘who finally came’ introduces a finite relative clause where Italian continues with *poscia si pose*. Adverbial *τέλος* marks the outcome, while *ἦλθεν* (third singular second aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι*) expresses arrival rather than settlement. Elided *ἔνθ᾽* (*ἔνθα*) combines *là dove* in one relative adverb. *ἐγεννήθην* (first singular aorist passive indicative of *γεννάω*) gives *nacqu’ io* as ‘I was born’, without an emphatic pronoun.

> 57 onde un poco mi piace che m’ ascolte.  

whence it pleases me a little to have thee listen to me.

> 57 „ Διὸ καὶ σέ μου μικρὸν ἀκούειν θέλω.  
> („ Wherefore I wish you also to listen to me a little.)

- *διὸ καί* ‘for which reason also’ makes *onde* an explicit inference. *θέλω* ‘I wish’ replaces impersonal *mi piace* with a personal present active indicative; *σέ*, the accusative subject of its present active infinitive *ἀκούειν*, replaces the unexpressed subject of *che m’ ascolte*. The enclitic genitive *μου* denotes the person heard. *μικρόν*, adverbial accusative ‘a little’, is placed with the listening rather than with the verb of pleasure.

> 58 Poscia che il padre suo di vita uscìo  
> 59 e venne serva la città di Baco,  

After her father went out of life, and the city of Bacchus came to be enslaved,

> 58 „ Τοῦ πατρὸς αὐτῆς τῆς ζωῆς ἐξελθόντος,  
> („ After her father had departed from life,)
>
> 59 „ Καὶ δουλωθείσης τῆς πόλεως τοῦ Βάκχου,  
> („ And the city of Bacchus had been enslaved,)

- For the two finite verbs under *Poscia che*, Greek has two coordinated genitive absolutes. *τοῦ πατρὸς αὐτῆς … ἐξελθόντος* ‘her father having gone out’ has the irregular genitive *πατρός* of *πατήρ*, possessive *αὐτῆς*, and the masculine genitive singular second aorist active participle of *ἐξέρχομαι*. The further genitive *τῆς ζωῆς* marks that from which he departed, closely following *di vita*.
- *καὶ δουλωθείσης τῆς πόλεως τοῦ Βάκχου* ‘and the city of Bacchus having been enslaved’ puts the participle before its subject. *δουλωθείσης* (aorist passive of *δουλόω*, genitive feminine singular) renders *venne serva* with one verb; *πόλεως* is the Attic genitive singular of *πόλις*. The aorist participles present both events as antecedent to the next finite verb, without a conjunction meaning ‘after’.

> 60 questa gran tempo per lo mondo gìo.  

she for a long time roamed the world.

> 60 „ Αὕτη τὸν κόσμον ἐπὶ μακρὸν διῆλθεν.  
> („ She traversed the world for a long time.)

- *αὕτη* ‘this woman’ resumes *questa* after the absolutes. *τὸν κόσμον … διῆλθεν* ‘she traversed the world’ makes the world an accusative of the space traversed rather than the complement of *per*. *διῆλθεν* is the third singular second aorist active indicative of *διέρχομαι*, with the augmented stem *ἠλθ-* after the preverb. *ἐπὶ μακρόν* ‘for a long while’ uses *ἐπί* with an accusative of duration, leaving ‘time’ understood where Italian has *gran tempo*.

> 61 Suso in Italia bella giace un laco  
> 62 al piè dell’ alpe, che serra Lamagna  
> 63 sopra Tiralli, ch’ ha nome Benaco.  

Up in beautiful Italy there lies a lake, at the foot of the Alps which shut in Germany above the Tyrol, [which] is called Benacus.

> 61 „ Ἐν Ἰταλίᾳ τῇ καλῇ κεῖται λίμνη  
> („ In fair Italy there lies a lake)
>
> 62 „ Ὑπ᾿ Ἄλπεις τὰς κυκλούσας τὴν Γερμανίαν  
> („ Beneath the Alps that encircle Germany)
>
> 63 „ Ὑπὲρ Τύριλιν, ἣ Βήνακος καλεῖται.  
> („ Above Tyrol, which is called Benacus.)

- *ἐν Ἰταλίᾳ τῇ καλῇ* ‘in Italy the fair’ puts the epithet after the proper name with its own article; there is no independent equivalent of *Suso*. *κεῖται λίμνη* closely follows *giace un laco*: the third singular present of *κεῖμαι* precedes its indefinite subject, which needs no article.
- *ὑπ᾿ Ἄλπεις τὰς κυκλούσας τὴν Γερμανίαν* ‘below the Alps encircling Germany’ gives *al piè dell’ alpe* by elided *ὑπό* with the accusative of position below. The plural mountain name takes an articular present active participle (*κυκλούσας*, feminine accusative plural of *κυκλόω*) in place of singular *che serra*; its object has the Greek geographical name and article.
- *ὑπὲρ Τύριλιν* renders *sopra Tiralli* by *ὑπέρ* with the accusative, ‘beyond’. *ἣ Βήνακος καλεῖται* ‘which is called Benacus’ resumes feminine *λίμνη* across the intervening description. The name is a predicate nominative with the present passive *καλεῖται*, replacing active *ch’ ha nome*.

> 64 Per mille fonti, credo, e più si bagna,  
> 65 tra Garda e Val Camonica, Apennino  
> 66 dell’ acqua che nel detto lago stagna.  

Through a thousand fountains, I believe, and more, the [A]pennine, between Garda and Val Camonica, is irrigated by the water which stagnates in that lake.

> 64 „ ΤΑ᾽πέννινον πλείονες πηγαὶ χιλίων  
> („ More than a thousand springs the Apennine)
>
> 65 „ Ἀρδεύουσ᾽ ἐντὸς τῆς κοιλάδος Καμούνων·  
> („ Water within the valley of the Camuni;)
>
> 66 „ Τὰ δ᾽ ὕδατ᾽ εἶτα σωρεύετ᾽ ἐν τῇ λίμνῃ.  
> („ And the waters then gather in the lake.)

- In *ΤΑ᾽πέννινον πλείονες πηγαὶ χιλίων ἀρδεύουσ᾽* the mountain name, printed with an unusual initial apostrophe, is the fronted accusative object, and *πηγαί* ‘springs’ the nominative plural subject. *ἀρδεύουσ᾽* is elided *ἀρδεύουσι*, third plural present active of *ἀρδεύω*: instead of *si bagna … Apennino*, the springs themselves ‘water the Apennine’. *πλείονες … χιλίων* ‘more than a thousand’ uses the comparative with a genitive of comparison, compressing *mille … e più* and omitting *credo*.
- *ἐντὸς τῆς κοιλάδος Καμούνων* ‘within the valley of the Camuni’ changes *tra Garda e Val Camonica* from an interval to a single locality; Garda drops out. *ἐντός* governs *κοιλάδος* (genitive singular of *κοιλάς*), itself qualified by the ethnic genitive plural *Καμούνων*.
- *τὰ δ᾽ ὕδατ᾽ εἶτα σωρεύετ᾽ ἐν τῇ λίμνῃ* ‘and the waters then collect in the lake’ makes a new clause of *dell’ acqua che nel detto lago stagna*. *εἶτα* supplies sequence, *σωρεύεται* expresses accumulation rather than stillness, and the article alone identifies the lake. *δέ*, *ὕδατα*, and *σωρεύεται* are all elided; the singular present middle-passive verb with neuter plural *τὰ ὕδατα* is regular Greek agreement.

> 67 Loco è nel mezzo là, dove il Trentino  
> 68 pastore e quel di Brescia e il Veronese  
> 69 segnar potría, se fesse quel cammino.  

At the middle there is a place where the Trentine pastor, and he of Brescia, and the Veronese might bless, if they went that way.

> 67 „ Μεσηγὺς δ᾽ ἔστι τόπος ἔνθα Τριδέντου,  
> („ And in the midst is a place where Trent's,)
>
> 68 „ Βριξίας καὶ Βηρώνης ἐξῆν ποιμέσιν  
> („ Brescia's, and Verona's shepherds might)
>
> 69 „ Εὐλογεῖν, εἰ πορείαν τήνδ᾽ ἐποιοῦντο.  
> („ Give blessing, if they made this journey.)

- *μεσηγὺς δ᾽ ἔστι τόπος ἔνθα* ‘and midway there is a place where’ fronts the poetic local adverb *μεσηγύς* for *nel mezzo*. Existential *ἔστι* precedes the indefinite noun, while relative *ἔνθα* introduces the description of the place.
- *Τριδέντου, Βριξίας καὶ Βηρώνης … ποιμέσιν* replaces the Italian sequence of adjectival and elliptical singular designations with three genitives of place depending on one plural *ποιμέσιν* (dative plural of *ποιμήν*). The dative marks the persons for whom it was open *εὐλογεῖν* ‘to bless’, a present active infinitive interpreting *segnar*.
- *ἐξῆν … εἰ πορείαν τήνδ᾽ ἐποιοῦντο* ‘it was open to them … if they made this journey’ renders *potría, se fesse quel cammino*. *ἐξῆν* is the impersonal imperfect indicative of *ἔξεστι*; the condition likewise has an imperfect indicative, not an optative. The imperfect of a verb of possibility can convey unrealized possibility without *ἄν*. *ἐποιοῦντο* (third plural imperfect middle of *ποιέω*) agrees in number with the persons understood: *πορείαν ποιεῖσθαι* is ‘to make a journey’. Elided *τήνδ᾽* (*τήνδε*) points to ‘this’ journey for Italian *quel*.

> 70 Siede Peschiera, bello e forte arnese  
> 71 da fronteggiar Bresciani e Bergamaschi,  
> 72 ove la riva intorno più discese.  

Peschiera, a fortress beautiful and strong to front the Brescians and the Bergamese, sits where the shore around is lowest.

> 70 „ Πισκαρία δέ, καλὸν κὠχυρὸν τεῖχος  
> („ And Peschiera, a fair and strong fortress)
>
> 71 „ Ἀπαντικρὺ Βριξίων καὶ Βεργαμίνων  
> („ Over against the Brescians and Bergamese,)
>
> 72 „ Παρὰ τὴν ὄχθην τὴν κατωτέρω κεῖται.  
> („ Lies beside the lower shore.)

- *Πισκαρία δέ … κεῖται* ‘and Peschiera lies’ transfers initial *Siede* to the end. Appositional *καλὸν κὠχυρὸν τεῖχος* takes *arnese* specifically as a fortification: the two neuter nominative adjectives agree with *τεῖχος*, not with the town name. *κὠχυρόν* is crasis of *καὶ ὀχυρόν*.
- *ἀπαντικρὺ Βριξίων καὶ Βεργαμίνων* ‘directly opposite the Brescians and Bergamese’ turns the purpose or capacity expressed by *da fronteggiar* into position: *ἀπαντικρύ* governs the ethnic genitives. *παρὰ τὴν ὄχθην τὴν κατωτέρω* ‘beside the lower shore’ similarly reduces *ove la riva intorno più discese* to a prepositional phrase. *παρά* takes the accusative of position alongside; the repeated article makes the comparative adverb *κατωτέρω* an attributive modifier. There is no verb of descent or separate *intorno*.

> 73 Ivi convien che tutto quanto caschi  
> 74 ciò che in grembo a Benaco star non può,  
> 75 e fassi fiume giù per verdi paschi.  

There all that in the bosom of Benacus cannot stay, has to descend and make itself a river, down through green pastures.

> 73 „ Ἐκεῖ δ᾽ ἐκρέει πᾶν περισσεῦον ὕδωρ,  
> („ And there flows out all the superfluous water,)
>
> 74 „ Ὃ χωρεῖν οὐ δύναται Βηνάκου στέρνα,  
> („ Which the bosom of Benacus cannot hold,)
>
> 75 „Ποιοῦν ποταμὸν διὰ χλωρῶν λειμώνων.  
> („Making a river through green meadows.)

- *ἐκεῖ δ᾽ ἐκρέει πᾶν περισσεῦον ὕδωρ* ‘and there all excess water flows out’ replaces *Ivi convien che tutto quanto caschi* with a direct present indicative (*ἐκρέει*, third singular of *ἐκρέω*, uncontracted). Neither necessity nor falling has a separate equivalent. Greek supplies the noun *ὕδωρ*, qualified by *πᾶν* and the present active participle *περισσεῦον* (neuter nominative singular of *περισσεύω*).
- *ὃ χωρεῖν οὐ δύναται Βηνάκου στέρνα* recasts *ciò che in grembo a Benaco star non può*: it is the bosom that cannot contain the water, rather than the water that cannot stay. On this construction *ὅ* is the neuter accusative object of transitive *χωρεῖν* ‘to hold’ (present active infinitive of *χωρέω*); *στέρνα* is the neuter plural subject of the singular *δύναται* (present middle indicative of *δύναμαι*), with possessive *Βηνάκου*.
- *ποιοῦν ποταμόν* ‘forming a river’ continues the description of *ὕδωρ* with a contracted present active participle of *ποιέω*, neuter nominative singular, instead of finite reflexive *fassi fiume*. *διὰ χλωρῶν λειμώνων* retains the route *per verdi paschi* with *διά* and the genitive plural; *giù* is not separately rendered.

> 76 Tosto che l’ acqua a correr mette co  
> 77 non più Benaco, ma Mincio si chiama  
> 78 fino a Governo, dove cade in Po.  

Soon as the water sets head to run, it is no longer named Benacus, but Mincio,—to Governo where it falls into the Po.

> 76 „Ἐντεῦθεν ῥέων ὁ ποταμὸς καλεῖται  
> („Flowing thence, the river is called)
>
> 77 „Οὐχὶ Βήνακος, ἀλλὰ Μίγκιος, ἕως  
> („Not Benacus, but Mincio, all the way)
>
> 78 „Εἰς τὸ Γοβέρνον, ἔνθ᾽ ἐκχεῖτ᾽ ἐν τῷ Πάδῳ.  
> („To Governo, where it pours out into the Po.)

- *ἐντεῦθεν ῥέων ὁ ποταμός* ‘the river flowing from there’ replaces the temporal clause *Tosto che l’ acqua a correr mette co* with a local adverb and a simultaneous present participle. *ῥέων* (present active of *ῥέω*) is masculine nominative singular with the new explicit subject *ὁ ποταμός*; there is no expression for the beginning of the flow.
- *καλεῖται οὐχὶ Βήνακος, ἀλλὰ Μίγκιος* puts the present passive before its two predicate nominatives, where Italian puts *si chiama* after the names. Emphatic *οὐχί* … *ἀλλά* gives ‘not … but’, without the temporal force of *non più*.
- *ἕως εἰς τὸ Γοβέρνον* combines the limit word *ἕως* with directional *εἰς* and the accusative, closely matching *fino a*. *ἔνθ᾽ ἐκχεῖτ᾽ ἐν τῷ Πάδῳ* ‘where it empties in the Po’ has elided *ἔνθα* and *ἐκχεῖται* (third singular present middle-passive of *ἐκχέω*). For *cade in Po* the verb expresses discharge, but the complement is local *ἐν* with the dative, not directional *εἰς*.

> 79 Non molto ha corso, che trova una lama,  
> 80 nella qual si distende e la impaluda,  
> 81 e suol di state talora esser grama.  

Not far has it flowed, when it finds a level, on which it spreads and makes a marsh thereof, and is wont in summer to be at times unwholesome.

> 79 „Μετ᾽ οὐ μακρὸν ῥοῦν προσπίπτει πεδιάδι,  
> („After no long course it falls upon a plain,)
>
> 80 „Ἔνθ᾽ ἐπεκτεινόμενος ἐπιλιμνάζει,  
> („Where spreading out it forms a marsh,)
>
> 81 „Πρόξενος νόσων καθιστάμενος θέρους.  
> („Becoming a cause of diseases in summer.)

- *μετ᾽ οὐ μακρὸν ῥοῦν* ‘after no long course’ nominalizes *Non molto ha corso*: elided *μετά* takes the accusative, and *ῥοῦν* is the contracted accusative singular of *ῥοῦς*. The main verb *προσπίπτει* (third singular present active of *προσπίπτω*) takes dative *πεδιάδι* (from *πεδιάς*) ‘comes upon a plain’, interpreting *lama* as the level ground encountered, not yet the marsh.
- *ἔνθ᾽ ἐπεκτεινόμενος ἐπιλιμνάζει* ‘where, spreading out, it forms a pool’ substitutes relative *ἔνθα* for *nella qual*. *ἐπεκτεινόμενος* (present middle-passive participle of *ἐπεκτείνω*, masculine nominative singular) refers to the river and retains the intransitive sense of *si distende*. *ἐπιλιμνάζει* is an intransitive present active indicative: it describes what the water does, without the accusative represented by *la* in *la impaluda*.
- *πρόξενος νόσων καθιστάμενος θέρους* ‘becoming a cause of diseases in summer’ specifies the sense given to *esser grama*. *καθιστάμενος*, present middle participle of *καθίστημι*, continues with the river as subject; *πρόξενος* is its predicate nominative, literally ‘one who brings about’, with genitive plural *νόσων*. *θέρους* (genitive singular of *θέρος*) is a genitive of time when. Neither the habitual *suol* nor the qualification *talora* has a distinct counterpart.

> 82 Quindi passando la vergine cruda  
> 83 vide terra nel mezzo del pantano,  
> 84 senza cultura e d’ abitanti nuda.  

The cruel virgin, passing that way, saw land amidst the fen, uncultivated and naked of inhabitants.

> 82 „Ἡ δ᾽ ἀγροῖκος παρθένος, ὅτε διέβη  
> („And the wild maiden, when she passed)
>
> 83 „Ἐκεῖθεν, εἶδεν ἐν μέσῳ τοῦ τενάγους  
> („That way, saw in the midst of the fen)
>
> 84 „Γῆν ἀκαλλιέργητον, γυμνὴν κατοίκων.  
> („An uncultivated land, bare of inhabitants.)

- *ἡ δ᾽ ἀγροῖκος παρθένος* puts the subject first, with connective *δέ*; feminine *ἀγροῖκος*, a two-termination adjective, interprets *cruda* as ‘wild’ or ‘uncouth’ rather than explicitly cruel. *ὅτε διέβη ἐκεῖθεν* ‘when she passed that way’ replaces the gerund *Quindi passando* with a temporal clause. *διέβη* is the third singular root aorist active indicative of *διαβαίνω*; *ἐκεῖθεν*, literally ‘from there’, follows the verb.
- *εἶδεν ἐν μέσῳ τοῦ τενάγους γῆν* delays the object of *vide terra* until after the location. *εἶδεν* is the third singular second aorist active indicative used for *ὁράω*. *ἐν μέσῳ* ‘in the midst’ takes a dependent genitive, *τοῦ τενάγους* (contracted genitive singular of neuter *τέναγος*), closely matching *nel mezzo del pantano*.
- *γῆν ἀκαλλιέργητον, γυμνὴν κατοίκων* pairs two feminine accusative singular adjectives with the anarthrous object *γῆν*. The privative compound *ἀκαλλιέργητος* ‘uncultivated’ condenses *senza cultura*; *γυμνήν* retains *nuda* but precedes its complement *κατοίκων*, a genitive of privation, where Italian puts *d’ abitanti* first.

## Lines 85–117 — The Naming of Mantua and the Passing Augurs

> 85 Lì, per fuggire ogni consorzio umano,  
> 86 ristette co’ suoi servi a far sue arti,  
> 87 e visse, e vi lasciò suo corpo vano.  

There, to shun all human intercourse, she halted with her ministers to do her arts; and there she lived and left her body vacant.

> 85 „Ἐκεῖ φεύγουσα πᾶσαν ἀνθρώπων σχέσιν  
> („There, shunning all intercourse with men,)
>
> 86 „Κατέστη σὺν τοῖς δούλοις, τὴν αὑτῆς τέχνην  
> („She settled with her servants, her own art)
>
> 87 „Ἀσκοῦσα καὶ ζήσασα μέχρι θανάτου.  
> („Practising, and lived until death.)

- *Ἐκεῖ* retains the opening *Lì*. The purpose infinitive *per fuggire* becomes *φεύγουσα* (nom. fem. sg., pres. act. participle of *φεύγω*), ‘shunning’: avoidance is presented as already in progress when she settles. *Πᾶσαν ἀνθρώπων σχέσιν* is its accusative object; the genitive plural ‘with human beings’ replaces the adjective *umano*, and stands between *πᾶσαν* and *σχέσιν*.
- *Κατέστη* (3rd sg., intransitive second aor. act. ind. of *καθίστημι*) means ‘she settled’, rather than simply *ristette*, ‘she stopped’. *Σὺν τοῖς δούλοις* takes the dative of accompaniment; the article suffices where Italian has the possessive *suoi*.
- *Τὴν αὑτῆς τέχνην ἀσκοῦσα*, ‘practising her own art’, turns the second purpose infinitive, *a far sue arti*, into another present active participle (from *ἀσκέω*). The art is singular and its possessor explicitly reflexive: *αὑτῆς* is the contracted form of *ἑαυτῆς*, gen. fem. sg.
- *Ζήσασα* (nom. fem. sg., aor. act. participle of *ζάω*) is coordinated with *ἀσκοῦσα*, but surveys the whole of the life rather than a continuing practice. Thus the only finite verb is *κατέστη*. *Μέχρι θανάτου*, ‘until death’, gives that life its limit, without reproducing either the repeated local *vi* or the image in *lasciò suo corpo vano*.

> 88 Gli uomini poi, che intorno erano sparti,  
> 89 s’ accolsero a quel loco, ch’ era forte  
> 90 per lo pantan che avea da tutte parti.  

Afterwards the men, that were scattered round, gathered together on that spot [which] was strong by reason of the marsh it had on every side.

> 88 „Ἄνθρωποι δὲ τὰς πέριξ οἰκοῦντες χώρας  
> („And the men who dwelt in the surrounding lands)
>
> 89 „Ἐκεῖ συνῆλθον, ὄντος τοῦ τόπου ’κείνου  
> („Gathered there, since that place was)
>
> 90 „Ὀχυροῦ πανταχόθεν ἐκ τοῦ τενάγους·  
> („Fortified on every side by the marsh;)

- Postpositive *δέ* marks the transition of *poi* without explicitly saying ‘afterwards’. *Ἄνθρωποι … τὰς πέριξ οἰκοῦντες χώρας* describes the men by an active participle (nom. masc. pl. of *οἰκέω*), ‘inhabiting the surrounding lands’, instead of the relative clause *che intorno erano sparti*. *Οἰκέω* takes the inhabited territory as its accusative object; *πέριξ* is an adverb used attributively between article and noun.
- *Ἐκεῖ συνῆλθον*, ‘there they came together’, renders *s’accolsero a quel loco* by an adverb and the 3rd pl. second aor. act. ind. of *συνέρχομαι*. The augment is internal to the compound, as in the simple aorist *ἦλθον*.
- *Ὄντος τοῦ τόπου ’κείνου ὀχυροῦ* supplies a reason rather than an identifying relative: ‘since that place was strong’. This is a genitive absolute with the pres. participle of *εἰμί* and predicate adjective *ὀχυροῦ*. *’Κείνου* loses the initial vowel of *ἐκείνου* by prodelision. *Πανταχόθεν*, ‘from every side’, now qualifies the strength of the place; *ἐκ τοῦ τενάγους*, ‘owing to the marsh’, dispenses with the second relative, *che avea*.

> 91 Fer la città sopra quell’ ossa morte;  
> 92 e per colei, che il loco prima elesse,  
> 93 Mantova l’appellar senz’ altra sorte.  

They built the city over those dead bones; and for her who first chose the place, they called it Mantua without other augury.

> 91 „Κἀπὶ τῶν ὀστῶν τῆς τεθνηκυίας πόλιν  
> („And over the bones of her who had died, a city)
>
> 92 „Ἔκτισαν ἐπώνυμον τῆς ἐκλεξάσης  
> („They founded, named after her who had chosen)
>
> 93 „Τὸν τόπον, ἣ Μάντυα τότ᾽ ὠνομάσθη.  
> („The place, which was then named Mantua.)

- *Κἀπί* is crasis for *καὶ ἐπί*; *ἐπί* with the genitive locates the city ‘upon the bones’. In *τῶν ὀστῶν τῆς τεθνηκυίας*, ‘the bones of the dead woman’, neither ‘those’ nor ‘dead’ qualifies the bones as in *quell’ ossa morte*. *Τεθνηκυίας* is the gen. fem. sg. perfect active participle of *θνῄσκω*, substantivized by the article; the perfect denotes the state resulting from death.
- *Πόλιν ἔκτισαν* (3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *κτίζω*) places the object before the verb, reversing *Fer la città*. The adjective *ἐπώνυμον* agrees with *πόλιν* and takes the genitive of the person after whom it is named. *Τῆς ἐκλεξάσης τὸν τόπον*, ‘of her who selected the place’, uses an articular aorist active participle of *ἐκλέγω* with an accusative object for *colei, che il loco … elesse*; *prima* has no separate equivalent.
- *Ἥ* is nominative feminine, looking back to *πόλιν*, not to the nearer masculine *τόπον*. *Ἣ Μάντυα τότ᾽ ὠνομάσθη*, ‘which was then named Mantua’, turns active *l’appellar* into the 3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ὀνομάζω*, with predicate nominative *Μάντυα*. *Τότ᾽* is elided *τότε*; it dates the naming but does not translate *senz’ altra sorte*, which is omitted.

> 94 Già fur le genti sue dentro più spesse,  
> 95 prima che la mattìa di Casalodi  
> 96 da Pinamonte inganno ricevesse.  

Once the inhabitants were denser in it, ere the folly of Casalodi was cheated by Pinamonte.

> 94 „Παμπληθὴς δ᾽ αὐτὴν λαὸς κατῴκει πάλαι,  
> („And a multitudinous people dwelt in it of old,)
>
> 95 „ Πρὶν ἢ τὴν Κασαλόδου μωρίαν δόλῳ  
> („ Before the folly of Casalodi by craft)
>
> 96 „ Ὑπερεξηπάτησεν ὁ Πιναμόντης.  
> („ Pinamonte utterly deceived.)

- *Παμπληθὴς … λαός*, ‘a very numerous people’, substitutes a singular collective and a positive adjective for plural *le genti sue* and comparative *più spesse*. The adjective leads, separated from its noun by *δ᾽ αὐτήν*: *δ᾽* is elided *δέ*, and *αὐτήν* is the city as direct object of *κατῴκει* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *κατοικέω*). Thus *dentro* becomes ‘inhabited it’, while *πάλαι*, ‘formerly’, renders *già*.
- *Πρὶν ἤ*, ‘before’, introduces a past event in the indicative, where *prima che* takes the subjunctive *ricevesse*. *Τὴν Κασαλόδου μωρίαν*, ‘the folly of Casalodi’, keeps the personification but makes folly the accusative object, not the subject receiving *inganno*.
- *Δόλῳ*, ‘by guile’, is an instrumental dative. *Ὑπερεξηπάτησεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ὑπερεξαπατάω*, augmented after the prefixes) intensifies the deception, ‘deceived utterly’. The former agent phrase *da Pinamonte* becomes the postponed nominative subject *ὁ Πιναμόντης*.

> 97 Però t’ assenno che, se tu mai odi  
> 98 originar la mia terra altrimenti,  
> 99 la verità nulla menzogna frodi.”  

Therefore I charge thee, if thou ever hearest other origin given to my city, let no falsehood defraud the truth."

> 97 „ Ὑπομιμνήσκω σε τάδ’ ὅπως, ἢν ἄλλην  
> („ I remind you of these things so that, if another)
>
> 98 „ Γενέθλην ποτ’ ἀκούσῃς ἐμῆς πατρίδος,  
> („ Origin of my fatherland you ever hear,)
>
> 99 „ Τὴν ἀλήθειαν μὴ δολώσῃ τὸ ψεῦδος.”  
> („ Falsehood may not falsify the truth.”)

- *Ὑπομιμνήσκω σε τάδ᾽* (1st sg. pres. act. ind. of *ὑπομιμνήσκω*) means ‘I remind you of these things’, less an injunction than *t’assenno*. The verb has two accusatives, the person *σε* and the matter recalled *τάδ᾽* (elided *τάδε*). The latter points back to the preceding account; there is no express connective for *però*.
- *Ὅπως* introduces the purpose, interrupted by *ἢν … ἀκούσῃς*, ‘if you should hear’. *Ἤν* is a contracted form of *ἐάν*, with the 2nd sg. aor. act. subj. of *ἀκούω*; enclitic *ποτ᾽* (elided *ποτε*) renders *mai*. Instead of *odi originar … altrimenti*, verb plus infinitive and adverb, Greek has an accusative noun phrase, *ἄλλην γενέθλην*, ‘another origin’. *Ἐμῆς πατρίδος*, ‘of my native land’, depends on the poetic noun *γενέθλην*.
- *Τὴν ἀλήθειαν μὴ δολώσῃ τὸ ψεῦδος*, ‘lest falsehood falsify the truth’, retains the striking object-first order of *la verità nulla menzogna frodi*. *Δολώσῃ* is 3rd sg. aor. act. subj. of *δολόω* under *ὅπως*; *μή* negates the purpose. Thus ‘no lie’ becomes the subject *τὸ ψεῦδος*, ‘falsehood’, of a negated verb.

> 100 Ed io: “Maestro, i tuoi ragionamenti  
> 101 mi son sì certi e prendon sì mia fede,  
> 102 che gli altri mi sarian carboni spenti.  

And I: "Master, thy words are to me so certain, and so take hold of my belief, that all others would be to me extinguished coals.

> 100 Κἀγώ· “Προστάτα, τοσοῦτον οἱ σοὶ λόγοι  
> (And I: “Master, to such a degree your words)
>
> 101 „ Σαφεῖς εἰσι, καὶ πίστιν ἐμπνέουσί μοι,  
> („ Are clear, and inspire faith in me,)
>
> 102 „ Ὥστ’ εἴη μοι πᾶς ἄλλος ἐσβηκὼς ἄνθραξ.  
> („ That every other would be to me an extinguished coal.)

- *Κἀγώ* is crasis for *καὶ ἐγώ* and, like *Ed io*, has no verb of speaking. *Προστάτα* is the vocative singular of *προστάτης*, ‘protector, guide’, rather than a literal ‘teacher’. *Οἱ σοὶ λόγοι* retains the possessive in *i tuoi ragionamenti*.
- *Τοσοῦτον* is an accusative of degree used adverbially, ‘to such an extent’, anticipating *ὥστε*. *Σαφεῖς* (nom. masc. pl. of *σαφής*) means ‘clear’ rather than *certi*; *εἰσι* agrees with *λόγοι*. Greek does not repeat *sì*: *καὶ πίστιν ἐμπνέουσί μοι*, ‘and inspire trust in me’, makes trust the object of *ἐμπνέω* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind.) and the speaker its dative recipient, not the possessor in *mia fede*.
- *Ὥστ᾽ εἴη μοι* introduces a finite result clause after elision of *ὥστε*. *Εἴη* is 3rd sg. pres. opt. of *εἰμί*, representing the hypothetical *sarian*; a Classical potential optative would ordinarily have *ἄν*. *Πᾶς ἄλλος*, ‘every other [word]’, and predicate *ἐσβηκὼς ἄνθραξ*, ‘a burnt-out coal’, are both singular, distributing Dante’s plural comparison word by word. *Ἐσβηκώς* is the intransitive perfect active participle of *σβέννυμι*: the coal has gone out.

> 103 Ma dimmi della gente che procede,  
> 104 se tu ne vedi alcun degno di nota:  
> 105 chè solo a ciò la mia mente rifiede.”  

But tell me of the people that are passing, if thou seest any of them worthy of note: for to that alone my mind recurs."

> 103 „ Ἀλλὰ λέγε μοι τοῦ προβαίνοντος πλήθους  
> („ But tell me, of the advancing multitude,)
>
> 104 „ Εἴ τινα ψυχὴν εἶδες ἀξίαν λόγου·  
> („ If you saw any soul worthy of mention;)
>
> 105 „ Τοῦτο γὰρ μόνον ἐπασχολεῖ τὸν νοῦν μου.”  
> („ For this alone occupies my mind.”)

- *Ἀλλὰ λέγε μοι* renders *Ma dimmi* by the 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *λέγω* and a dative. *Τοῦ προβαίνοντος πλήθους*, ‘of the advancing multitude’, replaces *gente che procede* by a collective neuter with an attributive present active participle of *προβαίνω*. The fronted genitive also supplies the whole from which ‘any soul’ is singled out.
- *Εἴ τινα ψυχὴν εἶδες* serves as an indirect question, ‘whether you saw any soul’. *Τινα* is indefinite and agrees with the supplied *ψυχήν*; *εἶδες* is the 2nd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*, looking back on what has been seen rather than using the present of *vedi*. *Ἀξίαν λόγου*, ‘worthy of mention’, agrees with *ψυχήν* and takes the genitive of worth.
- Postpositive *γάρ* gives the reason expressed by *chè*. *Τοῦτο … μόνον* is the neuter singular subject of *ἐπασχολεῖ* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἐπασχολέω*), and *τὸν νοῦν μου* its accusative object: ‘this alone occupies my mind’ reverses the relation in *a ciò la mia mente rifiede*, where the mind is what returns.

> 106 Allor mi disse: “Quel, che dalla gota  
> 107 porge la barba in su le spalle brune,  
> 108 fu, quando Grecia fu di maschi vota  
> 109 sì che appena rimaser per le cune,  

Then he said to me: "That one, who from the cheek stretches forth his beard upon his dusky shoulders, was an augur, when Greece was so empty of males, that hardly they remained even in the cradles;

> 106 Τότε δ’ εἶπέ μοι· “Κεῖνος, οὗπερ ὁ πώγων  
> (Then he said to me: “That one, whose beard)
>
> 107 „ Ἐκ τῆς γένυος φαιοὺς ὤμους καλύπτει,  
> („ From his cheek covers his dusky shoulders,)
>
> 108 „ Ἦν (ὅτ’ ἔρημος ὑπῆρχ’ Ἑλλὰς ἀῤῥένων,  
> („ Was (when Greece was bereft of men,)
>
> 109 „ Μόλις τινῶν μενόντων ἐν τοῖς σπαργάνοις),  
> („ Scarcely any remaining in swaddling-clothes))

- *Τότε δ᾽ εἶπέ μοι* follows *Allor mi disse*: elided *δέ* marks the transition, *εἶπε* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*, and its additional final accent is due to enclitic *μοι*. *Κεῖνος* is a shortened poetic form of *ἐκεῖνος*, ‘that one’.
- *Οὗπερ ὁ πώγων*, ‘whose beard’, replaces Italian subject-relative *che* by a possessive genitive relative, strengthened by *-περ*. The beard is now subject of *καλύπτει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *καλύπτω*), not the object of *porge*. *Ἐκ τῆς γένυος*, ‘from the jaw’, keeps the starting point, with the gen. sg. of *γένυς*; *φαιοὺς ὤμους*, ‘dusky shoulders’, is the direct object covered rather than the destination after *in su*.
- *Ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*) renders *fu*, but its predicate noun is withheld until line 110. The parenthesis begins *ὅτ᾽ ἔρημος ὑπῆρχ᾽ Ἑλλάς*: *ὅτε* and *ὑπῆρχε* are elided; the latter is 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ὑπάρχω*, used as a copula. Predicate *ἔρημος* is feminine here, with genitive of privation *ἀῤῥένων* (gen. pl. of *ἄρρην*); the paired breathings are an older spelling of medial double rho.
- *Μόλις τινῶν μενόντων*, ‘scarcely any remaining’, is a genitive absolute with an indefinite pronoun and the pres. act. participle of *μένω*. It replaces the explicit result construction *sì che* by an attendant circumstance. *Ἐν τοῖς σπαργάνοις* is literally ‘in swaddling-clothes’, not *per le cune*.

> 110 augure; e diede il punto con Calcanta  
> 111 in Aulide a tagliar la prima fune.  

and in Aulis he, with Calchas, gave the time for cutting the first cable.

> 110 „ Οἰωνοσκόπος, ὁ σὺν Κάλχαντι νεῦμα  
> („ An augur, who together with Calchas the signal)
>
> 111 „ Τέμνειν τὸν πρῶτον κάλων δοὺς ἐν Αὐλίδι·  
> („ Gave to cut the first cable in Aulis;)

- *Οἰωνοσκόπος*, literally ‘observer of birds’, completes *ἦν* across the parenthesis, just as *augure* completes *fu*. For the coordinated finite verb *e diede*, Greek has *ὁ … δούς*, ‘the one who gave’, in apposition to *κεῖνος*. *Δούς* is the nom. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *δίδωμι*; its article precedes the whole expanded phrase.
- *Σὺν Κάλχαντι* uses *σύν* with the dat. sg. of *Κάλχας*. *Νεῦμα* is the accusative object of *δούς*: Musurus takes *diede il punto* as ‘gave the signal’ rather than “gave the time”. *Τέμνειν* (pres. act. inf. of *τέμνω*) specifies the action signalled, and governs *τὸν πρῶτον κάλων* (acc. sg. of *κάλως*, ‘cable’). *Ἐν Αὐλίδι*, with the dat. sg. of *Αὐλίς*, comes last instead of preceding the infinitive as *in Aulide* does.

> 112 Euripilo ebbe nome, e così il canta  
> 113 l’alta mia Tragedía in alcun loco:  
> 114 ben lo sai tu, che la sai tutta quanta.  

Eurypylus his name; and my high Tragedy thus sings him in some place: well knowest it thou, who knowest the whole.

> 112 „ Εὐρύπυλος τοὔνομα, καθάπερ ᾄδει  
> („ Eurypylus by name, even as sings)
>
> 113 „ Ἐμοῦ που τοῦτον ἡ σαφὴς τραγῳδία,  
> („ Of him somewhere my clear tragedy,)
>
> 114 „ Ὡς οὐκ ἀγνοεῖς, εἰδὼς αὐτὴν ἐκ στήθους.  
> („ As you are not unaware, knowing it by heart.)

- *Εὐρύπυλος τοὔνομα*, ‘Eurypylus by name’, omits the verb of *ebbe nome*. *Τοὔνομα* is crasis for *τὸ ὄνομα*, an accusative of respect; the personal name remains nominative.
- *Καθάπερ ᾄδει*, ‘just as [it] sings’, subordinates the statement introduced by *e così*. *ᾌδει* is the contracted 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ᾄδω*; *τοῦτον* is its accusative object, an explicit ‘this man’ for *il*. *Που*, the enclitic indefinite adverb, compresses *in alcun loco* into ‘somewhere’.
- *Ἐμοῦ … ἡ σαφὴς τραγῳδία* separates the possessive genitive from its noun and places the subject after the verb. *Σαφής*, ‘clear, distinct’, changes the quality expressed by *alta*, rather than reproducing “high”.
- *Ὡς οὐκ ἀγνοεῖς*, ‘as you are not unaware’, uses litotes for *ben lo sai tu*; the 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἀγνοέω* needs no expressed subject. *Εἰδὼς αὐτήν*, ‘knowing it’, replaces the relative *che la sai* by a nom. masc. sg. participle agreeing with the addressee; *εἰδώς* is perfect active in form, from *οἶδα*, but present in sense. *Ἐκ στήθους*, ‘by heart’, specifies the manner of knowing rather than translating *tutta quanta* as a quantity.

> 115 Quell’ altro, che ne’ fianchi è così poco,  
> 116 Michele Scotto fu, che veramente  
> 117 delle magiche frode seppe il gioco.  

That other who is so small about the flanks was Michael Scott; and of a truth he knew the play of magic frauds.

> 115 „ Ὁ δὲ τὰ πλευρὰ κάτισχνος οὗτος ἄλλος  
> („ And this other, so gaunt in his flanks,)
>
> 116 „ Μιχαὴλ ἦν ὁ Σκόττος, ὃς ὄντως δόλων  
> („ Was Michael Scott, who truly of wiles)
>
> 117 „ Μαγγανευτικῶν ἠπίστατο τὴν τέχνην.  
> („ Of magic knew the art.)

- *Ὁ δὲ τὰ πλευρὰ κάτισχνος οὗτος ἄλλος* packs the relative clause *che ne’ fianchi è così poco* into an adjectival description. *Τὰ πλευρά* is accusative of respect, ‘as to the flanks’; *κάτισχνος*, ‘very lean’, makes *così poco* more specific. *Δέ* marks the change to another figure, and *οὗτος ἄλλος*, ‘this other’, uses a nearer demonstrative than *Quell’ altro*.
- *Μιχαὴλ ἦν ὁ Σκόττος* places the copula between the indeclinable personal name and its articular byname. *Ἦν* is again imperfect for *fu*. *Ὃς ὄντως*, ‘who truly’, retains the Italian relative *che* and adverb *veramente*.
- *Δόλων μαγγανευτικῶν*, ‘of magical deceits’, is a genitive plural depending on *τὴν τέχνην*. Musurus separates this genitive from its head by *ἠπίστατο* (3rd sg. imperf. ind. of the deponent *ἐπίσταμαι*), presenting *seppe* as enduring knowledge. The accusative object *τὴν τέχνην*, ‘the art’, interprets *il gioco* as a skill mastered, not literally a game.

## Lines 118–130 — The Diviners and the Setting Moon

> 118 Vedi Guido Bonatti, vedi Asdente,  
> 119 che avere inteso al cuoio ed allo spago  
> 120 ora vorrebbe, ma tardi si pente.  

See Guido Bonatti; see Asdente, who now would wish he had attended to his leather and his cord, but too late repents.

> 118 „ Βλέπε Γουΐδον Βονάτην, βλέπ᾽ Ἀσδέντην,  
> („ Behold Guido Bonatti, behold Asdente,)
>
> 119 „ Ὃς καταλιπὼν καλώδιον καὶ βύρσαν  
> („ Who having abandoned twine and leather)
>
> 120 „ Ἐπὶ βλάβῃ, νῦν μετανοεῖ βραδέως.  
> („ To his harm, now repents too late.)

- *Βλέπε … βλέπ᾽* reproduces the repeated *Vedi … vedi* with the present active imperative, 2nd sg., of *βλέπω*; the second *βλέπε* loses its final vowel before *Ἀσδέντην*. The names *Γουΐδον Βονάτην* and *Ἀσδέντην* are given Greek masculine accusative singular endings as direct objects.
- *ὃς καταλιπὼν καλώδιον καὶ βύρσαν* ‘who, having left cord and leather’ reverses both the order of *al cuoio ed allo spago* and the way the action is presented. Instead of what Asdente would wish to have attended to, Greek gives what he actually left. *ὅς* is nominative as subject of the relative clause, though its antecedent is accusative; *καταλιπών* (nom. masc. sg., second aor. act. participle of *καταλείπω*) agrees with it and marks action prior to the repentance. *καλώδιον* (neut. sg.) and *βύρσαν* (fem. sg.) are its accusative objects.
- *ἐπὶ βλάβῃ* ‘to his detriment’ adds the result of the abandonment (*ἐπί* with dat. sg. of *βλάβη*). There is no verb of wishing to match the conditional *vorrebbe*, nor an adversative for *ma*. Instead *νῦν*, corresponding to *ora*, goes with *μετανοεῖ* ‘he repents’ (contracted 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *μετανοέω*). The adverb *βραδέως* ‘tardily’ renders *tardi*; its force of ‘too late’ comes from the context, not from a comparative form.

> 121 Vedi le triste che lasciaron l’ ago,  
> 122 la spola e il fuso, e fecersi indovine;  
> 123 fecer malìe con erbe e con imago.  

See the wretched *women* who left the needle, the shuttle, and the spindle, and made themselves divineresses; they wrought witchcraft with herbs and images.

> 121 „ Ἰδὲ ταλαίνας, αἳ βελόνης κἀτράκτου  
> („ Behold the wretched women, who before needle and spindle)
>
> 122 „ Καὶ τῆς κερκίδος προείλοντο μαγείας,  
> („ And the shuttle preferred sorceries,)
>
> 123 „ Ἃς διέπραττον εἰκόσι καὶ βοτάναις.  
> („ Which they wrought with images and herbs.)

- For the next *Vedi* Musurus varies the imperative: *ἰδέ* is the 2nd sg. second aor. act. imperative belonging to *εἶδον* ‘I saw’. *ταλαίνας* (acc. fem. pl. of *τάλας*, fem. *τάλαινα*) stands without a noun like *le triste*, but also without the Italian article. *αἵ* takes up the same women in the nominative as subject of the relative clause.
- *βελόνης κἀτράκτου καὶ τῆς κερκίδος προείλοντο μαγείας* ‘they chose sorceries in preference to needle and spindle and shuttle’ compresses *lasciaron … e fecersi indovine* into one act of choice. *προείλοντο* is 3rd pl. second aor. mid. ind. of *προαιρέομαι* ‘choose for oneself, prefer’, with the augmented aorist stem *εἱλ-* of *αἱρέω*. Its object is *μαγείας* (acc. pl. of *μαγεία*), the practices rather than the practitioners of *indovine*.
- The things passed over stand first, in the genitive of comparison: *βελόνης* (from *βελόνη*), *ἀτράκτου* (from *ἄτρακτος*), and *τῆς κερκίδος* (from *κερκίς*). *κἀτράκτου* is crasis of *καὶ ἀτράκτου*. The spindle thus precedes the shuttle, and only the last item has the article.
- Where *fecer malìe* starts a fresh assertion, *ἃς διέπραττον* ‘which they practised’ subordinates it: *ἅς* is acc. fem. pl., referring to *μαγείας* and serving as object of *διέπραττον* (3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *διαπράττω*, with Attic *-ττ-* and augment after the preverb). The imperfect presents the practice as continuing or repeated, unlike the Italian preterite. *εἰκόσι καὶ βοτάναις* gives instrumental datives without a preposition (dat. pl. of *εἰκών* and *βοτάνη*), reversing *con erbe e con imago* and making the images explicitly plural.

> 124 Ma vienne omai, chè già tiene il confine  
> 125 d’ ambedue gli emisperi e tocca l’ onda  
> 126 sotto Sibilia Caino e le spine;  

But now come! for Cain and his thorns already holds the confine of both hemispheres, and under Seville touches the wave;

> 124 „ Ἐλθὲ νῦν· καὶ γὰρ τὸν ὅρον ἡμιπόλων  
> („ Come now; for the boundary of the hemispheres)
>
> 125 „ Δύο κατέχει Κάϊν φέρων ἀκάνθας,  
> („ Two Cain holds, carrying thorns,)
>
> 126 „ Ὑπό θ᾽ Ἵσπαλιν ἐγγὺς θαλάσσης κεῖται.  
> („ And beneath Seville near the sea he lies.)

- *Ἐλθὲ νῦν* ‘come now’ renders *Ma vienne omai* without an adversative or a separate equivalent for *ne*. *ἐλθέ* is the 2nd sg. second aor. act. imperative of *ἔρχομαι*. *καὶ γάρ* ‘for indeed’ introduces the reason given by *chè*; there is no distinct ‘already’ for this first *già*.
- *τὸν ὅρον ἡμιπόλων δύο κατέχει* fronts the object of *κατέχει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *κατέχω*) where Italian has verb first, *tiene il confine*. *ἡμιπόλων* is a genitive plural dependent on *ὅρον* ‘boundary’; *δύο*, placed after its noun across the line break, gives ‘of two hemispheres’ for *d’ambedue gli emisperi*. The noun is plural, not dual, and the numeral is used unchanged.
- *Κάϊν φέρων ἀκάνθας* ‘Cain carrying thorns’ retains the delayed subject but resolves *Caino e le spine* into a single subject and a participial phrase. *Κάϊν* is indeclinable, here nominative; the diaeresis keeps the vowels distinct. *φέρων* (nom. masc. sg., pres. act. participle of *φέρω*) agrees with it and takes *ἀκάνθας* (acc. pl. of *ἄκανθα*) as object.
- *ὑπό θ᾽ Ἵσπαλιν* ‘and beneath Seville’ keeps *sotto Sibilia* as a prepositional phrase, with *Ἵσπαλιν* accusative after *ὑπό*. Postpositive *τε* links the predicates; elision before the rough breathing of *Ἵσπαλιν* changes *τ᾽* to *θ᾽*. For *tocca l’onda*, *ἐγγὺς θαλάσσης κεῖται* substitutes position for contact: *κεῖται* (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *κεῖμαι*) means ‘lies’, and *ἐγγύς* takes the genitive *θαλάσσης* ‘of the sea’, not an accusative object ‘the wave’.

> 127 e già iernotte fu la luna tonda;  
> 128 ben ten dee ricordar, chè non ti nocque  
> 129 alcuna volta per la selva fonda.”  

and already yesternight the Moon was round; well must thou remember: for she did not hurt thee any time in the deep wood."

> 127 „ Ἡ σελήνη δ᾽ ἦν χθὲς νύκτωρ πλήρης ἤδη,  
> („ And the moon was already full last night,)
>
> 128 „ Ὡς δεῖ μεμνῆσθαί σ᾽· οὐ γὰρ διὰ δρυμῶνος  
> („ As you must remember; for through the wood)
>
> 129 „ Τοῦ ζοφώδους ὀλίγον ὠφέλησέ σε.”  
> („ Of gloom, she helped you not a little.”)

- *ἡ σελήνη δ᾽ ἦν χθὲς νύκτωρ πλήρης ἤδη* brings *la luna* to the front and moves *già* to the very end as *ἤδη*. Postpositive *δέ*, elided before *ἦν*, continues the sentence like *e*. *ἦν* is 3rd sg. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί* for *fu*; the predicate adjective *πλήρης* (nom. fem. sg.) interprets *tonda* ‘round’ as ‘full’. The two adverbs *χθές* ‘yesterday’ and *νύκτωρ* ‘by night’ unpack *iernotte*.
- *ὡς δεῖ μεμνῆσθαί σ᾽* ‘as you must remember’ makes *ben ten dee ricordar* a parenthetic clause introduced by *ὡς*, rather than rendering *ben* by an adverb of degree. Impersonal *δεῖ* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *δέω* in its sense ‘it is necessary’) governs *μεμνῆσθαι*, the perfect middle infinitive of *μιμνῄσκω*: the perfect expresses the state of remembering. *σ᾽* is elided enclitic *σε*, the accusative subject of the infinitive; the additional acute on *μεμνῆσθαί* marks the following enclitic. What is remembered, represented by *ne* in Italian, is left implicit.
- *οὐ γὰρ … ὀλίγον ὠφέλησέ σε* means ‘for she helped you not a little’: postpositive *γάρ* renders causal *chè*, but the negative goes with adverbial *ὀλίγον* (acc. neut. sg. of *ὀλίγος*). Musurus interprets *non ti nocque* as positive benefit, not merely absence of harm, and gives degree of help rather than the time expression *alcuna volta*. *ὠφέλησε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ὠφελέω*, with temporal augment) takes accusative *σε* as direct object; its extra acute before the enclitic does not alter the verb form.
- Between *οὐ* and *ὀλίγον* stands *διὰ δρυμῶνος τοῦ ζοφώδους* ‘through the gloomy wood’. *διά* with the genitive renders *per* as passage through; *δρυμῶνος* is gen. sg. of *δρυμών*. The postposed adjective *ζοφώδους* (gen. masc. sg. of *ζοφώδης*) has its own article agreeing with the noun. It interprets *fonda* by darkness rather than depth.

> 130 Sì mi parlava, ed andavamo introcque.  

Thus he spake to me, and we went on meanwhile.

> 130 Ταῦτ᾽ ἔλεγέ μοι, καὶ προὐβαίνομεν πρόσω.  
> (Thus he spoke to me, and we walked onward.)

- *Ταῦτ᾽ ἔλεγέ μοι* ‘these things he was saying to me’ turns adverbial *Sì* into an accusative neuter plural object, *ταῦτα* (from *οὗτος*), elided before *ἔλεγε*. The verb is 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *λέγω*, retaining the ongoing aspect of *parlava*; enclitic *μοι* is the dative recipient corresponding to *mi* and accounts for the extra acute on *ἔλεγέ*.
- *καὶ προὐβαίνομεν πρόσω* ‘and we were going forward’ keeps the coordination and the 1st pl. imperfect of *ed andavamo*. *προὐβαίνομεν* is imperf. act. ind. of *προβαίνω*, with crasis of the preverb’s final vowel and the internal augment (*προ-ε-* becoming *προὐ-*). The directional adverb *πρόσω* ‘onward’ reinforces *προ-* instead of translating the temporal *introcque*; simultaneity is left to the two coordinated imperfects.

## Summary — From Finite Verbs to Participles

Musurus often keeps the order in which Dante presents two actions while changing which one carries the sentence. In *vidi … venir*, the Greek stays close to the Italian: *εἶδον* takes *ἐρχομένας* as a supplementary participle, ‘I saw them coming’. Elsewhere the shift is more pointed. Dante’s finite *volle* becomes *θέλων* ‘wishing’, leaving *βλέπει* ‘he sees’ as the main assertion; the coordinated *fa ritroso calle* is then folded into *βαδίζων ἀναστροφάδην* ‘walking backwards’. The participles also distinguish kinds of action. The paired genitive absolutes with *ἐξελθόντος* and *δουλωθείσης* present the two events governed by *Poscia che* as complete before the main verb, whereas the perfect participles *ἐστραμμένον* and *τετραμμένην* describe positions in which the observer finds the figures, not movements he sees them make.

Small connectives can either preserve Dante’s syntax or alter its force. Postpositive *δέ* often carries the narrative forward like *e*, and *γάρ* regularly answers explanatory *chè*. The Italian result construction *sì … che* has a close counterpart in *οὕτως … ὥστε*, though the Greek completes it with an infinitive. By contrast, *τότε δέ* ‘and then’ turns another *sì che* from consequence into succession. The most consequential change comes in the saying on pity: *καίτοι* ‘although’ replaces temporal *quando*, while *ἤδη* ‘already’ replaces intensive *ben*. The paradox survives, but ‘when altogether dead’ becomes ‘although already dead’.

Musurus is especially close to Dante in repeated commands, retaining the doubling of *drizza* in *ἆρον, ἆρον* and of *vedi* in *βλέπε … βλέπ᾽*. His choice of a verb or its object, however, can settle a reading that the Italian leaves less explicit. In the comparison with the litanies, accusative *τὰς λιτανείας* makes them what people perform, not the subject that sets the pace. Likewise *νεῦμα* ‘signal’ interprets *il punto* as the cue given rather than the time appointed. At the close, Musurus even preserves a negative turn while reversing the verb: *non ti nocque* ‘did not harm you’ becomes *οὐ … ὀλίγον ὠφέλησέ σε* ‘helped you not a little’. The understatement remains, but what the reader must infer in Italian is expressed in Greek as a degree of help.
