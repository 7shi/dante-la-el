# Inferno 27

## Lines 1–24 — A Second Flame Finds Its Voice

> 1 Già era dritta in su la fiamma e queta  
> 2 per non dir più, e già da noi sen gía  
> 3 con la licenza del dolce poeta,  

The flame was now erect and quiet, having ceased to speak, and now went away from us with licence of the sweet Poet;

> 1 Ἡ φλὸξ ὄρθιος καὶ μὴ σαλεύουσ᾽ ἔτι  
> (The flame, upright and no longer swaying,)
>
> 2 Ἔπαυσε φθεγγομένη, κἀπήρχετ᾽ ἤδη  
> (Ceased speaking, and was already departing)
>
> 3 Τῇ τοῦ γλυκέως ποιητοῦ συναινέσει,  
> (With the consent of the sweet poet,)

- *Ἡ φλὸξ ὄρθιος* ‘the flame upright’ brings the subject forward from the middle of Dante’s line. *ὄρθιος* is predicative, nominative singular, used here with the feminine *φλόξ* as an adjective of two terminations. There is no finite equivalent of *era*: the flame’s posture is given as a circumstance of the action in line 2. *in su* is absorbed into *ὄρθιος*.
- *καὶ μὴ σαλεύουσ᾽ ἔτι* ‘and no longer swaying’ renders the adjective *queta* by negating an action. *σαλεύουσ᾽* is elided *σαλεύουσα*, present active participle, nominative feminine singular, of *σαλεύω*, used intransitively. *μή … ἔτι* applies to the participle; the first *già* is not separately translated.
- *ἔπαυσε φθεγγομένη* ‘ceased uttering sound’ makes *per non dir più* the main assertion. *ἔπαυσε* is third singular aorist active indicative of *παύω*, used absolutely; ordinary Attic for ceasing oneself would be the middle *ἐπαύσατο*. *φθεγγομένη*, present middle participle of *φθέγγομαι*, agrees with the subject and specifies the activity discontinued.
- *κἀπήρχετ᾽ ἤδη* ‘and was already going away’ has crasis of *καί* with *ἀπήρχετο*, then elision of final *-ο* before *ἤδη*. The verb is third singular imperfect middle indicative of *ἀπέρχομαι*, corresponding closely in aspect to *sen gía*; the prefix conveys departure, but *da noi* has no expressed equivalent. *τῇ … συναινέσει*, dative singular of *συναίνεσις*, gives the attendant authorization without a preposition. Between article and noun stands the possessive genitive *τοῦ γλυκέως ποιητοῦ*, with *γλυκέως* the genitive singular of *γλυκύς*.

> 4 quando un' altra, che dietro a lei venia,  
> 5 ne fece volger gli occhi alla sua cima  
> 6 per un confuso suon che fuor n' uscia.  

when another, that came behind it, made us turn our eyes to its top, for a confused sound that issued therefrom.

> 4 Ὅτ᾽ αὐτῆς ἄλλη κατόπιν ἐρχομένη  
> (When another coming behind it)
>
> 5 Πρὸς τὴν κορυφὴν αὑτῆς εἷλξε τὰς ὄψεις  
> (Drew our eyes toward its own peak)
>
> 6 Δι᾽ ἐξιόντος συμμιγοῦς τινος ἤχου.  
> (By a certain confused sound issuing forth.)

- *ὅτ᾽* is *ὅτε* elided, introducing the temporal clause. *ἄλλη* is feminine singular, with *φλόξ* understood as in *un’ altra*. The relative clause *che dietro a lei venia* becomes the present middle participle *ἐρχομένη* of *ἔρχομαι*, agreeing with *ἄλλη*. The genitive *αὐτῆς*, referring to the preceding flame, is placed before *ἄλλη* but belongs with *κατόπιν* ‘behind’.
- *πρὸς τὴν κορυφὴν αὑτῆς* ‘towards its own summit’ is fronted before the verb. The rough breathing distinguishes reflexive *αὑτῆς*, contracted from *ἑαυτῆς*, from the preceding *αὐτῆς*. *εἷλξε τὰς ὄψεις* ‘drew the eyes’ replaces causative *ne fece volger gli occhi* with a single transitive verb: *εἷλξε* is third singular sigmatic aorist active indicative of *ἕλκω*. The definite article, not an explicit ‘our’, accompanies *ὄψεις*, accusative plural of *ὄψις*.
- *δι᾽ ἐξιόντος συμμιγοῦς τινος ἤχου* ‘by means of a certain mingled sound’ uses elided *διά* with the genitive of means rather than the accusative of cause. *συμμιγοῦς* is the genitive singular of *συμμιγής*; indefinite *τινος* supplies the force of *un*. The relative *che fuor n’ uscia* is compressed into *ἐξιόντος*, present active participle of *ἔξειμι* ‘go out’, agreeing with *ἤχου*: the prefix gives the outward direction, but there is no separate pronoun for *n’*.

> 7 Come il bue Cicilian, che mugghiò prima  
> 8 col pianto di colui (e ciò fu dritto)  
> 9 che l' avea temperato con sua lima,  

As the Sicilian bull (which bellowed first with the lament of him—and that was right—who had tuned it with his file)

> 7 Ὡς βοῦς ὁ Σικελικός, οὗπερ ὁ πρῶτος  
> (As the Sicilian bull, whose first)
>
> 8 Μυκηθμὸς ἦν τὸ κλαῦμα (δικαίῳ λόγῳ)  
> (Bellowing was the weeping (with just reason))
>
> 9 Τοῦ διαμορφώσαντος αὐτὸν τῇ ῥίνῃ,  
> (Of him who had fashioned it with the file,)

- *ὡς* opens the comparison answered by *οὕτως* in line 13. *βοῦς ὁ Σικελικός* places the adjective after the noun with its own article. *οὗπερ* is the possessive genitive singular of the relative *ὅσπερ*, with strengthening *-περ*. Instead of *che mugghiò prima*, a relative subject with verb and adverb, Greek has *οὗπερ ὁ πρῶτος μυκηθμὸς ἦν* ‘whose first bellow was’: the action becomes a noun and priority an attributive adjective.
- *ἦν τὸ κλαῦμα* identifies the bellow with the weeping rather than representing the bull as bellowing *col pianto*. *ἦν* is third singular imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*. The parenthetic assertion *e ciò fu dritto* becomes a parenthetic dative of manner or grounds, *δικαίῳ λόγῳ* ‘with good reason’, without demonstrative or verb.
- *τοῦ διαμορφώσαντος αὐτὸν τῇ ῥίνῃ* ‘of the one who fashioned it with the file’ depends on *κλαῦμα*. Article and aorist active participle (genitive masculine singular of *διαμορφόω*) do the work of *di colui … che* and its pluperfect: the participle presents the fashioning as a completed act without a distinct pluperfect form. *αὐτόν* is masculine after *βοῦς*; instrumental *τῇ ῥίνῃ* has no expressed possessive corresponding to *sua*. *διαμορφόω* stresses giving shape rather than the tuning suggested by *temperato*.

> 10 mugghiava con la voce dell' afflitto,  
> 11 sì che, con tutto ch' ei fosse di rame,  
> 12 pure e' pareva dal dolor trafitto:  

kept bellowing with the sufferer’s voice; so that, although it was of brass, it seemed transfixed with pain

> 10 Τῇ πάσχοντος ἰδίᾳ φωνῇ ’μυκᾶτο,  
> (Bellowed with the sufferer's own voice,)
>
> 11 Ὥστε, καίπερ χάλκεος ὅλος ὤν, ὅμως  
> (So that, although wholly of bronze, yet)
>
> 12 Ὑπὸ τῆς λύπης κεντούμενος ἐδόκει,  
> (It seemed to be pierced by pain,)

- *τῇ πάσχοντος ἰδίᾳ φωνῇ ’μυκᾶτο* puts the means before the delayed verb of the comparison. Within the instrumental phrase *τῇ … ἰδίᾳ φωνῇ*, *πάσχοντος* is a substantival present active participle, genitive masculine singular of *πάσχω*: ‘with the sufferer’s own voice’. The present participle renders substantivized *afflitto* by an ongoing action; *ἰδίᾳ* makes ownership more emphatic.
- *’μυκᾶτο* stands for *ἐμυκᾶτο*, third singular imperfect middle indicative of *μυκάομαι* ‘bellow’, with contraction of *-άετο* and prodelision of the syllabic augment after *φωνῇ*. The imperfect answers *mugghiava*, in contrast to the first bellow, which Greek expressed nominally.
- *ὥστε* with indicative *ἐδόκει* introduces an actual result. Inside it, *καίπερ … ὤν* takes the place of finite *con tutto ch’ ei fosse*: the present participle of *εἰμί* is nominative masculine singular with the bull as subject. *χάλκεος*, the uncontracted form beside Attic *χαλκοῦς*, turns *di rame* into a predicate adjective. *ὅλος* ‘entirely’ qualifies the bull; it does not translate *tutto* in its Italian concessive function. *ὅμως* ‘nevertheless’ resumes the concession like *pure*.
- *ὑπὸ τῆς λύπης κεντούμενος ἐδόκει* ‘it seemed to be undergoing piercing by pain’ retains pain as the effective agent (*ὑπό* with genitive). *κεντούμενος* is the contracted present passive participle of *κεντέω*, suggesting a continuing action rather than the resultant state of *trafitto*. Musurus places it predicatively beside *ἐδόκει*, third singular imperfect active of *δοκέω*, on the pattern of *pareva … trafitto*; an infinitive construction would be more usual with Greek *δοκέω* ‘seem’.

> 13 così per non aver via nè forame  
> 14 dal principio del foco, in suo linguaggio  
> 15 si convertivan le parole grame.  

: thus, having at their commencement no way or outlet from the fire, the dismal words were changed into its language.

> 13 Οὕτως οὐδέν’ ἔχοντες πόρον ἐξόδου  
> (So, having no passage of exit)
>
> 14 Ἐν τῇ φλογὶ τὸ πρῶτον, ὁπότ’ ἐλάλει,  
> (In the flame at first, when it was speaking,)
>
> 15 Οἱ βαρύθυμοι συνεχέοντο λόγοι.  
> (The sorrowful words were confounded.)

- *οὕτως* answers *ὡς*. *οὐδέν᾽ ἔχοντες πόρον ἐξόδου* ‘having no passage of escape’ combines *via nè forame* into one noun phrase. *οὐδέν᾽* is elided *οὐδένα*, accusative masculine singular modifying *πόρον*; *ἐξόδου* is a dependent genitive specifying what the passage is for. *ἔχοντες*, present active participle of *ἔχω*, nominative masculine plural, anticipates *λόγοι* and gives the cause expressed by *per non aver*.
- *ἐν τῇ φλογί* locates the words in the flame rather than explicitly marking movement *del foco*, out of the fire. *τὸ πρῶτον* is an adverbial accusative ‘at first’, giving *dal principio* a temporal sense. *ὁπότ᾽ ἐλάλει* ‘when it was speaking’ introduces a temporal clause with elided *ὁπότε* and third singular imperfect active indicative of *λαλέω*; it has no direct clausal counterpart in Dante.
- *οἱ βαρύθυμοι … λόγοι* ‘the heavy-hearted words’ transfers a disposition of the speaker to the words, as does *parole grame*. *συνεχέοντο* is third plural imperfect middle/passive indicative of *συγχέω*, passive in sense, with internal augment. It means ‘were being mingled/confounded’, not ‘were being turned into’: the Greek evokes the indistinct sound but gives no equivalent for the destination of the change, *in suo linguaggio*.

> 16 Ma poscia ch' ebber colto lor viaggio  
> 17 su per la punta, dandole quel guizzo  
> 18 che dato avea la lingua in lor passaggio,  

But after they had found their road up through the point, giving to it the vibration which the tongue had given in their passage,

> 16 Ἀλλ’ ἐπεὶ τὴν δίοδον εὔπορον εὗρον  
> (But when they found the passage clear)
>
> 17 Ἐκ τῆς κορυφῆς ἕνεκα τῶν παλμάτων,  
> (Out of the peak because of the vibrations,)
>
> 18 Ἅπερ ἐποίει τὸ κίνημα τῆς γλώσσης,  
> (Which the movement of the tongue produced,)

- *ἀλλ᾽ ἐπεί* ‘but when’ combines elided *ἀλλά* with a temporal conjunction. *εὗρον* is third plural second aorist active indicative of *εὑρίσκω* (with *ευ-* rather than the alternative augmented *ηυ-*). The aorist in the temporal clause does duty for *ebber colto*. *τὴν δίοδον εὔπορον* supplies no possessive for *lor*: *εὔπορον*, outside the article–noun group, is naturally predicative, ‘found the passage clear’.
- *ἐκ τῆς κορυφῆς* ‘out of the summit’ gives the point of emergence rather than the upward course of *su per la punta*. *ἕνεκα τῶν παλμάτων* ‘because of the vibrations’ changes the construction and the causal relation: instead of the words giving the tip *quel guizzo*, the plural *πάλματα* (genitive plural of *πάλμα*) names what accounts for their exit. There is no dative recipient to answer *-le* in *dandole*.
- *ἅπερ ἐποίει τὸ κίνημα τῆς γλώσσης* ‘which the movement of the tongue produced’ makes *τὸ κίνημα*, not the tongue itself, the subject. *ἅπερ* is accusative neuter plural of *ὅσπερ*, agreeing in number and gender with *παλμάτων* but taking its case as object of *ἐποίει*. This contracted third singular imperfect active indicative of *ποιέω* gives an ongoing production in place of pluperfect *dato avea*. *in lor passaggio* has no separate rendering.

> 19 udimmo dire: “O tu, a cui io drizzo  
> 20 la voce, e che parlavi mo Lombardo,  
> 21 dicendo: ‘Issa ten va, più non t' adizzo’;  

we heard it say: “O thou, at whom I aim my voice! and who just now wast speaking Lombard, saying, ‘Now go, no more I urge thee’

> 19 Τότ’ ἠκούσαμεν· “Σύ, πρὸς ὃν ἀποτείνω  
> (Then we heard: “You, toward whom I direct)
>
> 20 „ Τὴν φωνήν, ὅστις λογγοβαρδικῶς ἄρτι  
> („ My voice, who in Lombard speech just now)
>
> 21 „ Εἶπας,” “Ἄπελθε τανῦν, οὐκέτ’ ἐπείγω,”  
> („ Said,” “Depart now, I urge you no more,”)

- *τότ᾽ ἠκούσαμεν* ‘then we heard’ supplies elided *τότε* in answer to *ἐπεί*. *ἠκούσαμεν* is first plural aorist active indicative of *ἀκούω*, with temporal augment. The utterance follows directly: no infinitive corresponds to *dire*. *σύ* alone, without *ὦ*, serves as the address *O tu*.
- *πρὸς ὃν ἀποτείνω τὴν φωνήν* ‘towards whom I direct the voice’ gives the destination *a cui* by *πρός* with accusative masculine singular relative. *ἀποτείνω* is first singular present active indicative; its ending carries *io*. As in Italian, the verb and its object are divided between lines.
- *ὅστις λογγοβαρδικῶς ἄρτι εἶπας* ‘you who just now said in Lombard’ continues without a conjunction corresponding to *e*. *ὅστις*, nominative masculine singular compound relative, characterizes the addressee; the verb remains second person after *σύ*. The adverbs of language and time render *Lombardo* and *mo*. Rather than keep both imperfect *parlavi* and participial *dicendo*, Musurus uses the single verb *εἶπας*, second singular aorist active indicative of ‘say’, the form in *-ας* beside *εἶπες*.
- *ἄπελθε τανῦν* ‘depart now’ reverses the order of *Issa ten va*. *ἄπελθε* is second singular second aorist active imperative of *ἀπέρχομαι*, without augment, from the stem of *ἦλθον*; *τανῦν* is the adverbial fusion of *τὰ νῦν*. *οὐκέτ᾽ ἐπείγω* ‘I no longer press’ uses elided *οὐκέτι* and first singular present active indicative of *ἐπείγω*. The object corresponding to *t’* is left implicit.

> 22 perch' io sia giunto forse alquanto tardo,  
> 23 non t' incresca restare a parlar meco:  
> 24 vedi che non incresce a me, ed ardo.  

; though I have come perhaps a little late, let it not irk thee to pause and speak with me: thou seest it irks not me, although I burn.

> 22 „ Εἰ καὶ μικρόν τι βράδιον ἴσως ἦλθον,  
> („ Even though perhaps I have come a little late,)
>
> 23 „ Δέομαι, μεῖνον ἐμοὶ συνομιλήσων.  
> („ I pray you, stay to converse with me.)
>
> 24 „ Μένοντά με δὴ καὶ φλεγόμενον βλέπεις.  
> („ You see that I indeed stay, even while burning.)

- *εἰ καὶ … ἦλθον* ‘even if I came’ renders concessive *perch’ io sia giunto* by *εἰ καί* with an indicative rather than a subjunctive. *ἦλθον* is here first singular, second aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι*. *μικρόν τι* is an accusative of degree, ‘by some small amount’, with comparative adverb *βράδιον* ‘later’ (from *βραδύς*); *ἴσως* preserves *forse*.
- *δέομαι, μεῖνον* ‘I beg you, stay’ substitutes a personal appeal and positive command for the impersonal negative wish *non t’ incresca restare*. *δέομαι* is first singular present middle indicative, used without an expressed complement; *μεῖνον* is second singular aorist active imperative of *μένω*. *ἐμοὶ συνομιλήσων* ‘in order to converse with me’ has a future active participle of purpose, nominative masculine singular of *συνομιλέω*, agreeing with the understood subject of the command. The compound verb takes a dative of the person addressed, not a prepositional phrase as in *meco*.
- *μένοντά με δὴ καὶ φλεγόμενον βλέπεις* replaces *vedi che* and its finite clause with a verb of perception governing an accusative and participles. *με* is the object of *βλέπεις* (second singular present active indicative of *βλέπω*); *μένοντα* and *φλεγόμενον* are accusative masculine singular, respectively present active of *μένω* and present passive of *φλέγω*. The extra accent on *μένοντά* is due to enclitic *με*.
- *δή* emphasizes what the addressee can see. Italian repeats *incresca … incresce*, whereas Greek repeats *μεῖνον … μένοντα*: ‘stay … you see me staying’. *καὶ φλεγόμενον* adds the simultaneous circumstance ‘even while being burned’; the concessive force of *ed ardo* is suggested by *καί* and the juxtaposition, not by a separate concessive clause.

## Lines 25–57 — Peace and War in Romagna

> 25 Se tu pur mo in questo mondo cieco  
> 26 caduto sei di quella dolce terra  
> 27 Latina, ond' io mia colpa tutta reco,  

If thou art but now fallen into this blind world from that sweet Latian land, whence I bring all my guilt,

> 25 „ Εἰ σύ γ’ ἔναγχος εἰς τυφλὸν τόνδε κόσμον  
> („ If you just now into this blind world)
>
> 26 „ Ἐκ γλυκείας ἔπεσες γῆς Λατινίδος  
> („ Have fallen from sweet Latian land,)
>
> 27 „ Ἐκείνης, ὅθεν πᾶν ἔγκλημ’ ἐμὸν φέρω,  
> („ That land, whence I bear all my guilt,)

- *Εἰ σύ γ’ ἔναγχος … ἔπεσες* renders *Se tu pur mo … caduto sei*. The expressed subject *σύ* is emphatic; *γ’* is the focusing particle *γε*, elided before *ἔναγχος* ‘just lately’. The condition has *εἰ* with the indicative, not a subjunctive or optative. *ἔπεσες* (2nd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *πίπτω*) makes the fall a single past event in place of the Italian compound perfect.
- *εἰς τυφλὸν τόνδε κόσμον* uses *εἰς* with the accusative of destination, where Italian has *in*; *τόνδε* (acc. sg. masc. of *ὅδε*) marks the world as ‘this one here’. Musurus brings the source phrase forward around the verb: *ἐκ γλυκείας … γῆς Λατινίδος ἐκείνης*, literally ‘out of sweet … land, Latian, that one’. *γλυκείας* (from *γλυκύς*), *Λατινίδος* and the delayed demonstrative *ἐκείνης* all agree with *γῆς* in the feminine genitive singular.
- *ὅθεν* is itself a relative adverb of source, ‘from where’, for *ond’*. In *πᾶν ἔγκλημ’ ἐμὸν φέρω*, ‘I bear all my guilt’, *ἔγκλημ’* is elided *ἔγκλημα*, ‘charge, ground of reproach’, somewhat more judicial than *colpa*. *πᾶν* and possessive *ἐμόν* qualify this neuter accusative singular, the object of *φέρω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.).

> 28 dimmi se i Romagnoli han pace o guerra:  
> 29 ch' io fui de' monti là intra Urbino  
> 30 e il giogo di che 'l Tever si disserra.”  

tell me if the Romagnuols have peace or war : for I was of the mountains there, between Urbino and the yoke from which the Tiber springs.”

> 28 „ Λέξον· εἰρήνην ἢ πόλεμον Ῥωμάνοι  
> („ Tell me: do the Romagnols peace or war)
>
> 29 „ Ἄγουσ’ ; ἤμην γὰρ ἐξ ὀρέων Οὐρβίνου  
> („ Keep? for I was from the mountains of Urbino)
>
> 30 „ Καὶ τῆς δειράδος, ὅθεν πηγάζει Θύμβρις.”  
> („ And from the ridge whence the Thymbris springs.”)

- *Λέξον* (2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *λέγω*) renders *dimmi* without a pronoun for ‘me’. Rather than subordinate a question with *se*, Musurus asks it directly: *εἰρήνην ἢ πόλεμον Ῥωμάνοι ἄγουσ’ ;* The alternatives stand first in the accusative; *ἄγουσ’* is elided *ἄγουσι*, 3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *ἄγω*, with *Ῥωμάνοι* nominative plural. The one verb serves both objects, ‘keep peace or carry on war’, for Italian *han*.
- Postpositive *γάρ* gives *ch’* its explanatory force. *ἤμην* ‘I was’ is a later 1st sg. imperfect of *εἰμί* (Attic *ἦ*, *ἦν*). *ἐξ* before a vowel is the form of *ἐκ*; poetic uncontracted *ὀρέων* is gen. pl. of *ὄρος* (Attic *ὀρῶν*). The Greek says ‘from the mountains of Urbino and the ridge’: *Οὐρβίνου* depends on *ὀρέων*, and *τῆς δειράδος* (from *δειράς*) is a second genitive under *ἐξ*. Neither *là* nor the relation *intra* ‘between’ is expressed.
- *ὅθεν πηγάζει Θύμβρις* replaces *di che ’l Tever si disserra* with a source adverb and an intransitive active, ‘whence Thymbris wells up’. *Θύμβρις*, the Greek river name, is the postponed nominative subject of *πηγάζει* (3rd sg. present).

> 31 Io era in giuso ancora attento e chino,  

I still was eager downwards and bent,

> 31 Ἀτενὴς καὶ προσκλινὴς ἤκουον ἔτι,  
> (Intent and leaning forward I was still listening,)

- *Ἀτενὴς καὶ προσκλινὴς ἤκουον ἔτι* makes listening, implicit in *era … attento*, the finite action: *ἤκουον* is the augmented 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἀκούω*, here ‘I was listening’. The two nominative singular adjectives describe the unexpressed subject while he listens: *ἀτενής* ‘intent’, *προσκλινής* ‘leaning towards’. The latter conveys inclination but does not separately specify *in giuso*. *ἔτι* ‘still’ moves to the end.

> 32 quando il mio duca mi tentò di costa,  
> 33 dicendo : “Parla tu, questi è Latino.”  

when my Leader touched me on the side, saying : “Speak thou; this is a Latian.”

> 32 Ὅτε πλευρόθεν ἥψατό μου Προστάτης  
> (When on my side my Master touched me,)
>
> 33 Λέγων· “Ὁμίλει, Λατῖνός ἐστιν οὗτος.”  
> (Saying: “Speak, this man is a Latian.”)

- Temporal *ὅτε* introduces *ἥψατό μου*: *ἥψατο* is 3rd sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἅπτω*, whose middle *ἅπτομαι* ‘touch’ governs the genitive. Thus enclitic *μου* belongs to the verb, not to *Προστάτης* ‘leader, protector’; *mio* has no separate equivalent. The extra acute on *ἥψατό* is due to the enclitic. *πλευρόθεν*, with adverbial *-θεν* ‘from’, renders *di costa* as ‘from the side’.
- *λέγων* (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *λέγω*) agrees with *Προστάτης* and accompanies the act of touching, like *dicendo*. *Ὁμίλει* is the contracted 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *ὁμιλέω* ‘converse, speak’; unlike *Parla tu*, it leaves the subject unexpressed. In *Λατῖνός ἐστιν οὗτος* the predicate comes first and the demonstrative subject last, reversing *questi è Latino*.

> 34 Ed io ch’ avea già pronta la risposta,  
> 35 senza indugio a parlare incominciai :  

And I, who had my answer ready then, began without delay to speak

> 34 Κἀγὼ τὴν ἀπόκρισιν ἔχων ἑτοίμην,  
> (And I, having my answer ready,)
>
> 35 Ἠρξάμην παραυτίκα τοιάδε λέγων·  
> (Began straightway speaking thus:)

- *Κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*. The relative clause *ch’ avea già pronta la risposta* becomes a circumstantial participle, *τὴν ἀπόκρισιν ἔχων ἑτοίμην* ‘having the answer ready’. *ἔχων* (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. of *ἔχω*) agrees with the speaker; *ἑτοίμην* is a predicate adjective agreeing with the accusative object *τὴν ἀπόκρισιν*. The participle presents a state obtaining when he began; *già* is not separately rendered.
- *Ἠρξάμην* (1st sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἄρχω*, middle ‘begin’) takes the supplementary present participle *λέγων*, where *incominciai* takes *a parlare*. *παραυτίκα* ‘straightaway’ renders the negative phrase *senza indugio* positively; *τοιάδε*, neut. acc. pl. of *τοιόσδε*, supplies an object pointing forward, ‘things such as these’.

> 36 “O anima, che se’ laggiù nascosta,  

: “O soul, that there below art hidden!

> 36 “Ὦ ψυχή, κεκρυμμένη νῦν οὖσα κάτω,  
> (“O soul, who are now hidden down below,)

- *Ὦ ψυχή* is a vocative singular. For the finite relative *che se’ … nascosta*, Musurus attaches feminine singular participles to the address: *κεκρυμμένη* (perf. mid./pass. of *κρύπτω*, passive in sense) expresses the resulting state ‘hidden’, and *οὖσα* (pres. of *εἰμί*) makes explicit ‘being’ in that state. *κάτω* gives the downward location of *laggiù* without a distinct ‘there’; *νῦν* ‘now’ is added.

> 37 Romagna tua non è, e non fu mai,  
> 38 senza guerra ne’ cor de’ suoi tiranni ;  
> 39 ma ’n palese nessuna or vi lasciai.  

thy Romagna is not, and never was, without war in the hearts of her tyrants ; but open[ly] just now I there left none.

> 37 „ Οὐκ ἔστιν, οὔτε ποτ᾽ ἦν, σὴ Ῥωμανία  
> („ Never is, nor ever was, your Romagna)
>
> 38 „ Βουλῇ τυράννων αὑτῆς ἄνευ πολέμου·  
> („ Without war in the counsel of its tyrants;)
>
> 39 „ Ἀλλὰ νῦν ἐκεῖ φανερὸν οὐκ ἀφῆκα.  
> („ Yet open war I left none there just now.)

- *Οὐκ ἔστιν, οὔτε ποτ᾽ ἦν* places both verbs before *σὴ Ῥωμανία*, whereas *Romagna tua* stands first. *ἔστιν* is present and *ἦν* imperfect indicative, both 3rd sg. of *εἰμί*; *οὔτε ποτ᾽* (elided *ποτε*) gives ‘nor ever’. *σή* is a possessive adjective agreeing with *Ῥωμανία*.
- *βουλῇ τυράννων αὑτῆς* replaces *ne’ cor de’ suoi tiranni* with ‘in the counsel of her own tyrants’. *βουλῇ* is dat. sg. of *βουλή*, marking the sphere in which the assertion holds; *τυράννων* depends on it, and reflexive *αὑτῆς* (contracted from *ἑαυτῆς*) refers possession back to Romagna. *ἄνευ πολέμου*, with genitive after *ἄνευ*, delays *senza guerra* to the end.
- *Ἀλλὰ νῦν ἐκεῖ φανερὸν οὐκ ἀφῆκα* recasts *ma ’n palese nessuna or vi lasciai*. *φανερόν* can qualify an understood accusative *πόλεμον*, ‘open [war]’, rather than serving as a prepositional adverbial like *’n palese*. Negation is on the verb instead of in a pronoun corresponding to *nessuna*. *ἀφῆκα* is 1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀφίημι*; *νῦν* and *ἐκεῖ* render *or* and *vi*.

> 40 Ravenna sta, come stata è molti anni:  

Ravenna stands, as it has stood for many years

> 40 „ Ἔστι Ῥαύεννα καθὼς ἐν πάλαι χρόνοις.  
> („ Ravenna is just as it was in days of old.)

- *Ἔστι Ῥαύεννα* puts the verb first and substitutes ‘is’ for *sta*. *καθὼς ἐν πάλαι χρόνοις* ‘as in times long past’ has no expressed second verb. *πάλαι*, normally an adverb ‘long ago’, qualifies *χρόνοις* within *ἐν* + dative. The comparison is with an earlier state rather than an explicit rendering of the duration *molti anni*.

> 41 l’ aquila da Polenta la si cova,  
> 42 sì che Cervia ricopre co’ suoi vanni.  

: the Eagle of Polenta broods over it, so that he covers Cervia with his pinions.

> 41 „ Ἐπωάζων ἀετὸς ἐκεῖ Πολέντου  
> („ Brooding there, the eagle of Polenta)
>
> 42 „ Τοῖς πτεροῖς αὑτοῦ τὴν Φικόκλην σκεπάζει·  
> („ Covers Ficocle with its wings;)

- *Ἐπωάζων ἀετὸς ἐκεῖ Πολέντου* demotes finite *la si cova* to *ἐπωάζων* ‘brooding’ (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *ἐπωάζω*), agreeing with *ἀετός*. *ἐκεῖ* ‘there’ indicates the place rather than supplying a pronominal object. The genitive *Πολέντου* renders *da Polenta* as an affiliation.
- *σκεπάζει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *σκεπάζω*) is now the principal verb, not a result clause introduced by *sì che*. It takes *τὴν Φικόκλην*, a Greek place name in the accusative for *Cervia*. *τοῖς πτεροῖς αὑτοῦ* ‘with its own wings’ uses the instrumental dative plural of *πτερόν* and the contracted reflexive genitive for *co’ suoi vanni*.

> 43 La terra, che fe’ già la lunga prova,  
> 44 e de’ Franceschi sanguinoso mucchio,  
> 45 sotto le branche verdi si ritrova.  

The city, which made erewhile the long probation, and sanguinary heap of [the] Frenchmen, finds itself again under the Green Clutches.

> 43 „ Γῆ δ᾽ ἡ μακροὺς ἀγῶνας ὑποστᾶσ᾽ ἤδη,  
> („ And the land that already endured long struggles,)
>
> 44 „ Καὶ τῶν Γαλατῶν αἱματώδη σωρείαν  
> („ And of the French a bloody heap)
>
> 45 „ Ποιήσασ’, ὑπ᾽ ὄνυχας χλωρούς ἐστ᾽ ἔτι.  
> („ Made, still lies beneath the green talons.)

- *Γῆ δ᾽ ἡ …* ‘and the land which …’ keeps the territorial sense of *terra*, adding connective *δέ* (elided). The article *ἡ* introduces two attributive participles in place of *che fe’*. Musurus distinguishes what the land underwent from what it made: *μακροὺς ἀγῶνας ὑποστᾶσ᾽ ἤδη* ‘having already endured long struggles’ turns singular *la lunga prova* into a plural object. *ὑποστᾶσ᾽* is elided *ὑποστᾶσα*, nom. sg. fem. second aor. act. participle of *ὑφίστημι*; *ἤδη* gives the temporal force of *già*.
- *τῶν Γαλατῶν αἱματώδη σωρείαν ποιήσασ’* delays the second participle until after its object. *ποιήσασ’* is elided *ποιήσασα* (nom. sg. fem. aor. act. of *ποιέω*); like *ὑποστᾶσα* it agrees with *γῆ*. *αἱματώδη* (acc. sg. fem. of *αἱματώδης*) qualifies *σωρείαν* ‘heap’; *τῶν Γαλατῶν* ‘of the Gauls’ gives the constituents of the heap, using a classical ethnonym for *Franceschi*.
- *ὑπ᾽ ὄνυχας χλωρούς* renders *sotto le branche verdi* with ‘under green talons’ (*ὄνυχας*, acc. pl. of *ὄνυξ*). *ὑπό* with accusative marks subjection here, not motion. *ἐστ᾽ ἔτι* (elided *ἐστί*) is ‘is still’, making *si ritrova* a continuing state rather than a reflexive act or an explicit return.

> 46 Il Mastin vecchio e il nuovo da Verrucchio,  
> 47 che fecer di Montagna il mal governo,  
> 48 là dove soglion fan de’ denti succhio.  

The old Mastiff of Verrucchio and the young, who of Montagna made evil governance, there, where they are wont, ply their teeth.

> 46 „ Βεῤῥουκκίου δ᾽ ὁ γέρων καὶ νέος κύων,  
> („ And the old and the young hound of Verrucchio,)
>
> 47 „ Οἱ κακώσαντες ἀνηλεῶς Μοντάγναν,  
> („ Who cruelly ill-treated Montagna,)
>
> 48 „ Ὀδὰξ ἐκεῖ δάκνουσιν, ὡς αὐτοῖς ἔθος.  
> („ Bite with their teeth there, as is their wont.)

- *Βεῤῥουκκίου δ᾽* places the genitive corresponding to *da Verrucchio* first; *ῤῥ* is the traditional spelling of doubled rho with smooth and rough breathings. In *ὁ γέρων καὶ νέος κύων* the shared singular noun *κύων* ‘hound’ follows the contrasting ‘old’ and ‘young’. *νέος* reads *nuovo* as young rather than merely new; *κύων* is less specific than *Mastin*. The ensuing participle and verb treat the pair as plural, not dual.
- *οἱ κακώσαντες* is an articular nom. pl. masc. aor. act. participle of *κακόω*, in apposition, ‘the ones who harmed’. *Μοντάγναν* is its accusative object. Thus *che fecer di Montagna il mal governo* becomes a transitive verb with a personal object, intensified by *ἀνηλεῶς* ‘mercilessly’.
- *ὀδὰξ … δάκνουσιν* ‘they bite with their teeth’ couples a poetic adverb with the 3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *δάκνω*, replacing *fan de’ denti succhio* with an explicit act of biting. *ἐκεῖ* gives *là*, but *dove soglion* becomes comparative *ὡς αὐτοῖς ἔθος* ‘as is customary for them’: *αὐτοῖς* is dative plural, *ἔθος* a neuter singular noun, with *ἐστί* understood.

> 49 Le città di Lamone e di Santerno  
> 50 conduce il leoncel dal nido bianco,  
> 51 che muta parte dalla state al verno;  

The cities of Lamone and Santerno guides [the] Lioncel of the white lair, who changes faction from the summer to the winter

> 49 „ Τῶν πρὸς Λαμόνῃ πόλεων καὶ Βαδρίνῳ  
> („ The cities by the Lamone and Vatrenus)
>
> 50 „ Ὁ λευκῆς ἄρχει φωλεᾶς λεοντίσκος,  
> („ Are ruled by the lioncel of the white lair,)
>
> 51 „ Μερίδ᾽ ἀλλάσσων ἐκ θέρους εἰς χειμῶνα·  
> („ Who changes his party from summer to winter;)

- *Τῶν πρὸς Λαμόνῃ πόλεων καὶ Βαδρίνῳ* preserves the cities’ initial position but not their case relation: *πόλεων* (gen. pl. of *πόλις*) is governed by *ἄρχει* ‘rules’, not an accusative object as with *conduce*. *πρός* with the datives *Λαμόνῃ* and *Βαδρίνῳ* means ‘by’ the two rivers, interpreting Italian *di* locally; the second river name, representing *Santerno*, is delayed beyond *πόλεων*.
- In *ὁ λευκῆς ἄρχει φωλεᾶς λεοντίσκος*, *ὁ* belongs with the delayed nominative *λεοντίσκος*, a diminutive corresponding to *leoncel*. The subject encloses both its genitive of affiliation, *λευκῆς … φωλεᾶς* ‘of a white lair’, and the verb *ἄρχει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἄρχω*). *φωλεά* makes *nido* a lair rather than a bird’s nest.
- *μερίδ᾽ ἀλλάσσων* ‘changing party’ replaces *che muta parte* with a nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *ἀλλάσσω* agreeing with *λεοντίσκος*. Its object *μερίδ᾽* is elided *μερίδα*, acc. sg. of *μερίς*. *ἐκ θέρους εἰς χειμῶνα* closely matches *dalla state al verno* with genitive of starting point (*θέρος*) and accusative of endpoint (*χειμών*).

> 52 e quella a cui il Savio bagna il fianco,  
> 53 così com’ ella sie’ tra il piano e il monte,  
> 54 tra tirannia si vive e stato franco.  

; and that [city] whose flank the Savio bathes, as it lies between the plain and mount, so lives it between tyranny and freedom.

> 52 „ Ἡ δὲ πόλις, ἧς βρέχει τὴν πλευρὰν Σάπις,  
> („ And the city whose flank the Sapis washes,)
>
> 53 „ Μέσον οὖσ’ ὄρους καὶ πεδιάδος, μέσον  
> („ Standing midway between mountain and plain, midway)
>
> 54 „ Ἐλευθερίας ζῇ νῦν καὶ τυραννίδος.—  
> („ Between freedom and tyranny now lives.—)

- *Ἡ δὲ πόλις* supplies the noun left implicit in *e quella*, with postpositive *δέ* marking the transition. *ἧς βρέχει τὴν πλευρὰν Σάπις* ‘whose flank Sapis wets’ converts Italian *a cui* into a possessive relative genitive dependent on *πλευράν*. Greek places the nominative river name after the verb and its object, reversing the order of *il Savio bagna il fianco*.
- *μέσον οὖσ’ ὄρους καὶ πεδιάδος* ‘being midway between mountain and plain’ reverses *il piano e il monte*. *οὖσ’* is elided *οὖσα*, nom. sg. fem. pres. participle of *εἰμί*, agreeing with *πόλις*; it replaces finite *sie’*. *μέσον* is adverbial, not a neuter adjective agreeing with the city. The genitives *ὄρους* (from *ὄρος*) and *πεδιάδος* (from *πεδιάς*) specify the limits.
- Repetition of *μέσον* carries the comparison without explicit equivalents of *così com’ … così*. *ἐλευθερίας … καὶ τυραννίδος* gives the second pair of limiting genitives, again in reverse Italian order; abstract *ἐλευθερία* ‘freedom’ replaces *stato franco*. *ζῇ* is contracted 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ζάω*, with the city itself as subject instead of impersonal *si vive*. *νῦν* ‘now’ is supplied.

> 55 Ora chi sei ti prego che ne conte;  

Now I pray thee, tell us who thou art

> 55 „ Ἤδη σύ, τίς εἶ, λέγε μοι, δέομαί σου·  
> („ Now you, tell me who you are, I pray you;)

- *Ἤδη σύ* renders *Ora* with an emphatic turn to the addressee, ‘now you’. *τίς εἶ* (interrogative nominative *τίς*, 2nd sg. pres. ind. *εἶ* of *εἰμί*) keeps the question ahead of the request. *λέγε μοι* ‘tell me’ uses the 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *λέγω* with a singular dative recipient, unlike “tell us”.
- *δέομαί σου* ‘I beg you’ comes last and stands independently: the Greek has an imperative and a parenthetic appeal rather than *ti prego che … conte* with a dependent subjunctive. *δέομαι* is 1st sg. present middle indicative and takes a genitive of the person entreated, hence *σου*; its final acute is added before the enclitic.

> 56 non esser duro più ch’ altri sia stato,  

; be not more hard than one has been to thee,

> 56 „ Μή γε δὴ σκληρότερος ἔσο τῶν ἄλλων·  
> („ Be not, I beg, harder than the others;)

- *Μή γε δὴ … ἔσο* negates an imperative with *μή*; focusing *γε* and emphatic *δή* lend urgency to the entreaty without corresponding to separate Italian words. *ἔσο* is a poetic 2nd sg. present imperative of *εἰμί*, with a middle-form ending, ‘be’.
- *σκληρότερος* (nom. sg. masc., comparative of *σκληρός*) is predicative. *τῶν ἄλλων* is the genitive of comparison, ‘than the others’: Musurus takes *altri* as plural and dispenses with *sia stato*. The Greek thus compares the addressee with other people directly, not with how someone has previously behaved towards him.

> 57 se il nome tuo nel mondo tegna fronte.”  

so may thy name on earth maintain its front.”

> 57 „ Εἴθ᾽ ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ μεγαλώνυμος εἴης.”  
> („ So may you be of great renown in the world.”)

- *Εἴθ᾽* is *εἴθε* ‘if only, would that’, elided before *ἐν*. With *εἴης* (2nd sg. pres. optative of *εἰμί*) the particle introduces an independent wish, not a conditional protasis; no *ἄν* is needed.
- *ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ* gives *nel mondo* with a locative dative. *μεγαλώνυμος* ‘of great name, renowned’ is a nominative predicate adjective referring to ‘you’: the Greek wishes renown to the person rather than making *il nome tuo* the subject of *tegna fronte*. The adjective retains the idea of a name but not the image of maintaining its front.

## Lines 58–90 — The Flame Speaks of Arms and Repentance

> 58 Poscia che il foco alquanto ebbe rugghiato  
> 59 al modo suo, l' aguta punta mosse  
> 60 di qua, di là, e poi diè cotal fiato :  

After the flame had roared awhile as usual, it moved the sharp point to and fro, and then gave forth this breath

> 58 Ἐπεὶ δ᾽ ὑπεσύριξεν ἡ φλὸξ μικρόν τι  
> (After the flame had hissed softly a little)
>
> 59 Ἴδιον τρόπον, ἔσεισε τὴν ὀξεῖαν  
> (In its own fashion, it shook its sharp)
>
> 60 Ἄκραν ἔνθεν κἀκεῖθεν, πνεύσασα τάδε·  
> (Tip to and fro, breathing forth these words:)

- *Ἐπεὶ δ᾽ ὑπεσύριξεν ἡ φλόξ* keeps the temporal clause of *Poscia che il foco alquanto ebbe rugghiato*, but the subject is ‘the flame’ rather than ‘the fire’, and the sound a low hiss or whistle rather than a roar. *Ὑπεσύριξεν* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ὑποσυρίζω*, with the augment inside the compound. The aorist after temporal *ἐπεί* suffices for the completed action of *ebbe rugghiato*; *δ᾽* is connective *δέ* elided.
- *Μικρόν τι* is an adverbial neuter accusative, ‘a little’; *ἴδιον τρόπον* is an accusative of manner, ‘in its own fashion’, without a preposition corresponding to *al*. *Ἔσεισε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *σείω*) makes *mosse* more specifically ‘shook’. Its object *τὴν ὀξεῖαν ἄκραν* reproduces adjective before noun in *l’aguta punta*; *ἔνθεν κἀκεῖθεν* gives the two directions of *di qua, di là*, with crasis of *καὶ ἐκεῖθεν*.
- In place of the second finite verb and explicit sequence in *e poi diè cotal fiato*, *πνεύσασα τάδε* gives ‘breathing forth the following’. *Πνεύσασα* (nom. sg. fem. aor. act. participle of *πνέω*) agrees with *φλόξ* and expresses an attendant action; *τάδε*, neut. acc. pl. of *ὅδε*, points forward to the words themselves. There is no separate ‘then’ or noun for ‘breath’.

> 61 " S' io credessi che mia risposta fosse  
> 62 a persona che mai tornasse al mondo,  
> 63 questa fiamma staria senza più scosse ;  

: "If I thought my answer were to one who ever could return to the world, this flame should shake no more;

> 61 “Εἴπερ ᾠόμην ὑπ᾽ ἀνδρὸς ἐρωτᾶσθαι  
> (“If I thought I were being questioned by a man)
>
> 62 „ Εἰς τὸν κόσμον μέλλοντος παλινοστῆσαι,  
> („ Who was about to return into the world,)
>
> 63 „ Ἡ φλὸξ ἥδ᾽ ἂν εἱστήκει μὴ σειομένη.  
> („ This flame would stand still without shaking.)

- *Εἴπερ ᾠόμην* ‘if indeed I thought’ uses emphatic *-περ* and the imperfect middle indicative of *οἴομαι* (1st sg.), not a subjunctive corresponding to *credessi*. The past indicative in the condition is answered by a past indicative with *ἄν* in the consequence: the supposition is contrary to what the speaker believes.
- *Che mia risposta fosse a persona* becomes *ὑπ᾽ ἀνδρὸς ἐρωτᾶσθαι*, ‘that I was being questioned by a man’. The present passive infinitive of *ἐρωτάω* has the same understood subject as *ᾠόμην*; *ὑπό* with the genitive marks the questioner as agent rather than the recipient of an answer. *Μέλλοντος*, gen. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *μέλλω*, agrees with *ἀνδρός* and takes the aor. act. infinitive *παλινοστῆσαι* ‘to return’. This participial construction gives a prospective sense to *che mai tornasse* without a separate word for *mai*; *εἰς τὸν κόσμον* marks the destination.
- *Ἡ φλὸξ ἥδ᾽* ‘this flame’ has the demonstrative *ἥδε* outside the article–noun group. *Ἂν εἱστήκει* is 3rd sg. pluperf. act. ind. of *ἵστημι* with modal *ἄν*: the intransitive perfect stem means ‘stand’, hence ‘would stand still’, not ‘would have set up’. *Μὴ σειομένη* (nom. sg. fem. pres. pass. participle of *σείω*) turns *senza più scosse* into ‘not being shaken’, without separately expressing *più*.

> 64 ma per ciò che giammai di questo fondo  
> 65 non tornò vivo alcun, s' i' odo il vero,  
> 66 senza tema d' infamia ti rispondo.  

but since none ever did return alive from this depth, if what I hear be true, without fear of infamy I answer thee.

> 64 „ Ἀλλ᾽ εἰδὼς μηδένα ποτ᾽ ἐκ βυθοῦ τοῦδε  
> („ But knowing that no one ever from this depth)
>
> 65 „ Ἐκεῖσ᾽ ἀνελθεῖν, εἰ τἀληθὲς ἀκούω,  
> („ Has gone up thither, if I hear the truth,)
>
> 66 „ Ἄνευ δέους αἰσχύνης φράσω σοι πάντα.  
> („ Without fear of shame I will tell you all.)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ εἰδώς* ‘but knowing’ replaces causal *ma per ciò che* with a circumstantial participle agreeing with the speaker: *εἰδώς* is nom. sg. masc. of the perfect active participle of *οἶδα*, present in sense. It governs an accusative and infinitive, *μηδένα ποτ᾽ … ἀνελθεῖν*, ‘that no one ever went up’. *Μηδένα* is acc. sg. masc. of *μηδείς*, subject of the infinitive; the *μη-* negative goes with the infinitival formulation, though *οὐδένα* would be natural in reporting a fact known.
- *Ἐκ βυθοῦ τοῦδε* renders *di questo fondo* with a genitive of source; *ἐκεῖσε* ‘thither’ adds a destination. *Ἀνελθεῖν*, second aor. act. infinitive of *ἀνέρχομαι*, represents the return as an ascent and leaves *vivo* unexpressed. *Εἰ τἀληθὲς ἀκούω* retains the qualification *s’i’ odo il vero*: *τἀληθές* is crasis for *τὸ ἀληθές*, a substantivized neuter adjective, ‘the truth’.
- *Ἄνευ δέους αἰσχύνης* keeps the nested construction of *senza tema d’infamia*: *ἄνευ* governs *δέους* (gen. sg. of *δέος*), and *αἰσχύνης* specifies what is feared, ‘shame’ or ‘disgrace’. *Φράσω σοι πάντα*, ‘I shall tell you everything’, substitutes the future active of *φράζω* for present *rispondo* and adds the neuter plural object *πάντα*; *σοι* retains the addressee in the dative.

> 67 Io fui uom d' arme, e poi fui cordelliero,  
> 68 credendomi, sì cinto, fare ammenda ;  

I was a man of arms; and then became a Cordelier, hoping, thus girt, to make amends;

> 67 „ Μαχητὴς ἤμην, ἀλλ᾽ ἔπειτα σχοινίτης,  
> („ I was a warrior, and afterward a Cordelier,)
>
> 68 „ Νομίσας οὕτω ζωσθεὶς δίκην ἐκτίσειν.  
> („ Thinking, thus girded, to make amends.)

- *Μαχητὴς ἤμην, ἀλλ᾽ ἔπειτα σχοινίτης* balances two predicate nominatives, ‘a fighter’ and ‘a cord-wearer’. *Ἤμην* is the later Greek 1st sg. imperfect of *εἰμί* (Classical Attic *ἦ*); it serves both predicates, whereas Italian repeats *fui*. *Ἀλλ᾽ ἔπειτα* makes *e poi* adversative, ‘but afterwards’; *σχοινίτης*, formed on *σχοῖνος* ‘cord’, preserves the visible derivation of *cordelliero*.
- *Νομίσας* (nom. sg. masc. aor. act. participle of *νομίζω*) gives the belief as a whole rather than as the ongoing *credendomi*. *Οὕτω ζωσθείς*, ‘thus girded’, keeps *sì cinto* as a participle predicated of the same speaker (*ζωσθείς*, aor. pass. of *ζώννυμι*). *Δίκην ἐκτίσειν* ‘to pay the penalty’ interprets *fare ammenda* in terms of payment; *ἐκτίσειν*, fut. act. infinitive of *ἐκτίνω*, marks what the speaker thought he would accomplish.

> 69 e certo il creder mio veniva intero,  
> 70 se non fosse il gran prete, a cui mal prenda,  
> 71 che mi rimise nelle prime colpe ;  

and certainly my hope were come in full, but for the Great Priest, may ill befall him! who brought me back to my first sins;

> 69 „ Ἡ δ᾽ ἐλπὶς ἡ ’μὴ πάντως ἂν ἐπληροῦτο,  
> („ And my hope would assuredly have been fulfilled,)
>
> 70 „ Εἰ μή μ᾽ ἔῤῥιπτε πάλιν ἱερεὺς Μέγας  
> („ Had not the High Priest cast me back again)
>
> 71 „ (Ὃς κακῶς ὄλοιτ᾽) εἰς πταίσματα τὰ πρῶτα.  
> („ (May he perish miserably!) into my former sins.)

- *Ἡ δ᾽ ἐλπὶς ἡ ’μή* makes *il creder mio* ‘my hope’, as in the English. The repeated article puts the possessive in second attributive position; *’μή* is *ἐμή* with prodelision of the initial vowel. *Πάντως* renders *certo*; *ἂν ἐπληροῦτο* (3rd sg. imperf. pass. ind. of *πληρόω*) recasts *veniva intero* as the unreal consequence ‘would have been fulfilled’.
- *Εἰ μή μ᾽ ἔῤῥιπτε πάλιν ἱερεὺς Μέγας* combines *se non fosse il gran prete* and the relative *che mi rimise* in a single condition, ‘if a High Priest had not cast me back’. *Ἔῤῥιπτε* is 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ῥίπτω*, with the initial rho doubled after the augment and the older spelling *ῤῥ*. The indicative pairs with *ἄν* in the preceding clause. Elided *με* is its object; *πάλιν* brings out the backward movement of *rimise*.
- *Ὃς κακῶς ὄλοιτ᾽* interrupts the verb and its complement: ‘who, may he perish miserably’. The relative is nominative subject, unlike the dative-like *a cui*; *ὄλοιτ᾽* is elided *ὄλοιτο*, 3rd sg. second aor. mid. optative of *ὄλλυμι*. The independent wish takes no *ἄν*. *Εἰς πταίσματα τὰ πρῶτα* gives the goal of the casting, ‘into faults, the former ones’; the article attaches to the postposed adjective.

> 72 e come e quare voglio che m' intenda.  

and how and why, I wish thee to hear from me.

> 72 „ Πῶς δὲ καὶ διὰ τί, νῦν μαθεῖν σε θέλω.  
> („ And how and why, I wish you now to learn.)

- *Πῶς δὲ καὶ διὰ τί* keeps the two fronted interrogatives of *e come e quare*: *διὰ τί* uses *διά* with the accusative, ‘on account of what?’ Postpositive *δέ* furnishes the connective. *Νῦν* ‘now’ is added.
- *Μαθεῖν σε θέλω* replaces *voglio che m’intenda* with an accusative and infinitive after ‘I wish’: *σε* is the subject of *μαθεῖν* (second aor. act. infinitive of *μανθάνω*), not its object. ‘I wish you to learn’ leaves the source *m’* implicit and makes the ‘how and why’ the matter to be learned.

> 73 Mentre ch' io forma fui d' ossa e di polpe,  
> 74 che la madre mi diè, l' opere mie  
> 75 non furon leonine, ma di volpe.  

Whilst I was the form of bones and pulp, which my mother gave me, my deeds were not those of the lion, but of the fox.

> 73 „ Ἐν ὅσῳ σχῆμ᾽ ὀστέων καὶ σαρκῶν εἶχον,  
> („ While I had the form of bones and flesh,)
>
> 74 „ Ἃ μήτηρ ἔδωκέ μοι, πᾶσα μου πρᾶξις  
> („ Which my mother gave me, every deed of mine)
>
> 75 „ Οὐκ ἦν λέοντος, ἀλλ᾽ ἀλώπεκος ἔργα.  
> („ Was not of the lion, but the works of the fox.)

- *Ἐν ὅσῳ* ‘during the time that’ introduces the temporal clause of *Mentre ch’io*. *Σχῆμ᾽ … εἶχον* ‘I had a form’ changes ‘was’ to possession: *σχῆμ᾽* is elided *σχῆμα*, acc. sg. neut., and *εἶχον* is here 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἔχω*. *Ὀστέων καὶ σαρκῶν* are genitives of material, ‘of bones and flesh’; *ὀστέων* is the uncontracted gen. pl. of *ὀστέον* (contracted *ὀστῶν*).
- *Ἃ μήτηρ ἔδωκέ μοι* takes up the bodily constituents collectively with the neuter plural relative *ἅ*, not the singular *σχῆμα*. Its accusative comes from its role as object of *ἔδωκε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *δίδωμι*); enclitic *μοι* is the recipient and accounts for the additional accent in *ἔδωκέ*. Anarthrous *μήτηρ* has no expressed possessive.
- *Πᾶσα μου πρᾶξις* distributes plural *l’opere mie* as ‘every action of mine’, hence singular *ἦν*. Instead of the adjective *leonine* followed by *di volpe*, Greek balances possessive genitives, *λέοντος* and *ἀλώπεκος*. But *οὐκ ἦν λέοντος, ἀλλ᾽ ἀλώπεκος ἔργα* shifts number: the postponed predicate noun *ἔργα* ‘works’ is plural although the subject *πρᾶξις* is singular. The result is less regular than the Italian plural throughout.

> 76 Gli accorgimenti e le coperte vie  
> 77 io seppi tutte, e sì menai lor arte,  
> 78 ch' al fine della terra il suono uscie.  

All wiles and covert ways I knew; and used the art of them so well, that to the ends of the earth the sound went forth.

> 76 „ Τὰς ἐπινοίας καὶ κρυπτὰς διεξόδους  
> („ The devices and secret ways)
>
> 77 „ ᾜδειν ἁπάσας· οὕτω δ᾽ ἐμηχανώμην,  
> („ I knew, all of them; and so cunningly did I contrive,)
>
> 78 „ Ὥστ᾽ ἐφημίσθην μέχρι τῶν γῆς περάτων.  
> („ That my fame went forth to the ends of the earth.)

- *Τὰς ἐπινοίας καὶ κρυπτὰς διεξόδους* fronts the objects as in Italian: ‘devices and hidden passages’. *Διεξόδους* is acc. pl. of the feminine second-declension noun *διέξοδος*. Delayed *ἁπάσας* ‘all without exception’ agrees in the feminine accusative plural with both objects, reproducing the delayed *tutte* without the Italian resumptive *le*.
- *ᾜδειν*, 1st sg. pluperfect of *οἶδα*, has the past-state sense ‘I knew’. *Οὕτω δ᾽ ἐμηχανώμην* ‘and I contrived in such a way’ condenses *sì menai lor arte*: *ἐμηχανώμην* is 1st sg. imperf. mid. ind. of *μηχανάομαι*, presenting habitual contrivance without an expressed noun for *arte* or pronoun for *lor*.
- *Οὕτω … ὥστ᾽* answers *sì … ch’*. Elided *ὥστε* takes a finite indicative for the actual result: *ἐφημίσθην* (1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *φημίζω*) ‘I became renowned’ replaces *il suono uscie*, making the speaker rather than the sound the subject. *Μέχρι τῶν γῆς περάτων* ‘as far as the bounds of earth’ uses *μέχρι* with the genitive plural of *πέρας* and embeds dependent *γῆς* between article and noun; the Italian limit is singular.

> 79 Quando mi vidi giunto in quella parte  
> 80 di mia etade, ove ciascun dovrebbe  
> 81 calar le vele e raccoglier le sarte,  

When I saw myself come to that period of my age at which every one should lower sails and gather in his ropes,

> 79 „ Ὁπότε δ᾽ ὅμως εἰς καιρὸν ἀφικόμην  
> („ But when, however, I came to that season)
>
> 80 „ Τῆς ἡλικίας, ἔνθ᾽ ἕκαστος ὀφείλει  
> („ Of life, where everyone ought)
>
> 81 „ Ἱστία χαλᾷν καὶ περιστέλλειν κάλως,  
> („ To lower the sails and gather in the ropes,)

- *Ὁπότε δ᾽ ὅμως … ἀφικόμην* gives ‘but when, however, I arrived’: connective *δέ* and concessive *ὅμως* add a turn not separately marked in *Quando*. *Ἀφικόμην* is 1st sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *ἀφικνέομαι*; the arrival itself becomes the finite verb, eliminating the perception and reflexive of *mi vidi giunto*.
- *Εἰς καιρὸν … τῆς ἡλικίας* turns *in quella parte di mia etade* into ‘at a season of life’, with no demonstrative or expressed possessor. *Ἔνθ᾽* is elided *ἔνθα*, a local relative adverb used of that time, closely following *ove*. *Ἕκαστος ὀφείλει* ‘each is bound’ substitutes a present indicative for conditional *ciascun dovrebbe*.
- *Ἱστία χαλᾶν καὶ περιστέλλειν κάλως* has two present active infinitives dependent on *ὀφείλει*, the first contracted from *χαλάω*, the second from *περιστέλλω* ‘gather in’. The objects frame the verbs: *ἱστία* is neut. acc. pl. of *ἱστίον*, and *κάλως* is acc. pl. of *κάλως* ‘rope’ (Attic declension), not the adverb *καλῶς* ‘well’.

> 82 ciò che pria mi piaceva allor m' increbbe,  

that which before had pleased me, grieved me then;

> 82 „ Ὃ πρίν μ᾽ ἤρεσκεν, ἀπεχθὲς τότ᾽ ἐδόκει.  
> („ What pleased me before, seemed hateful to me then.)

- *Ὃ πρίν μ᾽ ἤρεσκεν* renders *ciò che pria mi piaceva* by a free relative, ‘what formerly pleased me’, without a separate antecedent for *ciò*. *Ὅ* is neut. sg., subject within the relative clause; *πρίν* is adverbial. *Ἤρεσκεν* is 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἀρέσκω*. For the person pleased, elided accusative *με* follows Italian *mi* in sense but departs from the usual Classical Greek dative.
- *Ἀπεχθὲς τότ᾽ ἐδόκει* ‘then seemed hateful’ replaces *allor m’increbbe* with *ἐδόκει* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *δοκέω*) and predicate adjective *ἀπεχθές*, neut. sg. agreeing with the thing that formerly pleased. *Τότ᾽* is *τότε* elided; the person to whom it seemed hateful is not repeated.

> 83 e pentuto e confesso mi rendei ;  

and with repentance and confession I [became a monk];

> 83 „ Εἰς ἐπιστροφὴν καὶ μετάνοιαν ἦλθον·  
> („ To conversion and repentance I came;)

- *Εἰς ἐπιστροφὴν καὶ μετάνοιαν ἦλθον* ‘I came to conversion and repentance’ replaces the two participial descriptions *pentuto e confesso* with abstract nouns governed by one *εἰς*. *Ἦλθον*, here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*, makes the change a motion towards a state. *Μετάνοια* expresses repentance, but neither confession nor the specific interpretation “[became a monk]” of *mi rendei* is explicit in Greek.

> 84 ahi miser lasso ! e giovato sarebbe.  

ah woe alas! and it would have availed me.

> 84 „ Τοῦτ᾽ ἔμελλέ με σώσειν· ἀλλ᾽ οἴμοι τάλας !  
> („ That would have saved me; but alas, wretched me!)

- *Τοῦτ᾽ ἔμελλέ με σώσειν* places the consequence before the lament, reversing the Italian order. *Τοῦτ᾽* (elided *τοῦτο*) supplies the subject ‘this’; *ἔμελλε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *μέλλω*) with *σώσειν* (fut. act. infinitive of *σώζω*) presents an unrealized prospect, ‘this was to save me’, rather than reproducing the conditional perfect *giovato sarebbe*. Enclitic *με*, object of *σώσειν*, accounts for the extra accent on *ἔμελλέ*.
- *Ἀλλ᾽ οἴμοι τάλας* ‘but alas, wretched me’ makes the connection adversative. *Οἴμοι* is an exclamation of distress; masculine nominative *τάλας* describes the speaker in exclamation, answering *miser lasso* with a single adjective.

> 85 Lo principe de' nuovi Farisei,  
> 86 avendo guerra presso a Laterano,  
> 87 e non con Saracin, nè con Giudei,  

The Prince of the new Pharisees—waging war near to the Lateran, and not with Saracens or Jews;

> 85 „ Τῶν νεωτέρων Φαρισαίων ὁ Πρίγκιψ,  
> („ The Prince of the new Pharisees,)
>
> 86 „ Πόλεμον ποιῶν ἐγγὺς τοῦ Λατεράνου,  
> („ Waging war near the Lateran,)
>
> 87 „ Οὐ μὴν πρὸς Σαρακηνούς, οὔτ᾽ Ἰουδαίους,  
> („ And not against Saracens, nor against Jews,)

- *Τῶν νεωτέρων Φαρισαίων ὁ Πρίγκιψ* places the dependent genitive before the nominative title, reversing *Lo principe de’ nuovi Farisei*. *Νεωτέρων* is the comparative of *νέος*, ‘more recent’, for positive *nuovi*; *Πρίγκιψ* is a Greek rendering of the Latin title rather than a native noun such as *ἄρχων*. The nominative still awaits its main verb at the end of this extract.
- *Πόλεμον ποιῶν* ‘making war’ follows *avendo guerra* with a present participle but changes ‘having’ to ‘making’. *Ποιῶν*, contracted nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *ποιέω*, agrees with *ὁ Πρίγκιψ*. *Ἐγγὺς τοῦ Λατεράνου* uses *ἐγγύς* with the genitive for *presso a*.
- *Οὐ μὴν πρὸς Σαρακηνούς, οὔτ᾽ Ἰουδαίους* ‘not, however, against Saracens, nor Jews’ makes the hostile sense of *con* explicit with *πρός* and the accusative. *Μήν* is an adversative or emphatic particle, not a second negative; elided *οὔτε* continues the negation, and the one preposition serves both plural nouns. Greek makes *Saracin* plural to match *Ἰουδαίους*.

> 88 chè ciascun suo nimico era Cristiano,  
> 89 e nessuno era stato a vincer Acri,  
> 90 nè mercatante in terra di Soldano ;  

for every enemy of his was Christian, and none had been to conquer Acre, nor been a merchant in the Soldan's land—

> 88 „ (Χριστιανοὺς γὰρ εἶχ᾽ οὗτος ἐχθροὺς μόνον,  
> („ (For Christians only he had as enemies,)
>
> 89 „ Ὧν οὐδεὶς ἔδραμ᾽ ἀμυνούμενος Ἄκην,  
> („ None of whom had hastened to defend Acre,)
>
> 90 „ Ἀλλ᾽ οὔτ᾽ ἔμπορος ἐν τῇ γῇ τοῦ Σουλτάνου),  
> („ Nor yet a merchant in the Sultan's land),)

- *Χριστιανοὺς γὰρ εἶχ᾽ οὗτος ἐχθροὺς μόνον* ‘for it was only Christians that he had as enemies’ changes *ciascun suo nimico era Cristiano* from a singular copular statement to a plural object with a predicate accusative, *ἐχθρούς*. Postpositive *γάρ* renders explanatory *chè*. *Οὗτος* supplies the personal subject of elided *εἶχε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἔχω*); *μόνον* is adverbial ‘only’, not an adjective agreeing with *ἐχθρούς*.
- *Ὧν οὐδείς* ‘of whom no one’ replaces *e nessuno* with a relative connection: *ὧν* is a partitive genitive plural dependent on *οὐδείς*. *Ἔδραμ᾽* is elided *ἔδραμε*, 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *τρέχω*, ‘ran’ or ‘hastened’, for the less specific *era stato*. *Ἀμυνούμενος* is nom. sg. masc. future middle participle of *ἀμύνω*, expressing purpose after a verb of motion. With acc. sg. *Ἄκην* it gives ‘to defend Acre’: the purpose construction corresponds to *a vincer Acri*, but ‘defend’ is a different interpretation from “conquer”, not a Greek synonym for *vincer*.
- *Ἀλλ᾽ οὔτ᾽ ἔμπορος* continues ‘but nor yet a merchant’, combining adversative *ἀλλά* and negative *οὔτε*, both elided. *Ἔμπορος* is nominative singular, predicated of the same *οὐδείς* with a copula understood: Greek cannot carry *ἔδραμε* over as Italian can carry *era stato* over to *mercatante*. *Ἐν τῇ γῇ τοῦ Σουλτάνου* closely follows *in terra di Soldano*, with *ἐν* and the locative dative for the place and an articular genitive for its possessor.

## Lines 91–123 — Fraudulent Counsel and Failed Absolution

> 91 nè sommo ufficio, nè ordini sacri  
> 92 guardò in sè, nè in me quel capestro  
> 93 che solea far li suoi cinti più macri.  

regarded not the Highest Office nor Holy Orders in himself, nor in me that Cord which used to make those whom it girded leaner.

> 91 „ Οὐκ ἐσεβάσθη τὴν ὑψηλὴν ἀξίαν  
> („ Respected neither the high dignity)
>
> 92 „ Αὐτός, ἢ τάξεις σεπτάς, οὔτ᾽ ἐμὸν σχοῖνον,  
> („ Himself, nor the sacred orders, nor my cord,)
>
> 93 „ Ὃς εἴωθεν ἰσχναίνειν τοὺς ἐζωσμένους·  
> („ Which is wont to make lean those who are girded with it;)

- Musurus brings *οὐκ ἐσεβάσθη* ‘he did not revere’ ahead of the objects which Dante places before *guardò*. *Ἐσεβάσθη* is 3rd sg. aorist passive in form, active in sense, of *σέβομαι*. *Τὴν ὑψηλὴν ἀξίαν* ‘the high dignity’ renders *sommo ufficio* with a positive adjective, not a superlative; *τάξεις σεπτάς* renders *ordini sacri* with two accusative plurals, ‘orders worthy of reverence’.
- *Αὐτός* is intensive nominative, ‘he himself’, not a reflexive complement equivalent to *in sè*. Correspondingly, *in me quel capestro* becomes *ἐμὸν σχοῖνον* ‘my cord’, without the demonstrative. Italian *nè … nè … nè* is not reproduced symmetrically: Greek negates the verb, joins the second object with disjunctive *ἤ*, and adds the third with *οὔτ᾽* (elided *οὔτε*).
- *Ὃς* is masculine nominative singular, referring to accusative *σχοῖνον* but serving as subject in its own clause. *Εἴωθεν* (3rd sg. perfect active indicative of *ἔθω*) has the present force ‘is accustomed’, against past *solea*. The transitive present infinitive *ἰσχναίνειν* ‘to make lean’ absorbs *far … macri* but not the explicit comparative *più*. *Τοὺς ἐζωσμένους* is a substantivized perfect passive participle of *ζώννυμι*, ‘those who stand girded’; there is no separate equivalent of *suoi*.

> 94 Ma come Costantin chiese Silvestro  
> 95 dentro Siratti a guarir della lebbre,  

But as Constantine [sought] Silvestro [within] Soracte to cure his leprosy,

> 94 „ Ἀλλ’ ὡς Σίλβεστρον ᾔτησε Κωνσταντῖνος  
> („ But as Constantine asked Silvestro)
>
> 95 „ Ἐντὸς Σωράκτου θεραπεῦσαι τὴν λέπραν,  
> („ Within Soracte to cure his leprosy,)

- *Ἀλλ’ ὡς* (elided *ἀλλά*) opens the comparison answered by *οὕτως* in 96. For *Costantin chiese Silvestro* the order is reversed about the verb: *Σίλβεστρον ᾔτησε Κωνσταντῖνος*, object, verb, subject. *ᾜτησε* is the temporally augmented 3rd sg. aorist active indicative of *αἰτέω*, ‘asked’.
- *Ἐντὸς Σωράκτου* keeps the local force of *dentro Siratti*, with *ἐντός* governing a genitive. *Θεραπεῦσαι* is the aorist active infinitive of *θεραπεύω*, specifying what Silvestro was asked to do; *τὴν λέπραν* is its direct object. Thus Italian *a guarir della lebbre* becomes ‘to cure the leprosy’, without a prepositional complement or an expressed possessive.

> 96 così mi chiese questi per maestro  
> 97 a guarir della sua superba febbre;  

so this man called me as an adept to cure the fever of his pride;

> 96 „ Οὕτως ᾐτήσατό με παραγγελίας  
> („ So he asked of me instruction)
>
> 97 „ Πρὸς θεραπείαν τοῦ δοξομανοῦς τύφου.  
> („ To cure his glory-mad pride.)

- *Οὕτως ᾐτήσατό με παραγγελίας* ‘so he asked me for instructions’ repeats *αἰτέω*, but now in the 3rd sg. aorist middle, a request made on the subject’s own behalf. The final accent of *ᾐτήσατό* is added before enclitic *με*. Person (*με*) and thing requested (*παραγγελίας*, acc. pl.) are both accusative. Greek supplies no demonstrative for *questi* and puts the instruction itself in place of the role *per maestro*.
- The second *a guarir* is not another infinitive: *πρὸς θεραπείαν* is *πρός* with an accusative noun of purpose, ‘for the treatment’. *Τοῦ δοξομανοῦς τύφου* is the genitive of what is to be treated. *Δοξομανοῦς* (gen. sg. of *δοξομανής*, ‘mad for glory’) qualifies *τύφου* ‘conceit’: Greek makes pride the ailment rather than calling the fever *superba*, and leaves *sua* implicit.

> 98 domandommi consiglio, ed io tacetti,  
> 99 perché le sue parole parver ebbre.  

he demanded counsel of me; and I kept silent, for his words seemed drunken.

> 98 „ Ἐζήτει με συμβουλάς· ἀλλ’ ἐσιώπων,  
> („ He sought counsel of me; but I kept silent,)
>
> 99 „ Τῶν λόγων αὐτοῦ μεθύοντος δοκούντων.  
> („ Since his words seemed those of a drunken man.)

- *Ἐζήτει με συμβουλάς* follows *domandommi consiglio* in placing the person immediately after the verb, but *ἐζήτει* (3rd sg. imperfect active of *ζητέω*) presents an ongoing request, and *συμβουλάς* is plural. The double accusative continues the pattern of *ᾐτήσατό με παραγγελίας*; with *ζητέω* one would more ordinarily distinguish the person from whom something is sought by a preposition.
- *Ἀλλ’ ἐσιώπων* ‘but I remained silent’ makes *ed* adversative and replaces perfective *tacetti* with another imperfect (*ἐσιώπων*, here 1st sg. of *σιωπάω*). For finite causal *perché … parver*, Greek has a causal genitive absolute, *τῶν λόγων αὐτοῦ … δοκούντων*, with a present active participle of *δοκέω* agreeing with *λόγων*. Inside it *μεθύοντος* is genitive singular, a substantival participle of *μεθύω*: the words seemed ‘a drunk man’s’, rather than themselves being *ebbre*.

> 100 E poi mi disse: ‘Tuo cor non sospetti;  

And then he said to me: ‘Let not thy heart misdoubt;

> 100 „ Εἶτ’ εἶπεν· ‘“Ἔξω δισταγμοῦ παντὸς ἔσο·  
> („ Then he said: ‘“Be free from all doubt;)

- *Εἶτ’ εἶπεν* ‘then he said’ elides *εἶτα* before the vowel; *εἶπεν* is the 3rd sg. second aorist of *λέγω*, with movable *ν*. The recipient *mi* is omitted. *Ἔξω δισταγμοῦ παντὸς ἔσο* replaces the negative third-person injunction to *tuo cor* with a positive second-person one: ‘be outside all doubt’. *Ἔξω* governs the genitive; *ἔσο* is the 2nd sg. present imperative of *εἰμί*, the uncontracted form beside *ἔστω* (3rd sg.) and contracted *ἔσου* (2nd sg.).

> 101 finor t’ assolvo, e tu m’ insegna fare  
> 102 sì come Penestrino in terra getti.  

even now I do absolve thee, and do thou teach me so to act, that I may cast Penestrino to the ground.

> 101 ‚ „ Ἀπολύω σ’ ἀπὸ τοῦδ’, ἤν με διδάξῃς,  
> (‚ „ I absolve you henceforth, if you teach me)
>
> 102 ‚ „ Πῶς καταβαλεῖν δύναμαι Πραινεστῖνον.  
> (‚ „ How I can cast down Praenestino.)

- *Ἀπολύω σ’ ἀπὸ τοῦδ’* ‘I release you from this point’ renders *finor t’ assolvo* with a present active indicative and a temporal prepositional phrase. *Σ’* is elided *σε*; *τοῦδ’* is elided *τοῦδε*, gen. sg. of *ὅδε*. Greek turns *e tu m’ insegna*, an imperative coordinated with the declaration, into its condition: *ἤν με διδάξῃς* ‘if you teach me’. *Ἤν* is a contracted form of *ἐάν*; *διδάξῃς* is 2nd sg. aorist active subjunctive of *διδάσκω*.
- *Πῶς καταβαλεῖν δύναμαι Πραινεστῖνον* is an indirect question dependent on ‘teach’: ‘how I can throw down Praenestino’. *Δύναμαι* is 1st sg. present middle in form; its complement *καταβαλεῖν* is the second aorist active infinitive of *καταβάλλω*, not the future *καταβαλεῖν* in a prediction. The downward direction of *in terra getti* is in the prefix *κατα-*; neither *fare* nor ‘ground’ has a separate equivalent.

> 103 Lo ciel poss’ io serrare e disserrare,  
> 104 come tu sai: però son due le chiavi,  
> 105 che il mio antecessor non ebbe care.’  

Heaven I can shut and open, as thou knowest: for two are the keys that my predecessor held not dear.’

> 103 ‚ „ Οὐρανὸν ἔξεστί μοι κλείειν κἀνοίγειν,  
> (‚ „ Heaven it is in my power to lock and unlock,)
>
> 104 ‚ „ Ὡς οἶδας· ὅθεν αἱ κλεῖδές εἰσι δύο·  
> (‚ „ As you know; therefore the keys are two,)
>
> 105 ‚ „ Τοῦθ’ οὗπερ οὑμὸς προκάτοχος ἠμέλει.”’  
> (‚ „ Which my predecessor neglected.”’)

- *Οὐρανόν* keeps *lo ciel* emphatically first, as the shared object of *κλείειν κἀνοίγειν*. Italian personal *poss’ io* becomes impersonal *ἔξεστί μοι* ‘it is in my power’, with dative of the person and two present active infinitives. *Κἀνοίγειν* is crasis of *καὶ ἀνοίγειν*.
- *Ὡς οἶδας* follows *come tu sai*; *οἶδας* is a 2nd sg. perfect with present sense, a by-form of classical Attic *οἶσθα*. *Ὅθεν* gives *però* an inferential turn, ‘whence, therefore’. In *αἱ κλεῖδές εἰσι δύο*, ‘the keys are two’, the noun and verb are plural, not dual; *κλεῖδες* acquires a second accent before enclitic *εἰσι*.
- *Τοῦθ’ οὗπερ οὑμὸς προκάτοχος ἠμέλει* shifts from the plural keys as antecedent of *che* to a singular summation, ‘this very thing which my predecessor neglected’. *Τοῦθ’* is *τοῦτο* elided with aspiration before rough-breathed *οὗπερ*; *οὗ* is genitive as required by *ἀμελέω*, with intensive *-περ*. *Οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*. *Ἠμέλει* (3rd sg. imperfect active) expresses positively and duratively what *non ebbe care* expresses by negation.

> 106 Allor mi pinser gli argomenti gravi  
> 107 là ‘ve il tacer mi fu avviso il peggio,  

Then the weighty arguments impelled me to think silence worst;

> 106 „ Οὕτω σοβαροὶ λόγοι τότ’ ἔπεισάν με,  
> („ Then such weighty words persuaded me,)
>
> 107 „ Ὡς ἦν μοι χεῖρον τὸ σιωπᾷν τοῦ λέγειν·  
> („ That silence was for me worse than speech;)

- *Οὕτω σοβαροὶ λόγοι τότ’ ἔπεισάν με* ‘words so weighty then persuaded me’ retains *allor* as elided *τότ’* (*τότε*) and adds degree adverb *οὕτω*. *Λόγοι* renders *argomenti*; *ἔπεισαν* (3rd pl. aorist active of *πείθω*) replaces the spatial figure *mi pinser … là* with persuasion, and takes the additional accent before *με*.
- In place of relative *là ‘ve … mi fu avviso* comes a content clause with *ὡς*: *ἦν μοι χεῖρον τὸ σιωπᾷν τοῦ λέγειν*, ‘being silent was worse for me than speaking’. *Ἦν* is imperfect of *εἰμί*; *μοι* marks the person concerned. The articular present infinitive *τὸ σιωπᾷν* is the subject, neuter comparative *χεῖρον* its predicate. The genitive of comparison *τοῦ λέγειν* supplies a second articular infinitive where *il peggio* leaves the alternative unstated.

> 108 e dissi: ‘Padre, da che tu mi lavi  
> 109 di quel peccato, ov’ io mo cader deggio,  

and I said: ‘Father! since thou cleansest me from that guilt into which I now must fall,

> 108 „ Ὥστ’ εἶπον· ‘“Πάτερ, ἐπεὶ σύ γε μ’ ἐκπλύνεις  
> („ So I said: ‘“Father, since you wash me clean)
>
> 109 ‚ „ Ἧς ἔσομαι νῦν ἁμαρτίας ἐργάτης,  
> (‚ „ Of the sin of which I am now about to be the doer,)

- *Ὥστ’ εἶπον* (elided *ὥστε* plus 1st sg. aorist indicative) makes the speech an actual consequence, ‘so that I said’, rather than simply *e dissi*. *Πάτερ* is the vocative of *πατήρ*; *ἐπεί* is causal like *da che*. In *σύ γε μ’ ἐκπλύνεις* the expressed pronoun and restrictive-emphatic *γε* single out ‘you at any rate’; *μ’* is elided *με*, object of present active *ἐκπλύνεις* ‘wash clean’.
- *Ἧς ἔσομαι νῦν ἁμαρτίας ἐργάτης* draws the antecedent ‘sin’ inside the relative clause: literally ‘of which sin I shall now be the doer’. The genitive suits both the sin washed away and the complement of predicate *ἐργάτης*. *Ἔσομαι* is 1st sg. future of *εἰμί*. Greek keeps *mo* in *νῦν* but substitutes future agency for the local and necessitative *ov’ io … cader deggio*.

> 110 lunga promessa con l’ attender corto  
> 111 ti farà trionfar nell’ alto seggio.’  

large promise, with small observance of it, will make thee triumph in thy High Seat.’

> 110 ‚ „ Ὑπισχνούμενος πολλά, τελῶν δ’ ὀλίγα,  
> (‚ „ Promising much, and fulfilling little,)
>
> 111 ‚ „ Θριαμβεύσαις ἂν ἐπὶ τοῦ σεπτοῦ θρόνου.”’  
> (‚ „ May you triumph upon the sacred throne.”’)

- *Ὑπισχνούμενος πολλά, τελῶν δ’ ὀλίγα* ‘promising much but accomplishing little’ replaces *lunga promessa con l’ attender corto* with two circumstantial participles agreeing with the addressee: present middle of *ὑπισχνέομαι* and present active of *τελέω*, both nom. masc. sg. The contrast is quantitative, with substantival neuter plurals *πολλά* and *ὀλίγα* as objects; elided *δ’* (*δέ*) sets the second against the first.
- *Θριαμβεύσαις ἄν* is 2nd sg. aorist active optative of *θριαμβεύω* with potential *ἄν*: ‘you could triumph’, not a simple wish. The addressee becomes subject, and the potential replaces the confident causative future *ti farà trionfar*. *Ἐπὶ τοῦ σεπτοῦ θρόνου* uses *ἐπί* with a genitive of position; *σεπτοῦ* ‘revered’ interprets *alto* as dignity rather than height.

> 112 Francesco venne poi, com’ io fui morto,  
> 113 per me, ma un de’ neri cherubini  
> 114 gli disse: ‘Nol portar, non mi far torto.  

Saint Francis afterwards, when I was dead, came for me; but one of the Black Cherubim said to him: ‘Do not take him; wrong me not.

> 112 “Ὅτ’ ἐγὼ δ’ ἀπέθανον, ἦλθε Φραγκίσκος  
> (“And when I died, Francis came)
>
> 113 „ Ζητήσων μ᾽, ἀλλ᾽ εἷς μελάνων Χερουβίνων  
> („ To claim me, but one of the black Cherubim)
>
> 114 „ Εἶπ᾽ αὐτῷ· ‘“Μὴ τόνδ᾽ ἔκφερε, μή μ᾽ ἀδίκει·  
> („ Said to him: ‘“Do not carry him off, do not wrong me;)

- Greek fronts the temporal clause: *ὅτ’ ἐγὼ δ’ ἀπέθανον* ‘and when I died’. *Ὅτ’* is elided *ὅτε*; postpositive *δ’* connects the new sentence. *Ἀπέθανον* is 1st sg. second aorist active of *ἀποθνῄσκω*, with augment inside the compound. *Ἦλθε Φραγκίσκος* postpones the subject of ‘came’ and does not separately translate *poi*.
- For *per me*, *ζητήσων μ᾽* ‘in order to seek me’ supplies a purpose verbalized in a future active participle, nom. masc. sg. of *ζητέω*, agreeing with *Φραγκίσκος* after a verb of motion. *Ἀλλ᾽ εἷς μελάνων Χερουβίνων* closely follows *ma un de’ neri cherubini*: *εἷς* is substantive, ‘one’, with a partitive genitive plural.
- *Εἶπ᾽ αὐτῷ* has elided *εἶπε* and a dative recipient for *gli disse*. *Μὴ τόνδ᾽ ἔκφερε, μή μ᾽ ἀδίκει* uses *μή* with two 2nd sg. present active imperatives, of *ἐκφέρω* and *ἀδικέω*. Elided *τόνδε* ‘this man here’ is more pointed than the object pronoun in *nol*; the compound verb supplies ‘off’. The single verb *ἀδίκει* replaces *far torto*, with *με* as direct object instead of the person to whom wrong is done.

> 115 Venir sen dee laggiù tra’ miei meschini,  
> 116 perchè diede il consiglio frodolente,  
> 117 dal quale in qua stato gli sono a’ crini:  

He must come down amongst my menials; because he gave the fraudulent counsel, since which I have kept fast by his hair:

> 115 ‚ „ Κατελθεῖν εἰς τὸν ἐμὸν χῶρον ὀφείλει·  
> (‚ „ He must come down into my place;)
>
> 116 ‚ „ Ἔδωκε γὰρ συμβουλὴν δολιοφρόνως·  
> (‚ „ For he gave counsel craftily;)
>
> 117 ‚ „ Ἔκτοτε δ᾽ αὐτὸν ἐγὼ κρατῶ τῆς κόμης·  
> (‚ „ And ever since then I have held him by the hair;)

- *Κατελθεῖν … ὀφείλει* retains the framing infinitive and verb of obligation of *venir sen dee*, but the second aorist infinitive of *κατέρχομαι* itself expresses descent, without a separate *laggiù*. *Εἰς τὸν ἐμὸν χῶρον* ‘into my place’ has a goal in the accusative in place of the people in *tra’ miei meschini*.
- Postpositive *γάρ* makes *ἔδωκε γὰρ συμβουλήν* an explanatory main clause rather than a subordinate *perchè*-clause. *Ἔδωκε* is 3rd sg. aorist active of *δίδωμι*. Adverb *δολιοφρόνως* ‘with deceitful intent’ qualifies the giving, where *frodolente* qualifies *consiglio*.
- *Ἔκτοτε δ᾽* ‘and from then on’ replaces relative *dal quale in qua*. In *αὐτὸν ἐγὼ κρατῶ τῆς κόμης*, *κρατῶ* (1st sg. present active of *κρατέω*) denotes a hold still maintained. Greek makes the person held a direct object, the speaker an explicit subject, and the part grasped a genitive, *τῆς κόμης* ‘by the hair’ (collective singular), rather than reproducing *stato gli sono a’ crini*.

> 118 ch’ assolver non si può, chi non si pente,  
> 119 nè pentere e volere insieme puossi,  
> 120 per la contraddizion che nol consente.’  

for he who repents not, cannot be absolved; nor is it possible to repent and will a *thing* at the same time, the contradiction not permitting it.’

> 118 ‚ „ Οὐκ ἔστ᾽ ἄφεσις τῷ μὴ μετανοοῦντι·  
> (‚ „ There is no absolution for one who does not repent;)
>
> 119 ‚ „ Οὐ συνυπάρχει μετανοεῖν καὶ θέλειν·  
> (‚ „ Repenting and willing cannot coexist;)
>
> 120 ‚ „ Ἀντίφασις γὰρ οὐκ ἐπιτρέπει τοῦτο.”’  
> (‚ „ For contradiction does not permit this.”’)

- *Οὐκ ἔστ᾽ ἄφεσις* ‘there is no absolution’ gives an independent existential statement, leaving the explanatory *ch’* unexpressed. *Ἔστ᾽* is elided existential *ἔστι*. The noun *ἄφεσις* replaces *assolver*, and *τῷ μὴ μετανοοῦντι* replaces *chi non si pente* with a dative of the person for whom there is no absolution. *Μετανοοῦντι* is the contracted dat. masc. sg. present active participle of *μετανοέω*, substantivized by the article and generically negated with *μή*.
- *Οὐ συνυπάρχει μετανοεῖν καὶ θέλειν* keeps the two coordinated present infinitives of *pentere e volere*, without an expressed object of willing. *Συνυπάρχει* ‘coexists’ absorbs *insieme* in the prefix *συν-*; the singular verb presents the combination of the two acts as impossible, rather than giving each a separate subject.
- For *per la contraddizion che nol consente*, Greek promotes *ἀντίφασις* to subject of an independent explanatory sentence, marked by postpositive *γάρ*. *Οὐκ ἐπιτρέπει τοῦτο* ‘does not permit this’ keeps the negation and gives the rejected combination an explicit demonstrative, acc. neut. sg., for Italian *lo*.

> 121 O me dolente! come mi riscossi,  
> 122 quando mi prese, dicendomi: ‘Forse  
> 123 tu non pensavi ch’io loico fossi.’  

O wretched me! how I started when he seized me, saying to me: ‘May be thou didst not think that I was a logician!’

> 121 “Οἴμ᾽ ἐγὼ τάλας! οἷον ᾐσθόμην τρόμον,  
> (“Ah, wretched me! what trembling I felt,)
>
> 122 „ Ὅτε συνέλαβέ με λέγων· ‘“Τάχ᾽ ἴσως  
> („ When he seized me, saying: ‘“Perhaps)
>
> 123 ‚ „ Οὔ μ᾽ ἐνόμιζες λογικευτικὸν εἶναι.”’—  
> (‚ „ You did not think me to be a logician.”’—)

- *Οἴμ᾽ ἐγὼ τάλας* is an exclamation, with *οἴμ᾽* from *οἴμοι* before a vowel and *τάλας* agreeing with nominative *ἐγώ*, against *O me dolente*. *Οἷον ᾐσθόμην τρόμον* ‘what trembling I felt’ expands *come mi riscossi* into an exclamatory adjective and its separated noun, both acc. masc. sg., around *ᾐσθόμην*, 1st sg. second aorist middle indicative of *αἰσθάνομαι*.
- *Ὅτε συνέλαβέ με* retains the temporal conjunction and the completed seizure: *συνέλαβε* is 3rd sg. second aorist active of *συλλαμβάνω*, with internal augment and an additional accent before enclitic *με*. *Λέγων* is a simultaneous present active participle, nom. masc. sg., agreeing with the captor; unlike *dicendomi*, it leaves the hearer unexpressed.
- *Τάχ᾽ ἴσως* combines two adverbs of possibility for the single *forse* (*τάχ᾽* is elided *τάχα*). *Οὔ μ᾽ ἐνόμιζες* retains the imperfect of *non pensavi* (2nd sg. active of *νομίζω*); *οὔ* is accented before the enclitic. In place of *ch’io loico fossi*, *μ᾽ … λογικευτικὸν εἶναι* is accusative and infinitive: *με* is subject of the present infinitive *εἶναι*, and *λογικευτικόν* its predicate adjective, ‘skilled in reasoning’, agreeing with *με*. The infinitive expresses a state simultaneous with the thinking; Greek does not reproduce the Italian finite subjunctive.

## Lines 124–136 — Minos’s Sentence and the Departing Flame

> 124 A Minos mi portò; e quegli attorse  
> 125 otto volte la coda al dosso duro,  
> 126 e, poi che per gran rabbia la si morse,  
> 127 disse: ‘Questi è de’ rei del fuoco furo’;  

To Minos he bore me, who twined his tail eight times round his fearful back, and then biting it in great rage, said : 'This is a sinner for the thievish fire'

> 124 “Ἀπήγαγέ μ᾽ ὡς Μίνων· ὁ δὲ συστρέψας  
> (“He led me away to Minos; and he, coiling)
>
> 125 „ Τὴν οὐρὰν αὑτοῦ περὶ νεφροὺς ὀκτάκις,  
> („ His tail eight times around his loins,)
>
> 126 „ Ἔδακεν αὐτὴν μετὰ μεγάλης λύσσης  
> („ Bit it with great fury,)
>
> 127 „ Εἰπών· ‘“Ἐκ τῶν κρυπτικοῦ πυρός ἐσθ᾽ οὗτος.’”  
> („ Saying: ‘“This one is of the concealing fire.’”)

- *Ἀπήγαγέ μ᾽ ὡς Μίνων* ‘he led me away to Minos’ puts the verb first, where *A Minos mi portò* puts the destination first. *Ἀπήγαγέ* is the 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἀπάγω*, with reduplicated aorist stem and augment after the preverb; its additional final acute is due to the enclitic *μ᾽* (elided *με*, acc. sg.). *ὡς* with the accusative of a person expresses motion to that person; *Μίνων* is the Attic acc. sg. of *Μίνως*.
- *ὁ δὲ συστρέψας* ‘and he, having coiled’ takes up *e quegli* with pronominal *ὁ* and postpositive *δέ*. Musurus redistributes the actions among different verb forms: finite *attorse* becomes the aor. act. participle *συστρέψας* (nom. sg. masc., from *συστρέφω*); the subordinate *poi che … la si morse* supplies the Greek main verb *ἔδακεν*; and finite *disse* becomes another participle, *εἰπών*.
- *τὴν οὐρὰν αὑτοῦ περὶ νεφροὺς ὀκτάκις* ‘his own tail round his loins eight times’ puts the object of *συστρέψας* before the place and count, whereas *otto volte* precedes *la coda*. *αὑτοῦ* is the contracted reflexive gen. sg. of *ἑαυτοῦ*, referring possession back to the subject. *περί* with acc. pl. *νεφρούς* (from *νεφρός*, ‘kidney’, hence in the plural ‘loins’) makes the encircling motion explicit; it replaces *al dosso*, without an equivalent for *duro*. *ὀκτάκις* is a multiplicative adverb.
- *ἔδακεν αὐτὴν μετὰ μεγάλης λύσσης* ‘he bit it with great fury’: *ἔδακεν* is the augmented 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *δάκνω*, with movable *ν*. *αὐτήν* (acc. sg. fem.) resumes *οὐράν*, rendering *la*; there is no separate reflexive corresponding to *si*. *μετά* with the genitive of attendant circumstance gives the manner of biting rather than the cause expressed by *per gran rabbia*.
- *εἰπών* ‘saying’ is the nom. sg. masc. second aor. act. participle of *λέγω*, agreeing, like *συστρέψας*, with the subject of *ἔδακεν*. Within the speech, *ἐκ τῶν κρυπτικοῦ πυρός ἐσθ᾽ οὗτος* moves the partitive expression to the front and the demonstrative subject *οὗτος* to the end, reversing *Questi è de’ rei*. *ἐκ τῶν* gives ‘of the number of those’, with no overt noun for *rei*; gen. sg. *κρυπτικοῦ πυρός* characterizes that group. *κρυπτικοῦ* ‘concealing’ interprets *furo* through the fire’s capacity to hide, rather than reproducing “thievish.” *ἐσθ᾽* is *ἐστί* (3rd sg. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*) with elision of *ι* and aspiration of *τ* before rough-breathed *οὗτος*.

> 128 per ch’io là dove vedi son perduto,  
> 129 e sì vestito andando mi rancuro.”  

; therefore I, where thou seest, am lost ; and going thus clothed, in heart I grieve."

> 128 „ Καὶ δὴ νῦν ἐνταῦθ᾽ ὄλλυμαι, καθὰ βλέπεις,  
> („ And so now here I perish, as you see,)
>
> 129 „ Καὶ πάσχω βαίνων ἐν ἐνδύματι τῷδε.”  
> („ And suffer as I walk in this garment.”)

- *καὶ δὴ νῦν ἐνταῦθ᾽ ὄλλυμαι* ‘and so now here I perish’ replaces connective *per ch’* with continuative *καί* and emphatic *δή*, and supplies temporal *νῦν*. *ἐνταῦθ᾽* is locative *ἐνταῦθα* with final *α* elided; the *θ* already belongs to the word. *ὄλλυμαι* (1st sg. pres. mid. ind. of *ὄλλυμι*, intransitive in the middle) presents perdition as ongoing rather than as the state *son perduto*; the verb ending suffices for *io*.
- *καθὰ βλέπεις* ‘as you see’ follows the main verb. The Greek thus separates the place, *ἐνταῦθα*, from a parenthetic comparison introduced by *καθά*, instead of retaining the local correlation *là dove vedi*. *βλέπεις* is the 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *βλέπω*.
- *καὶ πάσχω βαίνων ἐν ἐνδύματι τῷδε* ‘and I suffer, walking in this garment’ puts the finite *πάσχω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.) before the circumstances of suffering. It generalizes *mi rancuro* without a reflexive. *βαίνων* (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *βαίνω*) closely follows the simultaneous gerund *andando*. For predicative *sì vestito*, Musurus uses *ἐν* with the dat. sg. neut. of the noun *ἔνδυμα*, specifying the garment by the agreeing proximal demonstrative *τῷδε* (from *ὅδε*).

> 130 Quand’ egli ebbe il suo dir così compiuto,  
> 131 la fiamma dolorando si partio,  
> 132 torcendo e dibattendo il corno acuto.  

When he his words had ended thus, the flame, sorrowing, departed, writhing and tossing its sharp horn.

> 130 Περάνασα δ᾽ ἐκτενῶς τὴν ὁμιλίαν  
> (Having thus brought its speech to an end,)
>
> 131 Ἡ φλὸξ ἤρξατο πορείας τῆς προτέρας,  
> (The flame resumed its former course,)
>
> 132 Συστρεφομένη καὶ σείουσ᾽ ὀξὺ κέρας·  
> (Twisting itself and shaking its sharp horn;)

- *περάνασα δ᾽ ἐκτενῶς τὴν ὁμιλίαν* ‘and having finished the discourse at length’ replaces the finite temporal clause *Quand’ egli ebbe il suo dir così compiuto* with an anterior participle. *περάνασα* is the nom. sg. fem. aor. act. participle of *περαίνω*, agreeing in anticipation with *ἡ φλόξ*: Italian distinguishes the subject *egli* from the following *la fiamma*, but Greek gives both actions one grammatical subject. *δ᾽* is elided postpositive *δέ*. The noun *ὁμιλίαν* (acc. sg. of *ὁμιλία*) replaces the substantivized infinitive *il suo dir*, without the possessive; *ἐκτενῶς* ‘at length, fully’ gives a more specific manner than *così*.
- *ἡ φλὸξ ἤρξατο πορείας τῆς προτέρας* ‘the flame began its former journey’ interprets *si partio* as resumption rather than simple departure. *ἤρξατο* (3rd sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἄρχω*, with temporal augment) takes the genitive of what is begun, *πορείας*; the agreeing adjective *προτέρας* ‘former’ is marked as attributive by *τῆς*. Neither the former journey nor its resumption is explicit in the Italian, and *dolorando* receives no distinct equivalent.
- *συστρεφομένη καὶ σείουσ᾽ ὀξὺ κέρας* ‘twisting itself and shaking a sharp horn’ retains the simultaneity of *torcendo e dibattendo* through two nom. sg. fem. present participles agreeing with *φλόξ*. *συστρεφομένη* (pres. mid./pass. of *συστρέφω*, here middle in sense) makes the twisting an action of the flame upon itself; *σείουσ᾽* (elided *σείουσα*, pres. act. of *σείω*) takes *ὀξὺ κέρας* as its object. *ὀξύ*, acc. sg. neut. of *ὀξύς*, precedes the anarthrous *κέρας*, reversing *il corno acuto*.

> 133 Noi passammo oltre, ed io e il duca mio,  
> 134 su per lo scoglio infino in su l’altr’ arco  

We passed on, I and my Guide, along the cliff up to the other arch

> 133 Προέβημεν δ’ Ἡγήτωρ ἐγώ τε πρόσω  
> (And my Guide and I went onward)
>
> 134 Ἐπὶ τοῦ κρημνοῦ μέχρις ἁψῖδος ἄλλης,  
> (Upon the crag as far as another arch,)

- *προέβημεν δ’ Ἡγήτωρ ἐγώ τε πρόσω* ‘and we went forward, Guide and I, onward’ renders *Noi passammo oltre* with *προέβημεν* (1st pl. root aor. act. ind. of *προβαίνω*, augment after the preverb) and reinforces *προ-* with the directional adverb *πρόσω*. There is no separate *noi*. The two nominatives identifying the plural subject follow the verb, with *Ἡγήτωρ* ‘Guide’ now first and without *mio*. Postpositive enclitic *τε* attached to *ἐγώ* links the two, rather than repeating the conjunction as in *ed io e il duca mio*; *δ’* is the elided clause connective *δέ*.
- *ἐπὶ τοῦ κρημνοῦ* ‘upon the crag’ uses *ἐπί* with the gen. sg. of *κρημνός* for the surface along which they move, without separately reproducing the repeated *su*. *μέχρις ἁψῖδος ἄλλης* ‘as far as another arch’ gives the endpoint *infino in su l’altr’ arco* with *μέχρις* governing the genitive. *ἁψῖδος* is gen. sg. of *ἁψίς*, and the agreeing *ἄλλης* follows rather than precedes it; the phrase is indefinite rather than articular.

> 135 che copre il fosso, in che si paga il fio  
> 136 a quei che scommettendo acquistan carco.  

that covers the foss, in which their fee is paid to those who, sowing discord, gather guilt.

> 135 Τάφρον σκεπούσης, ἔνθα πάσχουσιν ὅσοι  
> (Covering the ditch, where those suffer who,)
>
> 136 Σπείροντες διχόνοιαν θερίζουσ’ ἄτην.  
> (Sowing discord, reap ruin.)

- *τάφρον σκεπούσης* ‘covering a ditch’ replaces finite relative *che copre il fosso* with *σκεπούσης* (gen. sg. fem. pres. act. participle of *σκεπάζω*’s contracted synonym *σκεπέω*), agreeing with *ἁψῖδος*. Its object *τάφρον* (acc. sg. of *τάφρος*) precedes it and has no article.
- *ἔνθα πάσχουσιν ὅσοι* ‘where those suffer who …’ gives *in che* by the relative adverb *ἔνθα*. Instead of *si paga il fio a quei*, Musurus makes the recipients the subject of *πάσχουσιν* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *πάσχω*), dropping both the payment construction and its noun. The following free relative, introduced by nom. pl. masc. *ὅσοι* ‘all who’, serves as subject without a separate antecedent for *quei*.
- *σπείροντες διχόνοιαν θερίζουσ’ ἄτην* ‘sowing discord, reap ruin’ develops *scommettendo acquistan carco* into a matched pair of agricultural verbs. *σπείροντες* (nom. pl. masc. pres. act. participle of *σπείρω*) agrees with *ὅσοι* and, like the Italian gerund, expresses the activity by which the result is incurred. *διχόνοιαν* (acc. sg. of *διχόνοια*) makes explicit as the thing sown what *scommettendo* conveys verbally. *θερίζουσ’* is poetic elision of *θερίζουσι*, 3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *θερίζω*; its object *ἄτην* (acc. sg. of *ἄτη*) makes the harvest ‘ruin’ rather than the burden or guilt of *carco*.

## Summary — From Clause to Participle

Musurus keeps many of Dante’s syntactic landmarks while changing which action carries the finite verb. The comparison opened by *come* and resumed by *così* retains its frame in *ὡς … οὕτως*; in *πρὸς ὃν ἀποτείνω τὴν φωνήν* ‘towards whom I direct my voice’, even the division between verb and object falls where it does in Italian. Within such frames, however, Greek participles often replace finite clauses. The relative *che muta parte* becomes *μερίδ᾽ ἀλλάσσων* ‘changing faction’, while *τῶν λόγων … δοκούντων* ‘since the words seemed’ gives a causal genitive absolute for *perché … parver*. Participial tense helps distinguish the relations: the aorist *περάνασα* ‘having finished’ marks an action completed before the main verb, whereas the future *ζητήσων* ‘in order to seek’ draws a purpose out of the bare phrase *per me*.

Particles and changes of mood sometimes alter the force of an utterance. Postpositive *δέ* often supplies a light connective; elsewhere *ἀλλά* draws a sharper contrast than Italian *e*, as in *ἀλλ᾽ ἐσιώπων* ‘but I kept silent’. More consequentially, *ἤν* with the subjunctive *διδάξῃς* ‘if you teach me’ turns the coordinated command *e tu m’insegna* into a condition on the preceding declaration. The shift in *θριαμβεύσαις ἄν* ‘you could triumph’ is different: the potential optative qualifies the assurance of *ti farà trionfar*. By contrast, where Dante makes an outright wish for the addressee’s renown, Musurus uses *εἴθε* with the optative and no *ἄν*.

The Greek often preserves a verbal pattern without preserving the words that make it. For *incresca … incresce*, Musurus repeats *μεῖνον … μένοντα* ‘stay … staying’: the echo survives, but what the addressee sees is the speaker staying, not his willingness to stay. Other choices explain an image or leave part of it behind. *Κρυπτικοῦ πυρός* ‘of concealing fire’ interprets *fuoco furo* by what the fire does, whereas *συνεχέοντο* ‘were being mingled’ conveys the indistinctness of the words without saying, as *in suo linguaggio* does, what they become. At the close Musurus goes in the opposite direction, giving *scommettendo acquistan carco* a more explicit figurative balance: *σπείροντες* ‘sowing’ finds its answer in *θερίζουσι* ‘they reap’, but the harvest is *ἄτη* ‘ruin’, not *carco* ‘burden’.
