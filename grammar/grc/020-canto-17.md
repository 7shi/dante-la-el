# Inferno 17

## Lines 1–21 — The Face and Body of Fraud

> 1 “Ecco la fiera con la coda aguzza,  
> 2 che passa i monti, e rompe muri ed armi;  
> 3 ecco colei che tutto il mondo appuzza.”  

“Behold the savage beast with the pointed tail, that passes mountains, and breaks through walls and weapons; behold him that pollutes the whole world.”

> 1 “Ἰδοὺ τὸ τέρας τὴν οὐρὰν ὀξυτόμον,  
> (“Behold the monster with the sharp-pointed tail,)
>
> 2 „ Τιτραῖνον ὄρη, συντρίβον ὅπλα, τείχη·  
> („ Piercing mountains, shattering arms, walls;)
>
> 3 „ Ἰδοὺ βδέλυγμα τὸ μολῦνον τὸ σύμπαν.”  
> („ Behold the abomination that defiles the whole world.”)

- The repeated *Ἰδοὺ … Ἰδοὺ* preserves *Ecco … ecco*. Originally a second-person singular aorist middle imperative of *ὁράω*, *ἰδού* functions here as a presentative particle, ‘look, here is’. The neuter *τὸ τέρας* ‘the monster’ replaces the feminine *la fiera*. In *τὴν οὐρὰν ὀξυτόμον*, the tail is the respect in which the monster is sharp: Greek uses the accusative of the body part rather than a phrase corresponding to *con*.
- *Τιτραῖνον* ‘piercing’ and *συντρίβον* ‘shattering’ are neuter singular present active participles of *τιτραίνω* and *συντρίβω*, agreeing with *τέρας*. They replace the two finite verbs of *che passa … e rompe*, without either a relative pronoun or a conjunction. *ὄρη* (accusative plural of *ὄρος*) is the object of the first; *ὅπλα, τείχη* are the objects of the second, reversing *muri ed armi*. ‘Piercing mountains’ is stronger than merely passing them.
- For *colei che*, Greek supplies a new neuter noun, *βδέλυγμα* ‘abomination’, and adds the articular participle *τὸ μολῦνον* ‘that which defiles’ (present active, neuter singular, from *μολύνω*). This renders *appuzza* by a verb of pollution rather than of smell. Its object *τὸ σύμπαν* is a substantivized neuter singular, ‘the whole’, for *tutto il mondo*.

> 4 Sì cominciò lo mio duca a parlarmi,  

Thus began my Guide to speak to me;

> 4 Οὕτως ἤρξατο λέγων οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ·  
> (Thus my Guide began to speak;)

- *Οὕτως ἤρξατο* closely follows *Sì cominciò*: *ἤρξατο* is third-person singular aorist middle indicative of *ἄρχω*, whose middle means ‘begin’. But *λέγων* ‘speaking’ is a supplementary present active participle of *λέγω*, nominative masculine singular with the subject, rather than an infinitive like *a parlarmi*. The recipient *-mi* has no Greek equivalent.
- *οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ* keeps *lo mio duca* after the verb. *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, ‘the guide of mine’; the rough breathing of the article is retained. *ἡγήτωρ* is a poetic agent noun, ‘leader’.

> 5 ed accennolle che venisse a proda,  
> 6 vicino al fin de’ passeggiati marmi;  

and beckoned him to come ashore, near the end of our rocky path;

> 5 Αὐτῷ δ’ ἔνευσεν ἐλθεῖν ἐγγὺς τῆς ἄκρας  
> (And he beckoned to it to come near the edge)
>
> 6 Μέχρι τέλους ὧν διήλθομεν μαρμάρων.  
> (To the end of the marble stones we had traversed.)

- *Αὐτῷ δ’ ἔνευσεν* ‘and to it he nodded’ brings the recipient to the front. *αὐτῷ* is dative singular, compatible with a neuter antecedent, where the enclitic of *accennolle* refers to the feminine *fiera*. Postpositive *δέ* loses its final vowel before *ἔνευσεν*, third-person singular aorist active indicative of *νεύω*.
- *ἐλθεῖν*, second aorist active infinitive of *ἔρχομαι*, gives the action signalled, with *αὐτῷ* identifying who is to perform it; it replaces *che venisse* with its finite imperfect subjunctive. *ἐγγὺς τῆς ἄκρας* ‘near the edge’ attaches the idea of *vicino* to the rendering of *a proda*, while *μέχρι τέλους* ‘as far as the end’ gives the limit.
- *ὧν διήλθομεν μαρμάρων* expands *de’ passeggiati marmi*: the passive participial expression becomes an active relative clause with an explicit first-person plural verb, *διήλθομεν* ‘we traversed’ (second aorist active indicative of *διέρχομαι*, with internal augment). *μαρμάρων*, genitive plural dependent on *τέλους*, is postponed; *ὧν* takes its genitive by attraction, rather than the accusative expected for the thing traversed.

> 7 e quella sozza imagine di froda  
> 8 sen venne, ed arrivò la testa e il busto;  

and that uncleanly image of Fraud came onward, and landed his head and bust,

> 7 Ἡ δὲ τοῦ Δόλου δυσειδὴς εἰκὼν αὕτη  
> (And this foul image of Fraud)
>
> 8 Ἦλθε κεφαλὴν προτείνουσα καὶ στῆθος,  
> (Came stretching forward its head and chest,)

- *Ἡ δὲ τοῦ Δόλου δυσειδὴς εἰκὼν αὕτη* puts the article first and the demonstrative last, a regular Greek framing of the noun phrase. *αὕτη* ‘this’ changes the distance of *quella*; *δυσειδής* ‘ill-looking’ renders *sozza* specifically as a quality of appearance. Both agree with feminine nominative singular *εἰκών*; the genitive *τοῦ Δόλου* ‘of deceit’ moves inside the phrase before the adjective.
- *Ἦλθε* (third-person singular second aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι*) gives simply ‘came’ for *sen venne*. The coordinate *ed arrivò* becomes *προτείνουσα* ‘extending forward’, a present active participle of *προτείνω* agreeing with *εἰκών*. Thus the Greek expresses an action accompanying the coming, not a second assertion that the parts reached shore. *κεφαλὴν … καὶ στῆθος*, its two accusative objects, have no articles, and *στῆθος* specifies the chest for *il busto*.

> 9 ma in su la riva non trasse la coda.  

but drew not his tail upon the bank.

> 9 Ἀλλ’ οὐ τὴν οὐρὰν ἔσυρ’ ἐπὶ τῆς ὄχθης.  
> (But did not draw its tail upon the bank.)

- *Ἀλλ’ οὐ τὴν οὐρὰν ἔσυρ’* puts the negative and then ‘the tail’ before the verb, whereas *ma in su la riva non trasse la coda* puts the place first. *Ἀλλ’* is elided *ἀλλά*, and *ἔσυρ’* represents *ἔσυρε*, an augmented third-person singular active past form of *σύρω* ‘drag’, with final *-ε* elided before *ἐπί*. The article suffices for the body part without a possessive.
- *ἐπὶ τῆς ὄχθης* gives the bank as the surface on which the tail is not drawn. The genitive is local, rather than the accusative one might expect for motion onto the bank.

> 10 La faccia sua era faccia d’uom giusto,  

His face was the face of a just man,

> 10 Εἶχε πρόσωπον οἷον ἀνδρὸς δικαίου·  
> (It had a face like that of a just man;)

- *Εἶχε πρόσωπον* ‘it had a face’ replaces *La faccia sua era faccia*: *εἶχε* is third-person singular imperfect active indicative of *ἔχω*, with the creature as subject and *πρόσωπον* as accusative object. There is consequently no need for either *sua* or the repeated noun.
- *οἷον* ‘such as’, neuter singular agreeing with *πρόσωπον*, turns the equation into a comparison: ‘a face such as [that] of a just man’. *ἀνδρὸς δικαίου* is a possessive genitive, with *ἀνδρός* from *ἀνήρ*.

> 11 tanto benigna avea di fuor la pelle;  
> 12 e d’un serpente tutto l’altro fusto.  

so mild an aspect had it outwardly; and the rest was all a reptile's body.

> 11 Οὕτως ἦν ἡδὺς κατὰ τὴν ἐκτὸς ὄψιν,  
> (So gentle was it in its outward aspect,)
>
> 12 Ἀλλ’ ἅπας δράκων κατὰ τὸ λοιπὸν σῶμα.  
> (But all serpent in the rest of its body.)

- *Οὕτως ἦν ἡδὺς* ‘so gentle was he’ shifts *tanto benigna avea … la pelle* from having to being, the converse of the change in line 10. *ἦν* is third-person singular imperfect of *εἰμί*; *ἡδύς* is masculine nominative singular, describing the creature personally rather than agreeing with neuter *πρόσωπον* or translating *benigna* as an adjective of the skin.
- *κατὰ τὴν ἐκτὸς ὄψιν* ‘as regards the outward appearance’ uses *κατά* with the accusative of respect. The adverb *ἐκτός* stands attributively between article and noun: *di fuor* is incorporated into a phrase about appearance, and *pelle* is not translated as skin.
- *Ἀλλ’ ἅπας δράκων* ‘but wholly a serpent’ sharpens *e* to an adversative and leaves *ἦν* understood. *ἅπας* and *δράκων* are masculine nominative singular; instead of making *tutto l’altro fusto* the body described as a serpent’s, Musurus predicates ‘serpent’ of the creature and limits that predicate by a second phrase of respect, *κατὰ τὸ λοιπὸν σῶμα* ‘as regards the rest of the body’.

> 13 Due branche avea pilose infin l’ascelle;  
> 14 lo dosso e il petto ed ambedue le coste  
> 15 dipinte avea di nodi e di rotelle:  

He had two paws, hairy to the armpits; the neck and the breast, and both the flanks, were painted with knots and circlets:

> 13 Τριχώδεις βραχίονας μασχαλῶν μέχρις  
> (Hairy arms as far as the armpits)
>
> 14 Εἶχε· ῥάχιν δέ, στῆθος καὶ πλευρὰς ἄμφω  
> (It had; and back, chest, and both sides)
>
> 15 Κατεστιγμένας πέριξ ὄζοις καὶ γύροις.  
> (Dappled round about with knots and circlets.)

- *Τριχώδεις βραχίονας* ‘hairy arms’ brings the quality before the limb, using the accusative plurals of *τριχώδης* and *βραχίων*. *βραχίονας* interprets *branche* as arms rather than paws; the plural does not expressly translate *Due*. *μασχαλῶν μέχρις* ‘as far as the armpits’ postpones *μέχρις* after the genitive plural it governs. The governing *Εἶχε* is delayed until the next line.
- *ῥάχιν δέ, στῆθος καὶ πλευρὰς ἄμφω* supplies a second set of accusatives with *εἶχε* understood. *ῥάχιν* ‘back’ follows *lo dosso*, not the prose translation’s “the neck”; *στῆθος* and *πλευράς* give chest and sides. *ἄμφω* ‘both’ is the nominative-accusative dual form, here accompanying the ordinary accusative plural *πλευράς* for *ambedue le coste*.
- *Κατεστιγμένας* ‘marked all over’ is the perfect middle-passive participle of *καταστίζω*, passive in sense and predicative to the objects: ‘he had [them] marked’. This closely follows *dipinte avea* rather than making a finite passive clause. Its feminine accusative plural ending agrees with the nearest noun, *πλευράς*, although the description extends over the list. *πέριξ* ‘round about’ is added; *ὄζοις καὶ γύροις* are dative plurals specifying the markings, for *di nodi e di rotelle*.

> 16 con più color, sommesse e soprapposte  
> 17 non fer mai drappo Tartari nè Turchi,  
> 18 nè fur tai tele per Aragne imposte.  

never did Tartars or Turks [make cloth] with more colours, [groundwork and] broidery; nor by Arachne were such webs laid on her loom.

> 16 Οὔ ποθ᾽ ὕφηναν ποικιλόχρους, πολύχρους  
> (Never wove variegated, many-coloured)
>
> 17 Οἱ Τάρταροι τάπητας, οὔτε μὴν Τοῦρκοι,  
> (Carpets the Tartars, nor yet the Turks,)
>
> 18 Ἀλλ᾽ οὔτ᾽ Ἀράχνη ποτ᾽ ἂν ἱστοὺς τοιούτους.  
> (Nor would Arachne ever have woven such webs.)

- *Οὔ ποθ᾽ ὕφηναν* ‘never did they weave’ moves the negative and verb ahead of the colour words. *ποθ᾽* is *ποτε* elided before the rough breathing of *ὕφηναν*, with *τ* becoming *θ*. *ὕφηναν* is third-person plural aorist active indicative of *ὑφαίνω*; its temporal augment lengthens initial *υ* without changing the vowel letter. The specific verb ‘weave’ replaces *fer* ‘made’.
- *ποικιλόχρους, πολύχρους* ‘variegated, many-coloured’ are contracted accusative plural adjectives in *-χρους* for *-χρόους*, agreeing across the line break with *τάπητας* (from *τάπης*) ‘carpets’. Thus *drappo* becomes a plural and a more specific fabric; the Greek gives neither an explicit comparative for *più* nor distinct terms for *sommesse e soprapposte*.
- *Οἱ Τάρταροι* is the delayed nominative subject; *οὔτε μὴν Τοῦρκοι* ‘nor indeed Turks’ adds a second subject after the object, with *μήν* reinforcing the addition. *Ἀλλ᾽ οὔτ᾽ Ἀράχνη* continues ‘but neither [did/would] Arachne’; both *ἀλλά* and *οὔτε* are elided. The Greek makes *Ἀράχνη* nominative agent and *ἱστοὺς τοιούτους* ‘such webs’ accusative object, reversing the passive construction *fur tai tele per Aragne imposte*.
- *ποτ᾽ ἄν* ‘ever … would’ gives the last comparison a modal force absent from the preceding historical indicative; *ποτ᾽* is elided *ποτε*, here before a smooth breathing. A singular verb of weaving must be supplied in sense from *ὕφηναν*. There is no expressed optative, and nothing corresponding separately to the act of laying webs on a loom.

> 19 Come tal volta stanno a riva i burchi,  
> 20 che parte sono in acqua e parte in terra;  

As at times the wherries lie on shore, that are part in water and part on land;

> 19 Ὡς ποθ᾽ ἵστανται πρὸς τὴν ὄχθην οἱ λέμβοι  
> (As at times boats stand against the shore,)
>
> 20 Μέρος μὲν ἐν ὕδασι, μέρος δ᾽ ἐπ᾽ ἄμμου,  
> (Partly in the water, and partly upon the sand,)

- *Ὡς ποθ᾽ ἵστανται* ‘as boats sometimes stand’ follows *Come tal volta stanno*. Again *ποθ᾽* is *ποτε* before a rough breathing. *ἵστανται* is third-person plural present middle indicative of *ἵστημι*, intransitive here. *πρὸς τὴν ὄχθην* uses *πρός* with the accusative for position against the bank; *οἱ λέμβοι* retains the delayed subject but gives the less specific ‘boats’ for *i burchi*.
- *Μέρος μὲν … μέρος δ᾽* dispenses with the relative clause *che … sono*. Repeated *μέρος* is an adverbial accusative, ‘in part’, and the postpositive particles *μέν … δέ* distribute the two positions; *δ᾽* is elided before *ἐπί*. *ἐν ὕδασι* uses the dative plural of *ὕδωρ* for singular *in acqua*, while *ἐπ᾽ ἄμμου* (elided *ἐπί* with the genitive) specifies ‘on sand’ for *in terra*.

> 21 e come là tra li Tedeschi lurchi  

and as there amongst the guzzling Germans

> 21 Ὡς τ᾽ ἐν ταῖς χώραις τῶν λαιμάργων Τευτόνων  
> (And as in the lands of the gluttonous Teutons)

- *Ὡς τ᾽* repeats the comparative *ὡς* and links it to the first comparison by postpositive *τε*, elided before *ἐν*: the order is ‘as also’, against *e come*. The comparison remains syntactically open at the end of the extract.
- *ἐν ταῖς χώραις* ‘in the lands’ supplies a geographical noun in the dative plural for *là tra*. Consequently the people are expressed not as those ‘amongst’ whom something happens, but by a dependent genitive plural, *τῶν λαιμάργων Τευτόνων* ‘of the gluttonous Teutons’. The adjective *λαιμάργων* stands in attributive position before the ethnic noun, reversing *Tedeschi lurchi*.

## Lines 22–54 — The Beast at the Brink and the Seated Sufferers

> 22 lo bevero s’assetta a far sua guerra:  

the beaver adjusts himself to wage his war:

> 22 Ὁ κάστωρ κατοπτεύων ἄγραν ὀκλάζει,  
> (The beaver crouches watching for its prey,)

- *Ὁ κάστωρ … ὀκλάζει* ‘the beaver crouches’ renders *lo bevero s’assetta* with a more specific verb of posture. *Ὀκλάζει* is 3rd sg. present active indicative of *ὀκλάζω*, intransitive where the Italian is reflexive.
- Between subject and verb, *κατοπτεύων ἄγραν* ‘watching for prey’ interprets *a far sua guerra*. The purpose infinitive becomes a simultaneous present active participle (nom. masc. sg. of *κατοπτεύω*, agreeing with *κάστωρ*); its accusative object *ἄγραν* names the quarry rather than the warfare, and there is no possessive.

> 23 così la fiera pessima si stava  
> 24 su l’orlo che, di pietra, il sabbion serra.  

so lay that worst of savage beasts upon the brim which closes the great sand with stone.

> 23 Οὕτως εἱστήκει τὸ μυσαχθὲς θηρίον  
> (So stood that loathsome beast)
>
> 24 Εἰς τὸ περὶ τὴν ἄμμον λίθινον χεῖλος.  
> (Upon the stony rim around the sand.)

- *Οὕτως* answers the comparison just as *così* does. *Εἱστήκει* (3rd sg. pluperfect active indicative of *ἵστημι*) expresses a continuing posture, ‘stood’, not an action of standing something up: the pluperfect of the intransitive perfect *ἕστηκα* supplies the past state of *si stava*. The postponed subject *τὸ μυσαχθὲς θηρίον* is ‘the loathsome beast’; *μυσαχθές* (neut. sg. of *μυσαχθής*) replaces the superlative *pessima* with a positive adjective.
- *Εἰς τὸ περὶ τὴν ἄμμον λίθινον χεῖλος* compresses *su l’orlo che, di pietra, il sabbion serra*. Between article and noun stand both *περὶ τὴν ἄμμον* ‘around the sand’ and *λίθινον* ‘of stone’: the enclosing action of *serra* becomes a spatial relation, and the material becomes an adjective agreeing with *χεῖλος*. *Εἰς* with the accusative here denotes the position occupied, not the more usual classical motion to it.

> 25 Nel vano tutta sua coda guizzava,  
> 26 torcendo in su la venenosa forca  
> 27 che, a guisa di scorpion, la punta armava.  

In the void glanced all his tail, twisting upwards the venomed fork, which, as in scorpions, armed the point.

> 25 Ἔσειεν ἐν τῷ κενῷ τὴν οὐρὰν ὅλην,  
> (In the void it was shaking its whole tail,)
>
> 26 Ἧς ἀνωρθοῦτο τἄκρον ἐξωπλισμένον,  
> (Whose tip rose upright, armed)
>
> 27 Ὡς ὁ σκορπίος ἰοβολοῦντι κέντρῳ.  
> (Like the scorpion with its venomous sting.)

- *Ἔσειεν ἐν τῷ κενῷ τὴν οὐρὰν ὅλην* ‘it was shaking the whole tail in the void’ makes the beast the subject of a transitive verb: where *tutta sua coda guizzava*, the tail is now the accusative object. *Ἔσειεν* is the augmented 3rd sg. imperfect active of *σείω*, with movable *-ν*; *κενῷ* is a substantivized neuter adjective. The article suffices for *sua*, and *ὅλην* gives the extent of the movement.
- *Ἧς ἀνωρθοῦτο τἄκρον* ‘whose tip was raised upright’ attaches a relative clause to *οὐράν*: *ἧς* is possessive gen. fem. sg., and *τἄκρον* is crasis for *τὸ ἄκρον*, the neuter subject. *Ἀνωρθοῦτο* (3rd sg. imperfect middle/passive of *ἀνορθόω*, with internal augment) substitutes the tip’s upward position or movement for the beast’s active *torcendo in su*.
- *Ἐξωπλισμένον … ἰοβολοῦντι κέντρῳ* ‘armed with a venom-discharging sting’ transfers *armava* to a resultative perfect middle/passive participle of *ἐξοπλίζω*, nom. neut. sg. with *τἄκρον*. The dative of equipment *κέντρῳ* replaces the fork, whose divided shape is not expressed; the present active participle *ἰοβολοῦντι* (from *ἰοβολέω*) agrees with it and renders *venenosa* as a capacity for action. *Ὡς ὁ σκορπίος* supplies a generic nominative, ‘as the scorpion [is armed]’, for *a guisa di scorpion*.

> 28 Lo duca disse: “Or convien che si torca  
> 29 la nostra via un poco infino a quella  
> 30 bestia malvagia che colà si corca.”  

My Guide said: "Now must we bend our way a little, to that wicked brute which couches there."

> 28 Οὑμὸς δ᾽ Ἡγήτωρ εἶπεν· “Ἡμᾶς νῦν δέον  
> (And my Guide said: “Now must we)
>
> 29 „ Ἐκτραπῆναι τῆς ὁδοῦ πρὸς μικρὸν ἄχρι  
> („ Turn aside from the way a little as far as)
>
> 30 „ Τοῦ τεταμένου ᾽κεῖσε κακοῦ θηρίου.”  
> („ That evil beast stretched out yonder.”)

- *Οὑμὸς δ᾽ Ἡγήτωρ εἶπεν* ‘and my guide said’ adds both the connective *δέ*, elided before the vowel, and an explicit possessive: *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*. *Ἡγήτωρ* is an agent noun, *εἶπεν* the 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *λέγω*.
- *Ἡμᾶς νῦν δέον ἐκτραπῆναι τῆς ὁδοῦ* ‘we must now turn aside from the road’ replaces *convien che si torca la nostra via*. *Δέον* is the impersonal neuter present participle of *δεῖ*, used predicatively with *ἐστί* understood. The travellers, possessors of *via* in Italian, become the accusative subject *ἡμᾶς* of *ἐκτραπῆναι* (second aorist passive infinitive of *ἐκτρέπω*, intransitive in sense); the road is a genitive of separation.
- *Πρὸς μικρόν* gives the small extent of the deviation. *Ἄχρι* ‘as far as’ governs *τοῦ … κακοῦ θηρίου*: the Italian relative *che colà si corca* becomes the attributive *τεταμένου ᾽κεῖσε* ‘stretched out yonder’. *Τεταμένου* is gen. neut. sg. perfect middle/passive participle of *τείνω*, a settled posture rather than the act of lying down; *᾽κεῖσε* loses the initial vowel of *ἐκεῖσε* and has locative force here rather than its usual directional sense ‘thither’.

> 31 Però scendemmo alla destra mammella  
> 32 e dieci passi femmo in su lo stremo,  
> 33 per ben cessar la rena e la fiammella:  

Then we descended on the right, and made ten paces towards the edge, that we might quite avoid the sand and flames;

> 31 Καταβάντες τότ᾽ εἰς τὸ δεξιὸν μέρος,  
> (Having descended then upon the right side,)
>
> 32 Ὠδεύσαμεν ἐπ᾽ ἄκρας βήματα δέκα  
> (We walked along the edge ten paces)
>
> 33 Πρὸς ἀποφυγὴν τῶν φλογῶν καὶ τῆς ἄμμου.  
> (To avoid the flames and the sand.)

- *Καταβάντες τότ᾽ εἰς τὸ δεξιὸν μέρος* ‘having then descended to the right side’ subordinates *scendemmo* to the following main verb. *Καταβάντες* is nom. masc. pl., second aorist active participle of *καταβαίνω*, sharing the subject ‘we’; *τότ᾽* is elided *τότε*, temporal rather than explicitly consequential like *però*. *Μέρος* gives the directional sense of *mammella* without its anatomical image.
- *Ὠδεύσαμεν ἐπ᾽ ἄκρας βήματα δέκα* ‘we walked on the edge ten steps’ replaces *passi femmo* with *ὡδεύσαμεν* (1st pl. aorist active of *ὁδεύω*, with temporal augment) and an accusative of extent, *βήματα δέκα*. *Ἐπ᾽* is elided *ἐπί* with genitive *ἄκρας*, ‘on the edge’, rather than motion towards it.
- *Πρὸς ἀποφυγήν* ‘for avoidance’ renders the purpose infinitive *per ben cessar* by a preposition and action noun; *ben* has no separate equivalent. The objective genitives *τῶν φλογῶν καὶ τῆς ἄμμου* reverse the Italian order, and plural *φλογῶν* replaces diminutive singular *fiammella*.

> 34 e quando noi a lei venuti semo,  
> 35 poco più oltre veggio in su la rena  
> 36 gente seder propinqua al loco scemo.  

and when we came to him, I saw upon the sand, a little farther onwards, people sitting near the empty space.

> 34 Ὡς δ’ ἀφικόμεθ’ ἐγγὺς αὐτοῦ, τότ’ εἶδον  
> (And when we had come near to it, then I saw)
>
> 35 Τινὰς ἐπὶ τῆς ἄμμου μικρόν τι πόῤῥω  
> (Certain folk upon the sand a little way off)
>
> 36 Συγκαθημένους πλησίον τοῦ βαράθρου.  
> (Sitting together near the abyss.)

- *Ὡς δ’ ἀφικόμεθ’ ἐγγὺς αὐτοῦ, τότ’ εἶδον* ‘and when we came near it, then I saw’ balances temporal *ὡς* with an added *τότε*. *Ἀφικόμεθ’* is elided *ἀφικόμεθα*, 1st pl. second aorist middle of *ἀφικνέομαι*; *ἐγγύς* governs genitive *αὐτοῦ*, referring to neuter *θηρίον* rather than feminine *lei*. Both the Italian compound past *venuti semo* and the historical present *veggio* become aorists; *εἶδον* is here 1st sg. of the aorist belonging to *ὁράω*.
- *Τινὰς … συγκαθημένους* ‘some persons sitting together’ replaces collective *gente* and its dependent infinitive *seder* with an accusative plural indefinite and a supplementary participle after a verb of seeing. *Συγκαθημένους* (acc. masc. pl. present middle participle of *συγκάθημαι*) adds togetherness. Between the two come *ἐπὶ τῆς ἄμμου* (position, with genitive) and *μικρόν τι πόῤῥω* ‘a little way off’ (adverbial accusative of extent; no explicit comparative for *più*). *Πλησίον τοῦ βαράθρου* names the *loco scemo* directly as ‘the abyss’, with genitive after the adverb.

> 37 Quivi il maestro: “Acciocchè tutta piena  
> 38 esperienza d’ esto giron porti,”  
> 39 mi disse, “va, e vedi la lor mena.  

Here my Master said to me: "That thou mayest carry full experience of this round, go and see the state of these.

> 37 Οὑμὸς δ’ εἶπε Προστάτης· “Ἵν’ ἔχῃς γνῶσιν  
> (And my Protector said: “That you may have knowledge)
>
> 38 „ Τελείαν παντὸς τοῦ περιβόλου τούτου,  
> („ Complete of this whole enclosure,)
>
> 39 „ Νῦν ἄπελθ’ ὀψόμενος αὐτῶν βασάνους.  
> („ Go now to behold their torments.)

- *Οὑμὸς δ’ εἶπε Προστάτης* places the complete speech introduction before the quotation instead of interposing *mi disse* in it. *Προστάτης* ‘protector’ interprets *maestro*; possessive *οὑμός* again has crasis. There is no separate *quivi* or indirect object *mi*.
- *Ἵν’ ἔχῃς γνῶσιν τελείαν* ‘that you may have complete knowledge’ is a purpose clause with elided *ἵνα* and 2nd sg. present active subjunctive of *ἔχω*: possession of knowledge replaces *esperienza … porti*. *Τελείαν* agrees with *γνῶσιν*; *παντὸς τοῦ περιβόλου τούτου* ‘of this entire enclosure’ depends on that noun. Thus *tutta piena* is redistributed: completeness describes the knowledge, totality the object known.
- *Νῦν ἄπελθ’ ὀψόμενος αὐτῶν βασάνους* ‘now go away to see their torments’ adds *νῦν* and converts the second command of *va, e vedi* into a purpose participle after a verb of motion. *Ἄπελθ’* is elided *ἄπελθε* (2nd sg. second aorist active imperative of *ἀπέρχομαι*); *ὀψόμενος* is nom. masc. sg. future middle participle of *ὁράω*, active in meaning. *Βασάνους* specifies *mena* as sufferings, with possessive genitive *αὐτῶν* preceding its noun.

> 40 Li tuoi ragionamenti sian là corti;  

Let thy talk with them be brief,

> 40 „ Αἱ διαλέξεις ἔστωσάν σοι βραχεῖαι·  
> („ Let your conversations be brief;)

- *Αἱ διαλέξεις ἔστωσάν σοι βραχεῖαι* ‘let the conversations be brief for you’ retains the plural of *li tuoi ragionamenti* and the predicate adjective (nom. fem. pl. *βραχεῖαι*, from *βραχύς*). Italian jussive subjunctive *sian* becomes *ἔστωσαν*, 3rd pl. present imperative of *εἰμί*; its extra written accent is due to enclitic *σοι*. This dative marks the person concerned in place of possessive *tuoi*; *là* is not rendered.

> 41 mentre che torni parlerò con questa,  
> 42 che ne conceda i suoi omeri forti.”  

till thou returnest, I will speak with this *beast*, that he may lend us his strong shoulders."

> 41 „ Ἕως δ’ ᾖς ἀπών, συνομιλήσω τούτῳ,  
> („ And while you are away, I will converse with this one,)
>
> 42 „ Ἵν’ ὤμους αὐτοῦ στεῤῥοὺς ἡμῖν παράσχῃ.”  
> („ That he may lend us his sturdy shoulders.”)

- *Ἕως δ’ ᾖς ἀπών* ‘and while you are away’ gives the duration, not the ending, of the interval in *mentre che torni*. *ᾖς* is 2nd sg. present subjunctive of *εἰμί*, with predicative *ἀπών* (nom. masc. sg. present participle of *ἄπειμι* ‘be away’). For a prospective temporal clause classical Attic would commonly have *ἄν* with the subjunctive; here *ἕως* stands without it.
- *Συνομιλήσω τούτῳ* ‘I shall converse with this one’ renders *parlerò con questa* by the 1st sg. future active of *συνομιλέω* with a dative of the interlocutor; *τούτῳ* refers back to neuter *θηρίον*. *Ἵν’ … παράσχῃ* makes the purpose of *che ne conceda* explicit: *παράσχῃ* is 3rd sg. second aorist active subjunctive of *παρέχω* ‘furnish’. Its object is *ὤμους … στεῤῥούς* (acc. masc. pl.), with possessive genitive *αὐτοῦ* between noun and adjective; *ἡμῖν* is the dative recipient corresponding to *ne*.

> 43 Così ancor su per la strema testa  
> 44 di quel settimo cerchio, tutto solo  
> 45 andai, ove sedea la gente mesta.  

Thus also, on the utmost limit of that seventh circle, all alone I went to where the woful folk were seated.

> 43 Οὕτω βαδίζων παρὰ τὴν ἄκραν ὄχθην  
> (Thus walking along the utmost bank)
>
> 44 Ἑβδόμου τοῦδε κύκλου μεμονωμένος,  
> (Of this seventh circle all alone,)
>
> 45 Ἦλθον ἔνθ’ ἦσαν οἱ καθήμενοι μίγδην.  
> (I came where those sitting huddled together were.)

- *Οὕτω βαδίζων παρὰ τὴν ἄκραν ὄχθην* ‘thus walking along the extreme bank’ retains *così* but has no separate equivalent of *ancor*. *Βαδίζων*, nom. masc. sg. present active participle of *βαδίζω*, makes the walking simultaneous with the advance expressed by the main verb. *Παρά* with the accusative marks the course along the bank; *ἄκραν ὄχθην* renders *strema testa* without the image of a head.
- *Ἑβδόμου τοῦδε κύκλου* is a dependent genitive, ‘of this seventh circle’, with proximal *τοῦδε* (from *ὅδε*) for *quel*. *Μεμονωμένος* ‘left alone’ (nom. masc. sg. perfect middle/passive participle of *μονόω*) expresses the state of the subject for adjectival *tutto solo*.
- *Ἦλθον ἔνθ’ ἦσαν οἱ καθήμενοι μίγδην* ‘I came where the seated ones were together’ gives *andai* as arrival (1st sg. second aorist active of *ἔρχομαι*). Relative adverb *ἔνθα* loses its final vowel before *ἦσαν*. Italian *sedea la gente* becomes plural *ἦσαν* (imperfect of *εἰμί*) with substantivized *οἱ καθήμενοι* (nom. masc. pl. present middle participle of *κάθημαι*). *Μίγδην* ‘mingled together’ describes grouping, not the sadness of *mesta*.

> 46 Per gli occhi fuori scoppiava lor duolo;  

Through the eyes their grief was bursting forth;

> 46 Ἐξέφλυζ’ ἐξ ὀμμάτων αὐτῶν ἡ λύπη·  
> (From their eyes grief gushed forth;)

- *Ἐξέφλυζ’ ἐξ ὀμμάτων αὐτῶν ἡ λύπη* ‘out of their eyes their grief welled up’ retains the postponed subject of *scoppiava lor duolo*. *Ἐξέφλυζ’* is elided *ἐξέφλυζε*, 3rd sg. imperfect active of *ἐκφλύζω*, with augment after the prefix. Prefix *ἐξ-* and preposition *ἐξ* reinforce the outward movement of *per gli occhi fuori*. The genitive *αὐτῶν* immediately follows *ὀμμάτων* (gen. pl. of *ὄμμα*), where Italian *lor* directly qualifies *duolo*.

> 47 di qua, di là soccorrien con le mani,  
> 48 quando a’ vapori, e quando al caldo suolo.  

on this side, on that, they with their hands kept warding off, sometimes the flames, sometimes the burning soil.

> 47 Ταῖς χερσὶν ἠμύνοντο τῇδε κἀκεῖσε  
> (With their hands they warded off hither and thither)
>
> 48 Τοῦ τε πυρώδους ὄμβρου καὶ θερμῆς ἄμμου.  
> (Both the fiery rain and the hot sand.)

- *Ταῖς χερσὶν ἠμύνοντο τῇδε κἀκεῖσε* fronts the instrument, ‘with their hands’ (dat. pl. of *χείρ*). *Ἠμύνοντο* is 3rd pl. imperfect middle of *ἀμύνω*, ‘they defended themselves’, making the self-protective sense of *soccorrien* explicit. The local adverbs *τῇδε κἀκεῖσε* ‘this way and that’ render *di qua, di là*; *κἀκεῖσε* is crasis for *καὶ ἐκεῖσε*.
- *Τοῦ τε πυρώδους ὄμβρου καὶ θερμῆς ἄμμου* ‘from both the fiery rain and hot sand’ replaces alternating *quando … quando* with inclusive *τε … καί*. *Ὄμβρου* specifies *vapori* as a shower; *ἄμμου* specifies *suolo* as sand. The genitives have separative force, identifying what the sufferers protect themselves from; they are striking with *ἀμύνομαι*, which ordinarily takes the thing repelled in the accusative.

> 49 Non altrimenti fan di state i cani,  
> 50 or col ceffo, or coi piè, quando son morsi  
> 51 o da pulci o da mosche o da tafani.  

Not otherwise the dogs in summer do, now with snout, now with paw, when they are bitten by fleas, or flies, or breeses.

> 49 Οὐκ ἄλλως πως ποιοῦσιν οἱ κύνες θέρους  
> (Not otherwise do dogs act in summer)
>
> 50 Μυκτῆρί τε καὶ ποσίν, ὅταν δηχθῶσιν  
> (Both with muzzle and with feet, when they are bitten)
>
> 51 Ὑπὸ τῶν ψύλλων, ἢ μυιῶν ἢ μυώπων.  
> (By fleas, or flies, or gadflies.)

- *Οὐκ ἄλλως πως ποιοῦσιν οἱ κύνες θέρους* closely follows *Non altrimenti fan di state i cani*. Enclitic indefinite *πως* reinforces *ἄλλως*, ‘in some other way’, under negation. *Ποιοῦσιν* is 3rd pl. present active indicative of *ποιέω*, a customary present; *θέρους* (gen. sg. of *θέρος*) is a genitive of time within which.
- *Μυκτῆρί τε καὶ ποσίν* ‘with muzzle and feet alike’ again replaces alternation (*or … or*) with *τε … καί*. Both nouns are instrumental datives, singular of *μυκτήρ* and plural of *πούς* respectively; the second accent on *μυκτῆρί* is due to enclitic *τε*.
- *Ὅταν δηχθῶσιν* ‘whenever they are bitten’ makes the recurrent condition explicit: *ὅταν* combines *ὅτε* and *ἄν*, and *δηχθῶσιν* is 3rd pl. aorist passive subjunctive of *δάκνω*, presenting each bite as an event. A single *ὑπό* with the genitive marks all three alternative agents, *τῶν ψύλλων, ἢ μυιῶν ἢ μυώπων*; Italian repeats *da* with each.

> 52 Poi che nel viso a certi gli occhi porsi,  
> 53 ne’ quali il doloroso foco casca,  
> 54 non ne conobbi alcun; ma io m’accorsi  

After I had set my eyes upon the visages of several on whom the dolorous fire falls, I knew not any of them; but I observed

> 52 Ἐμβλέψας τοῖς ὄμμασιν εἴς τινας τούτων  
> (Having looked with my eyes upon some of these)
>
> 53 Τῶν ὑπ’ ἀλγεινῶν φλογῶν τοξευομένων,  
> (Who were pierced by the painful flames,)
>
> 54 Οὐδέν’ ἀνεγνώρισα, πλὴν εἰς ἑκάστου  
> (I recognized none, yet upon each one's)

- *Ἐμβλέψας τοῖς ὄμμασιν εἴς τινας τούτων* ‘having looked with my eyes at some of these’ replaces the finite temporal clause *poi che … gli occhi porsi* with a nom. masc. sg. aorist active participle of *ἐμβλέπω*, anterior to the main verb. The eyes become an instrumental dative rather than the object of *porsi*. *Εἴς* directs the gaze at the persons themselves, with no noun for *viso*; *τούτων* is a partitive genitive after accusative plural *τινας*.
- *Τῶν ὑπ’ ἀλγεινῶν φλογῶν τοξευομένων* expands *τούτων*: ‘of those being shot at by painful flames’. The article and present passive participle of *τοξεύω* are gen. masc. pl. Italian *ne’ quali il doloroso foco casca* has the fire as subject; Greek makes the persons the sufferers of the action and the plural flames its agent (*ὑπό* with genitive). The verb introduces the image of shooting rather than falling.
- *Οὐδέν’ ἀνεγνώρισα* ‘I recognized no one’ renders *non ne conobbi alcun* with a single negative object, elided *οὐδένα* (acc. masc. sg. of *οὐδείς*), and the 1st sg. aorist active indicative of *ἀναγνωρίζω*, internally augmented. Adversative *πλήν* answers *ma*, but *εἰς ἑκάστου* does not yet give a verb corresponding to *io m’accorsi*. The line breaks inside a phrase: *ἑκάστου* is possessive gen. sg., ‘each one’s’, not the object of *εἰς*, whose accusative complement is still to come.

## Lines 55–87 — Purses, a Warning, and the Shivering Fit

> 55 che dal collo a ciascun pendea una tasca,  
> 56 che avea certo colore e certo segno,  
> 57 e quindi par che il loro occhio si pasca.  

that from the neck of each there hung a pouch, which had a certain colour and a certain impress, and thereon it seems their eye is feasting.

> 55 Τὸν λαιμὸν βαλάντιον κρεμαστὸν εἶδον,  
> (Neck I saw a pouch hanging,)
>
> 56 Ἔχον χρώματα καὶ σημεῖ᾽ ὡρισμένα,  
> (Having distinct colours and emblems,)
>
> 57 Ἐφ’ οἷς ἑστιᾷν τοὺς ὀφθαλμοὺς ἐδόκουν.  
> (Upon which they seemed to feast their eyes.)

- *Τὸν λαιμὸν βαλάντιον κρεμαστὸν εἶδον* ‘at the neck I saw a pouch hanging’ replaces the dependent *che … pendea* with a main verb of sight. *Εἶδον* is the 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*; *βαλάντιον* is its neut. acc. sg. object, and *κρεμαστόν* a verbal adjective describing the pouch, not a finite verb of hanging. The fronted *τὸν λαιμόν* is a loose accusative of respect or bodily location: it does not express ‘from’ as a preposition with the genitive would. There is no separate equivalent of the distributive *a ciascun*.
- *Ἔχον* (pres. act. ptcp. neut. acc. sg. of *ἔχω*) continues the description of *βαλάντιον* in place of *che avea*. The two singulars with repeated *certo* become two plurals with one adjective, *χρώματα καὶ σημεῖ᾽ ὡρισμένα* ‘definite colours and devices’. *Σημεῖ᾽* is elided *σημεῖα*; *ὡρισμένα* is the perf. mid./pass. ptcp. neut. acc. pl. of *ὁρίζω*, literally ‘having been marked off, determined’.
- *Ἐφ’ οἷς* ‘upon which’ makes the devices or colours the explicit antecedent of the relative (*ἐπί* + dat. pl.; elision and aspiration before the rough breathing). For impersonal *par che* with the eye as subject of reflexive *si pasca*, Greek has personal *ἐδόκουν* ‘they seemed’ (3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *δοκέω*) with the contracted pres. act. inf. *ἑστιᾶν* of *ἑστιάω*: the owners are the implied subjects, and *τοὺς ὀφθαλμούς* ‘their eyes’ the plural direct object of ‘feast’.

> 58 E com’ io riguardando tra lor vegno,  
> 59 in una borsa gialla vidi azzurro,  
> 60 che d’ un leone avea faccia e contegno.  

And as I came amongst them looking, on a yellow purse I saw azure, that had the semblance and gesture of a lion.

> 58 Μεταξὺ δ’ αὐτῶν παρατηρήσας εἶδον  
> (And observing among them, I saw)
>
> 59 Βαλάντιον κρόκινον, κυανοῦν φέρον  
> (A saffron-yellow pouch, bearing a blue)
>
> 60 Σῆμα λέοντος ἔχον ὄψιν καὶ σχῆμα.  
> (Emblem that had the face and form of a lion.)

- *Μεταξὺ δ’ αὐτῶν* ‘and among them’ uses *μεταξύ* with the genitive; postpositive *δ’* is elided *δέ*. The motion of *vegno* is not expressed. Instead of the temporal *com’ io … vegno* with simultaneous *riguardando*, Greek subordinates the looking by *παρατηρήσας* (nom. masc. sg. aor. act. ptcp. of *παρατηρέω*), ‘having looked closely’, to the repeated *εἶδον* ‘I saw’.
- Italian puts the purse in a prepositional phrase and makes the colour *azzurro* itself the object of *vidi*. Greek makes *βαλάντιον κρόκινον* ‘a saffron-coloured pouch’ the object and introduces a second object under *φέρον* (pres. act. ptcp. neut. acc. sg. of *φέρω*, agreeing with the pouch): *κυανοῦν … σῆμα* ‘a dark-blue device’. *Κυανοῦν* is the contracted neut. acc. sg. of *κυάνεος*.
- *Σῆμα λέοντος ἔχον ὄψιν καὶ σχῆμα* takes up *che d’ un leone avea faccia e contegno*. *Ἔχον* now most naturally qualifies the device; *λέοντος* (gen. sg. of *λέων*) can be heard with *σῆμα* ‘device of a lion’ as well as with the following accusatives, ‘having a lion’s look and bearing’. *Σχῆμα* can denote posture as well as shape, preserving something of *contegno* beyond a mere outline.

> 61 Poi procedendo di mio sguardo il curro  
> 62 vidine un’ altra come sangue rossa  
> 63 mostrare un’ oca bianca più che burro.  

Then, my look continuing its course, I saw another of them, [red as] blood, display a goose more white than butter.

> 61 Εἶτα τὸ βλέμμα προχωρῶν εἶδον ἄλλο  
> (Then moving my gaze onward, I saw another)
>
> 62 Ἐρυθρότερον αἵματος, εἰκονίζον  
> (Redder than blood, portraying)
>
> 63 Ἐναργῶς χῆνα γάλακτος λευκοτέραν.  
> (Clearly a goose whiter than milk.)

- *Εἶτα τὸ βλέμμα προχωρῶν* ‘then, advancing my gaze’ transfers the movement to the observer: *προχωρῶν* (contracted pres. act. ptcp. nom. masc. sg. of *προχωρέω*) agrees with the subject of *εἶδον*, not with neuter *βλέμμα*. The accusative is loose with a normally intransitive verb; Greek follows the movement of *procedendo di mio sguardo il curro* without supplying a noun for *curro*.
- *Εἶδον ἄλλο* ‘I saw another’ leaves neuter *βαλάντιον* understood and has no separate partitive for *ne* in *vidine*. *Ἐρυθρότερον αἵματος* ‘redder than blood’ changes the comparison of equality *come sangue rossa* into a comparative adjective agreeing with *ἄλλο*, with gen. sg. *αἵματος* (from *αἷμα*) as the standard.
- *Εἰκονίζον* ‘portraying’ is a pres. act. ptcp. neut. acc. sg. of *εἰκονίζω*, describing the object seen, where Italian uses *mostrare* after a verb of sight. Greek adds *ἐναργῶς* ‘distinctly’. *Χῆνα … λευκοτέραν* is the feminine acc. sg. object and its agreeing comparative; *γάλακτος* (gen. sg. of *γάλα*) retains the comparative construction of *più che burro* but changes butter to milk.

> 64 Ed un, che d’ una scrofa azzurra e grossa  
> 65 segnato avea lo suo sacchetto bianco,  
> 66 mi disse : “ Che fai tu in questa fossa?  

And one who, with a sow azure and pregnant, had his argent sacklet stamped, said to me: “What art thou doing in this pit?

> 64 Εἷς δ’ οὗ τὸ λευκὸν βαλάντιον συαίνης  
> (And one whose white pouch of a sow)
>
> 65 Κυανῆς ἐπίτεκος εἶχε σημεῖον,  
> (Blue and pregnant bore the emblem,)
>
> 66 Εἶπέ μοι· “Σὺ τί ζητεῖς ἐν βόθρῳ τῷδε;  
> (Said to me: “What do you seek in this pit?)

- *Εἷς δ’ οὗ τὸ λευκὸν βαλάντιον … εἶχε σημεῖον* ‘and one whose white pouch bore a device’ changes the relative construction: *εἷς* (nom. masc. sg.) is the subject of *εἶπε*, but possessive *οὗ* makes his pouch the subject of *εἶχε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἔχω*). The man no longer ‘had his sacklet stamped’; the sack bears the emblem. *Λευκόν* gives *bianco* directly, and the ordinary *βαλάντιον* makes no fresh diminutive distinction for *sacchetto*.
- *Συαίνης κυανῆς* ‘of a blue sow’ is gen. fem. sg., dependent on *σημεῖον*; *κυανῆς* is contracted from *κυανέης*. *Ἐπίτεκος* ‘near giving birth’ takes *grossa* in the sense ‘pregnant’, not simply ‘large’. Its form is irregular here: as an epithet of genitive *συαίνης* one would expect gen. sg. *ἐπιτόκου*, not nominative *ἐπίτεκος*.
- *Εἶπέ μοι* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω* with enclitic dat. *μοι* (hence the additional accent on the verb). In *σὺ τί ζητεῖς* ‘you, what do you seek?’ explicit *σύ* retains *tu*, but *ζητεῖς* (contracted 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ζητέω*) asks the purpose rather than literally *che fai*. *Ἐν βόθρῳ τῷδε* uses the locative dative and the near demonstrative *ὅδε*, ‘in this very pit’.

> 67 Or te ne va; e perchè se’ vivo anco,  
> 68 sappi che il mio vicin Vitaliano  
> 69 sederà qui dal mio sinistro fianco.  

Get thee gone; and, as thou art still alive, know that my neighbour Vitaliano shall sit here at my left side.

> 67 „Ἄπιθι νῦν ἐντεῦθεν, καὶ ζῶν ὢν ἔτι,  
> („Depart now hence, and since you are still alive,)
>
> 68 „Ἴσθι τὸν γείτονά μου Βιταλιάνον  
> („Know that my neighbour Vitaliano)
>
> 69 „Ὧδε καθεδούμενον ἐπὶ λαιά μοι.  
> („Will sit here upon my left.)

- *Ἄπιθι νῦν ἐντεῦθεν* ‘go away now from here’ distributes *Or te ne va* between the 2nd sg. pres. imperative of *ἄπειμι* ‘go away’, the time adverb, and an explicit adverb of motion from a place. *Καὶ ζῶν ὢν ἔτι* ‘and being still alive’ renders causal *perchè se’ vivo anco* by a circumstantial participial phrase: *ζῶν* (contracted pres. act. ptcp. of *ζάω*) serves as predicate with *ὤν* (pres. ptcp. of *εἰμί*), both nom. masc. sg., referring to the addressee.
- *Ἴσθι* is the 2nd sg. imperative of the perfect with present sense *οἶδα*, ‘know’. In place of *sappi che* with a finite future it takes an accusative and supplementary participle: *τὸν γείτονά μου Βιταλιάνον … καθεδούμενον* ‘know my neighbour Vitaliano to be going to sit’. The name is in apposition to *γείτονα*; the extra accent on *γείτονά* precedes enclitic possessive *μου*. *Καθεδούμενον* is acc. masc. sg. of the contracted fut. mid. ptcp. of *καθέζομαι*.
- *Ὧδε* ‘here’ precedes the participle as *qui* follows *sederà*. *Ἐπὶ λαιά μοι* has the sense ‘on my left’, with *μοι* a dative of reference or possession and no noun for *fianco*. The printed *λαιά* does not show the iota subscript of the locative dat. fem. sg. *λαιᾷ* one would expect with static *ἐπί*.

> 70 Con questi Fiorentin son Padovano;  

With these Florentines am I, a Paduan;

> 70 „Σὺν Φλωρεντίνοις τοῖσδ’ εἰμὶ Παταυῖνος.  
> („With these Florentines I am a Paduan.)

- *Σὺν Φλωρεντίνοις τοῖσδ’* keeps *Con questi Fiorentin* at the front; *σύν* governs the dat. masc. pl., and the postposed demonstrative *τοῖσδ’* is *τοῖσδε* with final vowel elided before *εἰμί*. *Εἰμὶ Παταυῖνος* follows *son Padovano*: the verb supplies the person, and the ethnic adjective is an anarthrous predicate nominative.

> 71 spesse fiate m’intronan gli orecchi,  
> 72 gridando: ‘Vegna il cavalier sovrano,  
> 73 che recherà la tasca con tre becchi.’”  

many a time they din my ears, shouting: ‘Let the sovereign cavalier come, who will bring the pouch with three goats!’”

> 71 „Πολλάκις ἐκώφωσαν τὰς ἀκοάς μου  
> („Often have they deafened my ears)
>
> 72 „Κράζοντες· ‘“Ἱππεὺς ἐλθέτω κυριάρχης,  
> („Crying: ‘“Let the sovereign knight come,)
>
> 73 „Ὁ τὸ τρίῤῥυγχον βαλάντιον κομίσων.”’  
> („Who will bring the three-beaked pouch.”’)

- *Πολλάκις* condenses *spesse fiate* into one adverb. *Ἐκώφωσαν* ‘they deafened’ (3rd pl. augmented aor. act. ind. of *κωφόω*) sums up repeated occasions rather than reproducing the habitual present *m’intronan*. *Τὰς ἀκοάς μου* ‘my ears’ uses a possessive genitive instead of Italian *mi* with the body part.
- *Κράζοντες* (pres. act. ptcp. nom. masc. pl. of *κράζω*) agrees with the unexpressed subject, giving the accompanying action of *gridando*. *Ἱππεὺς ἐλθέτω κυριάρχης* puts the 3rd sg. second aor. act. imperative of *ἔρχομαι* between ‘knight’ and the appositional or predicative title ‘sovereign’; it gives a direct command for the Italian jussive subjunctive *Vegna*.
- *Ὁ … κομίσων* ‘the one who will bring’ is an articular fut. act. ptcp. nom. masc. sg. of *κομίζω*, in apposition to the knight, for finite relative *che recherà*. *Τὸ τρίῤῥυγχον βαλάντιον* changes *la tasca con tre becchi* to adjective plus noun, ‘the three-beaked pouch’: Musurus takes *becchi* as beaks, not as the goats of the English. The compound of *τρι-* and *ῥύγχος* shows the traditional spelling of doubled rho with smooth and rough breathings.

> 74 Qui distorse la bocca, e di fuor trasse  
> 75 la lingua, come ’l bue che il naso lecchi.  

Then he writhed his mouth, and thrust his tongue out, like an ox that licks his nose.

> 74 Εἶτα τὸ στόμα στρέψας ἔσυρεν ἔξω  
> (Then twisting his mouth, he thrust out)
>
> 75 Τὴν γλῶσσαν, καθάπερ βοῦς λείχων τὴν ῥῖνα.  
> (His tongue, just like an ox licking its nose.)

- *Εἶτα* ‘then’ takes temporal *Qui*; *τὸ στόμα στρέψας* ‘having twisted his mouth’ subordinates the first of the two Italian finite verbs by an aor. act. ptcp. nom. masc. sg. of *στρέφω*. *Ἔσυρεν ἔξω τὴν γλῶσσαν* ‘he drew his tongue out’ renders *di fuor trasse la lingua* with the augmented 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *σύρω*: the drawing out is pictured in progress after the twist. The articles suffice for the possessor of both body parts.
- *Καθάπερ βοῦς* ‘just like an ox’ has no article for generic *’l bue*. *Λείχων* (pres. act. ptcp. nom. masc. sg. of *λείχω*) replaces *che … lecchi* by a participle agreeing with *βοῦς*; *τὴν ῥῖνα* is the acc. sg. of *ῥίς*, ‘nose’.

> 76 Ed io, temendo no ’l più star crucciasse  
> 77 lui che di poco star m’avea monito,  
> 78 torna’ mi indietro dall’anime lasse.  

And I, dreading lest longer stay might anger him who had admonished me to stay short time, turned back from those fore-wearied souls.

> 76 Ἐγὼ δὲ φοβούμενος, μὴ μένων πλέον  
> (And I, fearing lest by staying longer)
>
> 77 Ἀπαρέσω τῷ μικρὸν στῆναί μ’ εἰπόντι,  
> (I should displease him who bade me stay but a little,)
>
> 78 Ἦλθον ὀπίσω, λιπὼν ψυχὰς ἐκκόπους.  
> (Came back, leaving the weary souls.)

- *Ἐγὼ δὲ φοβούμενος* keeps the emphatic *io* and renders *temendo* by the pres. mid./pass. ptcp. nom. masc. sg. of *φοβέομαι*. *Μή* introduces what is feared, corresponding to the apprehensive *no* in *temendo no*, not to a negation of the feared event. *Μένων πλέον* ‘by remaining longer’ changes the substantival *’l più star* into a circumstantial pres. act. ptcp. of *μένω* with the comparative used adverbially.
- *Ἀπαρέσω* ‘I should displease’ is naturally a 1st sg. aor. act. subj. of *ἀπαρέσκω* after *μή* (the future indicative has the same form). The speaker becomes the grammatical subject in place of the longer stay. Greek retains the subjunctive rather than shifting to an optative in past narration. *Τῷ … εἰπόντι* ‘him who told’ is the dat. masc. sg. articular second aor. act. ptcp. of *λέγω*, dative with ‘displease’ instead of the Italian direct object *lui*. The aorist participle conveys the prior instruction without a pluperfect.
- *Μικρὸν στῆναί μ’* ‘that I stay a little’ has adverbial *μικρόν* of duration, intransitive second aor. act. inf. *στῆναι* of *ἵστημι*, and elided enclitic *με* as its accusative subject. *Ἦλθον ὀπίσω* ‘I came back’ (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*) dispenses with reflexive *mi* in *torna’ mi*. *Λιπὼν ψυχὰς ἐκκόπους* ‘leaving weary souls’ turns *dall’anime lasse* from a phrase of separation into the object of *λιπών*, second aor. act. ptcp. nom. masc. sg. of *λείπω*; *ἐκκόπους* is the feminine accusative plural of a two-termination adjective.

> 79 Trovai lo duca mio ch’ era salito  
> 80 già in su la groppa del fiero animale,  

I found my Guide, who had already mounted on the haunch of the dreadful animal;

> 79 Εὗρον δ’ Ἡγήτορ’ ἐμὸν ἀναβάντ’ ἤδη  
> (And I found my Guide already mounted)
>
> 80 Ἐπὶ τὴν ῥάχιν τοῦ θηριώδους ζώου·  
> (Upon the back of the savage creature;)

- *Εὗρον δ’ Ἡγήτορ’ ἐμόν* ‘and I found my guide’ adds connective *δέ*. *Εὗρον* is the 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *εὑρίσκω* (without the temporal augment of *ηὗρον*); *Ἡγήτορ’* is elided *ἡγήτορα*, acc. sg. of *ἡγήτωρ*, with the postposed possessive adjective corresponding to *lo duca mio*.
- *Ἀναβάντ’ ἤδη* gives the guide’s state as found by an object-oriented participle rather than by *ch’ era salito*: *ἀναβάντ’* is elided *ἀναβάντα*, second aor. act. ptcp. acc. masc. sg. of *ἀναβαίνω*, with *ἤδη* marking prior completion. *Ἐπὶ τὴν ῥάχιν* has the accusative of the goal of mounting, ‘onto the back’, less specific than *in su la groppa*. *Τοῦ θηριώδους ζώου* ‘of the savage creature’ has the contracted gen. sg. of *θηριώδης* agreeing with neuter *ζώου*.

> 81 e disse a me: “ Or sie forte ed ardito.  

and he said to me: “Now be stout and bold!

> 81 Εἶπε δέ μοι· “Νῦν ἔσο θρασὺς κἀνδρεῖος·  
> (And he said to me: “Now be bold and courageous;)

- *Εἶπε δέ μοι* resumes the finite narration; postpositive *δέ* renders initial *e*. *Νῦν ἔσο* uses the poetic uncontracted 2nd sg. imperative of *εἰμί* (beside *ἔσσο*, Attic *ἴσθι*) for *Or sie*. *Θρασὺς κἀνδρεῖος* ‘bold and brave’ has two predicate nominatives; *κἀνδρεῖος* is crasis of *καὶ ἀνδρεῖος*. Greek distributes the force of *forte ed ardito* between boldness first and courage second.

> 82 Omai si scende per sì fatte scale;  

Now by such stairs must we descend;

> 82 „Οὐ δυνατὸν τοῦ λοιποῦ καταβῆν’ ἄλλως.  
> („Henceforth it is impossible to descend otherwise.)

- *Οὐ δυνατὸν τοῦ λοιποῦ καταβῆν’ ἄλλως* ‘henceforth it is not possible to descend otherwise’ makes the necessity implicit in *si scende* explicit by denying an alternative. *Δυνατόν* is an impersonal neuter predicate with *ἐστί* omitted; *τοῦ λοιποῦ* is a temporal genitive, ‘for the future’, for *Omai*. *Καταβῆν’* is elided *καταβῆναι*, second aor. act. inf. of *καταβαίνω*. The negative with *ἄλλως* replaces *per sì fatte scale*; Greek mentions the manner but has no word for stairs.

> 83 monta dinanzi: ch’ io voglio esser mezzo,  
> 84 sì che la coda non possa far male.”  

mount thou in front: for I wish to be in the middle, that the tail may not do hurt to *thee*.”

> 83 „Ἀνάβα πρόσθεν, ἐγὼ δ’ ἔσομ’ ἐν μέσῳ,  
> („Mount in front, and I will be in the middle,)
>
> 84 „Ἵν’ ἐκ τῆς οὐρᾶς μηδὲν κακὸν συμβῇ σοι.”  
> („So that no harm may befall you from the tail.”)

- *Ἀνάβα πρόσθεν* ‘mount in front’ uses the short 2nd sg. second aor. act. imperative of *ἀναβαίνω* (beside *ἀνάβηθι*). *Ἐγὼ δ’ ἔσομ’ ἐν μέσῳ* ‘and I shall be in the middle’ contrasts the two positions by *ἐγώ* and *δέ*: explanatory *ch’* and volitional *voglio esser* give way to a simple future. *Ἔσομ’* is elided *ἔσομαι*, 1st sg. fut. mid. ind. of *εἰμί*; *ἐν μέσῳ* is locative with the adjective substantivized.
- *Ἵν’* is elided *ἵνα*, introducing purpose for *sì che*. Instead of making the tail subject of *non possa far male*, Greek makes it the source (*ἐκ τῆς οὐρᾶς*) and *μηδὲν κακόν* ‘no harm’ the neut. nom. sg. subject of *συμβῇ* (3rd sg. second aor. act. subj. of *συμβαίνω*). There is no separate verb of ability. Final *σοι* is the explicit dative of the person to whom harm might happen, supplied in the English too.

> 85 Qual è colui, ch’ ha sì presso il riprezzo  
> 86 della quartana, ch’ ha già l’unghie smorte,  
> 87 e trema tutto, pur guardando il rezzo,  

As one who has the shivering of the quartan so near, that he has his nails already pale, and trembles all, still keeping the shade,

> 85 Ὡς ὁ τὸ ῥῖγος ἐγγίζον τεταρταίου  
> (As one who the approaching chill of quartan fever)
>
> 86 Αἰσθανόμενος, ὠχροὺς τ’ ὄνυχας ἔχων,  
> (Feels, and has pale nails,)
>
> 87 Τρέμει σύνολος, κρυερὸν τόπον βλέπων,  
> (Trembles all over, looking at a chilly place,)

- *Ὡς ὁ … αἰσθανόμενος* ‘as the man who feels’ starts the comparison with an articular participle in place of *Qual è colui, ch’ ha*. *Αἰσθανόμενος* is the pres. mid. ptcp. nom. masc. sg. of *αἰσθάνομαι*. It takes *τὸ ῥῖγος ἐγγίζον* ‘the chill approaching’ as accusative object with supplementary pres. act. ptcp. neut. acc. sg. of *ἐγγίζω*. Greek thus makes nearness a perceived action rather than reproducing *sì presso … ch’*. *Τεταρταίου* is a substantivized gen. sg., ‘of a quartan [fever]’, dependent on *ῥῖγος* (understand *πυρετοῦ* with the adjective).
- *Ὠχροὺς τ’ ὄνυχας ἔχων* ‘and having pale nails’ coordinates a second pres. act. ptcp. nom. masc. sg., *ἔχων*, with the participle of perception by enclitic *τε*, elided before *ὄνυχας* (acc. pl. of *ὄνυξ*). The pallor is an accompanying condition rather than a finite result clause, and *già* is not separately rendered.
- *Τρέμει σύνολος* ‘he trembles in his entirety’ gives the comparison its finite verb (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *τρέμω*); *σύνολος* is a predicate adjective referring to the whole person, like *tutto*. *Κρυερὸν τόπον βλέπων* ‘looking at a chilly place’ takes *guardando* as visual perception, unlike the English “keeping the shade”. *Βλέπων* is a simultaneous pres. act. ptcp. nom. masc. sg. with an accusative object; poetic *κρυερός* expresses the chill of *rezzo* rather than naming the shade, and there is no separate restrictive or emphatic particle for *pur*.

## Lines 88–120 — The Descent on Geryon

> 88 tal divenn' io alle parole porte;  

Such I became when these words were uttered;

> 88 Οὕτως ἤμην, ἀκούσας τοὺς λόγους τούτους·  
> (Such was I, having heard these words;)

- *Οὕτως ἤμην* ‘thus I was’ keeps the backward reference of *tal* in first position, but substitutes a state for *divenn’ io* ‘I became’. *ἤμην* is the later, middle-formed 1st sg. imperfect of *εἰμί*; the usual Attic form is *ἦ*. There is no separate pronoun for *io*.
- *ἀκούσας τοὺς λόγους τούτους* ‘having heard these words’ changes the standpoint of *alle parole porte*: instead of qualifying the words as uttered, Musurus qualifies the speaker as having heard them. *ἀκούσας* (nom. masc. sg., aor. act. participle of *ἀκούω*) has the subject of *ἤμην* understood; the demonstrative *τούτους* stands outside the article–noun group.

> 89 ma vergogna mi fer le sue minacce,  
> 90 che innanzi a buon signor fa servo forte.  

but his threats excited in me shame, which makes a servant brave in presence of a worthy master.

> 89 Ἀλλ’ ἐνέπλησάν μ’ αἰσχύνης τῆς ποιούσης  
> (Yet they filled me with that shame which makes)
>
> 90 Πρόσθεν καλοῦ κυρίου δοῦλον ἀνδρεῖον.  
> (A servant brave before a worthy master.)

- *Ἀλλ’ ἐνέπλησάν μ’ αἰσχύνης* ‘but they filled me with shame’ renders *ma vergogna mi fer le sue minacce*. *ἐνέπλησαν* (3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *ἐμπίπλημι*, with internal augment) takes the person filled in the accusative, *μ’* for *με*, and the contents in the genitive, *αἰσχύνης*. The Italian names the subject last; Greek leaves it unexpressed, so that the preceding *λόγους* supplies the sense ‘the words’ rather than expressly ‘his threats’. The extra accent in *ἐνέπλησάν* is due to the following enclitic.
- *τῆς ποιούσης* (gen. fem. sg., pres. act. participle of *ποιέω*) resumes *αἰσχύνης* with an article: ‘the shame that makes’. This replaces finite *che … fa*. *πρόσθεν* governs the genitive *καλοῦ κυρίου*; *δοῦλον ἀνδρεῖον* gives *ποιούσης* an accusative object and an agreeing predicate adjective, in the order of *servo forte*.

> 91 Io m' assettai in su quelle spallacce;  

I placed myself on those huge shoulders;

> 91 Ἐκάθισα τότ’ ἐπὶ τῶν φρικτῶν ὤμων·  
> (I sat down then upon those dreadful shoulders;)

- *Ἐκάθισα* (1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *καθίζω*) is intransitive, ‘I sat down’, for reflexive *m’ assettai*. *τότ’* is elided *τότε*, a time adverb supplied by Musurus. *ἐπὶ τῶν φρικτῶν ὤμων* gives the place with *ἐπί* and the genitive plural: the article replaces *quelle*, and *φρικτῶν* ‘dreadful’ interprets the expressive augmentative of *spallacce* rather than specifying size.

> 92 “Sì” (volli dir, ma la voce non venne  
> 93 com' io credetti) “fa che tu m'abbracce.”  

[I] wished to say, only the voice came not as I thought: “See that thou embrace me.”

> 92 Εἰπεῖν δ’ ἤθελον (ἀλλ᾽ οὐ, καθὼς ᾠόμην,  
> (And I wished to say (but not, as I thought,)
>
> 93 Ἦλθεν ἡ φωνή), “Λάβε μ᾽ ἐν σαῖς ἀγκάλαις.”  
> (Did my voice come), “Take me in your arms.”)

- *Εἰπεῖν δ’ ἤθελον* ‘and to speak I wished’ fronts the second aorist active infinitive of *λέγω*, dependent on *ἤθελον* (1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἐθέλω*). The imperfect presents the unfulfilled intention of *volli*. Postpositive *δ’* is elided *δέ*. Italian starts the intended speech with *Sì* before interrupting it with *volli dir*; Greek postpones the entire speech.
- *ἀλλ᾽ οὐ, καθὼς ᾠόμην, ἦλθεν ἡ φωνή* keeps *la voce non venne* almost word for word, but inserts the comparison between negation and verb. *ᾠόμην* (1st sg. imperf. mid. ind. of *οἴομαι*, with temporal augment) means ‘I supposed’; *ἦλθεν* is the 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι*.
- *Λάβε μ᾽ ἐν σαῖς ἀγκάλαις* ‘take me in your arms’ reduces *fa che tu m’abbracce*, imperative plus subjunctive, to the direct command *λάβε* (2nd sg. second aor. act. imperative of *λαμβάνω*). *σαῖς* is the dat. fem. pl. of possessive *σός*, agreeing with *ἀγκάλαις* after *ἐν*.

> 94 Ma esso, che altra volta mi sovvenne  
> 95 ad altro forse, tosto ch' io montai  
> 96 con le braccia m'avvinse e mi sostenne;  

But he, who at other times assisted me in other difficulties, soon as I mounted, clasped me with his arms, and held me up;

> 94 Ἀλλ᾽ οὗτος, ὅς μοι καὶ πρότερον πολλάκις  
> (But he, who to me before also often)
>
> 95 Ἐβοήθησεν, εὐθὺς ἅμ᾽ ἐπιβάντα  
> (Had given help, straightway as soon as I mounted)
>
> 96 Ἰσχυροῖς βραχίοσι συνέσφιγξέ με,  
> (Clasped me tightly with his strong arms,)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ οὗτος, ὅς μοι καὶ πρότερον πολλάκις ἐβοήθησεν* ‘but he, who had often helped me before as well’ keeps the demonstrative and intervening relative clause of *ma esso, che …*. *ὅς* is nominative as subject of *ἐβοήθησεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *βοηθέω*); *μοι* is the dative of the person helped. *πρότερον* ‘previously’ and *πολλάκις* ‘often’ expand *altra volta* into repeated occasions; *ad altro forse* has no distinct equivalent.
- *εὐθὺς ἅμ᾽ ἐπιβάντα* compresses finite *tosto ch’ io montai*: *εὐθύς* ‘immediately’ and *ἅμ᾽* (elided *ἅμα*, ‘at the same time’) mark the close coincidence. *ἐπιβάντα* is the acc. masc. sg. of the root aorist active participle of *ἐπιβαίνω*, ‘having mounted’; it agrees with the still unspoken object *με*, not the subject *οὗτος*.
- *ἰσχυροῖς βραχίοσι συνέσφιγξέ με* ‘with strong arms he held me tight’ makes *con le braccia* an instrumental dative plural of *βραχίων*, adding *ἰσχυροῖς*. One verb, *συνέσφιγξε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *συσφίγγω*, augment after the prefix), does duty for *m’avvinse e mi sostenne*: the firm grip is expressed, the supporting not separately predicated. The final accent in *συνέσφιγξέ* is additional before enclitic *με*.

> 97 e disse: “Gerion, moviti omai!  
> 98 le rote larghe e lo scender sia poco:  
> 99 pensa la nuova soma che tu hai.”  

then he said: “Geryon, now move thee! be thy circles large, and gradual thy descent: think of the unusual burden that thou hast.”

> 97 Λέγων· “Γηρυών, κινήθητι νῦν πρόσω,  
> (Saying: “Geryon, move forward now,)
>
> 98 „ Εὐρὺς τοῖς γύροις, τῇ καταβάσει πρᾶος·  
> („ Wide in your circles, gentle in your descent;)
>
> 99 „ Σκόπει περὶ τοῦ νέου βάρους, ὃ φέρεις.”  
> („ Take heed of the new burden that you bear.”)

- *Λέγων* ‘saying’ (nom. masc. sg., pres. act. participle of *λέγω*) attaches the speech to the preceding act instead of coordinating another finite verb, *e disse*. *Γηρυών* is direct address; *κινήθητι* (2nd sg. aor. pass. imperative of *κινέω*) has intransitive force, ‘move’, for reflexive *moviti*. *νῦν* renders *omai*; *πρόσω* adds ‘forward’.
- *Εὐρὺς τοῖς γύροις, τῇ καταβάσει πρᾶος* ‘wide in your turns, gentle in your descent’ changes the agreement of *le rote larghe e lo scender sia poco*. Both Greek adjectives are nominative masculine singular, predicated of the addressee, with ‘be’ understood. The things in respect of which he is to be wide and gentle stand in the dative, plural *γύροις* and singular *καταβάσει* (from *κατάβασις*). There is no conjunction, and the second phrase reverses the order of adjective and dative.
- *Σκόπει* (2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of contracted *σκοπέω*) with *περί* and genitive means ‘have regard for’, where *pensa* takes *la nuova soma* directly. *τοῦ νέου βάρους* is neuter singular (*βάρος*, gen. *βάρους*); its relative *ὃ* is accusative as object of *φέρεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *φέρω*). Thus *che tu hai* becomes ‘which you carry’.

> 100 Come la navicella esce del loco  
> 101 in dietro in dietro, sì quindi si tolse;  

As the bark goes from its station backwards, backwards, so the _monster_ took himself from thence;

> 100 Ὡς τἀκάτιον ἐξέρχεται τῆς ὄχθης  
> (As a little boat goes out from the shore)
>
> 101 Παλιμπρυμνηδόν, οὕτως ἀπῆρε, κᾆτα  
> (Stern foremost, so it departed, and then)

- *Ὡς τἀκάτιον ἐξέρχεται τῆς ὄχθης* ‘as the little boat goes out from the bank’ retains the diminutive of *navicella*. *τἀκάτιον* is crasis of *τὸ ἀκάτιον*, nom. neut. sg.; *ἐξέρχεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *ἐξέρχομαι*, active in meaning) takes a genitive of separation. The unspecified *loco* becomes specifically *ὄχθη* ‘bank’.
- *Παλιμπρυμνηδόν* ‘stern foremost’ is one nautical adverb for repeated *in dietro in dietro*. Correlative *ὡς … οὕτως* answers to *come … sì*; *ἀπῆρε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀπαίρω*, intransitive ‘put off’) needs neither a reflexive for *si* nor an adverb for *quindi*. Greek, like Italian, leaves the subject unnamed. The line already ends with *κᾆτα*, crasis of *καὶ εἶτα* ‘and then’, introducing the next movement.

> 102 e poi ch' al tutto si sentì a giuoco,  
> 103 là ov' era il petto, la coda rivolse,  
> 104 e quella tesa, come anguilla, mosse,  
> 105 e con le branche l' aria sè raccolse.  

and when he felt himself quite loose, there where his breast had been he turned his tail, and stretching moved it, like an eel, and with his paws gathered the air to him.

> 102 Ὅλος ἐσαλεύετο, καὶ πρὸς τὸ στῆθος  
> (It rocked wholly, and toward its breast)
>
> 103 Τὴν οὐρὰν στρέψας, αὐτὴν ἐντεταμένην  
> (Turning its tail, that tail outstretched)
>
> 104 Ἐκίνησ᾽ ὡς ἔγχελυν, καὶ τὸν ἀέρα  
> (It moved like an eel, and the air)
>
> 105 Τοῖς βραχίοσιν εἰς ἑαυτὸν ἐδέχθη.  
> (With its arms drew toward itself.)

- *Ὅλος ἐσαλεύετο* ‘he rocked bodily’ is an independent assertion after *κᾆτα*, not a temporal clause like *poi ch’ al tutto si sentì a giuoco*. *ὅλος* is predicative nom. masc. sg., where Italian has adverbial *al tutto*; *ἐσαλεύετο* (3rd sg. imperf. mid./pass. ind. of *σαλεύω*) expresses the motion rather than the consciousness of being free to move.
- *πρὸς τὸ στῆθος τὴν οὐρὰν στρέψας* ‘having turned his tail toward his breast’ replaces *là ov’ era il petto*, a clause locating the breast in its former position, with *πρός* and an accusative of direction. Finite *rivolse* becomes *στρέψας* (nom. masc. sg., aor. act. participle of *στρέφω*), preparatory to *ἐκίνησ᾽*.
- *αὐτὴν ἐντεταμένην ἐκίνησ᾽ ὡς ἔγχελυν* ‘he moved it, stretched taut, like an eel’ closely follows *e quella tesa, come anguilla, mosse* but puts the comparison after the verb. *αὐτήν* resumes *οὐράν*; *ἐντεταμένην* (acc. fem. sg., perf. mid./pass. participle of *ἐντείνω*) describes its resulting state. *ἐκίνησ᾽* is elided *ἐκίνησε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *κινέω*); accusative *ἔγχελυν* (from *ἔγχελυς*) aligns the comparison with the tail.
- *τὸν ἀέρα τοῖς βραχίοσιν εἰς ἑαυτὸν ἐδέχθη* ‘with his arms he received the air toward himself’ fronts the accusative object. *βραχίοσιν* is an instrumental dative, with movable *ν* before a vowel: the *branche* become ‘arms’. *εἰς ἑαυτόν* makes the direction of *sè raccolse* explicit; *ἐδέχθη* is a passive-form aorist (3rd sg. ind. of *δέχομαι*) used here transitively in the sense ‘received’.

> 106 Maggior paura non credo che fosse,  
> 107 quando Feton abbandonò li freni,  
> 108 per che il ciel, come pare ancor, si cosse;  

Greater fear there was not, I believe, when Phaeton let loose the reins, whereby the sky, as yet appears, was burnt;—

> 106 Μείζονα φόβον οὐ νομίζω γενέσθαι,  
> (Greater fear I do not think there was,)
>
> 107 Ὅτε Φαέθων ἔλιπε τὰς ἡνίας,  
> (When Phaeton let go the reins,)
>
> 108 Κατεκάη δ᾽ οὐρανός, ὡς φαίνετ᾽ ἔτι·  
> (And the sky was burnt, as still appears;)

- *Μείζονα φόβον οὐ νομίζω γενέσθαι* ‘I do not think a greater fear arose’ keeps *maggior paura* emphatically first. Where *non credo che fosse* takes a finite subjunctive, *νομίζω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.) takes an accusative and infinitive: *μείζονα* (acc. masc. sg. comparative of *μέγας*) agrees with *φόβον*, subject of *γενέσθαι* (second aor. mid. infinitive of *γίγνομαι*).
- *ὅτε Φαέθων ἔλιπε τὰς ἡνίας* keeps the temporal clause and active verb: *ἔλιπε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λείπω*) means ‘left, let go’; *τὰς ἡνίας* is acc. fem. pl. of *ἡνία*.
- *Κατεκάη δ᾽ οὐρανός* ‘and the sky was burned’ coordinates the result with postpositive *δέ* rather than marking the causal connection of *per che*. *κατεκάη* is 3rd sg. second aor. pass. ind. of *κατακαίω*, with augment after the prefix, for Italian *si cosse*. *ὡς φαίνετ᾽ ἔτι* ‘as still appears’ moves the parenthesis to the end; *φαίνετ᾽* represents *φαίνεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *φαίνω*), with poetic elision of final *-αι*.

> 109 nè quando Icaro misero le reni  
> 110 sentì spennar per la scaldata cera,  
> 111 gridando il padre a lui: “Mala via tieni!” —  

nor when poor Icarus felt his loins unfeathering by the heating of the wax, his father crying to him, “An ill way thou goest!”—

> 109 Οὐδ᾽ ὅτ᾽ Ἴκαρος ὁ δύστηνος ὑπέστη  
> (Nor when wretched Icarus suffered)
>
> 110 Τὴν πτερῶν πτῶσιν, τοῦ κηροῦ θερμανθέντος,  
> (The falling of his wings, as the wax grew warm,)
>
> 111 Αὐτῷ δ᾽ ὁ πατὴρ ἔκραξεν, “Ἐπλανήθης! ...”  
> (And his father cried to him, “You have gone astray!...”)

- *Οὐδ᾽ ὅτ᾽* (elided *οὐδέ, ὅτε*) continues the negated comparison, as does *nè quando*. *Ἴκαρος ὁ δύστηνος* gives *Icaro misero* an articular descriptive epithet. *ὑπέστη* (3rd sg. root aor. act. ind. of *ὑφίστημι*) means ‘underwent’, with an event as its accusative object: *τὴν πτερῶν πτῶσιν* ‘the falling of feathers’. *πτερῶν* is the gen. pl. of *πτερόν*, subjective with the action noun *πτῶσις*. Neither *le reni* nor the distinction between feeling and undergoing the loss is retained.
- *τοῦ κηροῦ θερμανθέντος* is a genitive absolute, ‘when/because the wax was warmed’: *θερμανθέντος* (gen. masc. sg., aor. pass. participle of *θερμαίνω*) agrees with *κηροῦ*. It develops *per la scaldata cera*, a prepositional phrase with an attributive participle, into a circumstance of the falling.
- *Αὐτῷ δ᾽ ὁ πατὴρ ἔκραξεν* ‘and to him his father cried’ fronts the dative recipient for *a lui* and makes *gridando il padre* an independent clause. *ἔκραξεν* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *κράζω*. *Ἐπλανήθης* (2nd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *πλανάω*, intransitive in sense) sums up present *mala via tieni* as an accomplished error, ‘you went astray’, without a noun for the road.

> 112 che fu la mia, quando vidi ch'i' era  
> 113 nell' aer d'ogni parte, e vidi spenta  
> 114 ogni veduta, fuor che della fiera.  

than was mine, when I saw myself in the air on all sides, and saw extinguished every sight, save of the beast.

> 112 Ἢ φόβον, ὃν ᾐσθόμην, ὅτ᾽ ἐν ἀέρι  
> (Than the fear which I felt, when in the air)
>
> 113 Εἶδον ἐμαυτὸν ἠωρημένον ἄνευ  
> (I saw myself suspended without)
>
> 114 Ἄλλης που θέας ἐκτὸς τῆς τοῦ θηρίου.  
> (Any sight anywhere save that of the beast.)

- *Ἢ φόβον, ὃν ᾐσθόμην* ‘than the fear which I felt’ finally supplies the second term of *μείζονα … ἤ*. *φόβον* repeats the accusative of line 106, where *che fu la mia* uses a finite verb and a possessive standing for the noun. *ὃν* is the accusative object of *ᾐσθόμην* (1st sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *αἰσθάνομαι*, with temporal augment).
- *ὅτ᾽ ἐν ἀέρι εἶδον ἐμαυτὸν ἠωρημένον* ‘when in the air I saw myself suspended’ replaces *vidi ch’i’ era* with a verb of perception, reflexive accusative, and supplementary participle. *εἶδον* is here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*; *ἠωρημένον* (acc. masc. sg., perf. mid./pass. participle of *αἰωρέω*) agrees with *ἐμαυτόν*. Suspension is supplied; *d’ogni parte* has no direct equivalent.
- *ἄνευ ἄλλης που θέας ἐκτὸς τῆς τοῦ θηρίου* ‘without any other sight anywhere, apart from that of the beast’ drops the second *vidi* and the image in *spenta*. *ἄνευ* governs genitive *ἄλλης … θέας*; enclitic indefinite *που* is local, ‘anywhere’ in this negative context. *ἐκτός* likewise takes a genitive: feminine *τῆς* stands for *τῆς θέας*, with dependent *τοῦ θηρίου* specifying what is seen, as in *quella della fiera*.

> 115 Ella sen va nuotando lenta lenta;  
> 116 ruota e discende, ma non me n' accorgo  
> 117 se non ch'al viso di sotto mi venta.  

He goes on swimming slowly, slowly; wheels and descends; but I perceive it not, otherwise than by a wind upon my face and from below.

> 115 Οἴχεται νηχόμενον σχολῇ καὶ βάδην,  
> (It goes swimming leisurely and slowly,)
>
> 116 Καταβαίνει κυκληδόν· ἐμοὶ δ’ οὐκ ἄλλο,  
> (It descends in circles; and to me there was nothing else,)
>
> 117 Πλὴν ἄνεμος κατ’ ὄψιν καὶ κάτωθέν μου.  
> (Save wind against my face and from below me.)

- *Οἴχεται νηχόμενον* ‘it goes away swimming’ pairs *οἴχεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *οἴχομαι*) with a participle of manner, as *sen va* takes *nuotando*. *νηχόμενον* (pres. mid. participle of *νήχομαι*) is nominative neuter singular, referring back to *θηρίον*, rather than feminine as in *ella … lenta*. *σχολῇ* (adverbial dative of *σχολή*, ‘at leisure’) and *βάδην* (‘at a walking pace’) vary the wording of *lenta lenta*.
- *Καταβαίνει κυκληδόν* ‘it descends circularly’ keeps only the second of *ruota e discende* as a finite verb (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *καταβαίνω*); the first becomes an adverb of manner. *ἐμοὶ δ’ οὐκ ἄλλο* ‘but to me nothing else’ substitutes an elliptical dative of the perceiver for *ma non me n’accorgo*. There is no Greek verb expressing perception here.
- *πλὴν ἄνεμος* ‘except a wind’ completes the ellipse. *πλήν* introduces an exception with *ἄνεμος* in the nominative, not a genitive governed by a preposition. The Italian verb *venta* thus becomes a noun; *κατ’ ὄψιν* (*κατά* with acc. sg. of *ὄψις*) gives *al viso*. *καὶ κάτωθέν μου* ‘and from below me’ coordinates a further specification, with the source already expressed by adverbial *-θεν*; enclitic *μου* accounts for the additional final accent on *κάτωθέν*.

> 118 Io sentia già dalla man destra il gorgo  
> 119 far sotto noi un orribile stroscio;  

Already, on the right hand, I heard the whirlpool make a hideous roaring under us;

> 118 Ἤκουον ἤδη χείμαῤῥον δεξιόθεν  
> (Already I heard a torrent on the right)
>
> 119 Ὑπ’ ἐμαυτὸν ποιοῦντα τρομερὸν φλοῖσβον·  
> (Beneath me making a terrible roar;)

- *Ἤκουον ἤδη* ‘I was already hearing’ retains the imperfect of *io sentia*: *ἤκουον* is here 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἀκούω*, with temporal augment. *χείμαῤῥον* (acc. masc. sg. of *χείμαρρος*) interprets *gorgo* as a rushing torrent rather than an eddy; *-ῤῥ-* is the traditional spelling marking smooth and rough breathings on successive rhos. *δεξιόθεν* ‘from the right’ renders *dalla man destra* by an adverb, again with *-θεν*, without a word for ‘hand’.
- *Ὑπ’ ἐμαυτὸν ποιοῦντα τρομερὸν φλοῖσβον* replaces *far … un orribile stroscio* with a supplementary participle after *ἤκουον*: *ποιοῦντα* (acc. masc. sg., pres. act. participle of *ποιέω*) agrees with *χείμαῤῥον* and takes *τρομερὸν φλοῖσβον* ‘a fearful rushing noise’ as object. *ὑπ’ ἐμαυτόν* (elided *ὑπό* with reflexive accusative) locates the sound beneath the speaker alone, changing plural *sotto noi* to singular.

> 120 per che con gli occhi in giù la testa sporgo.  

whereat, with eyes downwards, my head I stretched.

> 120 Διὸ κεφαλὴν κλίνας ἔβλεπον κάτω.  
> (Wherefore bending my head I looked downward.)

- *Διό* ‘for which reason’ is the connective corresponding to *per che*. *κεφαλὴν κλίνας* ‘having bent my head’ makes the main action of *la testa sporgo* preliminary: *κλίνας* is nom. masc. sg., aor. act. participle of *κλίνω*, with bare accusative *κεφαλήν* and the possessor understood. Bending replaces stretching out.
- *ἔβλεπον κάτω* ‘I was looking down’ makes the direction of the eyes, *con gli occhi in giù*, the main predication. *ἔβλεπον* is here 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *βλέπω*, not a historical present like *sporgo*; *κάτω* supplies the direction without a noun for the eyes.

## Lines 121–136 — The Wheeling Descent and Geryon’s Departure

> 121 Allor fu’ io più timido allo scoscio :  
> 122 però ch’ io vidi fochi e sentii pianti,  
> 123 ond’ io tremando tutto mi raccoscio.  

Then was I more timorous [as regards dismounting] : for I saw fires and heard lamentings, so that I cower all trembling.

> 121 Τότ’ ἤμην πλέον ἔμφοβος ἐκ τῆς θέας  
> (Then was I more terrified at the sight)
>
> 122 Τοῦ κρημνοῦ, φλόγας βλέπων, στόνους τ’ ἀκούων·  
> (Of the precipice, seeing flames and hearing groans;)
>
> 123 Ὅλος δ’ ἔντρομος εἰς ἐμαυτὸν συνῆλθον·  
> (And all trembling I drew myself together;)

- *Τότ’ ἤμην πλέον ἔμφοβος* ‘then I was more afraid’ keeps the order of *Allor fu’ io più timido*, but leaves the subject unexpressed. *Τότ’* is *τότε* with elision before the vowel of *ἤμην*. The latter is the later Greek first-person singular imperfect indicative of *εἰμί* (classical Attic *ἦ*); it makes the state of fear durative. *Πλέον*, the neuter singular comparative of *πολύς* used adverbially, modifies the predicate adjective *ἔμφοβος*, much as *più* modifies *timido*.
- *ἐκ τῆς θέας τοῦ κρημνοῦ* ‘from the sight of the precipice’ interprets *allo scoscio* quite differently from “[as regards dismounting]”. *Ἐκ* with the genitive expresses the source of the fear; *τοῦ κρημνοῦ* is an objective genitive dependent on *θέας*: the precipice is what is seen. The Greek carries the phrase over into line 122.
- *φλόγας βλέπων, στόνους τ’ ἀκούων* ‘seeing flames and hearing groans’ replaces the explicit cause *però ch’* and the finite past verbs *vidi* and *sentii* with two circumstantial present active participles, nominative masculine singular of *βλέπω* and *ἀκούω*, agreeing with the subject of *ἤμην*. They present the perceptions as concurrent with the fear. Each accusative object now precedes its participle; *φλόγας* specifies flames rather than *fochi*, and *στόνους* groans rather than *pianti*. The connective *τ’* is postpositive *τε* with elision.
- *ὅλος δ’ ἔντρομος* ‘and I, trembling all over’ brings *tutto* forward as *ὅλος* and replaces the gerund *tremando* with a second nominative adjective, *ἔντρομος*. Both describe the subject. Elided *δέ* connects the clauses without explicitly marking consequence as *ond’* does. *Εἰς ἐμαυτὸν συνῆλθον* ‘I drew together into myself’ renders *mi raccoscio* by a prepositional phrase with the masculine singular reflexive and an intransitive verb. *Συνῆλθον* is first-person singular second aorist active indicative of *συνέρχομαι*, with the augment after the prefix; it replaces Dante’s vivid present with a completed movement.

> 124 E vidi poi, chè nol vedea davanti,  
> 125 lo scendere e il girar per li gran mali  
> 126 che s'appressavan da diversi canti.  

And then I saw—[for] I had not seen [it] before—the sinking and the wheeling, through the great evils which drew near on diverse sides.

> 124 Ὅ,τι γὰρ οὐκ ἔβλεπον πρίν, εἶδον τότε,  
> (For what I did not see before, I saw then,)
>
> 125 Τὴν γυροειδῆ κατάβασιν, τοσούτων  
> (The circling descent, as so many)
>
> 126 Δεινῶν πάντοθεν ἡμῖν προσεγγιζόντων.  
> (Terrors drew near to us on every side.)

- *Ὅ,τι γὰρ οὐκ ἔβλεπον πρίν, εἶδον τότε* ‘for what I did not see before, I saw then’ reverses the order of *E vidi poi, chè nol vedea davanti*. *Ὅ,τι* is the accusative neuter singular of the indefinite relative *ὅστις* (distinguished in writing from the conjunction *ὅτι*). It is the object of *ἔβλεπον*, and the whole free relative is the object of *εἶδον*. Thus the object represented by *lo* in *nol* is built into the relative; *γάρ*, though placed second, connects the whole statement to the preceding one rather than introducing a parenthesis after ‘I saw’.
- *Ἔβλεπον* is first-person singular imperfect active indicative of *βλέπω*, negated by *οὐκ* before the vowel; *εἶδον* is the first-person singular second aorist active indicative used with *ὁράω*. The contrast of ongoing non-perception with an attained perception answers to *vedea* against *vidi*. *Πρίν* and *τότε* are both adverbs here, set against one another at the ends of their clauses.
- *τὴν γυροειδῆ κατάβασιν* ‘the circling descent’, accusative singular in apposition to what was seen, fuses the two substantivized infinitives *lo scendere e il girar*. Descent becomes the noun; wheeling becomes its attribute, *γυροειδῆ*, the contracted feminine accusative singular of *γυροειδής*.
- *τοσούτων δεινῶν πάντοθεν ἡμῖν προσεγγιζόντων* ‘as so many terrors approached us from every side’ replaces *per li gran mali che s’appressavan* with a genitive absolute. *Δεινῶν* is substantival, genitive neuter plural of *δεινός*; *τοσούτων* stresses number rather than the greatness expressed by *gran*. *Προσεγγιζόντων*, present active participle of *προσεγγίζω*, agrees with *δεινῶν* and supplies the simultaneous circumstance; there is no separate equivalent of *per*. The verb takes the supplied dative *ἡμῖν* for those approached. *Πάντοθεν*, with the suffix of motion from a place, turns *da diversi canti* into ‘from all sides’.

> 127 Come il falcon ch’ è stato assai sull’ali,  
> 128 che senza veder logoro o uccello,  
> 129 fa dire al falconiere : “ Oimè tu cali!”—  

As the falcon, that has been long upon his wings—that, without seeing bird or lure, makes the falconer cry, “Ah, ah! thou stoopest”—

> 127 Ὡς δ’ ἱέραξ, ὃς μακρὸν ἐπὶ πτερύγων  
> (And as a falcon, which long upon the wing)
>
> 128 Στάς, καὶ μηδὲν δέλεαρ ἰδὼν μήτ’ ὄρνιν,  
> (Having stayed, and seeing neither lure nor bird,)
>
> 129 Ὥστ’ ὀρνιθευτὴν λέγειν, ‘Φεῦ! καταβαίνεις!’  
> (So that the falconer says, ‘Alas! you are coming down!’)

- *Ὡς δ’ ἱέραξ* ‘and as a falcon’ opens the comparison answered by *οὕτω* in line 133. *Δ’* is elided *δέ*; the Greek has no article to match generic *il*. *Ὃς* is the nominative masculine singular relative referring to *ἱέραξ*: unlike *che* in *ch’ è stato*, it has no finite verb until *ἐπανέρχετ’* in line 130.
- *μακρὸν ἐπὶ πτερύγων στάς* ‘having remained long upon wings’ recasts *è stato assai sull’ali*. *Μακρόν* is an adverbial accusative of extent in time, *ἐπί* with genitive plural *πτερύγων* (from *πτέρυξ*) localizes the stay, and *στάς* is the nominative masculine singular participle of the intransitive second aorist of *ἵστημι*. It gathers the long stay into one circumstance preceding the return.
- *καὶ μηδὲν δέλεαρ ἰδὼν μήτ’ ὄρνιν* ‘and having seen no lure nor bird’ adds a second participle agreeing with *ὃς*. *Ἰδών* is the second aorist active participle corresponding to *εἶδον*. For *senza veder*, Greek puts the negative in the object phrase: *μηδέν*, accusative neuter singular of *μηδείς*, qualifies *δέλεαρ*, and *μήτ’* is *μήτε* elided before *ὄρνιν* (accusative singular of *ὄρνις*). The order is Dante’s, *logoro o uccello*, not the English “bird or lure”.
- *ὥστ’ ὀρνιθευτὴν λέγειν* ‘so as to make the fowler say’ converts *fa dire al falconiere* into a result clause: elided *ὥστε* takes the present active infinitive *λέγειν*, with *ὀρνιθευτήν* as its accusative subject, not an indirect object. The Greek noun denotes a catcher of birds rather than specifically a falconer. *Φεῦ* gives *Oimè* a single interjection; *καταβαίνεις* ‘you are coming down’ is second-person singular present active indicative of *καταβαίνω*, with no expressed *tu*.

> 130 discende lasso, onde si mosse snello,  
> 131 per cento rote, e da lungi si pone  
> 132 dal suo maestro, disdegnoso e fello :  

descends weary; then swiftly moves himself with many a circle, and far from his master sets himself disdainful and sullen

> 130 Ἔγκοπος ἐπανέρχετ’ ἔνθεν ἀπέπτη,  
> (Weary returns whence it flew up,)
>
> 131 Δι’ ἑλιγμῶν ἑκατόν, καὶ τοῦ κυρίου  
> (Through a hundred wheelings, and from the master)
>
> 132 Αὐτοῦ μακρὰν σκυθρωπὸς κἀπειθὴς μένει·  
> (Of it remains afar, sullen and disobedient;)

- *ἔγκοπος ἐπανέρχετ’ ἔνθεν ἀπέπτη* ‘weary, it returns whence it flew off’ takes *onde si mosse snello* as identifying the place to which the falcon descends, not as a fresh movement after the descent. Predicate *ἔγκοπος* represents *lasso*; *ἐπανέρχετ’* is *ἐπανέρχεται*, third-person singular present middle indicative of *ἐπανέρχομαι*, with the final *-αι* elided in poetic usage. It expresses return rather than simply downward motion. Relative adverb *ἔνθεν* introduces the point of departure; *ἀπέπτη* is third-person singular intransitive root aorist active indicative of *ἀποπέτομαι*, augmented after *ἀπο-*. The flight is explicit, but *snello* has no separate equivalent.
- *δι’ ἑλιγμῶν ἑκατόν* ‘through a hundred turns’ qualifies the return. *Δι’* is elided *διά* with the genitive plural of *ἑλιγμός*, and indeclinable *ἑκατόν* retains the precise numeral of *per cento rote*.
- *καὶ τοῦ κυρίου αὐτοῦ μακράν* ‘and far from its master’ places the genitive of separation before *μακράν*, an adverbial accusative. *Αὐτοῦ* is a possessive genitive dependent on *κυρίου*. Where *si pone* expresses taking up a position, *μένει*, third-person singular present active indicative of *μένω*, expresses remaining in it.
- *σκυθρωπὸς κἀπειθής* ‘sullen and disobedient’ are predicate adjectives agreeing with the falcon. They recast *disdegnoso e fello* as a look or temper and a refusal of obedience. *Κἀπειθής* is crasis of *καὶ ἀπειθής*; the initial words coalesce rather than merely losing a final vowel before a separate word.

> 133 così ne pose al fondo Gerione  
> 134 a piè a piè della stagliata rocca,  

: so at the bottom Geryon set us, close to the foot of the ragged rock;

> 133 Οὕτω Γηρυὼν ἐν τῷ βυθῷ κατῆλθε  
> (So Geryon descended into the depth)
>
> 134 Παρὰ τοὺς πόδας τῆς ἀποκρήμνου πέτρας.  
> (Close to the foot of the sheer rock.)

- *Οὕτω Γηρυὼν ἐν τῷ βυθῷ κατῆλθε* ‘so Geryon descended in the depth’ answers *ὡς* with *οὕτω*, as *così* answers *Come*. The Greek puts the nominative subject *Γηρυών* before its verb and changes *ne pose* into intransitive *κατῆλθε*, third-person singular second aorist active indicative of *κατέρχομαι*, with internal augment. The setting down of *us* is deferred to line 135. *Ἐν τῷ βυθῷ*, with the locative dative, describes the place attained rather than marking *al fondo* by a construction of motion into it.
- *παρὰ τοὺς πόδας τῆς ἀποκρήμνου πέτρας* ‘down beside the feet of the precipitous rock’ further defines the endpoint. *Παρά* with the accusative fits the motion of *κατῆλθε*; plural *πόδας* (from *πούς*) replaces the emphatic repetition *a piè a piè*. *Ἀποκρήμνου* is the feminine genitive singular of the two-termination adjective *ἀπόκρημνος*, agreeing with *πέτρας*: the emphasis in *stagliata* falls on the rock’s sheer face.

> 135 e, discarcate le nostre persone,  
> 136 si dileguò come da corda cocca.  

and, from our weight relieved, he bounded off like an arrow from the string.

> 135 Ἐκεῖθι δ’ ἡμᾶς ἀποθέμενος ἄμφω  
> (And there having set us both down)
>
> 136 ᾬχετο τάχος, ὡς ἐκ χορδῆς τὸ βέλος.  
> (He sped swiftly away, like an arrow from the bowstring.)

- *Ἐκεῖθι δ’ ἡμᾶς ἀποθέμενος ἄμφω* ‘and there, having set us both down’ supplies poetic locative *ἐκεῖθι* ‘there’ and links the action by elided *δέ*. Dante’s absolute participial phrase *discarcate le nostre persone* has the persons unloaded as its grammatical subject. In Greek it is Geryon who acts: *ἀποθέμενος*, nominative masculine singular second aorist middle participle of *ἀποτίθημι*, agrees with the subject of the following verb and takes *ἡμᾶς* as its direct object. The participle is unaugmented, and its action precedes the departure. *Ἄμφω* ‘both’, the nominative-accusative dual numeral, stands in apposition to the plural pronoun; neither *persone* nor the notion of weight is separately expressed.
- *ᾤχετο τάχος* ‘he was gone in haste’ renders *si dileguò* without an active verb of vanishing. *ᾬχετο* is third-person singular imperfect middle indicative of *οἴχομαι*, with temporal augment (*οι-* becoming *ῳ-*); this imperfect can express the accomplished departure, ‘was gone’. *Τάχος*, neuter singular accusative used adverbially, makes the speed explicit.
- *ὡς ἐκ χορδῆς τὸ βέλος* ‘as the arrow from a string’ leaves the arrow’s verb of motion understood. *Ἐκ* with genitive singular *χορδῆς* gives the starting point, as does *da corda*. The anarthrous prepositional phrase precedes the subject *τὸ βέλος*, retaining Dante’s final placement of *cocca* but substituting the whole missile for its nock.

## Summary — From Clause to Participle

Musurus often keeps Dante’s order of perception while changing which action gets the finite verb. In *sen venne, ed arrivò*, Italian asserts two successive actions; Greek retains the coming as *ἦλθε* ‘came’ and makes the extension of the head and chest simultaneous through *προτείνουσα* ‘stretching forward’. Elsewhere the choice of participle marks a different relation: *καταβάντες* ‘having descended’ makes the descent prior to the walking, while *ὀψόμενος* ‘in order to see’ turns the second command of *va, e vedi* into the purpose of the first. After verbs of perception the participle is especially useful: for *vidi ch’i’ era* Musurus gives *εἶδον ἐμαυτὸν ἠωρημένον* ‘I saw myself suspended’, making the observer himself the object seen.

Greek cases and particles sometimes follow the Italian phrasing closely. Repeated *ἰδού* preserves repeated *ecco*, and *ὡς … οὕτω* answers to *come … così*. Yet even a small connective can change the picture: *τε … καί* ‘both … and’ twice replaces an Italian alternation, first *quando … quando*, then *or … or*. A change of case can shift the emphasis just as economically. Where *le rote larghe* describes the turns themselves, *εὐρὺς τοῖς γύροις* ‘wide in your turns’ predicates the adjective of the addressee; the dative specifies the respect in which it applies. At other points Musurus chooses a single precise word over Dante’s repetition: *παλιμπρυμνηδόν* ‘stern foremost’ gives *in dietro in dietro* a specifically nautical direction.

The lexical departures range from a change of sensory emphasis to a choice between distinct readings. *Μολῦνον* ‘defiling’ conveys the force of *appuzza* without its smell; *γάλακτος* ‘than milk’ preserves the comparison of whiteness but replaces *burro*. More consequentially, *τρίῤῥυγχον* ‘three-beaked’ takes *becchi* as beaks rather than the goats of the English prose. Musurus need not be the freer translator, however: in the final comparison *ἔνθεν* ‘whence’ preserves the local force of *onde*, and *ἑκατόν* ‘a hundred’ keeps the number in *cento rote* where the prose gives only ‘many a circle’. Such details qualify the impression made by the frequent syntactic recasting: Musurus may reorganize a whole clause while preserving the force of a single adverb or numeral.
