# Inferno 8

## Lines 1–24 — The Signal Fires and Phlegyas’ Boat

> 1 Io dico seguitando, ch' assai prima  
> 2 che noi fussimo al piè dell' alta torre,  

I say continuing, that, long before we reached the foot of the high tower,

> 1 Λέγω καθεξῆς, ὅτι, πρὶν ἀφικέσθαι  
> (I say, continuing, that, before we arrived)
>
> 2 Εἰς τοὺς πόδας ὑψηλοῦ πύργου δὴ ᾽κείνου,  
> (At the foot of that high tower,)

- *Λέγω καθεξῆς, ὅτι* follows the order of *Io dico seguitando, ch’*, but leaves the pronoun to be understood from *λέγω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.) and replaces the gerund *seguitando* with the adverb *καθεξῆς*, ‘in sequence’. *ὅτι* introduces what is said.
- *πρὶν ἀφικέσθαι* means ‘before arriving’: after an affirmative main assertion Greek can express the subsequent event by *πρίν* and the infinitive. *ἀφικέσθαι* is the second aorist middle infinitive of *ἀφικνέομαι*; its unexpressed subject is the ‘we’ of *εἴδομεν* in line 4. It replaces the finite imperfect subjunctive *fussimo* and makes arrival explicit; there is nothing corresponding to *assai*.
- *εἰς τοὺς πόδας* gives the destination in the accusative and pluralizes *al piè*. In *ὑψηλοῦ πύργου δὴ ᾽κείνου* all three inflected words are genitive masculine singular, dependent on *πόδας*. The demonstrative adds ‘that’ to *dell’ alta torre*; *δή* lends emphasis, and *᾽κείνου* is *ἐκείνου* with prodelision of its initial vowel.

> 3 gli occhi nostri n' andar suso alla cima,  

our eyes went upwards to its summit,

> 3 Πρὸς τὴν ὀροφὴν ἀντείναντες τὰς ὄψεις,  
> (Raising our eyes toward its summit,)

- *Πρὸς τὴν ὀροφὴν ἀντείναντες τὰς ὄψεις* is literally ‘having raised our eyes toward the roof’. Dante makes *gli occhi nostri* the subject of *andar*; Musurus makes the observers the subject and *τὰς ὄψεις* (acc. pl. of *ὄψις*, ‘sight, eye’) their object. The article suffices for the possessive.
- *ἀντείναντες* is the syncopated form of *ἀνατείναντες* (nom. masc. pl., aor. act. participle of *ἀνατείνω*). It anticipates *εἴδομεν*: the upward movement expressed by *suso* is now preparatory to the main act of seeing. *πρός* with the accusative marks its direction; *ὀροφή*, ‘roof’, makes *cima* more specific.

> 4 per due fiammette che i vedemmo porre,  
> 5 e un' altra da lungi render cenno  
> 6 tanto, che a pena il potea l' occhio torre.  

because of two flamelets, that we saw put there, and another from far give signal back, so far that the eye could scarcely catch it.

> 4 Εἴδομεν δύο φαινούσας μικρὰς φλόγας,  
> (We saw two small flames shining,)
>
> 5 Ἄλλην τ᾽ ἀμειβομένην πρὸς τὸ σημεῖον,  
> (And another answering to the signal,)
>
> 6 Οὕτω μακρόθεν, ὥστ᾽ οὐκ ἦν διακρῖναι.  
> (From so far off that it was impossible to discern it.)

- *Εἴδομεν* (1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*) becomes the main verb. The causal *per* disappears, as do the placing of the lights in *porre* and the locative *i*. Instead *δύο φαινούσας μικρὰς φλόγας* gives the object and a participle of perception: ‘we saw two small flames shining’. *φαινούσας* (acc. fem. pl., pres. act. of *φαίνω*, here intransitive) presents what was visible at the time. *μικράς* unpacks the diminutive *fiammette*; the noun and its modifiers are plural, not dual, after *δύο*.
- *Ἄλλην τ᾽ ἀμειβομένην* adds a second object to the same verb: ‘and another [flame] answering’. *τ᾽* is postpositive *τε* elided before a vowel; *ἀμειβομένην* is the acc. fem. sg. present middle participle of *ἀμείβομαι*, ‘answer in turn’. Thus *render cenno* becomes a response *πρὸς τὸ σημεῖον*, ‘to the signal’, rather than the giving of a signal.
- *οὕτω μακρόθεν* combines *da lungi* and *tanto*: the adverb in *-θεν* means ‘from afar’. Elided *ὥστ᾽* (*ὥστε*) introduces an actual result with finite *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*). Impersonal *οὐκ ἦν διακρῖναι*, ‘it was not possible to distinguish [it]’, uses the aorist active infinitive of *διακρίνω*. It omits *l’ occhio* as subject and changes the qualified difficulty of *a pena il potea* into a negative.

> 7 Ed io mi volsi al mar di tutto il senno;  
> 8 dissi: "Questo che dice? e che risponde  
> 9 quell' altro foco? e chi son quei che il fenno?"  

And I turn[ed] to the Sea of all intelligence; [I] said: “What says this? and what replies yon other fire? And who are they that made it?”

> 7 Στραφεὶς δὲ πρὸς τὴν πηγὴν πάσης σοφίας,  
> (And turning toward the fount of all wisdom,)
>
> 8 Εἶπον· “Τί δηλοῖ τοῦτο; τί δέ γε τἆλλο  
> (I said: "What does this signify? And what does the other)
>
> 9 „Πῦρ ἀνταποκρίνεται ; τίνες δ᾽ οἱ δρῶντες ;”  
> (Fire reply? And who are they that do this?")

- *Στραφεὶς δέ* (nom. masc. sg., second aor. pass. participle of *στρέφω*) has intransitive force, ‘having turned’. It subordinates *mi volsi* to *εἶπον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind., ‘I said’); postpositive *δέ* carries the transition *Ed*. *πρὸς τὴν πηγὴν πάσης σοφίας* preserves the direction and the dependent genitive but changes *mar* to ‘fount’ and *tutto il senno* to ‘all wisdom’.
- *Τί δηλοῖ τοῦτο;* keeps *τοῦτο* as subject and interrogative *τί* as object, but interprets *dice* as ‘indicates’ (*δηλοῖ*, contracted 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *δηλόω*). In *τί δέ γε τἆλλο πῦρ ἀνταποκρίνεται;* the particles shift attention to the answering fire: ‘and what, for its part, does the other fire answer back?’ *τἆλλο* is crasis of *τὸ ἄλλο*, nom. neut. sg. with *πῦρ*; it does not reproduce the distance implied in *quell’*. *ἀνταποκρίνεται* is 3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *ἀνταποκρίνομαι*.
- *τίνες δ᾽ οἱ δρῶντες;* suppresses the copula and replaces *quei che il fenno* with an articular participle. *δρῶντες* (nom. masc. pl., contracted pres. act. participle of *δράω*) means ‘those doing [it]’, not ‘those who made it’; the object too is left implicit. *δ᾽* is elided *δέ*.

> 10 Ed egli a me: "Su per le sucide onde  
> 11 già puoi scorgere quello che s' aspetta,  
> 12 se il fummo del pantan nol ti nasconde."  

And he to me: “Over the squalid waves, already thou mayest discern what is expected, if the vapour of the fen conceal it not from thee.”

> 10 Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπεν· “Ἐπὶ τῶν ῥυπαρῶν κυμάτων  
> (And he said: "Upon the filthy waves)
>
> 11 „Τί συμβήσεται, δύνασ᾽ ἰδεῖν αὐτίκα,  
> (What is about to happen, you can see straightway,)
>
> 12 „Ἢν μή γ᾽ ἕλους ὁ καπνὸς ταῦτ᾽ ἀποκρύψῃ.”  
> (Unless the smoke of the marsh conceal these things.")

- *Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπεν* uses pronominal *ὁ* with *δέ*, ‘and he’, and supplies a verb (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind.) for *Ed egli a me*, dropping the recipient. *ἐπὶ τῶν ῥυπαρῶν κυμάτων* renders *Su per le sucide onde* by local *ἐπί* with the genitive plural, ‘upon the filthy waves’.
- *τί συμβήσεται* is an indirect question placed before its governing verb: ‘what will happen’, rather than *quello che s’ aspetta*. *συμβήσεται* is the 3rd sg. future middle indicative of *συμβαίνω*, with active sense. *δύνασ᾽ ἰδεῖν* renders *puoi scorgere*: *δύνασ᾽* is *δύνασαι* (2nd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *δύναμαι*) with final *-αι* elided; *ἰδεῖν* is the second aorist active infinitive of *ὁράω*. Postposed *αὐτίκα*, ‘straightway’, shifts the emphasis of *già* from present availability to immediacy.
- *Ἢν μή γ᾽* is conditional *ἤν* (a contraction equivalent to *ἐάν*), negative *μή*, and emphatic *γε* elided before *ἕλους*. The prospective condition takes *ἀποκρύψῃ* (3rd sg. aor. act. subj. of *ἀποκρύπτω*), where Italian has indicative *nasconde*. In *ἕλους ὁ καπνός*, ‘the smoke of a marsh’, the dependent genitive singular of *ἕλος* precedes the subject. *ταῦτ᾽* is elided *ταῦτα*, acc. neut. pl., ‘these things’: it replaces the singular object in *nol*; *ti* has no counterpart.

> 13 Corda non pinse mai da se saetta,  
> 14 che sì corresse via per l' aer snella,  

Never did cord impel from itself an arrow, that ran through the air so quickly,

> 13 Οὔποτε τόξον ἐξηκόντισε βέλος  
> (Never did a bow shoot forth an arrow)
>
> 14 Ἀέρα διασχίζον οὕτως ὠκέως,  
> (Cleaving the air so swiftly,)

- *Οὔποτε τόξον ἐξηκόντισε βέλος* preserves the emphatic negative, but substitutes the bow, *τόξον* (nom. neut. sg.), for its *corda*. *ἐξηκόντισε* is the 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐξακοντίζω*, ‘shoot forth’, with the temporal augment after the preverb. The compound conveys outward propulsion without a separate phrase for *da se*.
- *ἀέρα διασχίζον* replaces the relative clause *che sì corresse via per l’ aer*: *διασχίζον* (acc. neut. sg., pres. act. participle of *διασχίζω*) agrees with *βέλος*, the object of ‘shot’, and governs *ἀέρα* (acc. sg. of *ἀήρ*), ‘cleaving the air’. The arrow’s motion becomes simultaneous description, not a finite subjunctive. *οὕτως ὠκέως*, ‘so swiftly’, replaces adjectival *snella* with a poetic adverb from *ὠκύς* and prepares the comparison.

> 15 com' io vidi una nave piccioletta  
> 16 venir per l' acqua verso noi in quella,  

as a little bark which I saw come towards us then [through the water],

> 15 Ὅπως μικρόν τι σκάφος, ὅπερ τότ᾽ εἶδον  
> (As a small skiff, which I then saw)
>
> 16 Πρὸς ἡμᾶς ἐρχόμενον ἐπὶ κυμάτων,  
> (Coming toward us upon the waves,)

- *Ὅπως μικρόν τι σκάφος*, ‘as a certain small craft’, answers *οὕτως*. *ὅπως* is comparative, not purposive; the Greek follows the sense of *sì … com’* rather than the more usual Greek pairing of *οὕτως* with *ὡς*. The skiff itself is named as the point of comparison before the act of seeing is introduced. Enclitic indefinite *τι* and adjective *μικρόν* distribute between them the indefiniteness and diminutive force of *una nave piccioletta*.
- *ὅπερ τότ᾽ εἶδον* makes *com’ io vidi una nave* into a relative clause: *ὅπερ* is the strengthened relative (acc. neut. sg., object of 1st sg. *εἶδον*), and *τότ᾽* is *τότε* elided, rendering *in quella*. Greek uses the supplementary participle *ἐρχόμενον* (acc. neut. sg., pres. mid. of *ἔρχομαι*) with the verb of perception where Italian uses *venir*. *πρὸς ἡμᾶς* gives the destination first; *ἐπὶ κυμάτων*, ‘upon waves’, replaces *per l’ acqua*.

> 17 sotto il governo d'un sol galeoto,  
> 18 che gridava: "Or se' giunta, anima fella?"  

under the guidance of a single steersman, who cried : “Now art thou arrived, fell spirit?”

> 17 Κυβερνώμενον ὑφ᾽ ἑνὸς μόνου ναύτου  
> (Steered by one single sailor)
>
> 18 Κράζοντος· “Νῦν ὧδ᾽ ἦλθες, ψυχὴ κακόφρων!”  
> (Crying: "Now you have come hither, wicked soul!")

- *Κυβερνώμενον* (acc. neut. sg., contracted pres. pass. participle of *κυβερνάω*) continues the description of *σκάφος*. The noun phrase *sotto il governo* becomes ‘being steered’, with personal agent *ὑφ᾽ ἑνὸς μόνου ναύτου*. *ὑφ᾽* is elided *ὑπό*, its consonant aspirated before rough-breathed *ἑνός*. The numeral (gen. masc. sg. of *εἷς*) and *μόνου* preserve the double specification in *d’un sol*; *ναύτου* (gen. sg. of *ναύτης*) means ‘sailor’, the steering already expressed by the participle.
- *Κράζοντος* (gen. masc. sg., pres. act. participle of *κράζω*) agrees with *ναύτου*, not a genitive absolute: it turns *che gridava* into a simultaneous action of the agent. *Νῦν ὧδ᾽ ἦλθες* supplies ‘hither’ (*ὧδε*, elided) and replaces *se’ giunta* with a simple second aorist (2nd sg. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*). Musurus punctuates the utterance as an exclamation rather than a question. *ψυχὴ κακόφρων* is nominative in form used in address; the compound adjective, ‘evil-minded’, qualifies *ψυχή* in place of *fella*.

> 19 "Flegias, Flegias, tu gridi a voto,"  
> 20 disse lo mio signore, "a questa volta;  
> 21 più non ci avrai, che sol passando il loto."  

“Phlegyas, Phlegyas,” said my Lord, “this time thou criest in vain; thou shalt not have us longer than while we pass the wash.”

> 19 —“Ὦ Φλεγύα, Φλεγύα, μάτην κραυγάζεις  
> (—"O Phlegyas, Phlegyas, in vain you shout)
>
> 20 „Ὡς πρὸς τὸ παρόν·” εἶπεν οὑμὸς Προστάτης·  
> (As for the present," said my Protector;)
>
> 21 „Οὐχ ἕξεις ἡμᾶς πλὴν ὡς πέραν τῆς λίμνης.” —  
> ("You shall not have us except as far as across the lake."—)

- *Ὦ Φλεγύα, Φλεγύα* adds the particle of address and repeats the vocative singular of *Φλεγύας*. *μάτην κραυγάζεις* sets ‘in vain’ before the verb (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *κραυγάζω*), reversing *tu gridi a voto* and dispensing with the subject pronoun.
- *ὡς πρὸς τὸ παρόν* means ‘so far as concerns the present’: *πρός* governs the substantivized neuter singular present participle of *πάρειμι*, ‘be present’. It expands *a questa volta* and completes the qualification before *εἶπεν οὑμὸς Προστάτης*, whereas Dante inserts *disse lo mio signore* before the qualification. *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, nom. masc. sg.; *Προστάτης*, ‘protector’, interprets rather than directly reproduces *signore*.
- *Οὐχ ἕξεις ἡμᾶς* uses *οὐχ* before the rough breathing of *ἕξεις* (2nd sg. fut. act. ind. of *ἔχω*) and the explicit accusative pronoun for *non ci avrai*. *πλὴν ὡς πέραν τῆς λίμνης* restricts that future possession spatially, ‘except as far as the other side of the lake’. *πέραν* takes the genitive. The compressed *πλὴν ὡς* phrase answers Dante’s *più … che sol* without a comparative or a verb for *passando*; *λίμνη* replaces *loto*, ‘mud’.

> 22 Quale colui, che grande inganno ascolta,  
> 23 che gli sia fatto, e poi se ne rammarca,  

As one who listens to some great deceit which has been done to him, and then sore resents it:

> 22 Οἷος ὁ γνοὺς ἑαυτὸν ἀπατηθέντα  
> (Like one who learns that he has been deceived)
>
> 23 Σκληρῶς, εἶτα δὲ βαθέως δυσχεραίνει,  
> (Cruelly, and then deeply frets,)

- *Οἷος ὁ γνοὺς* begins a correlative comparison, ‘of such a sort as the man who has learned’. The article substantivizes *γνούς* (nom. masc. sg., root-aorist act. participle of *γιγνώσκω*). Learning replaces the specifically auditory *ascolta*, and the object of knowledge is no longer the noun *grande inganno*.
- *ἑαυτὸν ἀπατηθέντα* is a supplementary participial construction after *γνούς*: ‘that he himself had been deceived’. The reflexive accusative refers to the knower; *ἀπατηθέντα* (acc. masc. sg., aor. pass. participle of *ἀπατάω*) agrees with it. Where *che gli sia fatto* makes the deceit the subject of a finite passive subjunctive and the man its indirect object, Greek makes the man the subject of the passive participle. *σκληρῶς*, ‘harshly’, qualifies the deceiving in place of *grande* qualifying the deceit.
- *εἶτα δὲ βαθέως δυσχεραίνει* passes from the articular participle to a finite present (3rd sg. act. ind. of *δυσχεραίνω*, ‘be vexed, take it ill’). The loose change of construction retains Dante’s finite *e poi se ne rammarca*. *εἶτα δέ* marks the succession, *βαθέως* adds the degree, ‘deeply’, and there is no separate equivalent of *ne*.

> 24 fecesi Flegias nell' ira accolta.  

such grew Phlegyas in his gathered rage.

> 24 Τοιοῦτος ἦν Φλεγύας ὀργὴν δαμάσας.  
> (Such was Phlegyas, having curbed his wrath.)

- *Τοιοῦτος* (nom. masc. sg.), answering *οἷος*, supplies the predicate, ‘such’. *ἦν Φλεγύας* uses the imperfect indicative of *εἰμί*, ‘Phlegyas was’, for the change of state in *fecesi Flegias*.
- *ὀργὴν δαμάσας* gives *nell’ ira accolta* a different grammatical subject and force: the wrath is not qualified as ‘gathered’, but is the direct object of *δαμάσας* (nom. masc. sg., aor. act. participle of *δαμάζω*, ‘tame, subdue’). The participle agrees with Phlegyas and presents restraint as his own accomplished action, ‘having curbed his wrath’.

## Lines 25–57 — The Laden Skiff and the Accursed Spirit

> 25 Lo duca mio discese nella barca,  
> 26 e poi mi fece entrare appresso lui,  
> 27 e sol quand’ io fui dentro parve carca.  

My Guide descended into the skiff, and then made me enter after him; and not till I was in, did it seem laden.

> 25 Οὑμὸς δ᾽ Ἡγήτωρ ἐν τῷ σκάφει κατέβη,  
> (And my Guide stepped down into the skiff,)
>
> 26 Ἑαυτοῦ τ᾽ ἐγγὺς ἔπειτ᾽ εἰσήγαγέ με·  
> (And then brought me in beside himself;)
>
> 27 Μόνον δὲ μετ᾽ ἔμ᾽ ὁ λέμβος ἐπληρώθη.  
> (And only after me was the bark laden.)

- *Οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, the article and possessive adjective belonging to *Ἡγήτωρ*: Italian *Lo duca mio* puts the possessive last, Greek puts it first. Postpositive *δ᾽* (*δέ*) connects the sentence backwards. *Κατέβη* is the 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *καταβαίνω*. With this verb of motion, *ἐν τῷ σκάφει* (dat. sg. of neuter *σκάφος*) follows *nella barca* rather closely; *εἰς* with the accusative would distinguish motion into the skiff from position in it.
- *Ἑαυτοῦ τ᾽ ἐγγύς* means ‘and near himself’: *ἐγγύς* governs the genitive of the reflexive referring to the Guide. Thus *appresso lui* becomes spatial, ‘beside him’, while *ἔπειτ᾽* (*ἔπειτα*) conveys *poi*. Enclitic *τε* is elided before *ἐγγύς*. *Εἰσήγαγέ με* ‘he brought me in’ replaces causative *mi fece entrare* with the transitive compound *εἰσάγω* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind., with the reduplicated aorist of *ἄγω*); the final accent on the verb is due to enclitic *με*.
- *Μόνον δὲ μετ᾽ ἔμ᾽* ‘and only after me’ compresses *sol quand’ io fui dentro* into *μετά* with the accusative; both *μετά* and *ἐμέ* are elided. *Ὁ λέμβος* supplies an explicit subject, and *ἐπληρώθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *πληρόω*) says ‘was filled’, not *parve carca*, ‘seemed laden’. The change is from an appearance to an accomplished event.

> 28 Tosto che il duca ed io nel legno fui,  
> 29 secando se ne va l’ antica prora  
> 30 dell’ acqua più che non suol con altrui.  

Soon as my Guide and I were in the boat, its ancient prow went on, cutting more of the water than it is wont with others.

> 28 Ὡς δ᾽ εἰσήλθομεν ἔσω τοῦ σκάφους ἄμφω,  
> (And when we had both entered within the skiff,)
>
> 29 Τἀρχαῖον πορθμίδιον ἔβαιν᾽ ὀρύσσον  
> (The ancient ferry-boat went ploughing)
>
> 30 Αὔλακας ἤπερ ἄλλοτε βαθυτέρας.  
> (Furrows deeper than at other times.)

- Temporal *ὡς* with *εἰσήλθομεν* (1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *εἰσέρχομαι*) gives ‘when we entered’ for *Tosto che … nel legno fui*. The plural verb and postponed *ἄμφω* ‘both’ (nom. dual) replace the two named subjects and the singular *fui*. *Ἔσω τοῦ σκάφους* ‘inside the skiff’, with the genitive after *ἔσω*, reinforces the direction already expressed by *εἰσ-*; *legno* is rendered by the vessel itself, not its material.
- *Τἀρχαῖον* is crasis of neuter *τὸ ἀρχαῖον* and agrees with *πορθμίδιον* ‘little ferryboat’: the antiquity of *l’ antica prora* is transferred from the prow to the whole craft. *Ἔβαιν᾽* (*ἔβαινε*, 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *βαίνω*) substitutes past continuous motion for present *se ne va*. *Ὀρύσσον* (nom. sg. neut. pres. act. participle of *ὀρύσσω*) agrees with the boat and, like *secando*, tells how it moves.
- *Αὔλακας … βαθυτέρας* ‘deeper furrows’ is the accusative object of *ὀρύσσον*, literally ‘digging’; *βαθυτέρας* is the feminine accusative plural comparative of *βαθύς*. Instead of *più* water being cut, the furrows have greater depth. *Ἤπερ ἄλλοτε* ‘than at other times’ gives a temporal standard of comparison: neither the customary verb *suol* nor the other passengers of *con altrui* are expressed separately.

> 31 Mentre noi correvam la morta gora,  
> 32 dinanzi mi si fece un pien di fango,  
> 33 e disse: “Chi se’ tu, che vieni anzi ora?”  

Whilst we were running through the dead channel, there rose before me one full of mud, and said: "Who art thou, that comest before thy time?"

> 31 Ἐν ᾧ ᾽τρέχομεν ἐπὶ νεκρᾶς θαλάσσης,  
> (While we were speeding over the dead sea,)
>
> 32 Παρέστη τις πρὸς ἡμᾶς βορβόρου πλήρης  
> (There stood before us one full of mire)
>
> 33 Λέγων· “Τίς εἶ σύ; πῶς δ᾽ ἥκεις πρὶν τῆς ὥρας;”  
> (Saying: "Who are you? And how come you before the hour?")

- *Ἐν ᾧ* ‘in which [time], while’ introduces the equivalent of *Mentre*. *᾽Τρέχομεν* represents *ἐτρέχομεν* (1st pl. imperf. act. ind. of *τρέχω*) with the initial augment vowel suppressed after *ᾧ* (prodelision). *Ἐπὶ νεκρᾶς θαλάσσης* ‘over a dead sea’ replaces *la morta gora*, the direct object of *correvam*, with *ἐπί* and a genitive of the surface traversed.
- *Παρέστη* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *παρίστημι*, intransitive) means ‘came and stood by’; the subject *τις* ‘someone’ follows the verb, as *un pien di fango* follows *mi si fece*. *Πρὸς ἡμᾶς* ‘before us’ changes singular *mi* to plural. *Βορβόρου* is the genitive complement of *πλήρης* ‘full’, nominative singular masculine agreeing with *τις*.
- *Λέγων* (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *λέγω*) makes *e disse* attendant on *παρέστη* rather than a second finite verb. *Τίς εἶ σύ;* retains the explicit *tu*, but the Italian relative *che vieni* becomes a second question with *πῶς δ᾽* ‘and how?’ *Ἥκεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἥκω*) denotes arrival as a present fact; *πρὶν τῆς ὥρας* uses *πρίν* prepositionally with a genitive, ‘before the hour’, for *anzi ora*.

> 34 Ed io a lui: “S’ io vegno, non rimango;  
> 35 ma tu chi sei, che sei sì fatto brutto?”  

And I to him: "If I come, I remain not; but thou, who art thou, that hast become so foul?"

> 34 Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν· “Ἥκω μέν, ἀλλ᾽ οὐ μένω·  
> (And I to him: "I come indeed, yet I do not stay;)
>
> 35 „ Σὺ δὲ τίς; πῶς τ᾽ ἐγένου δυσειδὴς οὕτως;”  
> (And who are you? And how have you become so hideous?")

- *Κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*. *Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν* reproduces the verbless *Ed io a lui*, with *πρός* and the accusative marking the addressee. *Ἥκω μέν, ἀλλ᾽ οὐ μένω* ‘I have come, indeed, but do not stay’ replaces *S’ io vegno* with a concession of fact: postpositive *μέν* prepares the contrast, elided *ἀλλά* introduces it. Both verbs are 1st sg. present indicatives.
- *Σὺ δὲ τίς;* ‘but you, who [are you]?’ leaves out the copula. As in line 33, *che* with its indicative becomes an independent question: *πῶς τ᾽ ἐγένου* ‘and how did you become?’ *Τ᾽* is elided *τε*; *ἐγένου* is 2nd sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *γίγνομαι*, with predicate nominative *δυσειδής* ‘unsightly’. The aorist asks about the change resulting in *sei sì fatto brutto*; *οὕτως* renders *sì*.

> 36 Rispose: “Vedi che son un che piango.”  

He answered: "Thou seest that I am one who weep."

> 36 Ὁ δ᾽ ἀπεκρίθη· “Βλέπεις ὥς εἰμι κλαίων.”  
> (And he answered: "You see that I am one who weeps.")

- *Ὁ δ᾽ ἀπεκρίθη* ‘and he answered’ uses the article pronominally with *δέ* to mark the change of speaker. *Ἀπεκρίθη* is 3rd sg. aorist passive in form, active in sense, of *ἀποκρίνομαι*. *Βλέπεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind.) takes a content clause with *ὡς* ‘that’, corresponding to *Vedi che*.
- *Εἰμι κλαίων* ‘I am weeping’ combines the copula with the nominative singular masculine present active participle of *κλαίω*. It follows the order of *son un che piango* but does not reproduce *un che* as a pronoun and relative: the participle predicates the activity directly of the speaker.

> 37 Ed io a lui: “Con piangere e con lutto,  
> 38 spirito maledetto, ti rimani!  
> 39 ch’ io ti conosco, ancor sia lordo tutto.”  

And I to him: "With weeping, and with sorrow, accursed spirit, remain thou! for I know thee, all filthy as thou art."

> 37 Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν· “Ἀγωνιῶν καὶ κλαίων  
> (And I to him: "In anguish and weeping)
>
> 38 „ ᾈείποτ᾽ εἴης, ὦ κατάρατον πνεῦμα·  
> (May you ever remain, O accursed spirit;)
>
> 39 „ Γινώσκω γάρ σε, καίτοι βορβόρου πλήρη.”  
> (For I know you, even though full of mire.")

- After the repeated *Κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν*, *ἀγωνιῶν καὶ κλαίων* ‘in anguish and weeping’ turns the nouns of *Con piangere e con lutto* into present active participles (nom. sg. masc., from *ἀγωνιάω* and *κλαίω*) and reverses their order. They predicate a continuing condition of the person addressed.
- *ᾈείποτ᾽* (elided *ἀείποτε*) supplies ‘ever’; *εἴης* is the 2nd sg. present optative of *εἰμί*, independent and without *ἄν*, expressing a wish. Thus the imperative *ti rimani* becomes ‘may you ever be [in anguish and weeping]’. *Ὦ κατάρατον πνεῦμα* is a vocative phrase, neuter singular: *κατάρατον* agrees with *πνεῦμα*, whereas the masculine participles refer to the addressee as a person.
- Postpositive causal *γάρ* renders *ch’* in *ch’ io ti conosco*; *γινώσκω* is the form with medial *-ιν-* beside Attic *γιγνώσκω*. *Καίτοι βορβόρου πλήρη* ‘though full of mire’ replaces *ancor sia lordo tutto*: concessive *καίτοι* introduces a predicate adjective, *πλήρη* (acc. sg. masc. of *πλήρης*), agreeing with object *σε*. No finite counterpart to the Italian subjunctive *sia* is needed; the description of line 32 recurs in a different case.

> 40 Allora stese al legno ambo le mani:  
> 41 per che il maestro accorto lo sospinse,  
> 42 dicendo: “Via costà con gli altri cani!”  

Then he stretched both hands to the boat, whereat the wary Master thrust him off, saying: "Away there with the other dogs!"

> 40 Τότ᾽ ἀμφοῖν χερσὶ προσηρτήθη τῷ σκάφει·  
> (Then with both hands he clung to the skiff;)
>
> 41 Αὐτὸν δ᾽ ἀπέκρουσ᾽ ὁ συνετὸς Προστάτης  
> (But the prudent Protector thrust him back,)
>
> 42 Λέγων· “Ἄπιθ᾽ ἐντεῦθεν σὺν κυσὶν ἄλλοις.”  
> (Saying: "Begone from here with the other dogs.")

- *Τότ᾽* is elided *τότε*. *Ἀμφοῖν χερσί* ‘with both hands’ puts *ἀμφοῖν*, the oblique dual of *ἄμφω*, beside the instrumental dative **plural** of *χείρ*, not the dual *χεροῖν*. Italian *ambo le mani* is the direct object of *stese*; Greek instead says *προσηρτήθη τῷ σκάφει* ‘he attached himself to the skiff’. *Προσηρτήθη* is 3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *προσαρτάω*, with reflexive force here; the skiff is dative as the thing to which he clung.
- *Αὐτὸν δ᾽ ἀπέκρουσ᾽* ‘but him he thrust back’ fronts the object and links the actions with *δέ*, dropping the explicit consequence in *per che*. *Ἀπέκρουσ᾽* is elided *ἀπέκρουσε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀποκρούω*). The subject follows: *ὁ συνετὸς Προστάτης* ‘the discerning Protector’ renders *il maestro accorto*, changing the title and putting the adjective between article and noun.
- *Λέγων* corresponds directly to *dicendo*. *Ἄπιθ᾽* is *ἄπιθι* (2nd sg. pres. imperative of *ἄπειμι* ‘go away’, compounded with *εἶμι*), elided before *ἐντεῦθεν*. Italian *Via costà* points ‘away over there’; Greek commands departure ‘from here’. *Σὺν κυσὶν ἄλλοις* uses *σύν* with the dative plural (*κυσίν* from *κύων*) for *con gli altri cani*, without the article.

> 43 Lo collo poi con le braccia mi cinse,  
> 44 baciommi il volto, e disse: “Alma sdegnosa,  
> 45 benedetta colei che in te s’ incinse.  

And he put his arms about my neck, kissed my face, and said: "Indignant soul! blessed be she that bore thee.

> 43 Εἶτ᾽ ἀγκάλας περιθεὶς ἐμῷ τραχήλῳ,  
> (Then, putting his arms around my neck,)
>
> 44 Καί με φιλήσας εἶπε· “Ψυχὴ γενναία,  
> (And kissing me, he said: "Noble soul,)
>
> 45 „ Μάκαιρ᾽ ἡ μήτηρ, ἥτις ἐκύησέ σε.  
> (Blessed is the mother who conceived you!)

- *Εἶτ᾽* (*εἶτα*) renders *poi*. *Ἀγκάλας περιθείς* ‘having put his arms around’ makes *ἀγκάλας* the accusative object of *περιθείς* (nom. sg. masc. second aor. act. participle of *περιτίθημι*). Italian *Lo collo … mi cinse* makes the neck the object and the arms instrumental; Greek puts the neck in the dative, *ἐμῷ τραχήλῳ*, with an explicit possessive adjective.
- *Καί με φιλήσας εἶπε* ‘and having kissed me he said’ coordinates *φιλήσας* (nom. sg. masc. aor. act. participle of *φιλέω*) with *περιθείς*, leaving only *εἶπε* as a finite verb. The two Italian finite actions *cinse, baciommi* thus both precede the speech as participles; *με* denotes the person kissed, without *il volto*.
- *Ψυχὴ γενναία* is vocative, ‘noble soul’: *γενναία* renders *sdegnosa* by an approving quality rather than a word for indignation. *Μάκαιρ᾽* is elided *μάκαιρα* (nom. sg. fem. of *μάκαρ*), a predicate with the copula omitted. *Ἡ μήτηρ* supplies ‘the mother’ for *colei*; *ἥτις* (nom. sg. fem. of *ὅστις*) introduces the identifying description. *Ἐκύησέ σε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *κυέω*, with enclitic object) makes *in te s’ incinse* a transitive ‘conceived you’.

> 46 Quei fu al mondo persona orgogliosa;  
> 47 bontà non è che sua memoria fregi:  
> 48 così s’ è l’ ombra sua qui furiosa.  

In your world, that was an arrogant personage; good there is none to ornament the memory of him: so is his shadow here in fury.

> 46 „ Ἀλαζὼν οὗτος ὑπῆρξεν ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ·  
> (This man was an arrogant boaster in the world;)
>
> 47 „ Οὐκ ἔστ᾽ ἐσθλότης κοσμοῦσ᾽ αὐτοῦ τὴν μνήμην·  
> (There is no goodness adorning his memory;)
>
> 48 „ Τοὔνεκ᾽ αὐτοῦ ᾽στιν ἡ σκιὰ μανιώδης.  
> (Therefore his shade is furious.)

- *Ἀλαζὼν οὗτος ὑπῆρξεν* ‘a boaster this man was’ puts the predicate noun first. *Ἀλαζών* does duty for both *persona* and *orgogliosa*; proximal *οὗτος* renders *Quei*. *Ὑπῆρξεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ὑπάρχω*) is used copulatively for *fu*; *ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ* renders *al mondo* without adding a possessive.
- *Οὐκ ἔστ᾽ ἐσθλότης* ‘there is no goodness’ has existential *ἔστι* (elided) and the abstract noun from poetic *ἐσθλός* ‘good’. *Κοσμοῦσ᾽* is elided *κοσμοῦσα* (nom. sg. fem. pres. act. participle of *κοσμέω*), agreeing with *ἐσθλότης*: Italian *che … fregi*, a relative clause with subjunctive after the negative antecedent, becomes ‘adorning his memory’, with *αὐτοῦ* possessive and *τὴν μνήμην* the direct object.
- *Τοὔνεκ᾽* (elided poetic *τοὔνεκα*, from *τοῦ ἕνεκα*) means ‘for that reason’: it reads *così* as consequence rather than manner. In *αὐτοῦ ᾽στιν ἡ σκιὰ μανιώδης*, *᾽στιν* has lost the initial vowel of *ἐστίν* after *αὐτοῦ*. The possessive genitive precedes the verb, while predicate *μανιώδης* (nom. sg. fem.) follows *ἡ σκιά*; Italian *qui* is not represented.

> 49 Quanti si tengon or lassù gran regi,  
> 50 che qui staranno come porci in brago,  
> 51 di sè lasciando orribili dispregi!”  

How many up there now think themselves great kings, that shall lie here like swine in mire, leaving behind them horrible reproaches!"

> 49 „ Πόσοι νῦν ἄνω βασιλεῖς οἴοντ᾽ εἶναι,  
> (How many up above now deem themselves kings,)
>
> 50 „ Οἵπερ μενοῦσιν ἐν πηλῷ τῷδ᾽ ὡς χοῖροι,  
> (Who shall remain in this mud like swine,)
>
> 51 „ Αἶσχος μετὰ θάνατον φρικτὸν λιπόντες!”  
> (Leaving behind after death a dreadful disgrace!")

- Exclamatory *πόσοι* ‘how many’ is followed by *νῦν ἄνω* for *or lassù*. *Οἴοντ᾽* is elided *οἴονται* (3rd pl. pres. mid. ind. of *οἴομαι* ‘think’). With *εἶναι* its complement, no reflexive object is required for *si tengon*: those thinking are also the understood subject of the infinitive, so predicate *βασιλεῖς* is nominative plural. *Gran* has no separate equivalent.
- *Οἵπερ* is the nominative plural relative strengthened by *-περ*, ‘the very ones who’; *μενοῦσιν* is the contracted future active indicative, 3rd pl. of *μένω*, for *staranno*. *Ἐν πηλῷ τῷδε* ‘in this mud’ absorbs *qui* into a demonstrative (dat. sg. masc. of *ὅδε*), and the comparison *ὡς χοῖροι* ‘like swine’ follows rather than precedes the phrase for *in brago*.
- *Αἶσχος … φρικτόν* ‘a dreadful disgrace’ is one neuter singular object in place of plural *orribili dispregi*. *Λιπόντες* (nom. pl. masc. second aor. act. participle of *λείπω*) agrees with *οἵπερ*: where Italian *lasciando* presents the leaving as attendant, the aorist presents it as a whole. Greek omits *di sè* and supplies *μετὰ θάνατον* (*μετά* with acc.), ‘after death’.

> 52 Ed io: “Maestro, molto sarei vago  
> 53 di vederlo attuffare in questa broda,  
> 54 prima che noi uscissimo del lago.”  

And I: "Master, I should be glad to see him dipped in this swill, ere we quit the lake."

> 52 Κἀγώ· “Προστάτα, λίαν ἰδεῖν ἐπόθουν  
> (And I: "Protector, I greatly longed to see)
>
> 53 „ Αὐτὸν βυθιζόμενον ἐν τῷ βορβόρῳ  
> (Him plunged deep in this mire,)
>
> 54 „ Τῷδε, πρὶν ἐξέλθωμεν ἔτι τῆς λίμνης.”  
> (Before we yet depart out of the lake.")

- *Κἀγώ* again leaves the verb of speaking understood; *Προστάτα* is the vocative singular of *Προστάτης* for *Maestro*. *Λίαν ἰδεῖν ἐπόθουν* ‘I greatly longed to see’ renders *molto sarei vago di vederlo* with a verb of desire governing an infinitive. *Ἐπόθουν* is contracted 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ποθέω*: the Greek has a past indicative, not an explicit counterpart to the conditional *sarei*.
- *Ἰδεῖν* (second aor. act. infinitive of *ὁράω*) takes *αὐτὸν βυθιζόμενον*, ‘him being submerged’: the present middle/passive participle (acc. sg. masc. of *βυθίζω*) agrees with the object of perception and shows the action in progress. Italian instead attaches *-lo* to *veder-* and uses the infinitive *attuffare*. *Ἐν τῷ βορβόρῳ τῷδε* ‘in this mire’, with the demonstrative postponed across the line ending, repeats the noun for mud rather than introducing a counterpart to *broda*.
- *Πρὶν ἐξέλθωμεν … τῆς λίμνης* ‘before we leave the lake’ uses the 1st pl. second aor. act. subjunctive of *ἐξέρχομαι*, with the genitive of separation. Greek marks a prospective event where Italian has the imperfect subjunctive *uscissimo*. Bare *πρίν* with a subjunctive after an affirmative main clause is less usual than the classical *πρίν* with infinitive here; the finite construction stays close to *prima che noi*. *Ἔτι* adds the sense ‘yet’.

> 55 Ed egli a me: “Avanti che la proda  
> 56 ti si lasci veder, tu sarai sazio;  
> 57 di tal disio converrà che tu goda.”  

And he to me: "Before the shore comes to thy view, thou shalt be satisfied; it is fitting that thou shouldst be gratified in such a wish."

> 55 Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ· “Τὴν ὄχθην οὐ μὴ παύσῃ  
> (And he to me: "You shall not cease looking)
>
> 56 „ Βλέπων, καὶ θυμηδίαν, ἣν ποθεῖς, ἕξεις·  
> (Upon the shore, and you shall have the delight which you desire;)
>
> 57 „ Δεῖ γάρ σε χαίρειν ἐπὶ τῷ πόθῳ τούτῳ.”  
> (For it is fitting that you rejoice in this desire.")

- *Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ* matches the verbless *Ed egli a me*. The syntax then diverges sharply: in *Avanti che la proda ti si lasci veder*, the shore is the subject of a temporal clause; *τὴν ὄχθην* is accusative, the fronted object of *βλέπων* ‘looking at’. There is no Greek conjunction meaning ‘before’.
- *Οὐ μὴ παύσῃ* is an emphatic denial about the future. *Παύσῃ* (2nd sg. mid. of *παύω*) can formally be either aorist subjunctive or future indicative; *οὐ μή* admits both constructions. *Βλέπων* (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle) supplements the middle verb of ceasing: ‘you will certainly not cease looking at the shore’. This is not the Italian promise that satisfaction will precede the sight of it.
- *Καὶ θυμηδίαν, ἣν ποθεῖς, ἕξεις* ‘and delight, which you desire, you will have’ replaces *tu sarai sazio* with possession of an abstract noun. *Θυμηδίαν* is the fronted object of *ἕξεις* (2nd sg. fut. act. ind. of *ἔχω*); relative *ἣν* is independently accusative as object of *ποθεῖς* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ποθέω*), echoing *ἐπόθουν*.
- *Δεῖ γάρ σε χαίρειν* ‘for it is fitting for you to rejoice’ makes the final judgment explicitly explanatory with postpositive *γάρ*. Impersonal present *δεῖ* replaces future *converrà*; accusative *σε* is the subject of the present active infinitive *χαίρειν*, in place of *che tu goda*. *Ἐπὶ τῷ πόθῳ τούτῳ* uses *ἐπί* with the dative for the ground of rejoicing, ‘at this desire’, where *di tal disio* has *di* and a demonstrative of quality, ‘such’.

## Lines 58–90 — Argenti’s Rending and the Gates of Dis

> 58 Dopo ciò poco, vidi quello strazio  
> 59 far di costui alle fangose genti,  
> 60 che Dio ancor ne lodo e ne ringrazio.  

A little after this, I saw the muddy people make such rending of him, that even now I praise and thank God for it.

> 58 Μετὰ δ’ ὀλίγον σπαραχθέντ’ αὐτὸν εἶδον  
> (And after a little while I saw him torn to pieces)
>
> 59 Ὑπὸ τῶν ἐν βορβόρῳ τυρβαζομένων,  
> (By those wallowing in the mire,)
>
> 60 Καὶ Θεὸν ἔκτοτ’ εὐλογῶ καὶ δοξάζω.  
> (And God from that time forth I bless and glorify.)

- *Μετὰ δ’ ὀλίγον* ‘after a little while’ renders *Dopo ciò poco*: *μετά* governs the substantivized neuter accusative *ὀλίγον*; there is no demonstrative for *ciò*. Postpositive *δέ*, elided before the vowel, links the sentence to what precedes.
- *σπαραχθέντ’ αὐτὸν εἶδον* ‘I saw him torn apart’ reverses the perspective of *vidi quello strazio far di costui*. With *εἶδον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*), Greek has an accusative of the person perceived and a supplementary participle agreeing with it: *σπαραχθέντ’* is elided *σπαραχθέντα*, acc. sg. masc. aor. pass. of *σπαράσσω*. The participle, placed even before *αὐτόν*, presents the rending as a whole, rather than naming *quello strazio* as the object of *far*.
- *ὑπὸ τῶν ἐν βορβόρῳ τυρβαζομένων* expresses the agents of the passive by *ὑπό* with the genitive. For the adjective and noun *alle fangose genti* Musurus gives an articular present middle/passive participle (gen. pl. of *τυρβάζω*), ‘those in commotion in the mire’; *ἐν βορβόρῳ* specifies where, not what they are like.
- *καὶ Θεὸν ἔκτοτ’ εὐλογῶ καὶ δοξάζω* coordinates a new assertion instead of completing the Italian result construction *quello … che*. *ἔκτοτ’* (elided *ἔκτοτε*) means ‘ever since then’, rather than *ancor* ‘still’; there is no equivalent of pronominal *ne*. The two presents (1st sg. act. ind. of *εὐλογέω*, contracted, and *δοξάζω*) share the fronted accusative *Θεόν*: ‘bless and glorify’ replaces ‘praise and thank’.

> 61 Tutti gridavano: “A Filippo Argenti! ”  

All cried: "At Filippo Argenti!"

> 61 Πάντες ἔκραζον· “Κατ’ Ἀργέντου Φιλίππου!”  
> (All cried out: "Against Filippo Argenti!")

- *Πάντες ἔκραζον* closely matches *Tutti gridavano*: *πάντες* is substantival, nom. pl. masc. of *πᾶς*, and the augmented imperfect *ἔκραζον* (3rd pl. act. ind. of *κράζω*) represents the continuing cries.
- *Κατ’ Ἀργέντου Φιλίππου* makes the hostile force of *A* explicit by *κατά* with the genitive, ‘against’; the preposition loses its final vowel. Both names are declined, and their Italian order is reversed.

> 62 Lo Fiorentino spirito bizzarro  
> 63 in se medesmo si volgea co’ denti.  

The passionate Florentine spirit turned with his teeth upon himself.

> 62 Τὸ Φλωρέντειον δ’ αὐτὸ μανικὸν πνεῦμα  
> (And that furious Florentine spirit)
>
> 63 Καθ’ ἑαυτοῦ τοὺς αὑτοῦ ’στρεφεν ὀδόντας.  
> (Turned its own teeth against itself.)

- *Τὸ Φλωρέντειον δ’ αὐτὸ μανικὸν πνεῦμα* retains the neuter noun ‘spirit’, with article and adjectives all nom. sg. neuter. *μανικόν* ‘frenzied’ interprets *bizzarro*; *αὐτό* singles out the spirit itself, and connective *δ’* has no separate counterpart in Italian.
- *Καθ’ ἑαυτοῦ* ‘against itself’ uses *κατά* with the reflexive genitive; after elision the rough breathing of *ἑαυτοῦ* accounts for *θ* in *καθ’*. Instead of reflexive *si volgea* with instrumental *co’ denti*, Greek has transitive *’στρεφεν* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *στρέφω*, with the initial augment of *ἔστρεφεν* suppressed after *αὑτοῦ*). *τοὺς αὑτοῦ … ὀδόντας* is its accusative object, ‘its own teeth’; *αὑτοῦ* is the contracted reflexive, distinct from *αὐτοῦ*.

> 64 Quivi il lasciammo, che più non ne narro;  

Here we left him, so that of him I tell no more;

> 64 Ἐκεῖ ’λίπομεν αὐτόν, κοὐδὲν πλεῖν λέξω.  
> (There we left him, and I shall say no more.)

- *Ἐκεῖ ’λίπομεν αὐτόν* follows *Quivi il lasciammo*, but places the pronoun after the verb. *’λίπομεν* represents *ἐλίπομεν* (1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *λείπω*) with prodelision of the augment.
- *κοὐδὲν πλεῖν λέξω* ‘and I shall say nothing more’ coordinates what *che* introduces in Italian. *κοὐδέν* is crasis of *καὶ οὐδέν*; *πλεῖν* is the shortened comparative neuter of *πολύς*, used adverbially, not an infinitive here. *λέξω* (1st sg. fut. act. ind. of *λέγω*) changes the present *narro* to a future; *ne* has no expressed complement in Greek.

> 65 ma negli orecchi mi percosse un duolo,  
> 66 per ch’io avanti intento l’ occhio sbarro.  

but in my ears a wailing smote me, whereat I bent my eyes intently forward.

> 65 Ἀλλ’ εὐθὺς τἀμὰ γόος ἐξέπληξ’ ὦτα,  
> (But straightway a wailing struck my ears,)
>
> 66 Ἀτενέσι τ’ ὀφθαλμοῖς ἔμπροσθεν εἶδον.  
> (And with intent eyes I looked ahead.)

- *Ἀλλ’ εὐθὺς τἀμὰ γόος ἐξέπληξ’ ὦτα* supplies *εὐθύς* ‘straightway’. *γόος* ‘lamentation’ is the nominative subject of *ἐξέπληξ’* (elided *ἐξέπληξε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐκπλήσσω*). Where *negli orecchi mi percosse* has a locative phrase and a personal pronoun, the blow in Greek takes the ears as its direct object: *τἀμὰ … ὦτα* ‘my ears’ (crasis of *τὰ ἐμά*; *ὦτα* is acc. pl. of *οὖς*).
- *Ἀτενέσι τ’ ὀφθαλμοῖς ἔμπροσθεν εἶδον* ‘and with intent eyes I looked ahead’ replaces the causal relative *per ch’io* with enclitic *τε*, elided before *ὀφθαλμοῖς*. *ἀτενέσι* (dat. pl. of *ἀτενής*) agrees with the instrumental dative plural ‘eyes’. The singular object of *sbarro* thus becomes a plural adjunct of *εἶδον*; the Greek has an aorist of seeing, not a present of opening wide.

> 67 Lo buon maestro disse: “Omai, figliuolo,  
> 68 s’ appressa la città che ha nome Dite,  
> 69 co’ gravi cittadin, col grande stuolo.”  

The kind Master said: "Now, Son, the city that is named of Dis draws nigh, with its grave citizens, with its great company."

> 67 Ὁ δ’ ἀγαθὸς Προστάτης εἶπε. “Νῦν, τέκνον,  
> (And the good Protector said: "Now, child,)
>
> 68 „ Ἡμῶν ἔστ’ ἐγγὺς τοὔνομα Διτὸς πόλις,  
> (Near to us is the city named Dis,)
>
> 69 „ Λαοῦ πλήθουσα δεινοπαθῶν ἀστῶν τε.”  
> (Thronged with a multitude and with suffering citizens.")

- *Ὁ δ’ ἀγαθὸς Προστάτης εἶπε* gives *maestro* the sense ‘protector’ or ‘leader’. *εἶπε* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*, corresponding to *disse*. *Νῦν, τέκνον* renders *Omai, figliuolo* with a temporal adverb and the neuter vocative ‘child’, without a diminutive.
- *Ἡμῶν ἔστ’ ἐγγύς* ‘is near us’ replaces the movement of *s’appressa* with position: *ἔστ’* is elided *ἔστι*, and *ἐγγύς* takes the supplied genitive *ἡμῶν*. The subject *πόλις* is delayed. *τοὔνομα* (crasis of *τὸ ὄνομα*) is an accusative of respect, ‘in name’; *Διτός* is genitive, giving ‘the city of Dis’ rather than reproducing the relative clause *che ha nome Dite*.
- *Λαοῦ πλήθουσα δεινοπαθῶν ἀστῶν τε* ‘being full of a throng and of sorely suffering citizens’ substitutes a participle with genitives of contents for the two phrases with *co’*. *πλήθουσα* (nom. sg. fem. pres. act. participle of *πλήθω*) agrees with *πόλις*. The collective *λαοῦ* comes first, without a separate adjective for *grande*; final *τε* coordinates *ἀστῶν* with it. *δεινοπαθῶν* (gen. pl. of *δεινοπαθής*) interprets *gravi* in terms of what the citizens suffer.

> 70 Ed io: “Maestro, già le sue meschite  
> 71 là entro certo nella valle cerno  
> 72 vermiglie, come se di fuoco uscite  
> 73 fossero.” Ed ei mi disse: “Il foco eterno,  

And I: "Master, already I discern its mosques, distinctly there within the valley, red as if they had come out of fire."

> 70 Κἀγώ· “Προστάτα, βλέπω δὴ τὰς μεσγίδας  
> (And I: "Protector, I see already its mosques)
>
> 71 „ Αὐτῆς ἐρυθραινούσας ἐν τῇ κοιλάδι,  
> (Glowing red within the valley,)
>
> 72 „ Ὡσεὶ τοῦ πυρὸς ἄρτι διασωθείσας.”  
> (As if just rescued from the fire.")
>
> 73 Ὁ δ’ εἶπε· “Τἀέναον πῦρ καταφλέγει  
> (And he said: "The eternal fire burns)

- *Κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*, reproducing the verbless introduction *Ed io*. *Προστάτα* is the vocative of *προστάτης*. *βλέπω δή* places the verb before its object, unlike the delayed *cerno*; *δή* marks recognition or emphasis, doing service for the Italian *già … certo* without translating both adverbs separately.
- *τὰς μεσγίδας αὐτῆς ἐρυθραινούσας* gives ‘its mosques showing red’: *μεσγίδας* is the acc. pl. of the borrowed *μεσγίς*; possessive *αὐτῆς* refers to *πόλις*. The acc. pl. fem. present active participle of *ἐρυθραίνω* agrees with the object of perception, turning *vermiglie* into a visible process. *ἐν τῇ κοιλάδι* (dat. sg. of *κοιλάς*) renders *nella valle* without a separate ‘there’.
- *Ὡσεὶ τοῦ πυρὸς ἄρτι διασωθείσας* replaces *come se di fuoco uscite fossero* with *ὡσεί* ‘as if’ and another agreeing participle, *διασωθείσας* (acc. pl. fem. aor. pass. of *διασῴζω*). The genitive denotes separation; the sense is ‘as if just saved from the fire’, adding both *ἄρτι* and the notion of escape. The Italian pluperfect subjunctive extends into line 73; Greek finishes the comparison in 72.

> 74 ch’ entro l’ affoca, le dimostra rosse,  
> 75 come tu vedi in questo basso inferno.”  

And to me he said: "The eternal fire, which causes them to glow within, shows them red, as thou seest, in this low Hell."

> 74 „ Ἔντοσθεν, διδὸν αὐταῖς ἐρυθρὸν χρῶμα  
> (Within, giving them a red hue)
>
> 75 „ Ἐν τῷ κατωτέρῳ τῷδ᾽, ὡς ὁρᾷς, Ἅδῃ.”  
> (In this lower Hell, as you see.")

- Greek begins this reply in 73 with *Ὁ δ’ εἶπε* ‘and he said’: pronominal *ὁ* with *δέ* renders the change of speaker, but *mi* is omitted. *Τἀέναον πῦρ* (crasis of *τὸ ἀέναον*) is the subject of *καταφλέγει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *καταφλέγω*), ‘burns within’. Where *ch’ entro l’affoca* is relative and *le dimostra rosse* the main predicate, the burning is now the main predicate, without an expressed object.
- *διδὸν αὐταῖς ἐρυθρὸν χρῶμα* ‘giving them a red colour’ makes the visible effect participial. *διδόν* is nom. sg. neut. pres. act. participle of *δίδωμι*, agreeing with *πῦρ*; *αὐταῖς* is dative of recipient, referring to the mosques, and *ἐρυθρὸν χρῶμα* the accusative thing given.
- *ἐν τῷ κατωτέρῳ τῷδ᾽, ὡς ὁρᾷς, Ἅδῃ* uses the comparative *κατωτέρῳ* ‘lower’ for *basso*, and the dative of *Ἅδης* for *inferno*. Elided *τῷδε* ‘this’ agrees with the same dative singular. The parenthesis *ὡς ὁρᾷς* ‘as you see’ (2nd sg. contracted pres. act. ind. of *ὁράω*) is inserted before the noun rather than before the whole prepositional phrase.

> 76 Noi pur giugnemmo dentro all’ alte fosse,  
> 77 che vallan quella terra sconsolata;  
> 78 le mura mi parea, che ferro fosse.  

We now arrived in the deep fosses, which moat that joyless city; the walls seemed to me as if they were of iron.

> 76 Εἰς βαθείας νῦν ἀφικόμεθα τάφρους,  
> (Into deep moats we now arrived,)
>
> 77 Τἄθλιον ἄστυ ᾽κεῖνο περικυκλούσας·  
> (Surrounding that wretched city;)
>
> 78 Τὰ τείχη δ᾽ ἐῴκεσαν εἶν᾽ ἐκ σιδήρου.  
> (And its walls seemed to be of iron.)

- *Εἰς βαθείας νῦν ἀφικόμεθα τάφρους* separates adjective and noun around adverb and verb. *εἰς* with the accusative marks arrival into the moats; *βαθείας* takes *alte* as ‘deep’, and *νῦν* gives temporal force to *pur*. *ἀφικόμεθα* is 1st pl. second aor. mid. ind. of deponent *ἀφικνέομαι*; no pronoun is needed for *Noi*.
- *Τἄθλιον ἄστυ ᾽κεῖνο περικυκλούσας* makes *che vallan* an attributive description by a participle: *περικυκλούσας* (acc. pl. fem. pres. act. of *περικυκλόω*) agrees with *τάφρους* and governs *ἄστυ*. *Τἄθλιον* is crasis of *τὸ ἄθλιον*, ‘the wretched’; postposed *᾽κεῖνο* is *ἐκεῖνο* with its initial vowel suppressed. *terra* is thus explicitly ‘city’.
- *Τὰ τείχη δ᾽ ἐῴκεσαν εἶν᾽ ἐκ σιδήρου* ‘and the walls seemed to be of iron’ omits *mi* and replaces *che … fosse* with the infinitive *εἶν’* (elided *εἶναι*). *ἐῴκεσαν* is 3rd pl. pluperfect of *ἔοικα*, a perfect with present sense, so that its pluperfect describes a past appearance. The verb agrees in the plural with neuter plural *τὰ τείχη*, rather than taking the usual Attic singular. *ἐκ σιδήρου* specifies material instead of equating the walls directly with *ferro*.

> 79 Non senza prima far grande aggirata,  
> 80 venimmo in parte, dove il nocchier, forte,  
> 81 “Uscite,” ci gridò, “qui è l’ entrata.”  

Not before making a long circuit, did we come to a place where the boatman loudly cried to us: "Go out: here is the entrance."

> 79 Μόλις δὲ μετὰ μακρὰν περιοδείαν  
> (And scarcely after a long circuit)
>
> 80 Ἤλθομεν ἔνθ᾽ ὁ πορθμεὺς φωνῇ μεγάλῃ,  
> (We came where the ferryman with a loud voice,)
>
> 81 “Ἐξέλθετ᾽ ” ἔκραξ᾽, “ εἴσοδός ἐστιν ὧδε. ”  
> ("Go forth," cried out, "here is the entrance.")

- *Μόλις δὲ μετὰ μακρὰν περιοδείαν ἤλθομεν* ‘and only after a long circuit did we come’ recasts *Non senza prima far grande aggirata*: *μόλις* conveys the restrictive force without the two negatives, and *μετά* with an accusative noun replaces *far* with its object. *μακράν* specifies length. *ἤλθομεν* is 1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*.
- *ἔνθ᾽ ὁ πορθμεύς* ‘where the ferryman’ dispenses with the antecedent *in parte*. *ἔνθ᾽* is *ἔνθα* elided before *ὁ*. *φωνῇ μεγάλῃ* ‘with a loud voice’ renders adverbial *forte* by a dative of manner; the Greek does not express *ci* ‘to us’.
- *Ἐξέλθετ᾽* is elided *ἐξέλθετε*, 2nd pl. second aor. act. imperative of *ἐξέρχομαι*, giving *Uscite* as a single command to leave. *ἔκραξ᾽* (elided *ἔκραξε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *κράζω*) interrupts the speech as does *gridò*. *εἴσοδός ἐστιν ὧδε* puts ‘entrance’ first and ‘here’ last, reversing *qui è l’entrata*; the extra accent on *εἴσοδός* is due to enclitic *ἐστιν*.

> 82 Io vidi più di mille in sulle porte  
> 83 da’ ciel piovuti, che stizzosamente  
> 84 dicean: “Chi è costui, che senza morte  
> 85 va per lo regno della morta gente?”  

Above the gates I saw more than a thousand spirits, rained from [the] Heaven[s], who angrily exclaimed: "Who is that, who, without death, goes through the kingdom of the dead?"

> 82 Εἶδον ἐπὶ τῶν πυλῶν πλείους χιλίων  
> (I saw above the gates more than a thousand)
>
> 83 Οὐρανόθεν πεσόντας, καὶ λυσσαλέως  
> (Who had fallen from Heaven, and furiously)
>
> 84 Κράζοντας· “Τίς ὅδ᾽, ὅσπερ μὴ θανὼν ἔτι,  
> (Crying out: "Who is this who, not yet having died,)
>
> 85 „ Διὰ τοῦ κράτους τῶν νεκρῶν διαβαίνει; ”  
> (Passes through the realm of the dead?")

- *Εἶδον ἐπὶ τῶν πυλῶν πλείους χιλίων* puts the verb first and places the location before the number. *ἐπί* with the genitive denotes position ‘upon’; *πλείους* is the contracted acc. pl. masc. comparative of *πολύς* (for *πλείονας*), used substantivally as object of *εἶδον*. *χιλίων* is genitive of comparison, ‘more than a thousand’; as in Italian, there is no noun corresponding to English “spirits”.
- *Οὐρανόθεν πεσόντας, καὶ λυσσαλέως κράζοντας* coordinates two acc. pl. masc. participles agreeing with *πλείους*. *οὐρανόθεν* uses the poetic source suffix *-θεν*, ‘from heaven’; *πεσόντας* (second aor. act. of *πίπτω*) substitutes falling for the image in *piovuti*. *κράζοντας* (pres. act. of *κράζω*) presents the cries as in progress, replacing the relative imperfect *che … dicean*; *λυσσαλέως* ‘furiously’ intensifies *stizzosamente*.
- *Τίς ὅδ᾽, ὅσπερ μὴ θανὼν ἔτι* has an understood copula, ‘who [is] this?’ *ὅδ᾽* is elided *ὅδε*, nom. sg. masc.; *ὅσπερ* resumes it with an emphatic relative. *μὴ θανών* (nom. sg. masc. second aor. act. participle of *θνῄσκω*) turns *senza morte* into a negated circumstance, ‘without having died’; *ἔτι* adds ‘yet’. For a purely factual assertion of non-occurrence one would expect *οὐ*; *μή* presents the circumstance as a qualification.
- *Διὰ τοῦ κράτους τῶν νεκρῶν διαβαίνει* repeats the notion of passage in the preposition and the compound verb (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *διαβαίνω*). *διά* governs the genitive *τοῦ κράτους* ‘through the dominion’; dependent *τῶν νεκρῶν* ‘of the dead’ is a substantivized plural adjective for collective *della morta gente*.

> 86 E il savio mio maestro fece segno  
> 87 di voler lor parlar segretamente.  

And my sage Master made a sign of wishing to speak with them in secret.

> 86 Οὑμὸς δ᾽ Ἡγήτωρ ἐποίησε σημεῖον,  
> (And my Guide made a sign,)
>
> 87 Αὐτοῖς μυστικῶς διαλεχθῆναι θέλων.  
> (Wishing to speak with them secretly.)

- *Οὑμὸς δ᾽ Ἡγήτωρ* ‘and my guide’ has crasis of *ὁ ἐμός* in *οὑμός*, with rough breathing from the article. *Ἡγήτωρ* is a poetic agent noun for *maestro*; there is no adjective for *savio*. *ἐποίησε σημεῖον* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ποιέω* with an accusative object) follows *fece segno* closely.
- *Αὐτοῖς μυστικῶς διαλεχθῆναι θέλων* ends with *θέλων* (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *θέλω*), agreeing with the guide: ‘wishing to converse’, rather than *di voler* dependent on *segno*. *διαλεχθῆναι* is the aorist infinitive of *διαλέγομαι*, passive in form but active in sense. It takes the interlocutors in the dative *αὐτοῖς*; *μυστικῶς* renders *segretamente* adverbially.

> 88 Allor chiusero un poco il gran disdegno,  
> 89 e disser: “Vien tu solo, e quei sen vada,  
> 90 che sì ardito entrò per questo regno.  

Then they somewhat shut up their great disdain, and said: "Come thou alone; and let that one go, who has entered so daringly into this kingdom.

> 88 Τότε μικρὸν ἀνέντες τὴν πολλὴν λύσσαν  
> (Then slightly abating their great fury,)
>
> 89 Εἶπον· “Ἐλθὲ σὺ μόνος· οὗτος δ᾽ ἀπίτω,  
> (They said: "Come you alone; and let this one depart,)
>
> 90 „ Ὃς ἐτόλμησεν ἐλθεῖν εἰς κράτος τόδε.  
> (Who has dared to come into this realm.)

- *Τότε μικρὸν ἀνέντες τὴν πολλὴν λύσσαν εἶπον* subordinates *chiusero* to *disser*: ‘then, having relaxed their great fury a little, they said’. *ἀνέντες* is nom. pl. masc. second aor. act. participle of *ἀνίημι*, agreeing with the unexpressed subject of *εἶπον* (here 3rd pl. second aor. act. ind.). *μικρόν* is an adverbial neuter accusative of extent; *πολλήν* qualifies the accusative object *λύσσαν*. ‘Relaxing’ replaces the metaphor of shutting up, and ‘fury’ interprets *disdegno*.
- *Ἐλθὲ σὺ μόνος* maintains the expressed pronoun of *Vien tu solo*: *ἐλθέ* is 2nd sg. second aor. act. imperative of *ἔρχομαι*, and predicative *μόνος* agrees with emphatic *σύ*. *οὗτος δ᾽ ἀπίτω* contrasts ‘this one’ with ‘you’, using the nearer demonstrative for *quei*. *ἀπίτω* is 3rd sg. pres. imperative of *ἄπειμι* ‘go away’ (from *εἶμι*, not *εἰμί* ‘be’), answering to the Italian jussive subjunctive *sen vada*.
- *Ὃς ἐτόλμησεν ἐλθεῖν εἰς κράτος τόδε* makes the daring, rather than the entry, the finite act. Relative *ὅς* is the subject of *ἐτόλμησεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *τολμάω*); complementary *ἐλθεῖν* is the second aor. act. infinitive of *ἔρχομαι*. Thus *sì ardito entrò* becomes ‘dared to come’, without a degree word for *sì*. *εἰς* with the accusative makes entry into the realm explicit for *per questo regno*; postposed *τόδε* agrees with neuter *κράτος*.

## Lines 91–123 — The Gates Shut Against the Guide

> 91 Sol si ritorni per la folle strada;  
> 92 provi se sa: chè tu qui rimarrai,  
> 93 che gli hai scorta sì buia contrada.”  

Let him return alone his foolish way; try, if he can: for thou shalt stay here, that hast escorted him through so dark a country.”

> 91 „ Ἐπανελθέτω δι᾽ ἑῆς μωρᾶς τρίβου,  
> (Let him return by his own foolish path,)
>
> 92 „ Τρόποις οἷς οἶδε· σὺ δὲ μενεῖς ἐνταῦθα  
> (By the ways he knows; but you shall remain here,)
>
> 93 „ Ὁ τόνδ᾽ ἀγαγὼν εἰς τὴν ἀθλίαν χώραν. ”  
> (Who led this man into the wretched region.")

- *Ἐπανελθέτω* is the 3rd sg. second aorist active imperative of *ἐπανέρχομαι*, ‘let him go back’. *δι᾽ ἑῆς μωρᾶς τρίβου* retains the route construction of *per la folle strada* (*διά* with the genitive), but adds the poetic third-person possessive *ἑῆς*, gen. sg. fem. of *ἑός*, agreeing with *τρίβου*. The result is ‘by his own foolish path’, not strictly *sol* ‘alone’.
- *Τρόποις οἷς οἶδε*, ‘by such means as he knows’, replaces *provi se sa*: there is no second imperative and no conditional or indirect question. *τρόποις* is a dative of means; the relative *οἷς* is attracted into its antecedent’s dative, where as object of *οἶδε* one would expect *οὕς*. *οἶδε* is the 3rd sg. perfect active indicative of *οἶδα*, with present sense.
- *σὺ δὲ μενεῖς ἐνταῦθα* sets the explicit *σύ* against the subject of the imperative: adversative *δέ* replaces causal *chè*. *μενεῖς* is the contracted future active indicative, 2nd sg. of *μένω*. In apposition to *σύ*, *ὁ τόνδ᾽ ἀγαγών* ‘you who led this man’ substitutes an articular second aorist active participle (nom. sg. masc., *ἄγω*) for *che gli hai scorta*. The person escorted becomes the direct object *τόνδ᾽* (elided *τόνδε*); *εἰς τὴν ἀθλίαν χώραν* marks a destination, ‘into the wretched country’, rather than a region traversed. *ἀθλίαν* neither reproduces the image of *buia* nor takes an intensifier for *sì*.

> 94 Pensa lettor, se io mi sconfortai  
> 95 nel suon delle parole maledette:  
> 96 ch’ io non credetti ritornarci mai.  

Judge, Reader, if I was discouraged at the sound of the accursed words: for I believed not that I ever should return [hither].

> 94 Ἀναγνῶστα, νόησον ὁποίαν θλίψιν  
> (Reader, imagine what distress)
>
> 95 Ἐποίησάν μοι λόγοι φρικώδεις οὕτως·  
> (Words so dreadful wrought in me;)
>
> 96 Ἐπεπείσμην γὰρ οὔποτ᾽ ἂν ἐπιστρέψειν.  
> (For I was convinced that I should never return.)

- *Ἀναγνῶστα*, voc. sg. of *ἀναγνώστης*, precedes rather than follows *νόησον* (2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *νοέω*). *ὁποίαν θλίψιν ἐποίησάν μοι* gives the command an indirect exclamation as object: ‘conceive what distress they caused me’. *ὁποίαν* agrees with the accusative object *θλίψιν*; the speaker, subject of *mi sconfortai*, becomes the affected person in the dative *μοι*.
- *λόγοι φρικώδεις οὕτως* is the postponed nominative plural subject of *ἐποίησαν* (aor. act. ind. of *ποιέω*): ‘words dreadful in such a degree’. The words themselves cause the distress; *nel suon* has no separate equivalent, and *φρικώδεις* describes the words’ effect rather than calling them accursed. The written accent *ἐποίησάν* before enclitic *μοι* is additional to the verb’s own accent.
- *Ἐπεπείσμην γάρ* ‘for I had become convinced’ uses the 1st sg. pluperfect middle-passive indicative of *πείθω*, with both augment and reduplication. Unlike *non credetti*, the verb of belief is affirmative; the negative belongs inside its infinitival complement. *οὔποτ᾽ ἂν ἐπιστρέψειν* is ‘never to be going to return’, with elided *οὔποτε* and the future active infinitive of *ἐπιστρέφω*. The infinitive gives the prospect as seen at the time of conviction; its combination with modal *ἄν* is unusual beside the ordinary future infinitive of reported expectation. There is no adverb for *-ci*.

> 97 “O caro duca mio, che più di sette  
> 98 volte m’ hai sicurtà renduta, e tratto  
> 99 d’ alto periglio che incontra mi stette,  
> 100 non mi lasciar,” diss’ io, “così disfatto;  

“O my loved Guide, who more than seven times hast restored me to safety, and rescued from deep peril that stood before me, leave me not so undone,” I said;

> 97 “ Ἡγήτωρ φίλ᾽, ὃς πλέον ἤπερ ἑπτάκις  
> ("Dear Guide, who more than seven times)
>
> 98 „ Προστασίαν μοι παρέσχες, καὶ κινδύνων  
> (Have afforded me protection, and from perils)
>
> 99 „ Φρικτῶν, ἐν οἷς εὑρέθην, ἐξήγαγές με,  
> (Dreadful, in which I was found, have led me forth,)
>
> 100 „ Μὴ ἐγκαταλίπῃς με νῦν,” εἶπον, “ οὕτω  
> (Forsake me not now," I said, "so)

- *Ἡγήτωρ φίλ᾽* puts the title before its epithet: *ἡγήτωρ* is a nominative-shaped address, *φίλ᾽* the elided vocative *φίλε*. Neither *O* nor *mio* is separately expressed. *ὃς πλέον ἤπερ ἑπτάκις* keeps the relative clause and the numerical comparison; *πλέον* is adverbial, *ἤπερ* comparative ‘than’, and *ἑπτάκις* itself means ‘seven times’.
- *Προστασίαν μοι παρέσχες* ‘you afforded me protection’ substitutes simple bestowal for *sicurtà renduta*. *παρέσχες* (2nd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *παρέχω*) takes the accusative of the thing and the dative of the recipient. Although the subject is relative *ὅς*, the verb is second person after the address, as is the coordinated *ἐξήγαγές με* (2nd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἐξάγω*). The latter has an internal augment and an additional accent before enclitic *με*.
- *κινδύνων φρικτῶν … ἐξήγαγές με* ‘you led me out of dreadful dangers’ puts the genitive of separation before the compound verb. Singular *alto periglio* becomes plural; *ἐν οἷς εὑρέθην* ‘in which I found myself’ replaces *che incontra mi stette*. The relative now depends on *ἐν*, and the speaker, not the danger, is subject (*εὑρέθην*, 1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *εὑρίσκω*).
- *Μὴ ἐγκαταλίπῃς με νῦν* uses *μή* with the 2nd sg. second aorist active subjunctive of *ἐγκαταλείπω* for the prohibition; *νῦν* ‘now’ is supplied. *εἶπον* is the 1st sg. second aorist active indicative of *λέγω*, without a separate pronoun for *io*. The Greek reporting clause interrupts the request before *οὕτω δύστηνον* ‘so wretched’ (the adjective falls in line 101), the predicative accusative agreeing with *με*.

> 101 e se l’ andar più oltre c’ è negato,  
> 102 ritroviam l’ orme nostre insieme ratto.”  

“and if to go farther be denied us, let us retrace our steps together rapidly.”

> 101 „ Δύστηνον· ἀλλ᾽ εἴγ᾽ ἡμῖν ἰέναι πρόσω  
> (Wretched; but if to go onward for us)
>
> 102 „ Οὐκ ἔνι, τάχος χωρήσωμεν ὀπίσω.”  
> (Is not possible, let us swiftly turn back.")

- *ἀλλ᾽ εἴγ᾽* is elided *ἀλλὰ εἴ γε*, ‘but if indeed’, a stronger turn than *e se*. *ἡμῖν ἰέναι πρόσω οὐκ ἔνι* ‘it is not possible for us to go forward’ makes the passive *c’ è negato* impersonal. *ἔνι* stands for *ἔνεστι* in its sense ‘it is possible’; *ἡμῖν* names the persons for whom *ἰέναι* (pres. act. inf. of *εἶμι*) is possible or not.
- *τάχος χωρήσωμεν ὀπίσω* ‘let us go back quickly’ retains the first-person plural exhortation (*χωρήσωμεν*, aor. act. subj. of *χωρέω*). Neuter accusative *τάχος* is adverbial; *ὀπίσω* gives the direction without the image of finding *l’ orme nostre*. Joint action is implicit in the plural rather than expressed by a word for *insieme*.

> 103 E quel signor, che lì m’ avea menato,  
> 104 mi disse: “Non temer, chè il nostro passo  
> 105 non ci può torre alcun: da tal n’ è dato.  

And that Lord, who had led me thither, said to me: “Fear not, for our passage none can take from us: by Such has it been given to us.

> 103 Ὁ δὲ Προστάτης, ὃς ἤγαγέ μ᾽ ἐκεῖσε,  
> (And the Protector who had led me thither)
>
> 104 Εἶπε· “ Μὴ φοβοῦ· τὴν πορείαν κωλῦσαι  
> (Said: "Fear not; our passage to hinder)
>
> 105 „ Οὐδεὶς ἡμῖν δύναται βουλῇ τοῦ Δεῖνος·  
> (No one is able, by the will of Such-a-one:)

- *Ὁ δὲ Προστάτης* ‘and the protector’ changes *quel signor* from demonstrative and title to article and designation by function. *ὃς ἤγαγέ μ᾽ ἐκεῖσε* uses the second aorist of *ἄγω*, not a pluperfect for *m’ avea menato*; *ἐκεῖσε* specifically denotes motion thither. *μ᾽* is elided *με*. *Εἶπε* has no expressed recipient for *mi disse*.
- *Μὴ φοβοῦ* is a present middle imperative, 2nd sg. of *φοβέομαι*. The assurance follows without a conjunction for *chè*. *τὴν πορείαν κωλῦσαι* puts the passage and the act of hindering it first; *κωλῦσαι* (aor. act. inf. of *κωλύω*) is complementary to *δύναται* (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *δύναμαι*), with *οὐδείς* as subject. *ἡμῖν* marks those affected: the sense is ‘no one can hinder the journey for us’, not literally take it away.
- *βουλῇ τοῦ Δεῖνος* ‘by the will of Such-a-one’ compresses *da tal n’ è dato* into a causal or instrumental dative qualifying the assurance. There is no passive verb of giving. *τοῦ Δεῖνος* is the genitive of the indefinite designation *ὁ δεῖνα*; its capitalization here distinguishes the unnamed agent.

> 106 Ma qui m’ attendi; e lo spirito lasso  
> 107 conforta e ciba di speranza buona,  
> 108 ch’ io non ti lascerò nel mondo basso.”  

But thou, wait here for me; and comfort and feed thy wearied spirit with good hope: for I will not forsake thee in the low world.”

> 106 „ Ἀλλὰ πρόσμενέ μ᾽ ὧδε, καὶ θάῤῥος ἔχε  
> (But await me here, and have courage)
>
> 107 „ Ἐν πνεύματί σου, κἀγαθὴν τρέφ᾽ ἐλπίδα·  
> (In your spirit, and nourish good hope;)
>
> 108 „ Οὐ γὰρ ἐάσω σ᾽ ἔνδον τοῦ κάτω κόσμου.”  
> (For I will not leave you within the nether world.")

- *Ἀλλὰ πρόσμενέ μ᾽ ὧδε* closely follows *Ma qui m’ attendi*, with the place adverb last. *πρόσμενε* is the 2nd sg. present active imperative of *προσμένω*, with an additional accent before *μ᾽* (elided *με*, its accusative object).
- *θάῤῥος ἔχε ἐν πνεύματί σου* ‘have courage in your spirit’ makes *lo spirito* the seat of courage, not the object of *conforta*; *lasso* drops out. *θάῤῥος* is the neuter accusative object of present imperative *ἔχε*; the breathings on double rho are an older spelling convention. *πνεύματί* has an additional accent before enclitic possessive *σου*.
- *κἀγαθὴν τρέφ᾽ ἐλπίδα* reverses the feeding construction: it is good hope itself that one is to nourish, not the spirit *di speranza buona*. *κἀγαθήν* is crasis of *καὶ ἀγαθήν*; *τρέφ᾽* is elided *τρέφε*, 2nd sg. present active imperative. *Οὐ γὰρ ἐάσω σ᾽* uses the 1st sg. future active indicative of *ἐάω* with elided *σε*, ‘for I shall not leave you’; *ἔνδον τοῦ κάτω κόσμου* substitutes ‘within’ with genitive for *nel*. Adverbial *κάτω* in attributive position qualifies *κόσμου*.

> 109 Così sen va, e quivi m’ abbandona  
> 110 lo dolce padre, ed io rimango in forse:  
> 111 chè il sì, e il no nel capo mi tenzona.  

Thus the gentle Father goes, and leaves me here, and I remain in doubt: for yes and no contend within my head.

> 109 Οὕτως ἀπέρχετ᾽ ἐκεῖ καταλιπών με  
> (Thus he departs, leaving me there,)
>
> 110 Ὁ γλυκὺς πατήρ· ἐγὼ δ᾽ ἐν ἀμφιβόλῳ  
> (The sweet father; and I in doubt)
>
> 111 Μένω· τὸ Ναὶ γὰρ καὶ τ᾽ Οὔ νοῦν μου σαλεύει.  
> (Remain; for Yes and No toss my mind to and fro.)

- *Οὕτως ἀπέρχετ᾽ ἐκεῖ καταλιπών με* subordinates *e quivi m’ abbandona* to the verb of departure. *ἀπέρχετ᾽* is elided *ἀπέρχεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *ἀπέρχομαι*); *καταλιπών* is the nom. sg. masc. second aorist active participle of *καταλείπω*, ‘leaving me behind’. Both refer to the delayed *ὁ γλυκὺς πατήρ*, with prosaic article but a literal rendering of *dolce*.
- *ἐγὼ δ᾽ ἐν ἀμφιβόλῳ μένω* retains the explicit contrasting pronoun and closely matches *rimango in forse*: *ἐν* with the neuter adjective in the dative means ‘in uncertainty’. The finite verb is held over to the next line.
- *τὸ Ναὶ … καὶ τ᾽ Οὔ* turns the two particles into names with neuter articles, much as Italian uses *il*: *τ᾽* here is elided *τό*, not connective *τε*. *σαλεύει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *σαλεύω*) treats the pair as a single disturbing force. Rather than contending *nel capo*, they shake *νοῦν μου*, ‘my mind’, the direct object; postpositive *γάρ* retains causal *chè*.

> 112 Udir non potei quello ch’ a lor si porse:  

I could not hear that which [was] offered to them;

> 112 Τί ποτ᾽ εἶπ᾽ αὐτοῖς οὐ ᾽δυνάμην ἀκοῦσαι.  
> (What he said to them I could not hear.)

- *Τί ποτ᾽ εἶπ᾽ αὐτοῖς* substitutes an indirect question, ‘what ever did he say to them?’, for *quello ch’ a lor si porse*. *τί* is the interrogative object of elided *εἶπε*, and *ποτ᾽* is elided enclitic *ποτε*, lending emphasis. The personal active verb settles the Italian construction in favor of something the guide said; *αὐτοῖς* is the dative of the addressees.
- The whole question comes before *οὐ ᾽δυνάμην ἀκοῦσαι*. *᾽δυνάμην* represents imperfect middle indicative *ἐδυνάμην* (1st sg. of *δύναμαι*) with prodelision of the initial augmented vowel after *οὐ*. Greek thus expresses continuing inability where Italian has *non potei*; aorist active infinitive *ἀκοῦσαι* completes the modal verb.

> 113 ma ei non stette là con essi guari,  
> 114 che ciascun dentro a pruova si ricorse.  

but he had not long stood with them, when they all, vying with one another, rushed in again.

> 113 Οὐ πολὺ δὲ μετ᾽ αὐτῶν ἔμειν᾽· ἀλλ᾽ οὗτοι  
> (Yet not long did he stay with them; but they)
>
> 114 Ὑπεχώρησαν ἐπειγομένως ἔσω.  
> (Retreated hastily within.)

- *Οὐ πολὺ δὲ μετ᾽ αὐτῶν ἔμειν᾽* ‘but he did not stay long with them’ takes *πολύ* as an accusative of duration and *μετά* with the genitive of company. *ἔμειν᾽* is elided *ἔμεινε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *μένω*. There is no distinct adverb for *là*.
- *ἀλλ᾽ οὗτοι ὑπεχώρησαν* coordinates a new clause, ‘but these men withdrew’, for the temporal turn with *che*. Plural demonstrative and plural verb replace distributive *ciascun*; *ὑπεχώρησαν* is the 3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *ὑποχωρέω*, augmented after the prefix. *ἐπειγομένως ἔσω* ‘hurriedly inward’ gives haste rather than the rivalry of *a pruova*: *ἐπειγομένως* is an adverb formed on a participial stem, not a participle agreeing with the subject. Return is suggested by the verb of withdrawal rather than separately marked.

> 115 Chiuser le porte quei nostri avversari  
> 116 nel petto al mio signor, che fuor rimase,  
> 117 e rivolsesi a me con passi rari.  

These our adversaries closed the gates on the breast of my [Lord] who remained without; and turned to me with slow steps.

> 115 Οἱ δ᾽ ἡμῶν ἐχθροὶ κατέκλεισαν τὰς πύλας  
> (And our adversaries shut the gates)
>
> 116 Κατὰ πρόσωπον αὐτοῦ μείναντος ἔξω,  
> (In the face of him who remained outside,)
>
> 117 Καὶ βραδεῖ τῷ βήματι στραφέντος πρός με.  
> (And with slow step turned back toward me.)

- *Οἱ δ᾽ ἡμῶν ἐχθροί* ‘and our enemies’ places the possessive genitive within the article–noun group and drops demonstrative *quei*. Unlike *Chiuser le porte quei nostri avversari*, the subject precedes the verb and object. *κατέκλεισαν* is the 3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *κατακλείω*, with internal augment; *τὰς πύλας* is its accusative object.
- *Κατὰ πρόσωπον αὐτοῦ* ‘in his face’ substitutes face for *petto* and a pronoun for *al mio signor*. That genitive is carried through two agreeing gen. sg. masc. aorist participles: *μείναντος* (active, *μένω*) and *στραφέντος* (passive in form, intransitive in sense, *στρέφω*). Since *αὐτοῦ* also identifies whose face is meant, the participles are linked to the main construction, not an entirely detached genitive absolute.
- *μείναντος ἔξω* corresponds to the finite relative *che fuor rimase*, but *καὶ … στραφέντος πρός με* also subordinates *e rivolsesi a me*, instead of giving the guide a new finite verb. *βραδεῖ τῷ βήματι* is a dative of manner with the adjective outside the article–noun group, ‘with his tread slow’; singular *βήματι* replaces plural *passi*, and *βραδεῖ* interprets *rari* as slow.

> 118 Gli occhi alla terra, e le ciglia avea rase  
> 119 d’ ogni baldanza, e dicea ne’ sospiri:  
> 120 “Chi m’ ha negate le dolenti case?”  

He had his eyes upon the ground, and his eyebrows shorn of all boldness, and said with sighs: “Who hath denied me the doleful houses?”

> 118 Τοὺς δ᾽ ὀφθαλμοὺς πρὸς τὴν γῆν, τὰς ὀφρῦς τ᾽ ἄνευ  
> (With his eyes toward the ground, and his brows devoid)
>
> 119 Εὐθύμου ῥοπῆς, ἔλεγ᾽ ὑποστενάζων·  
> (Of cheerful bearing, he spoke with sighs:)
>
> 120 “Τίς ἔκλεισέ μοι τοὺς πολυαλγεῖς δόμους;”  
> ("Who has closed to me the woeful halls?")

- *Τοὺς δ᾽ ὀφθαλμοὺς πρὸς τὴν γῆν, τὰς ὀφρῦς τ᾽ ἄνευ εὐθύμου ῥοπῆς* retains the accusatives of *Gli occhi … e le ciglia avea* but supplies no expressed verb of having. The two phrases hang descriptively beside *ἔλεγε*, as if with ‘having’ understood. *πρός* with accusative gives the direction of the eyes; connective *τ᾽* (elided *τε*) attaches the second description.
- *ἄνευ* governs genitive *εὐθύμου ῥοπῆς*, ‘without a cheerful inclination’. This replaces the participial image *rase d’ ogni baldanza* with privation, neither ‘shorn’ nor ‘all’ being expressed. *ἔλεγ᾽* is elided *ἔλεγε* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *λέγω*), matching *dicea*; *ὑποστενάζων*, nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *ὑποστενάζω*, replaces *ne’ sospiri* with an action simultaneous with speaking.
- *Τίς ἔκλεισέ μοι τοὺς πολυαλγεῖς δόμους;* ‘Who closed the grievous halls to me?’ repeats the vocabulary of shutting rather than translating *negate* as a refusal. *ἔκλεισε* is the 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *κλείω*, with an additional accent before the affected-person dative *μοι*. *πολυαλγεῖς*, acc. pl. masc. of the compound adjective *πολυαλγής*, agrees with poetic *δόμους* and intensifies *dolenti*.

> 121 Ed a me disse: “Tu, perch’ io m’ adiri,  
> 122 non sbigottir: ch’ io vincerò la pruova,  
> 123 qual che alla difension dentro s’ aggiri.  

And to me he said: “Thou, be not dismayed, though I get angry: for I will master the trial, whatever be contrived within for hindrance.

> 121 Εἶτα πρὸς ἐμέ· “ Καίτοι παρωργισμένον  
> (Then to me: "Although angered)
>
> 122 „ Ὁρᾷς με, θάῤῥει· νικήσω τὸν ἀγῶνα,  
> (You see me, take courage; I shall win the struggle,)
>
> 123 „ Εἴπερ τι πρὸς ἄμυναν δράσουσιν ἔνδον.  
> (Even if they do anything for defence within.)

- *Εἶτα πρὸς ἐμέ* ‘then to me’ makes *Ed* temporal and leaves *disse* to be understood from *ἔλεγε*. *Καίτοι παρωργισμένον ὁρᾷς με* recasts *perch’ io m’ adiri* as a concession expressed through the addressee’s perception, ‘although you see me angered’. *ὁρᾷς* is the contracted 2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ὁράω*; *παρωργισμένον*, acc. sg. masc. perfect middle-passive participle of *παροργίζω*, is predicative to *με* and presents anger as a resulting state. Italian *Tu* survives in the verb endings, not as a separate pronoun.
- *θάῤῥει* (2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *θαρρέω*) substitutes positive ‘take courage’ for *non sbigottir*. *νικήσω τὸν ἀγῶνα* follows without a causal particle for *chè*; the 1st sg. future active indicative of *νικάω* takes the contest won as its accusative object. *ἀγῶνα* thus interprets *pruova* as a struggle.
- *Εἴπερ τι πρὸς ἄμυναν δράσουσιν ἔνδον* changes the indefinite relative *qual che … s’ aggiri* into a condition with a definite future verb. *εἴπερ* is strictly ‘if indeed’, concessive in context; enclitic *τι* is the indefinite neuter accusative object, ‘anything’, not interrogative *τί*. *δράσουσιν* (3rd pl. fut. act. ind. of *δράω*) supplies an unnamed plural agent. *πρὸς ἄμυναν* expresses purpose with the accusative, ‘for defence’, while *ἔνδον* retains *dentro* but moves it to the end.

## Lines 124–130 — Past Insolence and the City’s Opening

> 124 Questa lor tracotanza non è nuova,  

This insolence of theirs is nothing new:

> 124 „ Οὐκ ἔστιν αὐτῶν ἥδ᾽ αὐθάδεια νέα·  
> (This arrogance of theirs is not new;)

- *Οὐκ ἔστιν* puts the negated copula first, where the Italian begins with the subject, *Questa lor tracotanza*. *Οὐκ* is the form of *οὐ* before a vowel with smooth breathing; *ἔστιν* (3rd sg. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*) is accented after the negative and has movable *-ν* before *αὐτῶν*.
- *αὐτῶν ἥδ᾽ αὐθάδεια* ‘this self-will of theirs’ puts the possessive genitive plural of *αὐτός* before the demonstrative, reversing *Questa lor*. *ἥδ᾽* is *ἥδε* (nom. sg. fem. of *ὅδε*) with its final vowel elided. It agrees with the anarthrous *αὐθάδεια*; ordinary Attic prose would usually give the noun an article with this demonstrative. *νέα* (nom. sg. fem. of *νέος*) stands last as predicate adjective, like *nuova*.

> 125 chè già l’ usaro a men segreta porta,  

for they shewed it once at a less secret gate,

> 125 „ Ἔδειξαν αὐτὴν ἐν ἧττον κρυπτῇ θύρᾳ,  
> (They showed it at a less secret door,)

- *Ἔδειξαν αὐτήν* ‘they showed it’ interprets *l’usaro* ‘they exercised it’ as a display of insolence. *Ἔδειξαν* is the augmented 3rd pl. first aor. act. ind. of *δείκνυμι* (stem *δεικ-* plus *-σ-* gives *-ξ-*); *αὐτήν*, the accusative feminine singular of *αὐτός*, resumes *αὐθάδεια* as its direct object. Musurus leaves the explanation in asyndeton, without a conjunction for causal *chè* or an adverb for *già*: the aorist presents a past act but does not itself say “once.”
- *ἐν ἧττον κρυπτῇ θύρᾳ* ‘at a less hidden door’ takes *a* as marking location, hence *ἐν* with the dative. *ἧττον* is the Attic comparative adverb ‘less’ (neuter singular of *ἥττων* used adverbially); like *men*, it modifies the positive adjective. *κρυπτῇ* (from *κρυπτός*) agrees in the dative feminine singular with *θύρᾳ* (from *θύρα*).

> 126 la qual senza serrame ancor si trova.  

which still is found unbarred.

> 126 „ Ἥτις ἔτι νῦν ἄνευ κλήθρων ὑπάρχει.  
> (Which still now remains without bolts.)

- *Ἥτις* (nom. sg. fem. of the compound relative *ὅστις*) picks up the particular *θύρᾳ* in a supplementary relative clause, not an indefinite condition. Like *la qual*, it takes its gender and number from the antecedent but its case from its function as subject of its own verb.
- *ἔτι νῦν* ‘still now’ expands *ancor* and moves it ahead of *ἄνευ κλήθρων* ‘without bolts’. *ἄνευ* governs the genitive plural of *κλῆθρον*, making Italian singular *serrame* concrete and plural. *ὑπάρχει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ὑπάρχω*) expresses the continuing state: where *si trova* has reflexive form, the Greek simply says that the door exists in that condition.

> 127 Sovr’ essa vedestù la scritta morta;  

Over it thou sawest the dead inscription;

> 127 „ Ἐπ᾽ αὐτῆς εἶδες ἐπιγραφὴν θανάτου·  
> (Above it you saw the inscription of death;)

- *Ἐπ᾽ αὐτῆς* follows *Sovr’ essa* in position: *ἐπ᾽* is *ἐπί* with final *-ι* elided, here governing the genitive feminine singular of *αὐτός* for position ‘upon it’. *εἶδες* is the 2nd sg. second aor. act. ind. belonging to *ὁράω*, from its suppletive aorist *εἶδον*. The ending alone conveys the subject expressed in *vedestù*.
- *ἐπιγραφὴν θανάτου* ‘an inscription of death’ replaces the definite noun and agreeing adjective of *la scritta morta* with an anarthrous direct object (*ἐπιγραφήν*, acc. sg.) and a dependent genitive (*θανάτου*, gen. sg. of *θάνατος*). The Greek thus relates the inscription to death rather than calling the inscription itself dead.

> 128 e già di qua da lei discende l’ erta,  
> 129 passando per li cerchi senza scorta  
> 130 tal, che per lui ne fia la terra aperta.”  

and already, on this side of it, comes down the steep, passing the circles without escort, one by whom the city shall be opened to us.”

> 128 „ Ἐξ αὐτῆς ἤδη τὸν κρημνὸν καταβαίνει,  
> (From it already descends the steep,)
>
> 129 „ Τοὺς κύκλους διαβαίνων ὀπαδῶν ἄνευ  
> (Traversing the circles without attendants,)
>
> 130 „ Ὁ Δεῖνα, δι᾽ οὗ τἄστυ προσιτὸν ἔσται.”  
> (Such-a-one, through whom the city shall be accessible.")

- *Ἐξ αὐτῆς ἤδη* ‘from it already’ omits the connective *e* and puts the prepositional phrase before the adverb *ἤδη* (*già*). *Ἐξ*, the form of *ἐκ* before a vowel, governs the feminine singular genitive *αὐτῆς*. It identifies the door as the point of departure, rather than distinguishing its nearer side as *di qua da lei* does.
- *τὸν κρημνὸν καταβαίνει* ‘descends the steep’ puts the route before the verb, reversing *discende l’erta*. The adjective used substantivally in Italian becomes the masculine noun *κρημνός* ‘precipice, steep slope’, in the accusative of the ground traversed. *καταβαίνει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *καταβαίνω*) awaits its subject until line 130, as does *discende*.
- *Τοὺς κύκλους διαβαίνων* ‘traversing the circles’ turns *per li cerchi* into the accusative object of *διαβαίνω*, whose prefix *δια-* conveys passage through or across. In place of the invariable gerund *passando*, *διαβαίνων* is a pres. act. participle, nominative masculine singular in agreement with the delayed subject; it expresses action simultaneous with the descent. *ὀπαδῶν ἄνευ* ‘without attendants’ replaces singular *scorta* with the genitive plural of *ὀπαδός* and, unlike *senza* or *ἄνευ* in line 126, puts the governing word last.
- *Ὁ Δεῖνα* ‘so-and-so’ occupies the delayed subject position of *tal*. The masculine singular article and nominative *δεῖνα* designate a particular person left unnamed, rather than characterizing him as ‘such a one’. *δι᾽ οὗ* ‘through whom’ recasts *che per lui* as a single prepositional relative: *δι᾽* is elided *διά*, and *οὗ* is the genitive masculine singular of *ὅς*, indicating the person through whose intervention the result will follow.
- *τἄστυ προσιτὸν ἔσται* ‘the city will be accessible’ has *τἄστυ* by crasis of *τὸ ἄστυ* (the coronis marks the contraction). This neuter nominative singular subject renders *la terra* specifically as a city. *προσιτόν* (nom. sg. neut. of *προσιτός*) is a predicate adjective, not a passive participle: with *ἔσται* (3rd sg. fut. mid. ind. of *εἰμί*, with copular sense), it substitutes a future state of accessibility for *fia … aperta*. There is no dative corresponding to *ne*, “to us.”

## Summary — Participles and Shifts of Emphasis

Musurus often keeps Dante’s order while changing which action carries the sentence. At the opening, *λέγω καθεξῆς* follows *Io dico seguitando*, but an adverb, ‘in sequence’, replaces the gerund. Where Dante makes the eyes themselves rise, *ἀντείναντες* (‘having raised’) makes the observers responsible for the movement and subordinates it to *εἴδομεν* (‘we saw’). Participles recur especially after verbs of perception: *ἐρχόμενον* (‘coming’) answers to the Italian infinitive *venir*, while *σπαραχθέντα* (‘torn apart’) turns the Italian noun *strazio* into something predicated of the person seen. The present participle shows motion in progress; the aorist passive presents the rending as a whole.

In dialogue, Musurus stays close to Dante’s verbless changes of speaker: *κἀγὼ πρὸς αὐτόν* (‘and I to him’) preserves even the order of *Ed io a lui*. Elsewhere postpositive *δέ* marks a change of speaker, *μέν* prepares a contrast, and *γάρ* makes an explanation explicit. These particles follow the relation between clauses rather than mechanically translating a conjunction: *δέ* can replace causal *chè*, while *καί* can turn an Italian result clause into a fresh assertion. Crasis and elision give recurrent phrases compact forms, notably *κἀγώ* for *καὶ ἐγώ* and possessive *οὑμός* for *ὁ ἐμός*.

Musurus can preserve a construction while changing its image: *πρὸς τὴν πηγὴν πάσης σοφίας* (‘toward the fount of all wisdom’) retains the direction and dependent genitive of *al mar di tutto il senno*, but substitutes a source for a sea. Elsewhere the grammatical change is itself an interpretation. *ὀργὴν δαμάσας* (‘having curbed his wrath’) reads *nell’ ira accolta* as an act of restraint; *ἐπληρώθη* (‘was filled’) turns the appearance in *parve carca* into a fact. The sharpest departure comes in lines 55–56: the shore, subject of Dante’s clause with *Avanti che* (‘before’), becomes the object of *βλέπων* (‘looking at’), and *οὐ μὴ παύσῃ* (‘you will certainly not cease’) replaces the temporal limit with an emphatic prediction. At the close, by contrast, Musurus keeps the subject waiting until the last line, just as Dante does, even as *προσιτὸν ἔσται* (‘will be accessible’) shifts the emphasis from the act of opening to its result.
