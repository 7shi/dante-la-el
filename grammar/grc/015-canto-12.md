# Inferno 12

## Lines 1–18 — The Rocky Descent and the Infamy of Crete

> 1 Era lo loco, ove a scender la riva  
> 2 venimmo, alpestro, e per quel ch’ ivi er’ anco,  
> 3 tal, ch’ ogni vista ne sarebbe schiva.  

The place to which we came, in order to descend the bank, was alpine, and such, from what was there besides, that every eye would shun it.

> 1 Ὁ τόπος, ἔνθ᾽ ἤλθομεν τοῦ καταβῆναι  
> (The place where we came to descend)
>
> 2 Τὴν ὄχθην, τἀκεῖ τ᾽ ἦσαν οὕτω τραχέα,  
> (The bank, and the parts there, were so rugged,)
>
> 3 Ὥστε τὰς ὄψεις ἀπέτρεπεν ἡ θέα.  
> (That the spectacle turned away one's eyes.)

- *Ὁ τόπος, ἔνθ᾽ ἤλθομεν* ‘the place where we came’ puts the subject first, postponing the copula with which Dante opens (*Era lo loco*). The relative adverb *ἔνθα* renders *ove*; its final vowel is elided before *ἤλθομεν*. The latter is the augmented 1st pl. second aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι*, corresponding to *venimmo*.
- *τοῦ καταβῆναι τὴν ὄχθην* renders *a scender la riva* by a genitive articular infinitive of purpose. *καταβῆναι* is the second (root) aorist active infinitive of *καταβαίνω*; its understood subject is that of *ἤλθομεν*. *τὴν ὄχθην* is accusative, the bank traversed in descending, as *la riva* is construed directly with *scender*.
- *τἀκεῖ τ᾽ ἦσαν οὕτω τραχέα* makes ‘the things there’ a second subject alongside *ὁ τόπος*, instead of giving *per quel ch’ ivi er’ anco* as a further reason for the place’s appearance. *τἀκεῖ* is crasis for *τὰ ἐκεῖ*, the neuter plural article substantivizing an adverb; postpositive *τ᾽* is elided *τε* ‘and’. The compound subject has a plural verb (*ἦσαν*, 3rd pl. imperfect of *εἰμί*) and a neuter plural predicate (*τραχέα*, from *τραχύς*). ‘So rugged’ combines *alpestro* and *tal* in one expression of degree.
- *οὕτω … ὥστε* introduces an actual result: *τὰς ὄψεις ἀπέτρεπεν ἡ θέα* ‘the sight turned the eyes away’. *ἀπέτρεπεν* is 3rd sg. imperfect active indicative of *ἀποτρέπω*, not conditional like *sarebbe*. Musurus reverses the relation: *ogni vista*, the subject that would shun the place, becomes the plural object *τὰς ὄψεις*, without a word for *ogni*; the spectacle itself becomes the subject, *ἡ θέα*.

> 4 Qual è quella ruina, che nel fianco  
> 5 di qua da Trento l’ Adice percosse  
> 6 o per tremuoto o per sostegno manco,—  

As is the ruin, which struck the Adige in its flank, on this side Trent, caused by earthquake or by defective prop,—

> 4 Ὡς ἐν τῇ πτώσει τῇ ᾽πὶ τάδε Τριδέντου  
> (As in the landslide on this side of Trent)
>
> 5 Συμβάσῃ μιᾷ τῶν πλευρῶν Ἀτισῶνος  
> (Which befell one of the flanks of the Adige)
>
> 6 Ἢ διὰ σεισμόν, ἢ τῆς γῆς ὑπενδούσης,  
> (Either through an earthquake, or from the earth giving way beneath,)

- *Ὡς ἐν τῇ πτώσει* ‘as in the fall’ replaces *Qual è quella ruina*. Instead of immediately comparing one descent with another by a copula, Musurus sets the comparison within the landslide (*ἐν* with the dative of *πτῶσις*); the subject and verb of the *ὡς* clause come in lines 7–9.
- *τῇ ᾽πὶ τάδε Τριδέντου συμβάσῃ* repeats the article to attach an attributive phrase to *πτώσει*: ‘the one that occurred on this side of Trent’. *᾽πὶ* is *ἐπί* with its initial vowel lost by prodelision; *τάδε* is neuter accusative plural of *ὅδε*, with the genitive *Τριδέντου* marking the point of reference for *di qua da Trento*.
- *συμβάσῃ* is the dative feminine singular second aorist active participle of *συμβαίνω*, agreeing with *πτώσει*. Where *che … l’ Adice percosse* makes the landslide actively strike the river, Greek says that it ‘befell one of the flanks of the Adige’: *μιᾷ* is dative with *συμβάσῃ*, *τῶν πλευρῶν* a partitive genitive after ‘one’, and *Ἀτισῶνος* the possessive genitive defining the flanks. The explicit choice of ‘one’ among several is Musurus’s.
- *ἢ διὰ σεισμόν, ἢ τῆς γῆς ὑπενδούσης* keeps *o … o*, but varies the syntax where Italian repeats *per*. The first cause has *διά* with the accusative; the second is a causal genitive absolute, ‘the ground having given way beneath’. *ὑπενδούσης* is the genitive feminine singular second aorist active participle of *ὑπενδίδωμι*, with *τῆς γῆς* as its subject. This interprets rather than literally translates *per sostegno manco*.

> 7 chè da cima del monte, onde si mosse,  
> 8 al piano è sì la roccia discoscesa  
> 9 ch’ alcuna via darebbe a chi su fosse:  

for from the summit of the mountain, whence it moved, to the plain, the rock is shattered so, that it might give some passage to one that were above

> 7 Ἡ ᾽κ τῆς ὄρους κορυφῆς εἰς τὸ πεδίον  
> (The rock from the mountain's summit to the plain)
>
> 8 Ἀποῤῥαγεῖσα πέτρα κρημνώδης οὕτως  
> (Broken off, so precipitous)
>
> 9 Ἔσθ᾽, ὥστ᾽ ἄνωθεν οὐκ ἔνι καταβῆναι,  
> (Is, that from above one cannot descend,)

- There is no separate equivalent of explanatory *chè*: these lines supply the main predication within the comparison begun by *ὡς*. *Ἡ ᾽κ τῆς ὄρους κορυφῆς εἰς τὸ πεδίον ἀποῤῥαγεῖσα πέτρα* means ‘the rock broken away from the mountain’s summit to the plain’. The opening *ἡ* belongs to *πέτρα*; the intervening phrases give the starting point (*᾽κ*, prodelided *ἐκ*, with the genitive) and endpoint (*εἰς* with the accusative). In *τῆς ὄρους κορυφῆς* the feminine article belongs to *κορυφῆς*, not to the intervening neuter genitive *ὄρους*.
- *ἀποῤῥαγεῖσα*, nominative feminine singular second aorist passive participle of *ἀπορρήγνυμι*, makes the breaking away an attribute of *πέτρα*. The spelling *ῤῥ* marks the doubled rho in the compound. There is no relative clause for *onde si mosse*: the participle and its phrase of origin convey the movement. The finite predicate is instead *κρημνώδης οὕτως ἔσθ᾽* ‘is so precipitous’, with *κρημνώδης* nominative feminine singular and *ἔσθ᾽* for *ἐστί*, elided with aspiration before *ὥστε*.
- *ὥστ᾽ ἄνωθεν οὐκ ἔνι καταβῆναι* ‘so that it is not possible to descend from above’ reverses the sense of *ch’ alcuna via darebbe a chi su fosse*: Dante allows some passage, whereas Musurus expressly negates the possibility. *ὥστ᾽* is elided *ὥστε*; *ἔνι* stands for *ἔνεστι* in its impersonal sense ‘it is possible’, completed by the aorist infinitive *καταβῆναι*. *ἄνωθεν* ‘from above’ takes the place of the person described by *a chi su fosse*; neither the conditional *darebbe* nor the subjunctive *fosse* is matched by a Greek optative.

> 10 cotal di quel burrato era la scesa;  

: such of that rocky steep was the descent;

> 10 Τοιάδ᾽ ἡ κατάβασις ἦν κρημνοῦ ᾽κείνου.  
> (Such was the descent of that precipice.)

- *Τοιάδ᾽ ἡ κατάβασις ἦν κρημνοῦ ᾽κείνου* answers *ὡς* with ‘such was the descent’. Predicate *τοιάδ᾽* (elided *τοιάδε*, nominative feminine singular of *τοιόσδε*) stands first like *cotal* and agrees with the subject *ἡ κατάβασις*. *ἦν* is the 3rd sg. imperfect of *εἰμί*. Greek puts the dependent genitive last rather than before the verb as in *di quel burrato era la scesa*: *κρημνοῦ ᾽κείνου* ‘of that precipice’, with prodelision in *᾽κείνου* for *ἐκείνου*.

> 11 e in su la punta della rotta lacca  
> 12 l’ infamia di Creti era distesa,  
> 13 che fu concetta nella falsa vacca;  

and on the top of the broken cleft lay spread the infamy of Crete, which was conceived in the false cow;

> 11 Ἐπ᾽ ἄκρου δὲ τῆς πέτρας ἔκειτ᾽ ἐκτάδην  
> (And upon the top of the rock lay stretched out)
>
> 12 Ταἶσχος τῆς Κρήτης, τέρας, ὃ συνελήφθη  
> (The infamy of Crete, a monster, which was conceived)
>
> 13 Ἐν τῇ τῆς ψευδοῦς βοὸς αἰσχρᾷ γαστέρι.  
> (In the shameful belly of the false cow.)

- *Ἐπ᾽ ἄκρου δὲ τῆς πέτρας* renders *e in su la punta della rotta lacca*. Postpositive *δέ* supplies the connective; *ἐπ᾽* is elided *ἐπί*, here with the genitive of position. Substantival neuter *ἄκρου* ‘top’ takes the dependent genitive *τῆς πέτρας* ‘of the rock’: the Greek neither names a cleft nor repeats ‘broken’.
- *ἔκειτ᾽ ἐκτάδην* ‘lay at full length’ replaces *era distesa* with the 3rd sg. imperfect *ἔκειτο* of *κεῖμαι* (middle in form, elided before the vowel) and an adverb of posture. The verb now precedes its subject, *Ταἶσχος τῆς Κρήτης*, where *Ταἶσχος* represents *τὸ αἶσχος* in crasis. The neuter abstract noun ‘disgrace’ closely matches *l’ infamia*.
- *τέρας* ‘monster’ is supplied in apposition, nominative neuter singular; it gives the relative *ὃ* an immediately preceding concrete antecedent. *συνελήφθη*, 3rd sg. aorist passive indicative of *συλλαμβάνω*, reproduces *fu concetta* with a single finite verb, augmented after the preverb.
- *ἐν τῇ τῆς ψευδοῦς βοὸς αἰσχρᾷ γαστέρι* makes *nella falsa vacca* more explicit: ‘in the shameful belly of the false cow’. *ἐν* governs the dative *γαστέρι* (an uncontracted poetic form beside *γαστρί*, from *γαστήρ*); *τῇ* and the supplied adjective *αἰσχρᾷ* agree with it. Enclosed between article and adjective is the possessive genitive *τῆς ψευδοῦς βοός*, with feminine *βοός* from *βοῦς* and contracted *ψευδοῦς* from *ψευδής*.

> 14 e quando vide noi sè stesso morse,  
> 15 sì come quei, cui l’ ira dentro fiacca.  

and when he saw us he gnawed himself, like one whom anger inwardly consumes.

> 14 Ὡς δ᾿ εἶδεν ἡμᾶς ἤρξαθ᾿ ἑαυτὸν δάκνων,  
> (And when he saw us, he began biting himself,)
>
> 15 Καθάπερ τις, ὃν θυμὸς ἔνδον ἐμφλέγει.  
> (Like one whom wrath inflames within.)

- *Ὡς δ᾿ εἶδεν ἡμᾶς* renders *e quando vide noi*: temporal *ὡς* introduces the clause, and elided, postpositive *δέ* links it to what precedes. *εἶδεν* is 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative in the paradigm of *ὁράω*, with *ἡμᾶς* as its accusative object.
- *ἤρξαθ᾿ ἑαυτὸν δάκνων* ‘he began biting himself’ expands the single completed action *sè stesso morse*. *ἤρξαθ᾿* is *ἤρξατο*, 3rd sg. aorist middle indicative of *ἄρχω* in the sense ‘begin’; elision before rough-breathed *ἑαυτόν* changes *τ* to *θ*. The supplementary present active participle *δάκνων* (nominative masculine singular of *δάκνω*) presents the biting as in progress; the masculine singular reflexive accusative is its object.
- *καθάπερ τις, ὃν θυμὸς ἔνδον ἐμφλέγει* ‘just like someone whom anger inflames within’ substitutes indefinite enclitic *τις* for *quei*. *ὃν* is accusative masculine singular, direct object of *ἐμφλέγει* (3rd sg. present active indicative of *ἐμφλέγω*), rather than a formal counterpart to Italian *cui*. *θυμός* and *ἔνδον* closely follow *l’ ira dentro*, but ‘inflames’ changes the image of *fiacca* ‘breaks down’.

> 16 Lo savio mio inver lui gridò : “Forse  
> 17 tu credi che qui sia il duca d’ Atene,  
> 18 che su nel mondo la morte ti porse?  

My Sage cried towards him : "Perhaps thou thinkest the Duke of Athens may be here, who, in the world above, gave thee thy death ?

> 16 Πρὸς αὐτὸν οὑμὸς Σοφὸς ἔκραξεν· “Ἴσως  
> (Toward him my Sage cried out: "Perhaps)
>
> 17 „ Φρονεῖς ὧδ᾿ εἶναι τὸν τῶν Ἀθηνῶν ἥρω,  
> (You suppose the hero of Athens to be here,)
>
> 18 „ Τὸν ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ τὸν θάνατόν σοι δόντα.  
> (Who in the world gave death to you.)

- *Πρὸς αὐτὸν οὑμὸς Σοφὸς ἔκραξεν* puts the direction of address first, whereas *Lo savio mio inver lui gridò* begins with the speaker. *πρός* takes the accusative; *οὑμός* is crasis for *ὁ ἐμός*, with the possessive before the substantival adjective *Σοφός*. *ἔκραξεν* is 3rd sg. aorist active indicative of *κράζω*.
- *Ἴσως φρονεῖς ὧδ᾿ εἶναι* retains *Forse* as a sentence adverb but turns *tu credi che qui sia* into indirect statement by accusative and infinitive. *φρονεῖς* is the contracted 2nd sg. present active indicative of *φρονέω*; its ending suffices for *tu*. *ὧδ᾿* is elided locative *ὧδε* ‘here’, and *εἶναι* the present infinitive of *εἰμί*, not a finite subjunctive corresponding to *sia*.
- *τὸν τῶν Ἀθηνῶν ἥρω* is the accusative subject of *εἶναι*. *ἥρω* is the Attic accusative singular of *ἥρως* (beside *ἥρωα*): ‘hero’ replaces the title *duca*. The genitive plural *τῶν Ἀθηνῶν*, the Greek city name being plural, comes between the article and the noun it qualifies.
- *τὸν ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ τὸν θάνατόν σοι δόντα* turns *che … la morte ti porse* into an attributive participial phrase. The first *τόν* resumes *ἥρω* and belongs with *δόντα*, accusative masculine singular second aorist active participle of *δίδωμι*; the second *τόν* belongs to *θάνατον*, its direct object. Enclitic *σοι* is the dative recipient corresponding to *ti* and accounts for the additional accent in *θάνατόν*. Greek says simply ‘gave’ for *porse*, and *ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ* ‘in the world’ leaves *su* unexpressed.

## Lines 19–51 — The Minotaur, the Fallen Crag, and the River of Blood

> 19 Partiti, bestia, chè questi non viene  
> 20 ammaestrato dalla tua sorella,  
> 21 ma vassi per veder le vostre pene.”  

Get thee gone, Monster! for this one comes not, instructed by thy sister; but passes on to see your punishments.”

> 19 „ Ἐκποδών, κτῆνος, οὐκ ἦλθ᾿ οὗτος ἐνθάδε  
> (Begone, beast; this man did not come hither)
>
> 20 „ Κατὰ διδασκαλίαν τῆς ἀδελφῆς σου,  
> (According to the instruction of your sister,)
>
> 21 „ Ἀλλ᾿ ἔρχετ᾿ ὀψόμενος τὰς σὰς βασάνους.”  
> (But he comes to behold your torments.")

- *Ἐκποδών, κτῆνος* ‘out of the way, beast!’ replaces the reflexive imperative *Partiti* with an adverb used as a command; *κτῆνος* is the neuter singular vocative, identical in form to the nominative. The explanation follows without a conjunction corresponding to *chè*.
- *οὐκ ἦλθ᾿ οὗτος ἐνθάδε* ‘this one did not come here’ makes *questi* the postverbal subject *οὗτος*. *ἦλθ᾿* is elided *ἦλθε*, third singular second aorist indicative of *ἔρχομαι*: the aorist looks back on the coming, where *non viene* is present. The directional adverb *ἐνθάδε* is supplied.
- *κατὰ διδασκαλίαν τῆς ἀδελφῆς σου* ‘according to your sister’s instruction’ replaces the passive participle *ammaestrato* and its agent *dalla tua sorella* with *κατά* plus accusative and a subjective genitive: the sister gives the instruction. *σου* is the enclitic second-person possessive genitive.
- *ἀλλ᾿ ἔρχετ᾿ ὀψόμενος* ‘but he comes to see’ uses the present *ἔρχεται* after the aorist *ἦλθε*; both *ἀλλά* and the ending of *ἔρχεται* undergo elision. The future middle participle *ὀψόμενος* (nominative masculine singular, from *ὁράω*) expresses purpose after a verb of motion, in place of *per veder*. *τὰς σὰς βασάνους* retains the plural *pene* but makes the possessor singular, ‘your own torments’, rather than the plural of *vostre*.

> 22 Qual è quel toro che si slaccia in quella  
> 23 che ha ricevuto già ’l colpo mortale,  
> 24 che gir non sa, ma qua e là saltella:  

As a bull, that breaks loose, in the moment when he has received the fatal stroke, and cannot go, but plunges hither and thither:

> 22 Ὡς ὁ τὰ δεσμὰ συγκαταθλάσας ταῦρος  
> (Like the bull that breaks its bonds to pieces)
>
> 23 Καθ᾿ ἣν στιγμὴν βάλλεται θανατηφόρως,  
> (At the moment when it is struck mortally,)
>
> 24 Σκοτοδινιῶν πηδᾷ τῇδε κἀκεῖσε,  
> (Dizzy, leaps hither and thither,)

- *ὡς ὁ … ταῦρος* ‘as the bull …’ answers to *Qual è quel toro* without either the copula or a demonstrative. Between article and noun comes the attributive participle *τὰ δεσμὰ συγκαταθλάσας* ‘having smashed its bonds’: *συγκαταθλάσας* is nominative masculine singular, first aorist active of *συγκαταθλάω*. Greek gives the reflexive *si slaccia* an active verb and an explicit accusative object.
- *καθ᾿ ἣν στιγμήν* ‘at which instant’ renders *in quella che* by incorporating the time noun into the relative phrase. *καθ᾿* represents *κατά* elided and aspirated before rough-breathed *ἣν*, accusative feminine singular of *ὅς*. *βάλλεται θανατηφόρως* ‘it is struck mortally’ substitutes a present passive (third singular of *βάλλω*) and an adverb for *ha ricevuto già ’l colpo mortale*: neither the completed tense nor *già* is retained.
- *σκοτοδινιῶν* ‘reeling’ is a circumstantial present active participle (nominative masculine singular of *σκοτοδινιάω*). It interprets the inability in *gir non sa* as dizziness, rather than translating ‘go’ or ‘know how’. The main verb is now *πηδᾷ* ‘it leaps’ (third singular present active of *πηδάω*); there is no adversative corresponding to *ma*. *τῇδε κἀκεῖσε* gives the two directions of *qua e là*, with crasis in *κἀκεῖσε* for *καὶ ἐκεῖσε*.

> 25 vid’ io lo Minotauro far cotale.  

so I saw the Minotaur do.

> 25 Οὕτω τὸν Μινώταυρον εἶδον ποιοῦντα.  
> (So I saw the Minotaur doing.)

- *οὕτω* ‘thus’ brings forward the sense of *cotale* and answers the initial *ὡς*. In *τὸν Μινώταυρον εἶδον ποιοῦντα*, *εἶδον* is first singular second aorist active indicative of *ὁράω*; its subject needs no separate *io*. The Italian perception construction *vid’ … lo Minotauro far* becomes an accusative with supplementary participle: *ποιοῦντα* (present active of *ποιέω*) agrees with the object and presents the action as witnessed.

> 26 E quegli accorto gridò: “Corri al varco;  
> 27 mentre ch’ è in furia, è buon che tu ti cale.”  

And my wary Guide cried: “Run to the passage; whilst he is in fury, it is good that thou descend.”

> 26 Τότ᾿ ἔκραξ᾿ ὁ Προστάτης· “Διάβα τάχει·  
> (Then the Protector cried out: "Cross swiftly;)
>
> 27 „ Ἐν ᾧ μανιᾷ καλόν σοι καταβῆναι.”  
> (While he rages, it is good for you to descend.")

- *τότ᾿ ἔκραξ᾿ ὁ Προστάτης* ‘then the protector cried’ supplies a temporal connective and a noun in place of *E quegli accorto*. *Προστάτης* names his role rather than translating the adjective *accorto*; there is no possessive in the Greek. *τότ᾿* and *ἔκραξ᾿* are elided *τότε* and *ἔκραξε* (third singular first aorist active indicative of *κράζω*).
- *διάβα τάχει* ‘cross with speed’ transfers the idea of *al varco* to the verb and the urgency of *Corri* to the dative of manner *τάχει* (from *τάχος*). *διάβα* is a second singular aorist command from *διαβαίνω*, not the usual Attic *διάβηθι*.
- *ἐν ᾧ* ‘during which [time]’ functions as *mentre*. *μανιᾷ* (third singular present active of *μανιάω*) turns *è in furia* into a verb. *καλόν σοι καταβῆναι* ‘it is good for you to descend’ omits the copula and replaces *che tu ti cale* with a dative of the person concerned and the second aorist active infinitive of *καταβαίνω*.

> 28 Così prendemmo via giù per lo scarco  
> 29 di quelle pietre, che spesso moviensi  
> 30 sotto i miei piedi per lo nuovo carco.  

Thus we took our way downwards on the ruin of those stones, which often moved beneath my feet, from the unusual weight.

> 28 Οὕτω κατεβαίνομεν ἐπὶ θημώνων  
> (Thus we went down over heaps)
>
> 29 Ἐκ περιφερῶν λίθων ὑπὸ τοὺς πόδας  
> (Of round stones beneath our feet)
>
> 30 Τῇ βαρύτητι τῇ ᾿μῇ κυλινδουμένων.  
> (Rolling under my weight.)

- *οὕτω κατεβαίνομεν* ‘thus we were descending’ replaces *Così prendemmo via giù*: the direction is in the compound verb, and the internally augmented imperfect (first plural of *καταβαίνω*) presents the descent in progress rather than as the taking of a route.
- *ἐπὶ θημώνων ἐκ περιφερῶν λίθων* ‘over heaps made of rounded stones’ renders *per lo scarco di quelle pietre*. *ἐπί* with the genitive specifies the surface traversed; *ἐκ* with the genitive specifies the material. The heaps are plural, *περιφερῶν* ‘rounded’ is supplied, and *quelle* has no demonstrative equivalent.
- *κυλινδουμένων* ‘rolling’ is a present middle/passive participle, genitive masculine plural agreeing with *λίθων*, not a genitive absolute. It gives *moviensi* a more specific motion but drops *spesso*. *ὑπὸ τοὺς πόδας* uses *ὑπό* with the accusative for movement beneath the feet; the feet themselves have no possessive. Instead *τῇ βαρύτητι τῇ ᾿μῇ* ‘through my weight’ attaches possession to the causal dative. *τῇ ᾿μῇ* is the repeated article with *ἐμῇ* in second attributive position, its initial vowel lost by prodelision. *My* weight, not its newness (*nuovo*), is specified.

> 31 Io gìa pensando; e quei disse: “Tu pensi  
> 32 forse a questa rovina, ch’ è guardata  
> 33 da quell’ ira bestial ch’ io ora spensi.  

I went musing, and he said: “Perhaps thou art thinking of this fallen mass, guarded by that bestial rage, which I quelled just now.

> 31 Κἀγὼ μὲν ἐσκεπτόμην· ὁ δ᾿ εἶπε· “Σκέπτῃ  
> (And I was pondering; and he said: "You ponder)
>
> 32 „ Ἴσως περὶ τῆς πέτρας τῆς φρουρουμένης  
> (Perhaps about the rock which is guarded)
>
> 33 „Ὑπὸ τοῦ μανιῶντος, ὃν νῦν καθεῖλον·  
> (By the raging one, whom I just now quelled;)

- *κἀγὼ μὲν ἐσκεπτόμην· ὁ δ᾿ εἶπε* ‘and I was pondering; he, in turn, said’ balances the speakers with *μέν … δέ*. *κἀγώ* is crasis for *καὶ ἐγώ*; *ὁ* in *ὁ δέ* has pronominal force, corresponding to *quei*. *ἐσκεπτόμην* (first singular imperfect middle of *σκέπτομαι*) expresses the duration of *gìa pensando* without a verb of going; *εἶπε* is third singular second aorist active indicative of *λέγω*.
- *σκέπτῃ ἴσως* ‘you are pondering, perhaps’ repeats the verb as *pensando … pensi* does. *σκέπτῃ* is second singular present middle indicative; *περὶ τῆς πέτρας* uses *περί* with the genitive for the topic of thought. *πέτρα* ‘rock’ replaces *questa rovina*, without the demonstrative or an explicit indication of collapse.
- *τῆς φρουρουμένης* (genitive feminine singular present passive participle of *φρουρέω*) is attributive to *πέτρας*, replacing *ch’ è guardata*. *ὑπὸ τοῦ μανιῶντος* gives the personal agent, ‘by the one who rages’, with a substantivized present active participle from *μανιάω*, instead of the abstract *da quell’ ira bestial*. Consequently *ὃν* is masculine accusative, object of *καθεῖλον* ‘I brought down, subdued’ (first singular second aorist active of *καθαιρέω*): it is the raging one, not the rage, that the Greek verb acts on. *νῦν* retains *ora*.

> 34 Or vo’ che sappi, che l’ altra fiata  
> 35 ch’ i’ discesi quaggiù nel basso inferno,  
> 36 questa roccia non era ancor cascata.  

I would have thee know, that, when I went the other time, down here to the deep Hell, this rock had not yet fallen.

> 34 „Θέλω σ᾽ εἰδέναι τοίνυν, ὅτι τὸ πρῶτον  
> (I would have you know, then, that the first time)
>
> 35 „Ὅτε κατέβην εἰς βυθὸν ᾍδου τόνδε,  
> (When I descended into this depth of Hell,)
>
> 36 „Ἀπόῤῥωξ ἥδ᾽ ἦν οὔπω συμπεπτωκυῖα.  
> (This crag had not yet collapsed.)

- *θέλω σ᾽ εἰδέναι τοίνυν* ‘I want you, then, to know’ recasts *Or vo’ che sappi* with an accusative subject, *σ᾽* for *σε*, and *εἰδέναι*, the perfect active infinitive of *οἶδα* with present meaning. Postpositive *τοίνυν* marks the transition; *ὅτι* introduces what is to be known.
- *τὸ πρῶτον ὅτε κατέβην* ‘the first time, when I descended’ interprets *l’altra fiata* as the first occasion. *τὸ πρῶτον* is an adverbial neuter accusative; *κατέβην* is first singular root aorist active indicative of *καταβαίνω*. *εἰς βυθὸν ᾍδου τόνδε* ‘into this depth of Hades’ turns *quaggiù nel basso inferno* into a destination with *εἰς* and the accusative. The delayed demonstrative *τόνδε* agrees with *βυθόν*, not with the intervening genitive *ᾍδου*.
- *ἀπόῤῥωξ ἥδ᾽* ‘this crag’ is the nominative subject; *ἥδ᾽* is elided *ἥδε*, feminine singular of *ὅδε*. The spelling *ῤῥ* marks doubled rho within the compound. *ἦν οὔπω συμπεπτωκυῖα* ‘was not yet in a fallen state’ closely matches *non era ancor cascata*: imperfect *ἦν* plus the agreeing nominative feminine singular perfect active participle of *συμπίπτω* makes a periphrastic pluperfect.

> 37 Ma certo poco pria, se ben discerno,  
> 38 che venisse Colui che la gran preda  
> 39 levò a Dite del cerchio superno,  

But certainly, if I distinguish rightly, short while before He came, who took from Dis the great prey of the upmost circle,

> 37 „Ἀλλ᾽, ἐὰν μὴ λανθάνω, μικρὸν πρὶν ἔλθῃ  
> (But, if I am not mistaken, shortly before he came)
>
> 38 „Ὁ τὴν μεγάλην λείαν τοῦ πρώτου κύκλου  
> (Who the great spoil of the first circle)
>
> 39 „Ἐκ χειρῶν Διτὸς ἁρπάσας, ἡ ῥὼξ ἥδε  
> (Snatched from the hands of Dis, this cleft rock)

- *ἀλλ᾽, ἐὰν μὴ λανθάνω* ‘but, if I am not mistaken’ gives *se ben discerno* a negative formulation, without a separate equivalent of *certo*. *ἐάν* takes the present active subjunctive *λανθάνω* (first singular, formally identical to the indicative); *μή* is the conditional negative. The intended sense of the bare *λανθάνω* is less usual than its classical ‘escape notice’.
- *μικρὸν πρὶν ἔλθῃ* ‘a little before he should come’ uses an accusative of extent and the third singular second aorist active subjunctive of *ἔρχομαι*. The finite subjunctive after *πρίν* closely follows *pria … che venisse*; an infinitive would be more usual in Attic after an affirmative main statement about a past event.
- *ὁ … ἁρπάσας* ‘the one who snatched’ is the subject of *ἔλθῃ*: article and nominative masculine singular aorist active participle of *ἁρπάζω* replace *Colui che … levò*. Between them stand the object, *τὴν μεγάλην λείαν τοῦ πρώτου κύκλου*, with ordinal ‘first’ for spatial *superno*, and the source, *ἐκ χειρῶν Διτός* ‘out of the hands of Dis’. Greek supplies the hands and makes *Διτός* their dependent genitive in place of *a Dite*.
- At the end of line 39 *ἡ ῥὼξ ἥδε* ‘this cleft rock’ already introduces the subject of the following main verb, where the Italian waits until line 40 to name a different subject.

> 40 da tutte parti l’ alta valle feda  
> 41 tremò sì, ch’ io pensai che l’ universo  
> 42 sentisse amor, per lo quale è chi creda  
> 43 più volte il mondo in caos converso;  

on all sides the deep loathsome valley trembled so, that I thought the universe felt love, whereby, as some believe, the world has oft-times been converted into chaos;

> 40 „Ἐσείσθη πανταχόθεν τοσοῦτο σφόδρα,  
> (Was shaken on every side so violently,)
>
> 41 „Ὥστ᾽ ἐνόμιζον αἰσθάνεσθαι τὸ σύμπαν  
> (That I thought the universe felt)
>
> 42 „Τὸν ἔρωτα, δι᾽ οὗπερ, ὡς ἔστι δόξα,  
> (That love through which, as the opinion is,)
>
> 43 „Συχνῶς εἰς χάος ὁ κόσμος μετεβλήθη.  
> (The world has oftentimes been turned into chaos.)

- *ἐσείσθη πανταχόθεν τοσοῦτο σφόδρα* ‘was shaken from every side so violently’ makes the rock, not *l’alta valle feda*, the subject: neither epithet of the valley survives. *ἐσείσθη* is third singular aorist passive indicative of *σείω*, against intransitive *tremò*. The directional suffix of *πανταχόθεν* gives *da tutte parti* in one word; adverbial accusative *τοσοῦτο* with *σφόδρα* amplifies *sì*.
- *ὥστ᾽ ἐνόμιζον* ‘so that I thought’ takes the indicative for an actual result (*ὥστ᾽* for *ὥστε*, *ἐνόμιζον* first singular imperfect active of *νομίζω*). The imperfect presents *pensai* as an impression then entertained. Instead of *che l’universo sentisse amor* there is indirect statement: *αἰσθάνεσθαι* (present middle infinitive of *αἰσθάνομαι*), accusative subject *τὸ σύμπαν* ‘the whole’, and accusative object *τὸν ἔρωτα*.
- *δι᾽ οὗπερ* ‘through which very [love]’ has elided *διά* with the genitive masculine relative referring to *ἔρωτα*; the attached particle *-περ* emphasizes the reference. *ὡς ἔστι δόξα* ‘as is the belief’ turns *è chi creda* into an impersonal parenthesis. Consequently *ὁ κόσμος μετεβλήθη* ‘the world was changed’ is a finite assertion rather than a construction dependent on ‘believe’. *μετεβλήθη* is third singular aorist passive indicative of *μεταβάλλω*; *συχνῶς* renders *più volte* as ‘frequently’, and *εἰς χάος* gives the resulting state.

> 44 ed in quel punto questa vecchia roccia  
> 45 qui ed altrove tal fece riverso.  

and in that moment, here, and elsewhere, this ancient rock made such downfall.

> 44 „Τότε δ᾽ ἡ παλαίφατος ἀπόῤῥωξ αὕτη  
> (And at that time this ancient crag)
>
> 45 „Ἔπεσεν ὧδε, προσέτι τ᾽ ἀλλαχόθι.  
> (Fell down here, and elsewhere besides.)

- *τότε δ᾽* ‘and then’ condenses *ed in quel punto*. *ἡ παλαίφατος ἀπόῤῥωξ αὕτη* has the adjective inside the article–noun group and the demonstrative outside it. Poetic *παλαίφατος*, literally ‘spoken of long ago’, is a less neutral epithet than *vecchia*.
- *ἔπεσεν* (third singular second aorist active indicative of *πίπτω*) replaces *tal fece riverso* with the simple ‘fell’, dropping *tal*. The places follow the verb: *ὧδε* ‘here’, *προσέτι τ᾽ ἀλλαχόθι* ‘and elsewhere besides’. *τ᾽* is elided enclitic *τε*; *ἀλλαχόθι* has the poetic locative ending *-θι*.

> 46 Ma ficca gli occhi a valle: chè s’ approccia  
> 47 la riviera del sangue, in la qual bolle  
> 48 qual che per violenza in altrui noccia.”  

But fix thy eyes upon the valley: for the river of blood draws nigh, in which boils every one who by violence injures others.”

> 46 „Ἀλλ᾽ εἰς κοιλάδα βλέψον· ἐγγὺς γὰρ ἔστιν  
> (But look into the valley; for near at hand is)
>
> 47 „Ὁ τῶν αἱμάτων ῥύαξ, ἔνθ᾽ οἱ τοὺς ἄλλους  
> (The stream of blood, where those who others)
>
> 48 „Βίᾳ βλάπτοντες δεινῶς βράζουσι πάντες.”  
> (By violence harm, all boil dreadfully.")

- *ἀλλ᾽ εἰς κοιλάδα βλέψον* ‘but look into the valley’ replaces *ficca gli occhi* with a verb of looking and places its direction first. *βλέψον* is second singular first aorist active imperative of *βλέπω*; *εἰς* with the accusative interprets *a valle* as the goal of the gaze.
- *ἐγγὺς γὰρ ἔστιν ὁ τῶν αἱμάτων ῥύαξ* ‘for the stream of blood is near’ renders explanatory *chè* with postpositive *γάρ* and changes the motion of *s’approccia* to a state. The genitive *τῶν αἱμάτων*, placed between article and noun, is plural against singular *del sangue*; the Greek plural can suggest shed blood.
- *ἔνθ᾽* (elided *ἔνθα*) ‘where’ replaces *in la qual*. *οἱ τοὺς ἄλλους βίᾳ βλάπτοντες* ‘those harming others by force’ replaces indefinite singular *qual che … noccia* with an articular present active participle (nominative masculine plural of *βλάπτω*). The persons harmed are accusative objects rather than *in altrui*; *βίᾳ* is a dative of means or manner.
- *δεινῶς βράζουσι πάντες* ‘they all boil dreadfully’ delays the finite verb, pluralizes *bolle* to agree with *οἱ … βλάπτοντες*, and makes the universality explicit with *πάντες*. *βράζουσι* is third plural present active indicative of *βράζω*, used intransitively; *δεινῶς* is added.

> 49 O cieca cupidigia, e ria e folle,  
> 50 che sì ci sproni nella vita corta,  
> 51 e nell’ eterna poi sì mal c’ immolle!  

O blind cupidity [both wicked and foolish], which so incites us in the short life, and then, in the eternal, steeps us so bitterly!

> 49 Ὦ πάθος ἀπληστίας, ὀργῆς παράνου,  
> (O passion of greed, of senseless wrath,)
>
> 50 Τὸ κεντοῦν ἡμᾶς ἐν βραχεῖ βίῳ τῷδε,  
> (That goads us in this brief life,)
>
> 51 Ἵν᾽ αἰωνίως εἰς αἵματα βυθίζῃς !  
> (Only to plunge us eternally into blood!)

- *ὦ πάθος ἀπληστίας, ὀργῆς παράνου* ‘O passion of greed, of senseless wrath’ supplies the governing noun *πάθος* (neuter singular vocative) and sets two genitives beside one another without a conjunction. *ἀπληστίας* gives the sense of *cupidigia*, but *cieca* is not translated. *ὀργῆς παράνου* corresponds to the reading *ira … folle*, not Oelsner’s *e ria e folle*: wrath is a second noun, not wickedness a quality of greed. *παράνου* is the contracted genitive singular of *παράνους* ‘beside oneself’.
- *τὸ κεντοῦν ἡμᾶς* ‘that which goads us’ continues the address with an articular neuter singular present active participle of *κεντέω*, agreeing with *πάθος*. It preserves the image in *sproni* but not the finite second person of the relative clause or the intensifier *sì*. *ἐν βραχεῖ βίῳ τῷδε* ‘in this brief life’ adds a demonstrative: *βραχεῖ* (from *βραχύς*) and *τῷδε* (from *ὅδε*) are both dative masculine singular with *βίῳ*.
- *ἵν᾽ αἰωνίως εἰς αἵματα βυθίζῃς* ‘that you may plunge [us] eternally into blood’ changes the coordinated *e … poi* into a clause of purpose or intended outcome. Elided *ἵνα* governs *βυθίζῃς*, second singular present active subjunctive of *βυθίζω*, returning to direct second-person address; *ἡμᾶς* is understood again as object. *αἰωνίως* replaces *nell’eterna* with an adverb of duration, while *εἰς αἵματα* supplies an explicit destination for *immolle*. Neither *poi* nor the degree of suffering in *sì mal* is separately rendered.

## Lines 52–84 — The Centaurs at the Trench

> 52 Io vidi un’ ampia fossa in arco torta,  
> 53 come quella che tutto il piano abbraccia,  
> 54 secondo ch’ avea detto la mia scorta;  

I saw a wide fosse bent arcwise, as embracing all the plain, according to what my Guide had told me;

> 52 Εὐρεῖαν τότε κεκυρτωμένην τάφρον  
> (A broad, curved trench then)
>
> 53 Εἶδον, τὴν πᾶσαν κυκλοῦσαν πεδιάδα,  
> (I saw, encircling all the plain,)
>
> 54 Καθὼς ὁδηγὸς οὑμὸς προέλεγέ μοι.  
> (Just as my guide had foretold to me.)

- *Εὐρεῖαν τότε κεκυρτωμένην τάφρον εἶδον* puts the object before the verb: ‘a broad, curved trench I then saw’. *Εὐρεῖαν* (acc. sg. fem. of *εὐρύς*) and *κεκυρτωμένην* (perfect middle/passive participle of *κυρτόω*, passive in sense) both qualify *τάφρον*. The perfect expresses the resulting shape of the trench; there is no separate noun for *arco*. *Τότε* is supplied, and *Io* needs no pronoun in Greek: *εἶδον*, the second aorist active indicative used with *ὁράω*, is first person singular here.
- *Τὴν πᾶσαν κυκλοῦσαν πεδιάδα* is a second, articular description of *τάφρον*: ‘the one encircling the whole plain’. *Τήν* introduces *κυκλοῦσαν* (acc. sg. fem., present active participle of *κυκλόω*); the participle's object is *πᾶσαν … πεδιάδα* (from *πεδιάς*). Thus *come quella che … abbraccia* becomes a direct qualification rather than a comparison with a finite relative clause.
- *Καθὼς … προέλεγέ μοι* ‘as … he told me beforehand’ dispenses with *quel* or any other antecedent to the clause. Italian *avea detto* is pluperfect; *προέλεγέ* is imperfect active indicative, third singular, of *προλέγω*, with priority expressed by *προ-* and the recipient by enclitic dative *μοι*. *Ὁδηγὸς οὑμός* ‘my guide’ has crasis of *ὁ ἐμός* in the postposed possessive.

> 55 e tra il piè della ripa ed essa, in traccia  
> 56 correan Centauri armati di saette,  
> 57 come solean nel mondo andare a caccia.  

and between it and the foot of the bank were Centaurs, running one behind the other, armed with arrows, as they were wont on earth to go in hunting.

> 55 Μέσον δὲ ταύτης καὶ τοῦ κρημνοῦ κατ’ ἴλην  
> (And between this and the cliff, in a troop)
>
> 56 Διέτρεχον Κένταυροι, φέροντες τόξα,  
> (Centaurs were running to and fro, bearing bows,)
>
> 57 Ὡς ἔπραττον ἐν κόσμῳ κυνηγετοῦντες.  
> (As they used to do in the world when hunting.)

- *Μέσον δὲ ταύτης καὶ τοῦ κρημνοῦ* reverses *tra il piè della ripa ed essa*. *Μέσον* is adverbial, ‘between’, with two genitives: feminine *ταύτης* refers to the trench, and *τοῦ κρημνοῦ* denotes the steep bank itself, without *piè*. Postpositive *δέ* continues the description.
- *Κατ’ ἴλην* (elided *κατά* with acc. sg. of *ἴλη*) means ‘in troop formation’: Musurus takes *in traccia* collectively, without specifying ‘one behind another’. *Διέτρεχον* (third plural imperfect active of *διατρέχω*, with internal augment) renders the ongoing *correan*. *Φέροντες τόξα* ‘bearing bows’ replaces *armati di saette* with an active present participle agreeing with *Κένταυροι* and names the bows rather than the arrows.
- *Ὡς ἔπραττον … κυνηγετοῦντες* is ‘as they used to do … when hunting’. Habitual imperfect *ἔπραττον* (third plural of *πράττω*, with Attic *-ττ-*) stands for the earlier action as a whole; *κυνηγετοῦντες*, present active participle of *κυνηγετέω*, supplies its circumstance. There is no verb of going corresponding to *andare*; *ἐν κόσμῳ* closely follows *nel mondo*.

> 58 Vedendoci calar ciascun ristette,  
> 59 e della schiera tre si dipartiro  
> 60 con archi ed asticciuole prima elette.  

Perceiving us descend, they all stood still; and from the band three came forth with bows and javelins chosen first.

> 58 Ὡς δ’ εἶδον ἡμᾶς καταβαίνοντας, πάντες  
> (And when they saw us descending, all)
>
> 59 Ἐπέσχον, καὶ τρεῖς ἀπέστησαν τῆς ἴλης  
> (Halted, and three detached themselves from the troop)
>
> 60 Σὺν τόξοις καὶ βέλεσιν ἐξηρτυμένοις.  
> (With bows and arrows ready-fitted.)

- *Ὡς δ’ εἶδον ἡμᾶς καταβαίνοντας* makes *Vedendoci calar* a temporal clause, ‘when they saw us descending’. Here *εἶδον* is third plural; the accusative *ἡμᾶς* takes a supplementary present active participle, *καταβαίνοντας* (from *καταβαίνω*), where Italian has the infinitive *calar*. *Δ’* is elided *δέ*.
- Collective *πάντες ἐπέσχον* ‘all halted’ replaces distributive singular *ciascun ristette*. *Ἐπέσχον* is the intransitively used second aorist active of *ἐπέχω*, third plural. In *τρεῖς ἀπέστησαν τῆς ἴλης* ‘three withdrew from the troop’, *ἀπέστησαν* is the intransitive root aorist active of *ἀφίστημι*, with a genitive of separation. Greek puts the source after the verb rather than before *tre*.
- *Σὺν τόξοις καὶ βέλεσιν ἐξηρτυμένοις* uses *σύν* with two neuter dative plurals (*τόξον*, *βέλος*). *Ἐξηρτυμένοις*, perfect middle/passive participle of *ἐξαρτύω*, describes equipment in readiness, not a prior act of selection. Its form agrees with *βέλεσιν* and can qualify both coordinated nouns; unlike *elette*, it cannot refer to the three who carry them. There is no separate *prima*.

> 61 E l’ un gridò da lungi: “A qual martiro  
> 62 venite voi, che scendete la costa?  
> 63 Ditel costinci, se non, l’ arco tiro.”  

And one of them cried from far: “To what torment come ye, ye that descend the coast? Tell from thence; if not, I draw the bow.”

> 61 Εἷς δ’ ἔκραξε μακρόθεν· “Ἐπὶ βασάνῳ  
> (And one cried out from afar: "To torment)
>
> 62 „ Τίνι καταβαίνοντες ἔρχεσθ’ ἐκεῖσε ;  
> (Of what kind do you come descending thither?)
>
> 63 „ Λέγετε πόθεν· ἄλλως, τὸ βέλος ῥίπτω.”  
> (Tell from where; otherwise, I shoot the arrow.")

- *Εἷς δ’ ἔκραξε μακρόθεν* ‘and one cried from afar’ leaves the group to which *εἷς* belongs implicit. *Ἔκραξε* is third singular aorist active of *κράζω*; the adverb in *-θεν* expresses *da lungi* without a preposition.
- *Ἐπὶ βασάνῳ τίνι* ‘for what torment?’ has *ἐπί* with the dative of intended end, and interrogative *τίνι* agrees with *βασάνῳ* across the line break. *Καταβαίνοντες* (nom. pl. masc., present active) turns *che scendete* into a participle agreeing with the subject of *ἔρχεσθ’* (elided *ἔρχεσθε*, second plural present middle indicative of *ἔρχομαι*). *Ἐκεῖσε* ‘thither’ supplies a direction, not a noun corresponding to *la costa*.
- *Λέγετε πόθεν* has a second plural present active imperative and the interrogative ‘whence?’ rather than the demonstrative *costinci* ‘from where you are’; the object pronoun of *Ditel* drops out. *Ἄλλως* ‘otherwise’ compresses *se non*. *Τὸ βέλος ῥίπτω* (first singular present active) threatens the discharge of the missile rather than the drawing of *l’arco*.

> 64 Lo mio maestro disse: “La risposta  
> 65 farem noi a Chiron costà di presso;  
> 66 mal fu la voglia tua sempre sì tosta.”  

My Master said: “Our answer we will make to Chiron, there near at hand; unhappily thy will was always thus rash.”

> 64 Προστάτης δ’ ἐμὸς εἶπεν· “Ἐροῦμεν τοῦτο  
> (And my Protector said: "We will tell this)
>
> 65 „ Τῷ Χείρωνι σύνεγγυς ἡμεῖς αὐτίκα·  
> (To Chiron near at hand ourselves forthwith;)
>
> 66 „ Κακὴν θέλησιν ᾀείποτε δεικνύεις.”  
> (An evil will you always show.")

- *Προστάτης δ’ ἐμὸς εἶπεν* calls *lo mio maestro* ‘my protector’; *ἐμός* agrees with the anarthrous subject. *Εἶπεν*, third singular second aorist active, introduces *ἐροῦμεν τοῦτο* ‘we shall say this’. The contracted future *ἐροῦμεν* (first plural, the future used with *λέγω*) replaces *la risposta farem* with a verb of speaking and a demonstrative object.
- *Τῷ Χείρωνι* (dat. sg. of *Χείρων*) is the recipient. *Σύνεγγυς* ‘close at hand’ renders *costà di presso* without its explicit reference to ‘there’; emphatic *ἡμεῖς* retains *noi* despite the personal ending of *ἐροῦμεν*. *Αὐτίκα* ‘forthwith’ adds a time specification.
- *Κακὴν θέλησιν ᾀείποτε δεικνύεις* ‘you always display an evil will’ recasts the judgment. Where *mal* is adverbial and *la voglia tua* the subject of past *fu*, *κακήν* is an adjective agreeing with the object *θέλησιν*, and the addressee becomes the subject of *δεικνύεις* (second singular present active of *δεικνύω*). *ᾈείποτε* renders *sempre*; the degree and rashness in *sì tosta* are not separately expressed.

> 67 Poi mi tentò e disse: “Quegli è Nesso,  
> 68 che morì per la bella Deianira,  
> 69 e fe’ di sè la vendetta egli stesso;  

Then he touched me and said: “That is Nessus, who died for the fair Dejanira, and of himself took vengeance for himself;

> 67 Εἶθ’ ἥψατό μου λέγων· “Νέσσος ἔσθ’ οὗτος,  
> (Then he touched me, saying: "This is Nessus,)
>
> 68 „ Ὁ θανὼν καλῆς χάριν Δηϊανείρας,  
> (Who died for the sake of fair Dejanira,)
>
> 69 „ Αὐτός τ’ ἀπηνῶς ἑαυτὸν ἐκδικήσας.  
> (And himself cruelly avenged himself.)

- *Εἶθ’ ἥψατό μου λέγων* makes *disse* subordinate: ‘then he touched me, saying’. *Εἶθ’* is *εἶτα* elided, with *τ* aspirated before the rough breathing. *Ἥψατο* (third singular aorist middle of *ἅπτω*) takes the genitive of the person touched, enclitic *μου*; *λέγων* is a nominative singular present active participle.
- *Νέσσος ἔσθ’ οὗτος* fronts the predicate name and substitutes ‘this one’ for *Quegli*. *Ἔσθ’* represents *ἐστί* with elision and aspiration before *οὗτος*. *Ὁ θανών*, articular second aorist active participle of *θνῄσκω*, is in apposition to the name and replaces *che morì*. Postpositive *χάριν* ‘for the sake of’ governs the genitive *καλῆς … Δηϊανείρας*, standing between epithet and name.
- *Αὐτός τ’ ἀπηνῶς ἑαυτὸν ἐκδικήσας* continues the participial description with *τε* (elided) and the aorist active participle of *ἐκδικέω*. Intensive nominative *αὐτός* ‘himself’ and reflexive accusative *ἑαυτόν* ‘himself’ retain the Italian play on *sè … egli stesso*, but make Nessus the direct object of ‘avenge’. The noun *vendetta* is absorbed into the verb; *ἀπηνῶς* ‘cruelly’ is added.

> 70 e quel di mezzo, che al petto si mira,  
> 71 è il gran Chirone, il qual nudrì Achille;  
> 72 quell’ altro è Folo, che fu sì pien d’ ira.  

he in the middle, who is looking down upon his breast, is the great Chiron, he who nursed Achilles; that other is Pholus, who was so full of rage.

> 70 „ Ὁ δ’ ἐν τῷ μέσῳ, τὸ στῆθος αὑτοῦ βλέπων,  
> (And he in the midst, gazing at his own breast,)
>
> 71 „ Ὁ Χείρων ἐστίν, ὁ τροφὸς Ἀχιλλέως.  
> (Is Chiron, the rearer of Achilles.)
>
> 72 „ Ὁ δ’ ἄλλος Φόλος, ὅστις ἦν ὀργῆς πλήρης.  
> (And the other is Pholus, who was full of wrath.)

- *Ὁ δ’ ἐν τῷ μέσῳ* ‘and the one in the middle’ uses the article to substantivize a prepositional phrase. *Τὸ στῆθος αὑτοῦ βλέπων* turns *che al petto si mira* into a circumstantial present active participle of *βλέπω* with a direct object. The rough-breathed contracted reflexive genitive *αὑτοῦ* (= *ἑαυτοῦ*) makes explicit whose breast it is.
- *Ὁ Χείρων ἐστίν* supplies the predicate with the name articular but no epithet for *gran*. *Ὁ τροφὸς Ἀχιλλέως* ‘the rearer of Achilles’ replaces *il qual nudrì Achille* with an appositional agent noun; the person reared is now in the genitive (Attic *Ἀχιλλέως* from *Ἀχιλλεύς*), and there is no past-tense verb.
- *Ὁ δ’ ἄλλος Φόλος* parallels the preceding identification but omits the copula. *Ὅστις ἦν ὀργῆς πλήρης* retains a finite relative clause: *ὅστις*, nominative masculine singular, characterizes its antecedent; *ἦν* is imperfect of *εἰμί*, and predicate *πλήρης* governs the genitive of what fills him, *ὀργῆς*. The intensifier *sì* is omitted.

> 73 D’ intorno al fosso vanno a mille a mille,  
> 74 saettando quale anima si svelle  
> 75 del sangue più che sua colpa sortille.”  

Around the fosse they go by thousands, piercing with their arrows whatever spirit wrenches itself out of the blood farther than its guilt has allotted for it.”

> 73 „Περὶ τὴν τάφρον ἔρχονται μυριάδες,  
> (Around the trench they go in myriads,)
>
> 74 „Τοξεύοντες τὰς ψυχὰς τὰς ἐν αἱμάτων  
> (Shooting with arrows the souls that in the blood's)
>
> 75 „Ῥύακι παραβάσας τοὺς ποινῆς ὅρους.”  
> (Stream have transgressed the bounds of their punishment.")

- *Περὶ τὴν τάφρον ἔρχονται μυριάδες* retains the initial phrase of motion around the trench (*περί* with accusative). *Ἔρχονται* is third plural present middle of *ἔρχομαι*; *μυριάδες* ‘myriads’ is nominative plural, not a distributive phrase like *a mille a mille*. Although *μυριάδες* is feminine, masculine *τοξεύοντες* (present active participle of *τοξεύω*) agrees in sense with the Centaurs, just as *saettando* describes the agents of *vanno*.
- *Τὰς ψυχὰς τὰς … παραβάσας* ‘the souls that have overstepped’ replaces indefinite singular *quale anima* with an articular plural and a participle in the repeated-article attributive position. *Παραβάσας* is accusative feminine plural of the root aorist active participle of *παραβαίνω*: the transgression is presented as accomplished, not as the ongoing reflexive action of *si svelle*.
- *Ἐν αἱμάτων ῥύακι* is locative, ‘in a stream of blood’, rather than directional *del sangue* ‘out of the blood’. *Ἐν* governs *ῥύακι* (dat. sg. of *ῥύαξ*), not the intervening *αἱμάτων* (gen. pl. of *αἷμα*). *Τοὺς ποινῆς ὅρους* ‘the bounds of punishment’ is the object of *παραβάσας*: Greek interprets *più che sua colpa sortille* as a limit overstepped, suppressing the comparison, the possessive, and the finite verb of allotment.

> 76 Noi ci appressammo a quelle fiere snelle;  
> 77 Chiron prese uno strale, e con la cocca  
> 78 fece la barba indietro alle mascelle.  

We drew near those rapid beasts; Chiron took an arrow, and with the notch put back his beard upon his jaws.

> 76 Ὡς δ᾿ ἐγγὺς ἦμεν τῶν ὠκέων τεράτων,  
> (And when we were near the swift monsters,)
>
> 77 Χείρων ἔλαβε βέλος, καὶ τῇ γλυφίδι  
> (Chiron took an arrow, and with the notch)
>
> 78 Τὰ γένει᾿ ἔσυρ᾿ ὄπισθεν σιαγόνων,  
> (Drew back his beard behind his jaws,)

- *Ὡς δ’ ἐγγὺς ἦμεν* ‘and when we were near’ subordinates Italian's independent *Noi ci appressammo* to the next action. The movement becomes a state, with *ἦμεν* (first plural imperfect of *εἰμί*) and *ἐγγύς* governing *τῶν ὠκέων τεράτων*. *Ὠκέων* is genitive plural of poetic *ὠκύς* ‘swift’, agreeing with neuter *τεράτων* (from *τέρας*); ‘monsters’ replaces *fiere*, and the article replaces *quelle*.
- *Χείρων ἔλαβε βέλος* closely follows *Chiron prese uno strale*, with third singular second aorist active *ἔλαβε* from *λαμβάνω* and an anarthrous object. *Τῇ γλυφίδι*, instrumental dative singular of *γλυφίς* ‘notch’, corresponds to *con la cocca*.
- *Τὰ γένει’ ἔσυρ’ ὄπισθεν σιαγόνων* ‘he drew the beard back behind the jaws’ uses the neuter plural *γένεια* for singular *la barba*. The elided verb *ἔσυρ’* (from *σύρω*) expresses the physical movement directly, in place of *fece … indietro*; *ὄπισθεν* governs the genitive plural of *σιαγών*, giving *alle mascelle* the more specific relation ‘behind the jaws’.

> 79 Quando s’ ebbe scoperta la gran bocca,  
> 80 disse ai compagni: “Siete voi accorti,  
> 81 che quel di retro muove ciò ch’ ei tocca?  

When he had uncovered his great mouth, he said to his companions: “Have ye perceived that the one behind moves what he touches?

> 79 Καὶ στόμ᾿ ἀνοίξας εὐρὺ λέγει τοῖς ἄλλοις·  
> (And opening wide his mouth, he says to the others:)
>
> 80 “Παρετηρήσατ᾿ ἆρα, πῶς ὁ κατόπιν  
> ("Have you observed, then, how the one behind)
>
> 81 „Ἐρχόμενος κινεῖ πᾶν, ὅτου θιγγάνῃ;  
> (Who comes moves everything that he touches?)

- *Καὶ στόμ’ ἀνοίξας εὐρύ* ‘and having opened a wide mouth’ substitutes a nominative aorist active participle of *ἀνοίγω* for *Quando s’ebbe scoperta*. Its action precedes the main verb without an explicit temporal conjunction. *Στόμ’* is elided *στόμα*, with agreeing neuter accusative *εὐρύ* (from *εὐρύς*): the adjective qualifies the mouth, while ‘open’ replaces ‘uncover’.
- *Λέγει τοῖς ἄλλοις* changes past *disse* to the historical present (third singular active of *λέγω*) and *ai compagni* to a substantivized adjective in the dative, ‘to the others’.
- *Παρετηρήσατ’ ἆρα* ‘did you then observe?’ replaces *Siete voi accorti* with the second plural aorist active indicative of *παρατηρέω* (*παρετηρήσατε*, internally augmented and elided). Interrogative *ἆρα* marks the question; *πῶς* ‘how’ introduces what was observed, rather than simply corresponding to declarative *che*.
- *Ὁ κατόπιν ἐρχόμενος* ‘the one coming behind’ expands *quel di retro* with an articular present middle participle of *ἔρχομαι*. *Κινεῖ* is the contracted third singular present active of *κινέω*; its accusative object *πᾶν* ‘everything’ generalizes *ciò*. In *ὅτου θιγγάνῃ*, *ὅτου* (genitive singular relative form of *ὅστις*) is genitive because *θιγγάνω* ‘touch’ governs that case. The present active subjunctive gives an indefinite sense, ‘whatever he touches’; unlike the usual Attic indefinite relative construction, it has no *ἄν*.

> 82 Così non soglion fare i piè de’ morti.”  

The feet of the dead are not wont to do so.”

> 82 „Ἀλλ᾿ οἱ πόδες τῶν νεκρῶν οὐ δρῶσιν οὕτως.”  
> (Yet the feet of the dead do not act thus.")

- *Ἀλλ’ οἱ πόδες τῶν νεκρῶν* supplies an adversative *ἀλλά* (elided) and puts the subject first. *Πόδες* is nominative plural of *πούς*; the substantivized adjective *τῶν νεκρῶν* is possessive genitive plural, as in *i piè de’ morti*.
- *Οὐ δρῶσιν οὕτως* ‘do not act thus’ moves the equivalent of initial *Così* to the end. The contracted third plural present active indicative of *δράω*, used as a general present, does the work of both habitual *soglion* and its infinitive *fare*.

> 83 E il mio buon duca, che già gli era al petto  
> 84 dove le duo nature son consorti,  

And my good Guide, who was already at the breast of him, where the two natures are consorted,

> 83 Ὁ δ᾿ ἐμὸς Ἄρχων, ὡς ἦν ἐγγὺς τοῦ στήθους,  
> (And my Master, when he was near the breast,)
>
> 84 ᾯπερ αἱ δύο συναρμόζονται φύσεις,  
> (Where the two natures are joined together,)

- *Ὁ δ’ ἐμὸς Ἄρχων* ‘and my leader’ retains the possessive, here between article and noun, but drops *buon*. *Ἄρχων*, originally the present active participle of *ἄρχω*, functions as a title. *Ὡς ἦν ἐγγὺς τοῦ στήθους* ‘when he was near the breast’ makes *che già gli era al petto* temporal rather than relative. *Ἦν* is third singular imperfect of *εἰμί*; *ἐγγύς* takes the genitive singular of *στῆθος*. Neither *già* nor the personal reference of *gli* has a separate equivalent.
- *ᾯπερ* is the dative singular neuter relative referring to *στῆθος*, with locative force and the emphasizing particle *-περ*: ‘at the very place where’. *Αἱ δύο … φύσεις* uses an ordinary nominative plural with ‘two’, not the grammatical dual. *Συναρμόζονται*, third plural present middle/passive of *συναρμόζω*, means ‘are fitted together’, rendering *son consorti* with a single verb.

## Lines 85–117 — A Guide Along the Boiling Stream

> 85 rispose: “Ben è vivo, e sì soletto  
> 86 mostrarli mi convien la valle buia;  

replied: “Indeed he is alive, and solitary thus have I to shew him the dark valley;

> 85 Ἀπεκρίθη πρὸς αὐτόν· “Ναὶ μέν, ζῶν οὗτος·  
> (Answered him: "Indeed, this man is alive;)
>
> 86 „Ἀλλ᾿ ὀφείλω τήνδ᾿ αὐτῷ δεῖξαι κοιλάδα.  
> (Yet I must show him this valley.)

- *Ἀπεκρίθη πρὸς αὐτόν* makes the addressee of *rispose* explicit: ‘he answered him’. *Ἀπεκρίθη* is the 3rd sg. aorist of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, passive in form but not in sense; *πρός* takes the accusative of the person addressed.
- *Ναὶ μέν, ζῶν οὗτος* renders *Ben è vivo* as an emphatic affirmation, ‘yes indeed, this man is alive’. *Ζῶν* is the contracted nom. masc. sg. present active participle of *ζάω*, predicated of the demonstrative *οὗτος* without an expressed copula. *Ἀλλ᾿* (elided *ἀλλά*) then introduces a contrast where the Italian has *e*.
- *Ὀφείλω … δεῖξαι* ‘I am bound to show’ replaces impersonal *mostrarli mi convien* with a personal verb (1st sg. pres. act. ind.) and an aor. act. infinitive of *δείκνυμι*. The Italian clitic *-li* becomes the separate dative *αὐτῷ*. In *τήνδ᾿ … κοιλάδα* ‘this valley’, the elided demonstrative *τήνδε* and its noun (acc. fem. sg. of *κοιλάς*) enclose the recipient and infinitive. Neither *sì soletto* nor *buia* has a Greek equivalent.

> 87 necessità 'l c' induce, e non diletto.  

necessity brings him to it, and not sport.

> 87 „Ἡμᾶς πρὸς τοῦτ᾿ ἀνάγκη πείθει, κοὐ τέρψις.  
> (Necessity constrains us to this, and not pleasure.)

- *Ἡμᾶς πρὸς τοῦτ᾿ ἀνάγκη πείθει* ‘us necessity urges to this’ puts the personal object first. Greek has plural *ἡμᾶς*, not the singular “him” of the English; *πρὸς τοῦτ᾿* (elided *τοῦτο*, acc. neut. sg.) expresses the goal. *Πείθει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *πείθω*) makes *induce* an act of persuasion.
- *Κοὐ τέρψις* ‘and not pleasure’ retains *e non diletto*: *κοὐ* is crasis of *καὶ οὐ*, and *τέρψις* is nominative, a rejected alternative to the subject *ἀνάγκη*.

> 88 Tal si partì da cantare alleluia,  
> 89 che mi commise quest' uficio nuovo;  

From singing Alleluiah, came She who gave me this new office;

> 88 „Ἡ Δεῖν᾿ ἀλληλούϊα ψάλλειν λιποῦσα,  
> (Such-a-one, having left off singing alleluia,)
>
> 89 „Τὸ νέον τοῦτο χρέος ἐπέβαλέ μοι.  
> (Imposed this new duty upon me.)

- *Ἡ Δεῖν᾿* (elided *ἡ δεῖνα*) ‘such-and-such a woman’ takes up *Tal* without supplying a name; it is the article that marks the expression as feminine. *Ἀλληλούϊα ψάλλειν λιποῦσα* ‘having left off singing alleluia’ shifts the finite *si partì* into a circumstantial participle: *λιποῦσα* is nom. fem. sg., second aor. act. of *λείπω*. The present infinitive *ψάλλειν* names the activity left behind, rather than a place departed from; *ἀλληλούϊα* is indeclinable.
- Conversely, relative *che mi commise* becomes the main assertion, *τὸ νέον τοῦτο χρέος ἐπέβαλέ μοι* ‘she laid this new duty upon me’. *Χρέος* is acc. neut. sg., *νέον* attributive, *τοῦτο* demonstrative. *Ἐπέβαλε* is the 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἐπιβάλλω*, with augment after the preverb; its extra written accent is due to enclitic *μοι*, the dative of the person charged.

> 90 non è ladron, nè io anima fuia.  

he is no robber, nor I a thievish spirit.

> 90 „Οὐ λῃστὴς οὗτος, οὔτ᾿ ἐγὼ ψυχὴ φλαῦρα.  
> (He is no robber, nor I a wicked soul.)

- *Οὐ λῃστὴς οὗτος, οὔτ᾿ ἐγὼ ψυχὴ φλαῦρα* balances two verbless clauses, ‘this man no robber, nor I a base soul’. The subjects *οὗτος*, *ἐγώ* are both explicit; the first follows its predicate *λῃστής*, the second precedes *ψυχὴ φλαῦρα*. *Οὔτ᾿* is elided *οὔτε*. The nom. fem. sg. adjective *φλαῦρα* (from *φλαῦρος*) generalizes *fuia*: it conveys baseness, not specifically theft, so that the verbal link with *ladron* is lost.

> 91 Ma per quella virtù, per cui io muovo  
> 92 li passi miei per sì selvaggia strada,  
> 93 danne un de' tuoi, a cui noi siamo a pruovo,  

But by that virtue through which I move my steps on such a wild way, give us some one of thine whom we may follow,

> 91 „Ἀλλ᾿ ἄγε πρὸς ἀρετῆς, δι᾿ ἣν εὐθύνω  
> (But come, by that virtue through which I direct)
>
> 92 „Τὰ βήματά μου δι᾿ ὁδοῦ τῆσδ᾿ ἀγρίας,  
> (My steps along this savage way,)
>
> 93 „Ἡμῖν τινα δὸς τῶν σῶν, ὃς ὁδηγήσει,  
> (Give us one of yours, who shall guide us,)

- *Ἀλλ᾿ ἄγε πρὸς ἀρετῆς* ‘but come, by virtue’ introduces the appeal with an extra *ἄγε*, formally a 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *ἄγω*, used as an exhortation. *Πρός* with the genitive is the idiom of adjuration; the antecedent of *δι᾿ ἣν* has no demonstrative corresponding to *quella*. *Δι᾿* is elided *διά*: with acc. fem. sg. *ἣν* it gives the ground, ‘on account of which’.
- *Εὐθύνω τὰ βήματά μου* ‘I direct my steps’ makes *muovo* more specifically a verb of steering (1st sg. pres. act. ind.); *μου* is the enclitic possessive genitive. In *δι᾿ ὁδοῦ τῆσδ᾿ ἀγρίας*, by contrast, *διά* takes the genitive of the way traversed. *Τῆσδ᾿* is elided *τῆσδε*, agreeing with feminine *ὁδοῦ* and *ἀγρίας*: ‘along this wild road’, rather than *per sì selvaggia strada*.
- *Ἡμῖν τινα δὸς τῶν σῶν* distributes *danne un de’ tuoi* among the recipient *ἡμῖν*, the indefinite object *τινα*, the 2nd sg. aor. act. imperative *δός* (*δίδωμι*), and the partitive genitive *τῶν σῶν* ‘of your people’. *Ὃς ὁδηγήσει* makes the requested person subject, ‘who will guide’, instead of making *noi* follow him in *a cui noi siamo a pruovo*. The future active indicative presents the prospective service directly.

> 94 che ne dimostri là ove si guada,  
> 95 e che porti costui in su la groppa:  
> 96 chè non è spirto che per l' aer vada.”  

that he may shew us where the ford is, and carry over him upon his back, for he is not a spirit to go through the air.”

> 94 „Δείξει τ’ ἔνθ’ ἐστι τοῦ ῥύακος ὁ πόρος,  
> (And show where the ford of the stream is,)
>
> 95 „Καὶ τόνδ’ ἐπὶ τῆς αὑτοῦ ῥάχεως ἄξει·  
> (And carry this man upon his back;)
>
> 96 „Οὐ γὰρ πνεῦμ’ ἐστι βαδίζον δι’ ἀέρος.”  
> (For he is not a spirit walking through the air.")

- *Δείξει τ’ … καὶ … ἄξει* continues the relative clause with two more 3rd sg. future active indicatives (*δείκνυμι*, *ἄγω*), joined by *τε … καί*, rather than reproducing the subordinate subjunctives *dimostri*, *porti*. *Ἔνθ’* is *ἔνθα* elided before *ἐστι*: *ἔνθ’ ἐστι τοῦ ῥύακος ὁ πόρος* ‘where the ford of the stream is’ replaces impersonal *ove si guada* with a noun as subject and adds a dependent genitive (*ῥύακος*, from *ῥύαξ*). The recipient *ne* is understood.
- *Τόνδ’* (elided *τόνδε*, acc. masc. sg.) preserves the pointing force of *costui*. *Ἐπὶ τῆς αὑτοῦ ῥάχεως* ‘on his own back’ specifies where he will be carried: *ἐπί* takes the genitive *ῥάχεως* (of *ῥάχις*), and the rough breathing of possessive *αὑτοῦ*, contracted from *ἑαυτοῦ*, marks reference back to the bearer.
- *Οὐ γὰρ πνεῦμ’ ἐστι βαδίζον δι’ ἀέρος* replaces explanatory *chè* with postpositive *γάρ*. For relative *che … vada* Greek uses *βαδίζον*, nom. neut. sg. pres. act. participle of *βαδίζω*, modifying predicate *πνεῦμα* (elided *πνεῦμ’*): ‘a spirit walking through air’. *Δι’ ἀέρος* has *διά* with the genitive of the medium traversed.

> 97 Chiron si volse in sulla destra poppa,  
> 98 e disse a Nesso: “Torna, e sì li guida,  
> 99 e fa cansar, s' altra schiera v' intoppa.”  

Chiron bent round on his right breast, and said to Nessus: “Turn, and guide them then; and if another troop encounter you, keep it off.”

> 97 Τότε δ’ ὁ Χείρων ἐστράφη δεξιόθεν,  
> (Then Chiron turned upon his right side,)
>
> 98 Τῷ Νέσσῳ τ’ εἶπεν· “Ἐπίστρεψον, ὁδήγει,  
> (And said to Nessus: "Turn back, guide them,)
>
> 99 „Καὶ κατὰ λόχων ἄλλων τούτοις ἀμύνου.”  
> (And against other bands defend them.")

- *Τότε δ’ ὁ Χείρων ἐστράφη δεξιόθεν* adds ‘then’ and uses connective *δέ*. *Ἐστράφη*, 3rd sg. second aor. pass. ind. of *στρέφω*, has intransitive sense, ‘turned’, for reflexive *si volse*. *Δεξιόθεν*, a local adverb with the ending *-θεν* ‘from’, indicates the right side without translating *poppa*.
- *Τῷ Νέσσῳ τ’ εἶπεν* fronts the dative addressee; postpositive *τε* links this action to the turn. *Ἐπίστρεψον* is a 2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *ἐπιστρέφω*, ‘turn back’; *ὁδήγει* is a contracted 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *ὁδηγέω*, ‘guide’. Thus the turn is commanded as a single act, the guiding as an activity; *li* is not separately expressed.
- *Καὶ κατὰ λόχων ἄλλων τούτοις ἀμύνου* ‘and defend these men against other bands’ drops the condition *s’ altra schiera v’ intoppa*. The troop becomes plural, governed by adversative *κατά* with the genitive; *τούτοις* is the dative of those protected. The single 2nd sg. pres. middle imperative *ἀμύνου* (*ἀμύνω*) replaces causative *fa cansar*.

> 100 Noi ci movemmo con la scorta fida  
> 101 lungo la proda del bollor vermiglio,  
> 102 ove i bolliti facean alte strida.  

We moved onwards with our trusty guide, along the border of the purple boiling, wherein the boiled were making loud shrieks.

> 100 Ἄπιμεν σὺν τῇ πιστῇ νῦν συνοδίᾳ  
> (We move forward now with our trusty escort)
>
> 101 Παρὰ τὸ χεῖλος ἑψάνης ἐρυθρόχρου,  
> (Along the brink of the crimson boiling,)
>
> 102 Ἔνθ’ οἱ ζέοντες ἔκραζον ὀξυφώνως,  
> (Where those who boiled cried out shrilly,)

- *Ἄπιμεν … νῦν* ‘we go on now’ gives past *ci movemmo* narrative immediacy: *ἄπιμεν* is the 1st pl. pres. ind. of *ἄπειμι* ‘go away, go on’ (from *εἶμι*, not *εἰμί* ‘be’). *Σὺν τῇ πιστῇ … συνοδίᾳ* uses *σύν* with the dative; *συνοδίᾳ* denotes the escort or company rather than naming an individual guide.
- *Παρὰ τὸ χεῖλος* ‘along the lip’ uses *παρά* with the accusative for movement alongside. *Ἑψάνης ἐρυθρόχρου* is a dependent genitive, ‘of the red-hued boiling’; the contracted compound color adjective agrees with *ἑψάνης* and retains the red of *vermiglio*.
- *Ἔνθ’ οἱ ζέοντες ἔκραζον ὀξυφώνως* retains the local link *ove*, but *οἱ ζέοντες* ‘those boiling’ is an articular nom. masc. pl. **present active** participle of intransitive *ζέω*, not a passive like *i bolliti*. *Ἔκραζον* (3rd pl. imperf. act. ind. of *κράζω*) retains the ongoing action of *facean*; the verb itself expresses the cry, and the adverb *ὀξυφώνως* specifies its shrill pitch.

> 103 Io vidi gente sotto infino al ciglio;  

I saw people down in it even to the eyebrows;

> 103 Βεβυθισμένοι τ’ ἦσαν ἄχρις ὀφρύων.  
> (And were plunged up to the eyebrows.)

- *Βεβυθισμένοι τ’ ἦσαν* ‘and they were submerged’ continues with the preceding people as subject; *τε* thus links what *Io vidi gente* introduces as a new perception. The observer and the verb of seeing disappear. *Βεβυθισμένοι* is the reduplicated nom. masc. pl. perfect middle/passive participle of *βυθίζω*, passive here; with *ἦσαν* (3rd pl. imperf. of *εἰμί*) it describes the resulting state in past time. *Ἄχρις ὀφρύων* takes a genitive plural (*ὀφρύς*) for the limit, against singular *al ciglio*.

> 104 e il gran Centauro disse: “Ei son tiranni  
> 105 che dier nel sangue e nell' aver di piglio.  

and the great Centaur said: “These are tyrants who took to blood and plunder.

> 104 Ὁ δὲ Κένταυρος εἶπεν· “Οἵδ’ αἱμοβόροι  
> (And the Centaur said: "These bloodthirsty)
>
> 105 „Ἁρπαξίβιοί τ’ εἰσι τύραννοι πάντες,  
> (And rapacious are tyrants all,)

- *Ὁ δὲ Κένταυρος εἶπεν* carries the transition by postpositive *δέ* and omits *gran*. *Οἵδ’* (elided *οἵδε*, nom. masc. pl.) points more directly than *Ei*. The copula *εἰσι* links it to *τύραννοι πάντες* ‘tyrants all’, with *πάντες* supplied.
- In place of the past relative clause *che dier nel sangue e nell’ aver di piglio*, Musurus sets the coordinated nominative compounds *αἱμοβόροι ἁρπαξίβιοί τ’* before the copula: ‘blood-devouring and living by rapine’. What the tyrants did becomes what they are. *Τ’* is elided postpositive *τε*; the extra accent on *ἁρπαξίβιοί* belongs to the sequence with the enclitic.

> 106 Quivi si piangon li spietati danni;  

Here they lament their merciless offences;

> 106 „Τὰς ἀσπλάγχνους ἑαυτῶν θρηνοῦντες πράξεις.  
> (Lamenting their own pitiless deeds.)

- *Τὰς ἀσπλάγχνους ἑαυτῶν θρηνοῦντες πράξεις* ‘lamenting their own pitiless deeds’ is not an independent sentence. *Θρηνοῦντες* (nom. masc. pl. contracted pres. act. participle of *θρηνέω*) continues to describe the tyrants. The participle stands inside its object phrase: *ἀσπλάγχνους* agrees with acc. fem. pl. *πράξεις* despite its *-ους* ending, and reflexive gen. pl. *ἑαυτῶν* makes ownership explicit. *Danni* becomes ‘deeds’; the first *quivi* and the *si* construction of *si piangon* disappear in the active rendering.

> 107 quivi è Alessandro e Dionisio fero  
> 108 che fe' Cicilia aver dolorosi anni;  

here is Alexander; and fierce Dionysius, who made Sicily have years of woe;

> 107 „Ὧδ’ Ἀλέξανδρος, Διονύσιος κεῖσε,  
> (Here is Alexander, Dionysius yonder,)
>
> 108 „Ὁ Σικελίαν μακρὸν λυπήσας χρόνον·  
> (Who afflicted Sicily for a long time;)

- *Ὧδ’ Ἀλέξανδρος, Διονύσιος κεῖσε* distributes the names between ‘here’ (elided *ὧδε*) and ‘yonder’, with no verb or conjunction. *Κεῖσε* is properly directional, ‘thither’, but serves as a pointing adverb here. The Italian has a single *quivi è* and qualifies *Dionisio* with *fero*, which has no separate equivalent.
- *Ὁ Σικελίαν μακρὸν λυπήσας χρόνον* is an articular participial phrase in apposition to *Διονύσιος*, replacing *che fe’ Cicilia aver*. *Λυπήσας* (nom. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *λυπέω*) is itself causative, ‘having distressed’; *Σικελίαν* is its direct object. A second accusative, *μακρὸν … χρόνον*, expresses duration: ‘for a long time’ replaces *dolorosi anni*, with the pain now expressed by the verb.

> 109 e quella fronte ch' ha il pel così nero  
> 110 è Azzolino; e quell' altro ch' è biondo  
> 111 è Opizzo da Esti, il qual per vero  
> 112 fu spento dal figliastro su nel mondo.”  

and that brow which has the hair so black is Azzolino; and that other, who is blonde, is Obizzo of Este, who in verity was quenched by his step-son up in the world.”

> 109 „Ὁ δὲ μελανότριχον μέτωπον ἔχων  
> (And he who has a black-haired brow)
>
> 110 „Ἀζελήν ἐστιν· ὁ ξανθοκόμης ἄλλος  
> (Is Azzolino; the other, fair-haired,)
>
> 111 „Ὀβίζος Ἔστης, ὅν, ὡς ἀληθὴς φήμη,  
> (Is Obizzo of Este, whom, as true report says,)
>
> 112 „Ἴδιος υἱὸς ἐφόνευσ’ ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ.”  
> (His own son murdered in the world.")

- *Ὁ δὲ μελανότριχον μέτωπον ἔχων* ‘and the man having a black-haired brow’ reverses the relation in *quella fronte ch’ ha il pel così nero*: the man, not the brow, is subject. The article substantivizes *ἔχων* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *ἔχω*); *μελανότριχον* qualifies acc. neut. sg. *μέτωπον*, compressing hair and color into one compound and omitting *così*. *Ἀζελήν* is the predicate name with *ἐστιν*, not an object.
- *Ὁ ξανθοκόμης ἄλλος* ‘the other fair-haired one’ likewise turns *quell’ altro ch’ è biondo* into one nominal expression, here without a participle. *Ὀβίζος Ἔστης* supplies the predicate without repeating *ἐστιν*; the bare dependent genitive *Ἔστης* answers to *da Esti*.
- Relative *ὅν* is accusative because *il qual … fu spento* becomes active, ‘whom … murdered’. Parenthetic *ὡς ἀληθὴς φήμη* ‘as the report is true’ expands *per vero* by supplying a report and leaving its copula understood; *ἀληθής* is nom. fem. sg., agreeing with *φήμη*.
- *Ἴδιος υἱός* is the nominative subject, ‘his own son’, a different interpretation from *figliastro* and the English “step-son”. *Ἐφόνευσ’* is elided *ἐφόνευσε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *φονεύω*: explicit killing replaces figurative extinguishing. *Ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ* renders *nel mondo* with a static dative but drops *su*.

> 113 Allor mi volsi al poeta, e quei disse:  
> 114 “Questi ti sia or primo, ed io secondo.”  

Then I turned me to the Poet, and he said: “Let him be chief guide to thee now, and me second.”

> 113 Ὁ δὲ Ποιητής, πρὸς ὃν ἐστράφην, εἶπε·  
> (And the Poet, toward whom I turned, said:)
>
> 114 “Νῦν ὅδ’ ἔστω σοι πρῶτος, ἐγώ ’ν δευτέροις.”  
> ("Now let this one be first for you, and I second.")

- *Ὁ δὲ Ποιητής, πρὸς ὃν ἐστράφην, εἶπε* makes the Poet subject from the outset: the Italian first main clause *Allor mi volsi al poeta* is enclosed as a relative clause within the second. *Πρὸς ὅν* is ‘toward whom’; *ἐστράφην* is 1st sg. second aor. pass. ind. of *στρέφω* with the same intransitive use as *ἐστράφη* in 97. *Δέ* marks the transition without an explicit ‘then’.
- *Νῦν ὅδ’ ἔστω σοι πρῶτος* fronts *or* and closely matches *Questi ti sia … primo*: *ὅδ’* is elided *ὅδε*; *ἔστω* is the 3rd sg. pres. imperative of *εἰμί*, with dative of reference *σοι* and predicate nominative *πρῶτος*. *Ἐγώ ’ν δευτέροις* ‘I in second place’ leaves the verb understood and replaces singular predicate *secondo* with the idiomatic plural dative after *ἐν*. The spelling *’ν* marks loss of the initial vowel (prodelision), not elision from *ἐγώ*.

> 115 Poco più oltre il Centauro s' affisse  
> 116 sopra una gente, che infino alla gola  
> 117 parea che di quel bulicame uscisse.  

A little farther on, the Centaur paused beside a people which, as far as the throat, seemed to issue from that boiling stream.

> 115 Ὁ δὲ Κένταυρος ἀπέβλεψέ τι πρόσω  
> (And the Centaur looked a little further on)
>
> 116 Εἰς τοὺς ἐκ τοῦ βράζοντος αἱμώδους ῥείθρου  
> (At those who from the boiling bloody stream)
>
> 117 Ἐξέρχεσθ’ ἐοικότας ἄχρι τραχήλου.  
> (Seemed to emerge as far as the throat.)

- *Ὁ δὲ Κένταυρος ἀπέβλεψέ τι πρόσω* has the Centaur **look** a little farther ahead rather than stop farther on (*s’ affisse*). *Ἀπέβλεψε* is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀποβλέπω*, with internal augment; enclitic *τι* gives it its extra accent. *Τι* is an adverbial acc. neut. sg. of degree with *πρόσω*, ‘a little way forward’.
- *Εἰς τοὺς … ἐοικότας* gives the direction of the look, ‘at those who seemed’, instead of the position *sopra una gente*. *Τοὺς … ἐοικότας* is a substantivized acc. masc. pl. participle, replacing the collective noun and its singular relative clause. *Ἐοικότας* is perfect active from *ἔοικα*, a perfect with present sense (‘seem, be like’); the past time of *parea* is supplied by the narrative verb.
- *Ἐξέρχεσθ’* is the present middle infinitive *ἐξέρχεσθαι* of *ἐξέρχομαι*, complementary to *ἐοικότας*: Greek uses ‘seeming to emerge’ for *parea che … uscisse*. The final diphthong is elided before *ἐοικότας*. *Ἐκ τοῦ βράζοντος αἱμώδους ῥείθρου* supplies the source, with both the pres. act. participle *βράζοντος* and the adjective *αἱμώδους* agreeing with gen. neut. sg. *ῥείθρου*: ‘from the boiling, bloody stream’ expands *di quel bulicame*. The limit *ἄχρι τραχήλου* (*ἄχρι* with gen. sg. of *τράχηλος*, ‘up to the neck’) is kept for the very end rather than placed before the expression of seeming.

## Lines 118–139 — The Boiling Blood and the Ford

> 118 Mostrocci un' ombra dall' un canto sola,  

He shewed us a spirit by itself apart,

> 118 Σκιὰν ἡμῖν ἔδειξε πλευρόθεν μόνην  
> (He showed us a shade on one side alone,)

- *Σκιὰν ἡμῖν ἔδειξε* ‘a shade he showed us’ puts the object first, where *Mostrocci un’ ombra* puts the verb first. *Σκιάν* is the accusative singular of *σκιά*, without an article; *ἡμῖν* is the dative of the recipient, corresponding to the enclitic *-ci*. *Ἔδειξε* is the third-person singular first aorist active indicative of *δείκνυμι*.
- *πλευρόθεν* renders *dall’ un canto* by an adverb in *-θεν*, properly ‘from the side’, here indicating position, ‘off to one side’. *μόνην* (accusative feminine singular of *μόνος*) agrees with *σκιάν* across the intervening words, preserving the separation and final position of *sola*.

> 119 dicendo: “Colui fesse in grembo a Dio  
> 120 lo cor che in sul Tamigi ancor si cola.”  

saying: “That one, in God’s bosom, pierced the heart which still is venerated on the Thames.”

> 119 Λέγων· “Οὗτος ἐν Θεοῦ ναῷ καρδίαν  
> (Saying: "This one in God's temple a heart)
>
> 120 „Ἐνέπειρ’ ἀνδρὸς σεπτὴν νῦν ἐν Ταμέσει.”  
> (Pierced, a man's, which is now revered upon the Thames.")

- *Λέγων*, the nominative masculine singular present active participle of *λέγω*, agrees with the unexpressed subject of *ἔδειξε* and, like *dicendo*, denotes accompanying speech. *Οὗτος* ‘this one’, nominative masculine singular, replaces the more distant demonstrative *Colui*.
- *ἐν Θεοῦ ναῷ* ‘in God’s temple’ interprets the figurative *in grembo a Dio* as a place. *ἐν* governs the dative singular *ναῷ*; the possessive genitive *Θεοῦ* precedes its noun. *καρδίαν* is the accusative object of *Ἐνέπειρ’* (elided *ἐνέπειρε*, third-person singular aorist active indicative of *ἐμπείρω* ‘pierce’). Where *fesse … lo cor* puts the verb first, Greek puts the heart before the verb and its possessor after it, supplying *ἀνδρός* ‘of a man’ (genitive singular of *ἀνήρ*).
- The relative clause *che … ancor si cola* becomes *σεπτὴν νῦν ἐν Ταμέσει* ‘now revered on the Thames’. *σεπτήν* is a verbal adjective, accusative feminine singular agreeing with *καρδίαν*: it expresses the sense of the Italian passive without a relative pronoun or a finite verb. *νῦν* says ‘now’ rather than explicitly ‘still’; *ἐν* with the river name in the dative renders *in sul Tamigi*.

> 121 Poi vidi gente, che di fuor del rio  
> 122 tenea la testa ed ancor tutto il casso;  

Then some I saw, who kept the head and likewise all the chest out of the river;

> 121 Εἶτ’ εἶδον ἄλλους, οἳ τοῦ ῥύακος ἔξω  
> (Then I saw others, who outside the stream)
>
> 122 Εἶχον κεφαλήν, πρὸς δὲ καὶ θώραχ’ ὅλον·  
> (Held their head, and besides their whole chest too;)

- *Εἶτ’ εἶδον ἄλλους* ‘then I saw others’ follows *Poi vidi gente* in order, but replaces the collective singular *gente* with substantival *ἄλλους* (accusative masculine plural of *ἄλλος*). *Εἶτ’* is *εἶτα* with its final vowel elided; *εἶδον* is the first-person singular second aorist active indicative used for *ὁράω*.
- *οἳ … εἶχον* takes up *ἄλλους* with a nominative masculine plural relative and the third-person plural imperfect active indicative of *ἔχω*. The imperfect, like *tenea*, presents a continuing posture; the plural replaces the Italian agreement with *gente*. *τοῦ ῥύακος ἔξω* ‘outside the stream’ keeps the place before the verb, as in *di fuor del rio / tenea*, but puts the genitive of *ῥύαξ* before the governing adverb *ἔξω*.
- *κεφαλήν* and *θώραχ’ ὅλον* are distributive singular objects, ‘head’ and ‘whole chest’, without the articles of *la testa* and *tutto il casso*. In *πρὸς δὲ καί* ‘and besides also’, *πρός* is adverbial; the combination renders additive *ed ancor*. *θώραχ’* is *θώρακα* (accusative singular of *θώραξ*) with elision and aspiration of *κ* before the rough breathing of *ὅλον*. The adjective follows its noun rather than preceding it like *tutto*.

> 123 e di costoro assai riconobb' io.  

and of these I recognised many.

> 123 Ἐγὼ δὲ πολλοὺς ἀνεγνώρισα τούτων.  
> (And I recognized many of these.)

- *Ἐγὼ δὲ πολλοὺς ἀνεγνώρισα τούτων* reverses the framing of *e di costoro assai riconobb’ io*: the emphatic subject *ἐγώ* comes first and the partitive genitive *τούτων* ‘of these’ last. Postpositive *δέ* continues the narrative. *πολλούς* (accusative masculine plural of *πολύς*) gives *assai* an explicit nominal function, ‘many [men]’; *ἀνεγνώρισα* is the first-person singular aorist active indicative of *ἀναγνωρίζω*, with the augment after the prefix.

> 124 Così a più a più si facea basso  
> 125 quel sangue sì che cocea pur li piedi;  

Thus more and more that blood grew shallow, until it [cooked] the feet only;

> 124 Ἦν δὲ ταπεινούμενον τὸ ζέον αἷμα  
> (And the boiling blood grew lower)
>
> 125 Βαθμηδόν, ἕως ἐκάλυπτε τοὺς πόδας·  
> (By degrees, until it covered the feet;)

- *Ἦν … ταπεινούμενον τὸ ζέον αἷμα* ‘the boiling blood was being lowered’ recasts *si facea basso quel sangue*. *Ἦν* (third-person singular imperfect of *εἰμί*) with *ταπεινούμενον* (present middle-passive participle of *ταπεινόω*, nominative neuter singular) presents the lowering as in progress. Both *ταπεινούμενον* and *ζέον* agree with *αἷμα*, but only *ζέον* (present active participle of *ζέω*) stands inside the article–noun group: ‘the blood that boils’. Greek supplies that description in place of the demonstrative *quel*.
- Postpositive *δέ* links the sentence without a separate equivalent of *Così*. *Βαθμηδόν* ‘by degrees’, delayed until after *αἷμα*, renders the repeated *a più a più*. *ἕως* ‘until’ makes *sì che* temporal rather than consecutive. *ἐκάλυπτε* (third-person singular imperfect active indicative of *καλύπτω*) describes the resulting depth: the blood ‘covered the feet’ rather than *cocea pur li piedi*. *τοὺς πόδας* is the accusative plural of *πούς*; there is no separate word for restrictive *pur*.

> 126 e quivi fu del fosso il nostro passo.  

and here was our passage through the fosse.

> 126 Καὶ τὸν ῥύακα διέβημεν ἐκεῖθεν.  
> (And at that place we crossed over the stream.)

- *Καὶ τὸν ῥύακα διέβημεν ἐκεῖθεν* ‘and from there we crossed the stream’ changes *fu … il nostro passo* from a noun with a possessive into a personal verb. *διέβημεν* is the first-person plural root aorist active indicative of *διαβαίνω*, with internal augment; *τὸν ῥύακα*, the accusative of the space traversed, calls *il fosso* by the same word as *il rio*. Final *ἐκεῖθεν* is directional, ‘from there’, where *quivi* locates the passage.

> 127 “Sì come tu da questa parte vedi  
> 128 lo bulicame che sempre si scema,”  

“As thou seest the boiling stream, on this side, continually diminish,”

> 127 “Ὡς ἐκ τοῦ μέρους τούτου σὺ δὴ νῦν εἶδες  
> ("As on this side you have now seen)
>
> 128 „Τὸν ῥύακ’ ᾀεὶ σμικρυνόμενον βάδην,”  
> (The stream growing ever shallower step by step,")

- *Ὡς ἐκ τοῦ μέρους τούτου* follows *Sì come … da questa parte*: comparative *ὡς* introduces the first half of the comparison, and *ἐκ* governs the genitive singular of *μέρος*, with demonstrative *τούτου* outside the article–noun group. Placing this phrase before *σύ* brings the side into prominence.
- *σὺ δὴ νῦν εἶδες* ‘you indeed have now seen’ retains the explicit *tu*, adds the emphatic particle *δή* and temporal *νῦν*, and replaces present *vedi* with *εἶδες*, second-person singular second aorist active indicative of *ὁράω*. The Greek looks back on the observation as a whole.
- *Τὸν ῥύακ’ … σμικρυνόμενον* turns *lo bulicame che sempre si scema* into a perception construction: the accusative object and its supplementary participle mean ‘the stream diminishing’. *ῥύακ’* is elided *ῥύακα*, without the implication of boiling in *bulicame*; *σμικρυνόμενον* is the accusative masculine singular present middle-passive participle of *σμικρύνω*. *ᾀεί* (so spelled here, for usual *ἀεί*) gives *sempre*; *βάδην* ‘step by step’ adds the notion of gradation.

> 129 disse il Centauro, “voglio che tu credi  

said the Centaur, “so I would have thee to believe

> 129 Εἶπε Κένταυρος, “εἰδέναι βούλομαί σε,  
> (Said the Centaur, "I wish you to know,)

- *Εἶπε Κένταυρος* follows the verb–subject order of *disse il Centauro*, but leaves *Κένταυρος* without an article. *Εἶπε* is the third-person singular second aorist active indicative used for *λέγω*.
- *εἰδέναι βούλομαί σε* ‘I wish you to know’ has no explicit ‘so’ answering *ὡς*. *βούλομαι* is first-person singular present middle indicative, with active sense. Instead of *voglio che tu credi* with a finite subordinate verb, Greek uses *σε* as the accusative subject of *εἰδέναι*, the perfect active infinitive of *οἶδα* with present meaning. Knowing replaces believing, and the infinitive is placed before the verb of wishing. The extra accent on *βούλομαί* is due to enclitic *σε*.

> 130 che da quest' altra a più a più giù prema  
> 131 lo fondo suo, infin ch' ei si raggiunge  
> 132 ove la tirannia convien che gema.  

that, on this other, it lowers its bottom more and more, till it comes again to where tyranny is doomed to mourn.

> 130 „Ὅπως ἐξ ἄλλου πρὸς τὰ κάτω τὸ βάθος  
> (How on the other side downward the depth)
>
> 131 „Αὐτοῦ μᾶλλον ἔτ’ αὐξάνον ἀφικνεῖται,  
> (Of it increases yet more until it reaches)
>
> 132 „Ἔνθα στένειν προσήκει τὴν τυραννίαν.  
> (Where it is fitting for tyranny to groan.)

- *Ὅπως* ‘how’ introduces what the hearer is to know, rather than the proposition to be believed introduced by *che*. *ἐξ ἄλλου* ‘from the other [side]’ answers *ἐκ τοῦ μέρους τούτου* with *μέρους* understood; it does not repeat the demonstrative in *da quest’ altra*. *πρὸς τὰ κάτω* ‘towards the parts below’ expands *giù*: the article gives adverbial *κάτω* a nominal use after *πρός*.
- *τὸ βάθος αὐτοῦ* ‘its depth’ is the nominative neuter singular subject of *ἀφικνεῖται*, not an object corresponding to *lo fondo suo*. *αὐτοῦ* is a possessive genitive referring to the stream. *μᾶλλον ἔτ’ αὐξάνον* ‘increasing still more’ renders *a più a più … prema* by a present active participle of *αὐξάνω*, nominative neuter singular agreeing with *βάθος*. *ἔτ’* is elided *ἔτι*.
- *ἀφικνεῖται* ‘reaches’ is the third-person singular present middle indicative of deponent *ἀφικνέομαι*. Where *infin ch’ ei si raggiunge* subordinates the endpoint, Greek makes reaching the finite verb and increasing the accompanying process; it does not express ‘again’ separately. Relative *ἔνθα* ‘where’ introduces the destination. *στένειν προσήκει τὴν τυραννίαν* retains the impersonal force of *la tirannia convien che gema*: *προσήκει* ‘it is fitting’ (third-person singular present active indicative of *προσήκω*) takes the present active infinitive *στένειν* with accusative subject *τὴν τυραννίαν*, placed last.

> 133 La divina giustizia di qua punge  
> 134 quell' Attila che fu flagello in terra,  

Divine Justice here torments that Attila, who was a scourge on earth;

> 133 „Ἐβύθισ᾽ ἐκεῖ Θεία Δικαιοσύνη  
> (There Divine Justice has plunged)
>
> 134 „Ἀττίλαν, ὃς ἐγένετ᾽ ἐν τῇ γῇ μάστιξ,  
> (Attila, who was a scourge upon the earth,)

- *Ἐβύθισ᾽ ἐκεῖ Θεία Δικαιοσύνη* ‘there Divine Justice plunged’ puts the action first. *Ἐβύθισ᾽* is elided *ἐβύθισε*, third-person singular aorist active indicative of *βυθίζω*: a completed immersion replaces present *punge*. *ἐκεῖ* ‘there’ takes up *ἔνθα* rather than reproducing the near deixis of *di qua*. *Θεία Δικαιοσύνη* is anarthrous, with adjective and noun in the nominative feminine singular.
- *Ἀττίλαν* is the accusative object, without the demonstrative *quell’*. In *ὃς ἐγένετ᾽ ἐν τῇ γῇ μάστιξ*, the relative *ὅς* is nominative as subject of *ἐγένετ᾽* (elided *ἐγένετο*, third-person singular second aorist middle indicative of *γίγνομαι*). Thus *fu* becomes ‘became’ or ‘proved to be’; *ἐν τῇ γῇ* renders *in terra* with the locative dative, and *μάστιξ* ‘scourge’ is the predicate nominative, postponed to the end.

> 135 e Pirro e Sesto; ed in eterno munge  
> 136 le lagrime, che col bollor disserra  
> 137 a Rinier da Corneto, a Rinier Pazzo,  
> 138 che fecero alle strade tanta guerra.”  

and Pyrrhus and Sextus; and to eternity milks tears, which by the boiling it unlocks, from Rinier of Corneto, from Rinier Pazzo, who on the highways made so much war.”

> 135 „Πύῤῥον καὶ Σέξτον· ἐκεῖ τε δι᾽ αἰῶνος  
> (Pyrrhus and Sextus; and there for evermore)
>
> 136 „Δάκρυ᾽ ἀποσπᾷ ῥέοντ᾽ εἰς ζέον αἷμα  
> (It wrings forth tears flowing into the boiling blood)
>
> 137 „Ῥενείρου τοῦ Κορνέτου, Ῥενείρου Πάζου,  
> (From Rinier of Corneto, from Rinier Pazzo,)
>
> 138 „Τῶν ἐν ὁδοῖς πραξάντων μακρὰς δηώσεις.”  
> (Who on the highways wrought long ravages.")

- *Πύῤῥον καὶ Σέξτον* continues the accusative objects of *Ἐβύθισ᾽*. Greek joins the two with one *καί* rather than *e … e*; *Πύῤῥον* shows the traditional medial double rho with smooth and rough breathings. *ἐκεῖ τε δι᾽ αἰῶνος* begins the next predication: postpositive connective *τε* renders *ed*, *ἐκεῖ* repeats the location, and *δι᾽* (elided *διά*) with genitive singular *αἰῶνος* expresses duration, ‘through all time’, for *in eterno*.
- *Δάκρυ᾽ ἀποσπᾷ* ‘it wrenches away tears’ reverses *munge le lagrime* and drops the particular metaphor of milking. *Δάκρυ᾽* is elided *δάκρυα*, accusative neuter plural of *δάκρυον*; *ἀποσπᾷ* is the contracted third-person singular present active indicative of *ἀποσπάω*, still with Divine Justice as subject.
- *ῥέοντ᾽ εἰς ζέον αἷμα* ‘flowing into boiling blood’ recasts *che col bollor disserra*. *ῥέοντ᾽* is elided *ῥέοντα*, present active participle of *ῥέω* agreeing with the tears: they now perform the action of flowing instead of being the object of *disserra*. Instrumental *col bollor* becomes a destination, *εἰς* with accusative *αἷμα* qualified by the present active participle *ζέον*.
- *Ῥενείρου τοῦ Κορνέτου, Ῥενείρου Πάζου* gives the two sources of the tears in the genitive with *ἀποσπᾷ*, repeating the name as Italian repeats *a Rinier*. The designation *τοῦ Κορνέτου* recasts *da Corneto* in genitive form without a preposition of origin; *Πάζου* is the surname inflected in agreement.
- *Τῶν ἐν ὁδοῖς πραξάντων μακρὰς δηώσεις* replaces relative *che fecero* with an articular participle in apposition to both names. *Τῶν … πραξάντων* is genitive masculine plural, ‘of those who wrought’ (*πραξάντων*, first aorist active participle of *πράσσω*). *ἐν ὁδοῖς* makes *alle strade* explicitly local, with the dative plural of *ὁδός*. *μακρὰς δηώσεις*, accusative feminine plural, changes *tanta guerra* from a singular measured by intensity to plural ‘long ravages’, measured by extent or duration.

> 139 Poi si rivolse, e ripassossi il guazzo.  

Then he turned back, and repassed the ford.

> 139 Τὸν πόρον δ᾽ ἐπιστρέφων διέβη πάλιν.  
> (Then turning back, he crossed the ford again.)

- *Τὸν πόρον* ‘the crossing’ brings the object corresponding to final *il guazzo* to the front. Postpositive *δ᾽* (elided *δέ*) marks the transition without a separate temporal adverb for *Poi*.
- *ἐπιστρέφων* ‘turning back’ is the nominative masculine singular present active participle of *ἐπιστρέφω*, used intransitively. The first of the two coordinated Italian past verbs, *si rivolse*, thus becomes an accompanying action. *διέβη* is the third-person singular root aorist active indicative of *διαβαίνω*, taking *τὸν πόρον* directly; final *πάλιν* supplies the repetition in *ripassossi*, without reproducing the pronominal ending.

## Summary — Close Verbal Echoes, Recast Clauses

Musurus often keeps Dante’s choice of words while changing which action carries the sentence. He preserves the repetition of *pensando … pensi* with *ἐσκεπτόμην … σκέπτῃ*, ‘I was pondering … you ponder’, but dispenses with the verb of motion in *gìa pensando*. The participle is his most versatile means of recasting a clause. An article and participle can stand for a pronoun and relative clause, as *ὁ … ἁρπάσας*, ‘the one who snatched’, does for *Colui che … levò*. After a verb of perception, the participle instead presents an action as witnessed: Dante’s infinitive *far* becomes *ποιοῦντα*, ‘doing’, in agreement with the person seen. Musurus can also reverse the order of dependence: in lines 88–89, *si partì* becomes *λιποῦσα*, ‘having left’, while the relative clause *che mi commise* supplies the main verb, *ἐπέβαλε*, ‘imposed’.

The treatment of purpose and indirect statement shows both the variety of the Greek and the limits of its independence from the Italian. Musurus renders purpose first by a genitive articular infinitive, *τοῦ καταβῆναι*, ‘in order to descend’, and later by a future participle after a verb of motion, *ὀψόμενος*, ‘intending to see’. After verbs of thinking or wishing, an accusative and infinitive regularly replace a finite Italian clause. Yet *πρὶν ἔλθῃ*, ‘before he should come’, follows the finite subjunctive of *pria … che venisse* more closely than ordinary Attic usage in this past narrative. The particles likewise do more than reproduce conjunctions: *μέν … δέ* balances one speaker against another, while postpositive *γάρ* preserves the explanatory force of *chè*.

Some of the sharpest differences turn on a single word. In line 9 *οὐκ ἔνι*, ‘it is not possible’, reverses Dante’s *alcuna via darebbe*, ‘it would afford some passage’. In line 49 *ὀργῆς παράνου*, ‘of senseless wrath’, corresponds to the reading *ira* rather than Oelsner’s *ria*: what is a quality of *cupidigia* in the supplied Italian becomes a second passion in the Greek. Elsewhere the shift is interpretive rather than a matter of reading. At line 119 *ἐν Θεοῦ ναῷ*, ‘in God’s temple’, gives a precise location for *in grembo a Dio*, ‘in God’s bosom’. The Greek can thus be more explicit than the Italian even where it is less literal.
