# Inferno 4

## Lines 1–24 — Awakening and Descent to the First Circle

> 1 Ruppemi l' alto sonno nella testa  
> 2 un greve tuono, sì ch' io mi riscossi,  
> 3 come persona che per forza è desta;  

A heavy thunder broke the deep sleep in my head; so that I started like one who is awaked by force;

> 1 Ἀπέσεισ᾽ ἀπὸ κρατὸς τὸν βαθὺν ὕπνον  
> (There shook from my head the deep sleep)
>
> 2 Ἐκραγεῖσα φρικώδης βροντή, κἀνέστην  
> (A dreadful thunder bursting forth, and I rose up)
>
> 3 Αἴφνης, ὥσπερ τις, ὃς ἐξηγέρθη βίᾳ.  
> (Suddenly, like one who was awakened by force.)

- Like *Ruppemi*, *Ἀπέσεισ᾽* puts the finite verb before both object and subject. It is *ἀπέσεισε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀποσείω*), with the augment after the preverb and the final vowel elided before *ἀπό*. The change from ‘broke’ to ‘shook off’ changes *nella testa* from the place of the sleep to the place from which it is shaken: *ἀπὸ κρατός*, with the poetic gen. sg. *κρατός* of *κάρα*, ‘head’. The possessor expressed by Italian *-mi* is left implicit.
- *Τὸν βαθὺν ὕπνον* (acc. sg.) renders *l’ alto sonno* by ‘the deep sleep’. The delayed subject *φρικώδης βροντή* (nom. fem. sg.) gives *un greve tuono* the quality ‘dreadful’ rather than ‘heavy’. Musurus adds *ἐκραγεῖσα*, nom. fem. sg. of the second aorist passive participle of *ἐκρήγνυμι*, passive in form but intransitive in sense: ‘having burst forth’.
- *Κἀνέστην* is crasis for *καὶ ἀνέστην*; *ἀνέστην* is the 1st sg. intransitive root aorist indicative of *ἀνίστημι*, ‘I rose’. Coordination replaces the result construction *sì ch’ io mi riscossi*, and *αἴφνης* adds ‘suddenly’. In *ὥσπερ τις, ὃς ἐξηγέρθη βίᾳ*, indefinite *τις* supplies *persona*, and relative *ὅς* is the subject of *ἐξηγέρθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ἐξεγείρω*). Where *è desta* presents the person as awake, the aorist presents the awakening as an event; *βίᾳ*, dative of manner, renders *per forza*.

> 4 e l' occhio riposato intorno mossi,  
> 5 dritto levato, e fiso riguardai  
> 6 per conoscer lo loco dov' io fossi.  

and, having risen erect, I moved my rested eyes around, and looked stedfastly to know the place in which I was.

> 4 Τὰς ἀνειμένας ὄψεις πέριξ κινήσας,  
> (Moving my rested eyes all around,)
>
> 5 Ὅλος ὄρθιος, ἀτενὲς διεσκόπουν  
> (Wholly upright, I gazed intently)
>
> 6 Τοῦ διαγνῶναι τὸν τόπον, ἔνθ᾽ ἱστάμην.  
> (To discern the place where I was standing.)

- *Τὰς ἀνειμένας ὄψεις πέριξ κινήσας* makes *l’ occhio riposato intorno mossi* subordinate to the following verb: ‘having moved my rested eyes around’. *Κινήσας* is the nom. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *κινέω*, agreeing with the speaker. The Italian singular becomes the ordinary plural *ὄψεις* (acc. pl. of *ὄψις*), not a dual; *ἀνειμένας*, acc. fem. pl. perfect middle/passive participle of *ἀνίημι*, describes their resulting state, ‘relaxed’. *Πέριξ* is adverbial.
- *Ὅλος ὄρθιος* gives *dritto levato* as a posture, ‘wholly upright’, with two nominatives referring to the subject; the act of rising has already been expressed by *ἀνέστην*. *Ἀτενές* is a neuter singular adjective used adverbially, ‘intently’, for *fiso*. *Διεσκόπουν* (1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *διασκοπέω*, augmented after the preverb) presents *riguardai* as sustained scrutiny.
- *Τοῦ διαγνῶναι* is a genitive articular infinitive of purpose corresponding to *per conoscer*: *διαγνῶναι* is the root aorist active infinitive of *διαγιγνώσκω*, ‘to make out’, with *τὸν τόπον* as object. *Ἔνθ᾽* is *ἔνθα* elided before *ἱστάμην*. The local relative takes the indicative *ἱστάμην* (1st sg. imperf. mid. of *ἵστημι*), ‘where I was standing’, rather than reproducing the subjunctive *fossi* or the bare verb ‘be’.

> 7 Vero è, che in su la proda mi trovai  
> 8 della valle d' abisso dolorosa,  
> 9 che tuono accoglie d' infiniti guai.  

True is it, that I found myself upon the brink of the dolorous Valley of the Abyss, which gathers thunder of endless wailings.

> 7 Καὶ μὴν εὗρον ἐμαυτὸν ἐπὶ τοῦ χείλους  
> (And indeed I found myself upon the brink)
>
> 8 Τῆς ὀδυνηρᾶς κοιλάδος τῆς ἀβύσσου,  
> (Of the sorrowful valley of the abyss,)
>
> 9 Ἔνθ᾽ ἀντηχεῖ πάταγος ἀπείρων γόων.  
> (Where resounds a din of endless wailings.)

- *Καὶ μήν*, ‘and indeed’, does the work of *Vero è, che* without an impersonal verb or a subordinate clause. *Εὗρον ἐμαυτόν* reproduces *mi trovai* with an active verb (*εὗρον*, 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *εὑρίσκω*) and its explicit reflexive object. *Ἐπὶ τοῦ χείλους*, ‘on the lip’, uses *ἐπί* with the genitive for position; *χείλους* is the contracted gen. sg. of neuter *χεῖλος*.
- *Τῆς ὀδυνηρᾶς κοιλάδος τῆς ἀβύσσου* makes the attachments in *della valle d’ abisso dolorosa* explicit: ‘of the sorrowful valley’ depends on ‘lip’, and ‘of the abyss’ depends on ‘valley’. *Ὀδυνηρᾶς* is placed directly before *κοιλάδος* (gen. sg. of *κοιλάς*), rather than after the intervening ‘abyss’.
- The Italian makes the valley the subject of *accoglie* and *tuono* its object. Musurus makes the valley a place, *ἔνθ᾽* (‘where’, *ἔνθα* elided before *ἀντηχεῖ*), and the sound the subject: *ἀντηχεῖ πάταγος*, ‘a din resounds’ (*ἀντηχεῖ*, 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἀντηχέω*). *Ἀπείρων γόων* (gen. pl.) retains *d’ infiniti guai* but specifies the cries as lamentations.

> 10 Oscura, profonda era, e nebulosa,  
> 11 tanto che, per ficcar lo viso al fondo,  
> 12 io non vi discernea alcuna cosa.  

It was so dark, profound, and cloudy, that, with fixing my look upon the bottom, I there discerned nothing.

> 10 Ζοφώδης ἦν, βαθεῖα καὶ νεφελώδης  
> (Dark it was, deep, and misty)
>
> 11 Οὕτως, ὥστε τὸ βλέμμα πήξας εἰς βάθος  
> (So much so that, fixing my gaze into the depths,)
>
> 12 Οὐκ ἠδυνήθην πρᾶγμά τι διακρῖναι.  
> (I was not able to distinguish anything.)

- *Ζοφώδης ἦν, βαθεῖα καὶ νεφελώδης* closely follows the Italian predicate order, moving *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*) forward by one adjective. All three adjectives are nom. fem. sg., agreeing with understood *κοιλάς*; *ζοφώδης* and *νεφελώδης* have a common masculine and feminine form, whereas *βαθεῖα* is the distinct feminine of *βαθύς*.
- *Οὕτως, ὥστε* answers *tanto che*: *ὥστε* with a finite indicative states the actual result. Within that clause *πήξας* (nom. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *πήγνυμι*) takes *τὸ βλέμμα* as object: ‘having fixed my gaze’. The participle replaces *per ficcar* without specifying the precise relation of the effort to its result; *εἰς βάθος*, ‘into the depth’, gives the direction rather than naming a definite bottom.
- *Οὐκ ἠδυνήθην … διακρῖναι*, ‘I was unable to distinguish’, replaces the simple imperfect *non … discernea* with a statement of inability. *Ἠδυνήθην* is the 1st sg. passive-form aorist indicative of deponent *δύναμαι*, with the variant augment *ἠ-*; *διακρῖναι* is the complementary aorist active infinitive of *διακρίνω*. *Πρᾶγμά τι*, ‘anything’, is its accusative object, with enclitic indefinite *τι*; the Italian locative *vi* is not repeated.

> 13 “Or discendiam quaggiù nel cieco mondo,”  
> 14 cominciò il poeta tutto smorto;  
> 15 “io sarò primo, e tu sarai secondo.”  

“Now let us descend into the blind world here below,” began the Poet all pale; “I will be first, and thou shalt be second.”

> 13 “Ἄγ᾽ εἰς τὸν κόσμον τοῦ σκότους καταβῶμεν,”  
> ("Come, let us descend into the world of darkness,")
>
> 14 Ὁ ποιητής μοί φησιν, ἔξωχρος ὅλος·  
> (The poet says to me, wholly pale:)
>
> 15 “Ἐγὼ μὲν πρῶτος, σὺ δὲ δεύτερος ἔσῃ.”  
> ("I shall be first, and you shall be second.")

- *Ἄγ᾽*, elided *ἄγε* (2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *ἄγω*), is an exhortatory ‘come’ rather than a temporal equivalent of *Or*. *Καταβῶμεν* (1st pl. root aor. act. subj. of *καταβαίνω*) is hortatory like *discendiam*. The downward direction of *quaggiù* is conveyed by the compound verb, with no separate ‘here’; *εἰς τὸν κόσμον τοῦ σκότους*, ‘into the world of darkness’, substitutes a descriptive genitive for *cieco*.
- *Ὁ ποιητής μοί φησιν* replaces *cominciò il poeta* with a historical present, ‘the poet says to me’. *Φησιν* is 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *φημί*; the added dative *μοί* bears an accent before the following enclitic verb. *Ἔξωχρος ὅλος* gives *tutto smorto* in reverse order, both adjectives predicated of the speaker.
- *Ἐγὼ μὲν πρῶτος, σὺ δὲ δεύτερος ἔσῃ* balances the explicit pronouns with correlative *μέν … δέ*. The ordinals are predicate nominatives; where Italian supplies both *sarò* and *sarai*, Greek leaves the first-person copula understood. *Ἔσῃ* is the 2nd sg. future middle indicative of *εἰμί*, ‘you will be’.

> 16 Ed io, che del color mi fui accorto,  
> 17 dissi: “Come verrò, se tu paventi,  
> 18 che suoli al mio dubbiare esser conforto?”  

And I, who had remarked his colour, said: “How shall I come, when thou fearest, who art wont to be my strength in doubt?”

> 16 Ὡς δ᾽ εἶδον αὐτὸν ἐγὼ κατωχριῶντα,  
> (And when I saw him turning so pale,)
>
> 17 Εἶπον· “Πῶς ἐλεύσομαι, σοῦ πτοουμένου,  
> (I said: "How shall I come, when you are terrified,)
>
> 18 „Ὃς ἐν τοῖς ἐμοῖς ἐπαρήγεις μοι φόβοις;”  
> (Who bring me aid in my fears?")

- *Ὡς δ᾽ εἶδον* changes *Ed io, che …* from a relative description of *io* to a temporal clause, ‘and when I saw’; *δ᾽* is elided postpositive *δέ*. *Εἶδον* is the suppletive 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*. Instead of noticing *del color*, the speaker sees *αὐτὸν … κατωχριῶντα*, ‘him turning pale’: the supplementary present active participle of *κατωχριάω* agrees with the accusative object of perception. The explicit *ἐγώ* is placed between object and participle.
- *Εἶπον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind., serving as the aorist of *λέγω*) renders *dissi*. *Πῶς ἐλεύσομαι* retains the direct question and future of *Come verrò*; *ἐλεύσομαι* is the 1st sg. future middle indicative of *ἔρχομαι*, with active sense. *Σοῦ πτοουμένου* replaces finite *se tu paventi* with a genitive absolute, ‘with you alarmed’: *πτοουμένου* is the gen. masc. sg. present middle/passive participle of *πτοέω*, passive in sense.
- *Ὃς … ἐπαρήγεις* carries on the address to *σοῦ*: the relative is nominative as subject of its clause, but its second-person antecedent determines the person of *ἐπαρήγεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of poetic *ἐπαρήγω*, ‘come to the aid of’). The simple present can convey habitual action without a separate *suoli*. *Μοι* is the dative of the person aided, replacing *esser conforto* with a verb; *ἐν τοῖς ἐμοῖς … φόβοις*, ‘in my fears’, encloses verb and dative and interprets *al mio dubbiare* as fear rather than hesitation.

> 19 Ed egli a me: “L' angoscia delle genti,  
> 20 che son quaggiù, nel viso mi dipinge  
> 21 quella pietà, che tu per tema senti.  

And he to me: “The anguish of the people who are here below, on my face depaints that pity, which thou takest for fear.

> 19 Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ· “Τὸ τῶν πασχόντων ὧδε  
> (And he to me: "Of those who suffer here,)
>
> 20 „Ἄχθος ἐπ᾽ ἐμοῦ προσώπου διαγράφει  
> (The anguish paints upon my countenance)
>
> 21 „Τὸν οἶκτον αὐτόν, ὃν σὺ φόβον νομίζεις.  
> (That very pity which you suppose to be fear.)

- *Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ* closely follows *Ed egli a me*, likewise omitting the verb of speaking. *Ὁ δέ* uses the article pronominally, ‘and he’; *πρός* with the accusative marks the addressee. *Τὸ τῶν πασχόντων ὧδε ἄχθος* puts the dependent genitive between the article and its neuter singular noun *ἄχθος*, ‘burden, distress’. *Τῶν πασχόντων* is a substantivized gen. masc. pl. present active participle of *πάσχω*: *delle genti, che son quaggiù* becomes ‘of those suffering here’, with *ὧδε* expressing location but not ‘below’.
- *Ἐπ᾽ ἐμοῦ προσώπου* gives *nel viso mi* as ‘upon my face’: *ἐπ᾽* is elided *ἐπί* with the genitive of surface, and *ἐμοῦ* is possessive, agreeing in form here with the gen. neut. sg. of the possessive adjective *ἐμός*. *Διαγράφει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *διαγράφω*) takes *ἄχθος* as subject and *τὸν οἶκτον* as object: ‘traces the pity’.
- *Τὸν οἶκτον αὐτόν* uses intensive *αὐτόν* outside the article–noun group, ‘that very pity’, rather than a demonstrative for *quella*. In *ὃν σὺ φόβον νομίζεις*, *ὅν* is the accusative object and *φόβον* its predicate accusative: ‘which you consider fear’. *Νομίζεις* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *νομίζω*) thus expresses *per tema senti* as a judgment, without a preposition.

> 22 Andiam, chè la via lunga ne sospinge.”  

Let us go; for the length of way impels us.”

> 22 „Ἄγωμεν· ἡ μακρὰ γὰρ ὁδὸς ἐπείγει.”  
> (Let us go; for the long journey urges us on.")

- *Ἄγωμεν* (1st pl. pres. act. subj. of *ἄγω*, used intransitively) is hortatory, like *Andiam*. Postpositive causal *γάρ* renders *chè*, coming after *ἡ μακρά* within the subject phrase *ἡ μακρὰ … ὁδός*. Greek retains the Italian adjective and noun, ‘the long road’, rather than making length the subject. *Ἐπείγει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἐπείγω*) means ‘presses, urges on’; the object expressed by *ne* is understood.

> 23 Così si mise, e così mi fe' entrare  
> 24 nel primo cerchio che l' abisso cinge.  

Thus he entered, and made me enter, into the first circle that girds the abyss.

> 23 Οὗτος δὲ προβὰς ἤγαγέ μ᾽ εἰς τὸν πρῶτον,  
> (So going forward he led me into the first)
>
> 24 Ὃς τὴν ἄβυσσον περιτειχίζει, κύκλον.  
> (Circle that encompasses the abyss.)

- *Οὗτος δὲ προβὰς* means ‘and he, having gone forward’: *οὗτος* is a demonstrative pronoun, not the adverb ‘thus’, and neither occurrence of *così* has a direct counterpart. *Προβάς* (nom. masc. sg. root aor. act. participle of *προβαίνω*) subordinates *si mise* to a single finite verb. *Ἤγαγέ μ᾽* replaces the causative *mi fe’ entrare* with ‘led me’: *ἤγαγε* is the 3rd sg. reduplicated second aor. act. ind. of *ἄγω*, bearing an additional accent before enclitic *μ᾽* (acc. *με* elided before *εἰς*).
- *Εἰς τὸν πρῶτον … κύκλον* uses *εἰς* with the accusative for the destination. In a pronounced departure from *nel primo cerchio che …*, the entire relative clause intervenes between *πρῶτον* and *κύκλον*. Its *ὅς* agrees with that delayed noun in gender and number but is nominative as subject of *περιτειχίζει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *περιτειχίζω*). The compound makes *cinge* more concrete, ‘walls round’, and retains *τὴν ἄβυσσον* as its direct object.

## Lines 25–57 — Sighs and Longing in Limbo

> 25 Quivi, secondo che per ascoltare,  
> 26 non avea pianto, ma' che di sospiri,  
> 27 che l' aura eterna facevan tremare:  

Here there was no plaint, that could be heard, except of sighs, which caused the eternal air to tremble;

> 25 Ἐκεῖ γοῦν, ὅσον ἐξ ἀκοῆς εἰκάσαι,  
> (There indeed, so far as one could gather from hearing,)
>
> 26 Οὐκ ἦσαν κλαυθμοί, πλὴν στεναγμοὶ ποιοῦντες  
> (Were no lamentations, save sighs that made)
>
> 27 Τὸν αἰώνιον ὑποτρέμειν ἀέρα·  
> (The eternal air tremble;)

- *Ἐκεῖ γοῦν* renders *Quivi* with an added particle: ‘there, at any rate’. *ὅσον ἐξ ἀκοῆς εἰκάσαι* makes the qualification *secondo che per ascoltare* explicit: ‘so far as one could judge by hearing’. *εἰκάσαι* (aor. act. inf. of *εἰκάζω*) is an infinitive of limitation, with no expressed subject; *ἐξ* with the genitive indicates the evidence for the inference.
- *Οὐκ ἦσαν κλαυθμοί* ‘there were no lamentations’ replaces impersonal *non avea* and singular *pianto* with an existential imperfect (3rd pl. of *εἰμί*) and a plural nominative. After exceptive *πλήν*, *στεναγμοί* is likewise nominative: ‘only sighs’, not a genitive corresponding to *di sospiri*.
- *ποιοῦντες* (pres. act. ptcp., nom. masc. pl. of *ποιέω*) agrees with *στεναγμοί* and replaces the finite relative *che … facevan*. The causative construction is retained: *τὸν αἰώνιον … ἀέρα* is the accusative subject of *ὑποτρέμειν* (pres. act. inf. of *ὑποτρέμω*), ‘to quiver’, with the infinitive between adjective and noun.

> 28 e ciò avvenia di duol senza martiri,  
> 29 ch' avean le turbe, ch' eran molte e grandi,  
> 30 d' infanti e di femmine e di viri.  

and this arose from the sadness, without torment, of the crowds that were many and great, both of children, and of women and men.

> 28 Ἐγίνοντο δ᾽ ἐκ λύπης ἄνευ βασάνων,  
> (And these came from sorrow without torments,)
>
> 29 Ἥνπερ ᾐσθάνοντ᾽ ἐκεῖ μεγάλα πλήθη  
> (Which great multitudes felt there,)
>
> 30 Ἀναριθμήτων ἀνδρῶν, γυναικῶν, παίδων.  
> (Of countless men, women, and children.)

- *Ἐγίνοντο δ᾽* ‘and they arose’ takes the sighs as subject rather than reproducing singular *ciò avvenia*. *ἐγίνοντο* is the augmented 3rd pl. imperf. mid. of *γίνομαι*; *δ᾽* is elided postpositive *δέ*. *ἐκ λύπης* marks the source, *ἄνευ βασάνων* the absence of torments, both with the genitive.
- *ἥνπερ* (acc. fem. sg. of the relative with emphatic *-περ*) refers to *λύπης* but takes its case from its function in the new clause: it is the object of *ᾐσθάνοντ᾽*, elided *ᾐσθάνοντο* (3rd pl. imperf. mid. of *αἰσθάνομαι*, with temporal augment). Thus *ch’ avean le turbe* becomes ‘which the crowds felt’; *ἐκεῖ* supplies ‘there’.
- *μεγάλα πλήθη* (nom. neut. pl.) is the subject; the plural verb, rather than the usual singular with a neuter plural, suits the multitude of persons. The second Italian relative, *ch’ eran molte e grandi*, disappears: *μεγάλα* qualifies the crowds directly, while *ἀναριθμήτων* ‘countless’ qualifies the genitives specifying their members. *ἀνδρῶν, γυναικῶν, παίδων* reverses the Italian order and dispenses with its conjunctions; *παίδων* is less specific as to age than *infanti*.

> 31 Lo buon maestro a me: “Tu non dimandi  
> 32 che spiriti son questi, che tu vedi?  

The good Master to me : “Thou askest not what spirits are these thou seest?

> 31 Πρὸς ἐμὲ δ᾽ ὁ Προστάτης· “Οὐκ ἐρωτᾷς με,  
> (And my Master to me: "Do you not ask me)
>
> 32 „Τί πνεύματα ταῦτ᾽ ἐστίν, ἃ βλέπεις ὧδε;  
> (What spirits these are whom you see here?)

- *Πρὸς ἐμὲ δ᾽ ὁ Προστάτης* puts the addressee first (*πρός* with acc.) and, like *Lo buon maestro a me*, leaves the verb of speaking understood. *Προστάτης* is ‘guide, protector’ rather than ‘teacher’; *buon* has no separate equivalent.
- *Οὐκ ἐρωτᾷς με* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of contracted *ἐρωτάω*) supplies an object, ‘me’, absent from *Tu non dimandi*. *τί πνεύματα ταῦτ᾽ ἐστίν* retains the interrogative construction: ‘what spirits are these?’ *ταῦτ᾽* is elided *ταῦτα*, nom. neut. pl.; here the copula is singular with a neuter plural subject.
- *ἃ βλέπεις ὧδε* ‘which you see here’ has a neuter plural relative agreeing with *πνεύματα* in gender and number but accusative as object of *βλέπεις*. *ὧδε* adds a local adverb; the personal pronoun of *tu vedi* is unexpressed.

> 33 Or vo' che sappi, innanzi che più andi,  

I wish thee to know, before thou goest farther,

> 33 „Ἀλλ᾽ οὖν θέλω σ᾽ εἰδέναι, πρὶν προβῇς πρόσω.  
> (Now I wish you to know, before you go farther,)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ οὖν* (elided *ἀλλά* followed by *οὖν*) makes the transition *Or* adversative and resumptive: ‘but, then’. *θέλω σ᾽ εἰδέναι* replaces *vo’ che sappi* with an accusative and infinitive: *σ᾽* is elided *σε*, subject of *εἰδέναι*, the perfect active infinitive of *οἶδα* with present sense, ‘to know’.
- *πρὶν προβῇς πρόσω* closely follows *innanzi che più andi*: *προβῇς* is 2nd sg. aor. act. subj. of *προβαίνω*, prospective after *πρίν*, and *πρόσω* is ‘onward’ rather than a comparative. After an affirmative main clause an infinitive with *πρίν* would be more usual in Attic prose.

> 34 ch' ei non peccaro; e s' egli hanno mercedi,  
> 35 non basta, perchè non ebber battesmo,  
> 36 ch'è porte della fede che tu credi;  

that they sinned not ; and though they have merit, it suffices not : for they had not Baptism, which is the portal of the faith that thou believest

> 34 „Οὗτοι μὲν οὐχ ἥμαρτον, ἀλλ᾽ ἡ χρηστότης  
> (These did not sin; yet their goodness)
>
> 35 „ Οὐκ ἤρκεσ᾽ αὐτοῖς, ὡς οὐ τετυχηκόσι  
> (Did not suffice them, since they did not obtain)
>
> 36 „ Τοῦ βαπτίσματος, θύρας τῆς σῆς λατρείας.  
> (Baptism, the portal of your worship.)

- Where *ch’ ei non peccaro* still depends on ‘know’, *Οὗτοι μὲν οὐχ ἥμαρτον* begins an independent statement: ‘these, it is true, did not sin’. *ἥμαρτον* is 3rd pl. second aor. act. ind. of *ἁμαρτάνω*, with temporal augment; *οὐχ* is the form of the negative before a rough breathing. Concessive *μέν* prepares for *ἀλλά*.
- *ἀλλ᾽ ἡ χρηστότης οὐκ ἤρκεσ᾽ αὐτοῖς* ‘but their goodness did not suffice them’ suppresses the conditional form of *s’ egli hanno mercedi* and makes ‘goodness’ the subject. *ἤρκεσ᾽* is elided *ἤρκεσε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀρκέω*), past rather than present *basta*, with dative *αὐτοῖς*.
- *ὡς οὐ τετυχηκόσι τοῦ βαπτίσματος* gives the stated ground in place of finite *perchè non ebber battesmo*: ‘on the ground that they had not obtained baptism’. *τετυχηκόσι* (perf. act. ptcp., dat. masc. pl. of *τυγχάνω*) agrees with *αὐτοῖς*; the perfect presents the result, and *τυγχάνω* in the sense ‘obtain’ governs the genitive.
- *θύρας* is genitive **singular** of *θύρα*, in apposition to *τοῦ βαπτίσματος*: ‘the door of your worship’. This dispenses with the copula of *ch’è porte*; *τῆς σῆς λατρείας* substitutes ‘your worship’ for *della fede che tu credi*, absorbing the relative clause into the possessive.

> 37 e se furon dinanzi al Cristianesmo,  
> 38 non adorar debitamente Dio;  
> 39 e di questi cotai son io medesmo.  

; and seeing they were before Christianity, they worshipped not God aright ; and of these am I myself.

> 37 „ Γενόμενοι γὰρ πρὶν τῆς Χριστοῦ θρησκείας,  
> (For having been born before the religion of Christ,)
>
> 38 „ Οὐκ ἐλάτρευσαν τὸν Θεόν, ὥσπερ ἔδει·  
> (They did not worship God as was fitting;)
>
> 39 „ Εἷς δ᾽ ἐνθάδ᾽ εἰμὶ κἀγὼ μεταξὺ τούτων.  
> (And I myself am one here among these.)

- *Γενόμενοι γάρ* (second aor. mid. ptcp., nom. masc. pl. of *γίνομαι*) gives ‘for, having come into being’ in place of *e se furon*: Musurus takes the circumstance as given, not hypothetical. *πρὶν τῆς Χριστοῦ θρησκείας* uses *πρίν* prepositionally with a genitive; ‘the religion of Christ’ expands *Cristianesmo*.
- *Οὐκ ἐλάτρευσαν τὸν Θεόν* has an augmented aorist (3rd pl. act. ind. of *λατρεύω*) corresponding to *non adorar*. The accusative reproduces the direct object of Italian *adorar* rather than the usual Greek dative of the one served. *ὥσπερ ἔδει* ‘just as was requisite’ expands adverbial *debitamente* into a comparative clause, with the impersonal imperfect of *δεῖ*.
- *Εἷς δ᾽ ἐνθάδ᾽ εἰμὶ κἀγὼ μεταξὺ τούτων* ‘and I too am one here among these’ expands *e di questi cotai son io medesmo*. Predicate *εἷς* is emphatically first; *μεταξύ* governs the genitive. *ἐνθάδ᾽* is elided *ἐνθάδε*, supplied by Musurus, and *κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*: additive ‘I too’, rather than intensive ‘I myself’.

> 40 Per tai difetti, non per altro rio,  
> 41 semo perduti, e sol di tanto offesi,  
> 42 che senza speme vivemo in disio.”  

For such defects, and for no other fault, are we lost ; and only in so far afflicted, that without hope we live in desire.”

> 40 „ Δι᾽ ἐλλείψεις τοιαύτας, οὐχὶ δι᾽ ἄλλο  
> (Through such defects, not through any other)
>
> 41 „ Πταῖσμ᾽ ὀλώλαμεν· κόλασις δ᾽ ἡμῖν μόνη  
> (Fault, are we lost; and our only punishment)
>
> 42 „ Τὸ διάγειν ἐν πόθοις ἄνευ ἐλπίδος.“  
> (Is to live in longing without hope.")

- *Δι᾽ ἐλλείψεις τοιαύτας, οὐχὶ δι᾽ ἄλλο πταῖσμ᾽* closely preserves the parallel *Per tai difetti, non per altro rio*. Both instances of elided *διά* govern the accusative of cause; *οὐχί* is emphatic negation, and *πταῖσμ᾽* is elided *πταῖσμα* ‘stumble, fault’.
- *ὀλώλαμεν* is 1st pl. second perf. act. ind. of *ὄλλυμι*, with Attic reduplication. The perfect is intransitive and denotes the present result, ‘we are lost’, without the copula and participle of *semo perduti*.
- *κόλασις δ᾽ ἡμῖν μόνη τὸ διάγειν* turns *sol di tanto offesi, che …* into a nominal equation with an understood copula: ‘our only punishment [is] to pass our lives …’. *μόνη* agrees with *κόλασις*; *ἡμῖν* is a possessive dative, and *τὸ διάγειν* an articular present active infinitive. *ἐν πόθοις* pluralizes *in disio*; *ἄνευ ἐλπίδος*, with the genitive, moves *senza speme* to the close.

> 43 Gran duol mi prese al cuor, quando lo intesi,  
> 44 perocchè gente di molto valore  
> 45 conobbi, che in quel limbo eran sospesi.  

Great sadness took me at the heart on hearing this ; because I knew men of much worth, who in that Limbo were suspense.

> 43 Δεινὴν ᾐσθόμην λύπην ἀκούσας ταῦτα·  
> (Grievous sorrow I felt on hearing this;)
>
> 44 Πολλοὺς γὰρ ἄνδρας ἀξίας ὑπερόχου  
> (For many men of surpassing worth)
>
> 45 Ἔγνων ἐν τούτῳ τῷ κύκλῳ μετεώρους.  
> (I knew to be in suspense within this circle.)

- *Δεινὴν ᾐσθόμην λύπην* ‘I felt grievous sorrow’ keeps the adjective prominent but makes the speaker the subject and the sorrow his accusative object, reversing *Gran duol mi prese*. *ᾐσθόμην* is 1st sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *αἰσθάνομαι*. *al cuor* disappears; *ἀκούσας ταῦτα* (aor. act. ptcp., nom. masc. sg. of *ἀκούω*) replaces *quando lo intesi* with a temporal participle and plural object.
- Postpositive *γάρ* renders *perocchè*. *πολλοὺς … ἄνδρας ἀξίας ὑπερόχου* is ‘many men of surpassing worth’: the collective *gente* becomes a masculine plural, and the degree in *di molto valore* is redistributed between the number of men and the quality ascribed to them. *ἀξίας ὑπερόχου* is a genitive of quality; *ὑπερόχου* is feminine here, from two-termination *ὑπέροχος*.
- *ἔγνων* is 1st sg. root aor. act. ind. of *γιγνώσκω*. *μετεώρους* ‘suspended’ is a predicate adjective agreeing with *ἄνδρας* in the accusative: ‘I recognized them as suspended’, in place of the finite *che … eran sospesi*. *ἐν τούτῳ τῷ κύκλῳ* substitutes ‘in this circle’ for *in quel limbo*, changing both noun and demonstrative.

> 46 “Dimmi, Maestro mio, dimmi, Signore,”  
> 47 cominciai io, per voler esser certo  
> 48 di quella fede che vince ogni errore;  

“Tell me, Master ; tell me, Sir,” I began, desiring to be assured of that Faith which conquers every error

> 46 “ Προστάτα κἄρχων ἐμός, λέγε μοι, λέγε,”  
> ("My Master and Lord, tell me, tell me,")
>
> 47 Εἶπον περὶ πίστεως βεβαιωθῆναι  
> (I said, concerning faith to be assured)
>
> 48 Θέλων ἐκείνης, ἣ νικᾷ πᾶσαν πλάνην,  
> (Wishing, that which overcomes every error,)

- *Προστάτα κἄρχων ἐμός* collects the two addresses before the repeated imperative. *Προστάτα* is voc. sg. of *προστάτης*; *κἄρχων* is crasis of *καὶ ἄρχων*. *ἄρχων ἐμός* stands in the nominative in address, ‘my ruler’, where strictly vocative forms would be *ἄρχον ἐμέ*. The single possessive thus falls with the second title, not with *Maestro mio*.
- *λέγε μοι, λέγε* repeats the 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *λέγω* but gives the dative ‘to me’ only once, unlike *Dimmi … dimmi*. *Εἶπον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. in the paradigm of *λέγω*) gives simply ‘I said’ for *cominciai io*.
- *περὶ πίστεως βεβαιωθῆναι θέλων ἐκείνης* recasts *per voler esser certo*: *θέλων* (pres. act. ptcp., nom. masc. sg. of *θέλω*) expresses the speaker’s motive and governs *βεβαιωθῆναι* (aor. pass. inf. of *βεβαιόω*), ‘to be made certain’. *περί* with the genitive marks the matter on which assurance is sought; *ἐκείνης* agrees with *πίστεως* across the intervening verbs.
- *ἣ νικᾷ πᾶσαν πλάνην* follows *che vince ogni errore* closely. The relative *ἥ* takes feminine singular from *πίστεως* but nominative case as subject of contracted *νικᾷ* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *νικάω*); anarthrous singular *πᾶσαν πλάνην* is distributive, ‘every error’.

> 49 “uscicci mai alcuno, o per suo merto,  
> 50 o per altrui, che poi fosse beato?”  

; “did ever any, by his own merit, or by others’, go out from hence, that afterwards was blessed?”

> 49 “ Ἐξῆλθέ τίς ποτ᾽ ἐντεῦθεν δι᾽ ἰδίαν  
> ("Did anyone ever go forth from here through his own)
>
> 50 „ Ἀξίαν ἢ δι᾽ ἄλλου, γεγονὼς μάκαρ ;”  
> (Merit or another's, and become blessed?")

- *Ἐξῆλθέ τίς ποτ᾽ ἐντεῦθεν* unpacks *uscicci mai alcuno*: *ἐξῆλθε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἐξέρχομαι*) gives the motion out, *ἐντεῦθεν* its point of departure. *τις* ‘anyone’ and *ποτε* ‘ever’ are successive enclitics, accounting for the written accents in *Ἐξῆλθέ τίς*; *ποτ᾽* loses its final vowel before *ἐντεῦθεν*. This is a yes-or-no question, not interrogative *τίς* ‘who?’.
- *δι᾽ ἰδίαν ἀξίαν* has causal *διά* with the accusative, ‘on account of his own worth’. *ἢ δι᾽ ἄλλου* can be understood elliptically after it, ‘or on account of [the worth] of another’, as in *o per altrui*; taken directly with the expressed genitive, it means ‘or through another’, with a shift from cause to agency.
- *γεγονὼς μάκαρ* ‘having become blessed’ replaces *che poi fosse beato*. *γεγονώς* (perf. act. ptcp., nom. masc. sg. of *γίγνομαι*) agrees with *τις* and takes predicate *μάκαρ*, a poetic nominative singular. The perfect marks an attained state; there is no separate *poi* and no finite subjunctive.

> 51 E quei, che intese il mio parlar coverto,  
> 52 rispose: “Io era nuovo in questo stato,  

And he, understanding my covert speech, replied : “I was new in this condition,

> 51 Ὁ δ᾽ ἐννοήσας τῶν λόγων τὸ κρυψίνουν  
> (And he, perceiving the covert meaning of my words,)
>
> 52 Ἀπεκρίθη μοι· “ Νέηλυς ἐνταῦθ᾽ ἤμην,  
> (Answered me: "I was a newcomer here,)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ ἐννοήσας … ἀπεκρίθη μοι* renders *E quei, che intese … rispose*: pronominal *ὁ δέ* ‘and he’ is followed by a circumstantial aorist active participle (nom. masc. sg. of *ἐννοέω*) in place of the relative clause. *ἀπεκρίθη* is 3rd sg. aor. pass. in form, active in sense, of *ἀποκρίνομαι*; *μοι* adds the addressee.
- *τῶν λόγων τὸ κρυψίνουν* ‘the covert element of the words’ makes the adjective the substantive object of ‘perceiving’. *τὸ κρυψίνουν* is the contracted acc. neut. sg. of *κρυψίνους*; the preposed genitive specifies the words in which that quality lies. Italian instead makes *il mio parlar* the object and *coverto* its modifier; the Greek does not express *mio*.
- *Νέηλυς ἐνταῦθ᾽ ἤμην* gives ‘I was a newcomer here’: predicate *νέηλυς* is a poetic compound, literally ‘newly come’, and the local adverb replaces *in questo stato*. *ἐνταῦθ᾽* is *ἐνταῦθα* elided before *ἤμην*, a later 1st sg. imperfect of *εἰμί* in place of Attic *ἦ*.

> 53 quando ci vidi venire un possente  
> 54 con segno di vittoria coronato.  

when I saw a Mighty One come to us, crowned with sign of victory.

> 53 „ Ὅτε καταβάντ᾽ εἶδον ἰσχυρὸν ἄνδρα,  
> (When I saw a mighty One descending,)
>
> 54 „ Ὃς ἐστεφανωμένος σημείῳ νίκης  
> (Who, crowned with the sign of victory,)

- *ὅτε καταβάντ᾽ εἶδον ἰσχυρὸν ἄνδρα* keeps the temporal clause but replaces the infinitive of *vidi venire* with the participle usual in Greek after direct perception. *καταβάντ᾽* is elided *καταβάντα* (root aor. act. ptcp., acc. masc. sg. of *καταβαίνω*), agreeing with the object of *εἶδον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. in the paradigm of *ὁράω*). It specifies descent rather than mere coming; *ci* has no separate equivalent, and *ἄνδρα* supplies a noun for substantival *un possente*.
- *ὃς ἐστεφανωμένος σημείῳ νίκης* begins a relative clause whose finite verb comes in 55. *ἐστεφανωμένος* (perf. mid./pass. ptcp., nom. masc. sg. of *στεφανόω*, passive in sense) agrees with *ὅς*, not with accusative *ἄνδρα*: ‘who, in the state of having been crowned …’. The initial *ἐ-* is perfect reduplication by vowel prefix before *στ-*, not a past indicative augment. *σημείῳ* is an instrumental dative for *con segno*, with dependent genitive *νίκης*.

> 55 Trasseci l' ombra del primo parente,  
> 56 d' Abel suo figlio, e quella di Noè,  
> 57 di Moisè Legista, e ubbidiente;  

He took away from us the shade of our First Parent, of Abel his son, and that of Noah ; of Moses the Legislator and obedient ;

> 55 „Ἐξήγαγε τὴν σκιὰν τοῦ πρωτοπλάστου,  
> (Led forth the shade of the first-formed man,)
>
> 56 „Υἱοῦ τ᾽ Ἄβελ καὶ Νῶε καὶ Μωϋσέως  
> (And of his son Abel, and Noah, and Moses,)
>
> 57 „Εὐπειθοῦς νομοθέτου, καὶ πατριάρχου  
> (Obedient lawgiver, and the patriarch)

- *ἐξήγαγε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἐξάγω*, with internal augment) completes *ὅς*: ‘who led out’. The prefix conveys removal, but there is no distinct pronoun for *-ci* in *Trasseci*. *τὴν σκιὰν τοῦ πρωτοπλάστου* retains singular ‘shade’ with a dependent genitive, replacing *primo parente* with the substantivized adjective *πρωτόπλαστος*, ‘first-formed’.
- *υἱοῦ τ᾽ Ἄβελ καὶ Νῶε καὶ Μωϋσέως* continues the dependence on ‘shade’ without repeating *quella*. *τ᾽* is elided enclitic *τε*, coordinating with *καί*. *υἱοῦ* stands before the indeclinable name *Ἄβελ* in apposition; the possessive of *suo figlio* is implicit. *Νῶε* is likewise indeclinable but syntactically genitive, whereas *Μωϋσέως* is an inflected genitive.
- *εὐπειθοῦς νομοθέτου* places ‘obedient’ before ‘lawgiver’, both gen. masc. sg. in agreement or apposition with *Μωϋσέως*. Rather than coordinating a title and an adjective as in *Legista, e ubbidiente*, Musurus makes *εὐπειθοῦς* (from *εὐπειθής*) modify the title. Greek 57 already begins the next member of the list, *καὶ πατριάρχου* ‘and of the patriarch’, for which there are no words in Italian 57.

## Lines 58–90 — The Blessed and the Honoured Poets

> 58 Abraam patriarca, e David re,  
> 59 Israel con lo padre, e co’ suoi nati,  
> 60 e con Rachele, per cui tanto fe’,  
> 61 ed altri molti; e fecegli beati:  

Abraham the Patriarch; David the King; Israel with his father and his children, and [with] Rachel, for whom he did so much; and many others, and made them blessed;

> 58 „Ἀβραὰμ καὶ Δαυΐδ τοῦ βασιλέως,  
> (Abraham, and David the king,)
>
> 59 „Ἰσραήλ τε σὺν πατρὶ καὶ σὺν τοῖς τέκνοις  
> (And Israel with his father and with his children,)
>
> 60 „Καὶ σὺν Ῥαχήλ, ὑπὲρ ἧς ἔκαμε τόσον,  
> (And with Rachel, for whom he laboured so much,)
>
> 61 „Καὶ πολλῶν ἄλλων, μακαρίους ποιήσας.  
> (And many others, making them blessed.)

- *Ἀβραὰμ καὶ Δαυΐδ τοῦ βασιλέως* continues a list already in progress. The names are indeclinable, but the appositive *τοῦ βασιλέως* (gen. sg. of *βασιλεύς*) shows the case: the names depend on the preceding mention of the shade taken away. Musurus keeps *re*, with the Greek article, but leaves *patriarca* untranslated.
- *Ἰσραήλ τε σὺν πατρὶ καὶ σὺν τοῖς τέκνοις* adds the next name with enclitic *τε* ‘and’. The repeated *σύν* + dative follows the repeated *con*; *πατρί* is dat. sg. of *πατήρ*, *τέκνοις* dat. pl. of *τέκνον*. The relationship supplies the sense of possession without a Greek equivalent of *suoi*. A third *σύν* governs indeclinable *Ῥαχήλ*.
- *ὑπὲρ ἧς ἔκαμε τόσον* makes *per cui* specifically ‘for whose sake’: *ὑπέρ* governs the gen. fem. sg. relative *ἧς*. For the unspecific *fe’*, *ἔκαμε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *κάμνω*) gives ‘toiled’; neut. acc. sg. *τόσον* expresses extent, ‘so much’.
- *πολλῶν ἄλλων* (gen. pl.) continues the construction of the names. With *μακαρίους ποιήσας* Musurus changes *e fecegli beati* from a coordinated finite verb to a supplementary action of the same agent: *ποιήσας* is the nom. masc. sg. aor. act. participle of *ποιέω*. The object represented by *-gli* is understood, not expressed; *μακαρίους* is its predicate accusative, not an adjective agreeing with *πολλῶν ἄλλων*.

> 62 e vo’ che sappi che, dinanzi ad essi,  
> 63 spiriti umani non eran salvati.”  

and I wish thee to know, that, before these, no human souls were saved.”

> 62 „Θέλω δέ σε γινώσκειν, ὅτι πρὸ τούτων  
> (And I wish you to know that before these)
>
> 63 „Οὐδέν ποτ᾽ ἀνθρώπινον ἐσώθη πνεῦμα.”  
> (No human spirit was ever saved.")

- *Θέλω δέ σε γινώσκειν* replaces *vo’ che sappi*, with its finite subjunctive, by *θέλω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.) and the pres. act. infinitive *γινώσκειν* with accusative subject *σε*. Postpositive *δέ* carries on the speech; *ὅτι* introduces what is to be known.
- *πρὸ τούτων* takes *dinanzi ad essi* temporally; *πρό* governs the genitive. In place of plural *spiriti umani non eran salvati*, Musurus frames the clause with singular *οὐδέν … πνεῦμα* ‘not one … spirit’; *ἀνθρώπινον* agrees with *πνεῦμα* in the nominative neuter singular. *ποτ᾽* is elided *ποτε*, ‘ever’ under the negative. *ἐσώθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *σῴζω*) presents the saving as an event, rather than reproducing the Italian imperfect passive.

> 64 Non lasciavam l’ andar, perch’ ei dicessi,  
> 65 ma passavam la selva tuttavia,  
> 66 la selva dico di spiriti spessi.  

We ceased not to go, though he was speaking; but passed the wood meanwhile, the wood, I say, of crowded spirits.

> 64 Ταῦτα λέγοντος, ἐβαδίζομεν πρόσω,  
> (While he was speaking thus, we walked onward,)
>
> 65 Διαβαίνοντες ἐμμενῶς τὸν δρυμῶνα,  
> (Passing continually through the wood—)
>
> 66 Δρυμῶνα, λέγω, πνευμάτων καταπύκνων.  
> (The wood, I mean, of densely crowded spirits.)

- *Ταῦτα λέγοντος* turns concessive *perch’ ei dicessi* into a temporal genitive absolute, ‘as he was saying these things’. *λέγοντος* is gen. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *λέγω*, its subject understood; *ταῦτα* is its supplied neut. pl. object. The positive *ἐβαδίζομεν πρόσω* ‘we kept walking forward’ (1st pl. imperf. act. ind. of *βαδίζω*) gives the effect of *non lasciavam l’ andar* without its negation or substantivized infinitive.
- *διαβαίνοντες* (nom. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *διαβαίνω*) agrees with the walkers: Greek makes *ma passavam* a concurrent action rather than a contrasting finite clause. *ἐμμενῶς* ‘persistently, without intermission’ takes *tuttavia* in its continuative sense; *τὸν δρυμῶνα* (acc. sg. of *δρυμών*) is the tract traversed.
- *Δρυμῶνα, λέγω* repeats that accusative, now without the article, and retains parenthetic *dico*. *πνευμάτων καταπύκνων* is a genitive of contents, with the compound adjective (gen. neut. pl. of *κατάπυκνος*) intensifying *spessi*: ‘very densely packed’.

> 67 Non era lunga ancor la nostra via  
> 68 di qua dal sonno, quando’ io vidi un foco,  
> 69 ch’ emisperio di tenebre vincia.  

Our way was not yet far [since my slumber], when I saw a fire, which conquered a hemisphere of the darkness.

> 67 Ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἦν πολὺ μακρὰν ἡμῶν ὁ δρόμος  
> (But not very far had our journey gone)
>
> 68 Ἐκεῖθεν εἰς κορυφήν, ὅτε πῦρ εἶδον  
> (Thence to the summit, when I saw a fire)
>
> 69 Διῆκον δι᾽ ὀρφνώδους ἡμισφαιρίου.  
> (Piercing through a hemisphere of darkness.)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἦν πολὺ μακρὰν ἡμῶν ὁ δρόμος* adds a connective (*ἀλλ᾽*, elided *ἀλλά*) and postpones the subject *ὁ δρόμος*, with possessive genitive *ἡμῶν*. Instead of *lunga* qualifying *via*, *μακράν* is adverbial ‘far’, with neut. acc. *πολύ* marking degree. *ἦν* is 3rd sg. imperf. of *εἰμί*; *ancor* has no separate equivalent.
- *Ἐκεῖθεν εἰς κορυφήν* means ‘from there to a summit’: the directional adverb in *-θεν* gives the starting point, *εἰς* + accusative the goal. This is not the temporal reckoning of *di qua dal sonno* in the supplied Italian; it corresponds in sense to a reading with *sommo* rather than *sonno*.
- *ὅτε πῦρ εἶδον* keeps the temporal clause; *εἶδον* is 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*. For relative *ch’ … vincia*, *διῆκον* is the acc. neut. sg. pres. act. participle of *διήκω*, agreeing with *πῦρ*: ‘extending through’ rather than ‘conquering’. *διά* + gen. *ὀρφνώδους ἡμισφαιρίου* expresses the space penetrated, with ‘dark’ adjectival rather than *di tenebre* nominal.

> 70 Di lungi v’ eravamo ancora un poco,  
> 71 ma non sì, ch’ io non discernessi in parte,  
> 72 che orrevol gente possedea quel loco.  

We were still a little distant from it; yet not so distant, that I did not in part discern what honourable people occupied that place.

> 70 Μικρὸν μὲν ἦμεν ἀφεστῶτες ἐκεῖθεν,  
> (We were at a little distance from there,)
>
> 71 Ἀλλ᾽ οὐ τοσοῦτον, ὥστε μὴ διακρῖναι,  
> (Yet not so far as not to discern)
>
> 72 Ὅτι τόπος ἦν οὗτος ἀνδρῶν ἐξόχων.  
> (That this was a place of eminent men.)

- *Μικρὸν μὲν ἦμεν ἀφεστῶτες ἐκεῖθεν* expresses distance as a continuing state: *ἦμεν* (1st pl. imperf. of *εἰμί*) with *ἀφεστῶτες* (nom. masc. pl. perfect act. participle of *ἀφίστημι*, intransitive ‘standing apart’). *μικρόν* is an accusative of extent, *ἐκεῖθεν* ‘from there’; there is no separate *ancora*. Postpositive *μέν* prepares for the qualification with *ἀλλά*.
- *ἀλλ᾽ οὐ τοσοῦτον, ὥστε μὴ διακρῖναι* ‘but not so far as not to distinguish’ replaces *ma non sì, ch’ io non discernessi* by a degree expression and a consecutive infinitive. *μή* negates *διακρῖναι* (aor. act. inf. of *διακρίνω*); *οὐ* negates the degree. The infinitive has no expressed singular subject to mark the Italian switch from ‘we’ to *io*, and *in parte* drops out.
- *ὅτι τόπος ἦν οὗτος ἀνδρῶν ἐξόχων* gives the perception as a statement, ‘that this was a place of eminent men’. *οὗτος* is the subject, *τόπος* the predicate, and the men appear in a dependent genitive plural, rather than as the collective subject *orrevol gente* of active *possedea*.

> 73 “O tu, che onori ogni scienza ed arte,  
> 74 questi chi son, ch’ hanno cotanto orranza,  
> 75 che dal modo degli altri li diparte?”  

“O thou, that honourest every science and art; who are these, who have such honour, that it separates them from the manner of the rest?”

> 73 “Ὦ Σύ, σοφίαν καὶ τέχνην τιμῶν πᾶσαν,  
> ("O you who honour all wisdom and art,)
>
> 74 „Οὗτοι τίνες, οἳ τιμὴν ἔχουσι τόσην,  
> (Who are these who possess such great honour,)
>
> 75 „Ὥστε συνεδρεύουσι χωρὶς τῶν ἄλλων;”  
> (That they sit together apart from the rest?")

- *Ὦ Σύ* retains the emphatic direct address. *σοφίαν καὶ τέχνην τιμῶν πᾶσαν* changes relative *che onori* to the contracted pres. act. participle *τιμῶν* (from *τιμάω*, masc. sg., referring to *σύ*). *σοφίαν* ‘wisdom’ broadens *scienza*; final *πᾶσαν*, acc. fem. sg., can qualify both coordinated singular objects.
- *Οὗτοι τίνες* reproduces the demonstrative followed by the interrogative, both nom. masc. pl., but omits the copula of *questi chi son*. Relative *οἳ* resumes *οὗτοι* as subject of *ἔχουσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *ἔχω*); *τιμὴν … τόσην* is ‘honour so great’, preserving the lexical echo of *τιμῶν*.
- *ὥστε συνεδρεύουσι χωρὶς τῶν ἄλλων* uses a finite indicative after *ὥστε* for an actual result. *συνεδρεύουσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. of *συνεδρεύω*) supplies ‘sit together’; *χωρίς* governs the genitive ‘apart from the others’. The men, objects of *diparte*, become the subject, and there is no noun for *modo*.

> 76 E quegli a me: “L’onrata nominanza,  
> 77 che di lor suona su nella tua vita,  
> 78 grazia acquista nel ciel che sì gli avanza.”  

And he to me: “The honoured name, which sounds of them, up in that life of thine, gains favour in heaven which thus advances them.”

> 76 Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ· “Τὸ μέγα κλέος τούτων  
> (And he to me: "Their great renown,)
>
> 77 „Τὸ διαλάμψαν ἄχρι τῶν ἡμερῶν σου  
> (Which shines forth even down to your days,)
>
> 78 „Ἔδωκ’ αὐτοῖς θεόθεν τὴν χάριν ταύτην.”  
> (Has given them from God this grace.")

- *Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ* follows the verbless *E quegli a me*: pronominal *ὁ* with postpositive *δέ* marks the change of speaker; *πρός* + accusative marks the addressee. *τὸ μέγα κλέος τούτων* ‘the great renown of these men’ substitutes magnitude for *onrata* and attaches the equivalent of *di lor* directly to *κλέος*.
- *τὸ διαλάμψαν* repeats the article to introduce an attributive participle (nom. neut. sg. aor. act. of *διαλάμπω*, agreeing with *κλέος*). Where the name presently *suona*, the renown ‘has shone forth’; *ἄχρι τῶν ἡμερῶν σου* ‘as far as your days’ makes the limit temporal instead of locating the sound *su nella tua vita*.
- *ἔδωκ᾽ αὐτοῖς θεόθεν τὴν χάριν ταύτην* changes the direction of *grazia acquista*: renown ‘gave them this favour’. *ἔδωκ᾽* is elided *ἔδωκε*, 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *δίδωμι*; *αὐτοῖς* is the recipient dative. *θεόθεν* ‘from God’ denotes source rather than location *nel ciel*. Demonstrative *ταύτην* points to the favour described, in place of the clause *che sì gli avanza*.

> 79 Intanto voce fu per me udita:  

Meanwhile a voice was heard by me:

> 79 Τότε φωνὴ τοῖς ἐμοῖς ὠσὶν ἠκούσθη·  
> (Then a voice was heard in my ears:)

- *Τότε* ‘then’ marks succession more than the simultaneity of *Intanto*. *φωνὴ … ἠκούσθη* keeps the passive (*ἠκούσθη*, 3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ἀκούω*, with temporal augment). For the personal agent *per me*, *τοῖς ἐμοῖς ὠσίν* gives the organs of perception in the dative plural: *ὠσίν* is from *οὖς* ‘ear’, with the possessive adjective inside the article phrase.

> 80 “Onorate l’ altissimo poeta;  
> 81 l’ombra sua torna, ch’ era dipartita.”  

“Honour the great Poet! His shade returns that was departed.”

> 80 “Τὸν ἔξοχον ποιητὴν δεῦτε τιμᾶτε,  
> ("Come, honour the pre-eminent poet,)
>
> 81 „Οὗπερ ἀποῦσ’ ἡ σκιὰ νῦν ἐπιστρέφει.”  
> (Whose shade, that was absent, now returns.")

- *Τὸν ἔξοχον ποιητήν* fronts the accusative object; *ἔξοχον* ‘outstanding’ renders *altissimo* without a morphological superlative. *δεῦτε* is a plural summons, ‘come’, added before *τιμᾶτε* (2nd pl. pres. act. imperative of contracted *τιμάω*).
- *οὗπερ* (gen. masc. sg. relative *οὗ* strengthened by *-περ*) joins the second line to the first: ‘whose very shade’, for independent *l’ombra sua*. *ἀποῦσ᾽* is elided *ἀποῦσα*, nom. fem. sg. pres. participle of *ἄπειμι* ‘be absent’, agreeing with *ἡ σκιά*. It gives the state of absence rather than the prior departure of *ch’ era dipartita*; added *νῦν* sharpens the contrast with *ἐπιστρέφει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind.), ‘returns’.

> 82 Poichè la voce fu restata e queta,  
> 83 vidi quattro grand’ ombre a noi venire:  
> 84 sembianza avevan nè trista nè lieta.  

After the voice had paused, and was silent, I saw four great shadows come to us; they had an aspect neither sad nor joyful.

> 82 Τῆς φωνῆς σιγησάσης, τέσσαρας εἶδον  
> (When the voice grew silent, I saw four)
>
> 83 Σκιὰς μεγάλας πρὸς ἡμᾶς ἐρχομένας,  
> (Great shades coming towards us,)
>
> 84 Οὔτε λύπης ἢ χαρᾶς ἐχούσας ὄψιν.  
> (Having an aspect neither of sorrow nor of joy.)

- *Τῆς φωνῆς σιγησάσης* compresses *Poichè la voce fu restata e queta* into a temporal genitive absolute. *σιγησάσης* (gen. fem. sg. aor. act. participle of *σιγάω*) treats the onset of silence as a single event prior to *εἶδον*.
- *τέσσαρας εἶδον σκιὰς μεγάλας … ἐρχομένας* splits the object around the verb: *τέσσαρας* is the acc. pl. form for masculine or feminine (with *-σσ-* rather than distinctively Attic *-ττ-*). Where Italian has *vidi … venire*, Greek uses a participle of perceived action, *ἐρχομένας* (acc. fem. pl. pres. middle of *ἔρχομαι*) agreeing with *σκιάς*. *πρὸς ἡμᾶς* gives the direction of approach.
- *ἐχούσας* (acc. fem. pl. pres. act. participle of *ἔχω*) keeps the shades within the construction of *εἶδον*, where *sembianza avevan* starts a finite assertion. *ὄψιν* ‘aspect’ is its object; the adjectives *trista* and *lieta* become genitives of the nouns *λύπης* and *χαρᾶς* dependent on *ὄψιν*. The printed pairing is *οὔτε … ἢ* ‘neither … or’, not the regular correlative *οὔτε … οὔτε*.

> 85 Lo buon Maestro cominciò a dire:  

The good Master began to speak:

> 85 Προστάτης δ’ οὑμὸς ὧδ’ ἤρξατό μοι λέγων·  
> (And my Master began to speak to me thus:)

- *Προστάτης δ’ οὑμός* is ‘and my guide’: *προστάτης* suggests one who stands at the head, *buon* disappears, and possession is explicit. *δ’* elides *δέ*; *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, the article and possessive fused (nom. masc. sg.). It is not the relative *ὅς*.
- *ὧδ’ ἤρξατό μοι λέγων* adds manner and addressee: *ὧδ’* is elided *ὧδε* ‘thus’, *μοι* the enclitic dative ‘to me’, accounting for the additional acute on *ἤρξατό*. *ἤρξατο* is 3rd sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἄρχω*, middle ‘began’, with temporal augment. Beside Italian *cominciò a dire*, Greek has a supplementary participle *λέγων* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act.), not an infinitive.

> 86 “Mira colui con quella spada in mano,  
> 87 che vien dinanzi a’ tre sì come sire:  

“Mark him with that sword in hand, who comes before the three as their lord:

> 86 “Ὅρα τοῦτον, ὃς ἐν χειρὶ φέρων ξίφος  
> ("Behold him who, bearing a sword in hand,)
>
> 87 „Ἔρχεται πρὸ τῶν τριῶν ὡσεί τις ἄναξ.  
> (Comes before the three like a king.)

- *Ὅρα τοῦτον* gives *Mira colui* with the 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *ὁράω* and a demonstrative object. *ὃς … φέρων ξίφος ἔρχεται* brings the sword inside the relative clause: ‘who, carrying a sword, comes’. *φέρων* is nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *φέρω*, circumstantial to *ἔρχεται* (3rd sg. pres. middle of *ἔρχομαι*); *ἐν χειρί* is locative, dat. sg. of *χείρ*. There is no Greek demonstrative for *quella*.
- *πρὸ τῶν τριῶν* gives *dinanzi a’ tre* with *πρό* and the genitive of the numeral *τρεῖς*. *ὡσεί τις ἄναξ* ‘as if a king’ uses comparative *ὡσεί*, indefinite enclitic *τις*, and poetic *ἄναξ* in the nominative, referring to the subject rather than expressing possession by the three.

> 88 quegli è Omero poeta sovrano  
> 89 l’altro è Orazio satiro, che viene,  
> 90 Ovidio è il terzo, e l’ ultimo Lucano.  

that is Homer, the sovereign Poet; the next who comes is Horace the satirist; Ovid is the third, and the last is Lucan.

> 88 „Οὗτος Ὅμηρος, ποιητὴς κορυφαῖος·  
> (This is Homer, the sovereign poet;)
>
> 89 „Ὁ δ’ ἄλλος Ὁράτιος, σατυρογράφος·  
> (The next is Horace, the satirist;)
>
> 90 „Ὁ τρίτος Ὀβίδιος, Λουκανὸς δ’ οὗτος.  
> (The third is Ovid, and this one is Lucan.)

- *Οὗτος Ὅμηρος, ποιητὴς κορυφαῖος* has no expressed copula: ‘this is Homer, a poet foremost in rank’. *οὗτος* is proximal beside *quegli*; appositive *ποιητής* and its adjective *κορυφαῖος* are nominative masculine singular.
- *Ὁ δ’ ἄλλος Ὁράτιος, σατυρογράφος* continues with another verbless identification. *ὁ … ἄλλος* is substantival ‘the other’, *δ’* elided *δέ*. Appositive *σατυρογράφος* makes *satiro* an occupational compound, ‘writer of satire’; *che viene* is not repeated.
- *Ὁ τρίτος Ὀβίδιος* fronts the articular ordinal, reversing *Ovidio è il terzo*. *Λουκανὸς δ’ οὗτος* fronts the name instead: ‘and this one is Lucan’. Both clauses again leave ‘is’ understood. The demonstrative points out the remaining member without translating *l’ultimo* by an ordinal or by ‘last’.

## Lines 91–123 — The Poets’ Welcome and the Great Spirits

> 91 Però che ciascun meco si conviene  
> 92 nel nome, che sonò la voce sola,  
> 93 fannomi onore, e di ciò fanno bene.”  

Because each agrees with me in the name, which the one voice sounded, they do me honour : and therein they do well.”

> 91 „Ὡς δὲ τούτων ἕκαστος συγκοινωνεῖ μοι  
> (And since each of these shares with me)
>
> 92 „Κλήσεως, ἣν ἤχησε νῦν φωνὴ μία,  
> (In the title which one voice just sounded,)
>
> 93 „Τιμήν μοι παρέχουσι καλῶς ποιοῦντες.”  
> (They do me honour, and therein do well.")

- *Ὡς δὲ τούτων ἕκαστος* ‘and since each of these’ renders *Però che ciascun*. *Ὡς* introduces the reason, *δέ* connects it to what precedes, and the partitive genitive *τούτων*, placed before *ἕκαστος*, makes explicit the group from which ‘each’ is taken.
- *συγκοινωνεῖ μοι κλήσεως* ‘shares a designation with me’ recasts *meco si conviene nel nome*. The contracted present active indicative *συγκοινωνεῖ* (3rd sg., from *συγκοινωνέω*) agrees with *ἕκαστος*: *μοι* is the dative of the person with whom one shares, *κλήσεως* the genitive of the thing shared (gen. sg. of *κλῆσις*). There is no preposition corresponding to *nel*.
- *ἣν ἤχησε νῦν φωνὴ μία* follows the order of *che sonò la voce sola*, with the subject after the verb. *ἥν* is feminine singular like *κλήσεως*, but accusative as object of *ἤχησε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἠχέω*), used transitively, ‘uttered’. *μία* is the feminine numeral ‘one’; *νῦν* ‘just now’ is supplied.
- *τιμήν μοι παρέχουσι* ‘they accord me honour’ returns to the collective plural, as does *fannomi onore* after *ciascun*. *παρέχουσι* is 3rd pl. present active indicative of *παρέχω*, with accusative of the gift and dative of the recipient. *καλῶς ποιοῦντες* ‘doing well’ (nom. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *ποιέω*) turns *e di ciò fanno bene* into a participial comment on that very act; neither the conjunction nor *di ciò* needs a separate equivalent.

> 94 Così vidi adunar la bella scuola  
> 95 di quei signor dell’ altissimo canto,  
> 96 che sopra gli altri, com’ aquila, vola.  

Thus I saw assembled the goodly school of [those] lord[s] of highest song, [which], like an eagle, soars above the rest.

> 94 Οὕτω συνελθὸν τὸ μέγ’ εἶδον σχολεῖον  
> (Thus I saw assembled the great school)
>
> 95 Τῆς ποιήσεως τοῦ πρώτου διδασκάλου,  
> (Of the poetry of the foremost master,)
>
> 96 Ὃς ὡς ἀετὸς ὑπερίπταται πάντων.  
> (Who like an eagle soars above all others.)

- *Οὕτω συνελθὸν τὸ μέγ’ εἶδον σχολεῖον* puts *συνελθόν* in prominence immediately after ‘thus’. It is the neuter accusative singular second aorist active participle of *συνέρχομαι*, predicated of the object of *εἶδον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*): ‘I saw the school gathered together’, rather than the Italian perception verb with infinitive, *vidi adunar*. *τὸ μέγ’ … σχολεῖον* substitutes ‘the great school’ for *la bella scuola*; *μέγ’* is *μέγα* with final vowel elided before *εἶδον*.
- *τῆς ποιήσεως τοῦ πρώτου διδασκάλου* gives ‘of the foremost master of poetry’, with *τῆς ποιήσεως* set first. Against the supplied *di quei signor dell’ altissimo canto*, the master is singular, and pre-eminence belongs grammatically to him (*πρώτου*), not to the song. The Greek construes the passage as if it referred to one lord, not several.
- *ὃς ὡς ἀετὸς ὑπερίπταται πάντων* makes the antecedent of *che* unambiguous. Masculine singular *ὅς* refers to *διδασκάλου*, not to feminine *ποίησις* or neuter *σχολεῖον*; it is nominative as subject of *ὑπερίπταται* (3rd sg. pres. middle ind. of *ὑπερίπταμαι*, ‘fly above’). *ὡς ἀετός* is the comparison *com’ aquila*; the compound verb with genitive *πάντων* gives ‘above all’, rather than the separate phrase *sopra gli altri*.

> 97 Da ch’ ebber ragionato insieme alquanto,  
> 98 volsersi a me con salutevol cenno:  
> 99 e il mio maestro sorrise di tanto.  

After they had talked a space together, they turned to me with sign of salutation ; and my Master smiled thereat.

> 97 Μικρόν τι πρὸς ἀλλήλους διαλεχθέντες,  
> (After conversing a little with one another,)
>
> 98 Ἐστράφησαν πρὸς ἐμὲ προσνεύσαντές μοι·  
> (They turned to me with a sign of greeting;)
>
> 99 Προστάτης δ’ οὑμὸς τούτοις ἐπεμειδία.  
> (And my Master smiled upon them.)

- *Μικρόν τι πρὸς ἀλλήλους διαλεχθέντες* ‘having conversed a little with one another’ replaces *Da ch’ ebber ragionato* with an antecedent aorist participle. *διαλεχθέντες* is nominative masculine plural, aorist passive in form but active in sense, from *διαλέγομαι*. *μικρόν τι* is an adverbial neuter accusative for *alquanto*; *πρὸς ἀλλήλους* (reciprocal pronoun, acc. masc. pl.) specifies mutual address rather than simply *insieme*.
- *ἐστράφησαν πρὸς ἐμὲ προσνεύσαντές μοι* ‘they turned towards me, nodding to me’: *ἐστράφησαν* is 3rd pl. second aorist passive indicative of *στρέφω*, intransitive here like *volsersi*. *πρός* takes the full accusative pronoun *ἐμέ* for direction; *προσνεύσαντες*, the accompanying aorist active participle of *προσνεύω*, takes dative *μοι* for the recipient. The nod itself conveys *con salutevol cenno*, without an adjective for *salutevol*.
- *Προστάτης δ’ οὑμὸς τούτοις ἐπεμειδία* calls the *maestro* ‘my guide’ or ‘protector’. *δ’* is elided *δέ*; *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, the articular possessive placed after *προστάτης*. *ἐπεμειδία* is 3rd sg. imperfect active indicative of *ἐπιμειδιάω*, augmented after the preverb: ‘was smiling’, against the discrete past *sorrise*. The dative plural *τούτοις* can point to the persons smiled upon, as in the supplied Greek gloss, rather than to the occasion of the smile, *di tanto*.

> 100 E più d’ onore ancora assai mi fenno,  
> 101 ch’ esser mi fecer della loro schiera,  
> 102 sì ch’ io fui sesto tra cotanto senno.  

And greatly more besides they honoured me ; for they made me of their number, so that I was a sixth amid such intelligences.

> 100 Ἀλλ’ ἔτι πλείω τιμὴν ἀπένειμάν μοι,  
> (Yet still greater honour they bestowed on me,)
>
> 101 Εἰς τὴν ἑαυτῶν προσλαβόντες χορείαν,  
> (Taking me into their own company,)
>
> 102 Ὥστ’ ἤμην ἐν τηλίκοις ἀνδράσιν ἕκτος.  
> (So that I was sixth among such great men.)

- *Ἀλλ’ ἔτι πλείω τιμὴν ἀπένειμάν μοι* ‘but they accorded me still greater honour’ makes the connective adversative (*ἀλλ’* for *ἀλλά*) and brings the comparison into the noun phrase. *πλείω* is the contracted accusative feminine singular of the comparative *πλείων*, agreeing with *τιμήν*; *ἔτι* reinforces it. *ἀπένειμαν* (3rd pl. aor. act. ind. of *ἀπονέμω*) takes a direct object and dative *μοι*, as *παρέχουσι* did at line 93.
- *εἰς τὴν ἑαυτῶν προσλαβόντες χορείαν* ‘by admitting [me] into their own company’ specifies how they did honour, in place of the finite *ch’ esser mi fecer*. *προσλαβόντες* is the nominative masculine plural second aorist active participle of *προσλαμβάνω*; its object is understood. *εἰς* with the accusative expresses admission, and the plural reflexive genitive *ἑαυτῶν* makes *loro* explicitly refer back to the subject.
- *Ὥστ’ ἤμην ἐν τηλίκοις ἀνδράσιν ἕκτος* takes *sì ch’ io fui* as an actual result: *ὥστε*, elided, governs an indicative, not an infinitive. *ἤμην* is the postclassical 1st sg. imperfect of *εἰμί* (Attic *ἦ*); the resulting state is ‘I was sixth’, with predicate nominative *ἕκτος* reserved for the end. *ἐν τηλίκοις ἀνδράσιν* ‘among men so great’ makes the abstract singular *tra cotanto senno* personal and plural.

> 103 Così n’ andammo infino alla lumiera  
> 104 parlando cose, che il tacere è bello,  
> 105 sì com’ era il parlar colà dov’ era.  

Thus we went onwards to the light, speaking things which it is well to pass in silence, as it was well to speak there where I was.

> 103 Μέχρι τοῦ φωτὸς ἐπορευόμεθ’ οὕτω,  
> (Thus we walked onward as far as the light,)
>
> 104 Λόγους περὶ πραγμάτων σιωπητέων  
> (Discourse of things that must be passed in silence)
>
> 105 Ποιούμενοι, καθάπερ ἐκεῖ προσῆκεν.  
> (Making, even as it was fitting there.)

- *Μέχρι τοῦ φωτὸς ἐπορευόμεθ’ οὕτω* fronts the limit *infino alla lumiera*: *μέχρι* governs *τοῦ φωτός* (gen. sg. of *φῶς*). *οὕτω*, answering to initial *Così*, comes last. *ἐπορευόμεθ’* is *ἐπορευόμεθα*, 1st pl. imperfect middle indicative of the deponent *πορεύομαι*, with elision and aspiration of *τ* before the rough breathing of *οὕτω*. The imperfect presents *andammo* as motion in progress.
- *λόγους περὶ πραγμάτων σιωπητέων ποιούμενοι* renders *parlando cose* as ‘making discourse about matters’. *ποιούμενοι* (nom. masc. pl. pres. middle participle of *ποιέω*) agrees with the travellers and marks action concurrent with their walking. *λόγους* is its object; *περί* with the genitive introduces the subject of discussion. *σιωπητέων* is a verbal adjective in *-τέος* agreeing with *πραγμάτων*: ‘matters that must be kept silent’, a stronger formulation than *che il tacere è bello*.
- *καθάπερ ἐκεῖ προσῆκεν* ‘just as was fitting there’ leaves the act of speaking to be understood. *προσῆκεν* is the impersonal 3rd sg. imperfect active indicative of *προσήκω*. Greek retains the comparison and *colà*, but neither repeats an expression for speaking nor expands *ἐκεῖ* into *colà dov’ era*.

> 106 Venimmo al piè d’ un nobile castello,  
> 107 sette volte cerchiato d’ alte mura,  
> 108 difeso intorno d’ un bel fiumicello.  

We came to the foot of a Noble Castle, seven times circled with lofty Walls, defended round by a fair Rivulet.

> 106 Ἀφικόμεθ’ εἰς πόδας εὐγενοῦς πύργου,  
> (We arrived at the foot of a noble castle,)
>
> 107 Τείχεσ’ ὑψηλοῖς ἑπτάκις κυκλουμένου,  
> (Seven times encircled by lofty walls,)
>
> 108 Ῥύακί τε διαυγεῖ περιειργμένου·  
> (And surrounded by a pellucid stream;)

- *Ἀφικόμεθ’ εἰς πόδας εὐγενοῦς πύργου* follows *Venimmo al piè d’ un nobile castello* closely, though it has plural ‘feet’ and *πύργος* ‘tower’ for *castello*. *ἀφικόμεθ’* is elided *ἀφικόμεθα*, 1st pl. second aorist middle indicative of *ἀφικνέομαι*; the *θ* belongs to the ending *-μεθα*. *εἰς* takes accusative *πόδας* as the goal; *εὐγενοῦς* is genitive masculine singular of *εὐγενής*, agreeing with *πύργου*.
- *τείχεσ’ ὑψηλοῖς ἑπτάκις κυκλουμένου* ‘encircled seven times with high walls’ makes *sette volte* the single multiplicative adverb *ἑπτάκις*. *τείχεσ’* is elided *τείχεσι*, dative plural of neuter *τεῖχος*; with *ὑψηλοῖς* it denotes the means of enclosure. *κυκλουμένου* (pres. middle-passive participle of *κυκλόω*, passive in sense) is genitive masculine singular, continuing the description of *πύργου* across the line break.
- *ῥύακί τε διαυγεῖ περιειργμένου* adds a parallel dative and participle, linked by postpositive *τε*. *ῥύακι* (dat. sg. of *ῥύαξ*) is a stream without the explicit diminutive of *fiumicello*; *διαυγεῖ* (from *διαυγής*) specifies clarity for *bel*. *περιειργμένου* is the genitive masculine singular perfect middle-passive participle of *περιείργω*: ‘hemmed in all round’. The perfect presents the resulting enclosure; *difeso* is rendered in terms of the barrier, not by a verb meaning ‘defend’.

> 109 Questo passammo come terra dura;  

This we passed as solid land

> 109 Τοῦτον δὲ διέβημεν ὡσεὶ γῆν χέρσον.  
> (This we crossed over as though it were dry land.)

- *Τοῦτον δὲ διέβημεν* puts ‘this’ first as in *Questo passammo*. Masculine accusative *τοῦτον* resumes *ῥύαξ* as the direct object of *διέβημεν* (1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *διαβαίνω*, with internal augment). *δέ* supplies a narrative connective.
- *ὡσεὶ γῆν χέρσον* ‘as though [crossing] dry land’ retains the comparison *come terra dura*. The accusatives correspond to the object of crossing, not to the subject ‘we’; *χέρσον* is the feminine accusative singular of the two-termination adjective *χέρσος*. The contrast in Greek is with water, ‘dry’ rather than ‘hard’.

> 110 per sette porte intrai con questi savi;  

; through seven gates I entered with those sages

> 110 Δι’ ἑπτὰ θυρῶν σὺν τοῖς σοφοῖς εἰσέβην·  
> (Through seven gates I entered with the sages;)

- *Δι’ ἑπτὰ θυρῶν* has elided *διά* with the genitive for passage through; *ἑπτά* is indeclinable. *σὺν τοῖς σοφοῖς* precedes the verb, unlike *con questi savi*: *σύν* governs the dative, and the article with substantivized *σοφοῖς* identifies ‘the sages’ without a demonstrative.
- *εἰσέβην* ‘I went in’ is 1st sg. second aorist active indicative of *εἰσβαίνω*. It reproduces the switch to singular *intrai* and varies *διέβημεν* by changing the preverb from ‘across’ to ‘in’.

> 111 giugnemmo in prato di fresca verdura.  

; we reached a meadow of fresh verdure.

> 111 Ἤλθομεν δ’ εἰς λειμῶνα νεαρᾶς χλόης.  
> (And we came to a meadow of fresh greenery.)

- *Ἤλθομεν δ’ εἰς λειμῶνα* ‘and we came into a meadow’ returns to the plural with *ἤλθομεν*, 1st pl. second aorist active indicative of *ἔρχομαι*, from the stem also used in *συνελθόν* at line 94. Elided *δέ* supplies the link, and *εἰς* plus accusative gives *in prato* explicitly directional force.
- *νεαρᾶς χλόης* retains the descriptive genitive of *di fresca verdura*: ‘of young grass’, with *νεαρᾶς* agreeing with the feminine noun *χλόης*.

> 112 Genti v’ eran con occhi tardi e gravi,  
> 113 di grande autorità ne’ lor sembianti;  
> 114 parlavan rado, con voci soavi.  

On it were people with eyes slow and grave, of great authority in their appearance ; they spoke seldom, with mild voices.

> 112 Ψυχαὶ σοβαραὶ τὰς ὄψεις ἐμβριθεῖς τε  
> (Souls stately and grave in their looks)
>
> 113 Παρῆσαν ἐκεῖ, σεμνοπρεπεῖς τὴν θέαν,  
> (Were present there, majestic in appearance,)
>
> 114 Ὁμιλοῦσαι σπανίως φωνῇ γλυκείᾳ.  
> (Speaking seldom, with a gentle voice.)

- *Ψυχαὶ σοβαραὶ τὰς ὄψεις ἐμβριθεῖς τε* begins with ‘souls’, not *Genti*. *σοβαραί* and *ἐμβριθεῖς* (nom. fem. pl., the latter from *ἐμβριθής*) characterize the souls themselves as stately and weighty; postpositive *τε* couples the adjectives. *τὰς ὄψεις* is accusative of respect, ‘in their looks’. Thus *con occhi tardi e gravi* ceases to be a phrase about the eyes in particular, and there is no literal adjective ‘slow’.
- *παρῆσαν ἐκεῖ* ‘were present there’ delays *v’ eran* until the second line; *παρῆσαν* is 3rd pl. imperfect indicative of *πάρειμι*. *σεμνοπρεπεῖς τὴν θέαν* ‘majestic in appearance’ uses another nominative plural adjective and accusative of respect for *di grande autorità ne’ lor sembianti*. Neither the abstract noun ‘authority’ nor the possessive is retained.
- *ὁμιλοῦσαι σπανίως φωνῇ γλυκείᾳ* subordinates the finite imperfect *parlavan* to *παρῆσαν*: ‘speaking seldom’. *ὁμιλοῦσαι* is the nominative feminine plural present active participle of *ὁμιλέω*, agreeing with *ψυχαί*. *φωνῇ γλυκείᾳ* is a dative of manner, ‘in a sweet voice’, singular for *con voci soavi*.

> 115 Traemmoci così dall’ un de’ canti  
> 116 in luogo aperto, luminoso, ed alto,  
> 117 sì che veder si potean tutti quanti.  

Thus we retired on one of the sides, into a place open, luminous, and high, so that they could all be seen.

> 115 Μετέβημεν οὖν ἑστῶτες κατ᾿ ἰδίαν  
> (We therefore withdrew, standing apart by ourselves,)
>
> 116 Εἰς τόπον αἰπύν, ἀσκεπῆ καὶ φεγγώδη,  
> (To a lofty place, open and full of light,)
>
> 117 Ὅθεν ἐθεωροῦμεν πάντας καὶ πάντα.  
> (Whence we could behold all persons and all things.)

- *Μετέβημεν οὖν ἑστῶτες κατ᾿ ἰδίαν* replaces reflexive *Traemmoci* with *μετέβημεν* ‘we moved over’ (1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *μεταβαίνω*). Postpositive *οὖν* gives connective force to *così*. *ἑστῶτες* is the contracted nominative masculine plural perfect active participle of *ἵστημι*, intransitive and stative, ‘standing’. With the idiom *κατ᾿ ἰδίαν* ‘apart, by ourselves’ (*κατά* elided), it describes the separate position taken up, rather than translating the spatial detail *dall’ un de’ canti*.
- *εἰς τόπον αἰπύν, ἀσκεπῆ καὶ φεγγώδη* gives the destination with three masculine accusative singular adjectives. *αἰπύν* ‘high, steep’ (from *αἰπύς*) moves *alto* from last to first; *ἀσκεπῆ* ‘uncovered’ and *φεγγώδη* ‘full of light’ (from *ἀσκεπής*, *φεγγώδης*) render *aperto, luminoso*.
- *ὅθεν ἐθεωροῦμεν πάντας καὶ πάντα* substitutes a relative adverb of place, ‘from where’, for consecutive *sì che*. The contracted *ἐθεωροῦμεν* (1st pl. imperf. act. ind. of *θεωρέω*) makes the observers the subject: ‘we were viewing’, with no separate expression of the possibility in *si potean*. Masculine accusative *πάντας* and neuter accusative *πάντα* distinguish ‘everyone and everything’, extending personal *tutti quanti*.

> 118 Colà diritto, sopra il verde smalto,  
> 119 mi fur mostrati gli spiriti magni,  
> 120 che del vederli in me stesso n’ esalto.  

There direct, upon the green enamel, were shewn to me the great spirits, [so that] I glory within myself [for] having seen [them].

> 118 Ἐπὶ τῆς ποικιλανθοῦς ἐκεῖ δὴ χλόης  
> (There indeed upon the flower-strewn turf)
>
> 119 Τὰ μάλ᾿ ἔξοχα πνεύματ᾿ ἐφάνησάν μοι,  
> (The most eminent spirits appeared to me,)
>
> 120 Ἅπερ ἰδὼν ἔνδοξον ἐμαυτὸν κρίνω.  
> (At sight of whom I deem myself honoured.)

- *Ἐπὶ τῆς ποικιλανθοῦς ἐκεῖ δὴ χλόης* gives the surface with *ἐπί* and the genitive. *ποικιλανθοῦς* (gen. fem. sg. of *ποικιλανθής*) describes grass ‘of many-coloured flowers’, replacing the material and colour metaphor *verde smalto*. Between adjective and noun comes *ἐκεῖ δή* ‘there indeed’: *δή* points emphatically to the spot, rather than giving *diritto* a distinct directional equivalent.
- *τὰ μάλ᾿ ἔξοχα πνεύματ᾿ ἐφάνησάν μοι* ‘the very eminent spirits appeared to me’ fronts the subject that follows *mi fur mostrati*. *μάλ᾿* and *πνεύματ᾿* are elisions of *μάλα* and *πνεύματα*; *ἔξοχα* agrees with the latter in the neuter plural. *ἐφάνησαν* is 3rd pl. second aorist passive indicative of *φαίνω*, in its intransitive sense ‘appeared’, not ‘were shown’. The personal referents take plural agreement despite the neuter subject; *μοι* retains the beholder as dative.
- *ἅπερ ἰδὼν ἔνδοξον ἐμαυτὸν κρίνω* ‘having seen which, I judge myself honoured’ uses an emphatic relative (*ἅπερ*, neut. acc. pl. of *ὅσπερ*) for the spirits as object of *ἰδών*. This nominative masculine singular second aorist active participle of *ὁράω* replaces *del vederli* and supplies the ground of the judgment. Where the English takes *che* consecutively, Greek attaches the comment by the relative itself. *κρίνω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind.) takes reflexive object *ἐμαυτόν* and predicate accusative *ἔνδοξον*: *in me stesso n’ esalto* becomes a judgment about oneself, not an act of glorying within oneself.

> 121 Io vidi Elettra con molti compagni,  
> 122 tra’ quai conobbi Ettore, ed Enea,  
> 123 Cesare armato con gli occhi grifagni.  

I saw Electra with many companions : amongst whom I knew both Hector and Æneas ; Cæsar armed, with the falcon eyes.

> 121 Εἶδον Ἠλέκτραν σὺν πολλοῖς ἀκολούθοις,  
> (I saw Electra with many companions,)
>
> 122 Ἐν οἷς ἔγνων Ἕκτορα καὶ τὸν Αἰνείαν  
> (Among whom I knew Hector and Æneas,)
>
> 123 Καὶ τὸν Καίσαρ᾿ ἔνοπλον, δριμὺν τὸ βλέμμα.  
> (And Cæsar in arms, with keen gaze.)

- *Εἶδον Ἠλέκτραν σὺν πολλοῖς ἀκολούθοις* follows *Io vidi Elettra con molti compagni* without the explicit subject pronoun. *εἶδον* supplies the person, *Ἠλέκτραν* is its accusative object, and *σύν* governs the dative plural. *ἀκόλουθοι* suggests followers or attendants more specifically than *compagni*.
- *ἐν οἷς ἔγνων Ἕκτορα καὶ τὸν Αἰνείαν* keeps the relative construction *tra’ quai*: *οἷς* is dative masculine plural after *ἐν*, referring to *ἀκολούθοις*. *ἔγνων* ‘I recognized’ is 1st sg. second aorist active indicative of *γιγνώσκω*. The names take the accusative, third-declension *Ἕκτορα* without an article, first-declension *Αἰνείαν* with one.
- *καὶ τὸν Καίσαρ᾿ ἔνοπλον* adds a conjunction before the last object of *ἔγνων*, where the Italian simply juxtaposes *Cesare armato*. *Καίσαρ᾿* is elided accusative *Καίσαρα*; *ἔνοπλον* is an adjective, ‘in arms’, agreeing with it. *δριμὺν τὸ βλέμμα* likewise has masculine accusative *δριμύν* (from *δριμύς*) agreeing with Caesar, not with neuter *βλέμμα*: ‘keen as to his gaze’. This accusative of respect turns *con gli occhi grifagni* into a quality of the man, exchanging plural eyes for a singular look and leaving the falcon comparison unexpressed.

## Lines 124–151 — The Philosophers and the Return to Darkness

> 124 Vidi Cammilla e la Pentesilea  
> 125 dall’ altra parte, e vidi il re Latino,  
> 126 che con Lavinia sua figlia sedea.  

I saw Camilla and Penthesilea on the other hand, [and] saw the Latian king, sitting with Lavinia his daughter.

> 124 Εἶδον Πενθεσίλειαν καὶ τὴν Καμίλλαν  
> (I saw Penthesilea and Camilla)
>
> 125 Ἑτέρωθεν, βασιλέ᾿ εἶδον Λατῖνον,  
> (On the other side; I saw King Latinus,)
>
> 126 Συγκαθήμενον θυγατρὶ Λαυϊνίᾳ.  
> (Sitting beside his daughter Lavinia.)

- *Εἶδον* renders *vidi*: it is the first-person singular second-aorist active indicative belonging to *ὁράω*, formed on the stem *ἰδ-* with the indicative augment. *Πενθεσίλειαν καὶ τὴν Καμίλλαν* reverses *Cammilla e la Pentesilea*. Both names are accusative objects; the article still goes with the second name, but now that name is Camilla.
- *Ἑτέρωθεν* takes up *dall’ altra parte* in the same delayed position. The adverb in *-θεν* can denote direction ‘from the other side’, but here locates the figures ‘on the other side’. There is no conjunction for *e* before the second *εἶδον*. Instead Musurus puts the verb between *βασιλέ᾿* and *Λατῖνον*, ‘king’ and ‘Latinus’, in apposition. *Βασιλέ᾿* is the accusative singular *βασιλέα* of *βασιλεύς*, with final *-α* elided before the vowel.
- *Συγκαθήμενον* replaces the finite relative *che … sedea* with a present middle participle of *συγκάθημαι*, accusative masculine singular agreeing with *Λατῖνον*: ‘I saw him sitting together with …’. It presents the sitting as in progress at the time of the seeing. The companion is in the dative, *θυγατρὶ Λαυϊνίᾳ*, ‘with daughter Lavinia’; the kinship term comes first, and *sua* is left implicit. The diaeresis in the name marks *ϊ* as a separate vowel.

> 127 Vidi quel Bruto che cacciò Tarquino,  
> 128 Lucrezia, Julia, Marzia e Corniglia,  
> 129 e solo in parte vidi il Saladino.  

I saw that Brutus who expelled the Tarquin; Lucretia, Julia, Martia, and Cornelia; and by himself apart, I saw the Saladin.

> 127 Εἶδον τὸν Βροῦτον, διώκτην Ταρκυνίου,  
> (I saw Brutus, who drove out Tarquin,)
>
> 128 Λουκρητίαν, Μαρκίαν καὶ Κορνηλίαν,  
> (Lucretia, Martia, and Cornelia,)
>
> 129 Καὶ Σαλαδῖνον ἑστῶτα κατὰ μόνας.  
> (And Saladin standing apart by himself.)

- *Εἶδον τὸν Βροῦτον* retains *vidi* but uses the article rather than a demonstrative for *quel*. *Διώκτην Ταρκυνίου* replaces *che cacciò Tarquino* with ‘expeller of Tarquin’: *διώκτην*, accusative singular of the agent noun *διώκτης*, stands in apposition to *Βροῦτον*, and the person expelled becomes an objective genitive. The noun identifies Brutus without itself marking the past tense of *cacciò*.
- *Λουκρητίαν, Μαρκίαν καὶ Κορνηλίαν* continues the accusative list but omits *Julia*. *Καὶ Σαλαδῖνον* is governed by the same opening *εἶδον*; Musurus does not repeat *vidi* in line 129.
- *Ἑστῶτα*, accusative masculine singular of the intransitive perfect active participle of *ἵστημι*, adds a posture to *solo in parte*: ‘standing’, the state resulting from having taken a stand. *Κατὰ μόνας* is an idiomatic adverbial expression with *κατά* and a feminine accusative plural, ‘apart, alone’; *μόνας* does not agree with *Σαλαδῖνον*.

> 130 Poi che innalzai un poco più le ciglia,  
> 131 vidi il maestro di color che sanno,  
> 132 seder tra filosofica famiglia.  

When I raised my eyelids a little higher, I saw the Master of those that know, sitting amid a philosophic family.

> 130 Εἶτα τὰς ὄψεις ἀνυψώσας μικρόν τι  
> (Then raising my eyes a little higher,)
>
> 131 Εἶδον τὸν διδάσκαλον τῆς ἐπιστήμης  
> (I saw the master of knowledge)
>
> 132 Ἑδρεύοντ᾽ ἐν τῇ σοφῶν οἰκογενείᾳ.  
> (Seated amid the family of the wise.)

- *Εἶτα … ἀνυψώσας* recasts *Poi che innalzai*: the sequence is marked by the adverb ‘then’, not by a temporal conjunction. *Ἀνυψώσας* is the nominative masculine singular aorist active participle of *ἀνυψόω*, ‘having raised’, with the same subject as *εἶδον*. The aorist participle has no augment. *Τὰς ὄψεις*, accusative plural of *ὄψις*, substitutes ‘eyes’ or ‘looks’ for *le ciglia*. *Μικρόν τι* is an adverbial accusative of extent, ‘a little’; the upward direction is in the verb, with no separate comparative for *più*.
- *Τὸν διδάσκαλον τῆς ἐπιστήμης* changes the relation in *il maestro di color che sanno*: *διδάσκαλος* is ‘teacher’, and its dependent genitive names the subject taught, ‘of knowledge’, not the people who know.
- *Ἑδρεύοντ᾽* is *ἑδρεύοντα*, present active participle of *ἑδρεύω*, with *-α* elided before *ἐν*. Where *vidi … seder* has an infinitive, Greek uses the participle agreeing with the accusative person seen. *Ἐν τῇ σοφῶν οἰκογενείᾳ* is ‘in the family of wise men’: *ἐν* governs the dative singular *οἰκογενείᾳ*, and *σοφῶν*, a substantival genitive plural inserted between article and noun, replaces the adjective *filosofica*.

> 133 Tutti lo miran, tutti onor gli fanno;  

All regard him; all do him honour;

> 133 Πάντες θαυμάζουσ᾿ αὐτόν, πάντες τιμῶσιν.  
> (All admire him, all do him honour.)

- *Πάντες … πάντες* preserves the repeated *tutti* and the change to the present tense. *Θαυμάζουσ᾿*, for *θαυμάζουσι* with final *-ι* elided before *αὐτόν*, is third-person plural present active indicative of *θαυμάζω*: it interprets *lo miran* as ‘admire him’, not simply ‘look at him’.
- *Τιμῶσιν*, the corresponding contracted form of *τιμάω* with movable *-ν*, compresses *onor gli fanno* into ‘honour’. Both Greek verbs take a direct object: *αὐτόν* is expressed after the first and understood after the second, whereas Italian changes from accusative *lo* to dative *gli*.

> 134 quivi vid’ io Socrate e Platone,  
> 135 che innanzi agli altri più presso gli stanno;  

here I saw Socrates and Plato, who before the rest stand nearest to him;

> 134 Ἐκεῖ δὴ τὸν Σωκράτη καὶ Πλάτων᾿ εἶδον  
> (There indeed I saw Socrates and Plato)
>
> 135 Προϊστάμενον τῶν ἑστηκότων ἄλλων,  
> (Standing foremost before the others there,)

- *Ἐκεῖ δή* renders *quivi* with an added particle of emphasis, ‘there indeed’. The objects precede the verb: *τὸν Σωκράτη καὶ Πλάτων᾿ εἶδον*. *Σωκράτη* is the contracted accusative of *Σωκράτης*; *Πλάτων᾿* is *Πλάτωνα* with elision. The person of *εἶδον* makes a separate pronoun for *io* unnecessary.
- *Προϊστάμενον* is the accusative **singular** masculine present middle participle of *προΐστημι*, ‘standing in front’. It can agree in form with the nearer *Πλάτωνα*, but does not formally agree with the pair as Dante’s plural *stanno* does. *Τῶν ἑστηκότων ἄλλων*, ‘the others who stand there’, is genitive after the expression of precedence; *ἑστηκότων* is a genitive plural perfect active participle of *ἵστημι* with stative sense. The Greek retains *innanzi agli altri* but has no distinct equivalent for *più presso gli*, the comparison of proximity to him.

> 136 Democrito, che il mondo a caso pone,  

Democritus, who ascribes the world to chance;

> 136 Δημόκριτον, τὸν κόσμον τῇ Τύχῃ δόντα,  
> (Democritus, who ascribed the world to Chance,)

- *Δημόκριτον* continues the accusatives governed by *εἶδον*. *Τὸν κόσμον τῇ Τύχῃ δόντα* recasts *che il mondo a caso pone*: *δόντα*, accusative masculine singular second-aorist active participle of *δίδωμι*, agrees with *Δημόκριτον*. Its direct object is *τὸν κόσμον* and its dative recipient is *τῇ Τύχῃ*, ‘to Chance’. The present assertion *pone* becomes a completed attribution, ‘having assigned the world to Chance’; the capitalized noun makes Chance read as the recipient rather than *a caso* as an adverb.

> 137 Diogenes, Anassagora e Tale,  
> 138 Empedocles, Eraclito e Zenone;  

Diogenes, Anaxagoras, and Thales; Empedocles, Heraclitus, and Zeno;

> 137 Θάλητ᾽, Ἀναξαγόραν καὶ Διογένη,  
> (Thales, Anaxagoras, and Diogenes,)
>
> 138 Ἐμπεδοκλῆ, Ζήνωνα σὺν Ἡρακλείτῳ.  
> (Empedocles, Zeno with Heraclitus.)

- *Θάλητ᾽, Ἀναξαγόραν καὶ Διογένη* reverses the first and last names of line 137, retaining the middle name and the final conjunction. *Θάλητ᾽* represents elided *Θάλητα*, the accusative from the dental-stem inflection of *Θαλῆς*; *Διογένη* is the contracted accusative of *Διογένης*.
- *Ἐμπεδοκλῆ, Ζήνωνα σὺν Ἡρακλείτῳ* keeps Empedocles first but exchanges the positions of Heraclitus and Zeno. *Ἐμπεδοκλῆ* is the contracted accusative of *Ἐμπεδοκλῆς*; *Ζήνωνα* is the accusative of *Ζήνων*. Instead of making all three direct objects, Musurus attaches the last name by *σύν* with the dative, ‘Zeno with Heraclitus’.

> 139 e vidi il buono accoglitor del quale,  
> 140 Dioscoride dico: e vidi Orfeo,  

and I saw the good collector of the qualities, Dioscorides I mean; and saw Orpheus,

> 139 Τὸν ποιότητος βαθὺν ἐταστὴν εἶδον,  
> (I saw the profound investigator of quality,)
>
> 140 Διοσκορίδην, φημί, μεθ᾽ ὃν Ὀρφέα,  
> (Dioscorides, I mean, after whom Orpheus,)

- *Τὸν ποιότητος βαθὺν ἐταστὴν εἶδον* puts the object before *εἶδον* and drops the initial *e*. *Ποιότητος*, genitive singular of *ποιότης*, makes *del quale* explicitly ‘of quality’, the object of investigation. *Βαθὺν ἐταστήν* is ‘a profound investigator’: the accusative adjective from *βαθύς* replaces *buono*, and the agent noun from *ἐτάζω*, ‘examine’, interprets *accoglitor* as someone who investigates rather than collects.
- *Διοσκορίδην, φημί* preserves the delayed identification *Dioscoride dico*. The name is accusative in apposition to *ἐταστήν*; *φημί* is parenthetic first-person singular present active indicative, ‘I mean’. *Μεθ᾽ ὃν Ὀρφέα* then substitutes ‘after whom, Orpheus’ for *e vidi Orfeo*, leaving *εἶδον* understood. *Μεθ᾽* is *μετά* elided before the rough breathing of the relative *ὅν*, with aspiration of *τ* to *θ*. The relative is accusative after the preposition; *Ὀρφέα* is accusative as the next person seen.

> 141 Tullio, e Lino, e Seneca morale;  
> 142 Euclide geometra, e Tolommeo,  

Tully, [Linus], and Seneca the moralist; Euclid the geometer, and Ptolemæus;

> 141 Τούλλιον, Λῖνον, ἠθικόν τε Σενέκαν,  
> (Tully, Linus, and moral Seneca,)
>
> 142 Εὐκλείδην γεωμέτρην καὶ Πτολεμαῖον,  
> (Euclid the geometer, and Ptolemæus,)

- *Τούλλιον, Λῖνον, ἠθικόν τε Σενέκαν* retains all three names as accusatives. The enclitic connective *τε*, placed after *ἠθικόν*, joins the whole last phrase to the list; Dante’s *Seneca morale* becomes adjective before name, ‘ethical Seneca’, not a separate noun meaning ‘moralist’. *Σενέκαν* is a masculine accusative in *-αν*.
- *Εὐκλείδην γεωμέτρην καὶ Πτολεμαῖον* closely keeps the order and coordination of *Euclide geometra, e Tolommeo*. *Γεωμέτρην*, accusative singular of the masculine agent noun *γεωμέτρης*, is in apposition to *Εὐκλείδην*, without an article.

> 143 Ippocrate, Avicenna e Galieno,  
> 144 Averrois che il gran comento feo.  

Hippocrates, Avicenna, and Galen; Averrhoës, who made the great comment.

> 143 Ἱπποκράτη, Γαληνόν, σὺν δ᾽ Ἀβισίνᾳ  
> (Hippocrates, Galen, and with Avicenna)
>
> 144 Τὸν Ἀβεῤῥόην ὑπομνηματογράφον.  
> (Averrhoës the commentator.)

- *Ἱπποκράτη, Γαληνόν* places Galen immediately after Hippocrates; both remain accusative objects. *Σὺν δ᾽ Ἀβισίνᾳ τὸν Ἀβεῤῥόην* then gives ‘and with Avicenna, Averrhoës’: *Ἀβισίνᾳ* is dative after *σύν*, while *τὸν Ἀβεῤῥόην* returns to the accusative governed by *εἶδον*. The postpositive connective *δέ*, elided before the name, stands between preposition and complement. The spelling *ῤῥ* marks the doubled medial rho with smooth and rough breathings.
- *Ὑπομνηματογράφον*, accusative masculine singular in apposition, means ‘writer of commentaries’. The compound replaces the entire relative *che il gran comento feo*: it expresses the work of writing as a standing designation, without the past tense of *feo*, the particular singular work, or *gran*.

> 145 Io non posso ritrar di tutti appieno:  

I may not paint them all in full:

> 145 Ἁπάντων δ᾽ οὐ δύναμαι ποιεῖσθαι μνείαν·  
> (But of all of them I cannot make mention;)

- *Ἁπάντων δ᾽ οὐ δύναμαι ποιεῖσθαι μνείαν* gives ‘but I cannot make mention of all’. *Δ᾽* is connective *δέ* elided before *οὐ*. The fronted masculine genitive plural *ἁπάντων* depends on *μνείαν*, ‘mention of’; it does not depend on *δύναμαι*.
- *Δύναμαι*, first-person singular present middle indicative, takes the complementary present middle infinitive *ποιεῖσθαι* of *ποιέω*. *Μνείαν ποιεῖσθαι* is an idiom for mentioning, with *μνείαν* its accusative object. The change from *ritrar di tutti appieno* matters: the Greek disclaims even mentioning everyone, rather than portraying everyone fully. There is no separate word for *appieno*.

> 146 però che sì mi caccia il lungo tema,  
> 147 che molte volte al fatto il dir vien meno.  

for the long theme so chases me, that many times the word comes short of the reality.

> 146 Τοῦ θέματος γὰρ τὸ μῆκος κατεπείγει,  
> (For the length of the theme presses me on,)
>
> 147 Τῶν δ᾽ ἔργων λόγοι πολλάκις ἐλαττοῦνται·  
> (And words often fall short of the reality.)

- *Τοῦ θέματος γὰρ τὸ μῆκος κατεπείγει* gives ‘for the length of the theme presses [me] on’. Postpositive *γάρ* renders causal *però che*. The adjective of *il lungo tema* becomes the subject noun *τὸ μῆκος*, with the fronted dependent genitive *τοῦ θέματος* (from *θέμα*). *Κατεπείγει* is third-person singular present active indicative of *κατεπείγω*; the object expressed by Italian *mi* is understood.
- The result construction *sì … che* disappears. *Τῶν δ᾽ ἔργων λόγοι πολλάκις ἐλαττοῦνται* is a new coordinated assertion, ‘and words often fall short of deeds’. *Λόγοι*, nominative plural, replaces the substantivized infinitive *il dir*; *τῶν ἔργων*, fronted genitive of comparison, replaces *al fatto*. *Ἐλαττοῦνται*, third-person plural contracted present middle/passive indicative of *ἐλαττόω*, expresses being at a disadvantage in the comparison. *Πολλάκις* condenses *molte volte* into one adverb.

> 148 La sesta compagnia in due si scema;  

The company of six diminishes to two;

> 148 Ἡ δ᾽ ἑξὰς τῶν ποιητῶν τρέπετ᾽ εἰς δύο·  
> (And the company of six poets turns into two;)

- *Ἡ δ᾽ ἑξὰς τῶν ποιητῶν* interprets *la sesta compagnia* through a numeral noun rather than an ordinal adjective: *ἑξάς*, feminine nominative singular, is ‘a group of six’. The genitive plural *τῶν ποιητῶν* specifies its members, ‘of poets’, which the Italian line does not say explicitly. *Δ᾽* is connective *δέ* elided before the following vowel.
- *Τρέπετ᾽ εἰς δύο* is *τρέπεται* with elision of the final diphthong, third-person singular present middle/passive indicative of *τρέπω*, agreeing with the collective singular *ἑξάς*. ‘Turns into two’ replaces *in due si scema*: *εἰς* marks the resulting number, while the verb expresses change rather than diminution. *Δύο* is the numeral after *εἰς*, not a dual inflection of the verb.

> 149 per altra via mi mena il savio duca,  
> 150 fuor della queta, nell’ aura che trema;  

by another road the sage guide leads me, out of the quiet, into the trembling air;

> 149 Σοφὸς δ᾽ ἡγήτωρ δι᾽ ἄλλης ὁδοῦ μ᾽ ἄγει  
> (And my wise guide leads me by another road)
>
> 150 Ἐξ ἠρεμοῦντος εἰς ἀέρα δονοῦντα·  
> (Out of the tranquil into the trembling air;)

- *Σοφὸς δ᾽ ἡγήτωρ* brings the subject *il savio duca* forward, without the Italian definite article. *Δι᾽ ἄλλης ὁδοῦ* renders *per altra via* by *διά* with the genitive of the route traversed. *Μ᾽ ἄγει* keeps pronoun before verb as in *mi mena*: *μ᾽* is elided accusative *με*, and *ἄγει* is third-person singular present active indicative of *ἄγω*.
- *Ἐξ ἠρεμοῦντος εἰς ἀέρα* sets source against destination, *ἐξ* with genitive against *εἰς* with accusative. *Ἠρεμοῦντος*, genitive singular present active participle of *ἠρεμέω*, ‘being still’, leaves its noun unexpressed, as does *della queta*; the following *ἀέρα* suggests an understood *ἀέρος* in the first phrase.
- *Δονοῦντα*, accusative masculine singular present **active** participle of *δονέω*, agrees with *ἀέρα* and replaces the finite relative *che trema*. The intended contrast is still air versus trembling air, but the voice is notable: classical active *δονέω* ordinarily means ‘shake, agitate [something]’; for the air itself being shaken one would expect a middle/passive form.

> 151 e vengo in parte, ove non è che luca.  

and I come to a part where there is nought that shines.

> 151 Ἔρχομαι δ᾽ ἔνθα φέγγος ἰδεῖν οὐκ ἔστιν.  
> (And I come where there is no light to be seen.)

- *Ἔρχομαι δ᾽ ἔνθα* gives ‘and I come where …’. *Ἔρχομαι* is first-person singular present middle indicative with active sense; postpositive *δέ* supplies the connection made by initial *e*. The relative adverb *ἔνθα* introduces the destination directly, omitting the explicit antecedent *in parte* of *ove*.
- *Φέγγος ἰδεῖν οὐκ ἔστιν* changes *non è che luca*, an existential negative with the subjunctive *luca*, into an impersonal expression of possibility: ‘it is not possible to see light’. *Φέγγος* is the neuter accusative singular object of *ἰδεῖν*, the unaugmented second-aorist active infinitive corresponding to *εἶδον*. It is not the subject of *ἔστιν*: the Greek denies the possibility of seeing light rather than directly denying that anything shines.

## Summary — Italian Emphases in Greek Syntax

Musurus often preserves Dante’s order of emphasis even when he changes the construction. Like *Ruppemi*, *Ἀπέσεισ᾽* puts the verb first and delays the thunder that is its subject; but ‘shook off’ in place of ‘broke’ makes the head the place from which sleep is shaken, not the place where it is broken. The repetition of *tutti* survives as *πάντες … πάντες*, while the contrast of *io* and *tu* gains the balancing particles *μέν … δέ*. Elsewhere postpositive *δέ* links successive clauses or marks a change of speaker, and *γάρ* carries the force of *perocchè*. Musurus can be equally close in what he leaves unsaid: *ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ*, like *Ed egli a me*, needs no verb of speaking.

The participle is Musurus’s most characteristic means of recasting a clause. After a verb of perception, it often replaces an Italian infinitive: *ἑδρεύοντα*, ‘sitting’, agrees with the person seen where Dante has *vidi … seder*. Elsewhere a change of participial tense distinguishes action in progress from an event already complete: *λέγοντος*, ‘while he was speaking’, and *σιγησάσης*, ‘once it had fallen silent’, both stand in temporal genitive absolutes. Italian finite complements likewise yield to infinitives: *vo’ che sappi* becomes *θέλω σ᾽ εἰδέναι*, ‘I wish you to know’. Yet the preference is not mechanical: for the actual result in *tanto che*, Musurus retains a finite assertion after *ὥστε*.

Some of the most consequential departures are small shifts in diction or agreement. The renown that *suona*, ‘resounds’, becomes *κλέος … διαλάμψαν*, ‘renown that shone forth’; Caesar’s *occhi grifagni* become *δριμὺν τὸ βλέμμα*, ‘keen as to his gaze’, where an accusative of respect preserves the sharpness but not the falcon image. Two passages suggest readings different from the supplied Italian. At line 68 *εἰς κορυφήν*, ‘to a summit’, points to *sommo* rather than *sonno*, ‘sleep’. At lines 95–96 the singular *τοῦ πρώτου διδασκάλου*, ‘of the foremost master’, followed by masculine *ὅς*, ‘who’, assigns the eagle comparison to one master. Here Greek agreement does more than accommodate the Italian to another syntax: it commits the translation to a particular reading.
