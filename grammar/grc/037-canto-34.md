# Inferno 34

## Lines 1–18 — A Windmill Seen Through Mist

> 1 “ *Vexilla regis prodeunt inferni*  
> 2 verso di noi : però dinanzi mira,”  
> 3 disse il maestro mio, “ se tu il discerni.”  

“*Vexilla Regis prodeunt inferni* towards us: therefore look in front of thee,” my Master said, “if thou discernest him.”

> 1 “Ἄνακτος ᾍδου σήματ᾿ ἔξεισι πρόσω  
> ("The banners of the King of Hell go forth ahead)
>
> 2 „ Ἡμῶν ἔναντι· βλέπε νῦν ἔμπροσθέν σου,”  
> (Toward us; look now before you,")
>
> 3 Εἶπεν οὑμὸς Προστάτης, “εἰ διακρίνεις.”  
> (Said my Master, "if you can discern.")

- Musurus translates the Latin as well as the Italian. *Ἄνακτος ᾍδου* ‘of the lord of Hades’ puts the two dependent genitives before *σήματ᾿*, whereas *vexilla* comes first and *regis* is separated from *inferni* by the verb. *Ἄνακτος* is the genitive singular of poetic *ἄναξ*; *σήματ᾿* is *σήματα* (nom. pl. neut. of *σῆμα*), ‘signs’ in the sense of ‘standards’.
- *ἔξεισι* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἔξειμι*, ‘go forth’) corresponds to plural *prodeunt*: the singular verb with a neuter plural subject is regular Attic syntax. The adverb *πρόσω* ‘forward’ reinforces the verb. *Ἡμῶν ἔναντι* ‘over against us’ renders directional *verso di noi* by an expression of position, with the genitive pronoun placed before *ἔναντι*.
- *βλέπε* (2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *βλέπω*) renders *mira*. *νῦν* ‘now’ prompts the look but does not express the inference in *però*. *ἔμπροσθέν σου* makes the point of reference of *dinanzi* explicit: *σου* is the enclitic genitive of the second-person singular pronoun, hence the additional accent on *ἔμπροσθέν*.
- *Εἶπεν* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*, with movable *ν*) precedes its subject, as does *disse*. In *οὑμὸς Προστάτης* ‘my protector’, *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*: the possessive thus stands before the noun, unlike *mio* in *il maestro mio*. *εἰ διακρίνεις* ‘if you distinguish’ retains *se* and the second-person present indicative (*διακρίνω*), but leaves the object *il* unexpressed.

> 4 Come quando una grossa nebbia spira,  
> 5 o quando l’ emisperio nostro annotta  
> 6 par da lungi un molin che il vento gira :  

As, when a thick mist breathes, or when the night comes on our hemisphere, a mill, [which] the wind [turns], appears at distance:

> 4 Ὡς δ᾿ ὅτ᾿ ὀμίχλη πυκνὴ τὴν γῆν καλύπτει,  
> (And as when a thick mist covers the earth,)
>
> 5 Ἢ νὺξ κάτεισιν ἐφ᾿ ἡμετέρου πόλου,  
> (Or night descends upon our sky,)
>
> 6 Φαίνετ᾿ ἄποθεν στρεφόμενος ἀνέμῳ  
> (There appears afar, turned by the wind,)

- *Ὡς δ᾿ ὅτ᾿* ‘and as when’ is a poetic opening to a comparison: postpositive *δέ* and temporal *ὅτε* both lose their final vowel before a vowel. *ὀμίχλη πυκνή* ‘a dense mist’ reverses the order in *una grossa nebbia*. In place of intransitive *spira*, *καλύπτει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *καλύπτω*) takes a supplied object, *τὴν γῆν* ‘the earth’.
- *Ἢ νὺξ κάτεισιν* ‘or night comes down’ does not repeat *quando*: the first *ὅτε* serves both clauses. Musurus makes *νύξ* the subject of *κάτεισιν* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *κάτειμι*, with movable *ν*) rather than making *l’emisperio nostro* the subject of *annotta*. *ἐφ᾿ ἡμετέρου πόλου* ‘over our sky’ gives the region of the descent, with *ἐπί* governing the genitive; its final vowel is elided and *π* becomes *φ* before the rough breathing of *ἡμετέρου*.
- *Φαίνετ᾿ ἄποθεν* ‘appears from afar’ closely follows *par da lungi*: *φαίνετ᾿* is elided *φαίνεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *φαίνω*, intransitive here), and *ἄποθεν* is a single adverb of distance. The subject, *Μυλών* ‘mill’ (nom. sg. masc.), is postponed into Greek line 7. *στρεφόμενος* (pres. pass. participle of *στρέφω*, nom. sg. masc.) agrees with it: the active relative clause *che il vento gira* becomes ‘being turned by wind’, with *ἀνέμῳ* a dative of means or inanimate cause.

> 7 veder mi parve un tal ’dificio allotta ;  

such an edifice did I now seem to see;

> 7 Μυλών, οὕτω τότ᾿ εἶδον κινούμενόν τι  
> (A windmill, so then I saw something moving,)

- After *Μυλών* completes the comparison, *οὕτω* ‘so’ answers *Ὡς*, and *τότ᾿* (elided *τότε*) renders *allotta* ‘then’. Italian *un tal ’dificio* puts the resemblance in the noun phrase; Greek puts it in the adverb *οὕτω* and does not name an edifice.
- *εἶδον* ‘I saw’ (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. belonging to *ὁράω*) replaces the more tentative *veder mi parve*. Its object is the enclitic indefinite *τι* ‘something’ (acc. sg. neut. of *τις*), with the agreeing participle *κινούμενον* (pres. mid./pass. of *κινέω*): ‘I saw something moving’. The participle brings the motion of the preceding comparison into the perception itself.

> 8 poi per lo vento mi ristrinsi retro  
> 9 al duca mio, chè non li era altra grotta.  

and, for the wind, shrunk back behind my Guide, because no other shed was there.

> 8 Καὶ διὰ τὸν ἄνεμον ἔστην ὀπίσω  
> (And because of the wind I stood behind)
>
> 9 Τοὐμοῦ Προστάτου, μηδὲν ἄλλ᾿ ἔρυμ᾿ ἔχων.  
> (My Master, having no other shelter.)

- *Καὶ διὰ τὸν ἄνεμον* renders *poi per lo vento* with simple coordination instead of explicit succession; *διά* with the accusative gives the cause. *ἔστην* ‘I took my stand’ is the intransitive second aorist active indicative, first singular, of *ἵστημι*. It gives the resulting stance rather than the shrinking movement of reflexive *mi ristrinsi*.
- *ὀπίσω Τοὐμοῦ Προστάτου* follows *retro al duca mio*, but *ὀπίσω* takes the genitive. *Τοὐμοῦ* is crasis of *τοῦ ἐμοῦ*; *Προστάτου* (gen. sg. of *προστάτης*) repeats the designation previously used for *maestro*.
- *μηδὲν ἄλλ᾿ ἔρυμ᾿ ἔχων* ‘having no other shelter’ replaces the finite causal clause *chè non li era altra grotta*. *ἔχων* (nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *ἔχω*) agrees with the speaker and gives the reason circumstantially. The Italian construction of existence in a place becomes one of possession, without an equivalent of *li* ‘there’. *μηδέν*, the neuter of *μηδείς*, and *ἄλλ᾿* (elided *ἄλλο*) qualify accusative *ἔρυμ᾿* (elided *ἔρυμα*), ‘protection’ rather than specifically a recess or shed.

> 10 Già era, e con paura il metto in metro,  
> 11 là dove l’ ombre eran tutte coperte,  
> 12 e trasparean come festuca in vetro.  

Already I had come (and with fear I put it into verse) where the souls were [wholly] covered, and shone through like straw in glass.

> 10 Ἀφικόμην δή (φόβῳ δ᾿ ἐν μέτροις γράφω),  
> (I came indeed (and with fear I write in verse),)
>
> 11 Ἔνθ᾿ ἐπισκεπεῖς ἦσαν αἱ σκιαὶ πᾶσαι  
> (Where all the shades were covered over)
>
> 12 Καὶ διαφανεῖς, ὡς ἔμβρυ᾿ ἐν ὑέλῳ.  
> (And transparent, like embryos in glass.)

- *Ἀφικόμην δή* ‘I had indeed arrived’ renders the state *Già era … là* by the event of reaching the place. *Ἀφικόμην* is the first-person singular second aorist middle indicative of *ἀφικνέομαι*, a middle verb, not a Greek pluperfect; postpositive *δή* marks the arrival emphatically rather than literally saying ‘already’.
- *φόβῳ δ᾿ ἐν μέτροις γράφω* ‘and with fear I write in verse’ keeps the parenthetic present (*γράφω*, 1st sg. pres. act. ind.). The bare dative *φόβῳ* gives the attendant feeling of *con paura*; *ἐν μέτροις* is *ἐν* with the dative plural of *μέτρον*. The verb of writing replaces *metto*, and *lo*, its object, is not translated. Elided *δέ* stands second in its clause.
- *Ἔνθ᾿* (elided *ἔνθα*) ‘where’ does the work of *là dove*. *ἐπισκεπεῖς* ‘covered over’ is a predicate adjective (nom. pl. fem. of *ἐπισκεπής*) with *ἦσαν* (3rd pl. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*), not a passive participle. *αἱ σκιαὶ πᾶσαι* follows the order of *l’ombre … tutte*: *πᾶσαι*, outside the article–noun group, agrees with the shades, ‘all the shades’, rather than adverbially qualifying their covering.
- *Καὶ διαφανεῖς* adds a second predicate adjective (nom. pl. fem. of *διαφανής*) under the same *ἦσαν*, in place of the finite imperfect *trasparean*. It characterizes the shades as transparent or visible through a medium. *ὡς ἔμβρυ᾿ ἐν ὑέλῳ* keeps the comparative construction and the glass (*ἐν* with dat. sg. of *ὕελος*), but *ἔμβρυ᾿* is elided *ἔμβρυα*, neuter plural of *ἔμβρυον* ‘embryo’: neither the image nor the number reproduces singular *festuca* ‘straw’.

> 13 Altre sono a giacere, altre stanno erte,  
> 14 quella col capo e quella con le piante ;  
> 15 altra, com’ arco, il volto a’ piedi inverte.  

Some [are] lying; some stand upright, this on its head, and that upon its soles; another, like a bow, bends face to feet.

> 13 Αἱ μὲν μένουσ᾿ ὑπτίως, αἱ δ᾿ ὀρθοστάδην,  
> (Some remain lying on their backs, and others upright,)
>
> 14 Αἱ δ᾿ ἐπὶ ποδῶν, ἐπὶ κεφαλῆς ἄλλαι,  
> (Some upon their feet, others upon their head,)
>
> 15 Καί τις πρὸς πόδας στρέφουσ’ ὄψιν, ὡς τόξον.  
> (And one bending its face toward its feet, like a bow.)

- *Αἱ μὲν … αἱ δ᾿* ‘some … others’ uses the feminine plural article pronominally, referring back to *σκιαί*; correlative *μέν* and *δέ* distribute the groups. *μένουσ᾿* is elided *μένουσι* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *μένω*). With *ὑπτίως* it says more than *sono a giacere*: they ‘remain on their backs’. The verb is understood again with *ὀρθοστάδην*, an adverb ‘in a standing posture’, for *stanno erte*.
- *Αἱ δ᾿ ἐπὶ ποδῶν, ἐπὶ κεφαλῆς ἄλλαι* reverses the order of *quella col capo e quella con le piante* and changes the distributive singulars to plurals. *ἐπί* with the genitive identifies the point of support: *ποδῶν* is genitive plural of *πούς*, ‘feet’ rather than specifically ‘soles’; *κεφαλῆς* is genitive singular, one head apiece. The subjects frame the two phrases, first pronominal *αἱ*, then *ἄλλαι* ‘others’; there is no new finite verb.
- *Καί τις* ‘and one’ restores the singular of *altra* but not its explicit contrast ‘another’. *στρέφουσ’* is elided *στρέφουσα* (nom. sg. fem. pres. act. participle of *στρέφω*), agreeing with *τις* as one of the shades: where *inverte* is finite, the Greek gives an elliptical picture, ‘and one turning …’. *πρὸς πόδας* (acc. pl.) puts the direction before the object *ὄψιν* (acc. sg. of *ὄψις*, ‘face’); *ὡς τόξον* postpones *com’ arco* to the end.

> 16 Quando noi fummo fatti tanto avante,  
> 17 ch’ al mio maestro piacque di mostrarmi  
> 18 la creatura ch’ ebbe il sembiante.  

When we had proceeded on so far, that it pleased my Guide to show to me the Creature which was once so fair,

> 16 Ὅτε δὲ προέβημεν ἀρκούντως πρόσθεν,  
> (And when we had advanced far enough forward,)
>
> 17 Ἔνθ’ ἤρεσ’ ἐμὸν Προστάτην ἐμοὶ δεῖξαι  
> (Where it pleased my Master to show me)
>
> 18 Τὸ περικαλλὲς ὑπάρξαν ποτὲ πλάσμα,  
> (The creature that was once exceedingly fair,)

- *Ὅτε δὲ προέβημεν* ‘and when we advanced’ adds connective *δέ* and uses the first-person plural second aorist active indicative of *προβαίνω* (augment internal after the prefix). The simple intransitive verb replaces *fummo fatti … avante*. *ἀρκούντως πρόσθεν* ‘sufficiently far forward’ interprets the degree *tanto* as sufficiency rather than setting up a consecutive ‘so … that’ construction.
- *Ἔνθ’* ‘where, at which point’ accordingly takes the place of consecutive *ch’*. *ἤρεσ’* is elided *ἤρεσε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀρέσκω*, with temporal augment). The case construction is striking: Italian *al mio maestro piacque* marks the person pleased by *a*, and idiomatic Greek *ἀρέσκω* likewise takes a dative for that person. Musurus instead writes accusative *ἐμὸν Προστάτην*, intelligible as the subject of *δεῖξαι* ‘to show’ (aor. act. infinitive of *δείκνυμι*): what pleased was ‘for my protector to show me’. It is not the regular Greek construction for ‘it pleased my protector’.
- *ἐμοὶ δεῖξαι* detaches the recipient from Italian *mostrarmi*, using the full dative pronoun *ἐμοί* before the infinitive. Its direct object is *Τὸ … πλάσμα* (acc. sg. neut.), ‘the thing formed’, for *la creatura*. Between article and noun, *περικαλλὲς ὑπάρξαν ποτὲ* replaces the finite relative *ch’ ebbe il sembiante*: *ὑπάρξαν* (acc. sg. neut. aor. act. participle of *ὑπάρχω*) means ‘having been’, with predicate *περικαλλές* (neuter of *περικαλλής*, ‘exceedingly beautiful’) and temporal *ποτέ* ‘once’. Musurus thus predicates former beauty directly of the creature. In this he agrees with “was once so fair”, though the Italian text as supplied has *il sembiante* ‘the appearance’ without an expressed adjective of beauty.

## Lines 19–51 — Dis, His Three Faces and Six Wings

> 19 dinanzi mi si tolse, e fe' restarmi,  
> 20 “Ecco Dite,” dicendo, “ed ecco il loco,  
> 21 ove convien che di fortezza t' armi.”  

he took himself from before me, and made me stop, saying: “Lo Dis! and lo the place where it behoves thee arm thyself with fortitude.”

> 19 Ὑπεχώρησεν ἀπ’ ἔμπροσθέν μου, καί με  
> (He stepped aside from before me, and me)
>
> 20 Ἐπισχὼν εἶπ’· “Ἰδοὺ Δίς, ἴδε τὸν τόπον,  
> (Having halted, he said: "Behold Dis, behold the place,)
>
> 21 „ Ὅπου σε θάρσει περιφραχθῆναι πρέπει.”  
> (Where you must arm yourself with courage.")

- *Ὑπεχώρησεν* ‘he stepped aside’ (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ὑποχωρέω*) renders the reflexive *si tolse* by an intransitive active; the augment comes after *ὑπο-*. *ἀπ’ ἔμπροσθέν μου* ‘from in front of me’ makes the direction explicit with elided *ἀπό*; *ἔμπροσθεν* governs the genitive *μου* and bears an additional accent before that enclitic.
- Musurus reverses the grammatical ranking of *fe’ restarmi* and *dicendo*: *με ἐπισχών* ‘having stopped me’ is subordinate, and *εἶπ’* ‘he said’ is the finite verb. *ἐπισχών* is the nom. sg. masc. second aor. act. participle of *ἐπέχω*, with the same subject as *εἶπ’* (elided *εἶπε*, 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*). The accent on *καί* is retained before enclitic *με*.
- For the repeated *ecco … ed ecco* Musurus varies the construction: presentative *ἰδού* with nominative *Δίς*, then *ἴδε* ‘look at’ (2nd sg. second aor. act. imperative of *ὁράω*) with accusative *τὸν τόπον*. There is no second conjunction.
- *ὅπου* ‘where’ introduces the relative clause. Impersonal *πρέπει* ‘it is fitting’ governs *σε … περιφραχθῆναι* ‘for you to be fenced about’ (*σε* accusative subject; aor. pass. inf. of *περιφράσσω*). This replaces *convien che … t’ armi*, a finite reflexive subjunctive; *θάρσει* (dat. sg. of *θάρσος*) is instrumental, ‘with courage’, for *di fortezza*.

> 22 Com' io divenni allor gelato e fioco,  
> 23 nol domandar, lettor, ch' io non lo scrivo,  
> 24 però ch' ogni parlar sarebbe poco.  

How icy chill and hoarse I then became, ask not, O Reader! for I write it not, because all speech would fail to tell.

> 22 Ὡς δ’ ἐγενόμην ῥιγῶν, ἐπτοημένος,  
> (How chilled and terrified I then became,)
>
> 23 Ἀναγνῶστα, γράψαι σοι νῦν με μὴ θέλε·  
> (Reader, do not ask me to write for you now;)
>
> 24 Πᾶς μου γὰρ λόγος ἀνίκανος ἂν εἴη.  
> (For all my speech would be inadequate.)

- Exclamatory *ὡς* ‘how’ answers to *Com’*; *δ’* is elided postpositive *δέ*, marking the transition rather than translating *allor*. *ἐγενόμην* (1st sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *γίγνομαι*) takes two predicative participles, both nom. sg. masc.: *ῥιγῶν* ‘shivering’ (pres. act. of *ῥιγόω*) and *ἐπτοημένος* ‘terrified’ (perf. pass. of *πτοέω*). The first turns *gelato* into an ongoing bodily response; the second interprets *fioco* as a state of fright rather than of the voice. There is no conjunction between them.
- *Ἀναγνῶστα* (voc. sg. of *ἀναγνώστης*) brings *lettor* to the front. *γράψαι σοι νῦν με μὴ θέλε* says literally ‘do not wish me to write to you now’: *μὴ θέλε* is a negated 2nd sg. pres. act. imperative of *θέλω*, *γράψαι* the aor. act. infinitive of *γράφω*, *με* its subject and *σοι* its dative of recipient. Thus *nol domandar* and *ch’ io non lo scrivo* become a single prohibition, with no expressed object corresponding to *lo*.
- *πᾶς μου γὰρ λόγος* ‘for all speech of mine’ supplies a possessor for *ogni parlar*. Postpositive *γάρ* gives the reason; *ἀνίκανος ἂν εἴη* ‘would be unequal to it’ renders *sarebbe poco* with a predicate adjective and the potential optative (*εἴη*, 3rd sg. pres. opt. of *εἰμί*, with *ἄν*).

> 25 Io non morii, e non rimasi vivo:  
> 26 pensa oramai per te, s' hai fior d' ingegno,  
> 27 qual io divenni, d' uno e d' altro privo.  

I did not die, and did not remain alive: now think for thyself, if thou hast any grain of ingenuity, what I became, deprived of both *death and life*.

> 25 Οὐκ ἤμην νεκρὸς οὔτε ζῶν· νῦν δὴ σκέψαι  
> (I was neither dead nor alive; consider now)
>
> 26 Ἀπὸ σεαυτοῦ, κἂν νοῦν ὀλίγον ἔχῃς,  
> (For yourself, if you have even a little wit,)
>
> 27 Οἷος ἄνευ θανάτου καὶ ζωῆς ἤμην.  
> (What state I was in without death and life.)

- *οὐκ ἤμην νεκρὸς οὔτε ζῶν* ‘I was not dead nor living’ levels the distinction between *non morii* and *non rimasi vivo*. Both predicates depend on *ἤμην* (1st sg. imperf. of *εἰμί*); *νεκρός* is an adjective, *ζῶν* the contracted nom. sg. masc. pres. act. participle of *ζάω*. *οὔτε* continues the initial simple negative *οὐκ*.
- *νῦν δὴ σκέψαι* ‘now then consider’ moves the imperative up from line 26; *δή* lends insistence to *νῦν*. *σκέψαι* is the 2nd sg. aor. mid. imperative of *σκέπτομαι*. *ἀπὸ σεαυτοῦ* ‘from yourself’ (gen. sg. reflexive) makes the reader the source of the judgment, for *per te*.
- *κἂν νοῦν ὀλίγον ἔχῃς* ‘if you have even a little understanding’ has crasis of *καὶ ἐάν* in *κἄν*, with *ἔχῃς* (2nd sg. pres. act. subj. of *ἔχω*). The scalar force of ‘even’ and accusative *νοῦν ὀλίγον* replace the idiomatic *fior d’ ingegno*.
- *οἷος … ἤμην* ‘what sort of person I was’ depends on *σκέψαι*: predicative *οἷος* is nominative, and the imperfect again describes a state where *divenni* denotes becoming. *ἄνευ* governs the explicit genitives *θανάτου καὶ ζωῆς* in place of *d’ uno e d’ altro privo*.

> 28 Lo imperador del doloroso regno  
> 29 da mezzo il petto uscia fuor della ghiaccia;  

The Emperor of the dolorous realm, from mid breast stood forth out of the ice;

> 28 Ὁ τῶν Κλαυθμώνων τοῦ Κράτους Αὐτοκράτωρ  
> (The Emperor of the realm of Lamentations)
>
> 29 Ἐκ μέσου στήθους ἐξήρχετο τοῦ πάγου.  
> (Emerged from mid-breast out of the ice.)

- *ὁ τῶν Κλαυθμώνων τοῦ Κράτους Αὐτοκράτωρ* puts the governing noun *Αὐτοκράτωρ* last, after both dependent genitives: *τοῦ Κράτους* ‘of the dominion’ depends on *Αὐτοκράτωρ*, *τῶν Κλαυθμώνων* ‘of lamentations’ on *Κράτους*. Thus *doloroso* becomes a plural noun (*κλαυθμών*), and the order of the dependencies is the reverse of *imperador del doloroso regno*.
- *ἐκ μέσου στήθους* ‘from mid-breast’ closely follows *da mezzo il petto*, with *μέσου* agreeing with *στήθους* (gen. sg. neut. of *στῆθος*). *ἐξήρχετο* (3rd sg. imperf. mid. ind. of *ἐξέρχομαι*, augmented after the preverb) preserves the imperfect of *uscia*. The preverb carries the force of *fuor*; *τοῦ πάγου* is a genitive of separation.

> 30 e più con un gigante io mi convegno  
> 31 che i giganti non fan con le sue braccia:  

and I *in size* am liker to a giant, than the giants are to his arms:

> 30 Παρέβαλλον δ’ ἐμαυτὸν γίγαντι μᾶλλον  
> (And I compared better with a giant)
>
> 31 Ἢ γίγανθ’ ἑνὶ τῶν αὐτοῦ βραχιόνων.  
> (Than a giant with one of his arms.)

- *παρέβαλλον δ’ ἐμαυτὸν γίγαντι μᾶλλον* ‘and I compared myself rather to a giant’ turns present *io mi convegno* into an act of comparison in the imperfect. *παρέβαλλον* is here 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *παραβάλλω*, with internal augment; *ἐμαυτόν* is the accusative reflexive object, *γίγαντι* (dat. sg. of *γίγας*) the term to which it is compared. Postpositive *δ’* renders the link *e*; comparative *μᾶλλον* comes last rather than first as *più* does.
- After *ἤ* ‘than’, the same verb is understood: ‘than [I compared] a giant to one of his arms’. *γίγανθ’* is accusative *γίγαντα*, with elision and change of *τ* to *θ* before the rough breathing of *ἑνί*. The dative *ἑνί* (masc. sg. of *εἷς*) takes the partitive genitive *τῶν αὐτοῦ βραχιόνων*. Italian *i giganti* is plural subject of *fan*; Greek has one giant as object, and singles out one arm.

> 32 vedi oramai quant' esser dee quel tutto  
> 33 ch' a così fatte parti si confaccia.  

mark now how great that whole must be, which corresponds to such a part.

> 32 Κρῖνον νῦν ἐκ τοῦ μέρους, ὁποῖον εἶναι  
> (Judge now from that part, how great)
>
> 33 Τὸ πᾶν σῶμ’ αὐτοῦ κατὰ σύγκρισιν ἔδει.  
> (His whole body by comparison had to be.)

- *κρῖνον νῦν ἐκ τοῦ μέρους* ‘judge now from the part’ renders *vedi oramai* by the 2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *κρίνω* and anticipates *a così fatte parti*. The Greek has a singular, and *ἐκ* with the genitive makes the part the basis of the inference.
- *ὁποῖον εἶναι τὸ πᾶν σῶμ’ αὐτοῦ … ἔδει* ‘what sort of thing his whole body had to be’ depends on *κρῖνον*. *ἔδει* is the imperfect of impersonal *δεῖ*, against present *dee*; *τὸ πᾶν σῶμ’* (elided *σῶμα*) is the neuter accusative subject of *εἶναι*, and *ὁποῖον* its predicate. Greek supplies ‘body’ and ‘his’ for *quel tutto*, and asks of what kind, rather than explicitly of what size. *κατὰ σύγκρισιν* ‘by comparison’ replaces the relative clause *ch’ a così fatte parti si confaccia*.

> 34 S' ei fu sì bel com' egli è ora brutto  
> 35 e contra il suo Fattore alzò le ciglia,  
> 36 ben dee da lui procedere ogni lutto.  

If he was once as beautiful as he is ugly now, and lifted up his brows against his Maker, well may all affliction come from him.

> 34 Εἰ δ’ ἦν εὐειδὴς ὅσον εἰδεχθὴς ἤδη,  
> (And if he was as fair as he is hideous now,)
>
> 35 Καὶ πρὸς τὸν πλάστην αὐτοῦ κέρας ἀντῆρεν,  
> (And raised his horn against his Maker,)
>
> 36 Ὄντως ἐξ αὐτοῦ πηγάζειν δεῖ πᾶν ἄχθος.  
> (Truly from him must spring every woe.)

- *εἰ δ’ ἦν εὐειδὴς ὅσον εἰδεχθὴς ἤδη* ‘and if he was fair to the extent that he is loathsome now’: *εἰ* with indicative *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. of *εἰμί*) gives the condition, *δ’* the transition. The two predicate adjectives are nom. sg. masc.; neuter accusative *ὅσον* expresses extent, doing the work of *sì … com’*. No copula is expressed with the second adjective; *ἤδη* marks the contrast with the past.
- *καὶ πρὸς τὸν πλάστην αὐτοῦ κέρας ἀντῆρεν* continues the condition. *πρός* with the accusative is adversative, ‘against’; *πλάστης* is literally ‘one who moulds’. For *alzò le ciglia* Musurus substitutes ‘raised a horn’, with *κέρας* (acc. sg. neut.) as object of *ἀντῆρεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀνταίρω*); the preverb, too, conveys opposition.
- *ὄντως* ‘indeed’ takes up emphatic *ben*. *δεῖ* governs *πηγάζειν* (pres. act. inf. of *πηγάζω*) with *πᾶν ἄχθος* as its neuter accusative subject: ‘every burden must well up from him’. *ἐξ αὐτοῦ* retains the source *da lui*; the verb is more specific than *procedere*, and *ἄχθος* gives *lutto* the sense of a weight of suffering.

> 37 O quanto parve a me gran maraviglia,  
> 38 quando vidi tre facce alla sua testa!  
> 39 L' una dinanzi, e quella era vermiglia;  

Oh how great a marvel seemed it to me, when I saw three faces on his head! The one in front, and it was fiery red;

> 37 Ὤ, πόσον τότ’ ᾐσθόμην θαυμασμὸν μέγαν,  
> (Oh, how great a marvel I felt then,)
>
> 38 Ἐπὶ κεφαλῆς αὐτοῦ τρεῖς ἰδὼν ὄψεις,  
> (Seeing three faces upon his head,)
>
> 39 Τὴν μὲν ἔμπροσθεν ἐρυθρόχροον οὖσαν,  
> (The one in front being crimson-coloured,)

- *Ὤ, πόσον τότ’ ᾐσθόμην θαυμασμὸν μέγαν* changes *parve a me gran maraviglia* into ‘how great an amazement I felt then’. *ᾐσθόμην* is 1st sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *αἰσθάνομαι*, with temporal augment; the Italian experiencer *a me* becomes its subject. *πόσον* and *μέγαν* both mark the degree of *θαυμασμόν*, the accusative object. *τότ’* is elided *τότε*.
- *ἐπὶ κεφαλῆς αὐτοῦ* ‘on his head’ uses locative *ἐπί* with the genitive. *τρεῖς ἰδὼν ὄψεις* ‘on seeing three faces’ replaces finite *quando vidi* by a circumstantial participle: *ἰδών* (nom. sg. masc., second aor. act. of *ὁράω*) agrees with the speaker, and splits the accusative phrase *τρεῖς … ὄψεις*.
- *τὴν μὲν ἔμπροσθεν ἐρυθρόχροον οὖσαν* continues the accusative construction: ‘the one in front being red-coloured’. *τήν* picks out one of the *ὄψεις*; *μέν* prepares for *δέ*. *οὖσαν* (acc. sg. fem. pres. participle of *εἰμί*) and its predicate *ἐρυθρόχροον* agree with *τήν*. Thus *e quella era vermiglia* loses both its resumed subject and its finite verb.

> 40 l' altre eran due, che s' aggiungieno a questa  
> 41 sopr' esso il mezzo di ciascuna spalla,  
> 42 e si giungieno al loco della cresta;  

the others were two, that were adjoined to this, above the very middle of each shoulder; and they were joined [at] his crest;

> 40 Τὰς δ’ ἄλλας δύο προσαπτομένας ταύτῃ  
> (And the other two joined onto this one)
>
> 41 Ἐπὶ τοῦ μέσου τῶν ὤμων ἑκατέρου,  
> (Above the middle of each shoulder,)
>
> 42 Καὶ περὶ τὴν κορυφὴν συνεζευγμένας.  
> (And joined together around the crown.)

- *τὰς δ’ ἄλλας δύο προσαπτομένας ταύτῃ* ‘and the other two attached to this one’ balances *τὴν μέν*. The presentation *l’ altre eran due* and relative *che s’ aggiungieno* are absorbed into the continuing object of ‘seeing’. *προσαπτομένας* is a pres. mid./pass. participle (acc. pl. fem. of *προσάπτω*), with dative *ταύτῃ* for the face to which the others are attached. *δύο* accompanies plural forms, not a dual.
- *ἐπὶ τοῦ μέσου τῶν ὤμων ἑκατέρου* means ‘upon the middle of each of the two shoulders’. Genitive *ἑκατέρου* is distributive and itself takes partitive *τῶν ὤμων*; the singular *τοῦ μέσου* is governed by locative *ἐπί*. The Greek thus expresses the pair implicit in *ciascuna spalla* without a dual noun.
- *καὶ περὶ τὴν κορυφὴν συνεζευγμένας* ‘and joined together about the crown’ coordinates a second participle, this time perfect mid./pass. (acc. pl. fem. of *συζεύγνυμι*), stressing the resulting junction rather than repeating the imperfect as in *si giungieno*. *περί* with accusative gives the region directly; there is no separate noun for *loco*.

> 43 e la destra parea tra bianca e gialla;  

and the right seemed between white and yellow;

> 43 Ἡ δεξιὰ δ’ ἦν χρώματος ὠχρολεύκου·  
> (And the right one was of a yellowish-white colour;)

- *ἡ δεξιὰ δ’ ἦν χρώματος ὠχρολεύκου* ‘and the right one was of a pale-yellowish white colour’ resumes a finite construction. *ἡ δεξιά* is a substantivized feminine adjective with *ὄψις* understood; *ἦν* asserts where *parea* qualifies. *χρώματος* (gen. sg. of *χρῶμα*) with agreeing *ὠχρολεύκου* is a predicate genitive of quality. A compound adjective replaces *tra bianca e gialla*, blending the colours rather than placing the face between them.

> 44 la sinistra a vedere era tal, quali  
> 45 vengon di là onde il Nilo s' avvalla.  

the left was such to look on, as they who come from where the Nile [descends].

> 44 Ἡ δ’ ἀριστερὰ τὴν θέαν ἦν ὁμοία  
> (And the left in appearance was like)
>
> 45 Τῶν ἐρχομένων ὅθεν ὁ Νεῖλος ῥέει,  
> (Those who come from whence the Nile flows,)

- *ἡ δ’ ἀριστερὰ τὴν θέαν ἦν ὁμοία* ‘and the left was similar in appearance’ gives *a vedere* as an accusative of respect, *τὴν θέαν*. Predicate *ὁμοία* (nom. sg. fem.) replaces the correlatives *tal, quali*.
- *τῶν ἐρχομένων* ‘of those coming’ is an articular pres. mid. participle of *ἔρχομαι*, gen. pl., for the finite *vengon*. The genitive is awkward if taken directly with *ὁμοία*, for which a dative of the persons compared would be usual; the sense is ‘like [the appearance] of those coming’. *ὅθεν* includes the idea ‘from where’ without a separate *di là*; *ὁ Νεῖλος ῥέει* uses the uncontracted form *ῥέει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ῥέω*, Attic contracted *ῥεῖ*). It says simply ‘flows’, not explicitly ‘flows down’.

> 46 Sotto ciascuna uscivan due grandi ali,  
> 47 quanto si convenia a tanto uccello:  
> 48 vele di mar non vid' io mai cotali.  

Under each there issued forth two mighty wings, of size befitting such a bird: sea-sails I never saw so broad.

> 46 Δύ’ ἐχούσης ἑκάστης πτερὰ μεγάλα,  
> (Each having two great wings,)
>
> 47 Τοιάδ’ ὅπως ἥρμοζε πτηνῷ τοιῷδε·  
> (Such as befitted such a winged creature;)
>
> 48 Οὔποτ’ εἶδον τηλίκα νηῶν ἱστία.  
> (Never did I see ships' sails so huge.)

- *δύ’ ἐχούσης ἑκάστης πτερὰ μεγάλα* ‘each having two large wings’ replaces *Sotto ciascuna uscivan* with a genitive absolute. *ἑκάστης* (gen. sg. fem., each face) is the subject of *ἐχούσης* (agreeing pres. act. participle of *ἔχω*); *δύ’* is elided *δύο*, qualifying neuter accusative plural *πτερὰ μεγάλα*. Neither ‘under’ nor the motion of issuing is expressed.
- *τοιάδ’ ὅπως ἥρμοζε πτηνῷ τοιῷδε* ‘such as befitted a winged creature of this sort’ has *τοιάδ’* (elided *τοιάδε*, acc. pl. neut. of *τοιόσδε*) agreeing with *πτερά*. *ὅπως* introduces manner or correspondence, not purpose; *ἥρμοζε* is 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἁρμόζω*, with the recipient in the dative *πτηνῷ τοιῷδε*. Where *quanto … tanto* emphasizes scale, the paired Greek demonstratives denote kind.
- *οὔποτ’ εἶδον τηλίκα νηῶν ἱστία* ‘never did I see sails of ships so large’: *οὔποτ’* is elided *οὔποτε*, and *εἶδον* is here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*. *τηλίκα* (acc. pl. neut. of *τηλίκος*) now specifies size. Poetic, notably Homeric, *νηῶν* (gen. pl. of *ναῦς*; Attic *νεῶν*) changes *vele di mar* to ‘sails of ships’.

> 49 Non avean penne, ma di vipistrello  
> 50 era lor modo; e quelle svolazzava,  
> 51 sì che tre venti si movean da ello.  

No plumes had they; but were in form *and texture* like a bat’s: and he was flapping them, so that three winds went forth from him.

> 49 Πτίλα δ’ οὐκ εἶχεν· ἀλλ’ ἦν κατὰ τὸν τρόπον  
> (And feathers they had not; but they were after the fashion)
>
> 50 Τῶν νυκτερίδος· τούτων δὲ σειομένων,  
> (Of a bat's; and as these were flapped,)
>
> 51 Ἐξ αὐτῶν τρεῖς ἄνεμοι συνεξωρμῶντο,  
> (From them three winds rushed forth together,)

- *πτίλα δ’ οὐκ εἶχεν* fronts the object ‘feathers’, rather than the negated verb as in *Non avean penne*. *εἶχεν* (3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *ἔχω*) and following *ἦν* are singular with the understood neuter plural subject *πτερά*, regular Attic agreement where Italian has plural *avean*.
- *ἀλλ’ ἦν κατὰ τὸν τρόπον τῶν νυκτερίδος* ‘but they were after the fashion of a bat’s’ recasts *di vipistrello era lor modo*: *τρόπος* is not the subject but the accusative after *κατά*. The genitive plural article *τῶν* stands for *τῶν πτερῶν*, ‘of the wings’, with possessive *νυκτερίδος* (gen. sg. of *νυκτερίς*). *ἀλλ’* is elided *ἀλλά*.
- *τούτων δὲ σειομένων* ‘and as these were being shaken’ makes *e quelle svolazzava* a genitive absolute: demonstrative *τούτων* and pres. pass. participle *σειομένων* (gen. pl. neut. of *σείω*) refer to the wings. Italian has the flapper as subject and the wings as object; Greek leaves the agent unexpressed.
- *ἐξ αὐτῶν τρεῖς ἄνεμοι συνεξωρμῶντο* ‘from them three winds rushed out together’ is the main clause, not a result clause introduced by an equivalent of *sì che*. *αὐτῶν* points to the wings in place of singular *ello*. *συνεξωρμῶντο* (3rd pl. imperf. mid. ind. of *συνεξορμάω*, augmented after the preverbs) retains the imperfect of *si movean* but specifies both outward motion and simultaneous onset.

## Lines 52–84 — The Three Sinners and the Shaggy Stairs

> 52 Quindi Cocito tutto s' aggelava;  

Thereby Cocytus all was frozen;

> 52 Ὑφ’ ὧν συνεπήγνυτο Κωκυτὸς ἅπας.  
> (By which all Cocytus was frozen solid.)

- *Ὑφ’ ὧν* ‘by which’ turns the causal adverb *Quindi* into a connective relative looking back to the winds. *ὧν* is genitive plural after *ὑπό*; the spelling *ὑφ’* shows both elision of the final vowel and aspiration of *π* before the rough breathing.
- *συνεπήγνυτο* (3rd sg. imperfect middle/passive of *συμπήγνυμι*, with internal augment) renders *s’ aggelava* as a continuing process, ‘was freezing solid’. The verb precedes its subject; *ἅπας* (nom. masc. sg.) follows *Κωκυτός* as *tutto* follows *Cocito*, emphasizing the whole extent.

> 53 con sei occhi piangeva, e per tre menti  
> 54 gocciava il pianto e sanguinosa bava.  

with six eyes he wept, and down three chins gushed tears and bloody foam.

> 53 Ἓξ ὄμμασιν ἔκλαιε, κἀπὶ πωγώνων  
> (With six eyes he wept, and down his chins—)
>
> 54 Τριῶν ἔσταζε τὸ δάκρυ σὺν σιέλοις  
> (Three—trickled the tears with slaver)

- *Ἓξ ὄμμασιν ἔκλαιε* closely follows *con sei occhi piangeva*: the indeclinable numeral qualifies the instrumental dative plural of *ὄμμα*, without a preposition; *ἔκλαιε* is the imperfect active indicative, 3rd singular, of *κλαίω*.
- *κἀπὶ πωγώνων τριῶν* ‘and upon three chins’ substitutes *ἐπί* with the genitive for *per* expressing the course of the drops. *κἀπί* is crasis of *καὶ ἐπί*; the declined numeral *τριῶν* (gen. pl. of *τρεῖς*) comes after its noun, across the line break, rather than before it as in *per tre menti*.
- *ἔσταζε τὸ δάκρυ* ‘the tear dripped’ preserves both the imperfect (*στάζω*, 3rd sg. active) and the verb–subject order of *gocciava il pianto*, but replaces weeping as an action with its product, in the singular. Instead of coordinating *il pianto e sanguinosa bava*, Musurus makes the slaver an accompaniment, *σὺν σιέλοις αἱματοφύρτοις* ‘with blood-mingled slaver’. The adjective agrees in the dative plural and is carried over into line 55.

> 55 Da ogni bocca dirompea coi denti  
> 56 un peccatore, a guisa di maciulla,  
> 57 sì che tre ne facea così dolenti.  

In every mouth he champed a sinner with his teeth, like a brake; so that he thus kept three of them in torment.

> 55 Αἱματοφύρτοις. Ὀδὰξ ἕκαστον στόμα  
> (Stained with blood. With its teeth each mouth)
>
> 56 Ἕν’ ἁμαρτωλὸν ἔθραυε δίκην ὅλμου,  
> (Crushed one sinner like a mortar,)
>
> 57 Διὰ μιᾶς τε τρεῖς ἀθλίους συνέθλα.  
> (And at once mangled three wretches.)

- *Ὀδὰξ ἕκαστον στόμα … ἔθραυε* ‘with its teeth each mouth crushed’ gives *coi denti* by the adverb *ὀδάξ* and makes *ἕκαστον στόμα* (nom. neut. sg.) the subject, rather than rendering *Da ogni bocca* as a prepositional phrase. *ἔθραυε* (3rd sg. imperfect active of *θραύω*) takes *ἕν’ ἁμαρτωλόν* as object: *ἕν’* is elided *ἕνα*, masculine accusative of *εἷς*. The numeral makes the distribution more explicit than *un peccatore*.
- *δίκην ὅλμου* ‘in the manner of a mortar’ answers to *a guisa di maciulla*. *δίκην*, originally the accusative singular of *δίκη*, functions adverbially with a dependent genitive; the implement chosen is one for pounding rather than a brake.
- *διὰ μιᾶς τε τρεῖς ἀθλίους συνέθλα* ‘and at one stroke he crushed three wretches’: adverbial *διὰ μιᾶς* stresses simultaneity. Postpositive *τε* coordinates the statement instead of introducing a result as *sì che* does. *συνέθλα* (3rd sg. imperfect active of the contracted verb *συνθλάω*) repeats the idea of crushing; *τρεῖς ἀθλίους* is its accusative object, not a verb of making with a predicative adjective as in *tre ne facea così dolenti*. Neither *ne* nor *così* has a separate equivalent.

> 58 A quel dinanzi il mordere era nulla  
> 59 verso il graffiar, chè talvolta la schiena  
> 60 rimanea della pelle tutta brulla.  

To the one in front, the biting was nought, compared with the tearing: for at times the back of him remained quite stript of skin.

> 58 Ὡς πρὸς τὸν ἔμπροσθ᾽ αἱ δήξεις οὐδὲν ἦσαν  
> (As for the one in front, the bitings were nothing)
>
> 59 Πρὸς τοὺς ὄνυχας· ἔσθ᾽ ὅτε γὰρ ἡ ῥάχις  
> (Beside the claws; for at times his back)
>
> 60 Ἔμενε τοῦ δέρματος γεγυμνωμένη.  
> (Remained stripped bare of its skin.)

- *Ὡς πρὸς τὸν ἔμπροσθ’* ‘as regards the one in front’ sets the topic by *πρός* with the accusative instead of *A quel dinanzi*. The article makes the adverb *ἔμπροσθε* stand for a person; its final vowel is elided before *αἱ*. *αἱ δήξεις* ‘the bites’ pluralizes the substantivized infinitive *il mordere*; hence plural *ἦσαν* (3rd pl. imperfect of *εἰμί*), with predicate *οὐδέν* ‘nothing’.
- The second *πρός* with accusative is comparative: *πρὸς τοὺς ὄνυχας* ‘beside the claws’ puts the instrument for the action *verso il graffiar*. *ἔσθ’ ὅτε* ‘there are times when’, idiomatically ‘sometimes’, renders *talvolta*; *ἔσθ’* is *ἔστι* elided and aspirated before *ὅτε*. Postpositive *γάρ* gives the reason, *chè*.
- *ἡ ῥάχις ἔμενε … γεγυμνωμένη* keeps the subject and imperfect of *la schiena rimanea*, but gives the resulting condition by the perfect middle/passive participle of *γυμνόω*, nominative feminine singular agreeing with *ῥάχις*. *τοῦ δέρματος* is the genitive of what the back is stripped of, corresponding to *della pelle*; *tutta* has no separate intensive equivalent.

> 61 “Quell' anima lassù che ha maggior pena,”  
> 62 disse il maestro, “è Giuda Scariotto,  
> 63 che il capo ha dentro, e fuor le gambe mena.  

“That soul up there, which suffers greatest punishment,” said the Master, “is Judas Iscariot, he who has his head within, and outside plies his legs.

> 61 “Ἡ μᾶλλον ἄνω πάσχουσ’,” εἶπε Προστάτης  
> ("The one suffering more above," said the Master)
>
> 62 Οὑμός, “ψυχή ᾽στιν Ἰούδας Σκαριώτης,  
> (Of mine, "is the soul Judas Iscariot,)
>
> 63 „ Ἔχων κεφαλὴν ἔνδον, τὰ σκέλη δ᾽ ἔξω.  
> (Having his head inside, and his legs outside.)

- Read *ἡ μᾶλλον ἄνω πάσχουσ’ … ψυχή* together: ‘the soul suffering more above’. The speech tag intervenes between the attributive participle (*πάσχουσ’*, elided *πάσχουσα*, pres. act. nom. fem. sg. of *πάσχω*) and its noun. The article replaces the demonstrative *Quell’*, *ἄνω* gives *lassù*, and *che ha maggior pena* becomes ‘suffering more’, with comparative adverb *μᾶλλον*, not a grammatical superlative.
- *εἶπε Προστάτης οὑμός* ‘said my protector’ uses the 3rd sg. second aorist active of *λέγω*. *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*: the Greek supplies a first-person possessive absent from *il maestro*. In *ψυχή ᾽στιν Ἰούδας Σκαριώτης*, *᾽στιν* shows loss of the initial vowel of *ἐστίν* after *ψυχή* (prodelision); the two names are predicate nominatives.
- *ἔχων* (pres. act. participle, nom. masc. sg., of *ἔχω*) agrees with *Ἰούδας*, not *ψυχή*, and replaces the relative *che … ha*. The one participle serves both *κεφαλὴν ἔνδον* and *τὰ σκέλη δ’ ἔξω*, ‘having his head inside, but his legs outside’. *σκέλη* is accusative neuter plural of *σκέλος*; *δ’* is elided *δέ*. The opposed positions are retained, the movement of *mena* omitted.

> 64 Degli altri due ch' hanno il capo di sotto,  
> 65 quei che pende dal nero ceffo è Bruto:  

Of the other two, who have their heads beneath, that one, who hangs from the black visage is Brutus:

> 64 „ Τῶν δ᾽ ἄλλων δύο, τῶν τὴν κεφαλὴν κάτω  
> (And of the other two, who their head downward)
>
> 65 „ Ἐχόντων, ὁ μὲν μελάγχρους ἐστὶ Βροῦτος·  
> (Have, the swarthy one is Brutus;)

- *Τῶν δ’ ἄλλων δύο* is a partitive genitive, anticipating *ὁ μέν* here and *ὁ δέ* in line 67. *δύο* is left unchanged; the article, adjective and following participle are plural, not dual. The repeated article in *τῶν τὴν κεφαλὴν κάτω ἐχόντων* introduces an attributive participle (pres. act. gen. masc. pl. of *ἔχω*) for *ch’ hanno*. The accusative singular *τὴν κεφαλήν* is distributive, as is *il capo*: one head apiece.
- *ὁ μὲν μελάγχρους ἐστὶ Βροῦτος* ‘the dark-complexioned one is Brutus’ changes the attachment of the description. *μελάγχρους* (contracted nom. masc. sg. of *μελάγχροος*) characterizes the man identified by the predicate nominative *Βροῦτος*; in *quei che pende dal nero ceffo* it is the face from which he hangs that is black. Neither the hanging nor the face has a separate Greek equivalent.

> 66 vedi come si storce, e non fa motto;  

see how he writhes himself, and utters not a word;

> 66 „ Βλέπε πῶς λυγίζεται, λέξιν οὐ λέγων·  
> (See how he writhes, uttering not a word;)

- *Βλέπε* (2nd sg. present active imperative of *βλέπω*) introduces *πῶς λυγίζεται* ‘how he twists’, following *vedi come si storce*. The present middle/passive indicative of *λυγίζω* (3rd sg.) conveys the Italian reflexive.
- *λέξιν οὐ λέγων* ‘not saying a word’ subordinates *e non fa motto* as a simultaneous circumstance. *λέγων* is a present active participle, nominative masculine singular, with the cognate accusative *λέξιν* placed first; *οὐ* negatives the action as a matter of fact.

> 67 e l' altro è Cassio, che par sì membruto.  

and the other is Cassius, who seems so stark of limb.

> 67 „ Ὁ δὲ Κάσσιος, δοκῶν πιμελὴς οὕτως.  
> (And the other is Cassius, seeming so stout.)

- *ὁ δὲ Κάσσιος* ‘and the other, Cassius’ completes *ὁ μέν … ὁ δέ*; *ἐστί* is understood. *δοκῶν* (contracted pres. act. participle, nom. masc. sg., of *δοκέω*) replaces the finite relative *che par*. *πιμελής* is predicative, ‘fat’ or ‘stout’, not specifically well-limbed as *membruto* suggests; *οὕτως* ‘so’ follows rather than precedes the adjective.

> 68 Ma la notte risurge; ed oramai  
> 69 è da partir, chè tutto avem veduto.”  

But night is reascending; and now must we depart: for we have seen the whole.”

> 68 „ Ἀλλ᾽ ἡ νὺξ ἐπάνεισι· νῦν δ᾽ ἡμῖν ὥρα  
> (But night is returning; and now for us it is time)
>
> 69 „ Ἐστὶν ἀπελθεῖν· εἴδομεν γὰρ τὰ πάντα.”  
> (To depart; for we have seen everything.")

- *Ἀλλ’ ἡ νὺξ ἐπάνεισι* closely follows *Ma la notte risurge*. *ἀλλ’* is elided *ἀλλά*; *ἐπάνεισι* is the 3rd sg. present indicative of *ἐπάνειμι* (compound of *εἶμι*, ‘go’), ‘comes up again’. *νῦν δ’* gives the transition *ed oramai*.
- *ἡμῖν ὥρα ἐστὶν ἀπελθεῖν* ‘it is time for us to depart’ makes explicit both the time and the persons concerned. The dative indicates for whom it is time; *ἀπελθεῖν* is the second aorist active infinitive of *ἀπέρχομαι*, presenting departure as a single act. *εἴδομεν γὰρ τὰ πάντα* uses the 1st pl. second aorist active of *ὁράω* for the compound perfect *avem veduto*, and an articular neuter plural ‘all things’ for *tutto*.

> 70 Com' a lui piacque, il collo gli avvinghiai;  

As he desired, I clasped his neck;

> 70 Ἐπ᾽ αὐχέν᾽ αὐτοῦ κελεύσαντος ἠρτήθην·  
> (Upon his neck, as he bade me, I clung;)

- *αὐτοῦ κελεύσαντος* ‘when he bade [me]’ is a genitive absolute, with the aorist active participle (gen. masc. sg.) of *κελεύω*: the Italian clause *Com’ a lui piacque* expresses his pleasure rather than a command. The absolute is interposed between *ἐπ’ αὐχέν’* and its verb; *αὐτοῦ* supplies the participle's subject, while whose neck is meant can be inferred.
- *ἐπ’ αὐχέν’ … ἠρτήθην* ‘I hung on [his] neck’ replaces transitive *il collo gli avvinghiai* with *ἐπί* and the accusative (*αὐχέν’* for *αὐχένα*, from *αὐχήν*). Both preposition and noun undergo elision. *ἠρτήθην* is formally the 1st sg. aorist passive indicative of *ἀρτάω*, used of attaching oneself.

> 71 ed ei prese di tempo e loco poste,  
> 72 e, quando l' ale furo aperte assai,  
> 73 appigliò sè alle vellute coste;  

and he took opportunity of time and place; and when the wings were opened far, applied him to the shaggy sides,

> 71 Ὁ δ᾽ ἐκλέξας πρόσφορον καιρὸν καὶ τρόπον,  
> (And he, choosing a fitting time and manner,)
>
> 72 Ἅμα τῶν πτερῶν ἀνοιχθέντων ἀρκούντως,  
> (As soon as the wings were opened wide enough,)
>
> 73 Εἰς τὰς τριχώδεις προσηρτήθη λαγόνας·  
> (Clung fast to the shaggy flanks;)

- *ὁ δ’ ἐκλέξας πρόσφορον καιρὸν καὶ τρόπον* ‘and he, having chosen a suitable moment and manner’ subordinates the finite *ei prese … poste* to the eventual verb of attachment. *ὁ δέ* resumes the other subject; *ἐκλέξας* is the aorist active participle, nom. masc. sg., of *ἐκλέγω*. *καιρός* itself denotes an opportune time; *τρόπος* gives manner or means rather than the place expressed by *loco*.
- *ἅμα τῶν πτερῶν ἀνοιχθέντων ἀρκούντως* renders *quando l’ ale furo aperte assai* by a genitive absolute qualified by *ἅμα* ‘as soon as’. *ἀνοιχθέντων* is the aorist passive participle of *ἀνοίγω*, gen. neut. pl. agreeing with *τῶν πτερῶν*; *ἀρκούντως* interprets *assai* as ‘sufficiently’.
- *εἰς τὰς τριχώδεις … λαγόνας* ‘to the hairy flanks’ uses *εἰς* with the accusative of the point of attachment. The adjective describes hair rather than the velvet-like texture of *vellute*; *λαγόνας* is the accusative plural of *λαγών*. *προσηρτήθη* (3rd sg. aorist passive indicative of *προσαρτάω*, augmented after the prefix) answers to reflexive *appigliò sè* and echoes *ἠρτήθην* in line 70.

> 74 di vello in vello giù discese poscia  
> 75 tra il folto pelo e le gelate croste.  

and then from shag to shag descended down, between the tangled hair and frozen crusts.

> 74 Εἶτ᾽ εἰς μαλλὸν ἐκ μαλλοῦ κατέβη κάτω  
> (Then from tuft to tuft descended downward)
>
> 75 Μέσον τριχὸς δασείας πλακῶν τ᾽ ἐκ πάγου.  
> (Between the thick hair and the slabs of ice.)

- *Εἶτ’* (elided *εἶτα*) moves *poscia* to the front. *εἰς μαλλὸν ἐκ μαλλοῦ* reverses the order of *di vello in vello*: goal in the accusative first, source in the genitive second. *κατέβη κάτω* keeps the directional repetition of *giù discese*, in reverse order; *κατέβη* is the 3rd sg. root aorist active indicative of *καταβαίνω*.
- *μέσον* is adverbial, functioning as a preposition with two genitives for *tra*. *τριχὸς δασείας* ‘of thick hair’ uses *θρίξ* in the collective singular; *πλακῶν τ’ ἐκ πάγου* ‘and of slabs of ice’ replaces *gelate* by *ἐκ* with a genitive of material. *πλακῶν* is gen. pl. of *πλάξ*; postpositive *τε* is elided before *ἐκ*.

> 76 Quando noi fummo là dove la coscia  
> 77 si volge appunto in sul grosso dell' anche,  

When we had come to where the thigh revolves just on the swelling of the haunch,

> 76 Ἐπεὶ δὲ κατήλθομεν ἔνθα πρὸς τἄνω  
> (And when we came down to where at the top)
>
> 77 Τῶν ἰσχίων ὁ μηρὸς προστρέπεταί πως,  
> (Of the haunches the thigh turns as it were,)

- *Ἐπεὶ δὲ κατήλθομεν ἔνθα* ‘and when we came down to where’ gives *Quando noi fummo là dove* a verb of downward motion. *κατήλθομεν* is 1st pl. second aorist active indicative of *κατέρχομαι*; the ending suffices for *noi*. Relative adverb *ἔνθα* needs no separate antecedent for *là*.
- *πρὸς τἄνω τῶν ἰσχίων* ‘towards the upper parts of the hips’ precedes *ὁ μηρός* ‘the thigh’. *τἄνω* is crasis of *τὰ ἄνω*: the article substantivizes the adverb, in the accusative after *πρός*, with a partitive genitive. Upper position thus takes the place of the bulk in *sul grosso dell’ anche*.
- *προστρέπεταί πως* (3rd sg. present middle/passive indicative of *προστρέπω*) corresponds to reflexive *si volge*. Enclitic *πως* ‘somehow, as it were’ accounts for the extra accent on the verb and qualifies the turn less precisely than *appunto*.

> 78 lo duca con fatica e con angoscia  
> 79 volse la testa ov' egli avea le zanche,  

my Guide with labour and with difficulty turned his head where he had had his feet before,

> 78 Οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ ἐν κόπῳ κἀγωνίᾳ  
> (My Guide, with toil and struggle,)
>
> 79 Θεὶς τὴν κεφαλὴν ἔνθα τοὺς πόδας εἶχεν,  
> (Placing his head where he had had his feet,)

- *Οὑμὸς Ἡγήτωρ* ‘my guide’ adds the possessive to *lo duca*, with the same crasis as in line 62. *ἐν κόπῳ κἀγωνίᾳ* ‘in toil and struggle’ renders *con fatica e con angoscia* by one preposition governing both datives; *κἀγωνίᾳ* is crasis of *καὶ ἀγωνίᾳ*.
- *θεὶς τὴν κεφαλήν* ‘having placed his head’ converts finite *volse* into a preparatory action dependent on line 80. *θείς* is the masculine nominative singular second aorist active participle of *τίθημι*. *ἔνθα τοὺς πόδας εἶχεν* ‘where he had his feet’ mirrors *ov’ egli avea le zanche*, using the ordinary word for feet and the 3rd sg. imperfect active of *ἔχω*, not a pluperfect.

> 80 ed aggrappossi al pel come uom che sale,  
> 81 sì che in inferno io credea tornar anche.  

and grappled on the hair, as one who mounts; so that I thought we were returning into Hell again.

> 80 Εἷλε τῶν τριχῶν, ὥς τις πηδῶν ἐφ᾽ ἵππον,  
> (Grasped the hair, like one leaping upon a horse,)
>
> 81 Ὥστ᾽ ᾠόμην εἰς ᾍδην ἐπανακάμπτειν.  
> (So that I thought we were turning back into Hell.)

- *Εἷλε τῶν τριχῶν* ‘he caught hold of the hairs’ makes this the finite verb after *θείς*. *εἷλε* is the 3rd sg. second aorist active indicative of *αἱρέω*, from its suppletive aorist *εἷλον*. The genitive plural of *θρίξ* denotes that of which he takes hold, in place of Italian reflexive *aggrappossi* with *al pel*.
- *ὥς τις πηδῶν ἐφ’ ἵππον* ‘like someone leaping onto a horse’ gives *come uom che sale* a specifically equestrian interpretation. Indefinite *τις* and the agreeing present active participle *πηδῶν* (nom. masc. sg. of *πηδάω*) replace noun and relative clause. *ἐφ’* is *ἐπί* elided and aspirated before *ἵππον*, accusative of the goal.
- *ὥστ’ ᾠόμην* introduces an actual consequence with *ὥστε* (elided) and the indicative: ‘so that I thought’. *ᾠόμην* is the 1st sg. imperfect middle of deponent *οἴομαι*. Its complementary present active infinitive *ἐπανακάμπτειν* includes the sense of turning back expressed by *tornar anche*; with no separate accusative subject, it most naturally shares the subject ‘I’ of *ᾠόμην*. *εἰς ᾍδην* makes *in inferno* explicitly directional.

> 82 “Attienti ben, chè per sì fatte scale,”  
> 83 disse il maestro ansando com' uom lasso,  
> 84 “conviensi dipartir da tanto male.”  

“Hold thee fast! for by such stairs,” said my Guide, panting like a man forespent, “must we depart from so much ill.”

> 82 “Πρόσχες νῦν·” εἶπεν ὁ Προστάτης ἀσθμαίνων,  
> ("Hold fast now," said the Master, panting,)
>
> 83 Ὡς ἄνθρωπος κεκμηκώς, “διὰ κλιμάκων  
> (Like a man exhausted, "by ladders)
>
> 84 „ Τοιῶνδ᾽ ἀπαλλαγῆναι δεινῶν δεῖ τῶνδε.”  
> (Such as these we must escape from these horrors.")

- *Πρόσχες νῦν* ‘pay heed now’ gives *Attienti ben* as a warning rather than a reflexive command to cling. *πρόσχες* is the 2nd sg. second aorist active imperative of *προσέχω*; *νῦν* supplies urgency rather than translating *ben* as an adverb of manner. The speech tag comes immediately after the command; Musurus postpones the stairs phrase and does not reproduce causal *chè* by a conjunction.
- *εἶπεν ὁ Προστάτης ἀσθμαίνων* follows *disse il maestro ansando*, with a present active participle (nom. masc. sg. of *ἀσθμαίνω*) for the simultaneous action. In *ὡς ἄνθρωπος κεκμηκώς*, *κεκμηκώς* is the perfect active participle of *κάμνω*, nominative masculine singular: where *lasso* is an adjective, the Greek perfect expresses the lasting state of having grown weary.
- *διὰ κλιμάκων τοιῶνδ’* ‘by means of stairs of this sort’ renders *per sì fatte scale* with *διά* and the genitive of means. *τοιῶνδ’* is elided *τοιῶνδε*, gen. fem. pl. of *τοιόσδε*, agreeing back across the line break with *κλιμάκων* (from *κλίμαξ*).
- *ἀπαλλαγῆναι δεινῶν δεῖ τῶνδε* ‘one must get free of these horrors’ retains the impersonal necessity of *conviensi dipartir*. *δεῖ* (3rd sg. present indicative) governs the second aorist passive infinitive *ἀπαλλαγῆναι* of *ἀπαλλάσσω*, here intransitive in sense. The genitive of separation *δεινῶν … τῶνδε* is split by *δεῖ*: the substantivized neuter plural and its demonstrative replace the measure and singular of *da tanto male*.

## Lines 85–117 — Passing the Centre

> 85 Poi uscì fuor per lo foro d' un sasso,  
> 86 e pose me in su l'orlo a sedere;  
> 87 appresso porse a me l' accorto passo.  

Thereafter through the opening of a rock he issued forth, and put me on its brim to sit; then towards me he stretched his wary step.

> 85 Εἶτα δι᾽ ὀπῆς ἐξελθών τινος πέτρας,  
> (Then coming out through an opening of a rock,)
>
> 86 Καὶ καθίσας μ᾽ ἀτρέμας ἐπὶ τοῦ χείλους,  
> (And seating me gently upon its rim,)
>
> 87 Ἔθετ᾽ ἐγγύς μου τὸν φυλακτικὸν πόδα.  
> (He set beside me his watchful foot.)

- *Εἶτα* renders *Poi*, but of the three successive Italian finite verbs only the last remains finite. *Ἐξελθών* (nom. sg. masc., second aor. act. participle of *ἐξέρχομαι*) gives *uscì fuor* in one verb, ‘having come out’. *Δι᾽ ὀπῆς* is *διά* elided before a vowel, with the genitive of passage; *τινος πέτρας*, ‘of a certain rock’, belongs to *ὀπῆς* despite the intervening participle.
- *Καθίσας μ᾽* (aor. act. participle of *καθίζω*, with elided *με*) means ‘having seated me’: the transitive verb absorbs both *pose* and *a sedere*. *Ἀτρέμας*, ‘steadily, gently’, is supplied; *ἐπὶ τοῦ χείλους* with the genitive describes the position on the rim.
- *Ἔθετ᾽* is elided *ἔθετο*, 3rd sg. second aor. mid. ind. of *τίθημι*, ‘he set his foot’. *Ἐγγύς μου*, ‘near me’, with genitive, makes *appresso … a me* spatial rather than marking another ‘then’. *Τὸν φυλακτικὸν πόδα* transfers the watchfulness of *l’accorto passo* to the foot itself, accusative object of *ἔθετο*.

> 88 Io levai gli occhi, e credetti vedere  
> 89 Lucifero com' io l' avea lasciato,  

I raised my eyes, and thought to see Lucifer as I had left him;

> 88 Ὄμματ᾽ ἔτεινα, καί μοι βλέπειν ἐδόκουν  
> (I lifted up my eyes, and thought I saw)
>
> 89 Τὸν Ἑωσφόρον οἷον ἔλιπον πρῶτον·  
> (Lucifer just as I had left him at first;)

- *Ὄμματ᾽ ἔτεινα*, ‘I stretched forth my eyes’, fronts the object (elided neut. acc. pl. *ὄμματα*); *ἔτεινα* is 1st sg. aor. act. ind. of *τείνω*. Unlike *levai*, the Greek verb does not itself specify ‘up’.
- *Μοι βλέπειν ἐδόκουν*, ‘I seemed to myself to see’, makes *credetti* imperfect (*ἐδόκουν*, contracted from *ἐδόκεον*, here 1st sg. of *δοκέω*), with dative of the judge and present active infinitive. It is personal, not the impersonal *ἐδόκει μοι*: the subject of ‘seem’ is also the one seeing.
- *Τὸν Ἑωσφόρον*, ‘the dawn-bringer’, is the object of *βλέπειν*. *Οἷον* is masc. acc. sg., predicative, ‘such as [I left him]’; *ἔλιπον* (1st sg. second aor. act. of *λείπω*) replaces the Italian pluperfect. The added *πρῶτον*, ‘at first’, marks the earlier occasion.

> 90 e vidili le gambe in su tenere;  

and saw him with the legs turned upwards;

> 90 Εἶδον δ᾽ αὐτὸν ἔχοντα τοὺς πόδας ἄνω.  
> (And I saw him with his feet upward.)

- *Εἶδον δ᾽ αὐτόν* replaces the second *e* with mildly adversative, postpositive *δέ*, elided: ‘but I saw him’. After *εἶδον* (1st sg. second aorist of *ὁράω*), *ἔχοντα* (pres. act. participle, masc. acc. sg. of *ἔχω*) agrees with the person perceived, where Italian has *tenere* in the infinitive. *Τοὺς πόδας ἄνω* is ‘his feet up’, with the article for the possessor and an adverb, not an adjective agreeing with ‘feet’.

> 91 e s' io divenni allora travagliato,  
> 92 la gente grossa il pensi, che non vede  
> 93 qual è quel punto ch' io avea passato.  

and the gross people who see not what [that] point [is] which I had passed, [let them] judge if I grew [perplexed] then.

> 91 Ὡς δ᾽ ὅλος ἐξεστηκὼς τότ᾽ ἐγενόμην,  
> (And how utterly bewildered I then became,)
>
> 92 Λογισάσθωσαν οἱ χύδην, μὴ νοοῦντες  
> (Let the common folk reckon, who do not understand)
>
> 93 Διὰ τίνος σημείου διῆλθον ἄρτι.  
> (Through what point I had just passed.)

- *Ὡς … ὅλος ἐξεστηκὼς τότ᾽ ἐγενόμην*, ‘how completely beside myself I then became’, turns *s’ io divenni … travagliato* into an exclamation of degree dependent on ‘let them reckon’. *Ὅλος* agrees with the speaker but qualifies his state as a whole; *ἐξεστηκώς* is the intransitive perfect active participle of *ἐξίστημι*, nom. sg. masc., denoting the resulting state. *Τότ᾽* is *τότε*; *ἐγενόμην* is 1st sg. second aor. mid. of *γίγνομαι*.
- *Λογισάσθωσαν* is 3rd pl. aor. mid. imperative of *λογίζομαι*, with the preceding clause as its object in place of the Italian resumptive *il*. *Οἱ χύδην* substantivizes an adverb, ‘those of the common mass’, replacing collective singular *la gente grossa*. Hence the plural *μὴ νοοῦντες* (pres. act. participle of *νοέω*), ‘not comprehending’: *μή* characterizes the class, and intellectual apprehension replaces *vede*.
- *Διὰ τίνος σημείου*, ‘through what point’, is an indirect question with interrogative *τίνος*, unlike indefinite *τινος* in 85. It telescopes *qual è quel punto ch’*: there is no copula, demonstrative, or relative clause. *Διῆλθον* (1st sg. second aor. act. of *διέρχομαι*) replaces the pluperfect, with added *ἄρτι*, ‘just now’.

> 94 “Levati su,” disse il maestro, “in piede:  

“Rise up!” said the Master, “upon thy feet:

> 94 “Ἀνάστηθ᾽ ἐπὶ ποδῶν,” εἶπε Προστάτης,  
> ("Rise up upon your feet," said the Master,)

- *Ἀνάστηθ᾽ ἐπὶ ποδῶν* keeps the command together before the reporting clause. *Ἀνάστηθ᾽* is elided *ἀνάστηθι*, 2nd sg. imperative of the intransitive root aorist of *ἀνίστημι*, not a passive; it needs no counterpart to reflexive *-ti* in *Levati*. *Ἐπὶ ποδῶν* has the genitive plural ‘on foot’ against Italian singular *in piede*. In *εἶπε Προστάτης* the anarthrous title is the nominative subject: ‘guide’ or ‘protector’ for *il maestro*.

> 95 la via è lunga e il cammino è malvagio,  
> 96 e già il sole a mezza terza riede.”  

the way is long, and difficult the road; and [already] to middle tierce the Sun returns.”

> 95 “Ἡ τρίβος μακρά, τραχεῖα δ’ ἡ πορεία·  
> ("The path is long, and rough is the journey;)
>
> 96 „ Ὁ δ’ ἥλιος ἐπάνεισ’ ἡμιτριταῖος.”  
> (And the sun is returning to half-tierce.")

- *Ἡ τρίβος μακρά, τραχεῖα δ’ ἡ πορεία* omits both copulas. The predicate adjectives frame the two clauses: ‘the track [is] long, rough [is] the journey’. *Τραχεῖα* (fem. nom. sg. of *τραχύς*) selects the practical sense of *malvagio*, not a moral one; elided *δέ* links the clauses.
- *Ὁ δ’ ἥλιος ἐπάνεισ’* continues with another *δέ*. *Ἐπάνεισ’* is elided *ἐπάνεισι*, 3rd sg. of *ἐπάνειμι* ‘go back’, from *εἶμι* ‘go’, not *εἰμί* ‘be’. *Ἡμιτριταῖος* is a predicative adjective agreeing with the sun: Musurus compresses *a mezza terza* into a temporal description of the subject. There is no separate *già*.

> 97 Non era caminata di palagio  
> 98 là 'v' eravam, ma natural burella,  
> 99 ch' avea mal suolo e di lume disagio.  

It was no palace-hall, there where we stood, but natural dungeon with an evil floor and want of light.

> 97 Οὐκ ἦν τις περίπατος ἀνακτορίων,  
> (It was no promenade of a palace,)
>
> 98 Ἔνθ’ ἡμεῖς ἦμεν, ἀλλὰ φυσικὴ φάραγξ  
> (Where we were, but a natural cleft)
>
> 99 Ἐκ γῆς τραχείας φωτός τ’ ἐστερημένης.  
> (Of rough ground and deprived of light.)

- *Οὐκ ἦν τις περίπατος ἀνακτορίων* gives ‘it was not a palace walk’: *τις* is indefinite, *περίπατος* a place for walking rather than a hall, and *ἀνακτορίων* the genitive plural of *ἀνακτόριον*, dependent on it. *Ἔνθ’ ἡμεῖς ἦμεν*, ‘where we were’, has *ἔνθα* elided before *ἡμεῖς*; the explicit pronoun reinforces the 1st pl. imperfect of *εἰμί*.
- After *ἀλλά*, *φυσικὴ φάραγξ* is a second predicate nominative, ‘a natural cleft’, rather than *burella* in the sense ‘dungeon’. *Ἐκ γῆς τραχείας*, ‘of rough earth’, replaces *ch’ avea mal suolo*: a phrase of material stands for the relative clause of possession.
- *Φωτός τ’ ἐστερημένης* adds ‘and deprived of light’; *τ’* is elided enclitic *τε*, and *φωτός* is the genitive of deprivation with the perfect passive participle of *στερέω*. The actual fem. gen. sg. ending *-μένης* attaches the deprivation grammatically to *γῆς*, not to nominative *φάραγξ*, although it describes the condition within the cleft.

> 100 “Prima ch' io dell' abisso mi divella,  
> 101 maestro mio,” diss' io quando fui dritto,  
> 102 “a trarmi d' erro un poco mi favella.  

“Before I pluck myself from the Abyss,” said I when risen up, “O Master! speak to me a little, to draw me out of error.

> 100 “Πρὶν ἀπαλλαχθῶ τῆς ἀβύσσου τῆσδ’,” εἶπον,  
> ("Before I depart from this abyss," I said,)
>
> 101 Ὅτ’ ἀνωρθώθην, “μικρὸν δή τί μοι φράσον,  
> (When I had stood upright, "tell me just a little,)
>
> 102 „ Ἵν’ ἐξαγάγῃς μ’, ὦ Προστάτα, τῆς πλάνης·  
> (To lead me out of error, O Master:)

- *Πρὶν ἀπαλλαχθῶ* renders *Prima ch’ … mi divella* with *πρίν* and the 1st sg. aor. pass. subjunctive of *ἀπαλλάσσω*, intransitive in sense, ‘before I get free’. The bare subjunctive follows the Italian prospective subordinate clause closely; a *πρίν* with infinitive would be more usual in Classical prose here. *Τῆς ἀβύσσου τῆσδ’* is a genitive of separation; elided *τῆσδε* adds ‘this’ after the articular noun.
- *Εἶπον, ὅτ’ ἀνωρθώθην* moves ‘I said’ to the first line and keeps the temporal clause beside it. *Ὅτ’* is *ὅτε*; *ἀνωρθώθην* (1st sg. aor. pass. of *ἀνορθόω*) gives ‘when I was raised upright’ for the state *quando fui dritto*.
- *Μικρὸν δή τί μοι φράσον*, ‘do tell me some little thing’, turns adverbial *un poco* into a neuter accusative object (*μικρόν* with indefinite *τι*); *δή* presses the request. *Φράσον* is 2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *φράζω*, with dative recipient *μοι*. *Ὦ Προστάτα* delays *maestro mio* until the purpose clause and omits the possessive.
- *Ἵν’ ἐξαγάγῃς μ’ … τῆς πλάνης* replaces *a trarmi d’ erro* with finite *ἵνα* and subjunctive: *ἐξαγάγῃς* is 2nd sg. second aor. act. of *ἐξάγω*, with elided *με* as object and the genitive of what one is led out of.

> 103 Ov' è la ghiaccia? e questi com' è fitto  
> 104 sì sottosopra? e come in sì poc' ora  
> 105 da sera a mane ha fatto il sol tragitto? ”  

Where is the ice? and this, how is he fixed thus upside down? and how, in so short a time, has the Sun from eve to morn made transit?”

> 103 „ Ποῦ ’στιν ὁ πάγος ; οὗτος δὲ πῶς κατέστη  
> (Where is the ice? and how has he been set)
>
> 104 „ Οὕτως ἐπίκαρ ; πῶς τ’ ἐξ ἑσπέρας ἔβη  
> (Thus upside down? and how from evening has passed)
>
> 105 „ Ἥλιος ἐν ὀλίγαις ὥραις πρὸς ἕω ;”  
> (The sun in a few hours to morning?")

- *Ποῦ ’στιν ὁ πάγος;* has prodelision, loss of the initial vowel of *ἐστίν* after *ποῦ*. Masculine *ὁ πάγος* replaces feminine *la ghiaccia*. *Οὗτος δὲ πῶς κατέστη* preserves prominent demonstrative *questi*, but *κατέστη* (3rd sg. intransitive root aor. act. of *καθίστημι*) asks how he came to stand so, not how he ‘is fixed’.
- *Οὕτως ἐπίκαρ* renders *sì sottosopra* by two adverbs; poetic *ἐπίκαρ* means ‘crosswise, athwart’, less specifically ‘upside down’ than the Italian. *Πῶς τ’* introduces the coordinated question with elided *τε*.
- *Ἐξ ἑσπέρας ἔβη / Ἥλιος … πρὸς ἕω* sets the starting point before the verb and the goal last. *Ἔβη* is 3rd sg. root aor. act. of *βαίνω*, ‘went’, for *ha fatto … tragitto*. *Ἥλιος* is anarthrous subject; *ἕω* is the Attic accusative singular of *ἕως* ‘dawn’, after directional *πρός*. *Ἐν ὀλίγαις ὥραις* (dative plural) counts ‘a few hours’ rather than measuring *sì poc’ ora* in the singular.

> 106 Ed egli a me: “Tu immagini ancora  
> 107 d' esser di là dal centro, ov' io m' appresi  
> 108 al pel del vermo reo, che il mondo fora.  

And he to me: “Thou imaginest that thou art still upon the other side of the centre, where I caught hold on the hair of the evil Worm which pierces through the world.

> 106 Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ· “Σὺ τάχα φρονεῖς ἔτι  
> (And he to me: "You perhaps still imagine)
>
> 107 „ Εἶναι τοῦ κέντρου πέραν, ἔνθ’ ἐδραξάμην  
> (You are beyond the centre, where I grasped)
>
> 108 „ Τριχῶν σκώληκος τὴν γῆν διατρυπῶντος.  
> (The hairs of the worm that bores through the earth.)

- *Ὁ δὲ πρὸς ἐμέ* closely matches verbless *Ed egli a me*: *ὁ δέ* is pronominal, ‘and he’, with *πρός* and accusative for the addressee. *Σὺ τάχα φρονεῖς ἔτι* retains emphatic *Tu* but adds the qualification *τάχα*, ‘perhaps’. *Εἶναι* is the present infinitive of *εἰμί*, its subject understood from *φρονεῖς* as in *immagini … d’ esser*.
- *Τοῦ κέντρου πέραν* puts adverbial *πέραν* after the genitive it governs, ‘beyond the centre’. *Ἔνθ’ ἐδραξάμην* resumes the place with elided *ἔνθα*; *ἐδραξάμην* (1st sg. aor. mid. of *δράσσομαι*) takes the genitive of the thing grasped, *τριχῶν*, plural ‘hairs’ for collective *pel*.
- *Σκώληκος* (gen. sg. of *σκώληξ*) depends on *τριχῶν*; *reo* has no equivalent. *Τὴν γῆν διατρυπῶντος*, ‘boring through the earth’, replaces the finite relative clause *che il mondo fora* with a present active participle of *διατρυπάω*, masc. gen. sg. agreeing with *σκώληκος*, and interprets *mondo* as earth.

> 109 Di là fosti cotanto, quant' io scesi;  

Thou wast on that side, so long as I descended;

> 109 „ Ἐκεῖ μὲν σύ γ’ ἦς, ὅτε κατέβην· ὅμως  
> (There indeed you were, while I descended; yet)

- *Ἐκεῖ μὲν σύ γ’ ἦς*, ‘there, to be sure, you were’, renders *Di là fosti* with a locative adverb and the 2nd sg. imperfect of *εἰμί*. *Μέν* prepares a contrast; elided *γε* focuses *σύ*. *Ὅτε κατέβην* (1st sg. root aor. act. of *καταβαίνω*) says ‘when I descended’ rather than reproducing the correlation of duration *cotanto, quant’*. Line-final *ὅμως*, ‘yet’, already introduces the next clause.

> 110 quando mi volsi, tu passasti il punto  
> 111 al qual si traggon d' ogni parte i pesi;  

when I turned myself, thou *then* didst pass the point to which all gravities from every part are drawn;

> 110 „ Ὁπότ’ ἐστράφην, διέβης τὸ σημεῖον,  
> (When I turned round, you crossed the point)
>
> 111 „ Ἐφ’ ὅπερ ῥέπει πανταχόθεν τὰ βάρη.  
> (Toward which weights incline from every side.)

- *Ὁπότ’ ἐστράφην* has elided *ὁπότε* with the indicative of a definite past occasion, not an indefinite temporal clause. *Ἐστράφην* (1st sg. second aor. pass. of *στρέφω*) is intransitive in sense, ‘I turned’, for *mi volsi*. *Διέβης* (2nd sg. root aor. act. of *διαβαίνω*) directly governs accusative *τὸ σημεῖον*; no separate *tu* is needed.
- *Ἐφ’ ὅπερ* is *ἐπί* elided and aspirated before the strengthened relative *ὅπερ* (neut. acc. sg., antecedent *σημεῖον*), ‘towards which precisely’. Active *ῥέπει* ‘inclines’ replaces *si traggon*: the verb is 3rd sg. present, regularly singular with neuter plural subject *τὰ βάρη* (from *βάρος*). *Πανταχόθεν* expresses *d’ ogni parte* in one adverb of origin, ‘from every side’.

> 112 e se' or sotto l' emisperio giunto,  
> 113 ch' è contrapposto a quel che la gran secca  
> 114 coperchia, e sotto il cui colmo consunto  
> 115 fu l' uom che nacque e visse senza pecca;  

and now thou art arrived beneath the hemisphere opposed to that which canopies the great dry *land*, and underneath whose summit was consumed the Man, who without sin was born and lived;

> 112 „ Ἐν τῷ κάτω δ’ εἶ σὺ νῦν ἡμισφαιρίῳ,  
> (And now you are in the lower hemisphere,)
>
> 113 „ Ὅπερ ἔστ’ ἀντίθετον πρὸς τὸ καλύπτον  
> (Which is opposite to that which covers)
>
> 114 „ Τὴν ὅλην ξηράν, ἔνθα σταυρὸν ὑπέστη  
> (All the dry land, where the cross was endured by)
>
> 115 „ Ὁ γεννηθεὶς καὶ ζήσας ἀναμαρτήτως.  
> (Him who was born and lived without sin.)

- *Ἐν τῷ κάτω δ’ εἶ σὺ νῦν ἡμισφαιρίῳ* replaces *se’ … sotto l’emisperio giunto* with present location: ‘you are now **in** the lower hemisphere’. Adverb *κάτω* is attributive between article and noun; *ἡμισφαιρίῳ* is neut. dat. sg. after *ἐν*, separated from its article by connective *δέ*, verb, pronoun and adverb.
- *Ὅπερ ἔστ’ ἀντίθετον* has strengthened relative *ὅπερ*, here nominative, with elided *ἐστί* and a predicate adjective for *ch’ è contrapposto*. *Πρὸς τὸ καλύπτον* gives the term of opposition: the articular present active participle of *καλύπτω* (neut. acc. sg.) stands for *a quel che … coperchia*. Its object *τὴν ὅλην ξηράν* has substantival feminine *ξηράν* ‘dry land’ (understood *γῆν*); *ὅλην* makes *gran* ‘the whole’ rather than ‘great’.
- *Ἔνθα* ‘where’ replaces the more precise *sotto il cui colmo*: neither ‘under’ nor ‘summit’ survives. *Σταυρὸν ὑπέστη*, ‘underwent the cross’, interprets passive *consunto fu* by an active verb with an explicit accusative object. *Ὑπέστη* is 3rd sg. root aor. act. of *ὑφίστημι*.
- The postponed subject *ὁ γεννηθεὶς καὶ ζήσας* replaces *l’uom che nacque e visse* with one article governing two masc. nom. sg. participles: aor. passive of *γεννάω*, ‘was born’, and aor. active of *ζάω*, ‘lived’. There is no noun for ‘man’. Final *ἀναμαρτήτως* renders *senza pecca* by an adverb applying to the paired participles.

> 116 tu hai li piedi in su picciola spera,  
> 117 che l' altra faccia fa della Giudecca.  

thou hast thy feet upon a little sphere, which forms the other face of the Judecca.

> 116 „ Ἔχεις τοὺς πόδας ἐπὶ μικκύλης σφαίρας  
> (You have your feet upon a tiny sphere)
>
> 117 „ Πρὸς τὴν Ἰουδαϊκὴν ἀντικειμένης.  
> (That lies opposite to Judecca.)

- *Ἔχεις τοὺς πόδας* follows *tu hai li piedi*, with 2nd sg. pres. act. of *ἔχω* and the article, not a possessive, before the body part. *Ἐπὶ μικκύλης σφαίρας* uses the genitive for the surface on which the feet rest; *μικκύλης*, fem. gen. sg. of the expressive diminutive *μικκύλος*, sharpens *picciola* to ‘tiny’.
- *Πρὸς τὴν Ἰουδαϊκὴν ἀντικειμένης* is ‘lying opposite the Judaic [region]’. *Ἀντικειμένης* (pres. mid. participle of *ἀντίκειμαι*, fem. gen. sg.) agrees back with *σφαίρας*; *πρός* with the accusative marks what it faces. The articular feminine adjective *Ἰουδαϊκήν* stands for *Giudecca*. Rather than reproduce *fa* and its object *l’altra faccia*, Musurus makes the sphere itself the thing opposed.

## Lines 118–139 — The Fall and the Ascent to the Stars

> 118 Qui è da man, quando di là è sera;  

Here it is morn, when it is evening there;

> 118 „ Ὧδε μὲν ἕως, ἐκεῖ δ᾽ ἐστὶν ἑσπέρα.  
> (Here it is morning, while there it is evening.)

- *Ὧδε μὲν ἕως, ἐκεῖ δ᾽ ἐστὶν ἑσπέρα* ‘here dawn, but there it is evening’ replaces the temporal subordination of *quando* with two balanced assertions. The postpositive particles *μέν … δέ* set *ὧδε* against *ἐκεῖ*; *δ᾽* is elided before *ἐστίν*.
- *ἕως* is here the Attic feminine noun ‘dawn’, nominative singular, not the conjunction ‘until’ found in 137. It balances the nominative *ἑσπέρα*. The single expressed copula, *ἐστίν* (3rd sg. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*), stands in the second clause rather than the first as in *Qui è da man*.

> 119 e questi, che ne fe’ scala col pelo,  
> 120 fitto è ancora, sì come prima era.  

and this Fiend, who made a ladder for us with his hair, is still fixed as he was before.

> 119 „ Ἐκεῖνος δ᾽, οὗπερ ἡ θρὶξ ἦν ἡμῖν κλίμαξ,  
> (And he whose hair was a ladder for us)
>
> 120 „ Ἔτι νῦν βεβύθισται, καθὼς ἦν πρῶτον.  
> (Is even now sunk fast, just as he was at first.)

- *Ἐκεῖνος δ᾽* ‘and that one’ renders *e questi* with a distal rather than a proximal demonstrative. Like the Italian, the Greek has no noun corresponding to “Fiend.” In *οὗπερ ἡ θρὶξ ἦν ἡμῖν κλίμαξ* ‘whose very hair was a ladder for us’, *οὗπερ* is a possessive genitive (masc. sg. of *ὅσπερ*, the relative strengthened by *-περ*). Musurus makes *ἡ θρίξ* ‘the hair’ the subject, rather than the instrument expressed by *col pelo*. The singular is collective.
- The action *ne fe’ scala* becomes a nominal predicate: *ἦν* (3rd sg. imperf. ind. of *εἰμί*) joins *ἡ θρίξ* to the predicate nominative *κλίμαξ*, and *ἡμῖν* is a dative of advantage. *Ἔτι νῦν βεβύθισται* ‘even now he remains sunk’ renders *fitto è ancora* by the resultative perfect: *βεβύθισται* is 3rd sg. perf. mid./pass. ind. of *βυθίζω*, passive in sense.
- *καθὼς ἦν πρῶτον* ‘just as he was at first’ answers to *sì come prima era*. *καθώς* supplies the comparison without a separate equivalent of *sì*; *πρῶτον* is an adverbial neuter accusative.

> 121 Da questa parte cadde giù dal cielo;  

On this side fell he down from Heaven;

> 121 „ Πρὸς μέρος τόδε κατέπεσ᾽ οὐρανόθεν·  
> (Upon this side he fell down from heaven;)

- *Πρὸς μέρος τόδε* ‘toward this side’ retains the initial position of *Da questa parte* but makes direction explicit with *πρός* and the accusative. *τόδε* (neut. acc. sg. of *ὅδε*) agrees with *μέρος*.
- *κατέπεσ᾽* is elided *κατέπεσε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *καταπίπτω*, with augment after the preverb). Its prefix does the work of *giù* in *cadde giù*. The poetic adverb *οὐρανόθεν*, with the suffix *-θεν* ‘from’, renders *dal cielo* without a preposition.

> 122 e la terra, che pria di qua si sporse,  
> 123 per paura di lui fe’ del mar velo,  
> 124 e venne all’ emisperio nostro ; e forse  

and the land, which erst stood out here, through fear of him veiled itself with sea, and came to our hemisphere;

> 122 „ Ἡ δ᾽ ὧδε πρώην ὑψουμένη γῆ, τότε  
> (And the land that formerly rose here, then)
>
> 123 „ Φοβηθεῖσ᾽ αὐτὸν ἐκαλύφθη θαλάσσῃ,  
> (In fear of him covered itself with sea,)
>
> 124 „ Κ᾽ εἰς τὸ παρ᾽ ἡμῖν ἡμισφαίριον ἦλθε.  
> (And came into our hemisphere.)

- *Ἡ δ᾽ ὧδε πρώην ὑψουμένη γῆ* ‘and the land formerly rising here’ encloses the equivalent of *che pria di qua si sporse* between article and noun. *ὑψουμένη* (nom. fem. sg. pres. mid./pass. participle of *ὑψόω*) presents the former elevation as a continuing state rather than a completed act. *πρώην* marks it as past; the supplied *τότε* ‘then’ marks the transition to the finite verbs.
- *Φοβηθεῖσ᾽ αὐτόν* ‘having feared him’ replaces *per paura di lui* with a circumstantial participle of causal force. *φοβηθεῖσ᾽* is elided *φοβηθεῖσα* (nom. fem. sg. aor. pass. participle of *φοβέω*), agreeing with *γῆ*; the passive-form verb in the sense ‘fear’ takes the accusative *αὐτόν*. *ἐκαλύφθη θαλάσσῃ* ‘was covered with sea’ replaces the reflexive veil-making of *fe’ del mar velo* with *ἐκαλύφθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *καλύπτω*) and an instrumental dative.
- *Κ᾽ εἰς τὸ παρ᾽ ἡμῖν ἡμισφαίριον ἦλθε* renders *e venne all’ emisperio nostro*, postponing *ἦλθε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*) until after the destination, *εἰς* with the accusative. *Κ᾽* represents *καί* before a vowel; *παρ᾽* is elided *παρά*. The attributive phrase *παρ᾽ ἡμῖν* ‘on our side’ stands between article and noun in place of the possessive *nostro*.

> 125 per fuggir lui lasciò qui il loco voto  
> 126 quella che appar di qua, e su ricorse.”  

and perhaps, in order to escape from him, that which on this side appears left here the empty space, and upwards rushed."

> 125 „ Φεύγουσα δ᾽ ἴσως αὐτὸν ἔλιπε τόπον  
> (And perhaps fleeing him it left a space)
>
> 126 „ Κενὸν ὧδε, κἀντεῦθεν εἰς ὕψος ἤρθη.” —  
> (Empty here, and thence rose up on high." —)

- *Φεύγουσα δ᾽ ἴσως αὐτόν* ‘and perhaps in fleeing him’ carries *e forse* from the end of Italian 124 into the new clause. *φεύγουσα* (nom. fem. sg. pres. act. participle of *φεύγω*) takes *αὐτόν* as its object. It makes *per fuggir lui* an accompanying or motivating action rather than an expressly final infinitive. Its feminine subject is understood from *γῆ*: Musurus does not translate *quella che appar di qua* as a distinct subject.
- *ἔλιπε τόπον κενὸν ὧδε* ‘left a place empty here’ has *ἔλιπε* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λείπω*) with an anarthrous object, unlike *il loco*. *κενόν* agrees with *τόπον* and can be read predicatively, as *voto* can. *κἀντεῦθεν* is crasis of *καὶ ἐντεῦθεν* ‘and from here’; it supplies an explicit point of departure. *εἰς ὕψος ἤρθη* ‘was raised to a height’ replaces *su ricorse* with a prepositional goal and *ἤρθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *αἴρω*), without a verb of running.

> 127 Luogo è laggiù da Belzebù remoto  
> 128 tanto, quanto la tomba si distende,  

Down there, from Beelzebub as far removed as his tomb extends, is a space,

> 127 Κάτω ᾽στι τόπος, τοῦ Βελζεβοὺλ ἀπέχων  
> (Below there is a place, distant from Beelzebub)
>
> 128 Τοσοῦτον, ὅσον ὁ βυθὸς ἔχει μῆκος·  
> (As far as the abyss extends in length;)

- *Κάτω ᾽στι τόπος* ‘below there is a place’ moves *laggiù* to the front; *᾽στι* shows prodelision, loss of the initial vowel of *ἐστί* after *κάτω*. *τοῦ Βελζεβοὺλ ἀπέχων* substitutes an active participle for *da Belzebù remoto*: *ἀπέχων* (nom. masc. sg. pres. act. of *ἀπέχω*) agrees with *τόπος* and governs the genitive of separation, marked by the article before the indeclinable name.
- *Τοσοῦτον, ὅσον* preserves the degree correlation *tanto, quanto*: the neuter accusatives measure extent. *ὁ βυθὸς ἔχει μῆκος* ‘the abyss has length’ interprets *la tomba* as the depth or abyss and replaces *si distende* with *ἔχει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἔχω*) and its object *μῆκος*. There is no expressed possessive corresponding to “his.”

> 129 che non per vista, ma per suono è noto  
> 130 d’ un ruscelletto, che quivi discende  
> 131 per la buca d’ un sasso, ch’ egli ha roso  
> 132 col corso ch’ egli avvolge e poco pende.  

not known by sight but by the sound of a rivulet descending in it, along the hollow of a rock which it has eaten out with tortuous course and slow declivity.

> 129 Ὥστ᾽ ὄμμασιν ἔκφαντος οὐκ ἔστ᾽, ἀλλ᾽ ἤχῳ  
> (So that it is not visible to eyes, but by the sound)
>
> 130 Ῥύακος, ὅστις ἐκεῖθεν καταβαίνει  
> (Of a streamlet, which descends from there)
>
> 131 Διὰ κρημνοῦ ῥωγάδος, ἣν περιῤῥέων  
> (Through a fissure of a crag, which flowing round)
>
> 132 Διώρυξεν, ὢν μικρὸν ἐγκεκλιμένος.  
> (It has hollowed out, being gently inclined.)

- *Ὥστ᾽* (elided *ὥστε*) takes *che* as consecutive: ‘so that it is not manifest to the eyes, but by sound’. The indicative *ἔστ᾽* (elided *ἔστι*) asserts the result. *ἔκφαντος*, a predicate adjective agreeing with *τόπος*, renders *noto*; *ὄμμασιν* (dat. pl. of *ὄμμα*) names the organs rather than the faculty in *per vista*. *ἤχῳ* (dat. sg. of *ἦχος*) gives the contrasting means of perception.
- *Ῥύακος* (gen. sg. of *ῥύαξ*) depends on *ἤχῳ*, as *d’ un ruscelletto* depends on *suono*, but has no diminutive suffix. *ὅστις* (nom. masc. sg. of *ὅστις*) resumes the stream as subject of *καταβαίνει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *καταβαίνω*). *ἐκεῖθεν* means ‘from there’, making *quivi* a point of departure rather than the location of the descent.
- *Διὰ κρημνοῦ ῥωγάδος* ‘through a cleft of a crag’ uses *διά* with the genitive for *per la buca* and places the dependent *κρημνοῦ* before *ῥωγάδος* (gen. sg. of *ῥωγάς* ‘cleft’). The relative *ἥν* is feminine, referring specifically to the cleft, and accusative as the object of *διώρυξεν* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *διορύσσω* ‘dig through’, with internal augment). The Greek aorist replaces *ha roso* and expresses excavation rather than gnawing.
- *περιῤῥέων* ‘flowing round’ is the nom. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *περιρρέω*, agreeing with the stream. The printed *ῤῥ* marks doubled rho with the traditional breathings. Instead of *col corso ch’ egli avvolge*, the participle itself expresses the manner of excavation. *ὢν μικρὸν ἐγκεκλιμένος* ‘being slightly inclined’ likewise makes the stream the bearer of the slope in *e poco pende*: *ὤν* is the pres. participle of *εἰμί*, *ἐγκεκλιμένος* the perf. mid./pass. participle of *ἐγκλίνω*, both nom. masc. sg.; *μικρόν* is an accusative of degree.

> 133 Lo duca ed io per quel cammino ascoso  
> 134 entrammo a ritornar nel chiaro mondo;  

The Guide and I entered by that hidden road, to return into the bright world;

> 133 Ἡγήτωρ δ᾽ ἐγώ τ᾽ εἰς κρυπτὴν ὁδὸν τήνδε  
> (And the Guide and I into this hidden path)
>
> 134 Εἴσιμεν, εἰς φωτεινὸν νοστοῦντες κόσμον.  
> (Entered, returning to the bright world.)

- *Ἡγήτωρ δ᾽ ἐγώ τ᾽* ‘and guide and I’ has an anarthrous *ἡγήτωρ* for *Lo duca*. *δ᾽* links the sentence to what precedes; *τ᾽* (elided *τε*) attaches *ἐγώ* to the first subject. Though there are two persons, the verb and the participles referring to both are plural, not dual.
- *εἰς κρυπτὴν ὁδὸν τήνδε* ‘into this hidden road’ changes *per quel cammino ascoso* from route to goal and uses the proximal *τήνδε* (fem. acc. sg. of *ὅδε*) for *quel*. *Εἴσιμεν* is 1st pl. pres. act. ind. of *εἴσειμι* ‘go in’, not a past-tense form corresponding to *entrammo*; in context it brings the entry into the narrative present.
- *εἰς φωτεινὸν νοστοῦντες κόσμον* ‘returning to a bright world’ embeds *νοστοῦντες* (nom. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *νοστέω*) between the adjective and noun of its destination phrase. As in 125, an Italian infinitive of purpose (*a ritornar*) becomes a present participle describing the subjects’ action.

> 135 e senza cura aver d’ alcun riposo  
> 136 salimmo suso, ei primo ed io secondo,  

and, without caring for any rest, we mounted up, he first and I second,

> 135 Οὐ φροντίζοντες παύλης ἔβημεν ἄνω,  
> (Taking no thought of rest we climbed upward,)
>
> 136 Αὐτὸς μὲν πρῶτος, ἐγὼ δ᾽ αὐτοῦ κατόπιν,  
> (He first, and I behind him,)

- *Οὐ φροντίζοντες παύλης* ‘taking no thought of rest’ recasts *senza cura aver d’ alcun riposo* as a negative circumstantial participle. *φροντίζοντες* (nom. masc. pl. pres. act. of *φροντίζω*) takes the genitive *παύλης* ‘of cessation, rest’; *οὐ* presents their lack of concern as a fact. Neither *alcun* nor the opening *e* has a separate equivalent.
- *ἔβημεν ἄνω* ‘we went upward’ renders *salimmo suso* with the 1st pl. root aor. act. ind. of *βαίνω* and a directional adverb. *Αὐτὸς μὲν πρῶτος, ἐγὼ δ᾽ αὐτοῦ κατόπιν* distributes the plural subject into two nominatives contrasted by *μέν … δέ*. *πρῶτος* is predicative, as in *ei primo*; *io secondo* becomes ‘I behind him’, with *κατόπιν* used postpositionally with the genitive *αὐτοῦ*.

> 137 tanto ch’ io vidi delle cose belle  
> 138 che porta il ciel, per un pertugio tondo;  

so far that I distinguished through a round opening the beauteous things which Heaven bears;

> 137 Ἕως οὗ τὰ τοὐρανοῦ κατεῖδον κάλλη  
> (Until I beheld the beauties of heaven)
>
> 138 Διὰ χάσματος κυκλοτεροῦς, κἀκεῖθεν  
> (Through a round opening, and from there)

- *Ἕως οὗ … κατεῖδον* ‘until I caught sight of’ turns *tanto ch’ io vidi* from degree and result into a temporal limit. *ἕως οὗ* is a compound temporal connective with the genitive relative *οὗ*; the indicative suits an endpoint actually reached. *κατεῖδον* is here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *καθοράω*, formed on the suppletive stem of *εἶδον*.
- *τὰ τοὐρανοῦ … κάλλη* ‘the beauties of heaven’ replaces the partitive *delle cose belle* with a definite direct object, *τὰ … κάλλη* (acc. neut. pl. of *κάλλος*). The attributive genitive *τοὐρανοῦ* (crasis of *τοῦ οὐρανοῦ*) does duty for the whole relative *che porta il ciel*: the Greek has no verb corresponding to *porta*.
- *Διὰ χάσματος κυκλοτεροῦς* ‘through a round aperture’ follows the verb of seeing, as does *per un pertugio tondo*. *διά* takes the genitive; *κυκλοτεροῦς* is the contracted gen. neut. sg. of the adjective *κυκλοτερής*, agreeing with *χάσματος* (from *χάσμα*). Greek 138 already begins the next clause with *κἀκεῖθεν* (crasis of *καὶ ἐκεῖθεν*), corresponding to *e quindi* in Italian 139.

> 139 e quindi uscimmo a riveder le stelle.  

and thence we issued out, again to see the Stars.

> 139 Ἐξήλθομεν, ἵν᾽ ἴδωμεν πάλιν τἆστρα.  
> (We came forth, to see the stars again.)

- *Ἐξήλθομεν* (1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *ἐξέρχομαι*) resumes the plural after singular *κατεῖδον* and renders *uscimmo*. Here, unlike 125 and 134, Musurus explicitly marks the purpose: *ἵν᾽* (elided *ἵνα*) introduces *ἴδωμεν*, 1st pl. second aor. act. subj. of *ὁράω*, the unaugmented subjunctive corresponding to indicative *εἴδομεν*. The subjunctive is retained after a past main verb rather than shifted to an oblique optative.
- *πάλιν* carries the iterative sense of *ri-* in *riveder* as a separate adverb. *τἆστρα* (crasis of *τὰ ἄστρα*, acc. neut. pl. of *ἄστρον*) preserves both the definite article of *le stelle* and the final position of the object.

## Summary — From Clause to Participle

Musurus often preserves Dante’s sequence of perceptions and actions while changing which of them gets a finite verb. In lines 19–20 he reverses the Italian construction: *fe’ restarmi* becomes *με ἐπισχών* ‘having stopped me’, while *dicendo* becomes *εἶπ’* ‘he said’. A present participle can keep an action simultaneous with the main verb, as *λέξιν οὐ λέγων* ‘not saying a word’ does for the coordinate *e non fa motto*. Where the subject changes, a genitive absolute serves much the same purpose: *τούτων δὲ σειομένων* ‘as these were being shaken’ turns a finite active verb into a passive participle and leaves the agent unnamed. Musurus does not treat every infinitive alike, either. The purpose in *a ritornar* becomes an accompanying action in *νοστοῦντες* ‘returning’, whereas the final *a riveder* receives an explicit purpose clause, *ἵν᾽ ἴδωμεν πάλιν* ‘so that we might see again’.

Particles often carry distinctions that Italian leaves to word order or expresses by subordination. The repeated *altre … altre* becomes the correlative *αἱ μὲν … αἱ δέ* ‘some … others’; later, the same particles balance *ὧδε* ‘here’ against *ἐκεῖ* ‘there’ where Dante links the two statements with *quando* ‘when’. At other points Musurus follows the Italian construction closely. *Τοσοῦτον, ὅσον* ‘as far, as’ preserves the measure of *tanto, quanto*, and *κατέβη κάτω* ‘he descended down’ keeps the directional repetition of *giù discese*, only reversing its order. Even a difference in verb number need not signal a difference in sense: *ῥέπει* ‘inclines’ is singular where *si traggon* is plural because its subject, *τὰ βάρη* ‘the weights’, is neuter plural.

The most consequential departures are sometimes changes of a single word or its attachment. *Ἔμβρυα* ‘embryos’ replaces singular *festuca* ‘a straw’ while leaving the glass of the comparison intact. More subtly, *μελάγχρους* ‘dark-complexioned’ describes Brutus himself, whereas *nero* qualifies *ceffo* ‘visage’, the face from which he hangs. Musurus can also make a reading explicit rather than substitute a new image: *περικαλλές* ‘exceedingly beautiful’ predicates a former beauty that the supplied Italian *il sembiante* ‘the appearance’ leaves unqualified. The close is notably faithful in emphasis: although Greek expands the Italian infinitive and separates out the force of its prefix, *τἆστρα* ‘the stars’ still occupies the final position of *le stelle*.
