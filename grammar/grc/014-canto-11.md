# Inferno 11

## Lines 1–24 — The Descent Delayed and the Aim of Malice

> 1 In su l' estremità d' un' alta ripa,  
> 2 che facevan gran pietre rotte in cerchio,  
> 3 venimmo sopra più crudele stipa ;  

Upon the edge of a high bank, formed by large broken stones in a circle, we came above a still more cruel throng;

> 1 Ἐπ᾽ ἄκρας ὄχθης ὑψηλῆς, συγκειμένης  
> (Upon the edge of a high bank, composed)
>
> 2 Ἐκ πετρῶν ἀποῤῥώγων μεγάλων κύκλῳ,  
> (Of great broken rocks in a circle,)
>
> 3 Ἀφίγμεθ᾽ εἰς σωρείαν ἀλγεινοτέραν.  
> (We arrived at a more grievous throng.)

- *Ἐπ᾽ ἄκρας ὄχθης ὑψηλῆς* ‘on the edge of a high bank’ turns the noun *estremità* into an adjective. Both *ἄκρας* and *ὑψηλῆς* agree with *ὄχθης* (gen. sg. fem.), but *ἄκρος* applied to a whole can designate its outermost part. *Ἐπ᾽* is *ἐπί* elided before the vowel; the genitive marks position, not motion onto the bank.
- *συγκειμένης ἐκ πετρῶν ἀποῤῥώγων μεγάλων* ‘composed of great, broken-off rocks’ replaces the finite relative *che facevan*, whose subject is *gran pietre*, with a participle agreeing with the bank. *συγκειμένης* (gen. sg. fem. pres. mid. ptcp. of *σύγκειμαι*) expresses a state; *ἐκ* gives the material. *ἀποῤῥώγων* is gen. pl. of *ἀπορρώξ* ‘broken off’; the two breathings are a traditional spelling of doubled rho. *κύκλῳ*, the adverbial dative of *κύκλος*, gives *in cerchio* without a preposition.
- *Ἀφίγμεθ᾽ εἰς σωρείαν ἀλγεινοτέραν* ‘we have arrived at a more grievous heap’ substitutes arrival *at* something for *venimmo sopra*. *Ἀφίγμεθ᾽* is *ἀφίγμεθα* (1st pl. perf. mid. ind. of *ἀφικνέομαι*), with final *-α* elided: the perfect presents the position reached rather than simply narrating the coming. *σωρεία* represents *stipa* as a mass heaped together; *ἀλγεινοτέραν* (acc. sg. fem., comparative of *ἀλγεινός*) makes *più crudele* a comparison in painfulness.

> 4 e quivi per l' orribile soperchio  
> 5 del puzzo, che il profondo abisso gitta,  

and here, because of the horrible excess of stench which the deep abyss throws out,

> 4 Ἐκεῖ δ᾽ ἕνεκα τῆς φρικτῆς δυσωδίας,  
> (And there, on account of the horrible stench,)
>
> 5 Ἐκ τῆς βαθείας ἀνιούσης ἀβύσσου,  
> (Ascending from the deep abyss,)

- *Ἐκεῖ δ᾽ ἕνεκα τῆς φρικτῆς δυσωδίας* ‘and there, on account of the horrible stench’ gives *quivi* by the adverb *ἐκεῖ* and *e* by postpositive *δέ*, elided. *ἕνεκα* governs the genitive and here precedes it. The separate notion of excess in *soperchio* drops out: *φρικτῆς* now qualifies the stench itself.
- *ἐκ τῆς βαθείας ἀνιούσης ἀβύσσου* changes the direction of the syntax. In *che il profondo abisso gitta*, the abyss acts on the stench; in Greek the stench is itself ‘rising from the deep abyss’. *ἀνιούσης* (gen. sg. fem. pres. act. ptcp. of *ἄνειμι* ‘go up’) agrees with *δυσωδίας* in the preceding line, though it stands between *βαθείας* and its noun *ἀβύσσου*. The genitive after *ἐκ* marks the source.

> 6 ci raccostammo dietro ad un coperchio  
> 7 d' un grande avello, ov' io vidi una scritta  

we approached it under cover of a great monument, whereon I saw a writing

> 6 Ὄπισθεν τοῦ πώματος μεγάλου τάφου  
> (Behind the lid of a great tomb)
>
> 7 Ἔστημεν· ἐπ᾽ αὐτοῦ δὲ γράμματ᾽ ἀνέγνων  
> (We stood; and upon it I read an inscription)

- *ὄπισθεν τοῦ πώματος μεγάλου τάφου ἔστημεν* ‘behind the lid of a great tomb we took our stand’ retains both *coperchio* and *avello*. *ὄπισθεν* governs *τοῦ πώματος* (gen. sg. neut. of *πῶμα*); *μεγάλου τάφου* is a second genitive dependent on ‘lid’. The place comes first and the verb last. *ἔστημεν* (1st pl. intransitive root aor. act. ind. of *ἵστημι*) gives the position taken up, rather than the reflexive movement of *ci raccostammo*.
- *ἐπ᾽ αὐτοῦ δὲ γράμματ᾽ ἀνέγνων* ‘and on it I read letters’ replaces the relative *ov’* with a prepositional phrase and a new clause linked by *δέ*. *αὐτοῦ* can grammatically refer to the lid (neuter) or the tomb (masculine). *γράμματ᾽* is elided *γράμματα* (acc. pl. neut. of *γράμμα*), plural for the single *scritta*. *ἀνέγνων* (1st sg. root aor. act. ind. of *ἀναγιγνώσκω*, with internal augment) makes *vidi* specifically an act of reading; its ending supplies the person of *io*.

> 8 che diceva: “Anastasio papa guardo,  
> 9 lo qual trasse Fotin della via dritta.”  

that said: “I hold Pope Anastasius, whom Photinus drew from the straight way.”

> 8 Λέγοντ᾽· “Ἀναστάσιον Πάπαν φυλάττω  
> (Saying: “Pope Anastasius I guard,)
>
> 9 „Ὃν ἔσυρ᾽ ἔξω τῆς εὐθείας Φωτῖνος.”  
> („Whom Photinus drew out of the straight way.”)

- *λέγοντ᾽* is elided *λέγοντα* (acc. pl. neut. pres. act. ptcp. of *λέγω*), agreeing with *γράμματα*: ‘letters saying’, not a finite imperfect relative as in *che diceva*. *Ἀναστάσιον Πάπαν φυλάττω* keeps the Italian order, personal name and title in apposition before the verb. *φυλάττω* (1st sg. pres. act. ind., with Attic *-ττ-* for *-σσ-*) gives *guardo* as ‘I keep in my charge’.
- *ὃν ἔσυρ᾽ ἔξω τῆς εὐθείας Φωτῖνος* ‘whom Photinus dragged out of the straight [way]’ puts the subject last, even later than *Fotin*. *ὅν* is the masculine singular accusative relative, object of *ἔσυρ᾽* (elided *ἔσυρε*, 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *σύρω*). The imperfect replaces the completed action of *trasse*. *ἔξω* governs the genitive; the feminine adjective *τῆς εὐθείας* leaves *ὁδοῦ* ‘way’ understood, where Italian names *via*.

> 10 “Lo nostro scender convien esser tardo,  
> 11 sì che s' ausi prima un poco il senso  
> 12 al tristo fiato, e poi non fia riguardo.”  

“Our descent we must delay, till sense be somewhat used to the dismal blast, and then we shall not heed it.”

> 10 —“Βράδιον ἡμᾶς καταβῆναι προσήκει,  
> (—“It is fitting that we descend slowly,)
>
> 11 „Ἵν᾽ ἐθισθῶμεν πρῶτον εἰς τὴν δυσώδη  
> („So that we may first become accustomed to the foul)
>
> 12 „Ἀποφοράν· εἶτα γὰρ οὐκ ἔσται βλάβη.”  
> („Exhalation; for thereafter there will be no harm.”)

- *Βράδιον ἡμᾶς καταβῆναι προσήκει* ‘it befits us to descend more slowly’ turns *lo nostro scender* into an infinitive with an expressed accusative subject, *ἡμᾶς*. *καταβῆναι* is the root aor. act. inf. of *καταβαίνω*, dependent on impersonal *προσήκει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *προσήκω*). The comparative adverb *βράδιον* modifies ‘descend’ directly; there is no equivalent of *esser*.
- *ἵν᾽ ἐθισθῶμεν πρῶτον* ‘so that we may first become accustomed’ interprets *sì che* as purpose: elided *ἵνα* takes *ἐθισθῶμεν* (1st pl. aor. pass. subj. of *ἐθίζω*). The persons themselves, rather than *il senso*, undergo the habituation. *πρῶτον* retains *prima*, but *un poco* has no equivalent. *εἰς τὴν δυσώδη ἀποφοράν* ‘to the foul exhalation’ uses *εἰς* with the accusative in a construction close to Italian *al*; *δυσώδη* (acc. sg. fem. of *δυσώδης*) specifies smell rather than the more general *tristo*.
- *εἶτα γὰρ οὐκ ἔσται βλάβη* ‘for then there will be no harm’ is explanatory rather than simply continuative like *e poi*. *ἔσται* is 3rd sg. fut. ind. of *εἰμί*, middle in form, with nominative subject *βλάβη*. Musurus retains the existential construction of *non fia riguardo* but gives the thing that will no longer be present as harm, not heed or concern.

> 13 Così il maestro ; ed io : “Alcun compenso,”  
> 14 dissi lui, “trova, che il tempo non passi  
> 15 perduto.” Ed egli: “Vedi ch' a ciò penso.  

Thus the Master; and I said to him: “Find some compensation, that the time may not be lost.” And he: “Thou seest that I intend it.

> 13 Ταῦτ᾽ εἶπεν ὁ Προστάτης· ἐγὼ δὲ πάλιν·  
> (Thus spoke the Protector; and I in turn:)
>
> 14 “Πρὸς ἀνταμοιβὴν εὑρέ, πῶς μὴ παρέλθῃ  
> (“In compensation, find how may not pass)
>
> 15 „Ὁ καιρὸς μάτην.” Ὁ δ᾽ ἐπαναλαμβάνει·  
> („Time in vain.” And he resumes:)

- *Ταῦτ᾽ εἶπεν ὁ Προστάτης* ‘these things the Protector said’ supplies a verb for *Così il maestro*: *ταῦτ᾽* is elided *ταῦτα*, a neuter plural accusative object, and *εἶπεν* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*. *Προστάτης* names one who stands before or protects, rather than one who teaches. Conversely, *ἐγὼ δὲ πάλιν* ‘and I in turn’ leaves speaking understood where Dante inserts *dissi lui*; the addressee is not expressed.
- *πρὸς ἀνταμοιβὴν εὑρέ* ‘as a return, find’ turns the direct object *alcun compenso* into *πρός* with an accusative of purpose. What is to be found is now *πῶς μὴ παρέλθῃ ὁ καιρὸς μάτην* ‘how the time may not pass in vain’. *εὑρέ* is the 2nd sg. second aor. act. imperative of *εὑρίσκω*; *παρέλθῃ* is 3rd sg. second aor. act. subj. of *παρέρχομαι*. *πῶς* introduces the question of means, with *μή* negating the contemplated outcome. *μάτην* is an adverb in place of the predicate participle *perduto*.
- *ὁ δ᾽ ἐπαναλαμβάνει* ‘and he takes up the word again’ expands *Ed egli*: *ὁ* has pronominal force before elided *δέ*, and *ἐπαναλαμβάνει* is a historical present (3rd sg. act. ind.). The actual reply corresponding to *Vedi ch’ a ciò penso* is carried over into Greek line 16.

> 16 Figliuol mio, dentro da cotesti sassi,”  
> 17 cominciò poi a dir, “son tre cerchietti  
> 18 di grado in grado, come quei che lassi.  

My Son, within these stones,” he then began to say, “are three circlets in gradation, like those thou leavest.

> 16 “Ἰδοὺ τί φρονῶ, τέκνον· ἐντὸς τῶν λίθων  
> (“Behold what I am thinking, child: within the rocks)
>
> 17 „Τούτων τρεῖς εἰσι κυκλίσκοι διαφόρων  
> („Here there are three lesser circles of different)
>
> 18 „Βαθμῶν ἕκαστος, οἷς διῆλθες ὁμοῖοι,  
> („Grades each, similar to those you have passed through,)

- *Ἰδοὺ τί φρονῶ, τέκνον* ‘behold what I have in mind, child’ joins the preceding Italian reply to the new address. *ἰδού* functions as a presentative particle rather than a statement ‘thou seest’; interrogative *τί* is the object of contracted *φρονῶ* (1st sg. pres. act. ind. of *φρονέω*). *τέκνον* is a neuter singular vocative without the possessive *mio*. The interruption *cominciò poi a dir* is omitted after the Greek introduction of the speech in line 15.
- *ἐντὸς τῶν λίθων τούτων* ‘within these stones’ gives *dentro da* by *ἐντός* governing the genitive. The demonstrative stands outside the article–noun group in regular Greek order; *τούτων* does not mark the specific relation to the hearer that *cotesti* can convey. *τρεῖς εἰσι κυκλίσκοι* ‘there are three small circles’ retains the diminutive of *cerchietti* in *-ίσκος*; *εἰσι* is the existential 3rd pl. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*.
- *διαφόρων βαθμῶν ἕκαστος* ‘each of different grades’ substitutes a genitive of description for the progression *di grado in grado*. The distributive *ἕκαστος* is nominative masculine singular in apposition to the plural circles; the genitive *βαθμῶν* remains plural, making the expression less precise than ‘each of a different grade’.
- *οἷς διῆλθες ὁμοῖοι* ‘like those you traversed’ returns to plural agreement in nominative *ὅμοιοι*. The relative *οἷς* takes the dative required by ‘like’, by attraction to the case of its unexpressed antecedent, rather than the accusative expected as object of ‘traverse’. *διῆλθες* (2nd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *διέρχομαι*, augmented after the preverb) looks back on a completed passage instead of the present *lassi* ‘thou leavest’.

> 19 Tutti son pien di spirti maledetti ;  

They all are filled with spirits accurst;

> 19 „Ἅπαντες μεστοὶ πνευμάτων καταράτων.  
> („All of them full of accursed spirits.)

- *ἅπαντες μεστοὶ πνευμάτων καταράτων* ‘all full of accursed spirits’ retains the order of *Tutti son pien di spirti maledetti* but omits the copula. *ἅπαντες* (nom. pl. masc. of *ἅπας*) gathers all the circles together; predicate *μεστοί* agrees with them and governs the genitive of contents. *καταράτων* is gen. pl. neut. of the adjective *κατάρατος*, agreeing with *πνευμάτων*, not with the circles.

> 20 ma perchè poi ti basti pur la vista,  
> 21 intendi come, e perchè son costretti.  

but, that the sight of these hereafter may of itself suffice thee, hearken how and wherefore they are pent up.

> 20 „Ἀλλ᾽ ἵν᾽ ἔπειτ᾽ ἀρκῇ σοι μόνον ἡ θέα,  
> („But so that hereafter the mere sight may suffice you,)
>
> 21 „Γνῶθι, διὰ τί καὶ πῶς εἰσι δεσμῶται.  
> („Know why and how they are prisoners.)

- *ἀλλ᾽ ἵν᾽ ἔπειτ᾽ ἀρκῇ σοι μόνον ἡ θέα* closely follows the order of *ma perchè poi ti basti pur la vista*. The first three words show successive elisions of *ἀλλά*, *ἵνα*, and *ἔπειτα*. Purpose *ἵνα* governs *ἀρκῇ* (3rd sg. pres. act. subj. of *ἀρκέω*); *σοι* is the dative of the person for whom the sight suffices. *μόνον* is adverbial ‘merely’, not a feminine adjective agreeing with the postponed subject *ἡ θέα*.
- *γνῶθι, διὰ τί καὶ πῶς εἰσι δεσμῶται* ‘know why and how they are prisoners’ makes *intendi* an injunction to know: *γνῶθι* is the 2nd sg. root aor. act. imperative of *γιγνώσκω*. The indirect question retains the indicative *εἰσι* and reverses *come, e perchè*: *διὰ τί* ‘on account of what?’ precedes *πῶς*. Predicate *δεσμῶται* (nom. pl. masc. of *δεσμώτης*) names the persons as prisoners rather than describing them by the passive *son costretti*.

> 22 D' ogni malizia ch' odio in cielo acquista,  
> 23 ingiuria è il fine; ed ogni fin cotale  
> 24 o con forza, o con frode altrui contrista.  

Of all malice, which gains hatred in Heaven, the end is injury; and every such end, either by force or by fraud, aggrieveth others.

> 22 „Τὸ τέλος πάσης θεοστυγοῦς κακίας  
> („The end of every malice hated by God)
>
> 23 „Ἔστ᾽ ἀδικία, καὶ πᾶν τοιοῦτο τέλος  
> („Is injustice, and every such end)
>
> 24 „Βιαίως ἢ δολίως τοὺς ἄλλους βλάπτει.  
> („Harms others either by violence or by fraud.)

- *τὸ τέλος πάσης θεοστυγοῦς κακίας* ‘the end of every God-hated wickedness’ brings forward *il fine*, then attaches the genitive corresponding to *D’ ogni malizia*. *πάσης* with the singular noun is distributive. *θεοστυγοῦς* (gen. sg. fem. of *θεοστυγής*) compresses *ch’ odio in cielo acquista* into an attributive compound: being hated replaces acquiring hatred, and God replaces the local expression *in cielo*.
- *ἔστ᾽ ἀδικία* ‘is injustice’ completes the clause with elided *ἔστι* (3rd sg. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*). The article identifies *τὸ τέλος* as subject; anarthrous *ἀδικία* is the predicate. Italian foregrounds *ingiuria* in *ingiuria è il fine*, whereas Greek puts the corresponding noun last and gives it the sense ‘wrongdoing’.
- *καὶ πᾶν τοιοῦτο τέλος* retains *ed ogni fin cotale* as the subject of the next verb; *πᾶν* and *τοιοῦτο* are both nom. sg. neut., agreeing with *τέλος*. *βιαίως ἢ δολίως* ‘violently or deceitfully’ turns the two *con* phrases into parallel adverbs of manner, with only one disjunctive *ἤ* for *o … o*. *τοὺς ἄλλους βλάπτει* keeps object before verb as in *altrui contrista*: the substantivized accusative plural makes the persons affected explicit, while *βλάπτει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *βλάπτω*) denotes harming rather than specifically causing grief.

## Lines 25–57 — The Divisions of Violence and Fraud

> 25 Ma perchè frode è dell’ uom proprio male  
> 26 più spiace a Dio; e però stan di sutto  
> 27 gli frodolenti, e più dolor gli assale.  

But because fraud is a vice peculiar to man, it more displeases God; and therefore the fraudulent are placed beneath, and more pain assails them.

> 25 „Φύσει δ᾽ ὢν ἀνθρώπινον κακὸν ὁ δόλος  
> („And fraud, being by nature a human evil,)
>
> 26 „Μᾶλλον στυγητός ἐστι Θεῷ, καὶ πλέον  
> („Is more hateful to God, and further)
>
> 27 „Κάτω δόλιοι κεῖνται καὶ τιμωροῦνται.  
> („Below the fraudulent lie and are punished.)

- *Φύσει δ᾽ ὢν ἀνθρώπινον κακὸν ὁ δόλος* ‘but fraud, being by nature a human evil’ turns the finite cause introduced by *perchè* into a causal participle. *ὤν* (nom. sg. masc., pres. act. participle of *εἰμί*) agrees with the delayed subject *ὁ δόλος*; *ἀνθρώπινον κακόν* is its predicate, neuter because *κακόν* is used as a noun. The dative *φύσει* ‘by nature’ and the adjective *ἀνθρώπινον* together render *dell’ uom proprio*, without a separate word for ‘peculiar’. Postpositive *δέ*, elided to *δ᾽*, answers to *Ma*.
- *Μᾶλλον στυγητός ἐστι Θεῷ* ‘is more hateful to God’ replaces the verb *spiace* with a predicate adjective and copula; the person to whom fraud is hateful stands in the dative. In *καὶ πλέον κάτω* ‘and farther down’, the comparative goes with the following adverb of place: Musurus makes relative depth explicit but does not separately render the inference *però*.
- *δόλιοι κεῖνται καὶ τιμωροῦνται* ‘deceitful men lie and are punished’ gives the same subject to both verbs. *δόλιοι* is a substantival nominative plural; *κεῖνται* is 3rd pl. pres. ind. of *κεῖμαι*, stative in sense, and *τιμωροῦνται* is 3rd pl. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *τιμωρέω*, here passive. The latter replaces *più dolor gli assale*: neither the personified subject nor the comparison of pain survives.

> 28 De’ violenti il primo cerchio è tutto;  

All the first circle is for the violent;

> 28 „Πρῶτος κυκλίσκος ἐστὶν ὁ τῶν βιαίων.  
> („The first lesser circle is that of the violent.)

- *Πρῶτος κυκλίσκος ἐστὶν ὁ τῶν βιαίων* ‘the first little circle is the one of the violent’. The article *ὁ* allows *κυκλίσκος* to be understood again before the possessive genitive *τῶν βιαίων* (gen. pl. masc. of substantival *βίαιος*). Musurus thus moves *De’ violenti* from first place to last; the diminutive *κυκλίσκος* distinguishes *cerchio* from the subdivisions below, but there is no equivalent of *tutto*.

> 29 ma perchè si fa forza a tre persone,  
> 30 in tre gironi è distinto e costrutto.  

but as violence may be done to three persons, it is formed and distinguished into three rounds.

> 29 „Ἀλλ᾽ ὡς ἡ βία γίνετ᾽ ἐπὶ προσώπων  
> („But since violence is committed against persons)
>
> 30 „Τριῶν, εἰς περιβόλους τρεῖς διαιρεῖται·  
> („Three, it is divided into three precincts:)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ ὡς ἡ βία γίνετ᾽ ἐπὶ προσώπων τριῶν* ‘but since violence takes place against three persons’ retains the causal clause. *ἀλλ᾽* is elided *ἀλλά*; *γίνετ᾽* is elided *γίνεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *γίγνομαι*, in the spelling *γίνομαι*). In place of impersonal *si fa forza*, *ἡ βία* is the grammatical subject. *ἐπί* with the genitive specifies the persons affected; *τριῶν*, also genitive, is held over until the next line.
- *εἰς περιβόλους τρεῖς διαιρεῖται* ‘it is divided into three enclosures’ resumes the circle as subject. *διαιρεῖται* (3rd sg. pres. pass. ind. of *διαιρέω*) renders *è distinto*, absorbing *e costrutto* without a second verb. The juxtaposition of genitive *τριῶν* and accusative *τρεῖς* marks the change from persons to divisions.

> 31 A Dio, a sè, al prossimo si puone  
> 32 far forza; dico in loro, e in lor cose,  
> 33 come udirai con aperta ragione.  

To God, to one's self, and to one's neighbour, may violence be done; I say in them and in their things, as thou shalt hear with evident discourse.

> 31 „Πρὸς Θεόν, πρὸς ἑαυτόν, πρὸς τὸν πλησίον  
> („Against God, against oneself, against one's neighbour)
>
> 32 „Καὶ τἀνήκοντα τούτοις βίᾳ τις χρῆται,  
> („And what pertains to these, one employs violence,)
>
> 33 „ Ὡς δι᾽ ἐξηγήσεων σαφῶν μαθήσῃ.  
> („ As through clear explanations you shall learn.)

- *Πρὸς Θεόν, πρὸς ἑαυτόν, πρὸς τὸν πλησίον* repeats *πρός* with each accusative, as Italian repeats *a*. *ἑαυτόν* refers forward to indefinite *τις*; *τὸν πλησίον* makes the adverb *πλησίον* ‘near’ into a designation of a person by adding the masculine article.
- *Καὶ τἀνήκοντα τούτοις* ‘and the things pertaining to these’ extends the construction with *πρός* to property. *τἀνήκοντα* is crasis of *τὰ ἀνήκοντα* (acc. pl. neut., substantivized pres. act. participle of *ἀνήκω*); *τούτοις* is the dative complement of the participle. Musurus dispenses with the explanatory restart *dico in loro*: persons and possessions become coordinated targets of *βίᾳ τις χρῆται* ‘someone uses force’. *χρῆται* (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *χράομαι*) governs the dative *βίᾳ*; it states the action without translating *si puone* by a modal verb.
- *Ὡς δι᾽ ἐξηγήσεων σαφῶν μαθήσῃ* ‘as you will learn through clear explanations’ changes hearing to learning. *μαθήσῃ* is 2nd sg. fut. mid. ind. of *μανθάνω*, active in meaning; *δι᾽* is elided *διά*, with the genitive of means. The plural *ἐξηγήσεων* replaces singular *ragione*.

> 34 Morte per forza, e ferute dogliose  
> 35 nel prossimo si danno, e nel suo avere  
> 36 ruine, incendi e tollette dannose:  

By force, death and painful wounds may be inflicted upon one's neighbour; and upon his substance, devastations, burnings, and injurious extortions:

> 34 „ Φόνος βίαιος καὶ τραύματ᾽ ὀδυνώδη  
> („ Violent murder and painful wounds)
>
> 35 „ Τοὺς πέλας ζημιοῦσιν· αὐτῶν δ᾽ οὐσίας  
> („ Harm one's neighbours; and their substance,)
>
> 36 „ Ἐμπρησμοί, καταστροφαί, λεηλασίαι.  
> („ Arsons, destructions, plunderings.)

- *Φόνος βίαιος καὶ τραύματ᾽ ὀδυνώδη τοὺς πέλας ζημιοῦσιν* ‘violent killing and painful wounds harm those nearby’ changes *morte per forza* into a noun of action with an adjective, and *nel prossimo si danno* into an active transitive construction. *τραύματ᾽* is elided *τραύματα* (nom. pl. neut. of *τραῦμα*); *ὀδυνώδη* is the contracted neuter plural of *ὀδυνώδης*. The coordinated subjects take plural *ζημιοῦσιν* (3rd pl. pres. act. ind. of *ζημιόω*). As with *τὸν πλησίον*, an article turns an adverb, here *πέλας*, into a personal expression, now accusative plural.
- *αὐτῶν δ᾽ οὐσίας* ‘and their possessions’ supplies a second accusative object of *ζημιοῦσιν*, with possessive *αὐτῶν* referring to the plural neighbours. The new nominative subjects follow, the verb being understood: *ἐμπρησμοί, καταστροφαί, λεηλασίαι* ‘burnings, destructions, plunderings’. The first two reverse the order of *ruine, incendi*; the last renders *tollette* without a separate adjective for *dannose*.

> 37 onde omicidi, e ciascun che mal fiere,  
> 38 guastatori e predon, tutti tormenta  
> 39 lo giron primo per diverse schiere.  

wherefore the first round torments all homicides and every one who strikes maliciously, all plunderers and robbers, in different bands.

> 37 „ Ὅθεν φονεῖς καὶ πάντες κακῶς ποιοῦντες,  
> („ Wherefore murderers and all who do evil,)
>
> 38 „ Ἐξολοθρευταὶ καὶ λῃσταί, τιμωροῦνται  
> („ Destroyers and robbers, are punished)
>
> 39 „ Ἰληδὸν ἐντὸς τοῦ πρώτου περιβόλου.  
> („ In troops within the first precinct.)

- *Ὅθεν* ‘whence’ renders inferential *onde*. *φονεῖς καὶ πάντες κακῶς ποιοῦντες* ‘killers and all who do harm’ broadens *ciascun che mal fiere*: the singular relative clause becomes a plural present active participle (nom. pl. masc. of *ποιέω*) with *πάντες*, and the specific act of striking disappears. *ἐξολοθρευταὶ καὶ λῃσταί* ‘destroyers and robbers’ continues the list in the nominative.
- Italian delays its subject, *lo giron primo*, until after *tormenta*. Greek instead makes the offenders the subjects of passive *τιμωροῦνται* and the round a place, *ἐντὸς τοῦ πρώτου περιβόλου* ‘within the first enclosure’, with *ἐντός* governing the genitive. The distributive adverb *ἰληδόν* ‘troop by troop’, from *ἴλη* ‘troop’, expresses *per diverse schiere* without a separate adjective.

> 40 Puote uomo avere in sè man violenta  
> 41 e ne’ suoi beni: e però nel secondo  

A man may lay violent hand upon himself, and upon his property:

> 40 „ Ἑαυτὸν βίᾳ κοὐσίαν αὑτοῦ βλάψαι  
> („ Himself with violence and his own substance to harm)
>
> 41 „ Δύναταί τις· διόπερ ἐν τῷ δευτέρῳ  
> („ Is in a man's power; therefore in the second)

- *Ἑαυτὸν βίᾳ κοὐσίαν αὑτοῦ βλάψαι δύναταί τις* ‘someone can harm himself by force, and his own property’ postpones the modal verb and its indefinite subject. *βλάψαι* (aor. act. inf. of *βλάπτω*) complements *δύναται* (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *δύναμαι*); its two direct objects replace the Italian phrases with *in*. Instrumental *βίᾳ* conveys *violenta*, but the image of the hand is dropped.
- *κοὐσίαν* is crasis of *καὶ οὐσίαν*; singular *οὐσίαν* collects plural *beni*. *αὑτοῦ* is the contracted, rough-breathed form of reflexive *ἑαυτοῦ*, here a possessive genitive, beside the full accusative *ἑαυτόν* earlier. The additional accent in *δύναταί τις* is occasioned by enclitic *τις*.

> 42 giron convien che senza pro si penta  
> 43 qualunque priva sè del vostro mondo,  
> 44 biscazza e fonde la suo facultade,  
> 45 e piange là dove esser dee giocondo.  

and therefore in the second round must every one repent in vain who deprives himself of your world, gambles away and dissipates his wealth, and weeps there where he should be joyous.

> 42 „ Περιβόλῳ δεῖ μετανοεῖν εἰς μάτην  
> („ Precinct must repent in vain)
>
> 43 „ Τὸν ἑαυτὸν στεροῦντα τοῦ κόσμου ζώντων,  
> („ He who deprives himself of the world of the living,)
>
> 44 „ Ἢ τὴν αὑτοῦ φθείροντα περιουσίαν,  
> („ Or who wastes his own property,)
>
> 45 „ Καὶ κλαίειν ἀντὶ τοῦ γηθόσυνον εἶναι·  
> („ And weep instead of being joyful;)

- The Greek construction starts midway through 41: *διόπερ ἐν τῷ δευτέρῳ περιβόλῳ δεῖ μετανοεῖν εἰς μάτην* ‘for which very reason in the second enclosure it is necessary to repent in vain’. Inferential *διόπερ* renders *e però*; impersonal *δεῖ* with a present active infinitive (*μετανοεῖν*, from *μετανοέω*) replaces *convien che* with its subjunctive. *εἰς μάτην* is adverbial, answering to *senza pro*.
- *τὸν ἑαυτὸν στεροῦντα τοῦ κόσμου ζώντων* ‘the one depriving himself of the world of living people’ is the accusative subject of the infinitive. The article belongs to *στεροῦντα* (acc. sg. masc., pres. act. participle of *στερόω*), not to intervening *ἑαυτόν*, its object. The thing taken away stands in the genitive; *ζώντων* (gen. pl., pres. act. participle of *ζάω*, used substantivally) interprets rather than reproduces the possessive in *vostro mondo*.
- *ἢ τὴν αὑτοῦ φθείροντα περιουσίαν* ‘or the one ruining his own estate’ coordinates *φθείροντα* (acc. sg. masc., pres. act. participle of *φθείρω*) with *στεροῦντα* under the same article. The explicit alternative *ἤ* and single verb of wasting replace the Italian sequence *biscazza e fonde*: gambling is not specified.
- *καὶ κλαίειν ἀντὶ τοῦ γηθόσυνον εἶναι* ‘and to weep instead of being joyful’ makes *κλαίειν* a second present infinitive dependent on *δεῖ*, not a third participle describing the offender. *ἀντί* takes the genitive of the articular infinitive *τοῦ ... εἶναι*; poetic *γηθόσυνον* is a masculine accusative predicate agreeing with its understood subject. Substitution thus replaces both the local contrast *là dove* and the separate obligation *dee*.

> 46 Puossi far forza nella Deitade,  
> 47 col cor negando e bestemmiando quella,  
> 48 e spregiando Natura, e sua bontade:  

Violence may be done against the Deity, in the heart denying and blaspheming Him; and disdaining Nature and her bounty:

> 46 „ Δύναται χρῆσθαι βίᾳ τις πρὸς τὸ θεῖον  
> („ One can use violence against the Deity)
>
> 47 „ Βλασφημῶν κἀρνούμενος Θεὸν ἰδίᾳ,  
> („ Blaspheming and denying God in private,)
>
> 48 „ Ἢ τὴν φύσιν παρορῶν καὐτῆς τὴν χάριν.  
> („ Or despising nature and her bounty.)

- *Δύναται χρῆσθαι βίᾳ τις πρὸς τὸ θεῖον* ‘someone can use force against the divine’ keeps the possibility in *Puossi*, but makes the agent explicit. *δύναται* governs the present middle infinitive *χρῆσθαι* of *χράομαι*, itself governing *βίᾳ*. The target stands after *πρός* in the accusative; the substantivized neuter adjective *τὸ θεῖον* replaces the abstract feminine *Deitade*.
- *Βλασφημῶν κἀρνούμενος Θεὸν ἰδίᾳ* reverses *negando e bestemmiando*. The present participles are nominative singular masculine, agreeing with *τις*: active *βλασφημῶν* from *βλασφημέω*, middle *ἀρνούμενος* from *ἀρνέομαι*, with *καί* joined by crasis in *κἀρνούμενος*. The shared object *Θεόν* replaces the pronoun *quella*; the adverbial dative *ἰδίᾳ* ‘privately’ interprets rather than literally renders *col cor*.
- *ἢ τὴν φύσιν παρορῶν καὐτῆς τὴν χάριν* ‘or disregarding nature and her grace’ substitutes disjunctive *ἤ* for *e*. *παρορῶν* (nom. sg. masc., pres. act. participle of *παροράω*) governs both accusatives. *καὐτῆς* is crasis of *καὶ αὐτῆς*; the possessive genitive refers to *φύσιν*, and *χάριν* (acc. sg. of *χάρις*) interprets *bontade* as favour or bounty.

> 49 e però lo minor giron suggella  
> 50 del segno suo e Sodoma e Caorsa,  
> 51 e chi spregiando Dio col cor favella.  

and hence the smallest round seals with its mark both Sodom and Cahors, and all who speak with disparagement of God in their hearts.

> 49 „ Ὅθεν ὁ τρίτος περίβολος σφραγίζει  
> („ Wherefore the third precinct seals)
>
> 50 „ Ταὐτῷ σημάντρῳ Σόδομα καὶ Καόρσαν  
> („ With the same mark Sodom and Cahors)
>
> 51 „ Καὶ Θεὸν παρορῶντας ψυχῇ καὶ λόγοις.  
> („ And those who despise God in soul and words.)

- *Ὅθεν ὁ τρίτος περίβολος σφραγίζει* ‘whence the third enclosure seals’ preserves the active metaphor of *suggella* (*σφραγίζει*, 3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *σφραγίζω*), but identifies the enclosure by sequence, *τρίτος*, not by relative size, *minor*.
- *Ταὐτῷ σημάντρῳ* ‘with the same stamp’ is an instrumental dative; *ταὐτῷ* is crasis of *τῷ αὐτῷ*, neuter singular agreeing with *σημάντρῳ*. Identity replaces the possessive in *del segno suo*. *Σόδομα* and *Καόρσαν* are direct objects, the latter given a Greek accusative ending.
- *καὶ Θεὸν παρορῶντας ψυχῇ καὶ λόγοις* ‘and those disregarding God in soul and in words’ adds a personal object, *παρορῶντας* (acc. pl. masc., pres. act. participle of *παροράω*, used substantivally). *Θεόν* is its object. Instead of subordinating *spregiando* to finite *favella*, Greek makes disregarding the only verbal action and coordinates the datives *ψυχῇ* and *λόγοις* to express its inward and spoken manifestations.

> 52 La frode, ond’ ogni coscïenza è morsa,  
> 53 può l’ uomo usare in colui, ch’ in lui fida,  
> 54 e in quei che fidanza non imborsa.  

Fraud, which gnaws every conscience, a man may practise upon [one] who confide[s] in him; and upon [him] who repose[s] no confidence.

> 52 „ Δόλος, ἡ πάσης συνειδήσεως τύψις,  
> („ Fraud, the sting of every conscience,)
>
> 53 „ Ἢ πρὸς βλάβην γίνεται τῶν πίστιν δόντων,  
> („ Is committed either to the harm of those who gave trust,)
>
> 54 „ Ἢ τινων, πρὸς οὓς οὐκ ὀφείλεται πίστις.  
> („ Or of those to whom no trust is owed.)

- *Δόλος, ἡ πάσης συνειδήσεως τύψις* ‘fraud, the sting of every conscience’ makes *δόλος* the subject of *γίνεται*, whereas *La frode* is the fronted object of *può l’ uomo usare*. The relative clause *ond’ ogni coscïenza è morsa* becomes a noun in apposition: feminine nominative *τύψις* ‘prick, stroke’ has its own article and a dependent genitive, *πάσης συνειδήσεως*, denoting what feels the sting.
- *ἢ πρὸς βλάβην γίνεται τῶν πίστιν δόντων* ‘occurs either to the harm of those who gave trust’ dispenses with both the human subject and the modal verb. *πρός* with accusative *βλάβην* introduces the harm; the persons harmed depend on *βλάβην* in the genitive. *τῶν ... δόντων* is an articular aorist active participle (gen. pl. masc. of *δίδωμι*): *πίστιν* is what they gave. Thus present *fida* becomes a prior grant of trust; *in lui* is not expressed.
- *ἤ τινων, πρὸς οὓς οὐκ ὀφείλεται πίστις* ‘or of some to whom trust is not owed’ completes the alternatives *ἢ ... ἤ*. Indefinite *τινων* is another genitive dependent on *βλάβην*; its relative *οὕς* takes the accusative after *πρός*. *ὀφείλεται* (3rd sg. pres. pass. ind. of *ὀφείλω*) has nominative *πίστις* as subject. This changes the direction of the relation: *quei che fidanza non imborsa* does not repose confidence, whereas the Greek describes people to whom confidence is not due.

> 55 Questo modo di retro par che uccida  
> 56 pur lo vinco d’ amor che fa natura:  

This latter mode seems only to cut off the bond of love which Nature makes:

> 55 „ Ῥήγνυσι τὸ δεύτερον εἶδος τοῦ δόλου  
> („ The second kind of fraud breaks)
>
> 56 „ Τὸν δεσμὸν μόνον τῆς φυσικῆς ἀγάπης·  
> („ Only the bond of natural love;)

- *Ῥήγνυσι τὸ δεύτερον εἶδος τοῦ δόλου* ‘the second kind of fraud breaks’ opens with the finite verb *ῥήγνυσι* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ῥήγνυμι*). The postposed nominative subject makes *Questo modo di retro* explicitly the second *kind*, adding the defining genitive *τοῦ δόλου*. The direct assertion drops the qualification *par che* and substitutes breaking for *uccida*.
- *Τὸν δεσμὸν μόνον τῆς φυσικῆς ἀγάπης* ‘only the bond of natural love’ gives *ῥήγνυσι* its accusative object; *μόνον*, immediately after *δεσμόν*, conveys restrictive *pur*. *τῆς φυσικῆς ἀγάπης* is an attributive genitive phrase: instead of making Nature the subject of *fa* in a relative clause, Musurus makes *φυσικῆς* an adjective qualifying love.

> 57 onde nel cerchio secondo s’ annida  

hence in the second circle nests

> 57 „ Ὥστε κυκλίσκος ὁ δεύτερος ἐγκρύπτει  
> („ So that the second lesser circle holds within it)

- *Ὥστε κυκλίσκος ὁ δεύτερος ἐγκρύπτει* ‘so that the second little circle conceals’ renders *onde* by consecutive *ὥστε* with the indicative, presenting the result as a fact. In *κυκλίσκος ὁ δεύτερος* the ordinal stands in postpositive attributive position, with the article before the adjective.
- *ἐγκρύπτει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἐγκρύπτω*) changes the direction of the construction: *nel cerchio secondo* is the place where something *s’ annida*, whereas the Greek circle is the nominative subject that hides something within it. What Italian leaves to follow as subject is thus still awaited in Greek as direct object.

## Lines 58–90 — Fraud, Betrayal, and Degrees of Blame

> 58 ipocrisia, lusinghe e chi affattura,  
> 59 falsità, ladroneccio e simonia,  
> 60 ruffian, baratti, e simile lordura.  

hypocrisy, flattery, sorcerers, cheating, theft and simony, pandars, barrators, and like filth.

> 58 „ Ὑποκρισίαν, κολακείαν, κυβείαν,  
> („ Hypocrisy, flattery, jugglery,)
>
> 59 „ Σιμωνίαν, κιβδήλους, κλέπτας, φηλήτας,  
> („ Simony, counterfeiters, thieves, swindlers,)
>
> 60 „ Μαστρωπούς, παρομοίας τ’ ἀκαθαρσίας.  
> („ Pandars, and similar filth.)

- *Ὑποκρισίαν, κολακείαν* reproduce *ipocrisia, lusinghe* as abstract accusatives, both singular in Greek. The accusative series continues a construction already under way; it is not a series of nominatives naming the offences. For *chi affattura*, a person described by a relative clause, Musurus gives another abstract noun, *κυβείαν*, properly ‘dice-playing’, hence ‘trickery’, rather than a word for practising magic.
- *Σιμωνίαν* moves *simonia* to the head of the next line. The series then shifts to masculine accusative plurals denoting people: *κιβδήλους* (from *κίβδηλος*, ‘spurious’, here substantivized) and *κλέπτας* (from *κλέπτης*) turn *falsità* and *ladroneccio* into ‘counterfeiters’ and ‘thieves’. *Φηλήτας*, from *φηλήτης*, gives ‘cheats’ rather than the more specific *baratti*; it comes before *μαστροπούς*, ‘panders’, reversing the Italian order of these last two groups.
- *Παρομοίας τ’ ἀκαθαρσίας* returns to an abstract expression, but makes *simile lordura* plural, ‘and similar impurities’. *Παρομοίας* agrees with *ἀκαθαρσίας* in the feminine accusative plural; postpositive *τε*, elided before the vowel, joins this final item to the list.

> 61 Per l’ altro modo quell’ amor s’ obblia  
> 62 che fa natura, e quel ch’ è poi aggiunto,  
> 63 di che la fede spezïal si cria :  

In the other mode is forgotten that love which Nature makes, and also that which afterwards is added, giving birth to special trust:

> 61 „ Ὁ δ’ ἄλλος δόλος λησμονεῖ τὴν ἀγάπην,  
> („ And the other fraud forgets the love)
>
> 62 „ Ἣν μὲν ἐμπνέει φύσις, ἀλλ’ ἣν συσφίγγει  
> („ Which nature inspires, and that also which is bound close)
>
> 63 „ Ὁ δεσμὸς προσωπικῆς ἐμπιστοσύνης·  
> („ By the bond of personal trust;)

- *Ὁ δ’ ἄλλος δόλος* ‘but the other fraud’ turns the adverbial *per l’ altro modo* into a nominative subject, supplying *δόλος*. Consequently *quell’ amor s’ obblia*, with love as the thing forgotten, becomes active *λησμονεῖ τὴν ἀγάπην*: *λησμονεῖ* is the contracted 3rd singular present indicative of *λησμονέω*, with an accusative object. *Δ’* is elided *δέ*.
- *Ἣν μὲν ἐμπνέει φύσις* ‘which nature inspires’ makes the first relative pronoun the feminine accusative singular object of *ἐμπνέει* (present active indicative of *ἐμπνέω*, with the vowels left uncontracted); *φύσις* is the subject. *Ἀλλ’ ἣν συσφίγγει* picks up *μὲν* with ‘but [also the love] which …’, repeating *ἣν* without repeating its antecedent.
- The second relative likewise has an active verb followed by its subject: *συσφίγγει ὁ δεσμός* ‘the bond draws tight’. *Προσωπικῆς ἐμπιστοσύνης* ‘of personal trust’ depends on *δεσμός*. Thus *poi* and the passive *è … aggiunto* disappear, and the relation in *di che la fede spezïal si cria* is reversed: rather than trust arising from an added love, a bond of trust tightens love.

> 64 onde nel cerchio minore, ov’ è il punto  
> 65 dell’ universo, in su che Dite siede,  
> 66 qualunque trade in eterno è consunto.”  

hence in the smallest circle, at the centre of the universe and seat of Dis, every traitor is eternally consumed."

> 64 „ Ὅθεν εἰς τὸν ἔσχατον κυκλίσκον, κέντρον  
> („ Wherefore into the lowest lesser circle, the centre)
>
> 65 „ Τοῦ Σύμπαντος, ἔνθαπερ ὁ Δὶς ἑδρεύει,  
> („ Of the Universe, where Dis holds his seat,)
>
> 66 „ Πᾶς ὃς ἂν προδῷ φθαρήσετ’ αἰωνίως.”  
> („ Everyone who betrays shall be destroyed eternally.”)

- *Ὅθεν* has the inferential force ‘whence, therefore’ of *onde*. For static *nel cerchio minore* Musurus uses *εἰς* with the accusative, more literally ‘into’, where classical local expression would ordinarily have *ἐν* with the dative. *Κυκλίσκον* is a diminutive of *κύκλος*; *ἔσχατον* adds ‘last, furthest’ rather than translating the comparative *minore* by a comparative.
- *Κέντρον τοῦ Σύμπαντος* ‘centre of the whole’ is in accusative apposition to *κυκλίσκον*. This dispenses with *ov’ è il punto* as a separate clause. *Ἔνθαπερ* ‘just where’, a local relative strengthened by *-περ*, then replaces *in su che*; *ὁ Δὶς ἑδρεύει* retains a nominative subject and a present active verb, ‘Dis has his seat’.
- *Πᾶς ὃς ἂν προδῷ* ‘everyone who may betray’ expands *qualunque trade*: *πᾶς* is substantival, and relative *ὃς* with *ἄν* and the 3rd singular aorist active subjunctive of *προδίδωμι* makes the qualification indefinite. *Φθαρήσετ’* is elided *φθαρήσεται*, 3rd singular future passive indicative of *φθείρω*, ‘will be destroyed’, for present *è … consunto*; *αἰωνίως* reduces *in eterno* to an adverb.

> 67 Ed io : “ Maestro, assai chiaro procede  
> 68 la tua ragione, ed assai ben distingue  
> 69 questo baratro, e il popol che il possiede.  

And I: "Master, thy discourse proceeds most clearly, and excellently distinguishes this gulf, and the people that possess it.

> 67 Κἀγώ· “Προστάτα, καθαρῶς οἱ σοὶ λόγοι  
> (And I: “Protector, clearly your words)
>
> 68 „ Σαφηνίζουσι διακριδὸν ἐκεῖνο  
> („ Explain distinctly that)
>
> 69 „ Τὸ βάραθρον καὶ λαὸν τὸν ἐνοικοῦντα.  
> („ Pit and the people dwelling therein.)

- *Κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*, ‘and I’; as in *Ed io*, the reporting verb is understood. Vocative *προστάτα* (from *προστάτης*) addresses the interlocutor as ‘protector’ rather than *Maestro*. *Οἱ σοὶ λόγοι* ‘your words’ replaces singular *la tua ragione*; *σοί* here is the masculine nominative plural possessive adjective.
- The two Italian predicates *procede … e … distingue* become a single plural verb, *σαφηνίζουσι* (3rd plural present active indicative of *σαφηνίζω*), ‘make clear’. *Καθαρῶς* ‘clearly’ precedes the subject, and *διακριδόν* ‘distinctly’ follows the verb: the distinction expressed by *distingue* survives in the manner of explaining. Neither *assai* has a separate equivalent.
- *Ἐκεῖνο τὸ βάραθρον* ‘that gulf’ uses a distal demonstrative for *questo*, outside the article–noun group as is regular in Greek. The second accusative object, *λαὸν τὸν ἐνοικοῦντα*, has an articular present active participle (masculine accusative singular of *ἐνοικέω*) in place of *che il possiede*: the people ‘dwells therein’ rather than ‘possesses it’.

> 70 Ma dimmi : Quei della palude pingue,  
> 71 che mena il vento, e che batte la pioggia,  
> 72 e che s’ incontran con sì aspre lingue,  

But tell me: Those of the fat marsh; those whom the wind leads, and whom the rain beats; and those who meet with tongues so sharp,

> 70 „ Ἀλλ’ εἰπέ μοι νῦν· οἱ τοῦ πηλώδους ἕλους,  
> („ But tell me now: those of the muddy marsh,)
>
> 71 „ Οἵ τ’ ἀπάγοντ’ ἀνέμῳ, πλήττονταί τ’ ὄμβρῳ,  
> („ And those driven by the wind, and struck by the rain,)
>
> 72 „ Οἵ τ’ ἀντικρουόμενοι χαλεποῖς λόγοις,  
> („ And those clashing together with harsh words,)

- *Ἀλλ’ εἰπέ μοι νῦν* adds ‘now’ to *Ma dimmi*. *Εἰπέ* is the 2nd singular second aorist active imperative used with *λέγω*; enclitic *μοι* is the dative of the person addressed. *Οἱ τοῦ πηλώδους ἕλους* closely follows *Quei della palude*: the nominative plural article stands for ‘those’, qualified by a genitive. *Πηλώδους* (of *πηλώδης*) agrees with neuter *ἕλους* (of *ἕλος*); ‘muddy’ interprets *pingue* rather than reproducing ‘fat’.
- *Οἵ τ’ ἀπάγοντ’ ἀνέμῳ, πλήττονταί τ’ ὄμβρῳ* makes the people the subjects of present passives: *ἀπάγοντ’* is elided *ἀπάγονται* (from *ἀπάγω*), and *πλήττονται* is from *πλήττω*. Italian *il vento* and *la pioggia*, subjects of active verbs, become instrumental datives *ἀνέμῳ* and *ὄμβρῳ*. The elided instances of connective *τε* link the descriptions; there is no fresh pronoun before *πλήττονται*.
- *Οἵ τ’ ἀντικρουόμενοι* ‘and those clashing’ uses the article with the masculine nominative plural present middle participle of *ἀντικρούω* in place of finite reciprocal *s’ incontran*. Instrumental *χαλεποῖς λόγοις* ‘with harsh words’ interprets the image *con sì aspre lingue* without retaining either the tongues or the intensifier *sì*.

> 73 perchè non dentro della città roggia  
> 74 son ei puniti, se Dio gli ha in ira ?  

— why are they not punished in the red city, if God's anger be upon them?

> 73 „ Πῶς οὐκ ἐν τῷ φλογερῷ τιμωροῦντ᾽ ἄστει,  
> („ Why are they not punished in the fiery city,)
>
> 74 „ Εἴπερ ὁ Θεὸς δι᾽ ὀργῆς αὐτοὺς ἔχει ;  
> („ If indeed God holds them in wrath?)

- *Πῶς οὐκ … τιμωροῦντ’* ‘how is it that they are not punished?’ resumes the preceding nominatives; *ei* needs no additional pronoun. *Τιμωροῦντ’* is *τιμωροῦνται*, contracted 3rd plural present passive indicative of *τιμωρέω*, with final *-αι* elided before *ἄστει*. The verb intervenes between *τῷ φλογερῷ* and *ἄστει* (dative singular of *ἄστυ*): *ἐν* with the dative renders *dentro della*, and ‘fiery’ interprets the colour adjective *roggia*.
- *Εἴπερ* ‘if indeed’ strengthens *se* with *-περ*. *Ὁ Θεὸς δι᾽ ὀργῆς αὐτοὺς ἔχει* keeps God as subject, *αὐτούς* as object and *ἔχει* as the verb corresponding to *ha*. The Greek idiom *δι᾽ ὀργῆς ἔχειν* ‘to regard with anger’ uses *διά* with the genitive, not a literal local equivalent of *in ira*.

> 75 e se non gli ha, perchè sono a tal foggia ?”  

and if not, why are they in such plight?"

> 75 „ Εἰ δὲ μή, πῶς πάσχουσιν ἀλλοιοτρόπως ;”  
> („ And if not, why do they suffer in another manner?”)

- *Εἰ δὲ μή* ‘but if not’ leaves the condition elliptical as in *e se non gli ha*, but does not repeat either pronoun or verb. *Πῶς πάσχουσιν* (3rd plural present active indicative of *πάσχω*) turns *perchè sono* into ‘how do they suffer?’ The adverb *ἀλλοιοτρόπως* means ‘in a different manner’, a shift from the demonstrative *a tal foggia*, ‘in such a manner’.

> 76 Ed egli a me : “ Perchè tanto delira,”  
> 77 disse, “lo ingegno tuo da quel ch’ ei suole ?  

And he [said] to me: "Wherefore errs thy mind so much beyond its wont?

> 76 Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπέ μοι · “ Διὰ τί τόσον πλανῶνται  
> (And he said to me: “Why wander so far)
>
> 77 „ Αἱ σαὶ φρένες παρ᾽ ὅ,τι δοκεῖς συνήθως ;  
> („ Your thoughts beyond what you usually seem?)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπέ μοι* places the whole reporting clause before the question, bringing forward the Italian *disse*. *Ὁ δέ* uses the article pronominally, ‘and he’; *εἶπε* is the 3rd singular second aorist active indicative corresponding to *λέγω*, and *μοι* renders *a me*.
- *Διὰ τί τόσον πλανῶνται αἱ σαὶ φρένες* ‘why do your thoughts wander so much?’ retains the postponed subject of *delira … lo ingegno tuo*, but makes it plural. *Πλανῶνται* is the contracted 3rd plural present middle/passive indicative of *πλανάω*, here intransitive in sense; *τόσον* is an accusative of degree. *Σαί* agrees with feminine nominative plural *φρένες* (from *φρήν*).
- *Παρ᾽ ὅ,τι* uses *παρά* with the accusative to express deviation from a standard, ‘beyond what’; *ὅ,τι* is the neuter relative, not the conjunction *ὅτι*. In *δοκεῖς συνήθως* ‘you usually think/seem’, *δοκεῖς* is 2nd singular: whereas *ei suole* refers back to *lo ingegno*, Greek makes the addressee the subject and puts the habitual sense of *suole* in the adverb *συνήθως*.

> 78 ovver la mente dove altrove mira ?  

or are thy thoughts turned somewhere else?

> 78 „ Μήπως ἀλλαχοῦ τὸ σὸν πνεῦμα σκοπεύει ;  
> („ Or is your mind looking elsewhere?)

- *Μήπως* introduces a tentative second question, ‘is it perhaps that …?’, rather than translating *ovver* by a disjunctive conjunction. *Ἀλλαχοῦ* ‘elsewhere’ suffices for *dove altrove*. Here Musurus returns to a singular subject, *τὸ σὸν πνεῦμα* ‘your mind’, and *σκοπεύει* (3rd singular present active indicative of *σκοπεύω*) keeps the active ‘looks’ of *mira*.

> 79 Non ti rimembra di quelle parole,  
> 80 con le quai la tua Etica pertratta  
> 81 le tre disposizion, che il ciel non vuole ;  

Rememberest thou not the words wherewith thy Ethics treat of the three dispositions which Heaven wills not,

> 79 „ Οὐ μέμνησαι τῶν λόγων, καθ᾽ οὓς συμφώνως  
> („ Do you not remember the words, by which in accordance)
>
> 80 „ Τοῖς σῆς Ἠθικῆς κανόσι τρεῖς τῇ θείᾳ  
> („ With the rules of your Ethics three dispositions)
>
> 81 „ Θελήσει διαθέσεις εἴσ᾽ ἐναντίαι,  
> („ Are opposed to the divine Will,)

- *Οὐ μέμνησαι τῶν λόγων* expects an affirmative answer. *Μέμνησαι* is the 2nd singular perfect middle indicative of *μιμνῄσκω*, with the present sense ‘you remember’; the thing remembered is in the genitive. Italian *ti* has no separate equivalent, and *quelle parole* loses its demonstrative.
- *Καθ᾽ οὓς* ‘according to which’ refers to *λόγων* but takes the accusative from *κατά*; after elision its final consonant is aspirated before the rough breathing. This replaces instrumental *con le quai*. *Συμφώνως τοῖς σῆς Ἠθικῆς κανόσι* ‘in accordance with the rules of your Ethics’ adds *κανόσι*, dative plural of *κανών*, governed by the adverb *συμφώνως*. The Italian subject *la tua Etica* becomes a dependent genitive, and *pertratta* is not translated as a verb.
- *Τρεῖς … διαθέσεις* is now the nominative plural subject, not the object of *pertratta*. *Τῇ θείᾳ Θελήσει* (dative singular of *θέλησις* with agreeing adjective) is set between numeral and noun. The relative clause *che il ciel non vuole* becomes an affirmative predicate: *εἴσ᾽ ἐναντίαι* ‘are opposed’, with elided *εἰσί* and a feminine nominative plural adjective governing the dative ‘to the divine will’.

> 82 incontinenza, malizia e la matta  
> 83 bestialitade? e come incontinenza  
> 84 men Dio offende, e men biasimo accatta ?  

incontinence, malice, and mad bestiality? and how incontinence less offends God, and receives less blame?

> 82 „ Ἀκρασία, κακία καὶ θηριότης ;  
> („ Incontinence, malice, and bestiality?)
>
> 83 „ Ὅπως τε πρὸς τὸν Θεὸν ἧττον προσκρούει  
> („ And how against God less offends)
>
> 84 „ Ἀκρασία, μομφῆς οὖσ᾽ ἧττον ἀξία ;  
> („ Incontinence, being less worthy of blame?)

- *Ἀκρασία, κακία καὶ θηριότης* are nominative singulars in apposition to *τρεῖς … διαθέσεις*. The nouns closely match *incontinenza, malizia* and *bestialitade*, but there is nothing modifying *θηριότης* to render *matta*.
- *Ὅπως τε* ‘and how’ continues the appeal to memory despite the intervening question mark. *Πρὸς τὸν Θεὸν … προσκρούει* ‘comes into collision with God’ uses *πρός* with the accusative where *offende* takes *Dio* directly. *Ἧττον* is the comparative adverb ‘less’. The subject *Ἀκρασία* comes after the 3rd singular present active *προσκρούει*, rather than before the predicate as in Italian.
- *Μομφῆς οὖσ᾽ ἧττον ἀξία* ‘being less worthy of blame’ subordinates the second coordinate verb *accatta*. *Οὖσ᾽* is elided *οὖσα*, feminine nominative singular present participle of *εἰμί*, agreeing with *Ἀκρασία*. Predicate *ἀξία* governs the genitive *μομφῆς*; the second *ἧττον* qualifies worthiness, rather than the amount of blame received.

> 85 Se tu riguardi ben questa sentenza,  
> 86 e rechiti alla mente chi son quelli,  
> 87 che su di fuor sostengon penitenza,  

If thou rightly considerest this doctrine, and recallest to thy memory who they are that suffer punishment above, without,

> 85 „ Εἰ συννοεῖς ἀκριβῶς τὴν γνώμην ταύτην,  
> („ If you reflect carefully upon this maxim,)
>
> 86 „ Καὶ διὰ μνήμης ἔχεις, τίνες ἐκεῖνοι,  
> („ And hold in memory who those are,)
>
> 87 „ Οἵπερ οὐκ ἐνταῦθ᾽, ἀλλ᾽ ἄνω τιμωροῦνται,  
> („ Who not here, but above are punished,)

- *Εἰ συννοεῖς ἀκριβῶς τὴν γνώμην ταύτην* closely follows the conditional construction of *Se tu riguardi ben questa sentenza*. *Συννοεῖς* is the contracted 2nd singular present active indicative of *συννοέω*, ‘consider’; *ἀκριβῶς* specifies ‘carefully, precisely’. The demonstrative follows the articular object in the regular order *τὴν γνώμην ταύτην*.
- *Καὶ διὰ μνήμης ἔχεις* ‘and you bear in mind’ continues the condition with another present indicative; it substitutes an idiom of retention for *rechiti alla mente*, ‘bring back to mind’. In the dependent question *τίνες ἐκεῖνοι* ‘who those [are]’, interrogative and demonstrative are masculine nominative plurals, and the copula corresponding to *son* is understood.
- *Οἵπερ* ‘the very ones who’ adds emphatic *-περ* to the plural relative. *Οὐκ ἐνταῦθ᾽, ἀλλ᾽ ἄνω* ‘not here, but above’ replaces the two local indications *su di fuor* with an explicit contrast; *ἐνταῦθ᾽* is *ἐνταῦθα* elided. *Τιμωροῦνται* ‘are punished’ again uses the simple present passive rather than a verb and noun as in *sostengon penitenza*.

> 88 tu vedrai ben perchè da questi felli  
> 89 sien dipartiti, e perchè men crucciata  
> 90 la divina giustizia gli martelli.”  

thou easily wilt see why they are separated from these fell spirits, and why, with less anger, Divine Justice strikes them."

> 88 „ Ὄψει τότε, πῶς εἰσι κεχωρισμένοι  
> („ You will see then how they are separated)
>
> 89 „ Τῶν ὧδ᾽ ἐνόχων, καὶ πῶς σφυρηλατοῦνται  
> („ From these guilty ones here, and how they are hammered)
>
> 90 „ Ἧττον ὑπὸ τῆς Θείας Δικαιοσύνης.” —  
> („ Less by Divine Justice.”—)

- *Ὄψει τότε* ‘you will then see’ supplies the consequence of the condition. *Ὄψει* is the 2nd singular future middle indicative of *ὁράω*, with active meaning; *τότε* marks the consequence temporally rather than rendering *ben* as an adverb of degree or ease. Both instances of *perchè* become *πῶς*, ‘how’.
- *Εἰσι κεχωρισμένοι* is a periphrastic perfect passive indicative: the 3rd plural present of *εἰμί* with the masculine nominative plural perfect participle of *χωρίζω*, ‘they stand separated’. It renders *sien dipartiti* without the Italian subjunctive. The genitive of separation *τῶν ὧδ᾽ ἐνόχων* recasts *da questi felli* as ‘from the guilty ones here’: *ἐνόχων* is substantival, and elided *ὧδε* supplies the local force of *questi*.
- *Σφυρηλατοῦνται* (3rd plural present passive indicative of *σφυρηλατέω*) preserves the concrete ‘hammer’ of *martelli* but reverses the construction: those struck remain the subject, and *ὑπὸ τῆς Θείας Δικαιοσύνης* gives the personal agent in the genitive. *Ἧττον* ‘less’ modifies the hammering itself; Greek has no equivalent of *crucciata* attributing the lesser anger to Justice.

## Lines 91–115 — Art, Nature, and Usury

> 91 “O Sol, che sani ogni vista turbata,  
> 92 tu mi contenti sì, quando tu solvi,  
> 93 che, non men che saver, dubbiar m’ aggrata.  

“O Sun! who healest all troubled vision, thou makest so glad when thou resolvest me, that to doubt is not less grateful than to know.

> 91 —“Ὦ φωστήρ, πᾶσαν ὅρασιν θεραπεύων  
> (—“O luminary, who heal every sight)
>
> 92 „ Θολήν, σύ με φωτίζων εὐφραίνεις, ὥστε  
> („ Dimmed, you gladden me by enlightening me, so that)
>
> 93 „ Οὐχ ἧττον φίλον εἰδέναι μ’ ἢ διστάζειν.  
> („ No less dear is it to me to know than to doubt.)

- *Ὦ φωστήρ* ‘O giver of light’ renders *O Sol* by a word for a luminary rather than by the specific name of the sun. In place of the relative clause *che sani*, Musurus has *θεραπεύων* ‘healing’ (pres. act. participle, nom. masc. sg., of *θεραπεύω*), agreeing with the person addressed, subsequently named as the subject *σύ*. Its object is *πᾶσαν ὅρασιν … θολήν* ‘every dimmed sight’: the adjective *θολήν* (acc. fem. sg.) renders the participle *turbata* and is separated from its noun by the participle and the line break.
- *σύ με φωτίζων εὐφραίνεις* ‘you gladden me by enlightening me’ keeps the emphatic *tu* and the personal object *mi*. But *quando tu solvi* becomes a circumstantial present active participle, *φωτίζων* (nom. masc. sg. of *φωτίζω*): enlightening is the means of gladdening, not a separately expressed temporal clause. The verb also takes up *φωστήρ* in a way that *solvi* does not take up *Sol*.
- *ὥστε* introduces the result corresponding to *sì … che*, without a separate word for *sì*. In *οὐχ ἧττον φίλον εἰδέναι μ’ ἢ διστάζειν*, *φίλον* is neuter singular, ‘dear’, with the copula understood; *μ’* is elided *με*, accusative subject of the infinitive *εἰδέναι* (perfect active of *οἶδα*, present in sense). *διστάζειν* is the present active infinitive of *διστάζω*, ‘to hesitate, doubt’. The comparison is reversed: Greek has ‘no less dear … to know than to doubt’, where Italian makes *dubbiar* the subject of *m’aggrata* and *saver* the standard of comparison. *οὐχ* is the form of *οὐ* before the rough breathing of *ἧττον*.

> 94 Ancora un poco indietro ti rivolvi,”  
> 95 diss’ io, “là dove di’ che usura offende  
> 96 la divina bontade, e il groppo svolvi.”  

Turn thee yet a little back” [I said], “to where thou sayest that usury offends the Divine Goodness, and unravel the knot.”

> 94 „ Μικρὸν ἐπάνελθε νῦν,” εἶπον, “ὀπίσω,  
> („ Return a little now,” I said, “backward,)
>
> 95 „ Ἔνθ’ εἶναι φῂς μισητὴν τοκοληψίαν  
> („ Where you say usury is hateful)
>
> 96 „ Παρὰ τῷ Θεῷ, κἀπορίαν μου λῦσον.”  
> („ In the sight of God, and resolve my perplexity.”)

- *Μικρὸν ἐπάνελθε νῦν … ὀπίσω* ‘go back a little now … backwards’ replaces reflexive *ti rivolvi* with the intransitive second aorist active imperative *ἐπάνελθε* (2nd sg. of *ἐπανέρχομαι*). *μικρόν* is an accusative of extent used adverbially; *νῦν* gives immediacy rather than the continuative force of *ancora*. *ὀπίσω* reinforces the compound verb. Musurus inserts *εἶπον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*) before this final adverb, whereas *diss’ io* follows the whole command.
- *ἔνθ’* is *ἔνθα* ‘where’, elided before *εἶναι*; it does the work of *là dove*. *φῂς* (2nd sg. pres. act. ind. of *φημί*) governs an accusative and infinitive in place of *di’ che* with a finite verb: *τοκοληψίαν* ‘taking of interest’ is the subject of *εἶναι*, and *μισητήν* ‘hateful’ its predicate, both accusative feminine singular. Thus active *usura offende la divina bontade* becomes ‘taking interest is hateful in God's sight’; *παρὰ τῷ Θεῷ* uses *παρά* with the dative of the person judging, without a noun for *bontade*.
- *κἀπορίαν* is crasis for *καὶ ἀπορίαν*. *ἀπορίαν μου* ‘my perplexity’ makes *il groppo* explicit and adds the possessive genitive. *λῦσον* (2nd sg. aor. act. imperative of *λύω*) retains something of *svolvi*: the Greek verb can mean both ‘loose’ and ‘resolve’.

> 97 “Filosofia,” mi disse, “a chi l’ attende,  
> 98 nota non pure in una sola parte,  

He said to me: “Philosophy, to him who hears it, points out, not in one place alone,

> 97 Ὁ δέ· “Φιλοσοφία τοὺς μελετῶντας  
> (And he: “Philosophy, to those who study)
>
> 98 „ Αὐτὴν ἐν πολλοῖς κεφαλαίοις διδάσκει,  
> („ Her, in many chapters teaches,)

- *ὁ δέ* ‘and he’ is the Greek formula for a change of speaker: the article stands pronominally, *δέ* is postpositive, and both verb and recipient in *mi disse* are left unexpressed.
- *Φιλοσοφία τοὺς μελετῶντας αὐτήν … διδάσκει* ‘philosophy teaches those who study it’ changes *a chi l’attende* from an indirect object in the singular to the personal accusative governed by *διδάσκει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *διδάσκω*). The article makes *μελετῶντας* (pres. act. participle, acc. masc. pl., of *μελετάω*) substantive; *αὐτήν*, its object, refers back to *Φιλοσοφία*.
- *ἐν πολλοῖς κεφαλαίοις* ‘in many chapters’ turns the negative *non pure in una sola parte* into a positive and gives *parte* a more specifically textual sense. Musurus places the phrase before the teaching verb; the matter taught follows it.

> 99 come natura lo suo corso prende  
> 100 dal divino intelletto e da sua arte;  

how Nature takes her course from the Divine Intellect, and from its art;

> 99 „ Πῶς φύσις ἕλκει τὴν ἑαυτῆς πορείαν  
> („ How nature draws her own course)
>
> 100 „ Ἀπὸ θείου τε νοὸς καὶ θείας τέχνης.  
> („ From the divine mind and from divine art.)

- *πῶς* introduces the indirect question after *διδάσκει*, corresponding to *come*. *φύσις ἕλκει τὴν ἑαυτῆς πορείαν* follows the shape of *natura lo suo corso prende*, but *ἕλκει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *ἕλκω*) is literally ‘draws’: the course is drawn from a source. *ἑαυτῆς* is the feminine singular reflexive genitive, ‘her own’, placed between article and noun and referring to *φύσις*.
- A single *ἀπό* governs both genitives in *ἀπὸ θείου τε νοὸς καὶ θείας τέχνης*. Enclitic *τε* and *καί* pair the two sources. *νοός* is the uncontracted genitive of *νόος* (Attic contracted *νοῦς*, gen. *νοῦ*). The repetition of the adjective, masculine *θείου* and feminine *θείας*, gives ‘divine mind and divine art’ rather than *divino intelletto e … sua arte*, with its possessive.

> 101 e se tu ben la tua Fisica note,  
> 102 tu troverai non dopo molte carte,  

and if thou note well thy Physics, thou wilt find, not many pages from the first,

> 101 „ Ἢν δὴ τὴν σὴν Φυσικὴν καλῶς διέλθῃς,  
> („ And if you read through your Physics carefully,)
>
> 102 „ Εὑρήσεις οὐχὶ μετὰ πολλὰς σελίδας  
> („ You will find after not many pages)

- *ἢν δή* ‘if, then’ has conditional *ἤν*, a form of *ἐάν*, with the subjunctive; *δή* marks the transition rather than simply translating *e*. *τὴν σὴν Φυσικὴν καλῶς διέλθῃς* keeps *la tua Fisica* as direct object, but makes *ben … note* an act of going carefully through the work. *διέλθῃς* is 2nd sg. second aor. act. subj. of *διέρχομαι*, viewing the reading as a whole.
- The future apodosis *εὑρήσεις* (2nd sg. fut. act. ind. of *εὑρίσκω*) closely matches *tu troverai*. *οὐχὶ μετὰ πολλὰς σελίδας* translates *non dopo molte carte* almost word for word: emphatic *οὐχί* negates the phrase, and *μετά* with the accusative means ‘after’. There is no expressed starting point corresponding to the English “from the first”.

> 103 che l’ arte vostra quella, quanto puote,  
> 104 segue, come il maestro fa il discente,  

that your art, as far as it can, follows her, as the scholar does his master;

> 103 „ Τὴν σαυτοῦ τέχνην, ὡς οἷόν τε, τῇ φύσει  
> („ That your own art, as far as it can, nature)
>
> 104 „ Ὀπαδεῖν, ὡς μαθητὴς τῷ διδασκάλῳ·  
> („ Follows, as a pupil follows his teacher;)

- Instead of *che … segue*, Greek continues *εὑρήσεις* with an accusative and infinitive: *τὴν σαυτοῦ τέχνην … ὀπαδεῖν* ‘that your own art follows’. *σαυτοῦ* is the contracted form of *σεαυτοῦ*, 2nd sg. reflexive genitive. Musurus thus carries on the singular address of *la tua Fisica*, rather than reproducing the shift to plural *l’arte vostra*.
- Parenthetic *ὡς οἷόν τε* ‘as far as is possible’ is impersonal, with *ἐστί* understood; Italian *quanto puote* makes art itself the subject of ‘can’. For pronominal *quella*, Musurus supplies the noun *τῇ φύσει*, dative complement of *ὀπαδεῖν* (pres. act. infinitive of *ὀπαδέω*, ‘to follow, attend’).
- *ὡς μαθητὴς τῷ διδασκάλῳ* puts the nominative subject ‘pupil’ before the dative ‘teacher’, reversing the order of *il maestro … il discente* and making the two roles unambiguous. There is no Greek counterpart to the substitute verb *fa*: the verb of following is understood.

> 105 sì che vostr’ arte a Dio quasi è nipote.  

so that your art is, as it were, the grandchild of the Deity.

> 105 „ Ὥσθ’ υἱωνὴ Θεοῦ τις ἔσθ’ ἡ σὴ τέχνη.  
> („ So that your art is as it were a grandchild of God.)

- *ὥσθ’* is *ὥστε* before the rough breathing of *υἱωνή*, with elision and aspiration of the final stop. Here it introduces a finite result clause corresponding to *sì che*. In *υἱωνὴ Θεοῦ τις ἔσθ’ ἡ σὴ τέχνη* the predicate comes first and the articular subject last: ‘a granddaughter of God of a sort is your art’. *υἱωνή* is specifically feminine; *Θεοῦ* is a genitive of relationship in place of Italian *a Dio*.
- Enclitic indefinite *τις* qualifies the predicate noun and conveys the reservation in *quasi*, rather than modifying the verb with an adverb. *ἔσθ’* represents *ἐστί* before rough-breathed *ἡ*, again with elision and aspiration. *σή* continues the singular possessive for *vostr’*.

> 106 Da queste due, se tu ti rechi a mente  
> 107 lo Genesi dal principio, conviene  
> 108 prender sua vita ed avanzar la gente.  

By these two, if thou recallest to thy memory Genesis at the beginning, it behoves man to gain his bread and [to prosper].

> 106 „ Ἐκ δύο πηγῶν τούτων, ἢν εἰς νοῦν φέρῃς  
> („ From these two sources, if you call to mind)
>
> 107 „ Τὴν ἐν ἀρχῇ Γένεσιν, δεῖ τοὺς ἀνθρώπους  
> („ Genesis at its beginning, mankind must)
>
> 108 „ Ἐξαντλεῖν τόν τε βίον καὶ τὴν οὐσίαν.  
> („ Draw forth both livelihood and substance.)

- *ἐκ δύο πηγῶν τούτων* ‘from these two sources’ expands *da queste due*: *πηγῶν* supplies a noun which also prepares for *ἐξαντλεῖν* ‘to draw out’. *ἐκ* governs the genitive plural; *δύο* is unchanged, and *πηγῶν τούτων* is plural, not dual.
- *ἢν εἰς νοῦν φέρῃς* ‘if you bring to mind’ renders *se tu ti rechi a mente* without a reflexive pronoun. After conditional *ἤν*, *φέρῃς* is 2nd sg. pres. act. subj. of *φέρω*. Its object is *τὴν ἐν ἀρχῇ Γένεσιν*: by placing *ἐν ἀρχῇ* between article and title, Musurus attaches ‘at the beginning’ to *Γένεσιν*, not to the act of remembering.
- Impersonal *δεῖ* corresponds to *conviene*; *τοὺς ἀνθρώπους* is the accusative subject of its infinitive, explicitly plural where Italian has collective *la gente*. The single present active infinitive *ἐξαντλεῖν* (from *ἐξαντλέω*) governs two objects linked by *τε … καί*, *τόν τε βίον καὶ τὴν οὐσίαν* ‘both livelihood and property’. Thus *prender sua vita ed avanzar* is no longer a pair of actions: the gain implied in *avanzar* is represented by a second thing drawn from the sources. The possessive *sua* is not expressed.

> 109 E perchè l’ usuriere altra via tiene,  
> 110 per sè natura, e per la sua seguace  
> 111 dispregia, poichè in altro pon la spene.  

And because the usurer takes another way, he contemns Nature in herself and in her follower, placing elsewhere his hope.

> 109 „ Ἐπεὶ δ’ ὁ τοκογλύφος ἄλλως διάγει,  
> („ And since the usurer lives otherwise,)
>
> 110 „ Τὴν φύσιν αὐτὴν καὶ τὴν ὀπαδὸν ταύτης  
> („ Nature herself and her follower)
>
> 111 „ Προσβάλλει· καὶ γὰρ εἰς ἄλλον ἐλπίδ’ ἔχει.—  
> („ He insults; for in another he places his hope.—)

- *ἐπεὶ δ’* ‘and since’ combines causal *ἐπεί* with postpositive *δέ*, elided before *ὁ*. *ὁ τοκογλύφος* names the person where *τοκοληψία* named the practice. *ἄλλως διάγει* ‘lives differently’ renders *altra via tiene* by an adverb and the present indicative *διάγει* (3rd sg. of *διάγω*, used absolutely of passing one's life), without the image of a road.
- *τὴν φύσιν αὐτὴν καὶ τὴν ὀπαδὸν ταύτης* makes two coordinated accusative objects for the delayed *προσβάλλει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *προσβάλλω*, here ‘assails, affronts’), stronger than *dispregia*. In *τὴν φύσιν αὐτήν*, *αὐτήν* outside the article–noun group is intensive, ‘nature herself’, for *per sè natura*. *τὴν ὀπαδόν* is feminine, ‘the follower’, echoing *ὀπαδεῖν* in line 104; the dependent demonstrative genitive *ταύτης* means ‘of this one’, for *sua*.
- *καὶ γάρ* introduces a separate explanatory assertion, ‘for indeed’, in place of subordinate *poichè*. *εἰς ἄλλον ἐλπίδ’ ἔχει* is literally ‘he has hope directed to another’: *ἐλπίδ’* is elided *ἐλπίδα*, acc. sg. of *ἐλπίς*, and *ἔχει* replaces *pon*. The directional phrase retains something of *in altro*, but masculine *ἄλλον* suggests ‘another one’ rather than the English adverb “elsewhere”.

> 112 Ma seguimi oramai, chè il gir mi piace :  

But follow me now, as it pleases me to go:

> 112 „ Ἀλλ’ ἕπου μοι νῦν· καιρὸς γὰρ τοῦ προβαίνειν·  
> („ But follow me now; for it is time to go forward:)

- *ἀλλ’* is elided *ἀλλά*. *ἕπου* is the 2nd sg. pres. middle imperative of deponent *ἕπομαι*, which takes dative *μοι* for the person followed, in contrast to the direct object in *seguimi*. *νῦν* gives the immediate force of *oramai*.
- *καιρὸς γὰρ τοῦ προβαίνειν* ‘for it is time to go forward’ has an understood *ἐστί* and a genitive articular infinitive defining the occasion (*προβαίνειν*, pres. act. of *προβαίνω*). Postpositive *γάρ* gives a reason, but the judgment of timeliness replaces the personal inclination of *il gir mi piace*: there is no verb of pleasing or counterpart to its *mi*.

> 113 chè i Pesci guizzan su per l’ orizzonta  

for the Fishes [are quivering] on the horizon,

> 113 „Οἵ τ᾿ Ἰχθῦς φαίνοντ᾿ ἐφ᾿ ὁρίζοντος ἤδη·  
> („And the Fishes already appear upon the horizon;)

- *οἵ τ’ Ἰχθῦς* has the nominative plural article accented before enclitic *τε* (elided before *Ἰχθῦς*). This connective introduces the first of the following observations rather than repeating a causal conjunction for the second *chè*.
- *φαίνοντ’* is poetic elision of *φαίνονται* (3rd pl. pres. middle/passive ind. of *φαίνω*, here ‘appear, are visible’). Musurus substitutes visibility for the motion of *guizzan*. *ἐφ’ ὁρίζοντος* is *ἐπί* with the genitive, with elision and aspiration before the rough breathing; it is locative rather than directional. Added *ἤδη* ‘already’ makes the time explicit.

> 114 e il Carro tutto sovra il Coro giace,  

and all the Wain lies over Caurus,

> 114 „Ἡ δ᾿ Ἅμαξα πᾶσ᾿ ἐπὶ τοῦ Χώρου κεῖται·  
> („And the Wain lies wholly over Caurus;)

- *ἡ δ’ Ἅμαξα πᾶσ’* combines postpositive *δέ* (elided *δ’*) with the feminine noun *Ἅμαξα* for masculine *Carro*. *πᾶσ’* is elided *πᾶσα*, nom. fem. sg.; outside the article–noun group it means ‘the Wain in its entirety’, closely following *il Carro tutto*.
- *ἐπὶ τοῦ Χώρου* again uses *ἐπί* with the genitive for position, rendering *sovra il Coro* and retaining the article with the name. *κεῖται* (3rd sg. pres. ind. of *κεῖμαι*, middle in form) directly matches *giace*, including its position at the end.

> 115 e il balzo via là oltre si dismonta.”  

and yonder far onwards we go down the cliff.”

> 115 „Κρημνοῦ δ᾿ ἡ κατάβασις γίνεται ’κεῖθεν.”  
> („And the descent down the cliff is yonder.”)

- *κρημνοῦ δ’ ἡ κατάβασις* ‘and the descent of the cliff’ fronts *κρημνοῦ* (gen. sg. of *κρημνός*) as Italian fronts *il balzo*, but makes it dependent on the action noun *κατάβασις*. Postpositive *δέ* is elided before *ἡ*. The articular noun is the grammatical subject: Italian impersonal *si dismonta* becomes a statement about a descent, not about an expressed ‘we’.
- *γίνεται* (3rd sg. pres. middle ind. of *γίγνομαι*, in the spelling *γίνομαι*) supplies the finite verb, ‘takes place’. Final *’κεῖθεν* has lost the initial vowel of *ἐκεῖθεν* by aphaeresis; its suffix properly means ‘from there’. The one adverb thus compresses *via là oltre*, pointing to the place from which the descent proceeds without separately rendering the distance.

## Summary — The Grammar of Distinction

Musurus often keeps Dante’s order of emphasis while changing what carries it grammatically. The rocks that form the bank in *che facevan* become the material of which the bank is composed: *συγκειμένης* agrees with the bank, and *ἐκ* introduces the rocks. The inscription’s *che diceva* likewise becomes a participle, but here *λέγοντα* agrees with the plural *γράμματα*, ‘letters’. Articular participles readily stand for whole descriptions of people: *τῶν πίστιν δόντων*, ‘of those who gave trust’, gives the present *fida* the force of a prior act. A change of voice can redistribute an entire sentence: where Dante makes the first round the agent of *tormenta*, Musurus makes the offenders the subject of *τιμωροῦνται*, ‘are punished’, and the round the place of punishment.

The particles mark connections precisely, though not always the same connections as in Italian. *Perchè* introducing a purpose becomes *ἵνα* with the subjunctive; inferential *onde* can become *ὅθεν*, ‘whence’, or *ὥστε* with an indicative stating the result as fact. Postpositive *δέ* often carries the transition between speakers, while paired *τε … καί* binds terms within an explanation. Elsewhere Musurus follows the Italian construction closely: the repeated *πρός* preserves the threefold direction of *a Dio, a sè, al prossimo*. Yet after *εὑρήσεις*, ‘you will find’, an accusative and infinitive replaces *che* with a finite verb. In the comparison that follows, *μαθητής*, ‘pupil’, is nominative and *τῷ διδασκάλῳ*, ‘the teacher’, dative: the cases make the roles explicit even as Musurus reverses Dante’s order.

Some of the most consequential departures turn on small changes of relation. The person who does not repose trust becomes one to whom trust *οὐκ ὀφείλεται*, ‘is not owed’; and *ἧττον*, ‘less’, qualifies the hammering itself rather than the anger of Justice. Conversely, a lexical echo can preserve a connection across several lines: *ὀπαδεῖν*, ‘to follow’, returns in *ὀπαδόν*, ‘follower’, just as *segue* returns in *seguace*. The most characteristic freedom here is not a departure from verbal precision, but a shift in its location: a distinction made by an image or a finite clause in Dante may emerge in Musurus through case, verbal aspect, or the placement of a single modifier.
