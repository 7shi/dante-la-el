# Inferno 26

## Lines 1–18 — Florence’s Renown and the Rugged Ascent

> 1 Godi, Fiorenza, poi che sei sì grande,  
> 2 che per mare e per terra batti l’ ali,  
> 3 e per l’ inferno il tuo nome si spande.  

Joy, Florence, since thou art so great that over land and sea thou beatest thy wings, and thy name through Hell expands itself!

> 1 Χαίροις, ὦ Φλωρεντία· σοῦ γὰρ τὸ κλέος  
> (Rejoice, O Florence; for your renown)
>
> 2 Περιΐπταται κατὰ γῆν τε καὶ πόντον,  
> (Flies about over both land and sea,)
>
> 3 Ἐκτείνεται δὲ καὶ μέχρι βυθῶν ᾍδου.  
> (And stretches even to the depths of Hell.)

- *Χαίροις* ‘may you rejoice’ is the 2nd sg. present active optative of *χαίρω*, an independent wish without *ἄν*, in place of the imperative *Godi*. The address *Fiorenza* becomes *ὦ Φλωρεντία*, with the vocative particle.
- *σοῦ γὰρ τὸ κλέος* ‘for your renown’ replaces *poi che sei sì grande, che* with an independent explanation introduced by postpositive *γάρ*. There is no equivalent of *sì grande* or its consecutive *che*. The possessive genitive *σοῦ* is brought forward, and *τὸ κλέος* (nom. sg. neut.) anticipates *il tuo nome* from line 3.
- *Περιΐπταται* ‘flies about’ (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of *περιΐπταμαι*, with active sense) makes renown itself the subject of the flight: neither the second-person *batti* nor its object *l’ ali* is separately rendered. *κατὰ γῆν τε καὶ πόντον* takes both nouns in the accusative of extent, reversing *per mare e per terra*; enclitic *τε* after the first noun pairs with *καί* before the second.
- *Ἐκτείνεται δὲ καί* ‘and it extends even’ retains the same subject. *ἐκτείνεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid./pass. ind. of *ἐκτείνω*) corresponds to the Italian pronominal *si spande*; *δέ* connects the predicates, while *καί* marks the further reach. *μέχρι βυθῶν ᾍδου* ‘as far as the depths of Hades’ makes *per l’ inferno* a limit rather than an extent traversed: *μέχρι* governs gen. pl. *βυθῶν*, on which the possessive gen. sg. *ᾍδου* depends.

> 4 Tra li ladron trovai cinque cotali  
> 5 tuoi cittadini, onde mi vien vergogna,  
> 6 e tu in grande onranza non ne sali.  

Among the thieves I found five such, thy citizens; whereat shame comes on me, and thou to great honour mountest not thereby.

> 4 Μεταξὺ λῃστῶν εὗρον πολίτας πέντε  
> (Among robbers I found five citizens)
>
> 5 Ἐκ τῶν σῶν, ὅπερ περιβάλλει μ᾽ αἰσχύνῃ,  
> (Of yours, which wraps me in shame,)
>
> 6 Οὔτε σοι τιμὴν μεγάλην περιάπτει.  
> (Nor attaches great honour to you.)

- *Μεταξὺ λῃστῶν* ‘among robbers’ uses *μεταξύ* as a preposition with the genitive plural of *λῃστής*. *εὗρον* is the 1st sg. second aorist active indicative of *εὑρίσκω*, corresponding directly to *trovai*.
- *πολίτας πέντε ἐκ τῶν σῶν* ‘five citizens from among yours’ brings the noun (*πολίτας*, acc. pl. of *πολίτης*) before the indeclinable numeral and omits the characterization *cotali*. Instead of attaching *tuoi* directly to *cittadini*, Musurus adds a partitive phrase: *ἐκ* governs the substantivized possessive *τῶν σῶν* ‘your people’.
- *ὅπερ περιβάλλει μ᾽ αἰσχύνῃ* ‘which very thing clothes me in shame’ converts the adverbial *onde* into a neuter singular relative subject referring to the whole preceding fact; *-περ* gives the relative emphasis. In place of *mi vien vergogna*, with shame as subject, *περιβάλλει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *περιβάλλω*) takes the person affected in the accusative (*μ᾽*, elided *με*) and that with which he is clothed in the dative (*αἰσχύνῃ*).
- *Οὔτε σοι τιμὴν μεγάλην περιάπτει* ‘nor does it attach great honour to you’ continues with *ὅπερ* understood as subject. *οὔτε* introduces the negative second predicate without a preceding negative member. Where *tu … non ne sali* makes Florence the subject and *ne* expresses the source of honour, *περιάπτει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *περιάπτω*) makes honour the accusative object and Florence the dative recipient, *σοι*.

> 7 Ma se presso al mattin il ver si sogna,  
> 8 tu sentirai di qua da picciol tempo  
> 9 di quel che Prato, non ch’ altri, t’ agogna.  

But if the truth is dreamed of near the morning, thou shalt feel ere long what Prato, not to speak of others, craves for thee.

> 7 Ἀλλ᾽ εἰ πρωΐας ἐνύπνι᾽ ἀληθεύει,  
> (But if morning dreams speak true,)
>
> 8 Γνώσῃ μετὰ πάροδον οὐ μακροῦ χρόνου,  
> (You shall know after the passing of no long time,)
>
> 9 Τί σοι Πρᾶτον εὔχεται, πρὸς δέ τε κἄλλοι.  
> (What Prato wishes for you, and others besides.)

- *Ἀλλ᾽ εἰ* (elided *ἀλλά* before *εἰ*) closely follows *Ma se*. *πρωΐας* ‘in the morning’ is a genitive of time, not a phrase expressing proximity like *presso al mattin*. *ἐνύπνι᾽* is elided *ἐνύπνια* ‘dreams’, nom. pl. neut. of *ἐνύπνιον*. Instead of *il ver si sogna*, dreams are the subject and truth is expressed by the verb *ἀληθεύει* ‘speaks true’ (pres. act. ind. of *ἀληθεύω*), singular in the usual Attic agreement with a neuter plural subject.
- The open condition has present indicative after *εἰ* and future indicative in its main clause. *Γνώσῃ* ‘you will know’ (2nd sg. fut. mid. ind. of *γιγνώσκω*, active in sense) interprets *sentirai* as coming to know. *μετὰ πάροδον οὐ μακροῦ χρόνου* ‘after the passing of no long time’ expands *di qua da picciol tempo*: temporal *μετά* governs acc. sg. *πάροδον*, and *οὐ μακροῦ χρόνου* is the dependent genitive.
- *Τί σοι Πρᾶτον εὔχεται* makes *di quel che … t’ agogna* an indirect question dependent on *Γνώσῃ*. Interrogative *τί* is the accusative object, *σοι* the person for whom the wish is made, and *Πρᾶτον* the nominative neuter subject of *εὔχεται* (3rd sg. pres. mid. ind. of the deponent *εὔχομαι* ‘pray, wish’).
- *πρὸς δέ τε κἄλλοι* ‘and others besides’ gives the implication of *non ch’ altri* affirmatively. *πρός* is adverbial, ‘besides’; postpositive *δέ* and enclitic additive *τε* link the afterthought, and *κἄλλοι* is crasis of *καὶ ἄλλοι*. The added subject is nominative plural, with the act of wishing understood.

> 10 E se già fosse, non saria per tempo;  
> 11 così foss’ ei da che pure esser dee :  

And if it were already _come_, it would not be too early; so were it! since indeed it must be:

> 10 Εἰ δὲ συμβῆναι μέλλει, βέλτιον τάχει·  
> (And if it is fated to happen, better with speed:)
>
> 11 Τί γὰρ ὄφελος, εἰ πέπρωται γενέσθαι;  
> (For what is the use, if it is destined to come to pass?)

- *Εἰ δὲ συμβῆναι μέλλει* ‘but if it is to happen’ replaces the supposition *se già fosse* with a present indicative looking forward. *συμβῆναι* is the second aorist active infinitive of *συμβαίνω*, complementary to *μέλλει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *μέλλω*). The aorist presents the happening as a whole, not as past; *già* disappears, and the prospect expressed by *esser dee* is brought forward.
- *βέλτιον τάχει* ‘better quickly’ is a verbless judgment: *βέλτιον*, the neuter singular comparative of *ἀγαθός*, is predicative, and *τάχει* is the dative singular of *τάχος* used adverbially. It replaces the conditional negative *non saria per tempo* with a positive preference and conveys the force of *così foss’ ei* without a corresponding verb of wishing.
- *Τί γὰρ ὄφελος* ‘for what use [is it]?’ supplies a rhetorical question absent from the Italian. *τί* qualifies neuter singular *ὄφελος*, with the copula omitted; postpositive *γάρ* makes the question a reason for the preceding judgment. *εἰ πέπρωται γενέσθαι* ‘if it is destined to come about’ turns causal *da che pure esser dee* into a conditional premise. *πέπρωται* is an impersonal 3rd sg. perfect passive indicative, expressing a settled allotment; *γενέσθαι*, the second aorist middle infinitive of *γίγνομαι*, specifies what is destined.

> 12 chè più mi graverà, com’ più m’ attempo.  

for it will weigh the heavier on me as I grow older.

> 12 Γεραιτέρῳ μοι δυσφορώτερον τἆχθος.  
> (To me grown older, harder to bear is the burden.)

- *Γεραιτέρῳ μοι* ‘to me when older’ compresses *com’ più m’ attempo* into a comparative adjective agreeing with the speaker: *γεραιτέρῳ* is dat. sg. masc. of *γεραίτερος*, comparative of *γεραιός*, and *μοι* is the enclitic dative of the person affected. The adjective is predicative, not an adverb modifying the act of bearing.
- *δυσφορώτερον τἆχθος* ‘harder to bear [is] the burden’ replaces *più mi graverà* with a nominal clause. *τἆχθος* is crasis of *τὸ ἄχθος*, the nominative neuter singular subject; *δυσφορώτερον* is the agreeing comparative of *δύσφορος*. The two comparatives preserve the relation between the two Italian instances of *più*, but neither the future copula nor a conjunction answering to *chè* is expressed.

> 13 Noi ci partimmo, e su per le scalee,  
> 14 che n’avean fatte i borni a scender pria,  
> 15 rimontò il duca mio, e trasse mee ;  

We departed thence; and, by the stairs which the curbstones had [made for] us to descend before, my Guide remounted and drew me up;

> 13 Ἀπήλθομεν οὖν, καὶ διὰ τῶν κλιμάκων,  
> (So we departed, and along the steps,)
>
> 14 Δι᾽ ὧν τοὺς κρημνοὺς κατέβημεν τὰ πρῶτα,  
> (By which we had first descended the crags,)
>
> 15 Ὁ Προστάτης ἀνέβη κἀνείλκυσέ με.  
> (The Master climbed up and drew me up.)

- *Ἀπήλθομεν οὖν* ‘so we departed’ has the person of *Noi ci partimmo* in the ending alone. *ἀπήλθομεν* is the 1st pl. second aorist active indicative of *ἀπέρχομαι*, with augmented *ἦλθ-* after the preverb. The compound expresses departure, without a separate reflexive or locative pronoun; transitional, postpositive *οὖν* is added.
- *καὶ διὰ τῶν κλιμάκων* ‘and by way of the stairs’ depends on *ἀνέβη* in line 15. *διά* with gen. pl. *κλιμάκων* (from *κλίμαξ*) specifies the route; the upward direction of *su per* is left to the verb.
- *Δι᾽ ὧν τοὺς κρημνοὺς κατέβημεν τὰ πρῶτα* ‘by which we descended the crags at first’ repeats *διά*, now elided, with the gen. pl. relative *ὧν* referring to the stairs. Instead of making *i borni* the subject of *n’avean fatte*, Musurus makes the travellers the subject of *κατέβημεν* (1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *καταβαίνω*) and *τοὺς κρημνούς* the accusative of the ground descended. There is no verb of making: the infinitive *a scender* becomes the finite verb, and *pria* becomes adverbial acc. pl. neut. *τὰ πρῶτα*.
- *Ὁ Προστάτης* ‘the leader’ precedes rather than follows *ἀνέβη* and has no possessive answering to *mio*. *ἀνέβη* (3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ἀναβαίνω*) answers *κατέβημεν* with the opposite prefix, expressing ascent but not separately the repetition in *rimontò*. *κἀνείλκυσέ με* ‘and pulled me up’ has crasis of *καί* with *ἀνείλκυσε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀνέλκω*, augmented after the prefix); the additional acute on its final syllable is due to enclitic *με*.

> 16 e proseguendo la solinga via  
> 17 tra le schegge e tra’ rocchi dello scoglio,  
> 18 lo piè senza la man non si spedia.  

and pursuing our solitary way among the jaggs and branches of the cliff, the foot without the hand sped not.

> 16 Βαινόντων δ᾽ ἡμῶν τὴν ἐρημαίαν τρίβον,  
> (And as we walked along the solitary path,)
>
> 17 Μέσον ἀγμάτων καὶ πετρῶν τῆς γεφύρας,  
> (Amid the crags and rocks of the bridge,)
>
> 18 Ὁ ποῦς ἄνευ τῆς χειρὸς οὐκ εὐωδοῦτο.  
> (The foot without the hand could not make good way.)

- *Βαινόντων δ᾽ ἡμῶν* ‘and as we walked’ turns *e proseguendo* into a genitive absolute. *βαινόντων* is the gen. pl. masc. present active participle of *βαίνω*, with its subject *ἡμῶν* in agreement; its present stem marks action concurrent with the main verb. *δ᾽* is elided postpositive *δέ*. The absolute construction distinguishes the travellers from the grammatical subject of the main clause, *ὁ πούς*.
- *τὴν ἐρημαίαν τρίβον* ‘the solitary track’ retains the Italian noun phrase as an accusative, here of the route travelled with *βαίνω*. *ἐρημαίαν* (acc. sg. fem. of *ἐρημαῖος*) stands in attributive position before feminine *τρίβον*.
- *Μέσον ἀγμάτων καὶ πετρῶν* ‘amid fragments and rocks’ uses neuter acc. sg. *μέσον* adverbially as a preposition governing two genitive plurals, *ἀγμάτων* from *ἄγμα* ‘broken piece’ and *πετρῶν* from *πέτρα*. Unlike *tra le schegge e tra’ rocchi*, the Greek does not repeat the preposition. *τῆς γεφύρας* ‘of the bridge’ interprets *dello scoglio* by naming the structure rather than the rock.
- *Ὁ ποῦς ἄνευ τῆς χειρός* closely preserves the singular nouns and order of *lo piè senza la man*: *ποῦς* is nominative subject and *ἄνευ* governs gen. sg. *χειρός* (from *χείρ*). *οὐκ εὐωδοῦτο* ‘did not make good progress’ answers *non si spedia* with the imperfect middle/passive indicative, 3rd sg., of *εὐοδόω*. The internal lengthening to *ω* marks the augment, and the contracted ending *-οῦτο* belongs to the middle/passive inflection; as in the Italian, the imperfect describes the ongoing difficulty rather than a single failed step.

## Lines 19–51 — Grief and the Enveloping Flames

> 19 Allor mi dolsi, ed ora mi ridoglio,  
> 20 quand' io drizzo la mente a ciò ch' io vidi;  

I sorrowed then, and sorrow now again when I direct my memory to what I saw;

> 19 Τότ᾽ ἐλυπούμην, ἀλλὰ καὶ λυποῦμ᾽ ἔτι  
> (Then I grieved, and I still grieve even now)
>
> 20 Ἀναλογιζόμενος, οἷά τιν᾽ εἶδον.  
> (When I reflect on what things I saw.)

- *Τότ᾽ ἐλυπούμην* ‘then I was grieving’ renders *Allor mi dolsi*: the imperfect middle/passive *ἐλυπούμην* (1st sg. of *λυπέω*, middle/passive ‘feel grief’) presents as continuing what the Italian puts in the simple past. *Τότ᾽* is *τότε* with final vowel elided. *Ἀλλὰ καὶ λυποῦμ᾽ ἔτι* ‘but I am also grieving still’ sets the contracted present *λυποῦμαι* against the imperfect of the same verb. *Ἔτι* expresses persistence rather than the renewal in *ora mi ridoglio*.
- *Ἀναλογιζόμενος* ‘as I reflect’ replaces the finite temporal clause *quand’ io drizzo la mente* with a present middle participle (nom. masc. sg. of *ἀναλογίζομαι*) agreeing with the speaker. Instead of the directional *a ciò ch’ io vidi*, the verb takes as its content *οἷά τιν᾽ εἶδον* ‘what manner of things I saw’. *Οἷα* is neut. acc. pl., qualified by indefinite *τινά*, elided before *εἶδον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*).

> 21 e più lo ingegno affreno ch' io non soglio,  
> 22 perchè non corra, che virtù nol guidi;  

and curb my genius more than I am wont, lest it run where Virtue guides it not;

> 21 Μᾶλλον δ᾽ ἤ ποτε φρενὸς ἐκράτουν, ἵνα  
> (And more than ever I curbed my mind, so that)
>
> 22 Δίχ᾽ ἀρετῆς χαλινοῦ μὴ παρατρέχῃ.  
> (Without the bridle of virtue it might not run astray.)

- *Μᾶλλον δ᾽ ἤ ποτε* ‘and more than ever’ brings the comparison together at the front; *ποτε* ‘at any time’ replaces the clause of habitual action *ch’ io non soglio*. *Φρενὸς ἐκράτουν* ‘I kept control of my mind’ has the genitive of the thing controlled (*φρενός*, from *φρήν*) with *ἐκράτουν* (1st sg. impf. act. ind. of *κρατέω*). The Italian has a direct object, *lo ingegno*, and a present, *affreno*, not a past.
- The bridle implicit in *affreno* reappears as a noun in *δίχ᾽ ἀρετῆς χαλινοῦ* ‘apart from virtue’s bridle’: elided *δίχα* governs *χαλινοῦ*, on which *ἀρετῆς* depends. *Ἵνα … μὴ παρατρέχῃ* is a negative purpose clause, with the present active subjunctive (3rd sg. of *παρατρέχω*) retained after a past verb rather than changed to an optative. The single verb ‘run astray’ and the phrase with *δίχα* take the place of *non corra, che virtù nol guidi*; virtue is no longer the subject of a verb of guiding.

> 23 sì che se stella buona o miglior cosa  
> 24 m' ha dato il ben, ch' io stesso nol m' invidi.  

so that, if kindly star or something better have given to me the good, I may not grudge myself that gift.

> 23 Ὥστ᾽ εἴ τις ἀστὴρ ἀγαθός, ἤ τι κρεῖττον,  
> (So that if some kindly star, or something higher,)
>
> 24 Καλὰ μοὔδωκεν, ἐμαυτῷ μὴ φθονεῖν με.  
> (Has given me good gifts, I may not begrudge them to myself.)

- *Ὥστ᾽* (elided *ὥστε*) introduces a contemplated result, *ἐμαυτῷ μὴ φθονεῖν με* ‘for me not to begrudge myself’. Where *sì che … io stesso nol m’ invidi* has a finite subjunctive, Greek has the present active infinitive of *φθονέω* negated by *μή*, its subject explicit in the accusative *με* and the person begrudged in the reflexive dative *ἐμαυτῷ*. There is no expressed equivalent of the object *lo*.
- The intervening condition has *εἴ τις ἀστὴρ ἀγαθός, ἤ τι κρεῖττον* ‘if some good star, or something better’: indefinite *τις* is nominative masculine with *ἀστήρ*, whereas *τι κρεῖττον* is nominative neuter, the comparative doing the work of *miglior cosa*. *Καλὰ μοὔδωκεν* ‘gave me good things’ substitutes a substantival neuter plural for singular *il ben*. *Μοὔδωκεν* combines *μοι* and *ἔδωκεν* by crasis; the latter is 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *δίδωμι*, against the compound past *m’ ha dato*.

> 25 Quante il villan, ch' al poggio si riposa,  

As many fireflies as the peasant who is resting on the hill—

> 25 Ὅσας χωρίτης κείμενος ἐπὶ λόφου,  
> (As many as the peasant resting on the hill,)

- *Ὅσας* ‘as many as’ opens the comparison, but its noun, *πυγολαμπίδας*, is withheld until line 29, as *lucciole* is in Italian. The relative adjective is feminine accusative plural in agreement with that noun, not nominative with *χωρίτης* ‘countryman’, the anarthrous subject of the delayed verb *βλέπει*.
- *Κείμενος ἐπὶ λόφου* ‘lying on a hill’ reduces *ch’ al poggio si riposa* to a circumstantial participle, nom. masc. sg. present of *κεῖμαι*, agreeing with *χωρίτης*. The Greek specifies posture; *ἐπί* with the genitive denotes position.

> 26 nel tempo che colui che il mondo schiara  
> 27 la faccia sua a noi tien meno ascosa,  
> 28 come la mosca cede alla zanzara,  

at the time that he who lights the world least hides his face from us, when the fly yields to the gnat—

> 26 Καθ᾽ ἣν ἐποχὴν ὁ τὸ σύμπαν φωτίζων  
> (At the season when he who lights the universe)
>
> 27 Ἥττονα χρόνον ἡμῖν τὴν ὄψιν κρύπτει,  
> (Hides his face from us for a shorter time,)
>
> 28 Ὅτε κώνωπες διαδέχονται μυίας,  
> (When gnats succeed the flies,)

- *Καθ᾽ ἣν ἐποχήν* ‘at which season’ introduces the temporal qualification within the comparison. *Καθ᾽* is *κατά* elided with aspiration before the rough breathing of *ἥν*; the relative adjective agrees with *ἐποχήν* in the accusative governed by the preposition. *Ὁ τὸ σύμπαν φωτίζων* ‘the one illuminating the universe’ replaces *colui che il mondo schiara* with an articular present active participle (nom. masc. sg. of *φωτίζω*) enclosing its object *τὸ σύμπαν*, literally ‘the whole’.
- *Ἥττονα χρόνον* ‘for a shorter time’ is an accusative of duration, making explicitly temporal the degree expressed by *meno ascosa*. *Ἡμῖν τὴν ὄψιν κρύπτει* ‘hides the face from us’ retains the dative corresponding to *a noi*, but uses a simple present active verb for *tien … ascosa* and the article instead of *sua*.
- *Ὅτε κώνωπες διαδέχονται μυίας* recasts *come la mosca cede alla zanzara*: *ὅτε* is explicitly temporal, both insect names become plural, and the successors, *κώνωπες*, become the subject. *Διαδέχονται* (3rd pl. pres. mid. ind. of *διαδέχομαι*, ‘succeed, take over from’) takes the predecessors, *μυίας*, in the accusative.

> 29 vede lucciole giù per la vallea,  
> 30 forse colà dove vendemmia ed ara:  

sees down along the valley, there perchance where he gathers grapes and tills:

> 29 Βλέπει πυγολαμπίδας ἐν τῇ κοιλάδι,  
> (Sees fireflies down in the valley,)
>
> 30 Ἔνθ᾽ ἀροτριᾷ καὶ τὴν τρύγην συλλέγει,  
> (Where he ploughs and gathers the vintage,)

- *Βλέπει πυγολαμπίδας* supplies the governing verb and the noun anticipated by *ὅσας*: *πυγολαμπίδας* is acc. pl. of *πυγολαμπίς*. *Ἐν τῇ κοιλάδι* (dat. sg. of *κοιλάς*) gives a location, ‘in the valley’, without separate equivalents for *giù per*.
- *Ἔνθ᾽* (elided *ἔνθα*) ‘where’ takes up the location without the qualification *forse*. The two occupations change order: *ἀροτριᾷ* (3rd sg. contracted pres. act. ind. of *ἀροτριάω*) renders *ara*, and *τὴν τρύγην συλλέγει* ‘gathers the vintage’ expands *vendemmia* into verb and object.

> 31 di tante fiamme tutta risplendea  
> 32 l' ottava bolgia, sì com' io m' accorsi,  
> 33 tosto ch' io fui là 've il fondo parea.  

with flames thus numerous the eighth chasm was all gleaming, as I perceived, so soon as I came to where the bottom shewed itself.

> 31 Τοσαύταις φλοξὶν ὁ πᾶς ἐφεγγοβόλει  
> (With so many flames shone bright all over)
>
> 32 Ὄγδοος βόθρος, ὡς εἶδον ἅμ᾽ ἐκεῖσε  
> (The eighth ditch, as I saw as soon as thither)
>
> 33 Ἀναβάς, ὅθεν ἐσκοπεῖτο τὸ βάθος.  
> (I had gone up, whence the depth could be beheld.)

- *Τοσαύταις φλοξίν* ‘with so many flames’ answers *ὅσας*: the correlative adjectives agree with their respective nouns, but in different cases, since the flames are the means by which the ditch shines. *Φλοξίν* is dat. pl. of *φλόξ*. *Ὁ πᾶς … ὄγδοος βόθρος* makes *tutta* an adjective of the whole ditch; *ἐφεγγοβόλει* (3rd sg. impf. act. ind. of *φεγγοβολέω*) intervenes between *πᾶς* and the rest of its noun phrase.
- *Ὡς εἶδον* ‘as I saw’ renders *sì com’ io m’ accorsi* by a verb of sight. *Ἅμ᾽ ἐκεῖσε ἀναβάς* ‘as soon as I had gone up there’ replaces *tosto ch’ io fui là* with *ἅμα* elided and an aorist active participle (nom. masc. sg. of *ἀναβαίνω*). Greek specifies ascent, with directional *ἐκεῖσε* rather than a static ‘there’.
- *Ὅθεν ἐσκοπεῖτο τὸ βάθος* ‘from where the depth was in view’ specifies the point **from** which one looks. *Τὸ βάθος* ‘the depth’ replaces *il fondo* as subject; *ἐσκοπεῖτο* is 3rd sg. impf. middle/passive of *σκοπέω*, passive here, rather than a verb meaning ‘appear’ like *parea*.

> 34 E qual colui che si vengiò con gli orsi  
> 35 vide il carro d' Elia al dipartire,  
> 36 quando i cavalli al cielo erti levorsi,  

And as he, who was avenged by the bears, saw Elijah’s chariot at its departure, when the horses rose erect to heaven,—

> 34 Ὡς δ᾽ ἐκεῖνος, ὃς δίκην λαβὼν δι᾽ ἄρκτων  
> (And as he, who avenged himself by means of bears)
>
> 35 Εἶδ᾿ ἐφ᾿ ἅρματος ἀναληφθέντ᾿ Ἠλίαν,  
> (Saw Elijah caught up upon a chariot,)
>
> 36 Ὁρμῶντας τ᾿ εἰς οὐρανὸν εὐτόλμους ἵππους,  
> (And the bold horses rushing into heaven,)

- *Ὡς δ᾽ ἐκεῖνος, ὃς δίκην λαβὼν δι᾽ ἄρκτων* begins ‘and as that man, who, having obtained satisfaction through bears’. *Δίκην λαβών* uses an aorist active participle (nom. masc. sg. of *λαμβάνω*) with its object to render finite reflexive *si vengiò*; *διά* with gen. pl. *ἄρκτων* expresses agency or means. This rearranges the syntax: it is the relative *ὅς* that now supplies a subject for *εἶδ᾿*, leaving the opening *ὡς … ἐκεῖνος* loosely suspended.
- *Εἶδ᾿* is elided *εἶδε*, 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*. Its first object is not *il carro d’ Elia* but *Ἠλίαν* (acc. masc. sg.), with the supplementary participle *ἀναληφθέντ᾿* (elided *ἀναληφθέντα*, acc. masc. sg. aor. pass. of *ἀναλαμβάνω*): ‘saw Elijah taken up’. The chariot becomes a phrase of position, *ἐφ᾿ ἅρματος*, *ἐπί* elided and aspirated before the genitive.
- *Ὁρμῶντας τ᾿ … εὐτόλμους ἵππους* adds a second object of perception, ‘and bold horses rushing’: postpositive *τε* coordinates it with the first, and *ὁρμῶντας* is acc. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *ὁρμάω*. The ongoing motion perceived replaces the finite temporal clause *quando … levorsi*. *Εἰς οὐρανόν* retains the destination, but *εὐτόλμους* ‘bold’ is an added quality, not the posture expressed by *erti*.

> 37 chè nol potea sì con gli occhi seguire  
> 38 ch' ei vedesse altro che la fiamma sola,  
> 39 sì come nuvoletta, in su salire:  

for he could not so follow it with his eyes as to see other than the flame alone, like a little cloud, ascending up:

> 37 Οὐ δυνάμενος διασκοπεῖν τὴν ἄρσιν,  
> (Unable to follow the ascent with his eyes,)
>
> 38 Οὐδ᾿ ἄλλο τι καθορῶν ἢ φλόγα μόνην  
> (And beholding nothing else save the flame alone)
>
> 39 Ἀνυψουμένην ἐν σχήματι νεφέλης·  
> (Rising upward in the likeness of a cloud;)

- *Οὐ δυνάμενος διασκοπεῖν τὴν ἄρσιν* ‘being unable to observe the ascent closely’ gives the force of causal *chè* by a circumstantial present middle participle (nom. masc. sg. of *δύναμαι*). Its complementary present active infinitive *διασκοπεῖν* conveys close visual attention without a separate phrase for *con gli occhi*; the action noun *τὴν ἄρσιν* replaces the pronominal object of *seguire*.
- *Οὐδ᾿ ἄλλο τι καθορῶν ἢ φλόγα μόνην* ‘and discerning nothing else than a flame alone’ coordinates a second negated nominative participle (*καθορῶν*, contracted pres. act. of *καθοράω*) instead of reproducing the result construction *sì … ch’ ei vedesse*. *Ἄλλο τι* is neut. acc. sg.; after exceptive *ἤ*, *μόνην* agrees with *φλόγα*.
- *Ἀνυψουμένην* (acc. fem. sg. pres. middle/passive participle of *ἀνυψόω*) agrees with the flame perceived, where Italian has the infinitive *salire*. *Ἐν σχήματι νεφέλης* ‘in the shape of a cloud’ recasts *sì come nuvoletta* as *ἐν* with dat. sg. *σχήματι* and a dependent genitive; *νεφέλης* does not reproduce the diminutive.

> 40 tal si movea ciascuna per la gola  
> 41 del fosso, chè nessuna mostra il furto,  
> 42 ed ogni fiamma un peccatore invola.  

thus moved each of *those flames* along the gullet of the foss, for none of them shews the theft, and every flame steals a sinner.

> 40 Τοιαῦται φλόγες ἐν τῷ βάθει τοῦ βόθρου  
> (Even such flames in the depth of the ditch)
>
> 41 Ἐκινοῦντο μὴ δεικνύουσαι τὸ κλέμμα,  
> (Were moving, not showing what they stole,)
>
> 42 Ἀλλ᾿ ἑκάστης κρυπτούσης ἀλάστορ᾿ ἕνα.  
> (But each concealing a single accursed spirit.)

- *Τοιαῦται φλόγες … ἐκινοῦντο* ‘such flames were moving’ answers *ὡς* with an adjective of likeness qualifying a plural subject, instead of adverbial *tal* with singular *ciascuna*. *Ἐκινοῦντο* is 3rd pl. contracted impf. middle/passive of *κινέω*, intransitive here like *si movea*. *Ἐν τῷ βάθει τοῦ βόθρου* ‘in the depth of the ditch’ replaces motion along the figurative *gola* with a location.
- *Μὴ δεικνύουσαι τὸ κλέμμα* ‘not displaying the thing stolen’ replaces *chè nessuna mostra il furto* with a negated present active participle of *δείκνυμι*, nom. fem. pl. with *φλόγες*. There is no separate causal conjunction or negative pronoun: *μή* qualifies the participial description of the flames collectively.
- *Ἀλλ᾿ ἑκάστης κρυπτούσης ἀλάστορ᾿ ἕνα* ‘but each hiding one accursed being’ replaces additive *ed* with adversative *ἀλλά* and changes to a genitive absolute: *ἑκάστης* is its subject and *κρυπτούσης* its pres. act. participle (from *κρύπτω*). The absolute singles out each of the very flames already serving as subject. The action of *invola* is interpreted as concealment; elided *ἀλάστορα* (acc. sg. of *ἀλάστωρ*) is more charged than *peccatore*, and the numeral *ἕνα* stresses ‘one’.

> 43 Io stava sopra il ponte a veder surto,  
> 44 sì che, s' io non avessi un ronchion preso,  
> 45 caduto sarei giù senza esser urto.  

I stood upon the bridge, *having* risen so to look, that, if I had not caught a rock, I should have fallen down without being pushed.

> 43 Ἱστάμην ὀρθός, βλέπων ἐκ τῆς γεφύρας  
> (I stood upright, looking from the bridge)
>
> 44 Οὕτως, ὥστ᾿, εἰ μὴ στεῤῥᾶς εἰχόμην πέτρας,  
> (In such wise that, had I not clung to a firm rock,)
>
> 45 Ἔπιπτον ἄν, οὐδενὸς κάτω μ᾿ ὠθοῦντος.  
> (I should have fallen, without anyone pushing me down.)

- *Ἱστάμην ὀρθός* ‘I was standing upright’ combines the 1st sg. imperfect middle of *ἵστημι* with a predicate adjective, rendering *stava … surto* as posture rather than a completed act of rising. *Βλέπων ἐκ τῆς γεφύρας* ‘looking from the bridge’ replaces the purpose infinitive *a veder* with a simultaneous present active participle; the bridge is the point of observation (*ἐκ* + gen.) rather than the place one stands *sopra*.
- *Οὕτως, ὥστ᾿* renders *sì che* with a result clause containing a counterfactual condition. *Εἰ μὴ στεῤῥᾶς εἰχόμην πέτρας* ‘if I had not been holding on to a firm rock’ uses the imperfect middle indicative (1st sg. of *ἔχω*, with augment *ει-*); middle *ἔχομαι* takes the genitive of what one holds. The continuing grip replaces the prior act *avessi … preso*. *Στεῤῥᾶς* (gen. fem. sg. of *στερρός*, with the old breathing marks on doubled rho) adds firmness to *πέτρας*.
- *Ἔπιπτον ἄν* (1st sg. impf. act. ind. of *πίπτω* with counterfactual *ἄν*) gives the unreal consequence expressed by *caduto sarei*. *Οὐδενὸς κάτω μ᾿ ὠθοῦντος* changes *senza esser urto* from a passive infinitive to a genitive absolute with an explicit negative agent: ‘with nobody pushing me down’. *Ὠθοῦντος* is gen. masc. sg. pres. act. participle of *ὠθέω*; *μ᾿* is elided acc. *με*.

> 46 E il duca, che mi vide tanto atteso,  
> 47 disse: "Dentro da' fochi son gli spirti;  
> 48 ciascun si fascia di quel ch' egli è inceso."  

And the Guide, who saw me thus attent, said: “Within those fires are the spirits; each swathes himself with that which burns him.”

> 46 Οὑμὸς δ᾿ Ἡγήτωρ ἀτενίζοντ᾿ ἰδών με  
> (And my Guide, seeing me gazing intently,)
>
> 47 Εἶπ᾿· “Ἐντὸς πάσης φλογὸς ὑπάρχει πνεῦμα,  
> (Said: “Within every flame there is a spirit,)
>
> 48 „ Ἕκαστον ὡς ἔνδυμα τὴν φλόγα φέρον.”  
> („ Each wearing the flame as a garment.”)

- *Οὑμὸς δ᾿ Ἡγήτωρ* ‘and my Guide’ adds the possessive: *οὑμός* is crasis of *ὁ ἐμός*, with the rough breathing retained. *Ἀτενίζοντ᾿ ἰδών με* ‘having seen me gazing intently’ compresses *che mi vide tanto atteso* into two participles. *Ἰδών* is nom. masc. sg. second aor. act. of *ὁράω*, agreeing with the Guide; *ἀτενίζοντα* (elided) is acc. masc. sg. pres. act. of *ἀτενίζω*, predicated of *με*. The intense gaze is in the verb, not a separate degree word for *tanto*.
- *Εἶπ᾿* is elided *εἶπε*, 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *λέγω*. *Ἐντὸς πάσης φλογὸς ὑπάρχει πνεῦμα* ‘inside every flame there is a spirit’ makes *Dentro da’ fochi son gli spirti* distributive already in the first clause. *Ἐντός* governs the genitive; *πνεῦμα* is the singular nominative subject of existential *ὑπάρχει*.
- *Ἕκαστον … φέρον* continues with ‘each wearing’: both forms are nominative neuter singular referring to *πνεῦμα*, *φέρον* a present active participle of *φέρω*, not a fresh finite verb like *si fascia*. *Τὴν φλόγα* names the object worn, and *ὡς ἔνδυμα* ‘as a garment’ makes its function explicit. The clothing image survives, but neither the reflexive wrapping nor the burning in *di quel ch’ egli è inceso* receives its own verb.

> 49 "Maestro mio," rispos' io, "per udirti  
> 50 son io più certo; ma già m' era avviso  
> 51 che così fusse, e già voleva dirti:  

“Master,” I replied, “from hearing thee I feel more certain; but had already discerned it to be so, and already wished to say to thee:

> 49 —“Προστάτ᾿,” ἀπεκρινάμην, “ἐπεὶ σὺ λέγεις,  
> (—“Master,” I answered, “since you say it,)
>
> 50 „ Πέπεισμαι πάντως· ἀλλ᾿ ὅτι τοῦτ᾿ ἦν οὕτως  
> („ I am wholly convinced; yet that this was so)
>
> 51 „ Ἐφρόνουν· ὅμως ἤθελόν σ᾿ ἐρωτῆσαι,  
> („ I was thinking; still I wished to ask you,)

- *Προστάτ᾿* is elided *προστάτα*, voc. sg. of *προστάτης*, ‘protector, leader’, rather than a term specifically for a teacher; the possessive of *Maestro mio* drops out. *Ἀπεκρινάμην* is 1st sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἀποκρίνομαι*, ‘answer’, with the augment after the preverb; its ending supplies the subject of *rispos’ io*.
- *Ἐπεὶ σὺ λέγεις* ‘since you say it’ turns *per udirti* from an infinitive of hearing into a causal clause of speaking, with emphatically expressed subject *σύ*. *Πέπεισμαι πάντως* ‘I am wholly convinced’ substitutes a degree of completeness for comparative *più certo*. *Πέπεισμαι* is 1st sg. reduplicated perfect middle/passive indicative of *πείθω*, expressing the present state resulting from persuasion.
- *Ἀλλ᾿ ὅτι τοῦτ᾿ ἦν οὕτως ἐφρόνουν* ‘but that this was so I thought’ puts the content clause before its governing verb, *ἐφρόνουν* (1st sg. impf. act. ind. of *φρονέω*). Italian puts *m’ era avviso* first. *Ὅτι* introduces a statement with indicative *ἦν* (3rd sg. impf. of *εἰμί*), not an optative even after a past verb, against Italian subjunctive *fusse*; *τοῦτ᾿* is elided neut. nom. sg. *τοῦτο*.
- *Ὅμως ἤθελόν σ᾿ ἐρωτῆσαι* ‘nevertheless I wanted to ask you’ makes a contrast where *e già* continues the thought; neither occurrence of *già* has an explicit temporal adverb in Greek. *Ἤθελον* is 1st sg. impf. act. ind. of *ἐθέλω*, with an additional accent before enclitic *σ᾿* (elided *σε*). Its complementary *ἐρωτῆσαι* is the aorist active infinitive of *ἐρωτάω*: Musurus interprets *dirti* as asking, so the addressee is a direct object rather than the indirect object of ‘say’.

## Lines 52–84 — The Divided Flame and Virgil’s Question

> 52 Chi è in quel foco, che vien sì diviso  
> 53 di sopra, che par surger della pira  
> 54 ov' Eteòcle col fratel fu miso?”  

Who is in that fire, which comes so parted at the top, as if it rose from the pyre where Eteocles with his brother was placed?”

> 52 „ Τίς ἔστ᾿ ἐν φλογὶ τῇ διμερεῖ πρὸς τἄνω,  
> („ Who is in the flame that is cleft in two at the top,)
>
> 53 „ Τῇ προσιούσῃ, τῇ δοκούσῃ τ᾿ ἐκβαίνειν  
> („ Which is approaching, and seems to issue)
>
> 54 „ Ἐτεοκλέους ἐκ πυρᾶς ἀδελφοῦ τε ;”  
> („ From the pyre of Eteocles and his brother?”)

- *Τίς ἔστ᾿ ἐν φλογί* ‘who is in a flame?’ follows *Chi è in quel foco*, but has no demonstrative for *quel*. *Τίς* is the interrogative nominative singular; *ἔστ᾿* is *ἔστι* with final *-ι* elided before *ἐν*. The dative *φλογί*, from *φλόξ*, is followed by a series of attributes, each introduced or linked by an article in the feminine dative singular.
- *τῇ διμερεῖ πρὸς τἄνω* ‘the one twofold towards the top’ puts the division before the motion. The adjective *διμερής* replaces *sì diviso*: it specifies two parts but drops the Italian degree word *sì*. *τἄνω* is crasis of *τὰ ἄνω*, the article turning the adverb into a nominal expression after *πρός*.
- *τῇ προσιούσῃ, τῇ δοκούσῃ τ᾿ ἐκβαίνειν* ‘the one approaching, and seeming to issue’ turns *che vien* and *che par surger* into articular present participles. *προσιούσῃ* is from *πρόσειμι* ‘approach’ (the compound of *εἶμι* ‘go’); *δοκούσῃ*, from *δοκέω*, takes the present active infinitive *ἐκβαίνειν*. Postpositive *τε*, elided to *τ᾿*, links the latter description to the former. Greek coordinates ‘approaching’ and ‘seeming’ rather than reproducing the sequence *sì … che*.
- *Ἐτεοκλέους ἐκ πυρᾶς ἀδελφοῦ τε* ‘from the pyre of Eteocles and of his brother’ places *ἐκ πυρᾶς* between its two dependent genitives, *Ἐτεοκλέους* (from *Ἐτεοκλῆς*) and *ἀδελφοῦ*. Final *τε* coordinates the possessors. This dispenses with both the local relative *ov’* and the passive *fu miso*: the men are identified by their relation to the pyre, not by a verb of placing.

> 55 Risposemi : “ Là entro si martira  
> 56 Ulisse e Diomede, e così insieme  
> 57 alla vendetta vanno come all' ira;  

He answered me: “Within it there Ulysses is tortured, and Diomed; and thus they run together in punishment, as *erst* in wrath;

> 55 Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπεν· “Ἔνδον Ὀδυσσεὺς τιμωρεῖται  
> (And he said: “Within, Ulysses is punished)
>
> 56 „ Καὶ Διομήδης, ὁμοῦ βαίνοντες οὕτως  
> („ And Diomed, going together thus)
>
> 57 „ Ἐν τῇ δίκῃ νῦν, ὡς ζῶντες ἐν ταῖς ἔχθραις.  
> („ In their punishment now, as while living in their hatreds.)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπεν* ‘and he said’ uses the article pronominally with adversative or continuative *δέ* (here elided); *εἶπεν* is third singular second aorist active indicative of *λέγω*. Neither the answering implied by *Risposemi* nor its indirect object *-mi* is separately expressed. *Ἔνδον* ‘within’ likewise stands alone for *Là entro*.
- *Ὀδυσσεὺς τιμωρεῖται καὶ Διομήδης* puts the first name before the verb, the second after it. *τιμωρεῖται* (third singular present middle/passive of *τιμωρέω*, passive in sense) gives the judicial ‘is punished’ for *si martira*. The singular agrees with the first subject before the second is added, much as Italian has singular *si martira* before the two names.
- *ὁμοῦ βαίνοντες οὕτως* makes finite *così insieme … vanno* a present active participle of *βαίνω*, nominative masculine plural now agreeing with both men. *ἐν τῇ δίκῃ νῦν, ὡς ζῶντες ἐν ταῖς ἔχθραις* ‘now in punishment, as when alive in their enmities’ replaces the directional parallel *alla vendetta … all’ ira* with two states expressed by *ἐν* and the dative. *νῦν* and the temporal participle *ζῶντες* (from *ζάω*) supply an explicit time contrast; plural *ἔχθραις* gives ‘enmities’ rather than singular ‘wrath’.

> 58 e dentro dalla lor fiamma si geme  
> 59 l'aguato del caval, che fe' la porta  
> 60 ond' uscì de' Romani il gentil seme;  

and in their flame they groan for the ambush of the horse, that made the door by which the noble seed of the Romans came forth;

> 58 „ Ἐντὸς τῆς φλογὸς αὑτῶν κλαίουσιν ἤδη  
> („ Within their flame they now lament)
>
> 59 „ Τὸν ἵππου δόλον, ὃς ἤνοιξε τὴν πύλην,  
> („ The stratagem of the horse, which opened the gate,)
>
> 60 „ Ὅθεν ἐξῆλθ᾽ εὐγενὲς Ῥωμαίων σπέρμα.  
> („ Whence went forth the noble seed of the Romans.)

- *Ἐντὸς τῆς φλογὸς αὑτῶν* ‘inside their own flame’ renders *dentro dalla lor fiamma* with *ἐντός* governing the genitive. The rough breathing distinguishes reflexive *αὑτῶν*, the contracted form of *ἑαυτῶν*, from *αὐτῶν*. *κλαίουσιν ἤδη* (third plural present active of *κλαίω*) makes the sufferers the grammatical subject of ‘lament’; Italian *si geme* leaves them unexpressed. *ἤδη* ‘now, already’ is supplied.
- *τὸν ἵππου δόλον* ‘the horse’s stratagem’ is the accusative object of *κλαίουσιν*: *δόλος* emphasizes deceit rather than the lying in wait of *aguato*. In *ὃς ἤνοιξε τὴν πύλην* the masculine relative *ὅς* refers to *δόλον* but is nominative as subject of *ἤνοιξε* (third singular aorist active of *ἀνοίγω*). The stratagem ‘opened the gate’ rather than *fe’ la porta* ‘made the door’.
- *Ὅθεν ἐξῆλθ᾽ εὐγενὲς Ῥωμαίων σπέρμα* closely preserves the order of *ond’ uscì de’ Romani il gentil seme*. The relative adverb *ὅθεν* marks the point of departure; *ἐξῆλθ᾽* is elided *ἐξῆλθε*, the second aorist of *ἐξέρχομαι*, with the augment internal to the compound. Its postposed subject is neuter singular *σπέρμα*, qualified by *εὐγενές* ‘noble-born’ and the intervening possessive genitive plural *Ῥωμαίων*.

> 61 piangevisi entro l' arte, per che morta  
> 62 Deidamìa ancor si duol d' Achille,  
> 63 e del Palladio pena vi si porta.”  

within it they lament the artifice, whereby Deidamia in death still sorrows for Achilles; and there for the Palladium they suffer punishment.”

> 61 „ Θρηνοῦσ᾽ ἀπάτην, δι᾽ ἣν καίτοι θανοῦσα  
> („ They bewail the deceit, through which even in death)
>
> 62 „ Δηϊδάμεια μέμφετ᾽ ἔτ᾽ Ἀχιλλέως·  
> („ Deidamia still blames Achilles;)
>
> 63 „ Δίκην τ᾽ ὄφλουσι τῆς κλοπῆς παλλαδίου.”—  
> („ And they pay the penalty for the theft of the Palladium.”—)

- *Θρηνοῦσ᾽ ἀπάτην* ‘they bewail a deceit’ again changes an Italian *si* construction, *piangevisi*, into a personal active verb with an accusative object. *Θρηνοῦσ᾽* is elided *θρηνοῦσι*, third plural present of *θρηνέω*. *ἀπάτην* interprets *arte* specifically as deception; the locative *-vi … entro* is not repeated.
- *δι᾽ ἣν καίτοι θανοῦσα* ‘because of which, although dead’ gives *per che* a causal relative, *διά* with feminine accusative *ἥν* referring to *ἀπάτην*. *καίτοι* makes *morta* explicitly concessive. *θανοῦσα* is the feminine nominative singular second aorist active participle of *θνῄσκω*, literally ‘having died’, agreeing with the following *Δηϊδάμεια*.
- *μέμφετ᾽ ἔτ᾽ Ἀχιλλέως* means ‘still finds fault with Achilles’, a shift from *ancor si duol d’ Achille*: sorrow has become reproach. *μέμφετ᾽* is elided *μέμφεται*, third singular present of the middle *μέμφομαι*; *ἔτ᾽* is *ἔτι*. The personal genitive *Ἀχιλλέως* (from *Ἀχιλλεύς*) closely echoes Italian *d’ Achille*; an accusative or dative of the person would be more usual with *μέμφομαι*.
- *Δίκην τ᾽ ὄφλουσι τῆς κλοπῆς παλλαδίου* ‘and they incur a penalty for theft of the Palladium’ puts *δίκην*, the object of plural *ὄφλουσι*, first. *τε* connects this penalty to the preceding lament. Italian *pena vi si porta* is again made personal and loses *vi*. Musurus supplies *κλοπῆς* as the genitive specifying the offense; *παλλαδίου* in turn depends on ‘theft’ as the thing stolen.

> 64 “ S' ei posson dentro da quelle faville  
> 65 parlar,” diss' io, “ maestro, assai ten prego,  
> 66 e riprego che il prego vaglia mille,  

“If they within those sparks can speak,” said I, “Master! I pray thee much, and repray that my prayer may equal a thousand,

> 64 —“Εἰ δύναντ᾽ ἐκ τοῦ μέσου τῶν φλογῶν κείνων  
> (—“If they can from the midst of those flames)
>
> 65 „ Λαλεῖν,” εἶπον, “Προστάτα, παρακαλῶ σε,  
> („ Speak,” I said, “Master, I beseech you,)
>
> 66 „ Καὶ παρακαλοῦντ᾽ ἴσθι με χιλιάκις,  
> („ And know that I beseech you a thousand times,)

- *Εἰ δύναντ᾽ … λαλεῖν* ‘if they can … speak’ retains the open condition, *εἰ* with an indicative: *δύναντ᾽* is elided *δύνανται*, third plural present of the middle *δύναμαι*, governing the present active infinitive *λαλεῖν*. *ἐκ τοῦ μέσου τῶν φλογῶν κείνων* means ‘from the midst of those flames’: the speech issues *from* within, and flames replace *faville*. *κείνων* is the poetic shorter form of *ἐκείνων*, genitive feminine plural, following the articular noun in normal demonstrative position.
- *εἶπον, Προστάτα, παρακαλῶ σε* ‘I said, protector, I entreat you’: first singular second aorist *εἶπον* needs no pronoun for *io*. *Προστάτα* is the vocative singular of *προστάτης*, treating *maestro* as one who directs or protects. Contracted *παρακαλῶ* (first singular present active of *παρακαλέω*) takes accusative *σε*; *assai* has no separate equivalent.
- *καὶ παρακαλοῦντ᾽ ἴσθι με χιλιάκις* ‘and know that I entreat you a thousand times’ preserves the verbal echo of *prego … riprego* but not the noun *il prego*. *ἴσθι* is the second singular imperative of *οἶδα* ‘know’ (not of *εἰμί* here). It takes *με* with the supplementary participle *παρακαλοῦντ᾽*, elided *παρακαλοῦντα*, present active accusative masculine singular. *χιλιάκις* counts acts of entreaty in place of the wish that one prayer *vaglia mille*.

> 67 che non mi facci dell' attender niego,  
> 68 finchè la fiamma cornuta qua vegna;  

deny me not to wait until the horned flame comes hither;

> 67 „ Ἵν᾽ εὐδοκήσῃς ὧδ᾽ ἡμᾶς παραμεῖναι,  
> („ That you consent for us to remain here,)
>
> 68 „ Ἕως ἂν ἡ δίκερως φλὸξ πλησιάσῃ.  
> („ Until the two-horned flame draws near.)

- *Ἵν᾽ εὐδοκήσῃς* ‘that you may consent’ expresses the object or aim of the entreaty positively, replacing *non mi facci … niego*. Elided *ἵνα* introduces the second singular aorist active subjunctive of *εὐδοκέω*. *ὧδ᾽ ἡμᾶς παραμεῖναι* ‘for us to remain here’ gives *παραμεῖναι* (aorist active infinitive of *παραμένω*) its own accusative subject, plural *ἡμᾶς* rather than singular *mi*. *ὧδ᾽*, elided *ὧδε*, attaches the place to the waiting rather than, like *qua*, to the flame’s coming.
- *Ἕως ἂν ἡ δίκερως φλὸξ πλησιάσῃ* uses the prospective temporal construction *ἕως ἄν* with the subjunctive for *finchè … vegna*. *πλησιάσῃ*, third singular aorist active subjunctive of *πλησιάζω*, marks the approach as the endpoint of waiting. *δίκερως*, nominative feminine singular agreeing with *φλόξ*, specifies ‘two-horned’ for *cornuta*.

> 69 vedi che del disio ver lei mi piego.”  

thou seest how with desire I bend me towards it.”

> 69 „ Ἰδὲ πρὸς αὐτὴν πῶς ὁ πόθος ὠθεῖ με.”  
> („ See how longing drives me toward it.”)

- *Ἰδὲ πρὸς αὐτὴν πῶς ὁ πόθος ὠθεῖ με* ‘see how longing drives me towards it’ takes *vedi* as an imperative: *ἰδέ* is the second singular second aorist active imperative belonging to *ὁράω*. *πρός* with feminine accusative *αὐτήν* gives the direction and refers back to *φλόξ*. The phrase is brought forward before interrogative *πῶς*. Where *del disio … mi piego* makes the speaker bend, *ὁ πόθος* is the subject of transitive *ὠθεῖ* (third singular present active of *ὠθέω*) and *με* the object moved.

> 70 Ed egli a me: “ La tua preghiera è degna  
> 71 di molta lode, ed io però l' accetto;  

And he to me: “Thy request is worthy of much praise, and therefore I accept it;

> 70 Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπεν· “Ἡ σὴ παράκλησις ἀξία  
> (And he said: “Your entreaty is worthy)
>
> 71 „ Πολλῶν ἐπαίνων· διὸ καὶ συναινῶ σοι·  
> („ Of much praise; therefore I grant it to you;)

- *Ὁ δ᾽ εἶπεν* supplies a verb for *Ed egli a me* but drops the addressee. *Ἡ σὴ παράκλησις ἀξία πολλῶν ἐπαίνων* has the opposite ellipsis: no copula for *è*. Possessive *σή* modifies *παράκλησις*, the noun corresponding to the earlier *παρακαλῶ*; predicate *ἀξία* agrees with it and governs genitive plural *πολλῶν ἐπαίνων*, ‘of many praises’ for singular *di molta lode*.
- *διὸ καὶ συναινῶ σοι* ‘for which reason I also assent to you’ gives *però* its consequential force with *διό*. *συναινῶ*, contracted first singular present active of *συναινέω*, takes a dative of the person, *σοι*: the Greek assents to the petitioner rather than accepting the petition as direct object (*l’ accetto*). There is no independent pronoun for *io*.

> 72 ma fa che la tua lingua si sostegna.  

but do thou refrain thy tongue.

> 72 „ Ἀλλὰ σύ γ᾽ αὐτὸς ἐπίσχες τὴν σὴν γλῶσσαν.  
> („ But do you yourself restrain your tongue.)

- *Ἀλλὰ σύ γ᾽ αὐτὸς ἐπίσχες τὴν σὴν γλῶσσαν* ‘but you yourself, at least, hold back your tongue’ replaces *fa che … si sostegna* with a direct transitive command. *ἐπίσχες* is second singular second aorist active imperative of *ἐπέχω* (stem *σχ-*). The tongue, subject of the Italian subordinate clause, becomes accusative object. Explicit *σύ*, focusing *γε* (elided), and intensive nominative *αὐτός* set the addressee in contrast to the speaker of the next line.

> 73 Lascia parlare a me: ch' io ho concetto  
> 74 ciò che tu vuoi; ch' ei sarebbero schivi,  
> 75 perch' ei fur Greci, forse del tuo detto.”  

Let me speak: for I have conceived what thou wishest; and they, perhaps, because they were Greeks, might disdain thy words.”

> 73 „ Ἔα με λέγειν, ὡς ἐννοήσαντ᾽ ἤδη  
> („ Let me speak, since I have already perceived)
>
> 74 „ Τί θέλεις· καὶ γὰρ χαλεποὶ τάχ᾽ ἂν ἴσως,  
> („ What you wish; for scornful perchance they might,)
>
> 75 „ Ἕλληνες ὄντες, ἔσοιντο τοῖς σοῖς λόγοις.”  
> („ Being Greeks, be toward your words.”)

- *Ἔα με λέγειν* follows *Lascia parlare a me*, but *με* is accusative subject of the present active infinitive *λέγειν* after *ἔα*, second singular present imperative of *ἐάω*. *ὡς ἐννοήσαντ᾽ ἤδη τί θέλεις* ‘on the ground that I have already understood what you want’ replaces finite *ch’ io ho concetto* with *ὡς* and a causal participle giving the speaker’s stated reason. Elided *ἐννοήσαντα* is aorist active, accusative masculine singular in agreement with *με*; *ἤδη* stresses completion. The indirect question *τί θέλεις* is its object.
- *καὶ γὰρ χαλεποὶ τάχ᾽ ἂν ἴσως … ἔσοιντο* adds a further reason with *καὶ γάρ* ‘for indeed’. Predicate *χαλεποί* ‘hard to deal with, ill-disposed’ renders *schivi*. *ἔσοιντο* is the poetic expanded third plural present optative of *εἰμί* (ordinary Attic *εἶεν*); with *ἄν* it is potential, corresponding to *sarebbero*. Both elided *τάχα* and *ἴσως* express the uncertainty of the single *forse*.
- *Ἕλληνες ὄντες* ‘being Greeks’ makes *perch’ ei fur Greci* a causal participial phrase, nominative masculine plural, without the Italian past tense. *τοῖς σοῖς λόγοις* ‘towards your words’ is a dative of reference with *χαλεποί*, where *del tuo detto* is singular and introduced by *di*.

> 76 Poi che la fiamma fu venuta quivi,  
> 77 dove parve al mio duca tempo e loco,  
> 78 in questa forma lui parlare audivi:  

After the flame had come where time and place seemed fitting to my Guide, I heard him speak in this manner:

> 76 Ἐπεί, τῆς φλογὸς ἐγγισάσης, Ἡγήτωρ  
> (When, as the flame drew near, the Guide)
>
> 77 Ἐμὸς πρόσφορον εὗρε χρόνον καὶ τόπον,  
> (Of mine found a fitting time and place,)
>
> 78 Τότ’ ἤκουσ’ αὐτὸν προσφθεγγόμενον οὕτως·  
> (Then I heard him addressing them thus:)

- *Ἐπεί, τῆς φλογὸς ἐγγισάσης* begins a temporal clause with another temporal circumstance embedded in it. *τῆς φλογὸς ἐγγισάσης* is a genitive absolute, ‘the flame having drawn near’; *ἐγγισάσης* is the feminine genitive singular aorist active participle of *ἐγγίζω*. Italian makes the arrival the finite verb after *Poi che* and specifies *quivi, dove*; Greek drops the place-relative link.
- *Ἡγήτωρ ἐμὸς πρόσφορον εὗρε χρόνον καὶ τόπον* makes ‘my guide’ the nominative subject of *εὗρε*, third singular second aorist active of *εὑρίσκω*, in the clause governed by *ἐπεί*. Instead of *parve al mio duca*, the guide ‘found’ the time and place suitable. *πρόσφορον* is masculine accusative singular, applicable to both coordinated singular objects *χρόνον καὶ τόπον*.
- *Τότ’ ἤκουσ’ αὐτὸν προσφθεγγόμενον οὕτως* answers *ἐπεί* with *τότε* ‘then’, elided before *ἤκουσ᾿* (elided *ἤκουσα*, first singular aorist active of *ἀκούω*). Italian *lui parlare audivi* has an infinitive; Greek has the participle of direct perception, *προσφθεγγόμενον*, present middle accusative masculine singular of *προσφθέγγομαι*, agreeing with *αὐτόν*. The verb specifies addressing someone rather than merely speaking; final *οὕτως* compresses *in questa forma*.

> 79 “ O voi, che siete due dentro ad un foco,  

“O ye, two in one fire!

> 79 “ Ὑμεῖς οἱ πυρὸς ἐντὸς συνόντες ἄμφω,  
> (“You who are both together within the fire,)

- *Ὑμεῖς οἱ πυρὸς ἐντὸς συνόντες ἄμφω* ‘you who are both together inside fire’ dispenses with vocative *O* and replaces *che siete* with the articular participle *οἱ … συνόντες*, nominative masculine plural of *σύνειμι* ‘be together’, in apposition to *ὑμεῖς*. *πυρός*, genitive singular of *πῦρ*, precedes the governing *ἐντός*. Greek stresses togetherness by the compound verb rather than translating *un* with a numeral. *ἄμφω* ‘both’ is a dual form, but the pronoun and participle are plural.

> 80 s' io meritai di voi mentre ch' io vissi,  
> 81 s' io meritai di voi assai o poco,  
> 82 quando nel mondo gli alti versi scrissi,  

if I merited of you whilst I lived, if I merited of you much or little, when on earth I wrote the High Verses,

> 80 „ Εἴ ποτ’ εὔνους ἦν ὑμῖν ἐπὶ ζωῆς μου,  
> („ If ever I was kindly toward you during my life,)
>
> 81 „ Εἴ ποτ’ ὠφέλησ’ ὑμᾶς μικρὸν ἢ μέγα,  
> („ If ever I served you little or much,)
>
> 82 „ Ὅτ’ ἔγραφον ζῶν ἐν κόσμῳ τὰ θεῖ’ ἔπη,  
> („ When living in the world I wrote the divine verses,)

- *Εἴ ποτ’ εὔνους ἦν ὑμῖν ἐπὶ ζωῆς μου* ‘if ever I was well disposed towards you during my life’ interprets the first *s’ io meritai di voi* as a state of goodwill. *εὔνους* is the contracted nominative masculine singular predicate adjective; *ἦν* is here first singular imperfect indicative of *εἰμί* (beside *ἦ*), with dative *ὑμῖν* after *εὔνους*. Temporal *ἐπί* with genitive *ζωῆς* replaces the finite *mentre ch’ io vissi*.
- *Εἴ ποτ’ ὠφέλησ’ ὑμᾶς μικρὸν ἢ μέγα* repeats *εἴ ποτε* ‘if ever’, with elision, but varies the rendering of the repeated *meritai*: now ‘if ever I benefited you’. *ὠφέλησ᾿* is elided *ὠφέλησα*, first singular aorist active of *ὠφελέω*, with temporal augment and direct accusative *ὑμᾶς*. Neuter accusatives *μικρὸν ἢ μέγα* express extent, reversing *assai o poco*. Both conditions use indicatives referring to the speaker’s past, not potential optatives.
- *Ὅτ’ ἔγραφον ζῶν ἐν κόσμῳ τὰ θεῖ’ ἔπη* ‘when, living in the world, I was writing the divine verses’ uses elided *ὅτε* and first singular imperfect active *ἔγραφον*, presenting the writing in progress rather than as the completed act of *scrissi*. The circumstantial participle *ζῶν* (present active nominative masculine singular of *ζάω*) makes ‘living’ explicit again. *τὰ θεῖ’ ἔπη* has elided *θεῖα* agreeing with neuter plural *ἔπη* (from *ἔπος*): ‘divine’ interprets rather than literally translates *alti*.

> 83 non vi movete; ma l' un di voi dica  
> 84 dove per lui perduto a morir gissi.”  

move ye not; but let the one of you tell where he, having lost himself, went to die.”

> 83 „ Μὴ κινεῖσθε νῦν, ἀλλ’ εἷς ὑμῶν λεγέτω,  
> („ Move not now, but let one of you tell,)
>
> 84 „ Ποῦ δὴ πλανηθεὶς θανούμενος ἀπέβη.”  
> („ Whither after wandering he went away to die.”)

- *Μὴ κινεῖσθε νῦν* ‘do not move now’ closely follows the reflexive *non vi movete*: *κινεῖσθε* is second plural present middle/passive imperative of *κινέω*, middle in sense, negated by *μή*. *νῦν* is added. *ἀλλ’ εἷς ὑμῶν λεγέτω* uses elided *ἀλλά* and a third singular present active imperative of *λέγω* for the Italian jussive subjunctive *dica*. *εἷς* is nominative subject, with partitive genitive *ὑμῶν* as in *l’ un di voi*.
- *Ποῦ δὴ πλανηθεὶς θανούμενος ἀπέβη* is the indirect question after *λεγέτω*. *ποῦ* ‘where’, given emphasis by *δή*, is understood directionally with *ἀπέβη*, third singular second aorist active of *ἀποβαίνω* ‘go away, disembark’. *πλανηθείς*, aorist passive participle of *πλανάω*, takes *per lui perduto* as ‘having gone astray’, without a separate reflexive pronoun. *θανούμενος*, future middle participle of *θνῄσκω* (future *θανοῦμαι*), expresses purpose after the verb of motion, in place of *a morir*. Both participles are nominative masculine singular, describing the one whose destination is asked: the wandering precedes the going, the dying is still to come.

## Lines 85–117 — The Flame Speaks of the Voyage West

> 85 Lo maggior corno della fiamma antica  
> 86 cominciò a crollarsi mormorando,  
> 87 pur come quella cui vento affatica.  

The greater horn of the ancient flame began to shake itself, murmuring, just like a flame that struggles with the wind.

> 85 Τότε τῆς πάλαι φλογὸς τὸ μεῖζον κέρας  
> (Then the greater horn of the ancient flame)
>
> 86 Μορμυρίζον ἤρξατο διακλονεῖσθαι,  
> (Murmuring began to quiver,)
>
> 87 Καθάπερ φλόξ, ἣν πνέων ἄνεμος σείει.  
> (Just like a flame which a blowing wind shakes.)

- *Τότε* ‘then’ is supplied. Musurus puts *τῆς πάλαι φλογός* before the head phrase *τὸ μεῖζον κέρας*, reversing *Lo maggior corno della fiamma antica*. The adverb *πάλαι* ‘of old’ does the work of *antica* in attributive position between the article and *φλογός* (gen. sg. of *φλόξ*). *μεῖζον* is the comparative of *μέγας*, nominative neuter singular agreeing with *κέρας*.
- *Μορμυρίζον* (pres. act. participle of *μορμυρίζω*, nom. neut. sg.) agrees with the horn and expresses an action simultaneous with its shaking, like *mormorando*, but comes before the verb. *ἤρξατο* (3rd sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἄρχομαι*) takes the present middle-passive infinitive *διακλονεῖσθαι* ‘to be shaken to and fro’; the Greek does not require a separate reflexive for *-si* in *crollarsi*.
- *Καθάπερ* ‘just as’ gives the emphasis of *pur come*. For the demonstrative *quella* Greek repeats *φλόξ*. In *ἣν πνέων ἄνεμος σείει* ‘which a blowing wind shakes’, *ἥν* is the feminine accusative object of *σείει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind. of *σείω*); the wind is the subject, as in *cui vento affatica*. The added *πνέων* (pres. act. participle of *πνέω*, nom. masc. sg.) qualifies *ἄνεμος*; *σείει* makes the effect of *affatica* a physical shaking.

> 88 Indi la cima qua e là menando,  
> 89 come fosse la lingua che parlasse,  

Then carrying to and fro the top, as if it were the tongue that spake,

> 88 Εἶτα κινοῦν κορυφὴν τῇδε κἀκεῖσε,  
> (Then moving its tip to and fro,)
>
> 89 Ὡς ἂν εἴπερ ἦν ὁμιλοῦσά τις γλῶσσα,  
> (As though it were a speaking tongue,)

- *Εἶτα κινοῦν κορυφὴν τῇδε κἀκεῖσε* follows *Indi la cima qua e là menando* with the verb moved forward. *κινοῦν*, the contracted present active participle of *κινέω* (nom. neut. sg.), still agrees with *κέρας*, not with its feminine accusative object *κορυφήν*. The directional adverbs mean ‘this way and thither’; *κἀκεῖσε* is crasis for *καὶ ἐκεῖσε*.
- *Ὡς ἂν εἴπερ ἦν* expands *come fosse* into a hypothetical comparison, ‘as [it would be] if indeed it were’. *ἄν* gives the hypothetical force to the comparison; *εἴπερ* introduces the supposition, with emphatic *-περ*. *ἦν* is the 3rd sg. imperfect **indicative** of *εἰμί*, not an optative corresponding to the Italian subjunctive. *ὁμιλοῦσά τις γλῶσσα* ‘a sort of speaking tongue’ replaces the definite *la lingua* and its relative clause *che parlasse* with an indefinite predicate nominative and an agreeing present active participle of *ὁμιλέω*. The additional accent on *ὁμιλοῦσά* is due to enclitic *τις*.

> 90 gittò voce di fuori e disse : “Quando  

threw forth a voice, and said : " When

> 90 Ἀφῆκε φωνὴν ἐκτός, λέγον· “Ἀφ’ ὅτου  
> (It sent forth a voice without, saying: “From the time when)

- *Ἀφῆκε φωνὴν ἐκτός* ‘it let a voice out’ renders *gittò voce di fuori*. *ἀφῆκε* is the 3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἀφίημι*, with the *-κ-* aorist of *ἵημι*; *ἐκτός* is adverbial. Instead of the second finite verb *e disse*, *λέγον* ‘saying’ is a present active participle, nominative neuter singular referring back to *κέρας*.
- *Ἀφ’ ὅτου* gives *Quando* a starting-point sense, ‘from the time when’. *ὅτου* is genitive after *ἀπό*; elision before the rough breathing changes the final *π* to *φ*.

> 91 mi diparti’ da Circe, che sottrasse  
> 92 me più d’ un anno là presso a Gaeta,  
> 93 prima che sì Enea la nominasse,  

I departed from Circe, who beyond a year detained me there near Gaeta, ere Æneas thus had named it,

> 91 „ Ἀπεχωρίσθην Κίρκης, ἥ με κατεῖχε  
> („ I parted from Circe, who detained me)
>
> 92 „ Πλέον ἔτους ἔγκρυπτον ἐγγὺς Καιήτης,  
> („ More than a year hidden near Gaeta,)
>
> 93 „ Πρὶν τοὔνομα τόδ’ Αἰνείαν αὐτῇ δοῦναι,  
> („ Before Æneas gave this name to it,)

- *Ἀπεχωρίσθην Κίρκης* ‘I was separated from Circe’ uses the 1st sg. aor. pass. ind. of *ἀποχωρίζω* with intransitive force for *mi diparti’*. The augment is internal, after the preverb; the bare genitive *Κίρκης* marks separation.
- *ἥ με κατεῖχε* ‘who was keeping me’ has a nominative relative subject and an imperfect active indicative (3rd sg. of *κατέχω*), emphasizing duration where *sottrasse* is a simple past. *Πλέον ἔτους* ‘more than a year’ has adverbial neuter comparative *πλέον* with a genitive of comparison. *ἔγκρυπτον* ‘hidden’ is an accusative predicate agreeing with *με*: it makes explicit a sense of keeping someone out of sight in *sottrasse*. *ἐγγὺς Καιήτης* takes the genitive after ‘near’; there is no separate word for *là*.
- *Πρὶν … δοῦναι* replaces *prima che … nominasse* with *πρίν* and the aorist active infinitive of *δίδωμι*. *Αἰνείαν* is its accusative subject, *αὐτῇ* the dative recipient: ‘before Aeneas gave it this name’. Thus *sì* becomes part of the direct object *τοὔνομα τόδ’*. *τοὔνομα* is crasis of *τὸ ὄνομα*; *τόδ’* is *τόδε* elided before *Αἰνείαν*.

> 94 nè dolcezza di figlio, nè la pieta  
> 95 del vecchio padre, nè il debito amore  
> 96 lo qual dovea Penelope far lieta,  

neither fondness for my son, nor reverence for my aged father, nor the due love that should have cheered Penelope,

> 94 „ Οὔτε στοργὴ πρὸς υἱόν, ἀλλ’ οὔτε σέβας  
> („ Neither fondness for my son, nor reverence)
>
> 95 „ Πρὸς πατέρα πρεσβύτην, οὐδ᾽ ἁγνὸς ἔρως,  
> („ For my aged father, nor the pure love,)
>
> 96 „ Ὃς Πηνελόπην ἔμελλ᾽ εὐτυχῆ θήσειν,  
> („ Which should have made Penelope happy,)

- *Οὔτε στοργὴ πρὸς υἱόν, ἀλλ’ οὔτε σέβας πρὸς πατέρα πρεσβύτην* makes both Italian genitives, *di figlio* and *del vecchio padre*, explicitly objective: *πρός* with the accusative gives the person towards whom the feeling is directed. *στοργή* specifies familial affection for *dolcezza*; *σέβας* interprets *pieta* as reverence. *πρεσβύτην* (acc. sg. of the noun *πρεσβύτης*) characterizes the father as ‘an old man’. The possessives understood in English are not expressed in Greek.
- The sequence *οὔτε … ἀλλ’ οὔτε … οὐδ’* varies the repeated *nè*: *ἀλλ’* is elided *ἀλλά*, and *οὐδ’* is elided *οὐδέ*. The third nominative subject, *ἁγνὸς ἔρως* ‘pure love’, stresses the character of the love rather than the obligation expressed by *debito*.
- *Ὃς Πηνελόπην ἔμελλ’ εὐτυχῆ θήσειν* keeps *ἔρως* as antecedent of the masculine relative subject *ὅς*. *ἔμελλ’* (for *ἔμελλε*, 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *μέλλω*) with *θήσειν* (fut. act. inf. of *τίθημι*) means ‘was to make’, giving *dovea* a prospective sense. *Πηνελόπην* is the object and *εὐτυχῆ* its predicate, ‘happy’ (contracted acc. fem. sg. of *εὐτυχής*).

> 97 vincer poter dentro da me l’ardore  
> 98 ch’i’ ebbi a divenir del mondo esperto,  
> 99 e degli vizii umani e del valore ;  

could conquer in me the ardour that I had to gain experience of the world, and of human vice and worth

> 97 „ Ἠδυνήθησαν δαμάσαι μου τὸν πόθον  
> („ Could overcome within me the longing)
>
> 98 „ Τοῦ γενέσθαι μ᾽ ἔμπειρον τῶν ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ,  
> („ For me to become experienced in the things of the world,)
>
> 99 „ Γνῶναι τ᾽ ἀνθρώπων ἀρετὰς καὶ κακίας·  
> („ And to learn the virtues and vices of men;)

- *Ἠδυνήθησαν* (3rd pl. aor. pass. ind. in form, from *δύναμαι*, active in meaning) gathers up the three subjects, as does *poter*. Its initial *η-* is the variant augment found in this verb. It governs *δαμάσαι* ‘to subdue’ (aor. act. inf. of *δαμάζω*). *μου τὸν πόθον* ‘my longing’ renders *dentro da me l’ardore* by possession rather than location, and by a noun of desire rather than a metaphor of heat.
- *Τοῦ γενέσθαι μ’ ἔμπειρον* ‘to become experienced’ defines the object of the longing by a genitive articular infinitive instead of *ch’i’ ebbi a divenir*. *γενέσθαι* is the second aorist middle infinitive of *γίγνομαι*; *μ’* (elided *με*) is its accusative subject and *ἔμπειρον* its predicate. The adjective governs *τῶν ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ*, literally ‘of the things in the world’, expanding *del mondo* by substantivizing the prepositional phrase.
- *Γνῶναι τ’* adds a second infinitive, ‘and to learn’ (*γνῶναι*, aor. act. inf. of *γιγνώσκω*; *τ’* is elided postpositive *τε*). Where *esperto* serves both Italian complements, Musurus gives the second its own verb. *ἀνθρώπων* ‘of human beings’ replaces the adjective *umani*; *ἀρετὰς καὶ κακίας* are plural accusative objects, reversing the order of *vizii … e … valore* and rendering singular *valore* by plural ‘virtues’.

> 100 ma misi me per l’ alto mare aperto  
> 101 sol con un legno e con quella compagna  
> 102 picciola, dalla qual non fui deserto.  

; I put forth on the deep open sea, with but one ship, and with that small company, which had not deserted me.

> 100 „ Ἀλλ᾽ ἀπέραντα περιῆλθον πελάγη  
> („ But I roamed over boundless seas)
>
> 101 „ Ἐφ᾽ ἑνὸς μόνου σκάφους καὶ σὺν ἑταίροις,  
> („ Upon a single vessel and with those companions,)
>
> 102 „ Ὧν ἐν κινδύνοις οὐδεὶς κατέλιπέ με.  
> („ None of whom abandoned me in perils.)

- *Ἀλλ’ ἀπέραντα περιῆλθον πελάγη* ‘but I traversed boundless seas’ retains *ma*, but replaces *misi me per* with a verb of travel taking an accusative of extent traversed. *περιῆλθον* is here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *περιέρχομαι*, with the augmented stem of *ἦλθον* after the preverb. *ἀπέραντα … πελάγη* (acc. neut. pl.; *πελάγη* contracted from *πελάγεα*) renders *l’alto mare aperto* as a plural with a single adjective.
- *Ἐφ’ ἑνὸς μόνου σκάφους* ‘on a single vessel alone’ uses *ἐπί* with the genitive; elision and aspiration before *ἑνός* give *ἐφ’*. *ἑνός* is the genitive neuter of *εἷς*; *μόνου* reinforces it. Thus the first *con* in *sol con un legno* is locative, while the second is rendered by *σὺν ἑταίροις* with the dative of accompaniment. Plural ‘companions’ replaces the collective *compagna* without separately rendering *quella* or *picciola*.
- *Ὧν … οὐδεὶς κατέλιπέ με* ‘none of whom left me’ reverses the passive *dalla qual non fui deserto*: the relative *ὧν* is a partitive genitive dependent on the singular subject *οὐδείς*. *κατέλιπε* is the 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *καταλείπω*, with a second accent before enclitic *με*. *ἐν κινδύνοις* ‘in dangers’ is supplied.

> 103 L’ un lito e l’ altro vidi infin la Spagna,  
> 104 fin nel Morrocco, e l’ isola de’ Sardi,  
> 105 e l’ altre che quel mare intorno bagna.  

Both the shores I saw as far as Spain, far as Morocco ; and saw Sardinia and the other isles which that sea bathes round.

> 103 „ Εἶδον ἄμφω τὰς ἀκτὰς μέχρι Σπανίας  
> („ I saw both shores as far as Spain)
>
> 104 „ Καὶ μέχρι Μαυρουσίας καὶ Σαρδοῦς νήσου  
> („ And as far as Morocco and the island of Sardinia)
>
> 105 „ Κἄλλων, ἃς θάλασσ᾽ ἐκείνη περιβρέχει·  
> („ And others which that sea washes round:)

- *Εἶδον ἄμφω τὰς ἀκτάς* puts the verb first: *εἶδον* is here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind., the suppletive aorist of *ὁράω*. *ἄμφω* ‘both’ is the nominative-accusative dual form, used here with the plural accusative *τὰς ἀκτάς* for the distributive *L’un lito e l’altro*.
- *μέχρι Σπανίας καὶ μέχρι Μαυρουσίας* repeats *μέχρι* with the genitive for *infin … fin*. Greek continues the same construction through *καὶ Σαρδοῦς νήσου κἄλλων* ‘and as far as the island of Sardinia and others’: *Σαρδοῦς* is genitive of *Σαρδώ*, *νήσου* is also genitive, and *ἄλλων* implies *νήσων*. Thus the islands, direct objects of *vidi* in Italian, become further limits of travel. *κἄλλων* is crasis of *καὶ ἄλλων*.
- *ἃς θάλασσ’ ἐκείνη περιβρέχει* follows *che quel mare intorno bagna*: *ἅς* is feminine plural like the implied islands, but accusative as object of *περιβρέχει* (3rd sg. pres. act. ind.). The prefix *περι-* incorporates *intorno*. *θάλασσ’* is elided *θάλασσα*, the nominative subject with demonstrative *ἐκείνη*.

> 106 Io e i compagni eravam vecchi e tardi,  
> 107 quando venimmo a quella foce stretta,  
> 108 ov’ Ercole segnò li suoi riguardi,  
> 109 acciocchè l’uom più oltre non si metta ;  

I and my companions were old and tardy, when we came to that narrow pass, where Hercules assigned his landmarks to hinder man from venturing farther

> 106 „ Ἑταῖροι κἀγώ, γέροντες κεκμηκότες  
> („ My companions and I, old men and worn out)
>
> 107 „ Ἤδη, παρὰ τὸν πορθμὸν ἤλθομεν, ἔνθα  
> („ Already, came beside the strait, where)
>
> 108 „ Ἡρακλῆς ἱδρύσατο τὰς αὑτοῦ στήλας,  
> („ Hercules set up his pillars,)
>
> 109 „ Ἵνα μηδεὶς ἄνθρωπος βῇ περαιτέρω.  
> („ That no man should pass further beyond.)

- *Ἑταῖροι κἀγώ* reverses *Io e i compagni*; *κἀγώ* is crasis of *καὶ ἐγώ*. Instead of *eravam … quando venimmo*, Greek makes *ἤλθομεν* (1st pl. second aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*) the sole main verb. *γέροντες κεκμηκότες ἤδη* describes the subjects at the time: ‘old men, already worn out’. *κεκμηκότες* is the reduplicated perfect active participle of *κάμνω* (nom. masc. pl.); it interprets *tardi* through the resulting state of fatigue.
- *παρὰ τὸν πορθμόν* ‘to the side of the strait’ uses *παρά* with the accusative of motion. *πορθμός* gives the sense of *foce stretta* without a separate adjective for *stretta*; the article replaces *quella*. Locative *ἔνθα* renders *ov’*.
- *Ἡρακλῆς ἱδρύσατο τὰς αὑτοῦ στήλας* ‘Heracles set up his own pillars’ makes *segnò li suoi riguardi* more concrete in both verb and object. *ἱδρύσατο* is the 3rd sg. aor. mid. ind. of *ἱδρύω*; *αὑτοῦ*, the contracted reflexive genitive of *ἑαυτοῦ* (rough breathing), refers the pillars to the subject.
- *Ἵνα μηδεὶς ἄνθρωπος βῇ περαιτέρω* retains the negative purpose clause *acciocchè l’uom più oltre non si metta*. *μηδείς* ‘no’ supplies the negation within the subject phrase; *βῇ* is the 3rd sg. root aor. act. subj. of *βαίνω*. Greek retains the subjunctive of purpose after a past main verb rather than shifting to an optative. *περαιτέρω* is a comparative adverb, ‘farther’.

> 110 dalla man destra mi lasciai Sibilia,  
> 111 dall’ altra già m’ avea lasciata Setta.  

; on the right hand, I left Seville ; on the other, had already left Ceuta.

> 110 „ Ἵσπαλιν ἐκ δεξιᾶς χειρὸς παρῆλθον,  
> („ Seville on the right hand I passed by,)
>
> 111 „ Ἐκ δ᾽ εὐωνύμου παρέδραμέ με Σέπτα.—  
> („ And on the left Ceuta slipped past me.—)

- *Ἵσπαλιν … παρῆλθον* ‘I passed Seville’ gives *mi lasciai Sibilia* a non-reflexive verb with an accusative object. *παρῆλθον* is here 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *παρέρχομαι*. *ἐκ δεξιᾶς χειρός* closely matches *dalla man destra*: *ἐκ* with the genitive expresses the side on which something lies.
- *Ἐκ δ’ εὐωνύμου* ‘and on the left’ supplies the specific side for *dall’altra*, with *χειρός* understood; *εὐώνυμος* is the conventional euphemistic word for ‘left’, literally ‘of good name’. *δ’* is elided postpositive *δέ*. *παρέδραμέ με Σέπτα* ‘Ceuta passed me by’ reverses subject and object: *Σέπτα* is nominative, *με* accusative, and *παρέδραμε* the 3rd sg. second aor. act. ind. of *παρατρέχω*, accented again before the enclitic. Neither the anterior tense of *m’avea lasciata* nor *già* is separately conveyed.

> 112 ‘O frati,’ dissi, ‘che per cento milia  
> 113 perigli siete giunti all’ occidente,  

‘O brothers !’ I said, ‘who through a hundred thousand dangers have reached the West,

> 112 ‚ „ Ἄνδρες,” εἶπον, “οἵτινες διὰ μυρίων  
> (‚ „ Men,” I said, “who through ten thousand)
>
> 113 ‚ „ Κινδύνων εἰς τὴν δύσιν ἤλθετε σῶοι,  
> (‚ „ Perils have come safely to the West,)

- *Ἄνδρες* is a vocative plural, ‘men’, for *O frati*: neither the kinship term nor a vocative particle *ὦ* is retained. Parenthetic *εἶπον* is the 1st sg. second aor. act. ind. used for *λέγω*, corresponding to *dissi*.
- *οἵτινες* (nom. masc. pl. of *ὅστις*) resumes the addressees descriptively, ‘you who’. Hence *ἤλθετε* is second person plural (second aor. act. ind. of *ἔρχομαι*), despite the relative subject. *διὰ μυρίων κινδύνων* uses *διά* with the genitive for passage through dangers; *μυρίων* gives ‘ten thousand’ or, less numerically, ‘countless’, not the precise *cento milia*.
- *εἰς τὴν δύσιν* ‘to the setting [of the sun]’ renders *all’occidente*. The simple aorist *ἤλθετε* replaces *siete giunti*, and *σῶοι* ‘safe’ (nom. masc. pl. of *σῶος*) adds a predicate adjective describing the addressees on arrival.

> 114 a questa tanto picciola vigilia  
> 115 de’ vostri sensi, ch’è del rimanente,  
> 116 non vogliate negar l’ esperienza,  
> 117 di retro al sol, del mondo senza gente.  

deny not, to this the brief vigil of your senses that remains, experience of the unpeopled world behind the Sun.

> 114 ‚ „ Μὴ νῦν, ἐφ᾽ ὅσον τοῦ λοιποῦ βραχὺν χρόνον  
> (‚ „ Do not now, for the brief remaining time)
>
> 115 ‚ „ Ἐγρηγορούσας ἕξετε τὰς αἰσθήσεις,  
> (‚ „ That you shall keep your senses waking,)
>
> 116 ‚ „ Ἀποκνήσητε γνῶναι σαφῶς τὸν ὄντα  
> (‚ „ Shrink from learning clearly that which is)
>
> 117 ‚ „ Ἡλίου πέραν ἀκατοίκητον κόσμον.  
> (‚ „ Uninhabited world beyond the sun.)

- Musurus moves the prohibition to the front with *Μὴ νῦν* ‘do not now’, but delays its verb *ἀποκνήσητε* until line 116. The intervening *ἐφ’ ὅσον τοῦ λοιποῦ βραχὺν χρόνον … ἕξετε* recasts *a questa tanto picciola vigilia … ch’è del rimanente* as a temporal relative clause. *ἐφ’* is *ἐπί* elided and aspirated before *ὅσον*; the accusative phrase *ὅσον … βραχὺν χρόνον* measures duration, ‘for what short time’. *τοῦ λοιποῦ* ‘of what remains’ echoes *del rimanente*.
- *Ἐγρηγορούσας ἕξετε τὰς αἰσθήσεις* ‘you will have your senses awake’ translates the noun *vigilia* by a predicate participle. *ἐγρηγορούσας* is the perfect active participle of *ἐγείρω* (acc. fem. pl.); the perfect *ἐγρήγορα* denotes the state of being awake. It agrees with the object *τὰς αἰσθήσεις*, whereas *de’ vostri sensi* depends on *vigilia*. *ἕξετε*, the 2nd pl. fut. act. ind. of *ἔχω*, makes the addressees the subject and leaves possession of the senses implicit.
- *Μὴ … ἀποκνήσητε* is a prohibition with the 2nd pl. aor. act. subj. of *ἀποκνέω*, ‘do not shrink from’. Rather than follow *non vogliate negar l’esperienza* with a verb of willing and a noun for experience, Greek makes *γνῶναι* ‘to learn’ the infinitive complement of *ἀποκνήσητε*. *σαφῶς* ‘clearly’ is added.
- In *τὸν ὄντα Ἡλίου πέραν ἀκατοίκητον κόσμον*, the entire accusative phrase is the object of *γνῶναι*. *ὄντα* (pres. act. participle of *εἰμί*, acc. masc. sg.) agrees with *κόσμον* and gives the location an explicit construction, ‘the uninhabited world which lies beyond the sun’. *πέραν* follows and governs the genitive *Ἡλίου*: it interprets *di retro al sol* as ‘beyond the sun’. The verbal adjective *ἀκατοίκητον* ‘uninhabited’ compresses *senza gente*.

## Lines 118–142 — The Exhortation, Voyage, and Shipwreck

> 118 Considerate la vostra semenza:  

Consider your origin

> 118 ‚ „ Ἀναμνησθῶμεν οἵας ἐσμὲν γενέθλης·  
> (‚ „ Let us remember of what race we are;)

- *Ἀναμνησθῶμεν* ‘let us call to mind’ (1st pl. aor. pass. subj. of *ἀναμιμνῄσκω*, passive in form but meaning ‘remember’) turns the 2nd pl. imperative *Considerate* into a hortatory subjunctive: the speaker includes himself in the appeal.
- *οἵας ἐσμὲν γενέθλης* ‘of what stock we are’ replaces the direct object *la vostra semenza* with an indirect question. *οἵας* (gen. sg. fem. of *οἷος*) asks of what **kind**; it agrees across *ἐσμέν* (1st pl. pres. ind. of *εἰμί*) with the poetic *γενέθλης* (gen. sg. of *γενέθλη*). The genitive predicates descent; the person of the verb replaces the Italian possessive.

> 119 fatti non foste a viver come bruti,  
> 120 ma per seguir virtute e conoscenza.'  

: ye were not formed to live like brutes, but to follow virtue and knowledge.'

> 119 ‚ „ Οὐ γὰρ ἴδιον ἡμῶν τὸ ζῇν ὡς κτήνη,  
> (‚ „ For it is not fitting for us to live as beasts,)
>
> 120 ‚ „ Ἀλλὰ θηρεύειν ἀρετὴν κἀπιστήμην.”’  
> (‚ „ But to pursue virtue and knowledge.”’)

- *Οὐ γὰρ ἴδιον ἡμῶν* ‘for it is not characteristic of us’ makes the connection explanatory with postpositive *γάρ*. In place of *fatti non foste*, a past passive addressed to the companions, there is a present nominal predicate with *ἐστί* understood: *ἴδιον* (neut. sg. of *ἴδιος*) takes the genitive *ἡμῶν* ‘of us’.
- *τὸ ζῇν ὡς κτήνη* ‘living as beasts do’ is the subject of that predicate. Musurus uses the article with the present active infinitive of *ζάω* (written *ζῇν* here; usual Attic *ζῆν*); *κτήνη* is the contracted neut. pl. of *κτῆνος*. Thus *a viver* is no longer an infinitive dependent on ‘formed’.
- *Ἀλλὰ θηρεύειν* ‘but to hunt after’ answers *ma per seguir* without a purpose preposition. The present active infinitive of *θηρεύω* balances the preceding infinitive, with the positive predicate ‘is characteristic of us’ understood. Its accusative objects are *ἀρετήν* and *κἀπιστήμην*: the latter is crasis of *καὶ ἐπιστήμην*, ‘and knowledge’.

> 121 Li miei compagni fec' io sì acuti,  
> 122 con questa orazion picciola, al cammino,  
> 123 che appena poscia gli avrei ritenuti;  

With this brief speech I made my companions so eager for the voyage, that I could hardly then have checked them;

> 121 “Οὕτως ἐξώτρυν᾽ ἐμοὺς ἑταίρους αὕτη  
> (“So spurred on my companions this)
>
> 122 „ Ἡ μικρὰ προσλαλιὰ πρὸς τὸν διάπλουν,  
> („ Little speech toward the voyage across,)
>
> 123 „ Ὥστ᾽ εἶτα μόλις κατασχεῖν ἂν δυναίμην.  
> („ That afterwards I could scarcely have held them back.)

- *Οὕτως ἐξώτρυν᾽ ἐμοὺς ἑταίρους αὕτη ἡ μικρὰ προσλαλιά* ‘so strongly did this little address urge on my companions’ reverses the roles in *Li miei compagni fec' io sì acuti, con questa orazion picciola*. The speech, instrumental in Italian, is the delayed nominative subject; the speaker survives only in the possessive *ἐμούς*, agreeing with accusative *ἑταίρους*. *αὕτη* stands outside the article–adjective–noun group, the regular demonstrative construction.
- *ἐξώτρυν᾽* is elided *ἐξώτρυνε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *ἐξοτρύνω*, augmented after the preverb). The single verb ‘urged on’ replaces *fec' … acuti*; adverbial *οὕτως* anticipates the degree of the result. *πρὸς τὸν διάπλουν* specifies the goal more narrowly than *al cammino*: *διάπλουν* is the contracted acc. sg. of *διάπλους*, ‘crossing by water’.
- *Ὥστ᾽ εἶτα μόλις κατασχεῖν ἂν δυναίμην* ‘so that afterwards I could scarcely hold [them] back’: elided *ὥστε* introduces a result expressed as a possibility. *δυναίμην* (1st sg. pres. middle opt. of *δύναμαι*) with *ἄν* renders the conditional force of *avrei ritenuti*, not its past tense. Its complement *κατασχεῖν* is the second aor. act. inf. of *κατέχω*; the companions, expressed again by Italian *gli*, are understood as its object.

> 124 e, volta nostra poppa nel mattino,  
> 125 de' remi facemmo ale al folle volo,  
> 126 sempre acquistando dal lato mancino.  

and, turning the poop towards morning, we of our oars made wings for the foolish flight, always gaining on the left.

> 124 „ Πρὸς ἕω δὲ τρέψαντες σκάφους τὴν πρύμνην  
> („ And turning the stern of the vessel toward the dawn)
>
> 125 „ Κώπαις ὡς πτεροῖς πρὸς τὴν ἄφρονα πτῆσιν  
> („ With our oars as wings for the mad flight)
>
> 126 „ Ἐπ᾽ ἀριστερὰν ᾀεὶ πλέοντες ἦμεν.  
> („ We were ever sailing toward the left.)

- *Πρὸς ἕω δὲ τρέψαντες* ‘and having turned toward dawn’ fronts the direction *nel mattino*: *ἕω* is the Attic acc. sg. of *ἕως* ‘dawn’, governed by *πρός*. Where *volta nostra poppa* has a passive participle agreeing with *poppa*, *τρέψαντες* (nom. masc. pl. aor. act. participle of *τρέπω*) agrees with the voyagers and takes *τὴν πρύμνην* as object. The preposed genitive *σκάφους* ‘of the vessel’ replaces *nostra*.
- *Κώπαις ὡς πτεροῖς* ‘with oars as wings’ substitutes two instrumental datives for *de' remi facemmo ale*: the making disappears and *ὡς* marks the comparison. *πρὸς τὴν ἄφρονα πτῆσιν* keeps the direction of *al folle volo*; *ἄφρονα* is acc. sg. fem. of the two-termination adjective *ἄφρων*, agreeing with *πτῆσιν*.
- *Ἐπ᾽ ἀριστερὰν ᾀεὶ πλέοντες ἦμεν* ‘we were always sailing to the left’ supplies the main predication: *πλέοντες* (nom. masc. pl. pres. act. participle of *πλέω*) with imperfect *ἦμεν* gives a periphrastic imperfect. Sailing replaces *acquistando*, with no separate word for gaining ground. Elided *ἐπί* takes the directional accusative *ἀριστεράν* ‘to the left [hand]’; *ᾀεί* is the spelling here of the adverb ‘always’.

> 127 Tutte le stelle già dell' altro polo  
> 128 vedea la notte, e il nostro tanto basso,  
> 129 che non surgeva fuor del marin suolo.  

Night already saw the other pole, with all its stars; and ours so low, that it rose not from the ocean floor.

> 127 „ Ἀστέρας ἤδη θἀτέρου πόλου πάντας  
> („ Already all the stars of the other pole)
>
> 128 „ Τὴν νύκτ᾽ ἔβλεπον· ὁ δ᾽ ἡμέτερος κάτω  
> („ I beheld by night; while ours, low,)
>
> 129 „ Ὑπὸ τὸν ὁρίζοντα βάδην ἐχώρει.  
> („ Gradually went below the horizon.)

- *Ἀστέρας ἤδη θἀτέρου πόλου πάντας* keeps *Tutte le stelle* as the fronted object, but sets *πάντας* (acc. masc. pl. of *πᾶς*) at the far end of the group from *ἀστέρας*. *θἀτέρου* is the contracted article and adjective ‘of the other’ (Attic crasis corresponding to *τοῦ ἑτέρου*); both the adjective and *πόλου* are gen. sg.
- *Τὴν νύκτ᾽ ἔβλεπον* takes *la notte* as a temporal accusative, ‘by night’, not as the observing subject of the English prose. *νύκτ᾽* is elided *νύκτα*, acc. sg. of *νύξ*; *ἔβλεπον* is here 1st sg. imperf. act. ind. of *βλέπω* (formally also 3rd pl.).
- *ὁ δ᾽ ἡμέτερος* ‘whereas ours’ supplies *πόλος* from the genitive in the preceding clause; the article and possessive are now nominative, introducing a new subject. For the degree and negative result in *tanto basso, che non surgeva*, Musurus gives a positive description of motion: *κάτω ὑπὸ τὸν ὁρίζοντα βάδην ἐχώρει* ‘it moved downward, step by step, below the horizon’. *ἐχώρει* is 3rd sg. imperf. act. ind. of *χωρέω*; *βάδην* is an adverb of gradual progress. Directional *ὑπό* takes the accusative *ὁρίζοντα* (substantivized participle of *ὁρίζω*, with ‘circle’ understood), replacing *marin suolo*.

> 130 Cinque volte racceso, e tante casso  
> 131 lo lume era di sotto dalla luna,  
> 132 poi ch' entrati eravam nell' alto passo,  

Five times the light beneath the Moon had been rekindled and quenched as oft, since we had entered on the arduous passage,

> 130 „ Πεντάκις πλῆρες ἀφανές τε τοσάκις  
> („ Five times full, and as many times dark,)
>
> 131 „ Τὸ φῶς ἐφάνη κάτωθεν τῆς σελήνης,  
> („ Had the light appeared beneath the moon,)
>
> 132 „ Ἀφ᾽ ὅτου διέβημεν τὸν αἰπὺν πόρον,  
> („ Since we had passed through the steep strait,)

- *Πεντάκις πλῆρες ἀφανές τε τοσάκις* ‘five times full and as many times invisible’ replaces the participles *racceso … casso* with predicate adjectives. *πλῆρες* (from *πλήρης*) and *ἀφανές* (from *ἀφανής*) are nom. neut. sg., agreeing with the delayed *τὸ φῶς*. Postpositive *τε* attaches the second predicate to the first; the two adverbs of repetition frame the pair.
- *Τὸ φῶς ἐφάνη κάτωθεν τῆς σελήνης* ‘the light appeared beneath the moon’: *ἐφάνη* (3rd sg. second aor. pass. ind. of *φαίνω*, intransitive ‘appeared’) serves both adjectives, rather than reproducing *era* with participles. *κάτωθεν* with the genitive gives the position of the light as in *di sotto dalla luna*.
- *Ἀφ᾽ ὅτου διέβημεν* ‘since we crossed’ uses a temporal relative phrase, literally ‘from which [time]’; elided *ἀπό* becomes *ἀφ᾽* before the rough breathing. *διέβημεν* (1st pl. root aor. act. ind. of *διαβαίνω*, with internal augment) replaces pluperfect *entrati eravam*. It takes *τὸν αἰπὺν πόρον* directly as the passage traversed, rather than the passage entered; poetic *αἰπύν* (acc. masc. sg. of *αἰπύς*) gives *alto* the sense ‘steep’.

> 133 quando n' apparve una montagna bruna  
> 134 per la distanza, e parvemi alta tanto,  
> 135 quanto veduta non n' aveva alcuna.  

when there appeared to us a Mountain, dim with distance ; and to me it seemed the highest I had ever seen.

> 133 „ Ὅτ᾽ ὄρος ἡμῖν παρέστη μελανόχρουν  
> („ When there appeared before us a mountain dark-hued)
>
> 134 „ Ἐκ τῆς ἀποστάσεως, τοσούτου δ᾽ ὕψους,  
> („ From the distance, and of so great a height,)
>
> 135 „ Οἷον οὐδέποτ’ ἐγὼ πρότερον εἶδον.  
> („ Such as I had never seen before.)

- *Ὅτ᾽ ὄρος ἡμῖν παρέστη μελανόχρουν* follows *quando n' apparve una montagna bruna*: elided *ὅτε* introduces the temporal clause, *ἡμῖν* is the dative of the observers, and *παρέστη* (3rd sg. intransitive root aor. act. ind. of *παρίστημι*) means ‘stood before [us]’. *μελανόχρουν* ‘dark-hued’ is the contracted nom. neut. sg. of *μελανόχρους*, agreeing with *ὄρος*.
- *Ἐκ τῆς ἀποστάσεως* gives the cause of the dark appearance, as does *per la distanza*. *τοσούτου δ᾽ ὕψους* ‘and of such a height’ is a descriptive genitive, replacing *parvemi alta tanto*: there is no second verb of seeming or dative corresponding to *-mi*. *ὕψους* is the contracted gen. sg. of *ὕψος*.
- *Οἷον οὐδέποτ’ ἐγὼ πρότερον εἶδον* ‘such as I never saw before’ answers *τοσούτου* with a relative of quality, not a superlative. *οἷον* is acc. neut. sg., object of *εἶδον* (1st sg. second aor. act. ind. of *ὁράω*); its case belongs to the relative clause, not to the preceding genitive. Emphatic *ἐγώ*, elided *οὐδέποτε*, and *πρότερον* make the personal and temporal comparison explicit, without a separate equivalent of *alcuna* or the pluperfect *aveva*.

> 136 Noi ci allegrammo, e tosto tornò in pianto:  

We joyed, and soon our joy was turned to grief:

> 136 „ Ἥσθημεν χαρὰν τραπεῖσαν τάχ’ εἰς πένθος·  
> („ We rejoiced with a joy soon turned into grief;)

- *Ἥσθημεν χαράν* ‘we rejoiced a joy’ expands reflexive *ci allegrammo* into *ἥσθημεν* (1st pl. aor. pass. ind. of *ἥδομαι*, with active sense) and an accusative of kindred meaning. This supplies an explicit noun for what underwent the change in *tosto tornò in pianto*.
- *τραπεῖσαν* (acc. fem. sg. second aor. pass. participle of *τρέπω*) agrees with *χαράν*: the Italian second finite verb becomes a participle, ‘turned’. *τάχ᾽ εἰς πένθος* has elided *τάχα* ‘soon’ and *εἰς* with the accusative of the resulting state; *πένθος* names grief rather than weeping.

> 137 chè dalla nuova terra un turbo nacque,  
> 138 e percosse del legno il primo canto.  

for a tempest rose from the new land, and struck the forepart of our ship.

> 137 „ Ἐξεῤῥάγη γὰρ ἐκ τῆς νέας γῆς λαῖλαψ  
> („ For from the new land a whirlwind burst forth)
>
> 138 „ Κατὰ πρόσωπον πατάξασα τὸ σκάφος,  
> („ Striking the vessel full in the prow,)

- *Ἐξεῤῥάγη γὰρ ἐκ τῆς νέας γῆς λαῖλαψ* ‘for a squall burst forth from the new land’ puts the verb first and its nominative subject last. Postpositive *γάρ* gives the reason (*chè*). *ἐξεῤῥάγη* (3rd sg. second aor. pass. ind. of *ἐκρήγνυμι*, intransitive here) is more forceful than *nacque*; the compound shows the internal augment and doubled rho, printed with smooth and rough breathings. *ἐκ* with the genitive repeats the notion of emergence.
- *Κατὰ πρόσωπον πατάξασα τὸ σκάφος* ‘striking the vessel head-on’ turns coordinated *e percosse* into *πατάξασα* (nom. fem. sg. aor. act. participle of *πατάσσω*), agreeing with feminine *λαῖλαψ*. The whole vessel, *τὸ σκάφος*, is the direct object; *κατὰ πρόσωπον* is adverbial. Thus the Greek specifies the direction of impact where *del legno il primo canto* identifies the part struck.

> 139 Tre volte il fe' girar con tutte l' acque,  

Three times it made her whirl round with all the waters

> 139 „ Ὃ τρὶς κύμασι συμπεριεδινήθη·  
> („ Which three times was whirled round with the waves;)

- *Ὃ τρὶς κύμασι συμπεριεδινήθη* ‘which three times was whirled about together with the waves’ takes up *τὸ σκάφος* with *ὅ*, nom. neut. sg. relative pronoun. The vessel becomes the grammatical subject instead of the object of causative *il fe' girar*.
- *συμπεριεδινήθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *συμπεριδινέω*, augmented before the verbal stem) combines the circling of *περι-* with the joint motion of *συμ-*. *κύμασι* (dat. pl. of *κῦμα*) is the accompanying element: ‘waves’ replaces *acque*, without an equivalent for *tutte*.

> 140 alla quarta levar la poppa in suso,  
> 141 e la prora ire in giù, com' altrui piacque,  

; at the fourth, *made* the poop rise up and prow go down, as pleased *Another*,

> 140 „ Ἀλλὰ τέταρτον πρύμνης ἀρθείσης ἄνω,  
> („ But the fourth time, the stern being lifted up,)
>
> 141 „ Καὶ κάτω δύσης πρώρας, ὡς ἔδοξ’ ἄλλῳ,  
> („ And the prow plunging downward, as seemed good to Another,)

- *Ἀλλὰ τέταρτον* ‘but a fourth time’ adds an adversative and uses the neut. sg. ordinal adverbially, without the preposition of *alla quarta*. Instead of continuing *fe'* with the infinitives *levar* and *ire*, the Greek sets up two genitive absolutes, circumstantial to the final finite verb.
- *πρύμνης ἀρθείσης ἄνω* ‘the stern having been raised up’ has a passive participle (gen. fem. sg. aor. pass. of *αἴρω*) agreeing with *πρύμνης*. By contrast, *καὶ κάτω δύσης πρώρας* ‘and the prow having sunk down’ uses an intransitive **active** participle: *δύσης* is gen. fem. sg. of the root aor. participle *δύς* of *δύω*, agreeing with *πρώρας*. The reversed placement of *ἄνω* and *κάτω* brings the opposed directions close together.
- *ὡς ἔδοξ’ ἄλλῳ* ‘as seemed good to another’ closely matches *com' altrui piacque*. *ἔδοξ᾽* is elided *ἔδοξε* (3rd sg. aor. act. ind. of *δοκέω*), used impersonally with the dative *ἄλλῳ* for the person whose pleasure or decision it was.

> 142 infin che il mar fu sopra noi richiuso."  

till the sea was closed above us."

> 142 „ Τέλος ἡ θάλασσ’ ἐφ’ ἡμᾶς συνεκλείσθη.”  
> („ At last the sea closed over us.”)

- *Τέλος* ‘at last’ is an adverbial accusative: Musurus substitutes a main clause marking the outcome for the temporal limit introduced by *infin che*. *ἡ θάλασσ᾽* is elided *ἡ θάλασσα*, nominative subject; the form has *-σσ-* rather than Attic *-ττ-*.
- *ἐφ᾽ ἡμᾶς συνεκλείσθη* ‘closed together over us’ retains the Italian order of *sopra noi richiuso*. *ἐπί*, elided and aspirated before *ἡμᾶς*, takes the accusative for the direction of closure. *συνεκλείσθη* (3rd sg. aor. pass. ind. of *συγκλείω*, with internal augment) renders *fu … richiuso* in one finite form; the prefix suggests closing together rather than explicitly closing **again**.

## Summary — Preserving the Contrast, Recasting the Clause

Musurus often preserves the balance of an Italian construction while changing its grammar. The delayed comparison *quante … di tante* survives as *ὅσας … τοσαύταις*: the first adjective is accusative with the fireflies seen, the second dative with the flames by which the ditch shines. Elsewhere the balance is lexical rather than syntactic. The bridle implicit in *affreno* reappears one line later as *χαλινός* ‘bridle’; virtue, the subject of *guidi*, becomes the genitive *ἀρετῆς* ‘of virtue’ dependent on that noun.

The participle is Musurus’ most versatile means of recasting a clause. An Italian relative becomes an articular participle in *ὁ τὸ σύμπαν φωτίζων* ‘the one who lights the whole’; *a morir* becomes the future participle *θανούμενος* ‘to die’, marking purpose after a verb of motion. The genitive absolute lets Musurus distinguish the people acting from the grammatical subject: *βαινόντων … ἡμῶν* ‘as we walked’ leaves *ὁ πούς* ‘the foot’ free to be the subject of the main verb. Particles can also change the relation between clauses. Postpositive *δέ* often carries the narrative forward, but *ὅμως* ‘nevertheless’ gives *e già* an adversative turn, and *γάρ* ‘for’ makes the appeal following *Considerate* explicitly explanatory.

Some of the most consequential departures concern who acts or speaks. In the opening address, it is *τὸ κλέος* ‘renown’, not Florence herself, that flies. In the exhortation, *Ἀναμνησθῶμεν* ‘let us remember’ replaces the second-person imperative *Considerate*; the inclusive voice continues in *ἴδιον ἡμῶν* ‘characteristic of us’, where Dante has *fatti non foste*. At *vedea la notte*, the Greek commits to a reading by making *τὴν νύκτα* a temporal accusative, ‘by night’, rather than treating night as the observer. Yet Musurus can be close without being literal word for word: *ὡς ἔδοξ᾽ ἄλλῳ* ‘as seemed good to another’ preserves both the impersonal turn of *com’ altrui piacque* and its refusal to name the one whose will is meant.
