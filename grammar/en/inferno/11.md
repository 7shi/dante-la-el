# Inferno 11

## Lines 1–9 — The Stench and the Tomb’s Inscription

> 1 In su l’ estremità d’ un’ alta ripa,  
> 2 Che facevan gran pietre rotte in cerchio,  
> 3 Venimmo sopra più crudele stipa:  

Upon the edge of a high bank, formed by large broken stones in a circle, we came above a still more cruel throng.

- *estremità* ‘extremity’ → “edge”: the more general noun is rendered by a word specifically denoting a boundary.
- *Che facevan* ‘which … made’ → “formed by”: the active relative clause becomes a reduced passive construction; the stones become its agent rather than its subject.
- *gran pietre rotte* ‘large stones broken’ → “large broken stones”: “broken” precedes the noun instead of following it.
- *più crudele stipa* ‘more cruel throng’ → “a still more cruel throng”: the indefinite article and the intensifying “still” are supplied.

> 4 E quivi per l’ orribile soperchio  
> 5 Del puzzo, che il profondo abisso gitta,  
> 6 Ci raccostammo dietro ad un coperchio  
> 7 D’ un grande avello, ov’ io vidi una scritta  

And here, because of the horrible excess of stench which the deep abyss throws out, we approached it under cover of a great monument, whereon I saw a writing

- *quivi* ‘there’ → “here”: the spatial reference changes from ‘there’ to “here”.
- *Del puzzo* ‘of the stench’ → “of stench”: the definite article is omitted.
- *gitta* ‘throws’ → “throws out”: the particle “out” is supplied.
- *Ci raccostammo* ‘we drew ourselves close’ → “we approached it”: the reflexive construction becomes a transitive verb with the supplied object “it”.
- *dietro ad un coperchio* ‘behind a lid’ → “under cover”: a concrete lid and the position behind it are recast as an expression of shelter; the indefinite article disappears with that change.
- *avello* ‘tomb’ → “monument”: the specific word for a tomb becomes a broader term.
- *ov’* ‘where’ → “whereon”: the translation makes the relation explicitly ‘on’, rather than merely locative.

> 8 Che diceva: Anastasio papa guardo,  
> 9 Lo qual trasse Fotin della via dritta.  

that said: “I hold Pope Anastasius, whom Photinus drew from the straight way.”

- *diceva* ‘was saying’ → “said”: the Italian imperfect is rendered by the English simple past.
- *Anastasio papa guardo* ‘Anastasius pope I hold’ → “I hold Pope Anastasius”: the object moves after the verb, the title precedes the name, and the subject implicit in the verb ending is expressed.
- *Lo qual trasse Fotin* ‘whom drew Photinus’ → “whom Photinus drew”: the subject moves before the verb.
- *della via dritta* ‘from the way straight’ → “from the straight way”: the adjective precedes the noun instead of following it.

## Lines 10–19 — Delay and the Lower Circles

> 10 Lo nostro scender convien esser tardo,  
> 11 Sì che s’ ausi prima un poco il senso  
> 12 Al tristo fiato, e poi non fia riguardo.  

“Our descent we must delay, till sense be somewhat used to the dismal blast, and then we shall not heed it.”

- *Lo nostro scender convien esser tardo* ‘Our descending must be slow’ → “Our descent we must delay”: the substantival infinitive becomes a noun; the impersonal construction requiring slow descent becomes a personal obligation to postpone it, with “Our descent” placed before the subject and verb as their object.
- *Sì che* ‘so that’ → “till”: a conjunction expressing purpose or result becomes a temporal conjunction.
- *s’ausi prima un poco il senso* ‘the sense first accustom itself a little’ → “sense be somewhat used”: the reflexive construction becomes “be … used”; the subject moves before the verb and loses its definite article; *prima* is omitted, and *un poco* becomes “somewhat”.
- *Al tristo fiato* ‘to the sad breath’ → “to the dismal blast”: “dismal” renders *tristo*, while “blast” gives *fiato* a stronger sense than ‘breath’.
- *e poi non fia riguardo* ‘and then there will be no regard’ → “and then we shall not heed it”: the existential construction becomes a personal statement; the noun *riguardo* becomes the verb “heed”, with “it” supplied as its object.

> 13 Così il Maestro. Ed io: Alcun compenso,  
> 14 Dissi lui, trova, che il tempo non passi  
> 15 Perduto. Ed egli: Vedi ch’ a ciò penso.  

Thus the Master. And I said to him: “Find some compensation, that the time may not be lost.” And he: “Thou seest that I intend it.”

- *Ed io: Alcun compenso, Dissi lui, trova* ‘And I: Some compensation, I said to him, find’ → “And I said to him: “Find some compensation”: the reporting words are brought together before the speech, rather than interrupted by its opening words; the object moves from before the imperative to after it.
- *che il tempo non passi Perduto* ‘that the time may not pass lost’ → “that the time may not be lost”: “be” replaces *passi*, removing the explicit expression of time passing.
- *a ciò penso* ‘of that I think’ → “I intend it”: thinking of something is interpreted as intending it; the prepositional phrase becomes a direct-object pronoun and moves after the verb.

> 16 Figliuol mio, dentro da cotesti sassi,  
> 17 Cominciò poi a dir, son tre cerchietti  
> 18 Di grado in grado, come quei che lassi.  

“My Son, within these stones,” he then began to say, “are three circlets in gradation, like those thou leavest.

- *Figliuol mio* ‘Son my’ → “My Son”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *dentro da cotesti sassi* ‘inside of those stones near thee’ → “within these stones”: the compound preposition becomes “within”; *cotesti*, pointing to something near the person addressed, becomes “these”, without that distinction.
- *Cominciò poi a dir* ‘began then to say’ → “he then began to say”: “then” moves before the finite verb, and its subject is made explicit.
- *Di grado in grado* ‘from step to step’ → “in gradation”: the repeated noun construction becomes a single abstract noun with a preposition.
- *quei che lassi* ‘those that thou leavest’ → “those thou leavest”: the relative pronoun is omitted.

> 19 Tutti son pien di spirti maledetti:  

They all are filled with spirits accurst.

- *Tutti* ‘All’ → “They all”: an explicit pronoun is added alongside “all”.
- *son pien di* ‘are full of’ → “are filled with”: the adjective becomes a participle, and “with” replaces ‘of’.

## Lines 20–33 — The Divisions of Malice

> 20 Ma perchè poi ti basti pur la vista,  
> 21 Intendi come, e perchè son costretti.  

But, that the sight of these hereafter may of itself suffice thee, hearken how and wherefore they are pent up.

- *poi ti basti pur la vista* ‘hereafter may suffice thee the sight alone’ → “the sight of these hereafter may of itself suffice thee”: the subject moves before the verb, and the object pronoun follows it; “of these” is supplied after “sight”; *pur* becomes “of itself,” expressing independent sufficiency rather than simply modifying ‘sight’ with ‘alone’.
- *Intendi* ‘understand’ → “hearken”: understanding is rendered as attentive listening.
- *costretti* ‘constrained’ → “pent up”: the translation specifies confinement.

> 22 D’ ogni malizia ch’ odio in Cielo acquista,  
> 23 Ingiuria è il fine; ed ogni fin cotale  
> 24 O con forza, o con frode altrui contrista.  

Of all malice, which gains hatred in Heaven, the end is injury; and every such end, either by force or by fraud, aggrieveth others.

- *ogni malizia* ‘every malice’ → “all malice”: the distributive determiner becomes a collective one.
- *ch’ odio in Cielo acquista* ‘which hatred in Heaven gains’ → “which gains hatred in Heaven”: the verb moves before its object and the accompanying prepositional phrase.
- *Ingiuria è il fine* ‘injury is the end’ → “the end is injury”: the two terms of the equation exchange positions.
- *ogni fin cotale* ‘every end such’ → “every such end”: the modifier precedes the noun.
- *O con forza, o con frode* ‘either with force or with fraud’ → “either by force or by fraud”: “by” replaces ‘with’ to express means.
- *altrui contrista* ‘others saddens’ → “aggrieveth others”: the object follows the verb, and ‘saddens’ becomes the broader “aggrieveth”.

> 25 Ma perchè frode è dell’ uom proprio male,  
> 26 Più spiace a Dio; e però stan di sutto  
> 27 Gli frodolenti, e più dolor gli assale.  

But because fraud is a vice peculiar to man, it more displeases God; and therefore the fraudulent are placed beneath, and more pain assails them.

- *dell’ uom proprio male* ‘man’s peculiar evil’ → “a vice peculiar to man”: ‘evil’ becomes “vice,” and an indefinite article is supplied; the adjective follows the noun, while ‘of man’ becomes “to man,” dependent on “peculiar”.
- *Più spiace a Dio* ‘is more displeasing to God’ → “it more displeases God”: the Italian construction with a dative becomes an English transitive verb with “God” as its direct object.
- *stan di sutto Gli frodolenti* ‘stand below the fraudulent’ → “the fraudulent are placed beneath”: the subject moves before the verb, and active ‘stand’ becomes passive “are placed”.
- *gli assale* ‘them assails’ → “assails them”: the object pronoun follows the verb.

> 28 De’ violenti il primo cerchio è tutto,  

“All the first circle is for the violent.

- *De’ violenti il primo cerchio è tutto* ‘of the violent the first circle is all’ → “All the first circle is for the violent”: the opening possessive phrase moves after the verb and becomes “for the violent,” expressing allocation; *tutto* moves from the predicate to the beginning of the subject phrase.

> 29 Ma perchè si fa forza a tre persone,  
> 30 In tre gironi è distinto e costrutto.  

But as violence may be done to three persons, it is formed and distinguished into three rounds.

- *si fa forza* ‘one does violence’ → “violence may be done”: the impersonal active construction becomes passive, and “may” adds an explicit expression of possibility.
- *In tre gironi è distinto e costrutto* ‘in three rounds it is distinguished and constructed’ → “it is formed and distinguished into three rounds”: the prepositional phrase moves after the verbs, with “into” marking the resulting division; the two participles exchange order, and ‘constructed’ becomes “formed”.

> 31 A Dio, a sè, al prossimo si puone  
> 32 Far forza; dico in loro, e in lor cose,  
> 33 Come udirai con aperta ragione.  

To God, to one’s self, and to one’s neighbour, may violence be done: I say in them and in their things, as thou shalt hear with evident discourse.

- *al prossimo* ‘to the neighbour’ → “and to one’s neighbour”: the conjunction is supplied, and a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *si puone Far forza* ‘one can do violence’ → “may violence be done”: the impersonal active construction becomes passive, with “may” rendering ‘can’.
- *con aperta ragione* ‘with open reasoning’ → “with evident discourse”: *aperta* is interpreted as clear or manifest, while *ragione* is rendered as explanatory discourse rather than reasoning.

## Lines 34–45 — Violence Against Others and Oneself

> 34 Morte per forza, e ferute dogliose  
> 35 Nel prossimo si danno, e nel suo avere  
> 36 Ruine, incendi e tollette dannose:  

“By force, death and painful wounds may be inflicted upon one’s neighbour; and upon his substance, devastations, burnings, and injurious extortions:

- *Morte per forza* ‘death by force’ → “By force, death”: the phrase expressing means is moved before the noun.
- *ferute dogliose* ‘wounds painful’ → “painful wounds”: the adjective is moved before the noun.
- *Nel prossimo* ‘in the neighbour’ → “upon one’s neighbour”: the preposition changes to suit “inflicted”, and the definite article becomes the indefinite possessive “one’s”.
- *si danno* ‘are given’ → “may be inflicted”: “inflicted” replaces ‘given’, and “may” adds an explicit expression of possibility absent from the Italian present indicative.
- *nel suo avere* ‘in his possessions’ → “upon his substance”: the preposition changes, and possessions are expressed through the collective noun “substance”.
- *Ruine* ‘ruins’ → “devastations”: the rendering emphasizes destructive acts rather than their ruined results.
- *tollette dannose* ‘seizures harmful’ → “injurious extortions”: “extortions” interprets the unlawful takings specifically as extortions, and the adjective moves before the noun.

> 37 Onde omicidi, e ciascun che mal fiere,  
> 38 Guastatori e predon, tutti tormenta  
> 39 Lo giron primo per diverse schiere.  

wherefore the first round torments all homicides and every one who strikes maliciously, all plunderers and robbers, in different bands.

- *mal fiere* ‘ill strikes’ → “strikes maliciously”: the adverb follows the verb rather than preceding it, and “maliciously” makes the wrongful intention explicit.
- *Guastatori* ‘ravagers’ → “plunderers”: a word emphasizing destruction is rendered by one emphasizing the taking of property.
- *tutti tormenta Lo giron primo* ‘all torments the round first’ → “the first round torments all”: the subject moves before the verb, the adjective precedes “round”, and the listed objects follow rather than precede the verb. The resumptive *tutti*, which gathers together the preceding list, becomes “all” before “homicides” and a repeated “all” before “plunderers”.
- *per diverse schiere* ‘by different bands’ → “in different bands”: distributive *per* is rendered with “in”.

> 40 Puote uomo avere in sè man violenta  
> 41 E ne’ suoi beni: e però nel secondo  
> 42 Giron convien che senza pro si penta  
> 43 Qualunque priva sè del vostro mondo,  

A man may lay violent hand upon himself, and upon his property: and therefore in the second round must every one repent in vain who deprives himself of your world,

- *Puote uomo avere … man violenta* ‘can man have … hand violent’ → “A man may lay violent hand”: the subject moves before the modal and receives an indefinite article; “may” renders ability or possibility; “lay” replaces ‘have’; and the adjective moves before “hand”.
- *in sè* ‘in himself’ → “upon himself”: the preposition changes to fit the English expression “lay violent hand”.
- *ne’ suoi beni* ‘in his goods’ → “upon his property”: the preposition likewise changes, and the plural *beni* becomes the singular collective “property”.
- *convien che … si penta* ‘it is necessary that … repent’ → “must” and “repent”: an impersonal main verb followed by a subordinate subjunctive clause becomes a modal construction; the equivalent of *che* disappears.
- *senza pro* ‘without profit’ → “in vain”: the literal prepositional phrase becomes an English idiom expressing fruitlessness.
- *Qualunque* ‘whoever’ → “every one” and “who”: the single indefinite relative is expanded into an antecedent and a relative pronoun. “every one” is moved before “repent”, while its relative clause remains afterward.

> 44 Biscazza e fonde la sua facultade,  
> 45 E piange là dove esser dee giocondo.  

gambles away and dissipates his wealth, and weeps there where he should be joyous.

- *Biscazza* ‘gambles’ → “gambles away”: “away” explicitly expresses the loss of the wealth being gambled.
- *fonde* ‘melts’ → “dissipates”: the image of melting becomes a conventional expression for squandering wealth.
- *esser dee giocondo* ‘to be ought joyous’ → “he should be joyous”: the modal precedes the infinitive rather than following it, and “should” takes the bare infinitive “be”.

## Lines 46–56 — Violence Against God and Fraud

> 46 Puossi far forza nella Deitade,  
> 47 Col cor negando e bestemmiando quella,  
> 48 E spregiando Natura, e sua bontade:  

Violence may be done against the Deity, in the heart denying and blaspheming Him; and disdaining Nature and her bounty:

- *Puossi far forza* ‘one can do violence’ → “Violence may be done”: the impersonal construction becomes passive, with “Violence” as subject; ‘can’ becomes “may”.
- *nella Deitade* ‘in the Deity’ → “against the Deity”: the preposition expresses opposition rather than location.
- *Col cor* ‘with the heart’ → “in the heart”: an expression of means or manner becomes one of inward location.
- *quella* ‘that one’ → “Him”: the feminine demonstrative referring to *Deitade* becomes a masculine personal pronoun.
- *bontade* ‘goodness’ → “bounty”: the translation chooses the sense of beneficence rather than the broader quality of goodness.

> 49 E però lo minor giron suggella  
> 50 Del segno suo e Sodoma, e Caorsa,  
> 51 E chi, spregiando Dio, col cor favella.  

and hence the smallest round seals with its mark both Sodom and Cahors, and all who speak with disparagement of God in their hearts.

- *lo minor giron* ‘the smaller round’ → “the smallest round”: the comparative form is rendered as an English superlative.
- *Del segno suo* ‘of its mark’ → “with its mark”: instrumental *di* is rendered by “with”, and the possessive moves from after the noun to before it.
- *e Sodoma, e Caorsa* ‘and Sodom, and Cahors’ → “both Sodom and Cahors”: the repeated conjunction becomes the English correlative construction “both” … “and”.
- *chi* ‘whoever’ → “all who”: the indefinite relative becomes an explicitly plural expression.
- *spregiando Dio* ‘despising God’ → “with disparagement of God”: the participial construction becomes a prepositional phrase containing a noun, and moves from before the verb of speaking to after it.
- *col cor favella* ‘with the heart speaks’ → “speak” … “in their hearts”: the verb moves before the phrase modifying it and becomes plural; “in” replaces ‘with’, while the singular definite noun becomes plural with an added possessive.

> 52 La frode, ond’ ogni coscïenza è morsa,  
> 53 Può l’ uomo usare in quei, ch’ in lui si fida,  
> 54 E in quei che fidanza non imborsa.  

“Fraud, which gnaws every conscience, a man may practise upon those who confide in him; and upon those who repose no confidence.

- *La frode* ‘the fraud’ → “Fraud”: the definite article is omitted before the abstract noun.
- *ond’ ogni coscïenza è morsa* ‘by which every conscience is bitten’ → “which gnaws every conscience”: the passive becomes active, with the relative pronoun as subject and “every conscience” as object; ‘is bitten’ becomes “gnaws”.
- *Può l’ uomo usare* ‘can the man use’ → “a man may practise”: the subject moves before the modal verb, the generic definite article becomes indefinite, and ‘can use’ becomes “may practise”.
- *in quei, ch’ in lui si fida* ‘upon him who in him trusts’ → “upon those who confide in him”: singular *quei* and its singular verb become plural, and the phrase ‘in him’ moves after the verb.
- *E in quei che fidanza non imborsa* ‘and upon him who trust does not pocket’ → “and upon those who repose no confidence”: the singular referent and verb become plural; ‘pocket trust’ becomes “repose … confidence”, the object moves after the verb, and verbal negation becomes “no” modifying the noun.

> 55 Questo modo di retro par che uccida  
> 56 Pur lo vincol d’ amor che fa Natura:  

This latter mode seems only to cut off the bond of love which Nature makes:

- *modo di retro* ‘mode behind’ → “latter mode”: the following adverbial phrase becomes a preceding adjective, interpreting the backward reference as ‘last-mentioned’.
- *par che uccida Pur* ‘seems that it kills only’ → “seems only to cut off”: the finite subjunctive clause becomes an infinitive, ‘kills’ becomes “cut off”, and “only” moves from immediately before the object to before the infinitive.
- *che fa Natura* ‘which makes Nature’ → “which Nature makes”: the subject moves from after the verb to before it.

## Lines 57–69 — Fraud and Betrayal Distinguished

> 57 Onde nel cerchio secondo s’ annida  
> 58 Ipocrisia, lusinghe e chi affattura,  
> 59 Falsità, ladroneccio e simonia,  
> 60 Ruffian, baratti, e simile lordura.  

hence in the second circle nests hypocrisy, flattery, sorcerers, cheating, theft and simony, panders, barators, and like filth.

- *nel cerchio secondo* ‘in the circle second’ → “in the second circle”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *s’ annida* ‘nests itself’ → “nests”: the Italian pronominal verb becomes an English intransitive verb without a reflexive pronoun.
- *lusinghe* ‘flatteries’ → “flattery”: the plural becomes an uncountable singular.
- *chi affattura* ‘whoever practises sorcery’ → “sorcerers”: a singular relative construction becomes a plural noun naming the practitioners.
- *Falsità* ‘falsehood’ → “cheating”: the abstract noun is rendered as an activity.

> 61 Per l’ altro modo quell’ amor s’ obblia  
> 62 Che fa Natura, e quel ch’ è poi aggiunto,  
> 63 Di che la fede spezïal si cria:  

In the other mode is forgotten that love which Nature makes, and also that which afterwards is added, giving birth to special trust.

- *Per l’ altro modo* ‘by the other mode’ → “In the other mode”: the preposition expressing means becomes “In”.
- *quell’ amor s’ obblia* ‘that love is forgotten’ → “is forgotten that love”: the passive verb precedes its subject.
- *Che fa Natura* ‘which makes Nature’ → “which Nature makes”: the subject, *Natura*, moves before the verb; the relative pronoun remains its object.
- *e quel ch’ è poi aggiunto* ‘and that which is afterwards added’ → “and also that which afterwards is added”: “also” is supplied, and “afterwards” moves before the auxiliary.
- *Di che la fede spezïal si cria* ‘from which the trust special is created’ → “giving birth to special trust”: the passive relative clause becomes an active participial phrase, with creation expressed as “giving birth”; the definite article is omitted, and the adjective precedes the noun.

> 64 Onde nel cerchio minore, ov’ è il punto  
> 65 Dell’ universo, in su che Dite siede,  
> 66 Qualunque trade in eterno è consunto.  

Hence in the smallest circle, at the centre of the universe and seat of Dis, every traitor is eternally consumed.”

- *nel cerchio minore* ‘in the smaller circle’ → “in the smallest circle”: the comparative is rendered as a superlative, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *ov’ è il punto Dell’ universo* ‘where is the point of the universe’ → “at the centre of the universe”: the relative clause becomes a prepositional phrase, and “centre” makes the sense of *punto* explicit.
- *in su che Dite siede* ‘upon which Dis sits’ → “and seat of Dis”: the relative clause becomes a coordinated noun phrase; “and” is supplied, the verb becomes the noun “seat”, and its subject becomes a possessive complement.
- *Qualunque trade* ‘whoever betrays’ → “every traitor”: the indefinite relative construction becomes a noun phrase, with the action expressed through a noun naming its agent.
- *in eterno* ‘for eternity’ → “eternally”: the prepositional phrase becomes an adverb.

> 67 Ed io: Maestro, assai chiaro procede  
> 68 La tua ragione, ed assai ben distingue  
> 69 Questo baratro, e il popol che il possiede.  

And I: “Master, thy discourse proceeds most clearly, and excellently distinguishes this gulf, and the people that possess it.

- *assai chiaro procede La tua ragione* ‘very clearly proceeds thy reasoning’ → “thy discourse proceeds most clearly”: the subject moves before the verb and the adverbial phrase after it; *ragione* becomes “discourse” rather than ‘reasoning’, and *assai* is expressed by the intensifying “most”.
- *assai ben* ‘very well’ → “excellently”: the two-word expression becomes a single adverb.
- *il popol che il possiede* ‘the people that possesses it’ → “the people that possess it”: the Italian singular collective takes plural agreement in English, and the object pronoun moves after the verb.

## Lines 70–84 — Why Incontinence Receives Less Blame

> 70 Ma dimmi: Quei della palude pingue,  
> 71 Che mena il vento, e che batte la pioggia,  
> 72 E che s’ incontran con sì aspre lingue,  

But tell me: Those of the fat marsh; those whom the wind leads, and whom the rain beats; and those who meet with tongues so sharp,—

- *palude pingue* ‘marsh fat’ → “fat marsh”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *Che mena il vento* ‘whom leads the wind’ → “those whom the wind leads”: “those” is supplied before the relative pronoun, and the subject precedes the verb.
- *e che batte la pioggia* ‘and whom beats the rain’ → “and whom the rain beats”: the subject precedes the verb.
- *E che s’ incontran* ‘And who encounter one another’ → “and those who meet”: “those” is supplied, and the reciprocal force of *s’* is left implicit in “meet”.
- *con sì aspre lingue* ‘with so harsh tongues’ → “with tongues so sharp”: “sharp” renders *aspre*, and the adjective and its modifier follow the noun rather than precede it.

> 73 Perchè non dentro della città roggia  
> 74 Son ei puniti, se Dio gli ha in ira?  
> 75 E se non gli ha, perchè sono a tal foggia?  

why are they not punished in the red city, if God’s anger be upon them? And if not, why are they in such plight?”

- *Perchè non dentro della città roggia Son ei puniti* ‘Why not within the city red are they punished’ → “why are they not punished in the red city”: the passive verb and its subject move before the phrase of place; “not” follows the subject; “in” renders *dentro della*; and the adjective precedes “city”.
- *se Dio gli ha in ira* ‘if God has them in anger’ → “if God’s anger be upon them”: the construction with God as subject and the condemned as object becomes one with “God’s anger” as subject and “upon them” as its complement. The indicative *ha* becomes the subjunctive “be”.
- *E se non gli ha* ‘And if he does not have them’ → “And if not”: the clause is reduced to an elliptical condition, without an expressed verb or object.
- *a tal foggia* ‘in such a manner’ → “in such plight”: a phrase describing manner becomes one describing condition.

> 76 Ed egli a me: Perchè tanto delira,  
> 77 Disse, lo ingegno tuo da quel ch’ ei suole?  
> 78 Ovver la mente dove altrove mira?  

And he to me: “Wherefore errs thy mind so much beyond its wont? Or are thy thoughts turned somewhere else?

- *Perchè tanto delira* ‘Wherefore so much raves’ → “Wherefore errs” and “so much”: *delira* is rendered as “errs”, and the expression of degree moves from before the verb to after its subject.
- *Disse* ‘he said’ → omitted.
- *lo ingegno tuo* ‘the intellect thy’ → “thy mind”: “mind” renders *ingegno*, and the possessive precedes the noun instead of following it.
- *da quel ch’ ei suole* ‘from that which it is wont to do’ → “beyond its wont”: “beyond” replaces ‘from’, and the relative clause expressing habitual action becomes a possessive noun phrase.
- *la mente dove altrove mira* ‘the mind where elsewhere looks’ → “are thy thoughts turned somewhere else”: the singular “mind” becomes plural “thoughts”, with “thy” supplied; the active verb of looking becomes the passive “are … turned”; and the two expressions of place are combined as “somewhere else”. The auxiliary precedes the subject to form the English question.

> 79 Non ti rimembra di quelle parole,  
> 80 Con le quai la tua Etica pertratta  
> 81 Le tre disposizion, che il Ciel non vuole;  

Rememberest thou not the words wherewith thy Ethics treat of the three dispositions which Heaven wills not,

- *Non ti rimembra* ‘Does it not come to mind for thee’ → “Rememberest thou not”: the Italian impersonal construction, with the person remembering expressed by *ti*, becomes an active construction with that person as subject.
- *di quelle parole* ‘of those words’ → “the words”: the preposition is dropped as the phrase becomes the direct object of “Rememberest”, and the demonstrative becomes a definite article.
- *Con le quai* ‘with which’ → “wherewith”: the preposition and relative pronoun become a compound relative adverb.
- *la tua Etica pertratta* ‘thy Ethics treats’ → “thy Ethics treat of”: the Italian singular noun and verb become grammatically plural in English, and the transitive *pertratta* becomes “treat of”.
- *non vuole* ‘not wills’ → “wills not”: the negative follows the verb rather than preceding it.

> 82 Incontinenza, malizia e la matta  
> 83 Bestialitade? e come incontinenza  
> 84 Men Dio offende, e men biasimo accatta?  

incontinence, malice, and mad bestiality? And how incontinence less offends God, and receives less blame?

- *la matta Bestialitade* ‘the mad bestiality’ → “mad bestiality”: the definite article is omitted.
- *Men Dio offende* ‘less God offends’ → “less offends God”: the object follows the verb instead of preceding it.
- *e men biasimo accatta* ‘and less blame acquires’ → “and receives less blame”: “receives” renders *accatta*, and the verb precedes its object instead of following it.

## Lines 85–96 — Divine Justice and Usury

> 85 Se tu riguardi ben questa sentenza,  
> 86 E rechiti alla mente chi son quelli,  
> 87 Che su di fuor sostengon penitenza,  

If thou rightly considerest this doctrine, and recallest to thy memory who they are that suffer punishment above, without,

- *riguardi ben* ‘lookest well at’ → “rightly considerest”: looking becomes considering; *ben* becomes “rightly” and precedes the verb.
- *questa sentenza* ‘this judgment’ → “this doctrine”: “doctrine” presents the statement as a teaching rather than a judgment or pronouncement.
- *rechiti alla mente* ‘bringest to thy mind’ → “recallest to thy memory”: bringing becomes recalling, and “memory” replaces ‘mind’; the reflexive *ti* is represented by “thy”.
- *chi son quelli* ‘who are those’ → “who they are”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun, and the clause takes English indirect-question word order.
- *su di fuor* ‘up outside’ → “above, without”: the spatial expressions follow the verb and its object rather than preceding them; “without” means ‘outside’.
- *sostengon penitenza* ‘undergo penance’ → “suffer punishment”: both the verb and the noun are recast, with “punishment” replacing ‘penance’.

> 88 Tu vedrai ben perchè da questi felli  
> 89 Sien dipartiti, e perchè men crucciata  
> 90 La divina giustizia gli martelli.  

thou easily wilt see why they are separated from these fell spirits, and why, with less anger, Divine Justice strikes them.”

- *Tu vedrai ben* ‘thou wilt see well’ → “thou easily wilt see”: *ben* becomes “easily”, and the adverb moves before the auxiliary.
- *da questi felli* ‘from these wicked ones’ → “from these fell spirits”: the substantival adjective is expanded with the supplied noun “spirits”; the phrase follows “are separated” rather than preceding it.
- *Sien dipartiti* ‘they be separated’ → “they are separated”: the Italian subjunctive becomes an English indicative.
- *men crucciata* ‘less angered’ → “with less anger”: an adjective describing Divine Justice becomes a prepositional phrase expressing how it acts.
- *La divina giustizia* ‘the divine justice’ → “Divine Justice”: the definite article is omitted.
- *gli martelli* ‘them hammer’ → “strikes them”: “strikes” weakens the specific image of hammering; the object follows the verb, and the subjunctive becomes indicative.

> 91 O Sol, che sani ogni vista turbata,  
> 92 Tu mi contenti sì, quando tu solvi,  
> 93 Che, non men che saver, dubbiar m’ aggrata.  

“O Sun! who healest all troubled vision, thou makest so glad when thou resolvest me, that to doubt is not less grateful than to know.

- *ogni vista turbata* ‘every troubled sight’ → “all troubled vision”: the distributive ‘every’ becomes “all” with a mass noun; the adjective moves before the noun.
- *Tu mi contenti sì* ‘thou makest me so content’ → “thou makest so glad”: “glad” replaces ‘content’, the object *mi* is not expressed here, and “so” precedes the adjective rather than following the verb.
- *quando tu solvi* ‘when thou resolvest’ → “when thou resolvest me”: “me” is supplied as the object of a verb left without an expressed object in Italian.
- *non men che saver, dubbiar m’ aggrata* ‘not less than knowing, doubting pleases me’ → “to doubt is not less grateful than to know”: the subject moves first and the comparison follows the predicate; the active ‘pleases me’ becomes “is … grateful”, meaning ‘pleasing’, with *m’* left unexpressed.

> 94 Ancora un poco indietro ti rivolvi,  
> 95 Diss’ io, là dove di’ che usura offende  
> 96 La divina bontade, e il groppo svolvi.  

Turn thee yet a little back, to where thou sayest that usury offends the Divine Goodness, and unravel the knot.”

- *Ancora un poco indietro ti rivolvi* ‘yet a little back turn thyself’ → “Turn thee yet a little back”: the imperative and its reflexive pronoun move before the adverbial expressions.
- *Diss’ io, là dove di’* ‘said I, there where thou sayest’ → “to where thou sayest”: the reporting clause is omitted, and the locative ‘there’ becomes directional “to”.
- *e il groppo svolvi* ‘and the knot unravel’ → “and unravel the knot”: the object moves after the imperative.

## Lines 97–108 — Nature and Human Art

> 97 Filosofia, mi disse, a chi l’ attende,  
> 98 Nota non pure in una sola parte,  
> 99 Come Natura lo suo corso prende  
> 100 Dal divino Intelletto e da sua arte:  

He said to me: “Philosophy, to him who hears it, points out, not in one place alone, how Nature takes her course from the Divine Intellect, and from its art.

- *mi disse* ‘to me he said’ → “He said to me”: the reporting clause moves before *Filosofia*, and the indirect object follows the verb.
- *a chi l’ attende* ‘to whoever attends to it’ → “to him who hears it”: the indefinite relative becomes a demonstrative followed by a relative clause; attending to philosophy is rendered as hearing it.
- *Nota* ‘notes’ → “points out”: the simple verb becomes a phrasal verb expressing explicit indication.
- *non pure in una sola parte* ‘not only in a single part’ → “not in one place alone”: “place” replaces ‘part’, and the force of *pure* and *sola* is expressed by “one” and the final “alone”.
- *lo suo corso prende* ‘her course takes’ → “takes her course”: the verb moves before its object.

> 101 E se tu ben la tua Fisica note,  
> 102 Tu troverai non dopo molte carte,  
> 103 Che l’ arte vostra quella, quanto puote,  
> 104 Segue, come il maestro fa il discente,  

And, if thou note well thy Physics, thou wilt find, not many pages from the first, that your art, as far as it can, follows her, as the scholar does his master;

- *ben la tua Fisica note* ‘well thy Physics note’ → “note well thy Physics”: the verb moves before both the adverb and its object.
- *non dopo molte carte* ‘not after many leaves’ → “not many pages from the first”: “pages” replaces ‘leaves’; the preposition of sequence becomes an expression of distance from a starting point, with “the first” supplied.
- *l’ arte vostra quella, quanto puote, Segue* ‘the art of yours that one, as much as it can, follows’ → “your art, as far as it can, follows her”: the possessive moves before the noun, and the object moves after the verb. The demonstrative *quella*, referring to Nature, becomes the personal pronoun “her”; ‘as much as’ becomes “as far as”.
- *come il maestro fa il discente* ‘as the master does the learner’ → “as the scholar does his master”: Italian places the object, *il maestro*, before the verb and the subject, *il discente*, after it; English puts the subject first and the object last. ‘Learner’ becomes “scholar”, and the definite article before ‘master’ becomes the possessive “his”.

> 105 Sì che vostr’ arte a Dio quasi è nipote.  

so that your art is, as it were, the grandchild of the Deity.

- *a Dio quasi è nipote* ‘to God almost is grandchild’ → “is, as it were, the grandchild of the Deity”: the verb moves before its complement, and the relationship expressed by *a Dio* becomes an “of” phrase after “grandchild”. “The Deity” replaces ‘God’, and “the” is supplied before “grandchild”. *Quasi* is rendered as the qualification “as it were”, rather than the degree adverb ‘almost’.

> 106 Da queste due, se tu ti rechi a mente  
> 107 Lo Genesi dal principio, conviene  
> 108 Prender sua vita, ed avanzar la gente.  

By these two, if thou recallest to thy memory Genesis at the beginning, it behoves man to gain his bread, and multiply the people.

- *Da queste due* ‘From these two’ → “By these two”: the preposition presents the two as means rather than sources.
- *se tu ti rechi a mente* ‘if thou bringest to thyself to mind’ → “if thou recallest to thy memory”: the idiom ‘bring to mind’ becomes “recallest”; ‘mind’ becomes “memory”, and the reflexive *ti* is represented by the possessive “thy”.
- *Lo Genesi dal principio* ‘the Genesis from the beginning’ → “Genesis at the beginning”: the article before the title is omitted, and “at” replaces ‘from’.
- *conviene Prender sua vita, ed avanzar la gente* ‘it behoves the people to take their livelihood and advance’ → “it behoves man to gain his bread, and multiply the people”: Carlyle supplies “man” as the subject of the infinitives and takes *la gente* as the object of *avanzar*, rather than as the postposed subject of both infinitives. Accordingly, *avanzar* becomes transitive “multiply” rather than intransitive ‘advance’ or ‘prosper’. ‘Take their livelihood’ becomes the more concrete “gain his bread”, with the possessive referring to the supplied singular “man”.

## Lines 109–115 — Usury and the Descent

> 109 E perchè l’ usuriere altra via tiene,  
> 110 Per sè Natura, e per la sua seguace  
> 111 Dispregia, poichè in altro pon la spene.  

And because the usurer takes another way, he contemns Nature in herself and in her follower, placing elsewhere his hope.

- *altra via tiene* ‘another way holds’ → “takes another way”: the idiomatic ‘holds a way’ becomes “takes another way”, and the object moves after the verb.
- *Per sè Natura, e per la sua seguace Dispregia* ‘in herself Nature, and through her follower, he contemns’ → “he contemns Nature in herself and in her follower”: the verb moves before its object, and “Nature” precedes “in herself”; the second *per* is interpreted as “in” rather than ‘through’.
- *poichè in altro pon la spene* ‘since in something else he puts his hope’ → “placing elsewhere his hope”: the explicit causal clause becomes a participial phrase. ‘In something else’ becomes the adverb “elsewhere” and moves after the verb.

> 112 Ma seguimi oramai, che il gir mi piace:  

“But follow me now, as it pleases me to go;

- *che il gir mi piace* ‘for the going me pleases’ → “as it pleases me to go”: the causal connective becomes “as”; the substantival infinitive ‘the going’ becomes “to go”, postponed after an anticipatory “it”. The object pronoun moves after “pleases”.

> 113 Chè i Pesci guizzan su per l’ orizzonta,  

for the Fishes glide on the horizon,

- *Chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes the coordinating “for”.
- *guizzan* ‘dart’ → “glide”: the translation substitutes smooth movement for the Italian verb’s quick, darting movement.
- *su per l’ orizzonta* ‘up along the horizon’ → “on the horizon”: the fuller spatial expression becomes a simple indication of position.

> 114 E il Carro tutto sovra Coro giace,  

and all the Wain lies over Caurus,

- *il Carro tutto* ‘the Wain all’ → “all the Wain”: the expression of totality moves before the noun and its article.
- *sovra Coro giace* ‘over Caurus lies’ → “lies over Caurus”: the verb moves before the phrase indicating position.

> 115 E il balzo via là oltre si dismonta.  

and yonder far onwards we go down the cliff.”

- *il balzo via là oltre si dismonta* ‘the cliff, away there beyond, is descended’ → “yonder far onwards we go down the cliff”: the passive or impersonal *si* construction becomes an active first-person plural construction, “we go down”. The cliff moves from the beginning to the end, while the spatial expression moves to the front; its elements are reordered, and *oltre* is rendered directionally as “onwards” rather than ‘beyond’.

## Summary — Close Wording, Recast Syntax

Carlyle stays close to the Italian’s sequence of statements and often preserves its vocabulary, repetitions, and compressed phrasing. Expressions such as “spirits accurst”, “special trust”, and “Divine Intellect” closely follow their Italian counterparts, while “Thus the Master” retains the original’s elliptical construction. His most frequent departures concern word order: subjects move before verbs, objects follow them, and adjectives and possessives generally precede their nouns. Thus *Lo qual trasse Fotin* becomes “whom Photinus drew”, and *Figliuol mio* becomes “My Son”. More generally, subjects implicit in Italian verb endings are expressed as English pronouns, and Italian articles before possessives are omitted.

Changes of construction often go beyond rearrangement. Impersonal expressions become personal or passive ones: *Lo nostro scender convien esser tardo* becomes “Our descent we must delay”, while *Puossi far forza* becomes “Violence may be done”. Conversely, *ond’ ogni coscïenza è morsa* becomes the active “which gnaws every conscience”. Clauses are also compressed into other grammatical forms: *Qualunque trade* becomes “every traitor”, *in eterno* becomes “eternally”, and *Di che la fede spezïal si cria* becomes “giving birth to special trust”. These changes preserve the broad relation between ideas while altering how actions, agents, and qualities are expressed.

Word choice sometimes makes the Italian more explicit, sometimes broadens it, and sometimes replaces its imagery. *Costretti* becomes “pent up”, specifying confinement; *avello*, ‘tomb’, becomes the broader “monument”. The image in *fonde*, ‘melts’, gives way to “dissipates”, and *fidanza non imborsa*, ‘does not pocket trust’, becomes “repose no confidence”. Elsewhere the physical force of the wording is reduced: *martelli* becomes “strikes”, and *guizzan*, ‘dart’, becomes “glide”. By contrast, “blast” gives *fiato*, ‘breath’, greater force. Such choices make the prose less literal without consistently making it either stronger or milder.

Carlyle also supplies small clarifications and leaves some elements unexpressed. “Still” strengthens *più crudele*, “of these” specifies the reference of *la vista*, and “away” makes the loss implied in *Biscazza* explicit. “May” occasionally introduces possibility where the Italian has a present indicative. Among the omissions are *prima* and the reporting phrases *Disse* and *Diss’ io*. Larger adjustments occur where *dietro ad un coperchio*, ‘behind a lid’, becomes “under cover”, or where *poichè in altro pon la spene* becomes “placing elsewhere his hope”, dropping the explicit causal connective. Overall, the translation remains closely attached to the Italian wording, but repeatedly reshapes its syntax and idioms into English prose, with occasional interpretive choices that change emphasis or grammatical relationships.
