# Purgatorio 30

## Lines 1–15 — The Wain Stops and the Bride Is Called

> 1 Quando il settentrion del primo cielo,  
> 2 che nè occaso mai seppe nè orto,  
> 3 nè d' altra nebbia che di colpa velo,  

When the wain of the first heaven which setting nor rising never knew, nor veil of other mist than of sin,

- *il settentrion* ‘the seven northern stars’ → “the wain”: Okey uses the English figure of a wagon for the group of stars.
- *nè occaso mai seppe nè orto* ‘neither setting ever knew nor rising’ → “setting nor rising never knew”: the two objects are brought together before the verb; the first negative is absorbed into “never,” which renders *mai* under negation.
- *nè d' altra nebbia che di colpa velo* ‘nor of other mist than of fault a veil’ → “nor veil of other mist than of sin”: “veil” is moved before the phrases that qualify it, and “sin” gives *colpa* a specifically moral sense.

> 4 e che faceva lì ciascuno accorto  
> 5 di suo dover, come il più basso face  
> 6 qual timon gira per venire a porto,  

and which made there each one aware of his duty, even as the lower wain guides him who turns the helm to come into port,

- *come* ‘as’ → “even as”: “even” is supplied to emphasize the comparison.
- *il più basso* ‘the lower one’ → “the lower wain”: the noun understood in Italian is repeated in English.
- *face* ‘does’ → “guides”: where the Italian verb stands for the preceding action of making someone aware, the English names the action more specifically.
- *qual timon gira* ‘whoever the helm turns’ → “him who turns the helm”: the single relative *qual* is expanded into an object and a relative pronoun; the verb is placed before its object, and “the” is supplied.
- *a porto* ‘to port’ → “into port”: the preposition of destination becomes one of entry.

> 7 fermo si affisse, la gente verace,  
> 8 venuta prima tra il grifone ed esso,  
> 9 al carro volse sè, come a sua pace;  

had stopped still, the people of truth, who had first come between the grifon and it, turned them to the car as to their peace;

- *fermo si affisse* ‘motionless it fixed itself’ → “had stopped still”: the reflexive simple past becomes an intransitive past perfect; “still” follows the verb instead of preceding it.
- *la gente verace* ‘the truthful people’ → “the people of truth”: the adjective becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *venuta prima* ‘come first’ → “who had first come”: the participle agreeing with *gente* becomes a finite relative clause in the past perfect, with “first” before “come.”
- *al carro volse sè* ‘to the car it turned itself’ → “turned them to the car”: the destination is moved after the verb and its object; the singular collective is treated as plural, with “them” used reflexively.
- *sua pace* ‘its peace’ → “their peace”: the possessive likewise refers to the people as plural.

> 10 ed un di loro, quasi da ciel messo,  
> 11 “*Veni, sponsa, de Libano*” cantando  
> 12 gridò tre volte, e tutti gli altri appresso.  

and one of them as if sent from heaven “*Veni sponsa de Libano*” did shout thrice in song, and all the others after him.

- *quasi da ciel messo* ‘as if from heaven sent’ → “as if sent from heaven”: the participle is moved before the phrase of origin.
- *cantando gridò tre volte* ‘singing, shouted three times’ → “did shout thrice in song”: the gerund becomes a prepositional phrase and is moved after the shout and its count; the simple past is expressed with “did,” and the count by a single adverb.
- *appresso* ‘afterward’ → “after him”: the adverb is rendered as a preposition with an explicit reference to the first singer.

> 13 Quali i beati al novissimo bando  
> 14 surgeran presti ognun di sua caverna,  
> 15 la rivestita voce alleluiando,  

As the saints at the last trump shall rise ready each one from his tomb, with re-clad voice singing Halleluiah,

- *Quali* ‘such as’ → “As”: the inflected comparative adjective is rendered by a conjunction.
- *i beati* ‘the blessed’ → “the saints”: a noun replaces the substantivized adjective.
- *al novissimo bando* ‘at the last summons’ → “at the last trump”: the summons is named by the instrument that sounds it.
- *caverna* ‘cavern’ → “tomb”: Okey gives the contextual referent rather than the image.
- *la rivestita voce* ‘the re-clad voice’ → “with re-clad voice”: “with” makes the instrumental relation explicit, and the definite article drops out.
- *alleluiando* ‘hallelujahing’ → “singing Halleluiah”: a single gerund is expanded into a verb of singing and the word sung.

## Lines 16–48 — The Lady Appears and Ancient Love Returns

> 16 cotali, in su la divina basterna,  
> 17 si levar cento, *ad vocem tanti senis*,  
> 18 ministri e messaggier di vita eterna.  

such on the divine chariot rose up a hundred *ad vocem tanti senis*, ministers and messengers of life eternal.

- *in su la divina basterna* ‘up upon the divine litter’ → “on the divine chariot”: the compound preposition is reduced to “on”; the more specific *basterna*, a litter or covered carriage, becomes “chariot”.
- *si levar cento* ‘raised themselves a hundred’ → “rose up a hundred”: the reflexive verb becomes intransitive; the numeral still follows the verb.

> 19 Tutti dicean: “*Benedictus, qui venis*”;  

All were saying: “*Benedictus, qui venis*”;

- *dicean* ‘said’ → “were saying”: the English progressive makes the continuing action of the imperfect explicit.

> 20 e, fior gittando di sopra e dintorno,  
> 21 “*Manibus o date lilia plenis.*”  

and, strewing flowers above and around, “*Manibus o date lilia plenis.*”

- *fior gittando* ‘flowers casting’ → “strewing flowers”: the object follows the gerund, and “strewing” specifies a scattering rather than simply a throwing.

> 22 Io vidi già nel cominciar del giorno  
> 23 la parte oriental tutta rosata  
> 24 e l' altro ciel di bel sereno adorno,  

Ere now have I seen, at dawn of day, the eastern part all rosy red, and the rest of heaven adorned with fair clear sky,

- *Io vidi già* ‘I saw once before’ → “Ere now have I seen”: the time expression comes first, and the simple past becomes a present perfect with the auxiliary before the subject.
- *nel cominciar del giorno* ‘at the beginning of the day’ → “at dawn of day”: the substantivized infinitive is rendered by the specific time noun “dawn”, and “day” has no article.
- *la parte oriental* ‘the part eastern’ → “the eastern part”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *tutta rosata* ‘all rosy’ → “all rosy red”: “red” is supplied.
- *l' altro ciel* ‘the other sky’ → “the rest of heaven”: the adjective distinguishing the remainder becomes a noun with a partitive complement.
- *di bel sereno adorno* ‘with fair clearness adorned’ → “adorned with fair clear sky”: the participle is moved before its complement; the substantivized *sereno* is expanded to an adjective and noun.

> 25 e la faccia del sol nascere ombrata,  
> 26 sì che per temperanza di vapori,  
> 27 l' occhio la sostenea lunga fiata:  

and the face of the sun rise shadowed, so that by the tempering of the mists the eye long time endured him:

- *nascere* ‘be born’ → “rise”: the English uses the ordinary verb for the sun’s appearance.
- *per temperanza di vapori* ‘through moderation by vapours’ → “by the tempering of the mists”: “tempering” expresses the action rather than the abstract quality, “mists” renders *vapori*, and both English nouns take the definite article.
- *la sostenea* ‘it sustained’ → “endured him”: the object follows the verb; the feminine pronoun referring to *faccia* is rendered by a masculine pronoun referring to the sun.
- *lunga fiata* ‘a long while’ → “long time”: the duration is expressed without an article and is moved before the verb.

> 28 così dentro una nuvola di fiori,  
> 29 che dalle mani angeliche saliva  
> 30 e ricadeva in giù dentro e di fuori  

so within a cloud of flowers, which rose from the angelic hands and fell down again within and without,

- *dalle mani angeliche saliva* ‘from the hands angelic rose’ → “rose from the angelic hands”: the verb precedes the prepositional phrase, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *ricadeva in giù* ‘fell again downwards’ → “fell down again”: the prefix of the verb is rendered by a separate adverb placed after “down”.

> 31 sopra candido vel cinta d' oliva  
> 32 donna m' apparve, sotto verde manto,  
> 33 vestita di color di fiamma viva.  

olive-crowned over a white veil, a lady appeared to me, clad, under a green mantle, with hue of living flame.

- *sopra candido vel* ‘over white veil’ → “over a white veil”: the indefinite article is supplied, and the phrase follows rather than precedes the description of the crowning.
- *cinta d' oliva* ‘girded with olive’ → “olive-crowned”: “crowned” specifies where the olive is worn, and the prepositional complement becomes the first element of a compound.
- *donna m' apparve* ‘lady to me appeared’ → “a lady appeared to me”: the indefinite article is supplied, and the indirect object follows the verb.
- *sotto verde manto* ‘under green mantle’ → “under a green mantle”: the indefinite article is supplied, and the phrase is placed after “clad” rather than before it.
- *vestita di color di fiamma viva* ‘dressed in colour of flame living’ → “clad, under a green mantle, with hue of living flame”: “with hue” replaces ‘in colour’, the participle is separated from its complement, and “living” precedes its noun.

> 34 E lo spirito mio, che già cotanto  
> 35 tempo era stato che alla sua presenza  
> 36 non era di stupor, tremando, affranto,  

And my spirit, that now so long a time had passed, since, trembling in her presence, it had been broken down with awe,

- *che già cotanto tempo era stato che* ‘which already so much time it had been that’ → “that now so long a time had passed, since”: the impersonal expression of elapsed time becomes a clause with “so long a time” as subject; *già* becomes “now”, and the second *che* becomes the temporal conjunction “since”.
- *alla sua presenza* ‘at her presence’ → “in her presence”: the preposition changes, and the phrase is placed after “trembling”.
- *non era di stupor, tremando, affranto* ‘was not, by amazement, trembling, overcome’ → “trembling in her presence, it had been broken down with awe”: the Italian speaks of the interval during which the spirit had **not** been overcome; after “since”, English instead names the earlier occasion affirmatively and uses the past perfect. The participle “trembling” moves forward, and “with awe” follows “broken down”.

> 37 senza degli occhi aver più conoscenza,  
> 38 per occulta virtù che da lei mosse,  
> 39 d' antico amor sentì la gran potenza.  

without having further knowledge by mine eyes through hidden virtue which went out from her, felt the mighty power of ancient love.

- *degli occhi aver più conoscenza* ‘from the eyes to have further knowledge’ → “having further knowledge by mine eyes”: after “without” the infinitive becomes a gerund; the phrase expressing the source of knowledge moves to the end, takes instrumental “by”, and has “mine” in place of the definite article.
- *per occulta virtù* ‘by hidden power’ → “through hidden virtue”: “virtue” is used in the sense of an operative power.
- *che da lei mosse* ‘which from her moved’ → “which went out from her”: the verb precedes the phrase of origin, and “out” makes the movement away explicit.
- *d' antico amor sentì la gran potenza* ‘of ancient love felt the great power’ → “felt the mighty power of ancient love”: the possessive complement moves after “power”, and *gran* becomes “mighty”.

> 40 Tosto che nella vista mi percosse  
> 41 l' alta virtù, che già m' avea trafitto  
> 42 prima ch' io fuor di puerizia fosse,  

Soon as on my sight the lofty virtue smote, which already had pierced me ere I was out of my boyhood,

- *nella vista mi percosse l' alta virtù* ‘in the sight me struck the lofty power’ → “on my sight the lofty virtue smote”: the person struck is expressed by a possessive modifying “sight”, “on” replaces ‘in’, and the subject precedes the verb. “Virtue” again renders *virtù* in the sense of power.
- *già m' avea trafitto* ‘already me had pierced’ → “already had pierced me”: the object follows the participle.
- *prima ch' io fuor di puerizia fosse* ‘before I out of childhood were’ → “ere I was out of my boyhood”: the Italian subjunctive after the temporal conjunction becomes an indicative before its complement; “boyhood” is more specific than *puerizia*, and “my” is supplied.

> 43 volsimi alla sinistra col rispitto  
> 44 col quale il fantolin corre alla mamma,  
> 45 quando ha paura o quando egli è afflitto,  

I turned me to the left with the trust with which the little child runs to his mother when he is frightened or when he is afflicted,

- *alla sinistra* ‘to the left side’ → “to the left”: English uses “left” alone where the feminine Italian adjective implies a noun such as ‘hand’ or ‘side’.
- *col rispitto* ‘with the regard’ → “with the trust”: the rendering takes *rispitto* in the sense of reliance rather than simply regard.
- *il fantolin* ‘the little one’ → “the little child”: the diminutive is expressed by a separate adjective.
- *alla mamma* ‘to the mamma’ → “to his mother”: the definite article becomes a possessive, and the familiar *mamma* becomes “mother”.
- *ha paura* ‘has fear’ → “is frightened”: the verb and noun become a form of ‘be’ with a participial adjective.

> 46 per dicere a Virgilio: “Men che dramma  
> 47 di sangue m' è rimaso, che non tremi;  
> 48 conosco i segni dell' antica fiamma.”  

to say to Virgil: “Less than a drachm of blood is left in me that trembleth not; I recognise the tokens of the ancient flame.”

- *per dicere* ‘in order to say’ → “to say”: the infinitive alone expresses purpose.
- *men che dramma* ‘less than drachm’ → “Less than a drachm”: the indefinite article is supplied.
- *m' è rimaso* ‘to me has remained’ → “is left in me”: the perfect of ‘remain’ becomes a description of what is left, and the preverbal dative becomes a phrase of location after the predicate.
- *che non tremi* ‘that may not tremble’ → “that trembleth not”: the subjunctive in the relative clause becomes an indicative, with the negative after the verb.
- *conosco i segni* ‘I know the signs’ → “I recognise the tokens”: “recognise” specifies knowing again, and “tokens” is chosen for ‘signs’.

## Lines 49–81 — Virgil’s Departure and Beatrice’s Rebuke

> 49 Ma Virgilio n' avea lasciati scemi  
> 50 di sè, Virgilio dolcissimo patre,  
> 51 Virgilio a cui per mia salute die' mi:  

But Virgil had left us bereft of himself, Virgil sweetest Father, Virgil to whom for my weal I gave me up;

- *n’ avea lasciati* ‘us had left’ → “had left us”: the object pronoun follows the verb; the participle no longer marks agreement with the plural object.
- *die’ mi* ‘I gave myself’ → “I gave me up”: the reflexive is expressed by “me”, and “up” is supplied to convey surrender.

> 52 nè quantunque perdè l' antica matre  
> 53 valse alle guance nette di rugiada,  
> 54 che lagrimando non tornassero atre.  

nor did all that our ancient mother lost, avail to keep my dew-washed cheeks from turning dark again with tears.

- *perdè l’ antica matre* ‘lost the ancient mother’ → “our ancient mother lost”: the subject precedes the verb, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *valse* ‘availed’ → “did” … “avail”: English distributes the past tense between an auxiliary, inverted after “nor”, and an infinitive after the subject.
- *alle guance nette di rugiada* ‘for the cheeks clean of dew’ → “my dew-washed cheeks”: the beneficiary becomes the object of “keep”; the article becomes a possessive, and the description following the noun becomes a compound before it, taking the dew as what washed the cheeks.
- *che lagrimando non tornassero atre* ‘that, weeping, they should not turn dark again’ → “to keep” … “from turning dark again with tears”: the negative subjunctive clause becomes an infinitive of prevention with “from” and a gerund; the Italian gerund of accompanying action becomes a prepositional phrase at the end.

> 55 “Dante, perchè Virgilio se ne vada,  
> 56 non pianger anco, non pianger ancora:  
> 57 chè pianger ti convien per altra spada.”  

“Dante, for that Virgil goeth away, weep not yet, weep not yet, for thou must weep for other sword.”

- *se ne vada* ‘he should go away’ → “goeth away”: the subjunctive becomes indicative; the pronouns of the Italian verb of departure have no separate English equivalents.
- *non pianger anco, non pianger ancora* ‘not to weep yet, not to weep yet’ → “weep not yet, weep not yet”: the Italian infinitives used for negative commands become imperatives with the negative following; the two forms of the adverb become the same “yet”.
- *chè pianger ti convien* ‘because to weep is needful for thee’ → “for thou must weep”: the subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating “for”; the person affected becomes the subject of a modal verb, followed rather than preceded by the infinitive.

> 58 Quasi ammiraglio, che in poppa ed in prora  
> 59 viene a veder la gente che ministra  
> 60 per gli altri legni, ed a ben far la incuora,  

Even as an admiral, who at stern and at bow, comes to see the folk that man the other ships, and heartens them to brave deeds,

- *Quasi ammiraglio* ‘as admiral’ → “Even as an admiral”: “Even” reinforces the comparison, and the indefinite article is supplied.
- *ministra per gli altri legni* ‘serves aboard the other vessels’ → “man the other ships”: service aboard the vessels becomes a transitive verb with the ships as object; the singular verb agreeing with the collective noun becomes plural. The Italian names the ships by their material, literally ‘timbers’.
- *a ben far* ‘to do well’ → “to brave deeds”: an infinitive with an adverb becomes a plural noun with an adjective specifying bravery; the phrase moves after the verb of encouragement.
- *la incuora* ‘it he heartens’ → “heartens them”: the preceding singular object pronoun referring to the collective becomes a following plural pronoun.

> 61 in su la sponda del carro sinistra,  
> 62 quando mi volsi al suon del nome mio,  
> 63 che di necessità qui si regista,  

so on the left side of the car, when I turned me at sound of my name, which of necessity here is recorded,

- *in su la sponda del carro sinistra* ‘upon the side of the car, the left’ → “so on the left side of the car”: “so” is supplied to answer the comparison; the compound preposition becomes “on”, and the delayed adjective moves before “side”.
- *mi volsi* ‘myself I turned’ → “I turned me”: the reflexive follows the verb and takes the form “me”.
- *al suon del nome mio* ‘at the sound of the name of mine’ → “at sound of my name”: “sound” loses its article, and the possessive moves before “name”.
- *si regista* ‘registers itself’ → “is recorded”: the Italian passive construction with *si* becomes an English passive with an auxiliary.

> 64 vidi la donna, che pria m' apparìo  
> 65 velata sotto l' angelica festa,  
> 66 drizzar gli occhi ver me di qua dal rio.  

I saw the lady, who first appeared to me veiled beneath the angelic festival, directing her eyes to me on this side the stream.

- *pria m’ apparìo* ‘earlier to me appeared’ → “first appeared to me”: anteriority is expressed as priority, and the indirect object moves after the verb.
- *l’ angelica festa* ‘the angelic celebration’ → “the angelic festival”: “festival” gives the rejoicing the sense of a festive occasion.
- *drizzar gli occhi ver me* ‘to direct the eyes toward me’ → “directing her eyes to me”: the infinitive after a verb of perception becomes a participle; the article becomes a possessive, and “to” renders the direction.
- *di qua dal rio* ‘on this side of the stream’ → “on this side the stream”: the relation to the stream is expressed without a separate ‘of’.

> 67 Tutto che il vel che le scendea di testa,  
> 68 cerchiato dalla fronde di Minerva,  
> 69 non la lasciasse parer manifesta,  

Albeit the veil which fell from her head, crowned with Minerva’s leaves, did not let her appear manifest,

- *scendea di testa* ‘was descending from the head’ → “fell from her head”: the imperfect of descent becomes a simple past of falling, and the possessor of the head is specified.
- *cerchiato dalla fronde di Minerva* ‘encircled by the foliage of Minerva’ → “crowned with Minerva’s leaves”: encircling becomes crowning, “with” replaces the phrase with ‘by’, the collective foliage becomes plural leaves, and the possessor moves before the noun.
- *non la lasciasse parer* ‘might not her let appear’ → “did not let her appear”: the concessive subjunctive becomes an indicative with a negative auxiliary, and the object moves after “let”.

> 70 regalmente nell' atto ancor proterva  
> 71 continuò, come colui che dice  
> 72 e il più caldo parlar di retro serva:  

queenlike, in bearing yet stern, she continued, like one who speaks and holdeth back the hottest words till the last:

- *regalmente* ‘regally’ → “queenlike”: an adverb of royal manner becomes a comparison specifically with a queen.
- *nell’ atto ancor proterva* ‘in her demeanour still imperious’ → “in bearing yet stern”: the article drops out, and “stern” expresses severity rather than imperiousness.
- *come colui che dice* ‘like him who says’ → “like one who speaks”: the demonstrative becomes indefinite, and saying becomes speaking.
- *il più caldo parlar di retro serva* ‘the hottest speech for afterward reserves’ → “holdeth back the hottest words till the last”: the verb moves before its object; the nominal infinitive becomes plural “words”, and the temporal phrase specifies the very end.

> 73 “Guardami ben: ben son, ben son Beatrice.  

“Look at me well; verily am I, verily am I Beatrice.

- *Guardami ben* ‘regard me well’ → “Look at me well”: the direct object of the Italian verb becomes the object of “at”.
- *ben son, ben son* ‘indeed I am, indeed I am’ → “verily am I, verily am I”: the emphatic adverb is rendered by “verily”, with subject–verb inversion in both affirmations.

> 74 Come degnasti d' accedere al monte?  

How didst thou deign to draw nigh the mount?

- *Come degnasti* ‘how deignedst thou’ → “How didst thou deign”: the past-tense question is formed with an auxiliary.
- *d’ accedere al monte* ‘to approach the mount’ → “to draw nigh the mount”: a verb of approach with a prepositional complement becomes the phrase “draw nigh” without a preposition before “the mount”.

> 75 non sapei tu che qui è l' uom felice?”  

knewest thou not that here man is happy?”

- *non sapei tu* ‘not knewest thou’ → “knewest thou not”: the negative moves after the inverted verb and subject.
- *è l’ uom felice* ‘is the man happy’ → “man is happy”: the subject moves before the verb in the subordinate clause, and generic “man” has no article.

> 76 Gli occhi mi cadder giù nel chiaro fonte;  

Mine eyes drooped down to the clear fount;

- *Gli occhi mi cadder giù* ‘the eyes fell down for me’ → “Mine eyes drooped down”: the dative marking the person affected becomes a possessive before the noun, and falling becomes drooping.
- *nel chiaro fonte* ‘into the clear fount’ → “to the clear fount”: the preposition marks direction to rather than into.

> 77 ma, veggendomi in esso, i trassi all' erba,  
> 78 tanta vergogna mi gravò la fronte.  

but beholding me therein, I drew them back to the grass, so great a shame weighed down my brow.

- *veggendomi in esso* ‘seeing myself in it’ → “beholding me therein”: seeing becomes beholding, the reflexive is expressed by “me”, and the preposition and pronoun become one adverb.
- *i trassi all’ erba* ‘them I drew to the grass’ → “I drew them back to the grass”: the object pronoun follows the verb, and “back” is supplied.
- *tanta vergogna* ‘so much shame’ → “so great a shame”: quantity becomes degree, with an indefinite article.
- *mi gravò la fronte* ‘weighed upon the brow for me’ → “weighed down my brow”: “down” expresses the burden, and the dative becomes a possessive before “brow”.

> 79 Così la madre al figlio par superba,  
> 80 com' ella parve a me: perchè d' amaro  
> 81 sente 'l sapor della pietade acerba.  

So doth the mother seem stern to her child, as she seemed to me; for the savour of harsh pity tasteth of bitterness.

- *Così la madre al figlio par superba* ‘so the mother to the son seems proud’ → “So doth the mother seem stern to her child”: the auxiliary is inverted after “So”; the indirect complement moves to the end, ‘the son’ becomes “her child”, and apparent pride becomes sternness.
- *perchè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *d’ amaro sente ’l sapor della pietade acerba* ‘of something bitter tastes the savour of the pity harsh’ → “the savour of harsh pity tasteth of bitterness”: the subject moves before the verb, and the complement follows it. The adjective used substantivally becomes the abstract noun “bitterness”; “harsh” moves before “pity”, which loses its article, and selects the figurative sense of an adjective that can also describe a tart taste.

## Lines 82–114 — The Angels’ Song and Beatrice’s Reply

> 82 Ella si tacque, e gli angeli cantaro  
> 83 di subito: “*In te, Domine, speravi*”;  
> 84 ma oltre “*pedes meos*” non passaro.  

She was silent, and straightway the angels sang: “*In te, Domine, speravi*;” but beyond “*pedes meos*” they passed not.

- *si tacque* ‘fell silent’ → “was silent”: the reflexive verb marking the onset of silence becomes a verb of state.
- *gli angeli cantaro di subito* ‘the angels sang straightway’ → “straightway the angels sang”: the adverbial phrase becomes one adverb and moves before the subject.
- *non passaro* ‘not passed’ → “they passed not”: the negative follows the verb.

> 85 Sì come neve tra le vive travi  
> 86 per lo dosso d' Italia si congela,  
> 87 soffiata e stretta dagli venti schiavi,  

As the snow amid the living rafters along Italia’s back is frozen under blast and stress of Slavonian winds,

- *Sì come neve* ‘just as snow’ → “As the snow”: the reinforcement of the comparison is dropped and the definite article supplied.
- *tra le vive travi* ‘between the living beams’ → “amid the living rafters”: the preposition expresses surrounding rather than intervening position, and the beams are specifically rafters.
- *per lo dosso d' Italia* ‘along the back of Italia’ → “along Italia’s back”: the possessive precedes the noun.
- *si congela* ‘freezes’ → “is frozen”: the intransitive sense of the reflexive construction is rendered by a passive.
- *soffiata e stretta dagli venti schiavi* ‘blown and pressed by the winds Slavonian’ → “under blast and stress of Slavonian winds”: the two participles become nouns governed by “under”; the agent becomes an unarticled complement of “of”, and its adjective moves before the noun.

> 88 poi liquefatta in sè stessa trapela,  
> 89 pur che la terra, che perde ombra, spiri,  
> 90 sì che par foco fonder la candela:  

then melted trickles down through itself, if but the land that loseth shade do breathe, so that it seems fire melting the candle,

- *in sè stessa trapela* ‘through itself it trickles’ → “trickles down through itself”: the verb comes first and the direction “down” is supplied.
- *pur che* ‘provided that’ → “if but”: the condition is expressed by “if” qualified by “but” in the sense of ‘only’.
- *spiri* ‘breathe’ → “do breathe”: the subjunctive is expressed with an auxiliary.
- *par foco fonder la candela* ‘it seems fire to melt the candle’ → “it seems fire melting the candle”: the infinitive becomes a present participle.

> 91 così fui senza lagrime e sospiri  
> 92 anzi il cantar di quei che notan sempre  
> 93 retro alle note degli eterni giri.  

so without tears or sighs was I before the song of those who ever accord their notes after the melodies of the eternal spheres.

- *fui senza lagrime e sospiri* ‘I was without tears and sighs’ → “without tears or sighs was I”: the complement precedes the verb and subject, and “or” replaces the conjunction under the negative force of “without”.
- *il cantar* ‘the singing’ → “the song”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun for what is sung.
- *notan sempre* ‘sing notes always’ → “ever accord their notes”: the adverb moves before the verb; the notion of notes is made a separate object with a possessive, and the verb now expresses agreement.
- *alle note* ‘the notes’ (after *retro*) → “the melodies”: the individual notes become melodies; English “after” requires no further preposition.
- *degli eterni giri* ‘of the eternal circles’ → “of the eternal spheres”: the circles or revolutions are rendered as spheres.

> 94 Ma poi che intesi nelle dolci tempre  
> 95 lor compatire a me, più che se detto  
> 96 avesser: “Donna, perchè sì lo stempre?”  

But when I heard in their sweet harmonies their compassion on me, more than if they had said “Lady, why dost thou so shame him?”

- *poi che* ‘after that’ → “when”: the temporal conjunction does not explicitly mark succession.
- *nelle dolci tempre* ‘in the sweet harmonies’ → “in their sweet harmonies”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *lor compatire a me* ‘their feeling pity for me’ → “their compassion on me”: the infinitive becomes an abstract noun with a prepositional complement.
- *se detto avesser* ‘if said they had’ → “if they had said”: the auxiliary precedes the participle.
- *perchè sì lo stempre* ‘why so dost thou melt him’ → “why dost thou so shame him”: “so” follows the subject, the object follows the verb, and shaming is substituted for the figurative melting or unmanning.

> 97 lo gel che m' era intorno al cor ristretto,  
> 98 spirito ed acqua fessi, e con angoscia  
> 99 per la bocca e per gli occhi uscì del petto.  

the ice which had closed about my heart became breath and water, and with anguish through mouth and eyes issued from my breast.

- *che m' era intorno al cor ristretto* ‘which had about the heart drawn tight on me’ → “which had closed about my heart”: the participle is brought up to the auxiliary; the personal dative becomes a possessive modifying “heart”.
- *spirito ed acqua fessi* ‘breath and water it made itself’ → “became breath and water”: the reflexive is rendered by a verb of becoming, placed before its complement.
- *per la bocca e per gli occhi* ‘through the mouth and through the eyes’ → “through mouth and eyes”: both articles and the repeated preposition disappear.
- *del petto* ‘from the breast’ → “from my breast”: the possessive replaces the article.

> 100 Ella, pur ferma in su la detta coscia  
> 101 del carro stando, alle sustanzie pie  
> 102 volse le sue parole così poscia:  

She, standing yet fixed on the said side of the car, then turned her words to the pitying angels thus:

- *pur ferma in su la detta coscia del carro stando* ‘still fixed upon the said thigh of the car standing’ → “standing yet fixed on the said side of the car”: the participle moves to the front, the compound preposition becomes “on”, and “side” replaces the anatomical figure.
- *alle sustanzie pie* ‘to the substances compassionate’ → “to the pitying angels”: the beings are named rather than called substances; a participial adjective precedes the noun, and the whole phrase follows the verb and its object.
- *così poscia* ‘thus afterwards’ → “then” … “thus”: the temporal adverb moves before the verb, leaving the adverb of manner at the end.

> 103 “Voi vigilate nell' eterno dìe,  
> 104 sì che notte nè sonno a voi non fura  
> 105 passo che faccia il secol per sue vie:  

“Ye watch in the everlasting day, so that nor night nor sleep stealeth from you one step which the world may take along its ways;

- *notte nè sonno* ‘night nor sleep’ → “nor night nor sleep”: a first “nor” is supplied.
- *a voi non fura passo* ‘from you steals not a step’ → “stealeth from you one step”: the verb precedes the personal complement; the negative is carried by the preceding “nor … nor”, and bare *passo* receives the numeral “one”.
- *che faccia il secol* ‘that might make the world’ (with ‘the world’ as subject) → “which the world may take”: the subject precedes its verb, the subjunctive is expressed with “may”, and English takes rather than makes a step.

> 106 onde la mia risposta è con più cura  
> 107 che m' intenda colui che di là piagne,  
> 108 per che sia colpa e duol d' una misura.  

wherefore my answer is with greater care, that he who yon side doth weep may understand me, so that sin and sorrow be of one measure.

- *più cura* ‘more care’ → “greater care”: a comparative of degree replaces one of quantity.
- *che m' intenda colui che di là piagne* ‘that me understand he who on the other side weeps’ → “that he who yon side doth weep may understand me”: the subject and its relative clause precede the purpose verb, whose subjunctive becomes “may understand”; the object moves after it. “Yon side” has no preposition, and “doth” is supplied.
- *per che sia colpa e duol d' una misura* ‘in order that be fault and grief of one measure’ → “so that sin and sorrow be of one measure”: the subjects precede the subjunctive; fault is rendered specifically as sin, and grief as sorrow.

> 109 Non pur per opra delle rote magne,  
> 110 che drizzan ciascun seme ad alcun fine,  
> 111 secondo che le stelle son compagne;  

Not only by operation of the mighty spheres that direct each seed to some end, according as the stars are its companions,

- *delle rote magne* ‘of the wheels great’ → “of the mighty spheres”: wheels become spheres, and the adjective moves before the noun and stresses power.
- *le stelle son compagne* ‘the stars are companions’ → “the stars are its companions”: the possessive makes explicit what the stars accompany.

> 112 ma per larghezza di grazie divine,  
> 113 che sì alti vapori hanno a lor piova  
> 114 che nostre viste là non van vicine,  

but by the bounty of graces divine, which have for their rain vapours so high that our eyes reach not nigh them,

- *per larghezza* ‘by largesse’ → “by the bounty”: the abstract noun receives an article.
- *sì alti vapori hanno a lor piova* ‘such high vapours have for their rain’ → “have for their rain vapours so high”: the object moves after the verb and the phrase “for their rain”; the adjective and its qualifier follow “vapours”.
- *nostre viste là non van vicine* ‘our powers of sight there go not near’ → “our eyes reach not nigh them”: the faculty becomes the organ, going becomes reaching, and the place adverb is replaced by a pronoun after “nigh”; the negative follows the verb.

## Lines 115–145 — Beatrice Recounts His Straying

> 115 questi fu tal nella sua vita nuova  
> 116 virtualmente, ch' ogni abito destro  
> 117 fatto averebbe in lui mirabil prova.  

this man was such in his new life potentially, that every good talent would have made wondrous increase in him.

- *questi* ‘this one’ → “this man”: the demonstrative pronoun is expanded with a noun.
- *nella sua vita nuova* ‘in his life new’ → “in his new life”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *ogni abito destro* ‘every disposition apt’ → “every good talent”: a disposition becomes a talent; its adjective changes from ‘apt’ to “good” and precedes the noun.
- *fatto averebbe in lui mirabil prova* ‘made would have in him wondrous proof’ → “would have made wondrous increase in him”: the auxiliary precedes the participle, and the phrase ‘in him’ moves to the end. Where the Italian speaks of what would have given wondrous proof of itself, Okey speaks of growth.

> 118 Ma tanto più maligno e più silvestro  
> 119 si fa il terren col mal seme e non colto,  
> 120 quant' egli ha più del buon vigor terrestro.  

But so much the more rank and wild the ground becomes with evil seed and untilled, the more it hath of good strength of soil.

- *tanto più maligno e più silvestro* ‘so much more noxious and more wild’ → “so much the more rank and wild”: “rank” gives the first adjective an agricultural sense; English inserts “the” in the comparative and lets one “more” govern both adjectives.
- *si fa il terren* ‘becomes the ground’ → “the ground becomes”: the subject comes before the verb.
- *col mal seme e non colto* ‘with the evil seed and not tilled’ → “with evil seed and untilled”: the article disappears, and the separate negative becomes a prefix.
- *quant' egli ha più* ‘in proportion as it has more’ → “the more it hath”: the English correlative puts “the more” before the subject and verb.
- *del buon vigor terrestro* ‘of the good strength terrestrial’ → “of good strength of soil”: the article disappears, and a phrase naming the soil replaces the adjective.

> 121 Alcun tempo il sostenni col mio volto;  

Some time I sustained him with my countenance;

- *il sostenni* ‘him I sustained’ → “I sustained him”: the object follows the verb.

> 122 mostrando gli occhi giovinetti a lui,  
> 123 meco il menava in dritta parte volto.  

showing my youthful eyes to him I led him with me turned to the right goal.

- *gli occhi giovinetti* ‘the eyes youthful’ → “my youthful eyes”: a possessive replaces the article, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *meco il menava* ‘with me him I was leading’ → “I led him with me”: both complements move after the verb, in reverse order; the simple past leaves the continuing action of the imperfect unmarked.
- *in dritta parte volto* ‘in the right direction turned’ → “turned to the right goal”: the participle comes first, and direction is expressed in terms of a goal.

> 124 Sì tosto come in su la soglia fui  
> 125 di mia seconda etade, e mutai vita,  
> 126 questi si tolse a me, e diessi altrui.  

So soon as I was on the threshold of my second age, and I changed life, he forsook me, and gave him to others.

- *in su la soglia fui di mia seconda etade* ‘on the threshold I was of my second age’ → “I was on the threshold of my second age”: the verb moves before the prepositional phrase, bringing ‘of my second age’ next to the noun it qualifies.
- *questi si tolse a me* ‘this one withdrew himself from me’ → “he forsook me”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative, and the reflexive verb with a prepositional complement becomes a transitive verb.
- *diessi altrui* ‘gave himself to another’ → “gave him to others”: “him” has reflexive force without the reflexive form; the English chooses a plural for the number-indefinite *altrui*.

> 127 Quando di carne a spirto era salita,  
> 128 e bellezza e virtù cresciuta m' era,  
> 129 fu' io a lui men cara e men gradita;  

When I was risen from flesh to spirit, and beauty and virtue were increased within me, I was less precious and less pleasing to him;

- *di carne a spirto era salita* ‘from flesh to spirit I was risen’ → “I was risen from flesh to spirit”: the verb precedes the phrases of departure and arrival.
- *cresciuta m' era* ‘increased to me was’ → “were increased within me”: the auxiliary comes first and is plural with the two English subjects, where the Italian has a singular auxiliary and participle; the dative becomes a phrase of place.
- *fu' io a lui men cara e men gradita* ‘was I to him less dear and less pleasing’ → “I was less precious and less pleasing to him”: subject and verb change places, “precious” renders ‘dear’, and the complement shared by the adjectives moves to the end.

> 130 e volse i passi suoi per via non vera,  
> 131 imagini di ben seguendo false,  
> 132 che nulla promission rendono intera.  

and he did turn his steps by a way not true, pursuing false visions of good, that pay back no promise entire.

- *volse* ‘turned’ → “did turn”: auxiliary and infinitive replace the simple past.
- *i passi suoi* ‘the steps his’ → “his steps”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *per via non vera* ‘by way not true’ → “by a way not true”: English supplies an indefinite article.
- *imagini di ben seguendo false* ‘images of good following false’ → “pursuing false visions of good”: the participle moves before its object and becomes “pursuing”; “visions” renders ‘images’, with the adjective brought forward from its separated position.
- *nulla promission rendono intera* ‘no promise they render whole’ → “pay back no promise entire”: the verb moves before the object, and “pay back” gives it the sense of making good on a promise.

> 133 Nè impetrare spirazion mi valse,  
> 134 con le quali ed in sogno ed altrimenti  
> 135 lo rivocai; sì poco a lui ne calse.  

Nor did it avail me to gain inspirations, with which in dream and otherwise, I called him back; so little recked he of them.

- *Nè impetrare spirazion mi valse* ‘nor to obtain by entreaty inspirations availed me’ → “Nor did it avail me to gain inspirations”: English anticipates the infinitive subject with “it”, postpones the infinitive phrase, and uses “did” after “Nor”; “gain” does not express entreaty.
- *ed in sogno ed altrimenti* ‘both in dream and otherwise’ → “in dream and otherwise”: the first member of the paired conjunction is unmarked.
- *lo rivocai* ‘him I called back’ → “I called him back”: the object moves after the verb, between “called” and “back”.
- *sì poco a lui ne calse* ‘so little to him of them it mattered’ → “so little recked he of them”: the impersonal construction becomes personal, with the person concerned as subject; the pronominal complement moves to the end.

> 136 Tanto giù cadde, che tutti argomenti  
> 137 alla salute sua eran già corti,  
> 138 fuor che mostrargli le perdute genti.  

So low sank he, that all means for his salvation were already short, save showing him the lost people.

- *Tanto giù cadde* ‘so far down he fell’ → “So low sank he”: degree and downward direction are combined in “So low”, and falling becomes sinking.
- *tutti argomenti* ‘all expedients’ → “all means”: the English uses a more general noun.
- *alla salute sua* ‘to the salvation of his’ → “for his salvation”: the preposition expresses purpose, and the possessive precedes the noun.
- *fuor che mostrargli* ‘except to show him’ → “save showing him”: the infinitive becomes a gerund after “save”.
- *le perdute genti* ‘the lost peoples’ → “the lost people”: a collective replaces the Italian plural.

> 139 Per questo visitai l' uscio dei morti,  
> 140 ed a colui che l' ha quassù condotto  
> 141 li preghi miei, piangendo, furon porti.  

For this I visited the portal of the dead, and to him who has guided him up hither, weeping my prayers were borne.

- *l' uscio* ‘the door’ → “the portal”: a more formal noun is chosen.
- *l' ha quassù condotto* ‘him has up here led’ → “has guided him up hither”: the object and adverb move after the participle; “guided” renders ‘led’, and “up hither” gives the adverb an explicitly directional sense.
- *li preghi miei, piangendo, furon porti* ‘the prayers mine, weeping, were proffered’ → “weeping my prayers were borne”: the gerund moves before the subject, the possessive precedes the noun, and conveying replaces proffering.

> 142 Alto fato di Dio sarebbe rotto,  
> 143 se Lete si passasse, e tal vivanda  
> 144 fosse gustata senza alcuno scotto  
> 145 di pentimento che lagrime spanda.”  

God’s high decree would be broken, if Lethe were passed, and such viands were tasted, without some scot of penitence that may shed tears.”

- *Alto fato di Dio* ‘high decree of God’ → “God’s high decree”: the possessive construction replaces the following prepositional phrase.
- *Lete si passasse* ‘one should cross Lethe’ → “Lethe were passed”: English uses an explicit passive for the Italian *si* construction.
- *tal vivanda fosse gustata* ‘such fare were tasted’ → “such viands were tasted”: the singular noun becomes plural.
- *senza alcuno scotto* ‘without any payment’ → “without some scot”: “some” replaces ‘any’, and the older “scot” renders a payment or reckoning.
- *che lagrime spanda* ‘that tears it shed’ → “that may shed tears”: the subjunctive is expressed with “may”, and the object follows the verb.

## Summary — Close in Image, Freer in Syntax

Okey often keeps Dante’s imagery while rearranging the words that carry it. In *d' antico amor sentì la gran potenza*, the phrase ‘of ancient love’ comes first; in “felt the mighty power of ancient love” it follows the noun it qualifies. In *imagini di ben seguendo false*, the verb separates ‘images’ from ‘false’; “pursuing false visions of good” brings the verb forward and reunites adjective and noun. Yet Okey also retains marked orders such as “rose up a hundred”, as well as the threefold repetition of Virgil’s name and the Latin phrases.

His choice of words sometimes gives the referent rather than the figure: *caverna*, ‘cavern’, becomes “tomb”, and *legni*, literally ‘timbers’, becomes “ships”. Elsewhere he preserves an unusual image almost word for word, as in “the living rafters” for *le vive travi*. He supplies “wain” where *il più basso* says only ‘the lower one’, and adds “red” to *tutta rosata* in “all rosy red”. Conversely, *per la bocca e per gli occhi* becomes “through mouth and eyes”, without the articles or the second preposition. Two more general adjustments recur throughout: English supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending suffices, and omits the article before a possessive, as in *la mia risposta*, “my answer”.

Changes of construction range from the local to the level of a whole clause. The gerund *cantando* becomes “in song”; a dative marking the person affected becomes a possessive in *Gli occhi mi cadder giù*, “Mine eyes drooped down”. Where *pianger ti convien* makes weeping necessary ‘for thee’, “thou must weep” makes the person the subject. Okey does not, however, smooth every construction into ordinary English: “I turned me” preserves the reflexive of *volsimi*, and “verily am I, verily am I” answers the repeated *ben son, ben son* with an equally emphatic repetition.
