# Purgatorio 10

## Lines 1–18 — The Ascent Through the Cleft Rock

> 1 Poi fummo dentro al soglio della porta,  
> 2 che il malo amor dell' anime disusa,  
> 3 perchè fa parer dritta la via torta,  

When we were within the threshold of the gate, which the evil love of souls disuses, because it makes the crooked way seem straight,

- *Poi* ‘afterwards’ → “When”: the adverb is read temporally with the force of ‘once’ or ‘when’, making the opening clause subordinate.
- *il malo amor dell' anime disusa* ‘the evil love of the souls disuses’ → “the evil love of souls disuses”: the article before ‘souls’ is dropped.
- *fa parer dritta la via torta* ‘makes seem straight the way crooked’ → “makes the crooked way seem straight”: the noun phrase is moved before the infinitive, and its adjective is moved before the noun.

> 4 sonando la sentii esser richiusa;  

by the ringing sound I heard it was shut again;

- *sonando* ‘sounding’ → “by the ringing sound”: the gerund becomes a prepositional phrase specifying the means of perception.
- *la sentii esser richiusa* ‘it I heard to be shut again’ → “I heard it was shut again”: the object pronoun preceding the verb becomes the subject of a following finite clause.

> 5 e s' io avessi gli occhi volti ad essa,  
> 6 qual fora stata al fallo degna scusa?  

and had I turned mine eyes to it what would have been a fitting excuse for the fault?

- *s' io avessi gli occhi volti ad essa* ‘if I had the eyes turned to it’ → “had I turned mine eyes to it”: inversion replaces the conditional conjunction; the participle comes before the object, and a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *qual fora stata al fallo degna scusa* ‘what would have been for the fault worthy excuse’ → “what would have been a fitting excuse for the fault”: the prepositional phrase moves to the end, ‘worthy’ becomes “fitting”, and an indefinite article is supplied.

> 7 Noi salivam per una pietra fessa,  

We climbed through a cleft rock,

- *salivam* ‘were ascending’ → “climbed”: a simple past replaces the imperfect, and the verb specifies the manner of ascent.
- *una pietra fessa* ‘a rock cleft’ → “a cleft rock”: the participial adjective precedes the noun.

> 8 che si moveva d' una e d' altra parte,  
> 9 sì come l' onda che fugge e s' appressa.  

which was moving on one side and on the other, even as a wave that recedes and approaches.

- *si moveva* ‘moved itself’ → “was moving”: the English progressive renders the imperfect without a reflexive pronoun.
- *d' una e d' altra parte* ‘on one and on another side’ → “on one side and on the other”: the shared noun is placed with the first term, and the second term becomes definite.
- *l' onda* ‘the wave’ → “a wave”: an indefinite article replaces the generic definite article.
- *fugge* ‘flees’ → “recedes”: a less figurative verb is used.
- *s' appressa* ‘draws itself near’ → “approaches”: a simple verb replaces the pronominal construction.

> 10 “Qui si convien usare un poco d' arte,”  
> 11 cominciò il duca mio, “in accostarsi,  
> 12 or quinci or quindi, al lato che si parte.”  

“Here we must use a little skill,” began my Leader, “in keeping close, now hither now thither, to the side that is receding.”

- *si convien usare* ‘it is requisite to use’ → “we must use”: the impersonal expression becomes a personal modal construction.
- *un poco d' arte* ‘a little of art’ → “a little skill”: the partitive phrase becomes a direct quantification of the noun, and ‘art’ is rendered in the sense of practical skill.
- *il duca mio* ‘the guide of mine’ → “my Leader”: the possessive moves before the noun, and ‘guide’ is rendered as “Leader”.
- *in accostarsi* ‘in drawing oneself close’ → “in keeping close”: movement towards something becomes the maintenance of proximity, without a reflexive pronoun.
- *or quinci or quindi* ‘now on this side, now on that’ → “now hither now thither”: adverbs of direction replace the indications of side.
- *si parte* ‘departs’ → “is receding”: a progressive verb of withdrawal renders the simple pronominal present.

> 13 E ciò fece li nostri passi scarsi  
> 14 tanto, che pria lo scemo della luna  
> 15 rigiunse al letto suo per ricorcarsi,  

And this made our steps so scant, that the waning orb of the moon regained its bed to sink again to rest

- *scarsi tanto* ‘scant so much’ → “so scant”: the degree word moves before the adjective.
- *pria* ‘sooner’ → “regained”: the verb has no separate modifier corresponding to ‘sooner’; the temporal relation is expressed by the conjunction introducing the next clause.
- *lo scemo della luna* ‘the diminished part of the moon’ → “the waning orb of the moon”: the substantivized adjective is interpreted through a participle modifying the supplied noun “orb”.
- *rigiunse al letto suo* ‘came again to its bed’ → “regained its bed”: a transitive verb absorbs the sense of return and takes the destination as its direct object; the possessive precedes the noun.
- *per ricorcarsi* ‘in order to lie down again’ → “to sink again to rest”: an infinitive alone expresses purpose, and sinking to rest replaces the pronominal verb for lying down.

> 16 che noi fossimo fuor di quella cruna.  

ere we were forth from that needle’s eye.

- *che* ‘than’ → “ere”: a temporal conjunction replaces the second member of the Italian comparison.
- *fossimo* ‘we might be’ → “we were”: an indicative past replaces the imperfect subjunctive.
- *fuor di* ‘out of’ → “forth from”: an adverb and preposition of emergence render the expression of position outside.
- *quella cruna* ‘that eye’ → “that needle’s eye”: the English makes explicit the kind of eye denoted by the Italian noun.

> 17 Ma quando fummo liberi ed aperti  
> 18 su dove il monte indietro si rauna,  

But when we were free and on the open above, where the mount is set back,

- *aperti* ‘open’ → “on the open”: a plural predicate adjective becomes a locative prepositional phrase.
- *indietro si rauna* ‘back gathers itself’ → “is set back”: a passive expression replaces the reflexive verb, and the adverb follows rather than precedes it.

## Lines 19–51 — The Annunciation in Marble

> 19 io stancato ed ambedue incerti  
> 20 di nostra via, ristemmo su in un piano  
> 21 solingo più che strade per diserti.  

I wearied and both uncertain of our way, we stood still on a level place more solitary than roads through deserts.

- *ristemmo* ‘we stopped’ → “we stood still”: the verb for coming to a halt is rendered by a verb of posture with an adverb.
- *su in un piano* ‘up on a level’ → “on a level place”: the indication of height drops out, and the noun for a level stretch of ground becomes an adjective qualifying “place”.
- *solingo più che* ‘solitary more than’ → “more solitary than”: the comparative adverb comes before the adjective.

> 22 Dalla sua sponda, ove confina il vano,  
> 23 al piè dell' alta ripa, che pur sale,  
> 24 misurrebbe in tre volte un corpo umano;  

From its edge where it borders on the void, to the foot of the high bank which sheer ascends, a human body would measure in thrice;

- *che pur sale* ‘which only goes up’ → “which sheer ascends”: the restriction on the direction of the rise is rendered as an indication of steepness.
- *misurrebbe in tre volte un corpo umano* ‘would measure in three times a body human’ → “a human body would measure in thrice”: the subject moves from last place to first, its adjective precedes the noun, and the adverb “thrice” replaces numeral and noun.

> 25 e quanto l' occhio mio potea trar d' ale,  
> 26 or dal sinistro ed or dal destro fianco,  
> 27 questa cornice mi parea cotale.  

and so far as mine eye could wing its flight, now on the left now on the right side, such this cornice appeared to me.

- *quanto* ‘as far as’ → “so far as”: the extent implicit in the relative is made explicit.
- *l' occhio mio* ‘the eye of mine’ → “mine eye”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *trar d' ale* ‘ply wings’ → “wing its flight”: the noun for wings becomes a verb, and “its flight” is supplied as its object.
- *or dal sinistro ed or dal destro fianco* ‘now on the left and now on the right side’ → “now on the left now on the right side”: the conjunction between the alternatives drops out.
- *questa cornice mi parea cotale* ‘this cornice to me appeared such’ → “such this cornice appeared to me”: the predicative adjective moves to the front, and the expression for the beholder follows the verb.

> 28 Lassù non eran mossi i piè nostri anco,  

Thereon our feet had not yet moved,

- *Lassù* ‘up there’ → “Thereon”: the English indicates the surface on which the feet rest rather than its height.
- *non eran mossi i piè nostri anco* ‘were not moved our feet yet’ → “our feet had not yet moved”: the subject precedes the verb, the possessive precedes the noun, and English uses the perfect auxiliary “had” with “not yet” before the participle.

> 29 quand' io conobbi quella ripa intorno,  
> 30 che, dritta, di salita aveva manco,  
> 31 esser di marmo candido, e adorno  

when I discerned that circling bank (which, being upright, lacked means of ascent,) to be of pure white marble, and adorned

- *quella ripa intorno* ‘that bank round about’ → “that circling bank”: the indication of position becomes a participle before the noun.
- *dritta* ‘upright’ → “being upright”: a participle is supplied to carry the parenthetic adjective.
- *di salita aveva manco* ‘of ascent had a lack’ → “lacked means of ascent”: the expression of deficiency becomes a verb, “means” is supplied, and the phrase specifying what is lacking follows instead of preceding.
- *marmo candido* ‘marble bright white’ → “pure white marble”: the colour precedes the noun, with “pure” conveying the force of the Italian adjective.

> 32 d' intagli sì che non pur Policreto,  
> 33 ma la natura li avrebbe scorno.  

with sculptures so that not only Polycletus, but Nature there would be put to shame.

- *d' intagli* ‘of carvings’ → “with sculptures”: English changes the preposition and uses a less specific noun.
- *li avrebbe scorno* ‘there would have shame’ → “there would be put to shame”: having shame becomes a passive expression for being made to suffer it.

> 34 L' angel che venne in terra col decreto  
> 35 della molt' anni lagrimata pace,  
> 36 che aperse il ciel dal suo lungo divieto,  

The angel that came to earth with the decree of peace wept for since many a year, which opened heaven from its long ban,

- *della molt' anni lagrimata pace* ‘of the for-many-years lamented peace’ → “of peace wept for since many a year”: the article drops out; the participle follows “peace” and takes “for”; the duration follows the participle, with “since” and a distributive singular instead of the plural.

> 37 dinanzi a noi pareva sì verace  
> 38 quivi intagliato in un atto soave,  
> 39 che non sembiava imagine che tace.  

before us appeared so vividly graven there in gentle mien, that it seemed not an image which is dumb.

- *sì verace* ‘so true’ → “so vividly”: a predicative adjective becomes an adverb modifying the carving.
- *quivi intagliato* ‘there carved’ → “graven there”: the adverb of place follows the participle.
- *in un atto soave* ‘in a gesture gentle’ → “in gentle mien”: gesture becomes demeanour, the article drops out, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *che tace* ‘that keeps silent’ → “which is dumb”: an active verb becomes a copula and adjective.

> 40 Giurato si saria ch' ei dicesse: *Ave*,  

One would have sworn that he was saying: *Ave*;

- *Giurato si saria* ‘sworn would one have’ → “One would have sworn”: the indefinite subject comes first and the participle follows the auxiliaries.
- *ch' ei dicesse* ‘that he were saying’ → “that he was saying”: the imperfect subjunctive becomes an indicative past progressive.

> 41 però che ivi era imaginata quella,  
> 42 che ad aprir l' alto amor volse la chiave.  

for there she was fashioned who turned the key to open the supreme love.

- *però che* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *ivi era imaginata quella* ‘there was represented that one’ → “there she was fashioned”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun before the verb, and the English stresses the making of the figure.
- *che ad aprir l' alto amor volse la chiave* ‘who to open the high love turned the key’ → “who turned the key to open the supreme love”: the purpose phrase moves after verb and object, and “supreme” gives ‘high’ the force of a superlative.

> 43 Ed avea in atto impressa esta favella,  
> 44 *Ecce ancilla Dei*, propriamente,  
> 45 come figura in cera si suggella.  

And in her attitude were imprinted these words, *Ecce ancilla Dei*, as expressly as a figure is stamped on wax.

- *avea in atto impressa esta favella* ‘she had in her bearing impressed this utterance’ → “in her attitude were imprinted these words”: the possessive implicit in the Italian is expressed; what she had impressed in her bearing becomes the subject of a passive, and the singular utterance becomes plural words.
- *propriamente, come* ‘precisely, as’ → “as expressly as”: the adverb and the following comparison are joined in a comparison of degree.
- *figura in cera si suggella* ‘a figure in wax is sealed’ → “a figure is stamped on wax”: the place phrase follows the passive verb, which specifies stamping rather than sealing, and “on” replaces ‘in’.

> 46 “Non tener pure ad un loco la mente,”  
> 47 disse il dolce maestro, che m' avea  
> 48 da quella parte onde il core ha la gente;  

“Keep not thy mind only on one place,” said the sweet Master, who had me on that side where folk have the heart;

- *Non tener pure ad un loco la mente* ‘do not keep only at one place the mind’ → “Keep not thy mind only on one place,”: the negative follows the imperative rather than preceding an infinitive; the object moves next to the verb, with a possessive instead of the article, and “on” replaces ‘at’.
- *che m' avea da quella parte* ‘who me had at that side’ → “who had me on that side”: the object follows the verb, and the English preposition is “on”.
- *onde il core ha la gente* ‘where the heart has the people’ → “where folk have the heart”: the Italian puts object before verb and the singular collective subject last; English puts its articleless plural subject first, with plural agreement.

> 49 per ch' io mi mossi col viso, e vedea  
> 50 di retro da Maria, da quella costa  
> 51 onde m' era colui che mi movea,  

wherefore I moved my face about, and saw behind Mary, on that side of me where he was who was urging me on,

- *mi mossi col viso* ‘I moved myself with the face’ → “I moved my face about”: the reflexive construction gives way to a transitive verb with the face as its object; the article becomes a possessive, and “about” supplies the sense of turning.
- *vedea* ‘was seeing’ → “saw”: the Italian imperfect is rendered by the English simple past.
- *da quella costa* ‘on that side’ → “on that side of me”: the point of reference is made explicit.
- *onde m' era colui* ‘where to me was he’ → “where he was”: the personal reference has been carried back into the preceding phrase, and the demonstrative subject becomes a personal pronoun before the verb.
- *che mi movea* ‘who me was moving’ → “who was urging me on”: moving is interpreted as prompting, and the object follows the verb.

## Lines 52–84 — David and Trajan in Marble

> 52 un' altra storia nella roccia imposta:  
> 53 per ch' io varcai Virgilio, e femmi presso,  
> 54 acciocchè fosse agli occhi miei disposta.  

another story set in the rock, wherefore I crossed by Virgil and drew me nigh, that it might be displayed to mine eyes.

- *nella roccia imposta* ‘in the rock set’ → “set in the rock”: the participle precedes its prepositional phrase.
- *varcai Virgilio* ‘I passed Virgil’ → “I crossed by Virgil”: the direct object becomes the object of “by”.
- *femmi presso* ‘made myself near’ → “drew me nigh”: a verb of motion replaces ‘made’, with “me” used reflexively.
- *fosse agli occhi miei disposta* ‘might be to the eyes of mine displayed’ → “it might be displayed to mine eyes”: the participle moves next to its auxiliary, and the possessive precedes the noun.

> 55 Era intagliato lì nel marmo stesso  
> 56 lo carro e i buoi traendo l' arca santa,  
> 57 per che si teme officio non commesso.  

There was graven on the very marble the cart and the oxen drawing the sacred ark, whereby we fear an office not committed to us.

- *Era intagliato lì* ‘was carved there’ → “There was graven”: “There” moves to the front, where it can also be read as introducing the postponed subject rather than indicating place.
- *nel marmo stesso* ‘in the marble itself’ → “on the very marble”: “on” replaces ‘in’, and the emphasis of postposed *stesso* is carried by “very” before the noun.
- *l’ arca santa* ‘the ark holy’ → “the sacred ark”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *si teme officio* ‘one fears office’ → “we fear an office”: the impersonal construction becomes first-person plural, and the noun takes an indefinite article.
- *non commesso* ‘not entrusted’ → “not committed to us”: the recipient, implicit in Italian, is expressed.

> 58 Dinanzi parea gente; e tutta e quanta  
> 59 partita in sette cori, a' due miei sensi  
> 60 faceva dir l' un “No,” l' altro “Sì, canta.”  

In front appeared people; and the whole divided into seven choirs, to two of my senses, made the one say “no,” the other, “yes, they do sing.”

- *tutta e quanta* ‘all and entire’ → “the whole”: the reinforcing pair of adjectives agreeing with *gente* becomes a single nominal expression.
- *a’ due miei sensi* ‘to the two senses of mine’ → “to two of my senses”: the possessive construction becomes partitive.
- *faceva dir l’ un … l’ altro* ‘made say the one … the other’ → “made the one say” … “the other”: the first of the two subjects of the infinitive is moved before it.
- *canta* ‘it sings’ → “they do sing”: the singular referring to the collective *gente* becomes plural, and emphatic “do” reinforces the affirmation.

> 61 Similemente, al fummo degl' incensi  
> 62 che v' era imaginato, gli occhi e il naso  
> 63 ed al sì ed al no discordi fensi.  

In like wise, at the smoke of the incense which there was imaged, eyes and nose were made discordant with yes and no.

- *Similemente* ‘similarly’ → “In like wise”: an adverb becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *degl’ incensi* ‘of the incenses’ → “of the incense”: the plural becomes a mass noun in the singular.
- *gli occhi e il naso* ‘the eyes and the nose’ → “eyes and nose”: both articles are omitted.
- *ed al sì ed al no discordi fensi* ‘both as to the yes and as to the no at variance made themselves’ → “were made discordant with yes and no”: the reflexive becomes passive; the adjective follows the verb, and the two terms of disagreement follow the adjective, sharing one preposition and losing their articles and the initial ‘both’.

> 64 Lì precedeva al benedetto vaso,  
> 65 trescando alzato, l' umile salmista,  
> 66 e più e men che re era in quel caso.  

There went before the blessed vessel the lowly Psalmist, dancing, girt up; and more and less than king was he in that case.

- *precedeva al benedetto vaso* ‘preceded the blessed vessel’ → “went before the blessed vessel”: the verb of precedence and its complement are rendered by a motion verb and “before”.
- *trescando alzato, l’ umile salmista* ‘dancing, raised up, the humble psalmist’ → “the lowly Psalmist, dancing, girt up”: the subject moves ahead of the participles; “girt up” takes *alzato* to refer to the tucking up of his clothing.

> 67 D' incontra effigiata ad una vista  
> 68 d' un gran palazzo Micol ammirava,  
> 69 sì come donna dispettosa e trista.  

Figured opposite at a window of a great palace was Michal, looking on even as a woman scornful and sad.

- *D’ incontra effigiata* ‘opposite figured’ → “Figured opposite”: the participle precedes the adverb.
- *ad una vista* ‘at an opening for looking out’ → “at a window”: the opening is given a specific architectural name.
- *Micol ammirava* ‘Michal gazed’ → “was Michal, looking on”: the finite verb becomes a participle, while “was” makes the preceding description the main predicate.

> 70 Io mossi i piè del loco dov' io stava,  
> 71 per avvisar da presso un' altra storia  
> 72 che di retro a Micol mi biancheggiava.  

I moved my feet from the place where I stood, to scan closely another story which behind Michal shone white before me.

- *i piè* ‘the feet’ → “my feet”: the English possessive makes explicit whose feet they are.
- *per avvisar da presso* ‘in order to look at from close by’ → “to scan closely”: the infinitive alone marks purpose, and a phrase of physical proximity becomes an adverb modifying “scan”.
- *mi biancheggiava* ‘to me gleamed white’ → “shone white before me”: the single verb becomes verb and adjective, and the dative is rendered by a spatial phrase after them.

> 73 Quivi era storiata l' alta gloria  
> 74 del roman principato, il cui valore  
> 75 mosse Gregorio alla sua gran vittoria:  
> 76 io dico di Traiano imperadore;  

There was storied the high glory of the Roman prince whose worth moved Gregory to his great victory; of Trajan the emperor I speak;

- *del roman principato* ‘of the Roman principate’ → “of the Roman prince”: the office or rule is rendered by its holder.
- *il cui valore* ‘the worth of whom’ → “whose worth”: the possessive relative precedes the noun.
- *io dico di Traiano imperadore* ‘I speak of Trajan emperor’ → “of Trajan the emperor I speak”: the prepositional complement comes first, and the title in apposition takes an article.

> 77 ed una vedovella gli era al freno,  
> 78 di lagrime atteggiata e di dolore.  

and a poor widow was at his bridle in the attitude of tears and of grief.

- *una vedovella* ‘a little widow’ → “a poor widow”: “poor” conveys the pity implicit in the diminutive rather than suggesting smallness.
- *gli era al freno* ‘to him was at the bridle’ → “was at his bridle”: the dative becomes a possessive modifying “bridle”.
- *di lagrime atteggiata e di dolore* ‘of tears expressive in pose and of grief’ → “in the attitude of tears and of grief”: the participle becomes a prepositional phrase with a noun, followed by both its complements.

> 79 Intorno a lui parea calcato e pieno  
> 80 di cavalieri, e l' aquile nell' oro  
> 81 sopr' esso in vista al vento si movieno.  

Round about him appeared a trampling and throng of horsemen and the eagles in gold above him moved visibly to the wind.

- *parea calcato e pieno di cavalieri* ‘it seemed crowded and full of horsemen’ → “appeared a trampling and throng of horsemen”: the two predicative adjectives become nouns serving as the subject of “appeared”; “trampling” suggests movement rather than the pressure of a crowd.
- *nell’ oro* ‘in the gold’ → “in gold”: the article is omitted.
- *in vista* ‘in appearance’ → “visibly”: the phrase becomes an adverb qualifying the motion.
- *al vento si movieno* ‘to the wind moved themselves’ → “moved visibly to the wind”: an intransitive verb replaces the Italian pronominal form, and the phrase about the wind follows the verb.

> 82 La miserella intra tutti costoro  
> 83 parea dicer: “Signor, fammi vendetta  
> 84 del mio figliuol ch' è morto, ond' io m' accoro.”  

The poor creature among all these seemed to say: “Lord, do me vengeance for my son who is slain, whereby my heart is pierced.”

- *La miserella* ‘the poor little one’ → “The poor creature”: a noun is supplied for the substantivized diminutive adjective, conveying its note of pity without an explicit diminutive.
- *del mio figliuol* ‘of my son’ → “for my son”: English uses “for” to connect the vengeance with the person to be avenged; “son” does not mark the diminutive form.
- *ch’ è morto* ‘who is dead’ → “who is slain”: the rendering specifies a violent death.
- *ond’ io m’ accoro* ‘on account of which I grieve at heart’ → “whereby my heart is pierced”: the pronominal verb becomes a passive image, with the heart rather than the speaker as subject.

## Lines 85–117 — Duty Before Departure and the Approach of the Penitents

> 85 Ed egli a lei rispondere: “Ora aspetta  
> 86 tanto ch' io torni.” Ed quella: “Signor mio,”  
> 87 come persona in cui dolor s' affretta,  

And he to answer her: “Now wait until I return.” And she, like a person in whom grief is urgent: “My Lord,

- *a lei rispondere* ‘to her to answer’ → “to answer her”: the infinitive is retained, but the person answered becomes its direct object and follows it.
- *tanto ch' io torni* ‘so long that I return’ → “until I return”: one conjunction renders the expression of duration and its limit; the Italian subjunctive has no distinct form in the English.
- *Ed quella* ‘and that one’ → “And she”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun.
- *Signor mio, come persona in cui dolor s' affretta* ‘Lord of mine, like a person in whom grief hastens’ → “like a person in whom grief is urgent: “My Lord”: the comparison is moved before the address, the possessive precedes the noun, and a reflexive verb of motion becomes a copula and an adjective.

> 88 “se tu non torni?” Ed ei: “Chi fia dov' io  
> 89 la ti farà.” Ed quella: “L' altrui bene  
> 90 a te che fia, se il tuo metti in obblio?”  

if thou do not return?” And he: “One who shall be in my place will do it for thee.” And she: “What to thee will be another’s good deed if thou forget thine own?”

- *Chi fia dov' io* ‘whoever shall be where I [am]’ → “One who shall be in my place”: the relative acquires an explicit antecedent, and the elliptical clause of place becomes a prepositional phrase with a possessive.
- *la ti farà* ‘it for thee will do’ → “will do it for thee”: the two preverbal pronouns follow the verb, the second as a prepositional phrase.
- *Ed quella* ‘and that one’ → “And she”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun.
- *L' altrui bene a te che fia* ‘the good of another to thee what will be’ → “What to thee will be another’s good deed”: the interrogative comes first and the subject last; a possessive precedes the noun, and the substantive *bene* is expanded to adjective and noun.
- *il tuo metti in obblio* ‘thine own thou puttest into oblivion’ → “thou forget thine own”: a single verb replaces the verb and prepositional phrase, and the object follows it.

> 91 Ond' egli: “Or ti conforta, chè conviene  
> 92 ch' io solva il mio dovere, anzi ch' io mova:  
> 93 giustizia vuole e pietà mi ritiene.”  

Wherefore he: “Now comfort thee, for needs must I fulfil my duty ere I stir; justice wills and pity holds me back.”

- *ti conforta* ‘thyself comfort’ → “comfort thee”: the reflexive object follows the imperative.
- *chè conviene ch' io solva* ‘for it is necessary that I discharge’ → “for needs must I fulfil”: the impersonal verb and its subjunctive clause become a modal construction with an infinitive; the adverb reinforces the necessity.
- *anzi ch' io mova* ‘before I move’ → “ere I stir”: “stir” renders the verb of moving, and the temporal conjunction takes a verb with no distinct subjunctive form.
- *mi ritiene* ‘me restrains’ → “holds me back”: the verb is rendered by a verb and particle, with the object between them.

> 94 Colui, che mai non vide cosa nuova,  
> 95 produsse esto visibile parlare,  
> 96 novello a noi, perchè qui non si trova.  

He, who ne’er beheld a new thing, wrought this visible speech, new to us because here it is not found.

- *Colui* ‘that one’ → “He”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *mai non vide cosa nuova* ‘never saw thing new’ → “ne’er beheld a new thing”: the two Italian negative words yield one English negative; “beheld” renders ‘saw’, an article is supplied, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *produsse* ‘produced’ → “wrought”: the English uses a verb of making rather than ‘produced’.
- *non si trova* ‘one does not find [it]’ → “it is not found”: the Italian *si* construction is rendered by an explicit passive.

> 97 Mentr' io mi dilettava di guardare  
> 98 le imagini di tante umilitadi,  
> 99 e per lo fabbro loro a veder care;  

While I was rejoicing to look on the images of humilities so great and for their Craftsman’s sake precious to see,

- *mi dilettava* ‘I delighted myself’ → “I was rejoicing”: the reflexive imperfect becomes an intransitive progressive.
- *di guardare* ‘in looking at’ → “to look on”: the complement of delight becomes a to-infinitive, and “on” introduces what is looked at.
- *di tante umilitadi* ‘of such great humilities’ → “of humilities so great”: the indication of degree follows the noun.
- *per lo fabbro loro* ‘on account of the craftsman of them’ → “for their Craftsman’s sake”: the possessive precedes the noun, and the preposition is expanded to a construction with “sake”.
- *a veder care* ‘to see dear’ → “precious to see”: “precious” renders the adjective and precedes its infinitive.

> 100 “Ecco di qua, ma fanno i passi radi,”  
> 101 mormorava il poeta, “molte genti;  
> 102 questi ne invieranno agli alti gradi.”  

“Lo here,” murmured the Poet, “much people, but few they make their steps; these will send us on to the high stairs.”

- *Ecco di qua* ‘behold, on this side’ → “Lo here”: the indication of direction or side is reduced to “here”.
- *ma fanno i passi radi* ‘but they make the steps infrequent’ → “but few they make their steps”: the qualification of the steps is moved to the front and rendered in terms of number; a possessive replaces the definite article. The whole clause is postponed until after the people have been named.
- *mormorava il poeta* ‘the poet was murmuring’ → “murmured the Poet”: the imperfect becomes a simple past, and the reporting clause now immediately follows “Lo here”.
- *molte genti* ‘many people’ → “much people”: the plural is treated as a collective with “much”.
- *ne invieranno* ‘us they will send’ → “will send us on”: the object follows the verb, and “on” gives the sense of onward direction.
- *agli alti gradi* ‘to the high steps’ → “to the high stairs”: “stairs” renders the steps of ascent.

> 103 Gli occhi miei ch' a mirar eran intenti,  
> 104 per veder novitadi onde son vaghi,  
> 105 volgendosi ver lui non furon lenti.  

Mine eyes, that were intent on gazing to see new things whereof they are fain, were not slow in turning towards him.

- *Gli occhi miei* ‘the eyes of mine’ → “Mine eyes”: the possessive precedes the noun.
- *a mirar eran intenti* ‘on gazing were intent’ → “were intent on gazing”: the complement follows the adjective instead of preceding the verb.
- *novitadi* ‘novelties’ → “new things”: a single noun becomes an adjective and noun.
- *volgendosi ver lui non furon lenti* ‘turning themselves towards him they were not slow’ → “were not slow in turning towards him”: the gerund phrase is moved after the adjective as its complement with “in”, and the reflexive is rendered intransitively.

> 106 Non vo' però, lettor, che tu ti smaghi  
> 107 di buon proponimento, per udire  
> 108 come Dio vuol che il debito si paghi.  

I would not, reader, that thou be scared from a good purpose through hearing how God wills that the debt be paid.

- *Non vo' però* ‘I do not, however, wish’ → “I would not”: “would” expresses the wish, and the connective is omitted.
- *che tu ti smaghi di buon proponimento* ‘that thou lose heart in thy good purpose’ → “that thou be scared from a good purpose”: the reflexive subjunctive is interpreted as a passive of frightening; “from” marks the purpose as what one is frightened away from, and English supplies an indefinite article.
- *per udire* ‘for hearing’ → “through hearing”: “through” makes the causal relation explicit, and a gerund renders the Italian infinitive.
- *il debito si paghi* ‘the debt should get paid’ → “the debt be paid”: an English passive subjunctive renders the Italian subjunctive with *si*.

> 109 Non attender la forma del martire;  
> 110 pensa la succession; pensa che, al peggio,  
> 111 oltre la gran sentenza non può ire.  

Heed not the form of the pain; think what followeth, think that at worst beyond the great judgment it cannot go.

- *Non attender* ‘not to heed’ → “Heed not”: the Italian negative command uses an infinitive; the English uses an imperative with the negative after it.
- *del martire* ‘of the torment’ → “of the pain”: “pain” renders the noun for suffering.
- *pensa la succession* ‘think of the sequel’ → “think what followeth”: the noun with its article becomes a relative clause.
- *la gran sentenza* ‘the great sentence’ → “the great judgment”: “judgment” renders the judicial noun.
- *non può ire* ‘not can go’ → “it cannot go”: the negative is attached to the modal rather than preceding it.

> 112 Io cominciai: “Maestro, quel ch' io veggio  
> 113 mover a noi, non mi sembran persone,  
> 114 e non so che, sì nel veder vaneggio.”  

I began: “Master, that which I see moving towards us seems not persons to me, yet I know not what, so wanders my sight.”

- *mover a noi* ‘move to us’ → “moving towards us”: a participle replaces the infinitive after the verb of seeing, and “towards” specifies direction.
- *non mi sembran persone* ‘to me they do not seem persons’ → “seems not persons to me”: the verb becomes singular in agreement with “that which”, the negative follows it, and the person judging is moved to the end.
- *e non so che* ‘and I do not know what’ → “yet I know not what”: the conjunction is made adversative, and the negative follows the verb.
- *sì nel veder vaneggio* ‘so in seeing I wander’ → “so wanders my sight”: the act of seeing becomes a possessed noun and takes over as subject of the verb.

> 115 Ed egli a me: “La grave condizione  
> 116 di lor tormento a terra li rannicchia,  
> 117 sì che i miei occhi pria n' ebber tenzone.  

And he to me: “The grievous state of their torment doubles them down to earth so that mine eyes at first thereat were at strife.

- *condizione* ‘condition’ → “state”: another noun renders the condition.
- *a terra li rannicchia* ‘to earth it makes them crouch’ → “doubles them down to earth”: “doubles” renders the bending of the body; the object follows the verb, and the direction follows the object with an added “down”.
- *pria n' ebber tenzone* ‘at first had strife over it’ → “at first thereat were at strife”: “thereat” renders the pronominal *ne*, and having strife becomes being at strife.

## Lines 118–139 — The Proud Beneath the Stones

> 118 Ma guarda fiso là, e disviticchia  
> 119 col viso quel che vien sotto a quei sassi;  

But look steadily there and disentwine with thy sight what is coming beneath those stones;

- *fiso* ‘fixedly’ → “steadily”: the English stresses the steadiness of the gaze rather than its fixity.
- *col viso* ‘with the sight’ → “with thy sight”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *quel che vien* ‘that which comes’ → “what is coming”: a single relative stands for the demonstrative and relative together, and the simple present becomes progressive.

> 120 già scorger puoi come ciascun si picchia.”  

already thou canst discern how each one beats his breast.”

- *scorger puoi* ‘discern thou canst’ → “thou canst discern”: the modal verb precedes the infinitive.
- *si picchia* ‘beats himself’ → “beats his breast”: the part of the body struck is specified in place of the reflexive pronoun.

> 121 O superbi Cristian miseri lassi,  
> 122 che, della vista della mente infermi,  
> 123 fidanza avete ne' ritrosi passi;  

O ye proud Christians, wretched and weary, who, sick in mental vision, put trust in backward steps,

- *O superbi Cristian* ‘O proud Christians’ → “O ye proud Christians”: the pronoun of address is supplied.
- *miseri lassi* ‘wretched, weary’ → “wretched and weary”: a conjunction links the adjectives.
- *della vista della mente infermi* ‘of the sight of the mind sick’ → “sick in mental vision”: the adjective comes first, its complement is introduced by “in”, and the possessive phrase becomes the adjective “mental”.
- *fidanza avete* ‘trust have’ → “put trust”: the verb precedes its object, and placing trust replaces having it.
- *ne' ritrosi passi* ‘in the backward steps’ → “in backward steps”: the definite article is omitted.

> 124 non v' accorgete voi, che noi siam vermi  
> 125 nati a formar l' angelica farfalla,  
> 126 che vola alla giustizia senza schermi?  

perceive ye not that we are worms, born to form the angelic butterfly that flieth to judgment without defence?

- *non v' accorgete voi* ‘not yourselves do ye make aware’ → “perceive ye not”: a simple verb of perception renders the Italian pronominal verb, and the negative comes last.
- *alla giustizia* ‘to justice’ → “to judgment”: the act of judging is named rather than justice, and the definite article is omitted.
- *senza schermi* ‘without defences’ → “without defence”: a singular abstract noun replaces the plural.

> 127 Di che l' animo vostro in alto galla,  
> 128 poi siete quasi entomata in difetto,  
> 129 sì come verme, in cui formazion falla.  

Why doth your mind soar on high, since ye are as ’twere imperfect insects, even as the grub in which full form is wanting?

- *Di che* ‘from what’ → “Why”: a single interrogative adverb expresses the question about cause.
- *l' animo vostro in alto galla* ‘the spirit of yours on high floats’ → “doth your mind soar on high”: the question takes an auxiliary, the possessive precedes the noun, and floating becomes soaring, with the phrase of height following the verb.
- *quasi* ‘almost’ → “as ’twere”: approximation is expressed as a hypothetical comparison.
- *entomata in difetto* ‘insects in a state of deficiency’ → “imperfect insects”: an adjective before the noun replaces the phrase after it.
- *sì come verme* ‘just as a worm’ → “even as the grub”: the more specific noun takes the definite article, where the Italian noun has no article.
- *in cui formazion falla* ‘in which formation fails’ → “in which full form is wanting”: the process of formation becomes its result, “full” is supplied, and failure is expressed as lack.

> 130 Come per sostentar solaio o tetto,  
> 131 per mensola talvolta una figura  
> 132 si vede giunger le ginocchia al petto,  

As to support ceiling or roof is sometimes seen for corbel a figure joining knees to breast,

- *solaio* ‘floor’ → “ceiling”: the structure is named as seen from below.
- *per mensola talvolta una figura si vede* ‘for corbel sometimes a figure is seen’ → “is sometimes seen for corbel a figure”: the passive precedes its subject, the adverb falls inside the passive, and the phrase giving the figure's function follows the verb.
- *giunger le ginocchia al petto* ‘to join the knees to the breast’ → “joining knees to breast”: the infinitive governed by the verb of seeing becomes a participle, and both articles are omitted.

> 133 la qual fa del non ver vera rancura  
> 134 nascere a chi la vede: così fatti  
> 135 vid' io color, quando posi ben cura.  

which of unreality begetteth real discomfort in him who beholds it; in such wise saw I these when I gave good heed.

- *fa del non ver vera rancura nascere* ‘makes from the not-real real distress arise’ → “of unreality begetteth real discomfort”: an abstract noun renders the negated adjective; a single causative verb replaces the separated verb and infinitive, and “discomfort” is milder than ‘distress’.
- *a chi la vede* ‘to whoever sees it’ → “in him who beholds it”: the person affected becomes the person in whom the feeling arises; the relative acquires an explicit antecedent, and the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *così fatti* ‘thus fashioned’ → “in such wise”: the description of the figures becomes an adverbial phrase.
- *color* ‘those’ → “these”: the demonstrative changes from distant to near.
- *posi ben cura* ‘applied care well’ → “gave good heed”: the idiom changes, and the adverb modifying the verb becomes an adjective modifying the noun.

> 136 Ver è che più e meno eran contratti,  
> 137 secondo ch' avean più o meno addosso;  

True it is that more and less were they contracted, according as they had more or less upon them,

- *Ver è* ‘true is’ → “True it is”: English supplies a grammatical subject in anticipation of the following clause.
- *più e meno eran contratti* ‘more and less they were contracted’ → “more and less were they contracted”: the expressed subject is placed between the auxiliary and the participle.
- *secondo ch' avean più o meno addosso* ‘according as they had more or less on their backs’ → “according as they had more or less upon them”: the anatomical reference is left implicit.

> 138 e qual più pazienza avea negli atti,  
> 139 piangendo parea dicer: “Più non posso.”  

and he who had most patience in his bearing, weeping seemed to say: “I can no more.”

- *qual più pazienza avea* ‘whoever more patience had’ → “he who had most patience”: the relative is given an explicit antecedent, the verb precedes its object, and the comparative is read as a relative superlative.
- *negli atti* ‘in the gestures’ → “in his bearing”: a collective singular replaces the plural, and a possessive replaces the article.
- *Più non posso* ‘more I cannot’ → “I can no more”: the modal comes first, and the negative is grouped with “more” rather than with the verb.

## Summary — Italian Order and English Idiom

Okey often keeps Dante’s words but changes their order. In *fa parer dritta la via torta*, ‘makes seem straight the crooked way’, he brings the object forward: “makes the crooked way seem straight”. Yet he does not consistently prefer ordinary English order. In “such this cornice appeared to me” he gives *cotale* even greater prominence by moving it from the end to the beginning; in “in such wise saw I these” he keeps the verb before the subject, as in *vid' io*.

A change of construction often accounts for a change of wording. The impersonal *si convien usare*, ‘it is necessary to use’, becomes the personal “we must use”; the gerund *sonando*, ‘sounding’, becomes “by the ringing sound”. Okey sometimes makes explicit what a single Italian word conveys: *cruna* becomes “needle’s eye”, and *si picchia*, ‘beats himself’, becomes “beats his breast”. Elsewhere he compresses: *metti in obblio*, ‘put into oblivion’, becomes “forget”, and the connective *però* in *Non vo' però* has no separate equivalent. A choice of verb can also alter an image: for *galla*, ‘floats’, Okey uses “soar”.

Two routine adjustments generally pass unmarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where Italian gives the person by the verb ending, and drops the definite article before a possessive, as in *il mio dovere*, “my duty”. Such adjustments need not make the rendering any less close. Okey preserves the paradox of *visibile parlare* in “visible speech” and the balance of *più e men che re* in “more and less than king”, keeping both the terms and their order.
