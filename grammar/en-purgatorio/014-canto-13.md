# Purgatorio 13

## Lines 1–9 — The Bare Second Terrace

> 1 Noi eravamo al sommo della scala,  
> 2 ove secondamente si risega  
> 3 lo monte, che salendo altrui dismala.  

We were at the top of the stairway where a second time the mount is cut away which, by our ascent, frees us from evil.

- *secondamente* ‘secondly’ → “a second time”: the adverb becomes a phrase marking repetition.
- *si risega lo monte* ‘is cut back the mount’ → “the mount is cut away”: the passive construction with *si* becomes an English passive, with the subject moved before the verb.
- *salendo* ‘in climbing’ → “by our ascent”: the gerund becomes a noun with a possessive, and “by” makes the relation one of means.
- *altrui dismala* ‘one it frees from evil’ → “frees us from evil”: the indefinite *altrui* becomes the first-person “us” and moves after the verb; the single verb is rendered by a phrase.

> 4 Ivi così una cornice lega  
> 5 dintorno il poggio, come la primaia,  
> 6 se non che l' arco suo più tosto piega.  

There a cornice binds the hill around like unto the first, save that its curve more sharply bends.

- *così … come la primaia* ‘so … as the first’ → “like unto the first”: the two-part comparison becomes a single phrase, with no separate word for *così* before “a cornice”.
- *dintorno il poggio* ‘around the hill’ → “the hill around”: “around” follows the object instead of introducing it.
- *l' arco suo* ‘the arc of it’ → “its curve”: “curve” renders *arco*, and the possessive precedes the noun.
- *più tosto* ‘more quickly’ → “more sharply”: the translation expresses the tightness of the bend rather than the rate of turning.

> 7 Ombra non gli è, nè segno che si paia;  

No shade is there, nor figure which may be seen;

- *Ombra non gli è* ‘shade there is not’ → “No shade is there”: the negation moves from the verb to the noun, and the locative “there” follows the verb.
- *segno* ‘mark’ → “figure”: the English takes the mark specifically as a visible form.
- *che si paia* ‘that appears’ → “which may be seen”: appearing becomes being seen, and the subjunctive is rendered with “may”.

> 8 par sì la ripa, e par sì la via schietta  
> 9 col livido color della petraia.  

so naked the bank appears and even so the way, with the livid hue of the stone.

- *par sì la ripa, e par sì la via schietta* ‘appears so the bank, and appears so the way, bare’ → “so naked the bank appears and even so the way”: “naked” renders the shared adjective at the beginning rather than the end, paired with the first “so”; the first verb moves after “the bank”, the repeated verb is left understood, and “even” reinforces the second “so”.
- *della petraia* ‘of the mass of rock’ → “of the stone”: the collective noun is rendered by the name of the material.

## Lines 10–42 — The Sun as Guide and the Voices of Love

> 10 “Se qui per domandar gente s' aspetta,”  
> 11 ragionava il poeta, “io temo forse  
> 12 che troppo avrà d' indugio nostra eletta.”  

“If here we await people to ask of,” the poet was saying, “I fear perchance that our choice may have too great delay.”

- *per domandar gente s' aspetta* ‘for asking, people are awaited’ → “we await people to ask of”: the impersonal construction becomes first-person plural and active; the purpose phrase is moved after “people”, and “of” marks the people as those to whom the question would be put.
- *ragionava il poeta* ‘was speaking the poet’ → “the poet was saying”: the subject precedes the verb, and the verb of speaking becomes a verb introducing the words said.
- *troppo avrà d' indugio nostra eletta* ‘too much will have of delay our choice’ → “our choice may have too great delay”: the subject moves to the front; the future becomes a modal after the expression of fear, and the quantity expressed by *troppo … d' indugio* becomes an adjective phrase modifying “delay”.

> 13 Poi fisamente al sole gli occhi porse;  

Then fixedly on the sun his eyes he set;

- *gli occhi porse* ‘the eyes he directed’ → “his eyes he set”: a possessive replaces the definite article, and directing the eyes towards something is expressed as setting them on it.

> 14 fece del destro lato al mover centro,  
> 15 e la sinistra parte di sè torse.  

he made of his right side a centre of movement, and the left part of him did turn.

- *del destro lato* ‘of the right side’ → “of his right side”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *al mover centro* ‘for the moving a centre’ → “a centre of movement”: the noun comes first, and the substantivized infinitive with *a* becomes an abstract noun with “of”.
- *di sè torse* ‘of himself he turned’ → “of him did turn”: the reflexive becomes a personal pronoun, and the simple past is expressed with auxiliary “did”; the subject of the first verb continues to serve for the second.

> 16 “O dolce lume, a cui fidanza i' entro  
> 17 per lo nuovo cammin, tu ne conduci;”  
> 18 dicea, “come condur si vuol quinc' entro:  

“O sweet light, in whose trust I enter on the new way, do thou lead us,” said he, “as we would be led here within;

- *a cui fidanza* ‘with trust in whom’ → “in whose trust”: the light, the object of trust in Italian, is attached to “trust” by a possessive relative in English.
- *per lo nuovo cammin* ‘by the new way’ → “on the new way”: the preposition of route becomes the preposition used with “enter on”.
- *tu ne conduci* ‘thou us lead’ → “do thou lead us”: the imperative takes auxiliary “do”, and the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *dicea* ‘he was saying’ → “said he”: the imperfect becomes a simple past, with the subject after the verb.
- *come condur si vuol* ‘as one ought to guide’ → “as we would be led”: the impersonal modal construction becomes personal and passive; “would” can suggest the travellers’ wish rather than the manner of guidance called for.

> 19 tu scaldi il mondo, tu sopr' esso luci;  

thou givest warmth to the world, thou shinest upon it;

- *scaldi il mondo* ‘warmest the world’ → “givest warmth to the world”: the transitive verb becomes a verb and noun, and its direct object becomes the recipient.
- *sopr' esso luci* ‘upon it shinest’ → “shinest upon it”: the verb precedes the prepositional phrase.

> 20 s' altra ragione in contrario non pronta,  
> 21 esser den sempre li tuoi raggi duci.”  

if other reason urges not to the contrary, thy beams must ever be our guide.”

- *in contrario non pronta* ‘to the contrary urges not’ → “urges not to the contrary”: the directional phrase moves after the verb and its negative.
- *esser den sempre li tuoi raggi duci* ‘be must always thy rays guides’ → “thy beams must ever be our guide”: the subject moves before the modal, the infinitive after it; “beams” renders ‘rays’, and the plural predicate becomes a singular one with “our” supplied.

> 22 Quanto di qua per un migliaio si conta,  
> 23 tanto di là eravam noi già iti,  
> 24 con poco tempo, per la voglia pronta;  

As far as here counts for a mile, so far there had we already gone, in short time, by reason of our ready will;

- *Quanto di qua per un migliaio si conta* ‘as much as on this side for a mile is counted’ → “As far as here counts for a mile”: the measure is expressed explicitly as distance; ‘on this side’ becomes “here”, and the passive or impersonal reckoning becomes intransitive “counts”, before the measure.
- *tanto di là* ‘so much on that side’ → “so far there”: the correlative likewise becomes a measure of distance, and ‘on that side’ becomes “there”.
- *eravam noi già iti* ‘were we already gone’ → “had we already gone”: English forms the pluperfect of motion with ‘have’ rather than ‘be’.
- *con poco tempo* ‘with little time’ → “in short time”: “in” marks the time taken, and “short” describes duration rather than quantity.
- *per la voglia pronta* ‘through the eagerness ready’ → “by reason of our ready will”: the causal preposition is expanded, “will” renders the inclination to go, a possessive replaces the article, and the adjective precedes the noun.

> 25 e verso noi volar furon sentiti,  
> 26 non però visti, spiriti, parlando  
> 27 alla mensa d' amor cortesi inviti.  

and, flying towards us were heard, but not seen, spirits, speaking courteous invitations to the table of love.

- *verso noi volar furon sentiti* ‘towards us to fly were heard’ → “flying towards us were heard”: the infinitive dependent on the verb of perception becomes a participle, before its directional phrase.
- *non però visti* ‘not however seen’ → “but not seen”: the adversative adverb becomes a conjunction before the negative.
- *alla mensa d' amor cortesi inviti* ‘to the table of love courteous invitations’ → “courteous invitations to the table of love”: the object of “speaking” comes before the phrase specifying what the invitations are to.

> 28 La prima voce che passò volando,  
> 29 “*Vinum non habent,*” altamente disse,  
> 30 e retro a noi l' andò reiterando.  

The first voice which passed by in its flight loudly said, “*Vinum non habent,*” and went on repeating it behind us.

- *passò volando* ‘passed flying’ → “passed by in its flight”: “by” is supplied, and the gerund becomes a prepositional phrase with a possessive noun.
- *Vinum non habent, altamente disse* ‘Vinum non habent, loudly said’ → “loudly said, “*Vinum non habent,*””: the adverb and reporting verb move before the quoted words.
- *retro a noi l' andò reiterando* ‘behind us it went repeating’ → “went on repeating it behind us”: the directional phrase moves to the end, the object pronoun follows the participle, and “went on” brings out the continuative force of the verb and gerund.

> 31 E prima che del tutto non s' udisse  
> 32 per allungarsi, un' altra: “Io sono Oreste”  
> 33 passò gridando, ed anco non s' affisse.  

And ere it had wholly passed out of hearing through distance, another passed crying: “I am Orestes,” and also stayed not.

- *prima che del tutto non s' udisse* ‘before it should altogether cease to be heard’ → “ere it had wholly passed out of hearing”: the negative subjunctive of the perception verb becomes an affirmative pluperfect describing passage beyond hearing.
- *per allungarsi* ‘through getting farther away’ → “through distance”: an infinitive denoting increasing separation becomes a noun.
- *un' altra: Io sono Oreste, passò gridando* ‘another: I am Orestes, passed crying’ → “another passed crying: “I am Orestes,””: the verb and participle are brought forward to introduce the cry.
- *non s' affisse* ‘did not fix itself’ → “stayed not”: the reflexive verb becomes an intransitive one.

> 34 “O,” diss' io, “padre, che voci son queste?”  

“O Father,” said I, “what voices are these?”

- *O, diss' io, padre* ‘O, said I, father’ → ““O Father,” said I”: the reporting clause is moved after the complete address.

> 35 e com' io domandava, ecco la terza  
> 36 dicendo: “Amate da cui male aveste.”  

and as I was asking, lo the third saying: “Love them from whom ye have suffered evil.”

- *Amate da cui male aveste* ‘love [those] from whom evil you had’ → “Love them from whom ye have suffered evil”: “them” supplies an explicit antecedent for the relative; ‘had’ becomes “have suffered”, changing both the verb and the tense, and “evil” follows the verb.

> 37 E 'l buon maestro: “Questo cinghio sferza  
> 38 la colpa dell' invidia, e però sono  
> 39 tratte da amor le corde della ferza.  

And the good Master: “This circle doth scourge the sin of envy, and therefore the cords of the whip are drawn from love.

- *Questo cinghio sferza* ‘this girdle scourges’ → “This circle doth scourge”: “circle” renders the encircling band, and the simple present is expressed with auxiliary “doth”.
- *la colpa dell' invidia* ‘the fault of the envy’ → “the sin of envy”: “sin” gives the fault a specifically moral sense, and the abstract noun “envy” has no article.
- *sono tratte da amor le corde della ferza* ‘are drawn from love the cords of the whip’ → “the cords of the whip are drawn from love”: the subject, postponed in Italian, precedes the passive verb and its complement.

> 40 Lo fren vuol esser del contrario suono;  

The bit must be of contrary sound;

- *Lo fren* ‘the bridle’ → “The bit”: the rendering names the particular part that checks the animal.
- *vuol esser* ‘wants to be’ → “must be”: the idiomatic modal force of *volere* is expressed as necessity.
- *del contrario suono* ‘of the contrary sound’ → “of contrary sound”: the definite article is omitted.

> 41 credo che l' udirai, per mio avviso,  
> 42 prima che giunghi al passo del perdono.  

I think thou wilt hear it, as I opine, ere thou reachest the Pass of Pardon.

- *credo che l' udirai* ‘I believe that it thou wilt hear’ → “I think thou wilt hear it”: “think” renders belief, the complementizer is omitted, and the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *per mio avviso* ‘in my judgment’ → “as I opine”: a prepositional phrase with a possessive noun becomes a clause with a finite verb.
- *prima che giunghi al passo del perdono* ‘before thou come to the pass of the pardon’ → “ere thou reachest the Pass of Pardon”: the Italian subjunctive becomes an English present indicative, “reachest” takes a direct object instead of a goal with *a*, and “Pardon” has no article.

## Lines 43–75 — The Shades With Sewn Eyelids

> 43 Ma ficca gli occhi per l' aer ben fiso,  

But fix thine eyes through the air full steadily,

- *ficca gli occhi* ‘fasten the eyes’ → “fix thine eyes”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *ben fiso* ‘well fixed’ → “full steadily”: the adjective used adverbially becomes an adverb of manner, with “full” as its intensifier.

> 44 e vedrai gente innanzi a noi sedersi,  
> 45 e ciascun è lungo la grotta assiso.”  

and thou shalt see people sitting down in front of us, and each one along the cliff is seated.”

- *innanzi a noi sedersi* ‘in front of us seat themselves’ → “sitting down in front of us”: the reflexive infinitive after the verb of seeing becomes a participle with “down”; the phrase of place comes after it.
- *è lungo la grotta assiso* ‘is along the cliff seated’ → “along the cliff is seated”: the phrase of place moves from between the auxiliary and the participle to before the auxiliary.

> 46 Allora più che prima gli occhi apersi;  

Then wider than before mine eyes I opened;

- *più che prima* ‘more than before’ → “wider than before”: the comparative specifies in what respect the eyes were opened more.
- *gli occhi* ‘the eyes’ → “mine eyes”: the definite article becomes a possessive.

> 47 guarda' mi innanzi, e vidi ombre con manti  
> 48 al color della pietra non diversi.  

I looked before me, and saw shades with cloaks not different from the hue of the stone.

- *guarda' mi innanzi* ‘I looked me ahead’ → “I looked before me”: the pronoun attached in sense to the verb becomes the object of “before”.
- *al color della pietra non diversi* ‘from the hue of the stone not different’ → “not different from the hue of the stone”: the adjective and its negative precede their complement.

> 49 E poi che fummo un poco più avanti,  

And after we were a little further forward,

- *fummo un poco più avanti* ‘we were a little more forward’ → “we were a little further forward”: the comparative of degree becomes one of distance.

> 50 udi' gridar: “Maria, ora per noi,”  
> 51 gridar “Michele, e Pietro, e tutti i Santi.”  

I heard a cry: “Mary, pray for us”; a cry: “Michael, and Peter, and all Saints.”

- *udir* in *udi' gridar* ‘I heard cry out’ → “I heard a cry”: the infinitive dependent on the verb of hearing becomes a noun with an indefinite article.
- *gridar* ‘cry out’ → “a cry”: the repeated infinitive likewise becomes a noun phrase.
- *tutti i Santi* ‘all the Saints’ → “all Saints”: the definite article is dropped.

> 52 Non credo che per terra vada ancoi  
> 53 uomo sì duro, che non fosse punto  
> 54 per compassion di quel ch' io vidi poi:  

I believe not that on earth there goeth this day a man so hardened, who were not pierced with compassion at what I then saw;

- *per terra vada ancoi* ‘over the earth goes today’ → “on earth there goeth this day”: the preposition suggests location rather than movement over a surface; anticipatory “there” introduces the postponed subject. The Italian subjunctive after a negated verb of belief becomes an indicative, and the adverb of time becomes a noun phrase.
- *uomo sì duro* ‘man so hard’ → “a man so hardened”: an indefinite article is supplied, and a participial adjective replaces the simple adjective.
- *che non fosse punto* ‘that he would not be pierced’ → “who were not pierced”: the clause of consequence dependent on ‘so hard’ becomes a relative clause qualifying the man.
- *per compassion di quel ch' io vidi poi* ‘by compassion for that which I saw next’ → “with compassion at what I then saw”: the prepositions change, “what” combines antecedent and relative, and the adverb of sequence moves before the verb and becomes “then”.

> 55 chè, quand' io fui sì presso di lor giunto  
> 56 che gli atti loro a me venivan certi,  
> 57 per gli occhi fui di grave dolor munto.  

for when I had reached so nigh to them that their features came distinctly to me, heavy grief was wrung from mine eyes.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *quand' io fui sì presso di lor giunto* ‘when I had so near to them arrived’ → “when I had reached so nigh to them”: the Italian compound past with ‘be’ becomes an English past perfect with “had”; the participle moves up beside the auxiliary.
- *gli atti loro* ‘the gestures of theirs’ → “their features”: the possessive precedes the noun, and Okey takes what could be made out to be their features rather than their gestures or bearing.
- *a me venivan certi* ‘to me came clear’ → “came distinctly to me”: the predicative adjective becomes an adverb, and the phrase naming the perceiver moves to the end.
- *per gli occhi fui di grave dolor munto* ‘through the eyes I was milked of heavy grief’ → “heavy grief was wrung from mine eyes”: what is extracted, rather than the person from whom it is extracted, becomes the passive subject; the route becomes the source, the article becomes a possessive, and “wrung” replaces the image of milking.

> 58 Di vil cilicio mi parean coperti,  

With coarse haircloth they seemed to me covered,

- *Di vil cilicio* ‘in mean haircloth’ → “With coarse haircloth”: the preposition changes, and an adjective of low worth or quality becomes one of texture.
- *mi parean* ‘to me they seemed’ → “they seemed to me”: the experiencer follows the verb.

> 59 e l' un sofferia l' altro con la spalla,  
> 60 e tutti dalla ripa eran sofferti.  

and one was supporting the other with the shoulder, and all were supported by the bank.

- *l' un sofferia l' altro* ‘the one supported the other’ → “one was supporting the other”: the first article disappears, and the imperfect is rendered by a progressive.
- *dalla ripa eran sofferti* ‘by the bank were supported’ → “were supported by the bank”: the agent follows the passive verb.

> 61 Così li ciechi, a cui la roba falla,  
> 62 stanno ai perdoni a chieder lor bisogna,  

Even so the blind, to whom means are lacking, sit at Pardons begging for their needs;

- *Così* ‘so’ → “Even so”: an intensifier is added.
- *la roba falla* ‘property fails’ → “means are lacking”: the singular collective noun becomes an articleless plural, and the verb becomes a phrase denoting a state.
- *stanno* ‘stay’ → “sit”: the English specifies a posture.
- *a chieder lor bisogna* ‘to ask for their need’ → “begging for their needs”: the infinitive of purpose becomes a participle of accompanying action, the asking is specified as begging, and the singular becomes plural.

> 63 e l' uno il capo sopra l' altro avvalla,  

and one sinks his head upon the other,

- *l' uno il capo sopra l' altro avvalla* ‘the one the head upon the other lowers’ → “one sinks his head upon the other”: the first article is dropped, the article with the body part becomes a possessive, and the verb moves from the end to before its object.

> 64 perchè in altrui pietà tosto si pogna,  

so that pity may quickly be awakened in others,

- *in altrui pietà tosto si pogna* ‘in others pity quickly may be placed’ → “pity may quickly be awakened in others”: the phrase of place moves to the end, the adverb follows the modal, and the passive subjunctive of placing becomes a modal passive of awakening.

> 65 non pur per lo sonar delle parole,  
> 66 ma per la vista che non meno agogna.  

not only by the sound of their words, but by their appearance which pleads not less.

- *per lo sonar delle parole* ‘by the sounding of the words’ → “by the sound of their words”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a simple noun, and the article before ‘words’ becomes a possessive.
- *per la vista* ‘by the sight’ → “by their appearance”: Okey specifies the sight as the way the people look and substitutes a possessive for the article.
- *che non meno agogna* ‘which no less longs’ → “which pleads not less”: the verb of desire is interpreted as expressing an appeal, and the comparison follows it.

> 67 E come agli orbi non approda il sole,  
> 68 così all' ombre, là 'v' io parlav' ora,  
> 69 luce del ciel di sè largir non vuole:  

And as to the blind the sun profits not, so to the shades there where I was now speaking, heaven’s light will not be bounteous of itself;

- *non approda il sole* ‘does not profit the sun’ → “the sun profits not”: the postponed subject moves before the verb, and the negative follows the verb.
- *parlav' ora* ‘I spoke now’ → “I was now speaking”: the imperfect becomes a progressive, with the adverb between auxiliary and participle.
- *luce del ciel* ‘light of heaven’ → “heaven’s light”: the dependent noun becomes a possessive preceding the head noun.
- *di sè largir non vuole* ‘of itself to bestow does not will’ → “will not be bounteous of itself”: the verb of willing moves before its complement and takes the negative after it; the infinitive of giving becomes “be” with an adjective, followed by “of itself”.

> 70 chè a tutte un fil di ferro il ciglio fora,  
> 71 e cuce sì, come a sparvier selvaggio  
> 72 si fa, però che queto non dimora.  

for all their eyelids an iron wire pierces and stitches up, even as is done to a wild hawk because it abideth not still.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *a tutte un fil di ferro il ciglio fora, e cuce sì* ‘for all of them a thread of iron the eyelid pierces, and sews thus’ → “all their eyelids an iron wire pierces and stitches up”: the dative referring to all the shades becomes a possessive with “all” modifying the plural “eyelids”; this object is placed before the subject. The material precedes “wire” instead of following ‘thread’; “up” marks completion rather than manner.
- *come a sparvier selvaggio si fa* ‘as to a hawk wild one does’ → “even as is done to a wild hawk”: “even as” carries the emphatic comparison; the impersonal construction becomes a passive, the recipient follows the verb, and an article is supplied and the adjective placed before the noun.
- *però che queto non dimora* ‘because quiet it does not remain’ → “because it abideth not still”: the predicative adjective moves to the end, and the negative follows the verb.

> 73 A me pareva andando fare oltraggio,  
> 74 veggendo altrui, non essendo veduto:  

I seemed to do them wrong as I went my way seeing others, not being seen;

- *A me pareva* ‘to me it seemed’ → “I seemed”: the experiencer becomes the grammatical subject.
- *andando* ‘going’ → “as I went my way”: the gerund becomes a finite temporal clause, with “my way” supplied; it moves after the act of doing wrong.
- *fare oltraggio* ‘to do wrong’ → “to do them wrong”: the persons wronged are made explicit.

> 75 per ch' io mi volsi al mio consiglio saggio.  

wherefore I turned me to my wise Counsel.

- *per ch'* ‘for which reason’ → “wherefore”: the prepositional relative becomes a single connective adverb.
- *io mi volsi* ‘I myself turned’ → “I turned me”: the reflexive pronoun follows the verb.
- *al mio consiglio saggio* ‘to my counsel wise’ → “to my wise Counsel”: the adjective precedes the noun.

## Lines 76–108 — A Sienese Shade Answers Dante

> 76 Ben sapev' ei, che volea dir lo muto;  

Well knew he what the dumb would say,

- *che volea dir lo muto* ‘what wished to say the silent one’ → “what the dumb would say”: the subject moves before the verb; “would” expresses the wish, and “the dumb” designates the one not speaking.

> 77 e però non attese mia domanda,  
> 78 ma disse: “Parla, e sii breve ed arguto.”  

and therefore awaited not my questioning, but said: “Speak and be brief and to the point.”

- *non attese* ‘did not await’ → “awaited not”: the negative follows the verb.
- *mia domanda* ‘my question’ → “my questioning”: the act of asking takes the place of the question.
- *ed arguto* ‘and pointed’ → “and to the point”: a prepositional phrase replaces the adjective.

> 79 Virgilio mi venia da quella banda  
> 80 della cornice, onde cader si puote,  
> 81 perchè da nulla sponda s' inghirlanda;  

Virgil was coming with me on that side of the cornice whence one may fall because it is surrounded by no parapet;

- *mi venia* ‘with me came’ → “was coming with me”: the dative of accompaniment becomes a phrase after the verb, and the imperfect is rendered by the progressive.
- *da quella banda* ‘from that side’ → “on that side”: English gives the position rather than using a preposition of origin.
- *cader si puote* ‘fall one may’ → “one may fall”: the impersonal subject and modal come before the infinitive.
- *da nulla sponda s' inghirlanda* ‘with no rim it garlands itself’ → “it is surrounded by no parapet”: the reflexive image becomes a plain passive; the phrase naming the enclosing edge follows the verb, and “parapet” makes the kind of edge specific.

> 82 dall' altra parte m' eran le devote  
> 83 ombre, che per l' orribile costura  
> 84 premevan sì che bagnavan le gote.  

on the other side of me were the devout shades, who, through the horrible seam, were pressing forth tears so that they bathed their cheeks.

- *dall' altra parte m' eran* ‘on the other side to me were’ → “on the other side of me were”: the dative attached to the verb becomes a complement of “side”.
- *premevan* ‘were pressing’ → “were pressing forth tears”: the direction and the object of the pressing are made explicit.
- *sì che bagnavan le gote* ‘so that they bathed the cheeks’ → “so that they bathed their cheeks”: the possessive replaces the definite article.

> 85 Volsimi a loro, ed: “O gente sicura,”  
> 86 incominciai, “di veder l' alto lume,  
> 87 che il disio vostro solo ha in sua cura;  

I turned me to them and began: “O people assured of seeing the Light above, which alone your desire hath in its care;

- *Volsimi* ‘turned-myself’ → “I turned me”: the enclitic reflexive becomes a separate pronoun.
- *ed … incominciai* ‘and … I began’ → “and began”: the reporting verb, which interrupts the address in Italian, moves before it.
- *l' alto lume* ‘the high light’ → “the Light above”: an adverb after the noun replaces the adjective before it.
- *che il disio vostro solo ha in sua cura* ‘which the desire of yours alone has in its care’ → “which alone your desire hath in its care”: the possessive moves before the noun; “alone” moves next to its antecedent’s relative pronoun, making its scope clear.

> 88 se tosto grazia resolva le schiume  
> 89 di vostra coscienza, sì che chiaro  
> 90 per essa scenda della mente il fiume,  

so may grace quickly clear away the scum of your conscience, that the stream of memory may descend clearly through it,

- *se tosto grazia resolva* ‘if soon grace dissolve’ → “so may grace quickly clear away”: the conditional form of the wish becomes an explicit optative; the adverb moves after the subject, and dissolution becomes removal.
- *le schiume* ‘the foams’ → “the scum”: a singular mass noun replaces the plural.
- *sì che* ‘so that’ → “that”: the result clause has no separate “so”.
- *chiaro per essa scenda della mente il fiume* ‘clear through it may descend of the mind the river’ → “the stream of memory may descend clearly through it”: the subject moves before the verb and its dependent phrase after the noun; “stream” renders ‘river’, and “memory” specifies a sense of ‘mind’. The adjective describing the river becomes an adverb of manner, and the phrase of passage moves to the end.

> 91 ditemi, chè mi fia grazioso e caro,  
> 92 s' anima è qui tra voi che sia latina;  

tell me (for to me ’twill be gracious and dear) if any soul be among you that is Italian,

- *s' anima è qui tra voi* ‘if a soul is here among you’ → “if any soul be among you”: “any” makes the indefinite force explicit, “be” replaces the indicative, and ‘here’ is not separately rendered.
- *che sia latina* ‘that may be Latin’ → “that is Italian”: the subjunctive becomes indicative, and “Italian” gives the intended geographical sense.

> 93 e forse a lei sarà buon, s' io l' apparo.”  

and perchance it will be good for him if I know of it.”

- *a lei sarà buon* ‘for her it will be good’ → “it will be good for him”: the beneficiary moves to the end; the feminine pronoun referring to the grammatically feminine ‘soul’ becomes masculine in English.
- *s' io l' apparo* ‘if I learn it’ → “if I know of it”: acquiring knowledge becomes having knowledge, and the direct object becomes a prepositional complement.

> 94 “O frate mio, ciascuna è cittadina  
> 95 d' una vera città; ma tu vuoi dire,  
> 96 che vivesse in Italia peregrina.”  

“O brother mine, each one is a citizen of a true city; but thou wouldest say, that lived a pilgrim in Italy.”

- *è cittadina* ‘is citizen’ → “is a citizen”: English supplies the indefinite article before the predicate noun; it does not mark the noun as feminine.
- *tu vuoi dire* ‘thou wishest to say’ → “thou wouldest say”: a modal construction replaces the present-tense verb of wishing with its infinitive.
- *che vivesse in Italia peregrina* ‘that lived in Italy as a pilgrim’ → “that lived a pilgrim in Italy”: the predicative description moves before the place phrase; English does not distinguish the Italian imperfect subjunctive here or mark the pilgrim as feminine.

> 97 Questo mi parve per risposta udire  
> 98 più innanzi alquanto, che là dov' io stava;  

This meseemed to hear for answer somewhat farther on than there where I was;

- *mi parve* ‘it seemed to me’ → “meseemed”: the impersonal verb and its dative are expressed in a single English form.
- *per risposta udire* ‘for answer to hear’ → “to hear for answer”: the infinitive precedes the phrase qualifying it.
- *più innanzi alquanto* ‘farther on somewhat’ → “somewhat farther on”: the degree modifier moves before the comparative.
- *io stava* ‘I stood’ → “I was”: the translation gives location without specifying posture.

> 99 ond' io mi feci ancor più là sentire.  

wherefore I made me heard yet more that way.

- *mi feci ancor più là sentire* ‘myself I made still farther that way to hear’ → “I made me heard yet more that way”: the reflexive follows the finite verb; the active infinitive with passive sense becomes a past participle, placed before the adverbial phrase.

> 100 Tra l' altre vidi un' ombra che aspettava  
> 101 in vista; e, se volesse alcun dir: “Come?”  
> 102 lo mento, a guisa d' orbo, in su levava.  

Among the others I saw a shade that was expectant in look, and if one would ask, “how so?” its chin it lifted up after the manner of the blind.

- *aspettava in vista* ‘was waiting in appearance’ → “was expectant in look”: an adjective with “was” replaces the verb of waiting; “look” specifies outward expression.
- *se volesse alcun dir* ‘if wished anyone to say’ → “if one would ask”: the indefinite subject moves before the verb, and saying becomes asking.
- *Come* ‘how’ → “how so”: “so” is supplied.
- *lo mento, a guisa d' orbo, in su levava* ‘the chin, in the manner of a blind person, upwards it lifted’ → “its chin it lifted up after the manner of the blind”: a possessive replaces the article; “up” follows the verb, and the comparison moves to the end. The generic singular becomes the collective “the blind”.

> 103 “Spirto,” diss' io, “che per salir ti dome,  

“Spirit,” said I, “that dost subdue thee to mount up;

- *per salir ti dome* ‘in order to ascend thyself thou subduest’ → “dost subdue thee to mount up”: the verb is expressed with auxiliary “dost”; the reflexive follows it, and the infinitive of purpose moves to the end.

> 104 se tu se' quelli che mi rispondesti,  
> 105 fammiti conto o per loco o per nome.”  

if thou art that one who answered me, make thyself known to me by place or by name.”

- *mi rispondesti* ‘to me thou answeredst’ → “answered me”: the recipient follows the verb as its object.
- *fammiti conto* ‘make to me thyself known’ → “make thyself known to me”: the two enclitics become separate complements, with the recipient last.
- *o per loco o per nome* ‘either by place or by name’ → “by place or by name”: the first member of the paired conjunction is left unmarked.

> 106 “I' fui Sanese,” rispose, “e con questi  
> 107 altri rimondo qui la vita ria,  
> 108 lagrimando a Colui che sè ne presti.  

“I was a Sienese,” it answered, “and with these others here do cleanse my sinful life, weeping unto Him that he lend himself to us.

- *Sanese* ‘Sienese’ → “a Sienese”: English uses the designation as a noun with an indefinite article.
- *rimondo qui* ‘I cleanse here’ → “here do cleanse”: the adverb precedes the verb, which is expressed with auxiliary “do”.
- *la vita ria* ‘the life wicked’ → “my sinful life”: the article becomes a possessive; “sinful” specifies the sense of the adjective and precedes the noun.
- *Colui* ‘that one’ → “Him”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *sè ne presti* ‘himself to us he lend’ → “he lend himself to us”: the reflexive and dative move after the verb, the latter becoming a prepositional complement.

## Lines 109–141 — Sapia’s Envy and Dante’s Fear

> 109 Savia non fui, avvegna che Sapia  
> 110 fossi chiamata, e fui degli altrui danni  
> 111 più lieta assai, che di ventura mia.  

Sapient was I not albeit Sapia I was named, and of others’ hurt I was far more glad than of mine own good fortune.

- *Savia non fui* ‘Wise I was not’ → “Sapient was I not”: the verb comes before the subject; ‘wise’ becomes “Sapient”, retaining the play on the name that follows.
- *avvegna che Sapia fossi chiamata* ‘although I were called Sapia’ → “albeit Sapia I was named”: the two-word conjunction becomes “albeit”; the name is kept in front, but the Italian imperfect subjunctive becomes an English indicative, and ‘called’ becomes “named”.
- *e fui degli altrui danni più lieta assai* ‘and I was of the harms of others more glad by far’ → “and of others’ hurt I was far more glad”: the phrase naming the cause of gladness moves before the verb; the plural becomes a singular mass noun, and the intensifier moves before the comparative.
- *di ventura mia* ‘of fortune mine’ → “of mine own good fortune”: the possessive comes first; “own” reinforces the contrast, and “good” makes explicit the favorable sense of *ventura*.

> 112 E perchè tu non credi ch' io t' inganni,  
> 113 odi se fui, com' io ti dico, folle.  

And that thou mayst not think I deceive thee, hear if I was mad as I tell thee.

- *perchè tu non credi* ‘in order that thou not believe’ → “that thou mayst not think”: “mayst” marks purpose, and ‘believe’ becomes “think”.
- *ch' io t' inganni* ‘that I thee deceive’ → “I deceive thee”: the conjunction drops out, and the object follows the verb.
- *se fui, com' io ti dico, folle* ‘if I was, as I to thee say, mad’ → “if I was mad as I tell thee”: the predicate adjective moves next to “was”, and ‘to thee say’ becomes a verb followed by its personal object.

> 114 Già discendendo l' arco de' miei anni,  
> 115 eran li cittadin miei presso a Colle  
> 116 in campo giunti coi loro avversari,  
> 117 ed io pregai Iddio di quel ch' ei volle.  

Already when the arc of my years was descending, my townsmen, hard by Colle, were joined in battle with their foes, and I prayed God for that which he had willed.

- *Già discendendo l' arco de' miei anni* ‘I already descending the arc of my years’ → “Already when the arc of my years was descending”: the gerund becomes a finite temporal clause, and the arc, rather than the speaker descending it, becomes the subject.
- *eran li cittadin miei* ‘were my fellow citizens’ → “my townsmen,”: the subject precedes the auxiliary, which is deferred until after the phrase locating them; the civic relationship is rendered by “townsmen”.
- *in campo giunti* ‘in the field met’ → “joined in battle”: the participle comes first, and the field is rendered by the action taking place there.
- *coi loro avversari* ‘with their adversaries’ → “with their foes”: a shorter term replaces ‘adversaries’.
- *Iddio di quel ch' ei volle* ‘God for that which he willed’ → “God for that which he had willed”: the simple past becomes a pluperfect.

> 118 Rotti fur quivi, e volti negli amari  
> 119 passi di fuga, e veggendo la caccia,  
> 120 letizia presi a tutte altre dispari;  

There were they routed, and rolled back in the bitter steps of flight, and seeing the chase I took joy exceeding all other;

- *Rotti fur quivi* ‘Broken were they there’ → “There were they routed”: the adverb comes first and the participle last; ‘broken’ is given its military sense.
- *volti negli amari passi di fuga* ‘turned into the bitter steps of flight’ → “rolled back in the bitter steps of flight”: “rolled back” gives a more forceful motion than ‘turned’, and directional ‘into’ becomes “in”.
- *letizia presi* ‘joy I took’ → “I took joy”: the object follows the verb.
- *a tutte altre dispari* ‘unlike all others’ → “exceeding all other”: an adjective of difference becomes a participle of superiority; the plural referring to other joys becomes elliptical singular “other” with ‘joy’ understood.

> 121 tanto ch' io volsi in su l' ardita faccia,  
> 122 gridando a Dio: ‘Omai più non ti temo,’  
> 123 come fa il merlo per poca bonaccia.  

so much, that I lifted up my impudent face, crying to God: ‘Now I fear thee no more,’ as the blackbird doth for a little fair weather.

- *io volsi in su l' ardita faccia* ‘I turned upward the bold face’ → “I lifted up my impudent face”: lifting replaces turning, the definite article becomes possessive, and “impudent” gives ‘bold’ a specifically reproachful sense.
- *Omai più non ti temo* ‘Henceforth no longer thee I fear’ → “Now I fear thee no more”: “Now” marks the starting point rather than the ensuing time; the object follows the verb, and the negative phrase comes last.
- *come fa il merlo* ‘as does the blackbird’ → “as the blackbird doth”: the subject moves before the verb.

> 124 Pace volli con Dio in su lo stremo  
> 125 della mia vita; ed ancor non sarebbe  
> 126 lo mio dover per penitenza scemo,  

I would have peace with God on the brink of my life; and my debt were not yet reduced by penitence,

- *Pace volli* ‘Peace I wanted’ → “I would have peace”: the object moves after the verb, and the past verb of willing is expressed by “would have” with “peace” as its object.
- *in su lo stremo della mia vita* ‘at the very end of my life’ → “on the brink of my life”: the extremity is rendered by the spatial image “brink”.
- *ed ancor non sarebbe lo mio dover per penitenza scemo* ‘and still would not be my due by penitence diminished’ → “and my debt were not yet reduced by penitence”: the subject moves before the verb; what is due becomes a “debt”, the conditional becomes irrealis “were”, ‘still not’ becomes “not yet”, and the phrase of means follows the participle.

> 127 se ciò non fosse che a memoria m' ebbe  
> 128 Pier Pettinagno in sue sante orazioni,  
> 129 a cui di me per caritate increbbe.  

had it not been that Peter the Combseller remembered me in his holy prayers, who in his charity did grieve for me.

- *se ciò non fosse che* ‘if it were not that’ → “had it not been that”: inversion replaces the explicit ‘if’, and the condition is put in the past perfect.
- *a memoria m' ebbe Pier Pettinagno* ‘in memory me had Pier Pettinagno’ → “Peter the Combseller remembered me”: the subject moves first, the byname is translated as an occupational title, and the verb with its prepositional phrase becomes a single verb followed by its object.
- *a cui di me per caritate increbbe* ‘to whom, on my account, out of charity, it gave grief’ → “who in his charity did grieve for me”: the person affected becomes the subject of a personal verb; the cause becomes “in his charity”, with a possessive supplied, and the phrase referring to the speaker moves to the end.

> 130 Ma tu chi se', che nostre condizioni  
> 131 vai domandando, e porti gli occhi sciolti,  
> 132 sì come io credo, e spirando ragioni?”  

But who art thou that goest asking of our state, and bearest thine eyes unsewn, as I believe, and breathing dost speak?”

- *tu chi se'* ‘thou who art’ → “who art thou”: English puts the interrogative first and inverts verb and subject.
- *nostre condizioni vai domandando* ‘our conditions thou goest asking about’ → “goest asking of our state”: what is asked about moves after the verbal phrase, is introduced by “of”, and becomes singular.
- *gli occhi sciolti* ‘the eyes loose’ → “thine eyes unsewn”: the article becomes a possessive, and the general ‘loose’ is made specific.
- *sì come io credo* ‘just as I believe’ → “as I believe”: the reinforcing adverb is not separately rendered.
- *ragioni* ‘thou discoursest’ → “dost speak”: a simple verb becomes a less specific verb with an auxiliary.

> 133 “Gli occhi,” diss' io, “mi fieno ancor qui tolti;  
> 134 ma picciol tempo, chè poca è l' offesa  
> 135 fatta per esser con invidia volti.  

“Mine eyes,” said I, “from me here shall yet be taken; but for short time, for small is the offence they did through being turned in envy.

- *Gli occhi* ‘The eyes’ → “Mine eyes”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *mi fieno ancor qui tolti* ‘to me shall be yet here taken away’ → “from me here shall yet be taken”: the affected-person dative becomes “from me”; “here” moves before the auxiliary, and “yet” stands between the auxiliaries.
- *ma picciol tempo* ‘but a short time’ → “but for short time”: “for” explicitly marks duration.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *fatta* ‘done’ → “they did”: the passive participle modifying ‘offence’ becomes an active relative clause with the eyes as subject.
- *per esser con invidia volti* ‘through being with envy turned’ → “through being turned in envy”: the phrase of manner follows the participle, and ‘with’ becomes “in”.

> 136 Troppa è più la paura, ond' è sospesa  
> 137 l' anima mia, del tormento di sotto,  
> 138 che già lo incarco di laggiù mi pesa.”  

Greater far is the fear wherewith my soul is suspended, of the torment below, for even now the burden down there weighs upon me.”

- *Troppa è più la paura* ‘Far is greater the fear’ → “Greater far is the fear”: the separated elements of the intensified comparative are brought together before the verb, with the comparative first.
- *ond' è sospesa l' anima mia* ‘by which is suspended my soul’ → “wherewith my soul is suspended”: the subject precedes the passive verb rather than following it.
- *del tormento di sotto* ‘of the torment of below’ → “of the torment below”: the adverb of place modifies the noun without a preposition.
- *che* ‘so that’ → “for”: the final clause is presented as grounds for the comparison rather than as its consequence.
- *già lo incarco di laggiù mi pesa* ‘already the burden of down there on me weighs’ → “even now the burden down there weighs upon me”: “even now” stresses present experience; the preposition before the place adverb drops out, and the dative becomes a prepositional phrase after the verb.

> 139 Ed ella a me: “Chi t' ha dunque condotto  
> 140 quassù tra noi, se giù ritornar credi?”  

And she to me: “Who then hath led thee up here among us, if thou thinkest to return below?”

- *t' ha dunque condotto* ‘thee has then led’ → “then hath led thee”: “then” precedes the auxiliary, and the object follows the participle.
- *se giù ritornar credi* ‘if below to return thou thinkest’ → “if thou thinkest to return below”: the finite verb comes before its infinitive, and the directional adverb comes last.

> 141 Ed io: “Costui ch' è meco, e non fa motto:  

And I: “He who is with me and saith no word;

- *Costui* ‘This one’ → “He”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *non fa motto* ‘does not make a word’ → “saith no word”: ‘make’ becomes a verb of speaking, and the negative attaches to the noun rather than the verb.

## Lines 142–154 — A Request for Prayers and Remembrance

> 142 e vivo sono: e però mi richiedi,  
> 143 spirito eletto, se tu vuoi ch' io mova  
> 144 di là per te ancor li mortai piedi.”  

and I am living, and therefore do thou ask of me, spirit elect, if thou wouldst that yonder I lift yet for thee my mortal feet.”

- *vivo sono* ‘alive I am’ → “I am living”: the predicate adjective is rendered by “living” and follows the verb.
- *e però mi richiedi* ‘and therefore of me ask’ → “and therefore do thou ask of me”: the imperative takes an auxiliary and an explicit subject, and “of me” follows the verb.
- *se tu vuoi* ‘if thou wishest’ → “if thou wouldst”: the present verb of wishing becomes a modal form expressing willingness or desire.
- *ch' io mova di là* ‘that I move yonder’ → “that yonder I lift”: the adverb of place comes before the subject, and the general verb of motion becomes “lift”.
- *li mortai piedi* ‘the mortal feet’ → “my mortal feet”: a possessive replaces the definite article.

> 145 “Oh questa è ad udir sì cosa nuova,”  
> 146 rispose, “che gran segno è che Dio t' ami;  

“Oh this is so new a thing to hear,” she answered, “that ’tis a great token that God loveth thee;

- *ad udir sì cosa nuova* ‘to hear so new a thing’ → “so new a thing to hear”: the infinitive follows the noun phrase instead of preceding it, and “new” comes before “thing”; English also supplies the indefinite article.
- *che gran segno è* ‘that a great sign it is’ → “that ’tis a great token”: the predicate follows the copula rather than preceding it, the indefinite article is supplied, and “token” renders *segno*.
- *che Dio t' ami* ‘that God thee love’ → “that God loveth thee”: the Italian subjunctive becomes an English indicative, and the object follows the verb.

> 147 però col prego tuo talor mi giova.  

therefore profit me sometimes with thy prayers.

- *col prego tuo talor mi giova* ‘with thy prayer sometimes me benefit’ → “profit me sometimes with thy prayers”: the imperative moves from last to first, its object follows it, and the adverb precedes the phrase of means. The singular prayer becomes plural, with the possessive before the noun.

> 148 E chieggioti per quel che tu più brami,  
> 149 se mai calchi la terra di Toscana,  
> 150 che a' miei propinqui tu ben mi rinfami.  

And I beseech thee by all thou most desirest, if e’er thou tread the land of Tuscany, that thou restore my fame among my kinsfolk.

- *E chieggioti* ‘and I ask thee’ → “And I beseech thee”: “beseech” gives the request a more imploring force.
- *per quel che tu più brami* ‘by that which thou most desirest’ → “by all thou most desirest”: the demonstrative becomes the more inclusive “all”, and the relative pronoun is left unexpressed.
- *che a' miei propinqui tu ben mi rinfami* ‘that to my kinsfolk thou duly bring me back into repute’ → “that thou restore my fame among my kinsfolk”: the people addressed become the circle “among” whom the fame is restored, and their phrase moves to the end. The verb and its personal object are recast as “restore” with “my fame” as its object; *ben* has no separate rendering.

> 151 Tu li vedrai tra quella gente vana  
> 152 che spera in Talamone, e perderagli  
> 153 più di speranza che a trovar la Diana;  

Thou wilt see them among that vain people who put their trust in Talamone, and will lose there more hopes than in finding the Diana;

- *Tu li vedrai* ‘thou them wilt see’ → “Thou wilt see them”: the object follows the verb.
- *quella gente vana* ‘that people vain’ → “that vain people”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *che spera* ‘which hopes’ → “who put their trust”: the singular collective is construed as persons in the plural, and the verb of hoping becomes a phrase with “trust” as its noun.
- *più di speranza* ‘more of hope’ → “more hopes”: the partitive phrase with an abstract singular becomes a directly quantified plural.
- *a trovar* ‘at finding’ → “in finding”: the preposition changes, and a gerund renders the infinitive.

> 154 ma più vi perderanno gli ammiragli.”  

but the admirals shall lose most there.”

- *più vi perderanno gli ammiragli* ‘more there will lose the admirals’ → “the admirals shall lose most there”: the subject moves from last to first, the locative moves to the end, and the comparative ‘more’ is interpreted as a superlative.

## Summary — Close in Image, Freer in Syntax

Okey often keeps Dante’s images while changing the order in which their parts appear. In *sono tratte da amor le corde della ferza*, the cords, the whip, and love all remain, but the subject, “the cords of the whip”, moves from the end to the beginning. Elsewhere it is the verb that moves: *letizia presi*, ‘joy I took’, becomes “I took joy”. Yet Okey does not make the order uniformly idiomatic. “Well knew he” keeps the inversion of *Ben sapev’ ei*, and “the hill around” even postpones the word that comes first in *dintorno il poggio*.

Changes of construction often shift the emphasis without much change of sense. The gerund *salendo*, ‘in climbing’, becomes the noun phrase “by our ascent”, while *gridar* after a verb of hearing becomes “a cry”, a change Okey maintains when the infinitive recurs. A larger shift comes in *fui di grave dolor munto*: where Dante makes the speaker the subject, Okey makes grief itself the subject of “was wrung from mine eyes”. Word choice can likewise make a detail more specific: *più tosto*, ‘more quickly’, becomes “more sharply” of a bend, and *sciolti*, ‘loose’, becomes “unsewn” of the eyes. At other points closeness matters more than ordinary English diction: “Sapient” for *Savia* preserves the echo of *Sapia*.

Some additions make explicit what Italian leaves to be inferred: “pressing forth tears” gives *premevan* both a direction and an object. Conversely, *qui* has no separate equivalent in “among you”, and the repeated *par* is understood rather than repeated in “and even so the way”. Two quieter adjustments run throughout: English regularly supplies subject pronouns that Italian verb endings suffice to indicate, and drops the article before a possessive, as in *lo mio dover*, “my debt”. Distinct from that omission is the frequent use of an English possessive where Italian has only an article: *gli occhi* can thus become “his eyes”, “thine eyes”, or “mine eyes”, according to whose eyes are meant.
