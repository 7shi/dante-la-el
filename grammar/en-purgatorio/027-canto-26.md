# Purgatorio 26

## Lines 1–18 — The Shades Notice Dante’s Shadow

> 1 Mentre che sì per l' orlo, uno innanzi altro,  
> 2 ce n' andavamo, e spesso il buon maestro  
> 3 diceva: “Guarda; giovi ch' io ti scaltro.”  

While we were thus advancing, one in front of the other, along the brink, often the good Master said: “Give heed, let my skill avail thee.”

- *Mentre che sì … ce n’ andavamo* ‘while thus … we were going on our way’ → “While we were thus advancing”: the verb is brought forward, and the pronominal expression for going on one’s way becomes “advancing”.
- *per l’ orlo, uno innanzi altro* ‘along the brink, one before another’ → “one in front of the other, along the brink”: the phrases exchange places, and the second walker is made definite.
- *e spesso* ‘and often’ → “often”: the conjunction is dropped.
- *diceva* ‘was saying’ → “said”: the imperfect becomes a simple past, with “often” carrying the sense of repetition.
- *Guarda* ‘Look’ → “Give heed”: the command to look becomes an injunction to pay attention.
- *giovi ch’ io ti scaltro* ‘may it profit that I make thee wary’ → “let my skill avail thee”: the wish is expressed with “let”; the clause about what the speaker does for his hearer is replaced by a noun phrase denoting the speaker’s own skill, and the hearer becomes the object of “avail”.

> 4 Feria mi il sole in su l' omero destro,  
> 5 che già, raggiando, tutto l' occidente  
> 6 mutava in bianco aspetto di cilestro;  

On my right shoulder the sun was beating, that already with his rays was changing the whole face of the west from azure to white;

- *Feria mi il sole in su l’ omero destro* ‘was striking me the sun upon the shoulder right’ → “On my right shoulder the sun was beating”: the phrase of place comes first, the subject now precedes the verb, and the person struck is expressed by a possessive with the body part; the adjective moves before the noun.
- *raggiando* ‘radiating’ → “with his rays”: the gerund becomes a prepositional phrase of means.
- *tutto l’ occidente mutava in bianco aspetto di cilestro* ‘the whole west it was changing to a white appearance from sky-blue’ → “was changing the whole face of the west from azure to white”: the verb comes before its object; the word for appearance is recast as “face” and made part of the object rather than of the resulting state. The original colour now precedes the resulting colour.

> 7 ed io facea con l' ombra più rovente  
> 8 parer la fiamma; e pure a tanto indizio  
> 9 vid' io molt' ombre, andando, poner mente.  

and with my shadow, ruddier I made the flames appear, and even at so slight a sign many shades I saw, as they passed, give heed.

- *io facea con l’ ombra più rovente parer la fiamma* ‘I was making with the shadow more glowing appear the flame’ → “with my shadow, ruddier I made the flames appear”: the means and the comparative move before the subject, the shadow is identified by a possessive, and the imperfect becomes a simple past. “Ruddier” specifies colour; the singular flame becomes plural and precedes “appear”.
- *pure a tanto indizio* ‘even to such an indication’ → “even at so slight a sign”: the preposition changes, and the translation interprets the degree indicated by *tanto* as slightness.
- *vid’ io molt’ ombre* ‘saw I many shades’ → “many shades I saw”: the object moves to the front, and the subject precedes the verb.
- *andando* ‘going’ → “as they passed”: the gerund becomes a finite temporal clause and the motion is rendered as passing rather than simply going.

> 10 Questa fu la cagion che diede inizio  
> 11 loro a parlar di me; e cominciarsi  
> 12 a dir: “Colui non par corpo fittizio.”  

This was the cause which gave them an opening to speak of me; and one to the other they began to say: “He doth not seem a shadowy body.”

- *diede inizio loro a parlar* ‘gave a beginning to them to speak’ → “gave them an opening to speak”: the recipient moves before the direct object, and a beginning is rendered as an occasion to speak.
- *cominciarsi a dir* ‘they began to say to one another’ → “one to the other they began to say”: the reciprocal attached to the finite verb is expanded and placed first.
- *Colui* ‘that one’ → “He”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun.
- *non par* ‘seems not’ → “doth not seem”: English forms the negative with an auxiliary.
- *corpo fittizio* ‘body fictive’ → “a shadowy body”: an article is supplied, the adjective moves before the noun, and “shadowy” interprets the body’s being merely apparent rather than translating the adjective directly.

> 13 Poi verso me, quanto potevan farsi,  
> 14 certi si feron, sempre con riguardo  
> 15 di non uscir dove non fossero arsi.  

Then certain of them made towards me, so far as they could, ever on their guard not to come forth where they would not be burned.

- *verso me … certi si feron* ‘towards me … certain ones made their way’ → “certain of them made towards me”: the subject and verb are brought ahead of the direction, the group from which the subject is drawn is made explicit, and the reflexive idiom becomes intransitive “made”.
- *quanto potevan farsi* ‘as far as they could make their way’ → “so far as they could”: the qualification follows the completed main clause, and the repeated verb of motion is left understood.
- *sempre con riguardo* ‘always with caution’ → “ever on their guard”: the phrase with an abstract noun becomes a possessive idiom.
- *dove non fossero arsi* ‘where they were not burned’ → “where they would not be burned”: the imperfect subjunctive describing a possible place is rendered with conditional “would”.

> 16 “O tu che vai, non per esser più tardo,  
> 17 ma forse reverente, agli altri dopo,  
> 18 rispondi a me che in sete ed in foco ardo;  

“O thou that goest behind the others, not for being slacker but perchance for reverence, make answer unto me who in thirst and fire do burn;

- *agli altri dopo* ‘after the others’ → “behind the others”: the spatial relation is expressed by a preposition before its object, and the whole phrase is moved next to “goest”, ahead of the explanation.
- *più tardo* ‘more slow’ → “slacker”: the comparative is expressed by an ending, with a shift from slowness to slackness.
- *ma forse reverente* ‘but perhaps reverent’ → “but perchance for reverence”: the adjective describing the addressee becomes a noun in a phrase explicitly giving his motive.
- *rispondi* ‘answer’ → “make answer”: the single verb becomes a verb and noun.
- *in sete ed in foco* ‘in thirst and in fire’ → “in thirst and fire”: the preposition is not repeated.
- *ardo* ‘I burn’ → “do burn”: the present is expressed with auxiliary “do”.

## Lines 19–51 — The Shades’ Question and Greeting

> 19 nè solo a me la tua risposta è uopo:  
> 20 chè tutti questi n' hanno maggior sete  
> 21 che d' acqua fredda Indo o Etiopo.  

nor alone to me is thine answer needful, for all these have greater thirst for it than Indian or Ethiop for cold water.

- *la tua risposta è uopo* ‘thine answer is a need’ → “is thine answer needful”: the verb comes before the subject, and the noun of necessity becomes an adjective.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *n’ hanno maggior sete* ‘for it have greater thirst’ → “have greater thirst for it”: the complement expressed by the pronoun moves after “thirst”.
- *che d’ acqua fredda Indo o Etiopo* ‘than for water cold Indian or Ethiop’ → “than Indian or Ethiop for cold water”: the subjects of the implied verb move before the prepositional phrase, and the adjective comes before its noun.

> 22 Dinne com' è che fai di te parete  
> 23 al sol, come se tu non fossi ancora  
> 24 di morte entrato dentro dalla rete.”  

Tell us how it is that thou makest of thee a wall against the sun, as if thou wert not yet caught within death’s net.”

- *com’ è* ‘how is’ → “how it is”: English uses an impersonal subject before the verb.
- *parete al sol* ‘wall to the sun’ → “a wall against the sun”: English supplies the indefinite article and makes the obstructive force of the preposition explicit.
- *non fossi ancora di morte entrato dentro dalla rete* ‘hadst not yet entered inside the net of death’ → “thou wert not yet caught within death’s net”: an active pluperfect of entering becomes a passive of capture; the equivalent of “within” thus marks position rather than the endpoint of motion. The dependent *di morte*, separated from its noun in Italian, becomes a possessive immediately before “net”.

> 25 Sì mi parlava un d' essi, ed io mi fora  
> 26 già manifesto, s' io non fossi atteso  
> 27 ad altra novità ch' apparve allora:  

Thus spake one of them to me, and already would I have revealed myself, had I not been intent on another strange thing which then appeared;

- *mi parlava un d’ essi* ‘to me was speaking one of them’ → “spake one of them to me”: the indirect object moves after the subject, and the Italian imperfect is rendered by a simple past.
- *io mi fora già manifesto* ‘I should already have made myself manifest’ → “already would I have revealed myself”: the adverb is brought to the front, the auxiliary precedes the subject, and making oneself manifest becomes revealing oneself.
- *s’ io non fossi atteso* ‘if I had not been intent’ → “had I not been intent”: inversion replaces the conditional conjunction.
- *altra novità* ‘another novelty’ → “another strange thing”: a quality expressed in the noun becomes an adjective qualifying “thing”.
- *ch’ apparve allora* ‘which appeared then’ → “which then appeared”: the adverb moves before the verb.

> 28 chè per lo mezzo del cammino acceso  
> 29 venia gente col viso incontro a questa,  
> 30 la qual mi fece a rimirar sospeso.  

for through the midst of the fiery path, people were coming with their faces opposite to these, who made me pause in wonderment.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the causal clause is introduced by a coordinating conjunction.
- *cammino acceso* ‘path aflame’ → “fiery path”: a participial adjective after the noun becomes an adjective before it.
- *venia gente* ‘was coming a company’ → “people were coming”: the collective singular takes plural agreement in English; the subject moves before the verb, and the progressive renders the imperfect.
- *col viso incontro a questa* ‘with the face facing this one’ → “with their faces opposite to these”: the singular with the definite article becomes a plural with a possessive, and the singular demonstrative referring to a company becomes plural.
- *la qual mi fece a rimirar sospeso* ‘which made me pause to gaze’ → “who made me pause in wonderment”: the English relative treats the company as persons; the adjective denoting suspension becomes the verb “pause”, and gazing becomes an expression of wonder.

> 31 Lì veggio d' ogni parte farsi presta  
> 32 ciascun' ombra, e baciarsi una con una,  
> 33 senza restar, contente a breve festa:  

There I see on either side each shade make haste, and one kiss the other without staying, satisfied with short greeting:

- *d’ ogni parte* ‘on every side’ → “on either side”: the English specifies a pair of sides.
- *farsi presta ciascun’ ombra* ‘make itself ready each shade’ → “each shade make haste”: the subject moves before the infinitive, and an English verbal idiom replaces the reflexive verb and adjective.
- *baciarsi una con una* ‘kiss each other, one with one’ → “one kiss the other”: reciprocity is conveyed by the subject and direct object instead of a reflexive pronoun and a phrase with *con*.
- *senza restar* ‘without to stay’ → “without staying”: English uses the gerund after the preposition.
- *contente a breve festa* ‘content with brief rejoicing’ → “satisfied with short greeting”: “satisfied” renders the feminine plural adjective without marking number, and “greeting” gives the particular form the rejoicing takes.

> 34 così per entro loro schiera bruna  
> 35 s' ammusa l' una con l' altra formica,  
> 36 forse ad espiar lor via e lor fortuna.  

even so within their dark battalions one ant rubs muzzle with another, perchance to spy out their way and their fortune.

- *così* ‘so’ → “even so”: “even” reinforces the comparison.
- *per entro* ‘through within’ → “within”: one preposition replaces the compound expression.
- *schiera bruna* ‘dark troop’ → “dark battalions”: the singular becomes plural, the military term is more specific, and the adjective moves before the noun.
- *s’ ammusa l’ una con l’ altra formica* ‘muzzles one another, the one ant with the other’ → “one ant rubs muzzle with another”: the reciprocal verb becomes a verb and noun; “one ant” is placed together before the verb, and “another” replaces the definite ‘the other’.
- *ad espiar* ‘to investigate’ → “to spy out”: a phrasal verb renders the infinitive.

> 37 Tosto che parton l' accoglienza amica,  
> 38 prima che il primo passo lì trascorra,  
> 39 sopragridar ciascuna s' affatica;  

Soon as they break off the friendly greeting, ere the first step there speeds onward, each one strives to shout loudest,

- *parton l’ accoglienza amica* ‘they end the welcome friendly’ → “they break off the friendly greeting”: a phrasal verb renders the ending of the action, “greeting” specifies the welcome, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *prima che il primo passo lì trascorra* ‘before the first step there pass on’ → “ere the first step there speeds onward”: “ere” introduces the clause without a separate equivalent for *che*; an indicative replaces the subjunctive, and “speeds” emphasizes the swiftness of the movement.
- *sopragridar ciascuna s’ affatica* ‘to outshout each one exerts herself’ → “each one strives to shout loudest”: the infinitive moves after the finite verb; the reflexive becomes the intransitive “strives”, and a superlative adverb conveys the competitive force of the prefix.

> 40 la nuova gente: “Soddoma e Gomorra,”  
> 41 e l' altra: “Nella vacca entra Pasife,  
> 42 perchè il torello a sua lussuria corra.”  

the new people, “Sodom and Gomorrah,” and the other: “Pasiphaë enters the cow that the young bull may haste to her lust.”

- *Nella vacca entra Pasife* ‘Into the cow enters Pasiphaë’ → “Pasiphaë enters the cow”: the subject moves to the front, and the prepositional phrase becomes a direct object after the verb.
- *perchè il torello a sua lussuria corra* ‘in order that the young bull to her lust run’ → “that the young bull may haste to her lust”: “that” with the modal “may” renders the purpose conjunction and subjunctive; running becomes hastening, and the goal follows the verb.

> 43 Poi come gru, ch' alle montagne Rife  
> 44 volasser parte, e parte in ver l' arene,  
> 45 queste del gel, quelle del sole schife:  

Then like cranes that should fly, some to the Rhipean mountains, others towards the sands; these shy of the frost, those of the sun,

- *ch’ alle montagne Rife volasser parte* ‘that to the mountains Rhipean might fly in part’ → “that should fly, some to the Rhipean mountains”: the modal and infinitive render the imperfect subjunctive; the verb is brought forward, “some” designates a portion of the birds, and the adjective precedes “mountains”.
- *e parte* ‘and in part’ → “others”: a pronoun designates the second portion, and the conjunction is left out.
- *queste del gel, quelle del sole schife* ‘these of the frost, those of the sun, averse’ → “these shy of the frost, those of the sun”: “shy of” renders aversion; the shared adjective moves into the first phrase and is understood in the second.

> 46 l' una gente sen va, l' altra sen viene,  
> 47 e tornan lagrimando ai primi canti,  
> 48 ed al gridar che più lor si conviene;  

the one people passes on, the other comes away, and weeping they return to their former chants, and to the cry which most befits them;

- *sen va* ‘goes away’ → “passes on”: the English idiom presents departure as continued passage.
- *sen viene* ‘comes along’ → “comes away”: the English particle gives the pronominal verb a sense of movement away.
- *tornan lagrimando* ‘they return weeping’ → “weeping they return”: the participle moves before the finite clause.
- *ai primi canti* ‘to the first songs’ → “to their former chants”: the definite article becomes a possessive, “former” relates the songs to the preceding action, and “chants” specifies the manner of singing.
- *al gridar* ‘to the shouting’ → “to the cry”: a substantivized infinitive denoting an action becomes a noun for the utterance.
- *che più lor si conviene* ‘which more to them is fitting’ → “which most befits them”: the comparative is read as a superlative, and a transitive verb with a following direct object replaces the pronominal verb with a preceding indirect object.

> 49 e raccostarsi a me, come davanti,  
> 50 essi medesmi che m' avean pregato,  
> 51 attenti ad ascoltar nei lor sembianti.  

and those very same who had entreated me, drew close to me as before, intent on listening in their appearance.

- *raccostarsi a me, come davanti* ‘drew near again to me, as before’ → “drew close to me as before”: the verb and its complements move after the entire subject and relative clause. The sense of ‘again’ in the prefix is left to “as before”; the contracted reflexive past form is rendered by the nonreflexive “drew close”.
- *essi medesmi* ‘they themselves’ → “those very same”: a demonstrative reinforced by “very same” replaces the emphatic personal pronoun.
- *che m’ avean pregato* ‘who me had entreated’ → “who had entreated me”: the object pronoun moves after the verb.
- *attenti ad ascoltar* ‘intent to listen’ → “intent on listening”: a preposition and gerund replace the infinitive construction.
- *nei lor sembianti* ‘in their looks’ → “in their appearance”: a singular abstract noun replaces the plural.

## Lines 52–84 — Dante’s Living Body and the Souls’ Confession

> 52 Io, che due volte avea visto lor grato,  
> 53 incominciai: “O anime sicure  
> 54 d' aver, quando che sia, di pace stato,  

I, who twice had seen their desire, began: “O souls, certain of having, whenever it may be, a state of peace,

- *due volte* ‘two times’ → “twice”: an adverb replaces the counted noun.
- *lor grato* ‘their pleasure’ → “their desire”: Okey takes what would please them as what they wish for.
- *quando che sia* ‘whenever it be’ → “whenever it may be”: the modal “may” conveys the Italian subjunctive.
- *di pace stato* ‘of peace a state’ → “a state of peace”: the noun precedes its complement in English.

> 55 non son rimase acerbe nè mature  
> 56 le membra mie di là, ma son qui meco  
> 57 col sangue suo e con le sue giunture.  

my members have not remained yonder, green or ripe, but here are with me, with their blood and with their joints.

- *non son rimase … le membra mie di là* ‘have not remained … the members of mine yonder’ → “my members have not remained yonder”: the delayed subject moves before the verb, the possessive moves before the noun, and the English perfect takes “have” rather than ‘be’.
- *acerbe nè mature* ‘unripe nor ripe’ → “green or ripe”: “green” renders ‘unripe’; the alternatives follow “yonder” and are linked by “or” under the preceding negation, rather than by a negative conjunction.
- *ma son qui meco* ‘but are here with me’ → “but here are with me”: the adverb precedes the verb.

> 58 Quinci su vo per non esser più cieco;  

Hence upward I go to be blind no longer;

- *per non esser più cieco* ‘in order not to be blind any more’ → “to be blind no longer”: the infinitive alone marks purpose, and the negation is gathered into “no longer” after the adjective.

> 59 donna è di sopra che n' acquista grazia,  
> 60 per che il mortal pel vostro mondo reco.  

there is a lady above who winneth grace for us, wherefore I bring my mortal body through your world.

- *donna è di sopra* ‘a lady is above’ → “there is a lady above”: “there” introduces an existential construction, with the noun after the verb.
- *n' acquista grazia* ‘for us acquires grace’ → “winneth grace for us”: the unstressed pronoun becomes a prepositional phrase after the object, and ‘acquires’ becomes “winneth”.
- *il mortal pel vostro mondo reco* ‘the mortal through your world I bring’ → “I bring my mortal body through your world”: the verb moves before its object; the substantivized adjective is expanded with “body”, and the definite article becomes a possessive.

> 61 Ma, se la vostra maggior voglia sazia  
> 62 tosto divegna, sì che il ciel v' alberghi,  
> 63 ch' è pien d' amore e più ampio si spazia,  

But—so may your greater desire soon be satisfied, so that the heaven may house you which is filled with love and broadest spreads—

- *se la vostra maggior voglia sazia tosto divegna* ‘if your greater desire satisfied soon become’ → “so may your greater desire soon be satisfied”: the wish cast as a condition becomes a direct wish with “may”; “soon” precedes the predicate, and ‘become’ is rendered by “be”.
- *sì che il ciel v' alberghi* ‘so that the heaven you lodge’ → “so that the heaven may house you”: “may” renders the subjunctive, and the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *pien d' amore* ‘full of love’ → “filled with love”: a participle with “with” replaces the adjective with ‘of’.
- *più ampio si spazia* ‘more widely extends itself’ → “broadest spreads”: the comparative is taken in a superlative sense, and the Italian pronominal verb becomes an intransitive English verb.

> 64 ditemi, acciocchè ancor carte ne verghi,  
> 65 chi siete voi, e chi è quella turba  
> 66 che se ne va di retro ai vostri terghi?”  

tell me that I may yet trace it on paper, who are ye and what is that throng which is going away behind your backs?”

- *acciocchè ancor carte ne verghi* ‘in order that yet pages I may inscribe of it’ → “that I may yet trace it on paper”: “may” conveys the subjunctive, and “yet” comes between auxiliary and verb. The pages, the direct object of ‘inscribe’, become the material “on paper”, while *ne* becomes the direct object “it”.
- *e chi è quella turba* ‘and who is that throng’ → “and what is that throng”: “what” replaces the personal interrogative.
- *se ne va* ‘goes away’ → “is going away”: the English present is progressive.

> 67 Non altrimenti stupido si turba  
> 68 lo montanaro, e rimirando ammuta,  
> 69 quando rozzo e salvatico s' inurba,  
> 70 che ciascun' ombra fece in sua paruta;  

Not otherwise the dazed highlander grows troubled and stares about speechless, when rough and savage he enters the city, than each shade did in its appearance;

- *stupido si turba lo montanaro* ‘dazed, grows troubled the mountaineer’ → “the dazed highlander grows troubled”: the subject moves before the verb; ‘dazed’, predicated of the mountaineer as he reacts, is attached directly to “highlander”.
- *rimirando ammuta* ‘looking about, falls silent’ → “stares about speechless”: looking becomes the finite action, intensified to “stares”, while the change into silence becomes the adjective “speechless”.
- *s' inurba* ‘betakes himself into town’ → “he enters the city”: the pronominal verb becomes a transitive verb with an explicit noun object.

> 71 ma poichè furon di stupore scarche,  
> 72 lo qual negli alti cor tosto s' attuta,  

but after they were unladen of their bewilderment, which in lofty hearts soon is calmed,

- *poichè furon di stupore scarche* ‘after they were of astonishment free’ → “after they were unladen of their bewilderment”: “unladen” precedes its complement; “bewilderment” interprets the astonishment, and “their” is supplied.
- *s' attuta* ‘subsides’ → “is calmed”: the pronominal verb is rendered by a passive.

> 73 “Beato te, che delle nostre marche,”  
> 74 ricominciò colei che pria m' inchiese,  
> 75 “per viver meglio, esperienza inbarche!  

“Blessed thou,” began again the shade that first did ask of me, “who, for a holier life, art embarking knowledge of our borders!

- *che delle nostre marche* ‘who of our borders’ → “who” … “of our borders”: both the relative pronoun and the complement are postponed until after the intervening speech tag; the complement now follows “knowledge”.
- *ricominciò colei* ‘began again she’ → “began again the shade”: the feminine demonstrative pronoun is replaced by an explicit noun.
- *pria m' inchiese* ‘earlier questioned me’ → “first did ask of me”: “first” renders ‘earlier’, the simple past becomes “did ask”, and the object pronoun becomes a following phrase with “of”.
- *per viver meglio* ‘in order to live better’ → “for a holier life”: the infinitive becomes a noun, and the adverb becomes an adjective with a specifically moral sense.
- *esperienza inbarche* ‘experience thou embarkest’ → “art embarking knowledge”: “knowledge” renders ‘experience’; it follows the verb, which becomes progressive.

> 76 La gente che non vien con noi offese  
> 77 di ciò per che già Cesar, trionfando,  
> 78 ‘Regina’ contra sè chiamar s' intese:  

The people who come not with us offended in that for which Cæsar of old in his triumph heard ‘Regina’ called out against him;

- *La gente che non vien con noi offese* ‘the people that comes not with us offended’ → “The people who come not with us offended”: the Italian collective is grammatically singular; English treats it as plural, with personal “who” and plural “come”.
- *di ciò per che* ‘of that for which’ → “in that for which”: the complement of the offence takes “in” rather than ‘of’.
- *già Cesar* ‘of old Cæsar’ → “Cæsar of old”: the temporal expression moves after the name.
- *trionfando* ‘triumphing’ → “in his triumph”: a prepositional phrase with a possessive replaces the gerund.
- *‘Regina’ contra sè chiamar s' intese* ‘“Regina” against himself called he heard himself’ → “heard ‘Regina’ called out against him”: “heard” moves first and loses its reflexive; the active infinitive becomes the participle “called out”, and the phrase with ‘against’ follows it, with an ordinary rather than reflexive pronoun.

> 79 però si parton ‘Soddoma’ gridando,  
> 80 rimproverando a sè, com' hai udito,  
> 81 ed aiutan l' arsura vergognando.  

therefore they part from us crying out ‘Sodom’ reproving themselves as thou hast heard, and aid the burning by their shame.

- *si parton* ‘they depart’ → “they part from us”: the point of separation is made explicit.
- *‘Soddoma’ gridando* ‘“Sodom” crying’ → “crying out ‘Sodom’”: the verb precedes the cry, and “out” is supplied.
- *rimproverando a sè* ‘reproaching to themselves’ → “reproving themselves”: the persons reproved become the direct object.
- *vergognando* ‘being ashamed’ → “by their shame”: the gerund becomes a possessive noun phrase, with “by” explicitly marking means.

> 82 Nostro peccato fu ermafrodito;  

Our sin was hermaphrodite;

- Literal.

> 83 ma perchè non servammo umana legge,  
> 84 seguendo come bestie l' appetito,  

but because we observed not human law, and followed our lusts like brute beasts,

- *seguendo come bestie l' appetito* ‘following like beasts the appetite’ → “and followed our lusts like brute beasts”: the dependent gerund becomes a coordinated past-tense verb. The object moves before the comparison; the singular with a definite article becomes the possessive plural “our lusts”, more specific than ‘the appetite’, and “brute” is supplied.

## Lines 85–117 — Guido Guinizelli Named and Praised

> 85 in obbrobrio di noi, per noi si legge,  
> 86 quando partiamci, il nome di colei  
> 87 che s' imbestiò nell' imbestiate schegge.  

to our infamy by us is read, when we part us, the name of her who imbruted herself in the brute-like framework.

- *in obbrobrio di noi* ‘in reproach of us’ → “to our infamy”: the preposition changes, and the possessive moves before the noun.
- *partiamci* ‘part-us’ → “we part us”: the reflexive attached to the verb becomes a separate pronoun after it.
- *s' imbestiò* ‘herself imbruted’ → “imbruted herself”: the reflexive follows the verb.
- *nell' imbestiate schegge* ‘in the bestialized pieces of wood’ → “in the brute-like framework”: the plural pieces become a singular collective, and the participle becomes an adjective of resemblance.

> 88 Or sai nostri atti, e di che fummo rei;  

Now knowst thou our deeds and what we were guilty of;

- *sai* ‘thou knowest’ → “knowst thou”: the expressed subject follows the verb after “Now”.
- *di che fummo rei* ‘of what we were guilty’ → “what we were guilty of”: the preposition moves to the end of the clause.

> 89 se forse a nome vuoi saper chi semo,  
> 90 tempo non è da dire, e non saprei.  

if haply thou wouldst know who we are by name, there is no time to tell, nor could I.

- *a nome vuoi saper chi semo* ‘by name thou wishest to know who we are’ → “thou wouldst know who we are by name”: the phrase “by name” moves from before the verb of wishing to the end of the indirect question, and a modal construction replaces the verb of wishing with its dependent infinitive.
- *tempo non è da dire* ‘time there is not to tell’ → “there is no time to tell”: the existential construction takes introductory “there”, and the negation moves onto the noun.
- *e non saprei* ‘and I should not know how’ → “nor could I”: the conjunction absorbs the negative, and conditional knowing how becomes modal ability, with the subject inverted.

> 91 Farotti ben di me volere scemo:  

Thy desire of me, I will indeed make to wane:

- *Farotti ben* ‘I shall indeed make for thee’ → “I will indeed make”: the initial verb moves after its object; the enclitic dative is recast as the possessive “Thy” on that object.
- *di me volere scemo* ‘of me the desire diminished’ → “desire of me,” … “to wane”: the noun precedes its complement, and the adjective describing the resulting state becomes an infinitive of change.

> 92 son Guido Guinizelli, e già mi purgo  
> 93 per ben dolermi prima ch' all' estremo.”  

Guido Guinicelli am I, and already purge me, because I full repentance made before the end.”

- *son Guido Guinizelli* ‘I am Guido Guinizelli’ → “Guido Guinicelli am I”: the name is put first and the subject last.
- *mi purgo* ‘myself I purge’ → “purge me”: the reflexive follows the verb and takes the simple pronoun form.
- *per ben dolermi* ‘for repenting well’ → “because I full repentance made”: the causal infinitive becomes a finite past-tense clause; the reflexive verb becomes a noun with “made”, and the adverb becomes an adjective of degree.
- *prima ch' all' estremo* ‘sooner than at the end’ → “before the end”: one preposition replaces the comparison with a point in time.

> 94 Quali nella tristizia di Licurgo  
> 95 si fer due figli a riveder la madre,  
> 96 tal mi fec' io, ma non a tanto insurgo,  

As in the sorrow of Lycurgus two sons became on beholding again their mother, so became I, but not to such height do I rise,

- *Quali* ‘such as’ → “As”: the predicative adjective expressing what the sons became is reduced to a comparative conjunction.
- *si fer due figli* ‘made themselves two sons’ → “two sons became”: a reflexive construction becomes an intransitive verb, with the subject moved before it.
- *a riveder la madre* ‘at seeing-again the mother’ → “on beholding again their mother”: a gerund replaces the infinitive, the prefix becomes a separate adverb, and a possessive replaces the article.
- *tal mi fec' io* ‘such I made myself’ → “so became I”: the corresponding adjective becomes “so”, and the reflexive construction again becomes intransitive.
- *ma non a tanto insurgo* ‘but not to so much do I rise’ → “but not to such height do I rise”: “height” makes the degree explicit, and English uses auxiliary inversion after the fronted negative phrase.

> 97 quand' i' odo nomar sè stesso il padre  
> 98 mio, e degli altri miei miglior, che mai  
> 99 rime d' amore usar dolci e leggiadre;  

when I hear name himself the father of me, and of others my betters, who ever used sweet and graceful rhymes of love;

- *il padre mio* ‘the father mine’ → “the father of me”: a prepositional phrase replaces the postposed possessive and parallels the next complement.
- *degli altri miei miglior* ‘of the others better than I’ → “of others my betters”: the definite article disappears, and the comparison is expressed by a plural noun in apposition.
- *che mai rime d' amore usar dolci e leggiadre* ‘who ever rhymes of love used sweet and graceful’ → “who ever used sweet and graceful rhymes of love”: the verb moves before its object, and both adjectives move before the noun they qualify.

> 100 e senza udire e dir pensoso andai,  
> 101 lunga fiata rimirando lui,  
> 102 nè per lo foco in là più m' appressai.  

and without hearing and speaking, pondering I went, long time gazing at him, nor because of the fire drew I nigher thither.

- *senza udire e dir* ‘without to hear and to speak’ → “without hearing and speaking”: English uses gerunds after the preposition rather than infinitives.
- *pensoso* ‘thoughtful’ → “pondering”: an adjective becomes an active participle.
- *lunga fiata* ‘a long while’ → “long time”: the duration is expressed by a bare noun phrase.
- *nè per lo foco* ‘nor on account of the fire’ → “nor because of the fire”: the causal sense of the simple preposition is made explicit.
- *in là più m' appressai* ‘thither more I drew myself near’ → “drew I nigher thither”: the reflexive verb and degree adverb become “drew” with a comparative, the subject is inverted after “nor”, and the direction comes last.

> 103 Poichè di riguardar pasciuto fui,  

When I was filled with beholding,

- *Poichè di riguardar pasciuto fui* ‘after with looking sated I was’ → “When I was filled with beholding”: the temporal conjunction becomes “When”; the auxiliary precedes the participle, and its complement moves to the end, with a gerund for the infinitive.

> 104 tutto m' offersi pronto al suo servigio,  
> 105 con l' affermar che fa credere altrui.  

I offered me all ready to his service, with the oath which compels another’s belief.

- *tutto m' offersi pronto* ‘wholly myself I offered ready’ → “I offered me all ready”: the reflexive follows the verb, and the opening expression of entirety moves next to “ready”.
- *con l' affermar* ‘with the affirming’ → “with the oath”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun specifying the form of affirmation.
- *che fa credere altrui* ‘that makes another believe’ → “which compels another’s belief”: the causative verb is strengthened, and the infinitive and its understood subject become a noun and its possessor.

> 106 Ed egli a me: “Tu lasci tal vestigio,  
> 107 per quel ch' i' odo, in me e tanto chiaro,  
> 108 che Lete nol può tor, nè farlo bigio.  

And he to me: “Thou leavest, by that which I hear, traces so deep and so clear, that Lethe cannot take them away, nor make them dim.

- *tal vestigio* ‘such a trace’ → “traces so deep”: the singular becomes plural, “deep” specifies the quality implied by ‘such’, and the object moves after the phrase about hearing.
- *in me* ‘in me’ → omitted: the translation does not express where the traces remain.
- *Lete nol può tor* ‘Lethe it cannot take away’ → “Lethe cannot take them away”: the object follows the infinitive and becomes plural to agree with “traces”.
- *nè farlo bigio* ‘nor make it grey’ → “nor make them dim”: the singular enclitic becomes a separate plural object, and the colour adjective becomes one of diminished clarity.

> 109 Ma, se le tue parole or ver giuraro,  
> 110 dimmi che è cagion per che dimostri  
> 111 nel dire e nel guardare avermi caro.”  

But if thy words just now sware truth, tell me, what is the cause wherefore thou showest in speech and look that thou holdest me so dear.”

- *ver giuraro* ‘truth swore’ → “sware truth”: the object follows the verb.
- *che è cagion* ‘what is cause’ → “what is the cause”: English supplies a definite article for the predicate noun.
- *nel dire e nel guardare* ‘in the speaking and in the looking’ → “in speech and look”: simple nouns replace the two substantivized infinitives, and a single preposition serves both.
- *avermi caro* ‘to have me dear’ → “that thou holdest me so dear”: a finite clause replaces the infinitive, “holdest” replaces ‘have’, and “so” is supplied.

> 112 Ed io a lui: “Li dolci detti vostri  
> 113 che, quanto durerà l' uso moderno,  
> 114 faranno cari ancora i loro inchiostri.”  

And I to him: “Your sweet ditties, which so long as modern use shall last, will make their very ink precious.”

- *Li dolci detti vostri* ‘the sweet sayings of yours’ → “Your sweet ditties”: the possessive moves to the front, and “ditties” gives the sayings a specifically lyrical sense.
- *quanto durerà l' uso moderno* ‘as long as shall last the use modern’ → “so long as modern use shall last”: the subject moves before the verb, the adjective precedes the noun, and the definite article disappears.
- *faranno cari ancora i loro inchiostri* ‘will make dear even their inks’ → “will make their very ink precious”: the predicate adjective moves after the object and takes the sense ‘precious’; “very” renders the emphasis of ‘even’, and the plural becomes a mass noun.

> 115 “O frate,” disse, “questi ch' io ti scerno  
> 116 col dito” (ed additò un spirto innanzi)  
> 117 “fu miglior fabbro del parlar materno.  

“O brother,” said he, “this one whom I distinguish to thee with my finger” (and he pointed to a spirit in front) “was a better craftsman of the mother tongue.

- *questi* ‘this man’ → “this one”: the masculine demonstrative pronoun is expressed by a demonstrative with “one”.
- *ch' io ti scerno* ‘whom I for thee distinguish’ → “whom I distinguish to thee”: the dative pronoun becomes a prepositional complement following the verb.
- *col dito* ‘with the finger’ → “with my finger”: the possessive replaces the article.
- *additò un spirto* ‘he indicated a spirit’ → “he pointed to a spirit”: the direct object becomes a prepositional object.
- *miglior fabbro* ‘better smith’ → “a better craftsman”: English supplies an indefinite article, and the occupational noun is broadened.
- *del parlar materno* ‘of the speech maternal’ → “of the mother tongue”: the substantivized infinitive becomes “tongue”, and the postposed adjective becomes an attributive noun before it.

## Lines 118–148 — Praise of Arnaut and Two Requests for Prayer

> 118 Versi d' amore e prose di romanzi  
> 119 soperchiò tutti, e lascia dir gli stolti  
> 120 che quel di Lemosì credon ch' avanzi.  

In verses of love, and prose tales of romance, all he surpassed, and let fools talk, who think that he of Limoges excels.

- *Versi d' amore* ‘verses of love’ → “In verses of love”: the preposition is supplied to mark the respect in which he surpassed the others.
- *prose di romanzi* ‘proses of romances’ → “prose tales of romance”: the plural noun becomes a modifier of the supplied “tales”, and the plural ‘romances’ becomes a singular name for the genre.
- *soperchiò tutti* ‘surpassed all’ → “all he surpassed”: the object moves before the verb.
- *lascia dir gli stolti* ‘let talk the fools’ → “let fools talk”: the subject of the infinitive moves before it, and the definite article is dropped.
- *che quel di Lemosì credon ch' avanzi* ‘who him of Limoges believe to excel’ → “who think that he of Limoges excels”: the verb of opinion comes first; ‘he of Limoges’, placed before *credon* in Italian, stands inside the English subordinate clause. The subjunctive becomes an indicative.

> 121 A voce più ch' al ver drizzan li volti,  
> 122 e così ferman sua opinione  
> 123 prima ch' arte o ragion per lor s' ascolti.  

To rumour rather than to truth they turn their faces, and thus do fix their opinion ere art or reason is listened to by them.

- *A voce più ch' al ver* ‘to report more than to the true’ → “To rumour rather than to truth”: “rumour” gives the sense of public report; the comparison of degree becomes one of preference, and the substantivized adjective becomes an abstract noun.
- *drizzan li volti* ‘direct the faces’ → “they turn their faces”: the verb of directing becomes “turn”, and the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *ferman* ‘fix’ → “do fix”: the simple verb is expanded with an auxiliary.
- *arte o ragion per lor s' ascolti* ‘art or reason by them be heard’ → “art or reason is listened to by them”: the passive construction with *si* becomes an English passive with a preposition; the agent moves to the end, and the subjunctive becomes an indicative.

> 124 Così fer molti antichi di Guittone,  
> 125 di grido in grido pur lui dando pregio,  
> 126 fin che l' ha vinto il ver con più persone.  

So did many of our fathers with Guittone, shouting in turn and praising him alone; but truth has prevailed at length with most persons.

- *molti antichi* ‘many forebears’ → “many of our fathers”: a partitive construction and the possessive “our” are supplied.
- *di Guittone* ‘concerning Guittone’ → “with Guittone”: the English uses “with” after “did”.
- *di grido in grido* ‘from cry to cry’ → “shouting in turn”: the repeated noun becomes a participle with an adverbial phrase expressing succession.
- *pur lui dando pregio* ‘only to him giving esteem’ → “and praising him alone”: the verb and noun become a single transitive verb, the recipient becomes its direct object, and “and” coordinates the two English participles.
- *fin che* ‘until’ → “but” and “at length”: a temporal subordinate clause becomes a contrasting coordinate clause with a temporal adverb.
- *l' ha vinto il ver* ‘him has conquered the true’ → “truth has prevailed”: the subject moves before the verb and becomes an abstract noun; the verb becomes intransitive, leaving the object pronoun unrepresented.
- *con più persone* ‘with more persons’ → “with most persons”: the comparative is read as denoting the majority.

> 127 Or, se tu hai sì ampio privilegio,  
> 128 che licito ti sia l' andare al chiostro,  
> 129 nel quale è Cristo abate del collegio,  

Now if thou hast such ample privilege, that ’tis permitted thee to go to the cloister wherein Christ is abbot of the college,

- *sì ampio privilegio* ‘so ample a privilege’ → “such ample privilege”: the degree expressed by ‘so’ modifying the adjective is expressed by “such” qualifying the noun phrase.
- *licito ti sia l' andare* ‘lawful to thee be the going’ → “’tis permitted thee to go”: the adjective and subjunctive copula become an indicative passive expression; the nominal infinitive becomes a to-infinitive anticipated by “’tis”.
- *nel quale è Cristo abate* ‘in which is Christ abbot’ → “wherein Christ is abbot”: the preposition and relative pronoun become a relative adverb, and the subject moves before the verb.

> 130 fagli per me un dir di un paternostro,  
> 131 quanto bisogna a noi di questo mondo,  
> 132 dove poter peccar non è più nostro.”  

do me there the saying of a Pater Noster so far as is needful to us of this world, where power to sin is no more ours.”

- *fagli per me* ‘do there for me’ → “do me there”: the beneficiary becomes an indirect object without a preposition, and the locative follows it.
- *un dir di un paternostro* ‘a saying of a Pater Noster’ → “the saying of a Pater Noster”: the first indefinite article becomes definite.
- *quanto bisogna* ‘as much as is needed’ → “so far as is needful”: quantity becomes extent, and the impersonal verb is rendered by a copula and adjective.
- *poter peccar* ‘to be able to sin’ → “power to sin”: the first infinitive becomes a noun denoting ability.
- *non è più nostro* ‘is not any longer ours’ → “is no more ours”: the negation and the adverb of continuance are combined after the verb.

> 133 Poi, forse per dar loco altrui secondo,  
> 134 che presso avea, disparve per lo foco,  
> 135 come per l' acqua pesce andando al fondo.  

Then perchance to give place to another following close, he vanished through the flames, like a fish going through the water to the bottom.

- *altrui secondo, che presso avea* ‘to another next in order, whom he had close by’ → “to another following close”: the indication of order becomes a participle modifying “another”; the relative clause is reduced to “close”, with no equivalent for the verb of having.
- *per lo foco* ‘through the fire’ → “through the flames”: a singular noun becomes a different, plural noun.
- *per l' acqua pesce andando al fondo* ‘through the water a fish going to the bottom’ → “a fish going through the water to the bottom”: an article is supplied before “fish”, and the phrase describing its passage moves after the participle.

> 136 Io mi feci al mostrato innanzi un poco,  
> 137 e dissi ch' al suo nome il mio disire  
> 138 apparecchiava grazioso loco.  

A little forward I drew me towards the one he had pointed out, and said that my desire was preparing a grateful place for his name.

- *Io mi feci … innanzi un poco* ‘I made myself forward a little’ → “A little forward I drew me”: the reflexive idiom is rendered with “drew”; the adverbial phrase is reversed and moved to the front.
- *al mostrato* ‘to the one shown’ → “towards the one he had pointed out”: the preposition becomes directional, and the substantivized passive participle becomes a relative clause with an explicit agent and a past-perfect verb.
- *al suo nome il mio disire apparecchiava grazioso loco* ‘for his name my desire prepared gracious place’ → “my desire was preparing a grateful place for his name”: the phrase “for his name” moves from before the subject to the end of the clause; the ongoing sense of the imperfect is expressed by the past progressive; an article is supplied, and “grateful” renders the adjective in the sense of ‘pleasing’.

> 139 Ei cominciò liberamente a dire:  

Willingly he began to say:

- *cominciò liberamente a dire* ‘began freely to say’ → “Willingly he began to say”: the adverb is interpreted as expressing readiness and is moved to the front.

> 140 *“Tan m' abelis vostre cortes deman,*  
> 141 *qu' ieu no-m puesc, ni-m vueil a vos cobrire.*  

“So doth your courteous request please me that I cannot, nor will I, hide me from you.

- *Tan m' abelis vostre cortes deman* ‘so pleases me your courteous request’ → “So doth your courteous request please me”: the auxiliary permits inversion after “So”; the subject precedes the main verb, and the object follows it.
- *qu' ieu no-m puesc, ni-m vueil a vos cobrire* ‘that I cannot myself, nor wish myself, from you to hide’ → “that I cannot, nor will I, hide me from you”: the two reflexive clitics become one object of the shared infinitive; “will” expresses willingness, with inversion after “nor”, and “from you” moves after the verb.

> 142 *Ieu sui Arnaut, que plor e vau cantan;*  

I am Arnault that weep and go a-singing;

- *vau cantan* ‘go singing’ → “go a-singing”: the participle takes the English prefix “a-”.

> 143 *consiros vei la passada folor,*  
> 144 *e vei jausen lo jorn, qu' esper, denan.*  

in thought I see my past madness, and I see with joy the day which I await before me.

- *consiros* ‘pensive’ → “in thought”: an adjective describing the speaker becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *la passada folor* ‘the past folly’ → “my past madness”: the article becomes a possessive, and “madness” gives a stronger sense than ‘folly’.
- *jausen* ‘rejoicing’ → “with joy”: the participle becomes a prepositional phrase.
- *denan* ‘ahead’ → “before me”: the speaker is made the explicit point of reference.

> 145 *Ara vos prec, per aquella valor*  
> 146 *que vos guida al som de l' escalina,*  
> 147 *sovegna vos a temps de ma dolor.”*  

Now I pray you, by that Goodness which guideth you to the summit of the stairway, be mindful in due time of my pain.”

- *vos prec* ‘you I pray’ → “I pray you”: the object moves after the verb.
- *aquella valor* ‘that worth’ → “that Goodness”: the quality of worth or excellence is interpreted as goodness.
- *vos guida* ‘you guides’ → “guideth you”: the object follows the verb.
- *sovegna vos* ‘may it come to mind for you’ → “be mindful”: the impersonal subjunctive with an indirect object becomes a personal imperative with a predicate adjective.
- *a temps* ‘in time’ → “in due time”: “due” makes the sense of fitting time explicit.

> 148 Poi s' ascose nel foco che gli affina.  

Then he hid him in the fire which refines them.

- *s' ascose* ‘himself he hid’ → “he hid him”: the reflexive clitic becomes a postverbal object pronoun used reflexively.
- *gli affina* ‘them refines’ → “refines them”: the object follows the verb.

## Summary — Reordering the Phrase, Preserving the Image

Okey often brings a delayed subject forward, moves an object pronoun after its verb, or reunites words separated in the Italian. Thus *agli altri dopo* becomes “behind the others” and stands next to “goest” rather than after the explanation that intervenes in Italian. Yet Okey does not invariably prefer ordinary English order: for *soperchiò tutti*, ‘he surpassed all’, he writes “all he surpassed”, creating an inversion where the Italian has none. Two routine changes generally go unmarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending suffices, and drops the article before a possessive, as in *le membra mie*, “my members”.

Changes of construction often shift the emphasis without greatly altering the sense. The gerund *raggiando*, ‘radiating’, becomes a phrase of means, “with his rays”; the adjective *reverente*, ‘reverent’, becomes an explicit motive, “for reverence”. Sometimes the recasting goes further: *ch’ io ti scaltro*, ‘that I make thee wary’, becomes “let my skill avail thee”, transferring attention from the effect on the hearer to the speaker’s ability. Word choice can likewise narrow or broaden an image: “ruddier” gives *più rovente* a specifically visual sense, while “craftsman” is less specific than *fabbro*, ‘smith’.

Additions and omissions are often small but telling. In rendering *tal vestigio* as “traces so deep”, Okey changes the number and supplies a quality not named in the Italian; he also leaves out *in me*, which says where the impression remains. Elsewhere he keeps strikingly close, retaining the unusual predicate of *Nostro peccato fu ermafrodito* in “Our sin was hermaphrodite” and the linked images of enclosure and refinement in “the fire which refines them”. Fidelity to an image or a turn of phrase thus coexists with considerable freedom in the ordering and grammatical form of its words.
