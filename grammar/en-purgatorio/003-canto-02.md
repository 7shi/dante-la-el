# Purgatorio 2

## Lines 1–12 — Dawn and Delay by the Sea

> 1 Già era il sole all' orizzonte giunto,  

Already had the sun reached the horizon,

- *Già era il sole all' orizzonte giunto* ‘already was the sun at the horizon arrived’ → “Already had the sun reached the horizon”: the Italian pluperfect has ‘be’ as its auxiliary. English uses ‘have’ and makes the horizon the direct object of “reached”, so the participle comes before it rather than after it.

> 2 lo cui meridian cerchio coperchia  
> 3 Jerusalem col suo più alto punto;  

whose meridian circle covers Jerusalem with its highest point,

- *più alto* ‘most high’ → “highest”: the superlative expressed in two words becomes an inflected adjective.

> 4 e la notte che opposita a lui cerchia  
> 5 uscìa di Gange fuor con le bilance,  

and night which opposite to him revolves, from Ganges forth was issuing with the Scales,

- *e la notte* ‘and the night’ → “and night”: the definite article is dropped.
- *cerchia* ‘circles’ → “revolves”: a different verb renders the circular motion.
- *uscìa di Gange fuor* ‘issued from Ganges forth’ → “from Ganges forth was issuing”: the phrases of origin and direction come before the verb rather than after it, and the imperfect is rendered by the past progressive.

> 6 che le caggion di man quando soperchia:  

that fall from her hand when she prevails;

- *che le caggion di man* ‘that to her fall from hand’ → “that fall from her hand”: the dative pronoun before the verb becomes a possessive modifying “hand”.
- *soperchia* ‘exceeds’ → “prevails”: the verb is taken in the sense of gaining the upper hand.

> 7 sì che le bianche e le vermiglie guance,  
> 8 là dove io era, della bella Aurora,  
> 9 per troppa etate divenivan rance.  

so that fair Aurora’s white and ruddy cheeks, there where I was, through too great age were turning orange.

- *le bianche e le vermiglie guance* ‘the white and the vermilion cheeks’ → “white and ruddy cheeks”: neither article is retained in the possessive construction, and “ruddy” describes the colour more generally.
- *della bella Aurora* ‘of fair Aurora’ → “fair Aurora’s”: the possessive in *-’s* precedes “white and ruddy cheeks”, whereas the Italian leaves the possessor until after the intervening clause of place.
- *per troppa etate* ‘through too much age’ → “through too great age”: excess is expressed in terms of degree rather than quantity.
- *divenivan rance* ‘became orange’ → “were turning orange”: the imperfect becomes a past progressive, with “turning” for the change of colour.

> 10 Noi eravam lunghesso il mare ancora,  

We were alongside the ocean yet,

- *il mare* ‘the sea’ → “the ocean”: a more specific noun replaces ‘sea’.

> 11 come gente che pensa suo cammino,  

like folk who ponder o’er their road,

- *gente che pensa suo cammino* ‘a people that considers its road’ → “folk who ponder o’er their road”: the singular collective is construed as plural in “who ponder” and “their”; the direct object of *pensa* becomes the object of “o’er”.

> 12 che va col core, e col corpo dimora;  

who in heart do go and in body stay;

- *che va col core* ‘that goes with the heart’ → “who in heart do go”: the singular verb becomes plural to agree with the plural antecedent and takes the auxiliary “do”; the phrase with ‘with’ becomes “in heart”, loses its article, and precedes the verb.
- *e col corpo dimora* ‘and with the body stays’ → “and in body stay”: “in” again replaces ‘with’, the article is dropped, and the verb is plural rather than singular.

## Lines 13–45 — The Celestial Pilot Draws Near

> 13 ed ecco, qual sul presso del mattino  
> 14 per li grossi vapor Marte rosseggia  
> 15 giù nel ponente sopra il suol marino:  

and lo, as on the approach of morn, through the dense mists Mars burns red, low in the West o’er the ocean-floor;

- *sul presso del mattino* ‘at the nearness of morning’ → “on the approach of morn”: nearness in time is expressed as approach.
- *per li grossi vapor* ‘through the thick vapours’ → “through the dense mists”: the vapours are rendered more specifically as mists.
- *rosseggia* ‘glows red’ → “burns red”: the single verb of colour is expanded into a verb and an adjective, with a stronger image of fire.
- *giù* ‘down’ → “low”: position is expressed as height.
- *sopra il suol marino* ‘above the floor marine’ → “o’er the ocean-floor”: the adjective following the noun becomes the first element of a compound noun.

> 16 cotal m' apparve, s' io ancor lo veggia,  
> 17 un lume per lo mar venir sì ratto,  
> 18 che il mover suo nessun volar pareggia;  

such to me appeared—so may I see it again!—a light coming o’er the sea so swiftly, that no flight is equal to its motion;

- *s' io ancor lo veggia* ‘if I again may see it’ → “so may I see it again!”: the optative force of the Italian *se* with the subjunctive is made explicit; the object follows the verb, and the adverb moves to the end.
- *per lo mar venir* ‘over the sea to come’ → “coming o’er the sea”: the infinitive becomes a participle and precedes the phrase of place.
- *il mover suo nessun volar pareggia* ‘its moving no flying matches’ → “no flight is equal to its motion”: the two substantivized infinitives become nouns; the subject moves before the verb, and the fronted object becomes the complement of an adjective.

> 19 dal qual com' io un poco ebbi ritratto  
> 20 l' occhio per domandar lo duca mio,  
> 21 rividil più lucente e maggior fatto.  

from which, when I had a while withdrawn mine eyes to question my Leader, I saw it brighter and bigger grown.

- *un poco ebbi ritratto* ‘a little I had withdrawn’ → “had a while withdrawn”: the indefinite measure is taken as one of time, and the adverbial is placed between auxiliary and participle.
- *l' occhio* ‘the eye’ → “mine eyes”: the singular becomes plural, and the English uses a possessive where Italian uses the definite article with a part of the body.
- *rividil* ‘I saw it again’ → “I saw it”: the sense of repetition in the prefix is not separately rendered, and the object pronoun becomes a separate word.

> 22 Poi d' ogni lato ad esso m' apparìo  
> 23 un non sapeva che bianco, e di sotto  
> 24 a poco a poco un altro a lui uscìo.  

Then on each side of it appeared to me a something white; and from beneath it, little by little, another whiteness came forth.

- *ad esso* ‘to it’ → “of it”: English attaches the pronoun to the noun of position with a different preposition.
- *m' apparìo* ‘to me appeared’ → “appeared to me”: the indirect object follows the verb.
- *un non sapeva che bianco* ‘an I-knew-not-what white’ → “a something white”: the expression containing a first-person verb is reduced to an indefinite pronoun.
- *di sotto … a lui* ‘from below … to it’ → “from beneath it”: the later pronoun is brought forward and made the complement of the preposition.
- *un altro* ‘another’ → “another whiteness”: a noun is supplied from the preceding description.

> 25 Lo mio maestro ancor non fece motto  
> 26 mentre che i primi bianchi apparser ali;  

My Master yet did speak no word, until the first whitenesses appeared as wings;

- *ancor non fece motto* ‘still did not make a word’ → “yet did speak no word”: the idiom with ‘make’ becomes one with “speak”, and the negative passes from verb to noun.
- *mentre che* ‘while’ → “until”: after the negative main clause the conjunction is rendered as marking the end of the silence.
- *i primi bianchi* ‘the first white things’ → “the first whitenesses”: the substantivized adjective becomes an abstract noun in the plural.
- *apparser ali* ‘appeared wings’ → “appeared as wings”: English supplies a link before the predicate noun.

> 27 allor che ben conobbe il galeotto,  
> 28 gridò: “Fa, fa che le ginocchia cali;  

then, when well he knew the pilot, he cried: “Bend, bend thy knees;

- *ben conobbe* ‘well he recognized’ → “well he knew”: recognition at that moment is expressed as knowledge.
- *Fa, fa che le ginocchia cali* ‘do, do so that the knees thou lower’ → “Bend, bend thy knees”: the repeated introductory imperative and its subjunctive clause are compressed into a repeated command naming the action itself; lowering becomes bending, the object follows the verb, and the definite article becomes a possessive.

> 29 ecco l' Angel di Dio: piega le mani:  
> 30 omai vedrai di sì fatti ufficiali.  

behold the Angel of God: fold thy hands: henceforth shalt thou see such ministers.

- *piega le mani* ‘bend the hands’ → “fold thy hands”: English names the gesture with a different verb and uses a possessive for the definite article.
- *omai vedrai* ‘henceforth thou wilt see’ → “henceforth shalt thou see”: the inflected future becomes an auxiliary construction, with inversion after the initial adverb.
- *di sì fatti ufficiali* ‘of officials of such a kind’ → “such ministers”: the partitive construction disappears, the expression of kind is compressed into a determiner, and office is expressed in terms of ministry.

> 31 Vedi che sdegna gli argomenti umani,  
> 32 sì che remo non vuol nè altro velo  
> 33 che l' ale sue tra liti sì lontani.  

Look how he scorns all human instruments, so that oar he wills not, nor other sail than his wings, between shores so distant.

- *Vedi che* ‘see that’ → “Look how”: the verb of perception becomes a direction to look, and the following clause is introduced as one of manner.
- *gli argomenti umani* ‘the means human’ → “all human instruments”: the general definite article becomes an explicit universal, the means are called instruments, and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *remo non vuol* ‘oar he does not want’ → “oar he wills not”: English keeps the object first but places the negative after the verb.
- *l' ale sue* ‘the wings of his’ → “his wings”: the postposed possessive precedes the noun.

> 34 Vedi come l' ha dritte verso il cielo,  
> 35 trattando l' aere con l' eterne penne,  
> 36 che non si mutan come mortal pelo.”  

See how he has them heavenward turned, plying the air with eternal plumes, that are not mewed like mortal hair.”

- *l' ha dritte verso il cielo* ‘them he has upright towards the sky’ → “he has them heavenward turned”: the object pronoun follows the verb; the directional phrase becomes an adverb and precedes a participle expressing orientation rather than uprightness.
- *trattando l' aere* ‘handling the air’ → “plying the air”: a different verb expresses the action upon the air.
- *con l' eterne penne* ‘with the eternal plumes’ → “with eternal plumes”: the definite article is omitted.
- *non si mutan* ‘do not change’ → “are not mewed”: the pronominal verb becomes a passive, with a verb specifically denoting the shedding of a covering.

> 37 Poi come più e più verso noi venne  
> 38 l' uccel divino, più chiaro appariva;  
> 39 per che l' occhio da presso nol sostenne,  

Then as more and more towards us came the bird divine, brighter yet he appeared, wherefore mine eye endured him not near:

- *più chiaro appariva* ‘brighter he appeared’ → “brighter yet he appeared”: “yet” is supplied to bring out the correspondence with the preceding increase.
- *per che* ‘for which reason’ → “wherefore”: the preposition and relative are combined into one connective.
- *l' occhio* ‘the eye’ → “mine eye”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *da presso nol sostenne* ‘from close by did not endure him’ → “endured him not near”: the phrase of proximity becomes a final adverb, and both the negative and the object pronoun move after the verb.

> 40 ma chinail giuso; e quei sen venne a riva  
> 41 con un vasello snelletto e leggiero,  
> 42 tanto che l' acqua nulla ne inghiottiva.  

but I bent it down, and he came on to the shore with a vessel so swift and light that the waters nowise drew it in.

- *ma chinail giuso* ‘but I lowered it down’ → “but I bent it down”: lowering the eye is expressed as bending it; the attached object pronoun becomes a separate word.
- *quei sen venne a riva* ‘that one came his way to shore’ → “he came on to the shore”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun, the pronominal idiom of motion is rendered with “on”, and English supplies the article before “shore”.
- *un vasello snelletto e leggiero, tanto che* ‘a little vessel nimble and light, to such a degree that’ → “a vessel so swift and light that”: the diminutive colouring is lost, nimbleness is rendered as speed, and the expression of degree is divided, with “so” placed before the adjectives.
- *l' acqua nulla ne inghiottiva* ‘the water swallowed nothing of it’ → “the waters nowise drew it in”: the singular becomes plural; the negative quantity becomes an adverb, the partitive pronoun becomes a direct object, and swallowing is rendered as drawing in.

> 43 Da poppa stava il celestial nocchiero,  
> 44 tal che parea beato per iscritto;  

On the stern stood the celestial pilot, such, that blessedness seemed writ upon him,

- *Da poppa* ‘at the stern’ → “On the stern”: the preposition of position is changed, and the article is supplied.
- *tal che parea beato per iscritto* ‘such that he seemed blessed in writing’ → “such, that blessedness seemed writ upon him”: the predicate adjective becomes an abstract noun and the subject of “seemed”; the phrase of manner becomes a participle, and the former subject is represented by “upon him”.

> 45 e più di cento spirti entro sediero.  

and more than a hundred spirits sat within.

- *entro sediero* ‘within sat’ → “sat within”: the adverb moves after the verb.

## Lines 46–78 — The Newcomers’ Song and Greeting

> 46 “*In exitu Israel de* Egitto,”  
> 47 cantavan tutti insieme ad una voce,  
> 48 con quanto di quel salmo è poscia scritto.  

“*In exitu Israel de Aegypto*,” sang they all together with one voice, with what of that psalm is thereafter written.

- *In exitu Israel de Egitto* ‘at Israel’s going out from Egypt’ → “*In exitu Israel de Aegypto*”: Okey gives the incipit wholly in Latin, where Dante ends it with the Italian form.
- *ad una voce* ‘at one voice’ → “with one voice”: English changes the preposition.
- *quanto* ‘all that’ → “what”: the English relative does not explicitly express the full extent conveyed by the Italian.

> 49 Poi fece il segno lor di santa croce;  

Then made he to them the sign of Holy Cross,

- *il segno lor* ‘the sign to them’ → “to them the sign”: the indirect object precedes the direct object.

> 50 ond' ei si gittar tutti in su la piaggia,  
> 51 ed ei sen gì, come venne, veloce.  

whereat they all flung them on the strand and quick even as he came he went his way.

- *ei si gittar tutti* ‘they themselves flung, all’ → “they all flung them”: “all” moves next to the subject, and the reflexive is rendered by “them” after the verb.
- *in su la piaggia* ‘onto the strand’ → “on the strand”: a single preposition replaces the compound expression of motion.
- *ed ei sen gì, come venne, veloce* ‘and he went away, as he came, swift’ → “and quick even as he came he went his way”: the final adjective moves to the front, the comparison comes before the main verb and gains the emphatic “even”, and ‘went away’ becomes “went his way”.

> 52 La turba che rimase lì selvaggia  
> 53 parea del loco, rimirando intorno  
> 54 come colui che nuove cose assaggia.  

The throng that remained there seemed strange to the place, gazing around like one who assayeth new things.

- *selvaggia parea del loco* ‘unfamiliar it seemed with the place’ → “seemed strange to the place”: the predicate adjective follows the verb, and “to” replaces the Italian complement with *di*.
- *come colui che* ‘like that one who’ → “like one who”: the demonstrative becomes indefinite.
- *nuove cose assaggia* ‘new things tastes’ → “assayeth new things”: the object follows the verb, and Okey takes the verb in the sense of trying or testing.

> 55 Da tutte parti saettava il giorno  
> 56 lo sol, ch' avea con le saette conte  
> 57 di mezzo il ciel cacciato il Capricorno,  

On every side the sun, who with his arrows bright had chased the Goat from midst of heaven, was shooting forth the day,

- *Da tutte parti* ‘from all sides’ → “On every side”: position replaces direction, and the plural becomes distributive singular.
- *saettava il giorno lo sol* ‘was shooting the day the sun’ → “the sun” … “was shooting forth the day”: the subject moves ahead of the verb and object, with the relative clause now intervening; “forth” makes the outward motion explicit.
- *le saette conte* ‘the arrows conspicuous’ → “his arrows bright”: the article becomes a possessive, and Okey takes the adjective in the sense of visible brilliance.
- *avea … di mezzo il ciel cacciato il Capricorno* ‘had … from mid-heaven chased the Goat’ → “had chased the Goat from midst of heaven”: the auxiliary joins the participle after the phrase about the arrows, and the phrase of removal follows the object instead of preceding the participle.

> 58 quando la nuova gente alzò la fronte  
> 59 ver noi, dicendo a noi: “Se voi sapete,  
> 60 mostratene la via di gire al monte.”  

when the new people lifted up their faces towards us, saying to us: “If ye know show us the way to go to the mount.”

- *la nuova gente alzò la fronte* ‘the new company raised the brow’ → “the new people lifted up their faces”: the collective and its singular body part are construed as plural; the article becomes a possessive, the brow becomes the face, and “up” accompanies the verb.
- *mostratene la via di gire* ‘show to us the way of going’ → “show us the way to go”: the attached dative becomes a separate pronoun, and the infinitive with *di* becomes an English infinitive modifying “way”.

> 61 E Virgilio rispose: “Voi credete  
> 62 forse che siamo esperti d' esto loco;  
> 63 ma noi siam peregrin, come voi siete.  

And Virgil answered: “Ye think perchance that we have experience of this place, but we are strangers even as ye are.

- *credete* ‘believe’ → “think”: Okey uses a verb of supposition.
- *siamo esperti d’ esto loco* ‘we are experienced in this place’ → “we have experience of this place”: the predicate adjective becomes a noun governed by “have”; the Italian subjunctive after the verb of belief has no distinct counterpart here.
- *peregrin* ‘pilgrims’ → “strangers”: Okey renders the word by unfamiliarity with the place rather than by travelling.
- *come voi siete* ‘as ye are’ → “even as ye are”: “even” adds emphasis.

> 64 Dianzi venimmo, innanzi a voi un poco,  
> 65 per altra via che fu sì aspra e forte,  
> 66 che lo salire omai ne parrà gioco.”  

We came but now, a little while before you, by other way which was so rough and hard, that the climbing now will seem but play to us.”

- *Dianzi venimmo* ‘a short time ago we came’ → “We came but now”: the time expression follows the verb and is rendered by the idiom “but now”.
- *innanzi a voi un poco* ‘before you a little’ → “a little while before you”: the measure moves before the comparison, and “while” specifies time.
- *per altra via* ‘by another way’ → “by other way”: “other” stands without the English indefinite article.
- *forte* ‘strong’ → “hard”: the adjective is rendered in the sense of difficulty.
- *lo salire* ‘the to-climb’ → “the climbing”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a verbal noun in *-ing*.
- *omai ne parrà gioco* ‘henceforth to us it will seem play’ → “now will seem but play to us”: “now” renders the time adverb, the dative moves to the end, and “but” adds the sense of ‘merely’.

> 67 L' anime che si fur di me accorte,  
> 68 per lo spirare, ch' io era ancor vivo,  
> 69 maravigliando diventaro smorte;  

The souls who had observed me by my breathing that I was yet alive, marvelling grew pale;

- *si fur di me accorte* ‘had become aware, concerning me’ → “had observed me”: the reflexive expression becomes a transitive verb with “me” as its direct object; the following *che* clause still states what was perceived.
- *per lo spirare* ‘by the act of breathing’ → “by my breathing”: the substantivized infinitive becomes an *-ing* form, and the article becomes a possessive identifying the breather.

> 70 e come a messagger, che porti olivo,  
> 71 tragge la gente per udir novelle,  
> 72 e di calcar nessun si mostra schivo:  

and as to a messenger, who bears the olive, the folk draw nigh to hear the news, and none shows himself shy at trampling;

- *a messagger* ‘to messenger’ → “to a messenger”: English supplies an indefinite article.
- *porti olivo* ‘may bear olive’ → “bears the olive”: the subjunctive characterizing the imagined messenger becomes indicative, and English supplies the definite article.
- *tragge la gente* ‘draws near the folk’ → “the folk draw nigh”: the subject precedes the verb, and the collective takes plural rather than singular agreement.
- *per udir novelle* ‘in order to hear news’ → “to hear the news”: the bare English infinitive expresses purpose, and the Italian plural without an article becomes a definite mass noun.
- *di calcar nessun si mostra schivo* ‘of trampling no one shows himself shy’ → “none shows himself shy at trampling”: the fronted complement follows “shy”, with “at” and an *-ing* form in place of *di* and the infinitive.

> 73 così al viso mio s' affissar quelle  
> 74 anime fortunate tutte quante,  
> 75 quasi obbliando d' ire a farsi belle.  

so on my face those souls did fix their gaze, fortunate every one, well nigh forgetting to go and make them fair.

- *al viso mio* ‘to the face of mine’ → “on my face”: the preposition changes with the English verb of looking, and the possessive precedes the noun.
- *s’ affissar quelle anime* ‘fixed themselves those souls’ → “those souls did fix their gaze”: the subject moves before the verb; the simple past becomes “did fix”, and the reflexive gives way to an explicit object, “their gaze”.
- *fortunate tutte quante* ‘fortunate all of them’ → “fortunate every one”: a distributive singular replaces the emphatic plural.
- *quasi* ‘almost’ → “well nigh”: a two-word idiom renders the adverb.
- *d’ ire a farsi belle* ‘to go to make themselves beautiful’ → “to go and make them fair”: coordination with “and” replaces the second dependent infinitive, the simple pronoun “them” does reflexive duty, and “fair” renders the plural adjective without number marking.

> 76 Io vidi una di lor trarsi davante  
> 77 per abbracciarmi con sì grande affetto,  
> 78 che mosse me a far lo simigliante.  

I saw one of them draw forward to embrace me with such great affection, that he moved me to do the like.

- *trarsi davante* ‘to draw oneself forward’ → “draw forward”: after “saw” the infinitive is bare, and the verb is used intransitively rather than reflexively.
- *per abbracciarmi* ‘in order to embrace me’ → “to embrace me”: the bare expression of purpose needs no separate equivalent of *per*, and the attached object becomes a separate pronoun.
- *con sì grande affetto* ‘with so great affection’ → “with such great affection”: “such” qualifies the noun phrase in place of the degree adverb modifying the adjective.

## Lines 79–111 — Dante Meets Casella and Asks for a Song

> 79 O ombre vane, fuor che nell' aspetto!  

O shades empty save in outward show!

- *vane* ‘vain’ → “empty”: the adjective is rendered in the sense of lacking substance.
- *nell' aspetto* ‘in the appearance’ → “in outward show”: the article disappears, and the adjective makes explicit the outward character of the appearance.

> 80 tre volte retro a lei le mani avvinsi,  
> 81 e tante mi tornai con esse al petto.  

thrice behind it my hands I clasped, and as often returned with them to my breast.

- *tre volte* ‘three times’ → “thrice”: numeral and noun become one adverb.
- *retro a lei* ‘behind her’ → “behind it”: English needs no preposition corresponding to *a* after “behind”; the pronoun referring to the grammatically feminine shade becomes neuter.
- *le mani* ‘the hands’ → “my hands”: the possessive replaces the article.
- *e tante* ‘and as many times’ → “and as often”: the expression of frequency replaces the numeral expression with its understood noun.
- *mi tornai* ‘I turned myself back’ → “returned”: an intransitive verb takes the place of the pronominal construction.
- *al petto* ‘to the breast’ → “to my breast”: the possessive replaces the article.

> 82 Di maraviglia, credo, mi dipinsi;  
> 83 per che l' ombra sorrise e si ritrasse,  
> 84 ed io, seguendo lei, oltre mi pinsi.  

With wonder methinks I coloured me, whereat the shade smiled and drew back, and I, following it, flung me forward.

- *Di maraviglia* ‘of wonder’ → “With wonder”: English uses a different preposition for what colours the face.
- *credo* ‘I believe’ → “methinks”: a personal verb becomes an impersonal expression meaning ‘it seems to me’.
- *mi dipinsi* ‘myself I painted’ → “I coloured me”: the verb changes and the reflexive object follows it.
- *per che* ‘on account of which’ → “whereat”: a single relative adverb expresses the connection.
- *si ritrasse* ‘drew herself back’ → “drew back”: the English verb needs no reflexive object.
- *seguendo lei* ‘following her’ → “following it”: the feminine pronoun becomes neuter.
- *oltre mi pinsi* ‘forward myself I pushed’ → “flung me forward”: the verb is stronger and comes first instead of last.

> 85 Soavemente disse ch' io posasse;  

Gently it bade me pause:

- *disse ch' io posasse* ‘said that I should pause’ → “it bade me pause”: the shade's words are construed as a request; a verb of bidding takes an object and a bare infinitive instead of a clause in the subjunctive.

> 86 allor conobbi chi era e 'l pregai  
> 87 che per parlarmi un poco s' arrestasse.  

then knew I who it was, and did pray him that he would stay a while to speak to me.

- *chi era* ‘who he was’ → “who it was”: English uses the identifying construction with “it”.
- *'l pregai* ‘him I begged’ → “did pray him”: the past tense is expressed with an auxiliary, and the object follows the verb.
- *che per parlarmi un poco s' arrestasse* ‘that, to speak to me a little, he should stop’ → “that he would stay a while to speak to me”: the subjunctive becomes a construction with “would”, the pronominal verb becomes intransitive, and the purpose phrase comes last. The indication of duration goes with staying rather than speaking.

> 88 Risposemi: “Così com' io t' amai  
> 89 nel mortal corpo, così t' amo sciolta:  
> 90 però m' arresto; ma tu perchè vai?”  

He answered me: “Even as I loved thee in the mortal body so do I love thee freed; therefore I stay: but wherefore goest thou?”

- *Così com' io t' amai* ‘just as I thee loved’ → “Even as I loved thee”: the object pronoun moves after the verb.
- *così t' amo sciolta* ‘so, set free, I love thee’ → “so do I love thee freed”: the auxiliary permits inversion after “so”, and the object follows the verb. The final adjective keeps its position, but English lacks the feminine agreement that marks it as describing the speaking shade rather than the addressee.
- *m' arresto* ‘I stop myself’ → “I stay”: an intransitive verb replaces the pronominal one.
- *ma tu perchè vai* ‘but thou why goest’ → “but wherefore goest thou”: the explicit subject moves after the verb in the English question.

> 91 “Casella mio, per tornare altra volta  
> 92 là dove son, fo io questo viaggio,”  
> 93 diss' io; “ma a te com' è tanta ora tolta?”  

“Casella mine, to return here once again where I am, make I this journey,” said I, “but how hath so much time been taken from thee?”

- *per tornare altra volta / là dove son* ‘to return another time there where I am’ → “to return here once again where I am”: “here” replaces ‘there’ and moves before the time expression; another occasion is expressed as a repetition.
- *ma a te com' è tanta ora tolta* ‘but from thee how is so much time taken’ → “but how hath so much time been taken from thee”: the person affected moves from the front to the end, and English uses a perfect passive for the Italian construction with present *è* and a past participle.

> 94 Ed egli a me: “Nessun m' è fatto oltraggio,  
> 95 se quei, che leva e quando e cui gli piace,  
> 96 più volte m' ha negato esto passaggio:  
> 97 chè di giusto voler lo suo si face.  

And he to me: “No wrong is done me, if he who bears away when and whom he pleases hath many times denied me this passage; for of a just will his will is made.

- *Nessun m' è fatto oltraggio* ‘no wrong to me is done’ → “No wrong is done me”: English brings the noun next to its determiner instead of leaving it after the participle, and moves the recipient after the verb.
- *quei, che leva* ‘that one who lifts’ → “he who bears away”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative, and the English verb phrase expresses removal.
- *e quando e cui* ‘both when and whom’ → “when and whom”: the first member of the paired conjunction is dropped.
- *gli piace* ‘it pleases him’ → “he pleases”: the person pleased becomes the subject.
- *più volte m' ha negato* ‘several times to me he has denied’ → “hath many times denied me”: the frequency phrase moves between auxiliary and participle, and the recipient follows the participle.
- *lo suo si face* ‘his is made’ → “his will is made”: English repeats the noun understood in the possessive, and renders the construction with *si* by an explicit passive.

> 98 Veramente da tre mesi egli ha tolto  
> 99 chi ha voluto entrar con tutta pace.  

Truly for three months past he hath taken, in all peace, whoso hath wished to enter.

- *da tre mesi* ‘since three months ago’ → “for three months past”: English expresses a duration extending to the present rather than using the preposition of starting point.
- *con tutta pace* ‘with all peace’ → “in all peace”: the preposition changes, and the phrase moves from after the infinitive to immediately after the verb of taking, attaching it to the taking rather than to the entering.

> 100 Ond' io che era ora alla marina volto,  
> 101 dove l' acqua di Tevere s' insala,  
> 102 benignamente fui da lui ricolto.  

Wherefore I, who now was turned to the seashore where Tiber’s wave grows salt, kindly by him was garnered in.

- *era ora* ‘was now’ → “now was”: the adverb moves before the verb.
- *l' acqua di Tevere* ‘the water of Tiber’ → “Tiber’s wave”: a wave stands for the water, and a preceding possessive replaces the following prepositional phrase.
- *s' insala* ‘salts itself’ → “grows salt”: a verb expressing change of state with *si* becomes a linking verb and adjective.
- *fui da lui ricolto* ‘was by him gathered’ → “by him was garnered in”: the agent moves before the auxiliary, and a verb phrase suggesting gathering into store replaces the participle.

> 103 A quella foce ha egli or dritta l' ala;  
> 104 però che sempre quivi si raccoglie  
> 105 qual verso d' Acheronte non si cala.”  

To that mouth now he hath set his wings, because evermore are gathered there, they who to Acheron sink not down.”

- *ha egli or dritta l' ala* ‘has he now directed the wing’ → “now he hath set his wings”: the adverb moves first, the subject precedes the auxiliary, and the singular with an article becomes a possessive plural; setting the wings conveys directing them.
- *sempre quivi si raccoglie / qual* ‘always there gathers whoever’ → “evermore are gathered there, they who”: the place adverb moves after the verb; a singular pronominal construction becomes a plural passive, with an explicit plural antecedent for the relative.
- *verso d' Acheronte* ‘towards Acheron’ → “to Acheron”: direction is expressed as destination.
- *non si cala* ‘does not lower himself’ → “sink not down”: an intransitive verb and directional adverb replace the pronominal verb, and the negative follows the verb.

> 106 Ed io: “Se nuova legge non ti toglie  
> 107 memoria o uso all' amoroso canto,  
> 108 che mi solea quetar tutte mie voglie,  

And I: “If a new law take not from thee memory or skill in that song of love which was wont to calm my every desire,

- *nuova legge* ‘new law’ → “a new law”: English supplies the indefinite article.
- *non ti toglie* ‘does not take from thee’ → “take not from thee”: the Italian indicative becomes an English conditional subjunctive, with the negative and the person affected after the verb.
- *uso* ‘practice’ → “skill”: the translation expresses proficiency rather than its exercise.
- *all' amoroso canto* ‘to the loving song’ → “in that song of love”: the preposition suits the English noun of proficiency; a demonstrative replaces the article, and a phrase following the noun replaces the adjective preceding it.
- *mi solea quetar tutte mie voglie* ‘was wont to quiet for me all my desires’ → “was wont to calm my every desire”: the separate dative pronoun drops out, and the plural with ‘all’ becomes a distributive singular.

> 109 di ciò ti piaccia consolare alquanto  
> 110 l' anima mia, che, con la sua persona  
> 111 venendo qui, è affannata tanto.”  

may it please thee therewith to solace awhile my soul, that, with its mortal form journeying here, is sore distressed.”

- *di ciò ti piaccia* ‘with that may it please thee’ → “may it please thee therewith”: the instrumental phrase becomes an adverb and moves after the expression of wishing; “may” expresses the optative subjunctive.
- *consolare alquanto* ‘to comfort somewhat’ → “to solace awhile”: the adverb is taken as specifying duration rather than degree.
- *l' anima mia* ‘the soul of mine’ → “my soul”: the postposed possessive becomes a determiner before the noun.
- *con la sua persona* ‘with its body’ → “with its mortal form”: “form” renders the bodily sense of the noun, and “mortal” is supplied.
- *venendo qui* ‘coming here’ → “journeying here”: the verb expresses travel rather than motion towards the speaker.
- *è affannata tanto* ‘is distressed so much’ → “is sore distressed”: the degree adverb moves before the participle.

## Lines 112–133 — The Song and the Summons to the Mount

> 112 “*Amor che nella mente mi ragiona,*”  

“*Love that in my mind discourseth to me,*”

- *nella mente* ‘in the mind’ → “in my mind”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *mi ragiona* ‘to me discourses’ → “discourseth to me”: the indirect object follows the verb.

> 113 cominciò egli allor sì dolcemente,  
> 114 che la dolcezza ancor dentro mi suona.  

began he then so sweetly, that the sweetness yet within me sounds.

- *ancor* ‘still’ → “yet”: “yet” has the temporal sense ‘still, even now’.

> 115 Lo mio maestro ed io e quella gente  
> 116 ch' eran con lui parevan sì contenti,  
> 117 come a nessun toccasse altro la mente.  

My Master and I and that people who were with him, seemed so glad as if to aught else the mind of no one of them gave heed.

- *contenti* ‘content’ → “glad”: a different adjective for the state of those listening.
- *come a nessun toccasse altro la mente* ‘as if to no one should anything else touch the mind’ → “as if to aught else the mind of no one of them gave heed”: the Italian subject, *altro*, becomes the object of “to”; *la mente*, the direct object of ‘touch’, becomes the subject of “gave heed”. The dative *a nessun*, indicating whose mind is touched, becomes “of no one”, with “of them” supplied. “As if” makes explicit the hypothetical comparison expressed by *come* with the subjunctive.

> 118 Noi eravam tutti fissi ed attenti  
> 119 alle sue note; ed ecco il veglio onesto,  

We were all fixed and intent upon his notes; and lo the old man venerable,

- *il veglio onesto* ‘the venerable old man’ → “the old man venerable”: the single noun *veglio* is expanded to “old man”; the adjective retains its Italian position after the noun.

> 120 gridando: “Che è ciò, spiriti lenti?  

crying: “What is this ye laggard spirits?

- *spiriti lenti* ‘spirits slow’ → “ye laggard spirits”: “ye” marks the direct address explicitly, and “laggard” precedes the noun.

> 121 qual negligenza, quale stare è questo?  

what negligence, what tarrying is this?

- *stare* ‘staying’ → “tarrying”: the substantival infinitive becomes an English verbal noun, with the sense of delay.

> 122 Correte al monte a spogliarvi lo scoglio,  
> 123 ch' esser non lascia a voi Dio manifesto.”  

Haste to the mount and strip you of the slough, that lets not God be manifest to you.

- *Correte* ‘run’ → “Haste”: urgency rather than the specific movement is expressed.
- *a spogliarvi lo scoglio* ‘to strip off yourselves the slough’ → “and strip you of the slough”: the infinitive of purpose becomes a coordinated imperative; the thing stripped off, a direct object in Italian, is introduced by “of”.
- *esser non lascia a voi Dio manifesto* ‘be does not let to you God manifest’ → “lets not God be manifest to you”: “not” follows the finite verb; “God” precedes the infinitive of which it is the subject, and “to you” follows the predicate adjective.

> 124 Come quando, cogliendo biada o loglio,  
> 125 li colombi adunati alla pastura,  
> 126 queti senza mostrar l' usato orgoglio,  

As doves when gathering wheat or tares, all assembled at their repast, quiet and showing not their wonted pride,

- *Come quando, cogliendo* ‘as when, gathering’ → “As doves when gathering”: the subject is brought forward between the two conjunctions.
- *biada* ‘grain’ → “wheat”: a particular grain replaces the general term.
- *li colombi* ‘the doves’ → “doves”: the definite article is dropped in the generic comparison.
- *adunati alla pastura* ‘gathered at the feeding’ → “all assembled at their repast”: “all” is supplied, the article becomes a possessive, and the feeding is rendered as a meal.
- *senza mostrar* ‘without showing’ → “and showing not”: a preposition with an infinitive becomes a coordinated negative participial phrase.
- *l’ usato orgoglio* ‘the wonted pride’ → “their wonted pride”: the possessive replaces the definite article.

> 127 se cosa appare ond' elli abbian paura,  
> 128 subitamente lasciano star l' esca  
> 129 perchè assaliti son da maggior cura:  

if aught be seen whereof they have fear, straightway let stay their food, because they are assailed by greater care;

- *se cosa appare* ‘if something appears’ → “if aught be seen”: the indefinite noun becomes a pronoun, and the indicative verb of appearing becomes a passive subjunctive of seeing.
- *ond’ elli abbian paura* ‘of which they may have fear’ → “whereof they have fear”: the Italian relative clause has a subjunctive referring to the unspecified thing; the English gives no distinct sign of that mood.
- *l’ esca* ‘the food’ → “their food”: the possessive replaces the definite article.
- *assaliti son* ‘assailed are’ → “they are assailed”: the auxiliary precedes the participle.

> 130 così vid' io quella masnada fresca  
> 131 lasciar lo canto, e gire in ver la costa,  
> 132 come uom che va, nè sa dove riesca;  

so saw I that new company leave the singing, and go towards the hillside, like one who goes, but knoweth not where he may come forth;

- *quella masnada fresca* ‘that company fresh’ → “that new company”: “new” renders *fresca* in the sense ‘newly arrived’ and precedes its noun.
- *lo canto* ‘the song’ → “the singing”: the verbal noun emphasizes the activity rather than the song.
- *in ver* ‘in the direction of’ → “towards”: a compound expression becomes a single preposition.
- *come uom che va* ‘like a man who goes’ → “like one who goes”: the generic noun becomes an indefinite pronoun.
- *nè sa* ‘nor knows’ → “but knoweth not”: negative coordination becomes an adversative conjunction with the negation on the verb.
- *dove riesca* ‘where he should come out’ → “where he may come forth”: “may” conveys the subjunctive of the indirect question.

> 133 nè la nostra partita fu men tosta.  

nor was our parting less quick.

- *la nostra partita fu* ‘our departure was’ → “was our parting”: the verb precedes the subject after “nor”, and “parting” renders the noun of departure.

## Summary — Close in Phrase, Freer in Construction

Okey often keeps the Italian order even where English would ordinarily reverse it: *l’uccel divino* remains “the bird divine”, and the object still comes first in “oar he wills not”. Elsewhere he rearranges the words to bring related terms together. The delayed *della bella Aurora* becomes “fair Aurora’s” before the description of her cheeks, and the possessive construction remains intact despite the intervening clause of place in the Italian. Two regular adjustments generally pass unremarked in the comparison: Okey supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending suffices, and omits the article that Italian can place before a possessive, as in *Lo mio maestro*, “My Master”.

A close verbal echo need not mean an identical choice of words. The relation between *dolcemente* and *dolcezza* survives in “sweetly” and “sweetness”, while *rosseggia*, ‘glows red’, becomes the more fiery “burns red”, and *biada*, ‘grain’, the more specific “wheat”. Okey sometimes supplies a word to make a reference explicit: *un altro*, ‘another’, becomes “another whiteness”. Conversely, “I saw it” leaves unexpressed the sense of ‘again’ in *rividil*.

Changes of construction range from a shift of preposition to a reversal of grammatical roles. In *le caggion di man* the dative identifies the possessor; in “fall from her hand” the possessive does that work directly. The substantivized infinitives *volar* and *mover* become “flight” and “motion”, while the verb of matching gives way to “is equal to”. More freely still, where *altro* is what touches *la mente*, ‘the mind’, Okey makes the mind the subject that “gave heed”. Yet he can recast the grammar while keeping a conspicuous feature of the phrasing: “Bend, bend thy knees” dispenses with the dependent clause after *Fa, fa* but preserves the doubled command.
