# Purgatorio 12

## Lines 1–15 — Walking Erect and Looking Down

> 1 Di pari, come buoi che vanno a giogo,  
> 2 m' andava io con quella anima carca,  
> 3 fin che il sofferse il dolce pedagogo.  

Even in step, like oxen which go in the yoke, I went beside that burdened spirit, so long as the sweet pedagogue suffered it.

- *Di pari* ‘abreast’ → “Even in step”: the phrase of equality is made explicit in terms of the walkers’ step.
- *a giogo* ‘to yoke’ → “in the yoke”: the preposition changes and the definite article is supplied.
- *m' andava io* ‘went I on my way’ → “I went”: the emphatic subject moves before the verb, the pronominal turn is dropped, and the imperfect of continuing motion becomes a simple past.
- *con* ‘with’ → “beside”: accompaniment is rendered as position.
- *quella anima carca* ‘that soul burdened’ → “that burdened spirit”: “spirit” renders ‘soul’, and the adjective moves before the noun.
- *fin che* ‘until’ → “so long as”: the English expresses the duration rather than the limit.
- *il sofferse il dolce pedagogo* ‘it suffered the sweet pedagogue’ → “the sweet pedagogue suffered it”: the subject moves before the verb and the object pronoun after it.

> 4 Ma quando disse: “Lascia lui, e varca,  
> 5 chè qui è buon con la vela e coi remi,  
> 6 quantunque può ciascun, pinger sua barca”;  

But when he said: “Leave him, and press on, for here ’tis well that with sail and with oars, each one urge his bark along with all his might”;

- *varca* ‘pass on’ → “press on”: the English verb adds a sense of effort.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *è buon … pinger* ‘it is good … to push’ → “’tis well that” … “urge” … “along”: the predicative adjective becomes “well”, and the infinitive becomes the verb of a finite subjunctive clause; the sense of onward motion is brought out by “along”.
- *con la vela e coi remi* ‘with the sail and with the oars’ → “with sail and with oars”: both definite articles are dropped.
- *quantunque può ciascun* ‘as much as can each one’ → “each one” … “with all his might”: the postposed subject of ‘can’ becomes the subject of “urge”; the clause of degree becomes a phrase of manner and moves to the end.
- *sua barca* ‘his boat’ → “his bark”: a more literary term renders the noun.

> 7 dritto, sì come andar vuolsi, rife' mi  
> 8 con la persona, avvegna che i pensieri  
> 9 mi rimanessero e chinati e scemi.  

erect, even as is required for walking I made me again with my body, albeit my thoughts remained bowed down and shrunken.

- *sì come andar vuolsi* ‘just as one ought to walk’ → “even as is required for walking”: the impersonal expression of obligation becomes a passive, and the infinitive becomes a phrase with “for”.
- *rife' mi* ‘remade myself’ → “I made me again”: the prefix is rendered by a separate adverb after the pronoun, and “me” does duty for the reflexive.
- *con la persona* ‘with the person’ → “with my body”: the noun is taken in its bodily sense, and a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *avvegna che i pensieri mi rimanessero* ‘although the thoughts remained to me’ → “albeit my thoughts remained”: the dative of the person concerned becomes a possessive on the subject, and the Italian concessive subjunctive has no distinct marking in English.
- *e chinati e scemi* ‘both bent and diminished’ → “bowed down and shrunken”: the first member of the paired conjunction is dropped; “down” makes the direction explicit, and diminution is expressed as shrinkage.

> 10 Io m' era mosso, e seguia volentieri  
> 11 del mio maestro i passi, ed ambo e due  
> 12 già mostravam come eravam leggieri,  

I had moved me, and willingly was following my master’s steps, and both of us already were showing how light of foot we were,

- *Io m' era mosso* ‘I myself was moved’ → “I had moved me”: the Italian pluperfect of a reflexive verb takes the auxiliary ‘be’; English uses “had” and places the reflexive object after the participle.
- *seguia volentieri* ‘was following willingly’ → “willingly was following”: the progressive brings out the imperfect, and the adverb moves before the verb.
- *del mio maestro i passi* ‘of my master the steps’ → “my master’s steps”: the preposed phrase with ‘of’ becomes an English possessive, replacing the article on ‘steps’.
- *ambo e due* ‘both and two’ → “both of us”: the doubled expression is reduced to “both”, with the persons specified by “of us”.
- *mostravam* ‘showed’ → “were showing”: the imperfect is rendered by a progressive.
- *come eravam leggieri* ‘how we were light’ → “how light of foot we were”: the adjective moves up next to “how”, and “of foot” restricts its sense to walking.

> 13 quando mi disse: “Volgi gli occhi in giue:  

when he said to me: “Turn thine eyes downward:

- *mi disse* ‘to me he said’ → “he said to me”: the indirect object moves after the verb.
- *gli occhi* ‘the eyes’ → “thine eyes”: the possessive replaces the definite article.

> 14 buon ti sarà, per tranquillar la via,  
> 15 veder lo letto delle piante tue.”  

good will it be, for solace of thy way, to see the bed of the soles of thy feet.”

- *ti sarà* ‘to thee will be’ → “will it be”: the dative is dropped, and anticipatory “it” stands for the infinitive subject that follows.
- *per tranquillar la via* ‘to ease the way’ → “for solace of thy way”: the infinitive of purpose becomes a noun after “for”; its object becomes a phrase with “of”, and a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *delle piante tue* ‘of the soles of thine’ → “of the soles of thy feet”: “feet” makes explicit what the noun for ‘soles’ implies, and the possessive, which follows ‘soles’ in Italian, now precedes and qualifies “feet”.

## Lines 16–48 — The Images of Fallen Pride

> 16 Come, perchè di lor memoria sia,  
> 17 sopra i sepolti le tombe terragne  
> 18 portan segnato quel ch' elli eran pria;  

As in order that there be memory of them, the tombs on the ground over the buried bear figured what they were before;

- *di lor memoria sia* ‘of them memory may be’ → “there be memory of them”: English introduces existential “there”, puts the verb before “memory”, and moves “of them” after it.
- *sopra i sepolti le tombe terragne* ‘over the buried the ground-level tombs’ → “the tombs on the ground over the buried”: the subject comes before the phrase locating it; the adjective becomes the prepositional phrase “on the ground”.
- *segnato* ‘marked’ → “figured”: the English makes the pictorial sense of the participle explicit.

> 19 onde lì molte volte se ne piagne  
> 20 per la puntura della rimembranza,  
> 21 che solo ai pii dà delle calcagne:  

wherefore there, many a time men weep for them, because of the prick of remembrance which only to the pitiful gives spur;

- *molte volte* ‘many times’ → “many a time”: the plural becomes an idiom with a singular noun.
- *se ne piagne* ‘one weeps for them’ → “men weep for them”: the impersonal construction becomes a personal one with a generic plural subject; the complement expressed by the preverbal pronoun follows the verb.
- *della rimembranza* ‘of the remembrance’ → “of remembrance”: English uses the abstract noun without the definite article.
- *ai pii* ‘to the compassionate’ → “to the pitiful”: the adjective is taken in the sense of feeling pity; “pitiful” here has that active sense.
- *dà delle calcagne* ‘plies the heels’ → “gives spur”: an idiom of urging on with the heels becomes one of spurring; the plural body part gives way to singular “spur”.

> 22 sì vid' io lì, ma di miglior sembianza,  
> 23 secondo l' artificio, figurato  
> 24 quanto per via di fuor dal monte avanza.  

so saw I sculptured there, but of better similitude according to the craftsmanship, all that which for road projects from the mount.

- *lì … ma di miglior sembianza, secondo l' artificio, figurato* ‘there, but of better likeness according to the craftsmanship, figured’ → “sculptured there, but of better similitude according to the craftsmanship”: the participle comes forward, before even the adverb of place, and “sculptured” specifies the manner of figuring.
- *quanto* ‘as much as’ → “all that which”: the single relative of extent is expanded into an antecedent and a relative pronoun.
- *di fuor dal monte avanza* ‘out from the mount extends’ → “projects from the mount”: “projects” comes before its complement and incorporates the outward direction.

> 25 Vedea colui, che fu nobil creato  
> 26 più ch' altra creatura, giù dal cielo  
> 27 folgoreggiando scendere da un lato.  

I saw him who was created nobler far than other creature, on one side descending like lightning from heaven.

- *Vedea* ‘I was seeing’ → “I saw”: the imperfect becomes a simple past, as it does in the subsequent repetitions.
- *fu nobil creato più ch' altra creatura* ‘was noble created more than other creature’ → “was created nobler far than other creature”: the participle precedes the adjective, the separated comparative marker is absorbed into “nobler”, and “far” is supplied as an intensifier.
- *giù dal cielo folgoreggiando scendere da un lato* ‘down from heaven flashing like lightning to descend on one side’ → “on one side descending like lightning from heaven”: the last phrase comes first; the infinitive becomes a participle, and the Italian participle becomes a comparison. “From heaven” follows the comparison, with no separate word for ‘down’.

> 28 Vedea Briareo, fitto dal telo  
> 29 celestial, giacer dall' altra parte,  
> 30 grave alla terra per lo mortal gelo.  

I saw Briareus, transfixed by the celestial bolt, on the other side, lying on the earth heavy with the death chill.

- *Vedea* ‘I was seeing’ → “I saw”: imperfect to simple past.
- *dal telo celestial* ‘by the bolt celestial’ → “by the celestial bolt”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *giacer dall' altra parte* ‘to lie on the other side’ → “on the other side, lying”: the locative comes first and the infinitive becomes a participle.
- *grave alla terra* ‘heavy to the earth’ → “on the earth heavy”: the phrase with ‘to’ describes what bears the weight; English turns it into a locative attached to “lying” and puts “heavy” after it.
- *per lo mortal gelo* ‘because of the mortal chill’ → “with the death chill”: the causal relation is expressed by “with”, and the adjective becomes an attributive noun.

> 31 Vedea Timbreo, vedea Pallade e Marte,  
> 32 armati ancora intorno al padre loro,  
> 33 mirar le membra de' giganti sparte.  

I saw Thymbræus, I saw Pallas and Mars, armed yet, around their father, gazing on the scattered limbs of the giants.

- *Vedea … vedea* ‘I was seeing … I was seeing’ → “I saw” … “I saw”: both imperfects become simple pasts.
- *intorno al padre loro* ‘around the father of theirs’ → “around their father”: the postposed possessive comes before the noun.
- *mirar* ‘to gaze at’ → “gazing on”: a participle replaces the infinitive after the verb of seeing.
- *le membra de' giganti sparte* ‘the limbs of the giants scattered’ → “the scattered limbs of the giants”: the participle moves from the end of the phrase to a position immediately before the noun it qualifies.

> 34 Vedea Nembrot a piè del gran lavoro,  
> 35 quasi smarrito, e riguardar le genti  
> 36 che in Sennaar con lui superbi foro.  

I saw Nimrod at the foot of his great labour, as though bewildered, and looking at the people who were proud with him in Shinar.

- *Vedea* ‘I was seeing’ → “I saw”: imperfect to simple past.
- *del gran lavoro* ‘of the great labour’ → “of his great labour”: “his” replaces the definite article.
- *quasi smarrito* ‘as if lost’ → “as though bewildered”: the participle is rendered specifically in its sense of mental disorientation.
- *riguardar* ‘to look at’ → “looking at”: the infinitive dependent on the verb of seeing becomes a participle.
- *le genti* ‘the peoples’ → “the people”: a singular collective stands for the plural.
- *in Sennaar con lui superbi foro* ‘in Shinar with him proud were’ → “were proud with him in Shinar”: the verb and its predicate come first, and the two prepositional phrases follow in reverse order.

> 37 O Niobe, con che occhi dolenti  
> 38 vedeva io te, segnata in su la strada,  
> 39 tra sette e sette tuoi figliuoli spenti!  

O Niobe, with what sorrowing eyes I saw thee graven upon the road between seven and seven thy children slain!

- *occhi dolenti* ‘eyes sorrowing’ → “sorrowing eyes”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *vedeva io te* ‘was seeing I thee’ → “I saw thee”: the explicit subject precedes the verb, and the imperfect becomes a simple past.
- *segnata* ‘marked’ → “graven”: the English specifies a manner of marking.
- *spenti* ‘extinguished’ → “slain”: the figurative participle is rendered by a word explicitly denoting killing.

> 40 O Saul, come in su la propria spada  
> 41 quivi parevi morto in Gelboè,  
> 42 che poi non sentì pioggia nè rugiada!  

O Saul, how upon thine own sword there didst thou appear dead on Gilboa, which thereafter felt nor rain nor dew!

- *la propria spada* ‘one’s own sword’ → “thine own sword”: the subject-referring possessive is made explicitly second person.
- *parevi* ‘thou wert seeming’ → “didst thou appear”: the imperfect becomes a simple past expressed with an inverted auxiliary.
- *in Gelboè* ‘in Gilboa’ → “on Gilboa”: the preposition is adapted to a mountain.
- *poi non sentì pioggia nè rugiada* ‘afterwards did not feel rain nor dew’ → “thereafter felt nor rain nor dew”: the negative on the verb is replaced by a first “nor” before “rain”.

> 43 O folle Aragne, sì vedea io te  
> 44 già mezza aragna, trista in su gli stracci  
> 45 dell' opera che mal per te si fe'!  

O mad Arachne, so saw I thee already half spider, sad upon the shreds of the work which to thy hurt was wrought by thee!

- *vedea* ‘was seeing’ → “saw”: imperfect to simple past.
- *dell' opera che mal per te si fe'* ‘of the work which to thy hurt was made’ → “of the work which to thy hurt was wrought by thee”: “wrought” renders the verb of making; the passive reading of the *si* construction is retained, but “by thee” supplies an agent not expressed as such in Italian.

> 46 O Roboam, già non par che minacci  
> 47 quivi il tuo segno; ma pien di spavento  
> 48 nel porta un carro prima che altri il cacci!  

O Rehoboam, now thine image there seems no more to threaten; but full of terror a chariot beareth it away ere chase be given!

- *già non par che minacci quivi il tuo segno* ‘now it does not seem that thy image threatens there’ → “now thine image there seems no more to threaten”: the postposed noun becomes the subject of “seems”; “there” moves with it, and the finite subjunctive clause becomes an infinitive. “Image” specifies the sense of ‘sign’, and “no more” gives the negation an explicitly temporal force.
- *nel porta un carro* ‘it away carries a chariot’ → “a chariot beareth it away”: the subject comes first; the object and the directional sense of the combined pronouns follow the verb.
- *prima che altri il cacci* ‘before another chases him’ → “ere chase be given”: the active subjunctive becomes a passive construction with “chase” as a noun; neither the indefinite pursuer nor the pronominal object is expressed.

## Lines 49–81 — The Fall of the Proud and the Approaching Angel

> 49 Mostrava ancor lo duro pavimento  
> 50 come Almeon a sua madre fe' caro  
> 51 parer lo sventurato adornamento.  

It showed—the hard pavement—again how Alcmæon made the luckless ornament seem costly to his mother.

- *Mostrava ancor lo duro pavimento* ‘showed again the hard pavement’ → “It showed—the hard pavement—again”: the postposed subject is anticipated by “It”, then named before the adverb.
- *a sua madre fe' caro parer lo sventurato adornamento* ‘to his mother made costly seem the luckless ornament’ → “made the luckless ornament seem costly to his mother”: the ornament comes immediately after the causative verb, the infinitive before its complement, and the phrase naming the mother last.

> 52 Mostrava come i figli si gittaro  
> 53 sopra Sennacherib dentro dal tempio,  
> 54 e come, morto lui, quivi il lasciaro.  

It showed how his sons flung themselves upon Sennacherib within the temple, and how, him slain, there they left him.

- *i figli* ‘the sons’ → “his sons”: the article becomes a possessive.
- *si gittaro* ‘themselves flung’ → “flung themselves”: the reflexive follows the verb.
- *morto lui* ‘dead he’ → “him slain”: the pronoun comes first, in object form, and the participle specifies a violent death.
- *il lasciaro* ‘him they left’ → “they left him”: the object follows the verb.

> 55 Mostrava la ruina e il crudo scempio  
> 56 che fe' Tamiri, quando disse a Ciro:  
> 57 “Sangue sitisti, ed io di sangue t' empio.”  

It showed the destruction and the cruel slaughter which Tomyris wrought when she said to Cyrus: “For blood thou didst thirst and with blood I fill thee!”

- *ruina* ‘ruin’ → “destruction”: a different noun.
- *che fe' Tamiri* ‘which wrought Tomyris’ → “which Tomyris wrought”: the subject precedes the verb.
- *Sangue sitisti* ‘blood thou thirstedst for’ → “For blood thou didst thirst”: English marks the object of thirst with a preposition, puts it before the noun, and forms the past with an auxiliary.
- *io di sangue t' empio* ‘I with blood thee fill’ → “with blood I fill thee”: the prepositional phrase precedes the subject and the object follows the verb.

> 58 Mostrava come in rotta si fuggiro  
> 59 gli Assiri, poi che fu morto Oloferne,  
> 60 ed anche le reliquie del martiro.  

It showed how in a rout the Assyrians fled, after Holofernes was slain, and also the relics of the assassination.

- *in rotta* ‘in rout’ → “in a rout”: the indefinite article is supplied.
- *si fuggiro gli Assiri* ‘fled away the Assyrians’ → “the Assyrians fled”: the subject precedes the verb; the pronominal form of the Italian verb has no separate equivalent.
- *fu morto Oloferne* ‘was slain Holofernes’ → “Holofernes was slain”: the subject precedes the passive.
- *martiro* ‘martyrdom’ → “assassination”: the noun is rendered by the particular manner of killing rather than by a term for the suffering or death.

> 61 Vedeva Troia in cenere e in caverne:  
> 62 O Ilion, come te basso e vile  
> 63 Mostrava il segno che lì si discerne!  

I saw Troy in ashes and in ruins: O Ilion, thee how base and vile It showed—the sculpture which there is discerned!

- *Vedeva* ‘I was seeing’ → “I saw”: a simple past renders the imperfect.
- *in cenere e in caverne* ‘in ash and in hollows’ → “in ashes and in ruins”: the first noun becomes plural; the second is rendered by the state of the place rather than the hollows visible in it.
- *come te basso e vile* ‘how thee low and vile’ → “thee how base and vile”: the object precedes the exclamatory adverb, and “base” renders ‘low’.
- *Mostrava il segno* ‘showed the image’ → “It showed—the sculpture”: “It” anticipates the postposed subject; the more specific noun identifies the image as carved.
- *che lì si discerne* ‘which there one discerns’ → “which there is discerned”: the Italian construction with *si* becomes an English passive.

> 64 Qual di pennel fu maestro o di stile,  
> 65 che ritraesse l' ombre e i tratti, ch' ivi  
> 66 mirar farieno ogn' ingegno sottile?  

What master were he of brush or of graver, who drew the shades and the lineaments, which there would make every subtle wit stare?

- *Qual di pennel fu maestro o di stile* ‘what master of brush or of stylus was there’ → “What master were he of brush or of graver”: the two phrases naming instruments are brought together after the verb and pronoun; the second names a cutting tool specifically. Hypothetical “were he” replaces the past indicative.
- *che ritraesse* ‘who might depict’ → “who drew”: a simple past replaces the imperfect subjunctive of the relative clause; the verb specifies drawing.
- *i tratti* ‘the features’ → “the lineaments”: a different noun for the depicted features or strokes.
- *mirar farieno ogn' ingegno sottile* ‘gaze would make every wit subtle’ → “would make every subtle wit stare”: the infinitive moves from before the causative to after its object, and the adjective precedes its noun; “stare” gives the gaze a sense of wonder.

> 67 Morti li morti, e i vivi parean vivi:  
> 68 non vide me' di me chi vide il vero,  
> 69 quant' io calcai fin che chinato givi.  

Dead seemed the dead, and the living, living. He saw not better than I who saw the reality of all that I trod upon while I was going bent down.

- *Morti li morti, e i vivi parean vivi* ‘dead the dead, and the living seemed living’ → “Dead seemed the dead, and the living, living”: the expressed verb is transferred from the second clause to the first, leaving the second elliptical.
- *non vide* ‘not saw’ → “He saw not”: the negative follows the verb, and “He” anticipates the subject expressed later by the relative.
- *di me* ‘than me’ → “than I”: the comparison uses the subject form, as for an understood verb.
- *il vero, quant' io calcai* ‘the true, as much as I trod’ → “the reality of all that I trod upon”: the substantivized adjective becomes an abstract noun; the extent of what was trodden becomes an “of” phrase depending on that noun, and the English verb takes “upon”.
- *fin che chinato givi* ‘as long as bent I went’ → “while I was going bent down”: a progressive renders the imperfect; the description of posture follows the verb and adds “down”.

> 70 Or superbite, e via col viso altiero,  
> 71 figliuoli d' Eva, e non chinate il volto,  
> 72 sì che veggiate il vostro mal sentiero!  

Now wax proud and on with haughty visage, ye children of Eve, and bow not down your faces, so that ye may see your evil path!

- *superbite* ‘grow proud’ → “wax proud”: the single imperative verb becomes a verb with a predicative adjective.
- *col viso altiero* ‘with the visage haughty’ → “with haughty visage”: the article drops out and the adjective precedes its noun.
- *figliuoli d' Eva* ‘children of Eve’ → “ye children of Eve”: a pronoun marks the address.
- *non chinate il volto* ‘do not lower the face’ → “bow not down your faces”: the negative follows the verb; “bow” takes “down”, and the singular with definite article becomes a possessive plural.
- *sì che veggiate* ‘so that you see’ → “so that ye may see”: the modal renders the subjunctive.

> 73 Più era già per noi del monte volto,  
> 74 e del cammin del sole assai più speso,  
> 75 che non stimava l' animo non sciolto;  

Already more of the mount was circled by us, and of the sun’s path much more spent, than the mind, not set free, esteemed;

- *Più era già per noi del monte volto* ‘more was already by us of the mount turned’ → “Already more of the mount was circled by us”: the adverb comes first, the partitive phrase joins “more”, and the agent follows the completed passive; “circled” interprets the turning as movement around the mount.
- *del cammin del sole* ‘of the path of the sun’ → “of the sun’s path”: the dependent noun becomes a possessive before “path”.
- *che non stimava l' animo non sciolto* ‘than estimated the mind not loosed’ → “than the mind, not set free, esteemed”: the negative after the comparative has no separate English equivalent. The subject and its participial modifier precede the verb, and the single participle is rendered by a phrase.

> 76 quando colui, che sempre innanzi atteso  
> 77 andava, incominciò: “Drizza la testa;  
> 78 non è più tempo da gir sì sospeso.  

when he, who ever in front of me alert was going, began: “Lift up thy head, this is no time to go thus engrossed.

- *colui* ‘that one’ → “he”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *innanzi* ‘ahead’ → “in front of me”: the person relative to whom the position is reckoned is supplied.
- *andava* ‘went’ → “was going”: the progressive brings out the continuing action of the imperfect.
- *Drizza la testa* ‘straighten the head’ → “Lift up thy head”: the direction of motion is explicit, and a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *non è più tempo* ‘it is no longer time’ → “this is no time”: a demonstrative is supplied, the negative modifies the noun, and the sense of ‘longer’ has no separate equivalent.
- *sì sospeso* ‘so suspended’ → “thus engrossed”: the description is interpreted as a state of absorbed attention.

> 79 Vedi colà un angel che s' appresta  
> 80 per venir verso noi; vedi che torna  
> 81 dal servigio del dì l' ancella sesta.  

See there an angel who is making ready to come towards us; look how the sixth handmaiden is returning from the day’s service.

- *s' appresta* ‘prepares himself’ → “is making ready”: the simple present becomes progressive, and an intransitive phrase replaces the reflexive construction.
- *vedi che* ‘see that’ → “look how”: both the imperative and the connective change.
- *torna dal servigio del dì l' ancella sesta* ‘returns from the service of the day the handmaiden sixth’ → “the sixth handmaiden is returning from the day’s service”: the subject moves before the verb, the ordinal before its noun, and the simple present becomes progressive; the phrase depending on ‘service’ becomes a preceding possessive.

## Lines 82–114 — The Angel and the Upward Stair

> 82 Di riverenza gli atti e il viso adorna,  
> 83 sì che i diletti lo inviarci in suso:  

Adorn with reverence thy bearing and thy face, so that it may delight him to send us upward;

- *Di riverenza … adorna* ‘with reverence … adorn’ → “Adorn with reverence”: the imperative moves from the end of the clause to the beginning, ahead of both the prepositional phrase and the objects.
- *gli atti e il viso* ‘the gestures and the face’ → “thy bearing and thy face”: the plural gestures become a singular term for deportment, and both definite articles become possessives.
- *sì che i diletti* ‘so that to him may give delight’ → “so that it may delight him”: English introduces an anticipatory subject and puts the person pleased after the verb as its direct object.
- *lo inviarci in suso* ‘the sending-us on high’ → “to send us upward”: the article-marked infinitive, the Italian subject of *diletti*, becomes a to-infinitive anticipated by “it”; the attached object pronoun becomes a separate word, and the phrase of direction becomes one adverb.

> 84 pensa che questo dì mai non raggiorna.”  

think that this day never dawns again.”

- *mai non raggiorna* ‘never not dawns anew’ → “never dawns again”: English uses one negative where Italian has negative concord, and gives the iterative force of the verb in a separate adverb.

> 85 Io era ben del suo ammonir uso,  
> 86 pur di non perder tempo, sì che in quella  
> 87 materia non potea parlarmi chiuso.  

Right well was I used to his monitions never to lose time, so that in that matter he could not speak to me darkly.

- *Io era ben … uso* ‘I was well … accustomed’ → “Right well was I used”: the adverb is intensified and moved to the front, the subject follows the finite verb, and “used” precedes its complement rather than following it.
- *del suo ammonir* ‘to his admonishing’ → “to his monitions”: a singular nominalized infinitive becomes a plural noun for the individual warnings.
- *pur di non perder tempo* ‘ever about not losing time’ → “never to lose time”: the insistence of *pur* and the negation of *non* are taken together as “never,” modifying the loss of time rather than the admonishing; *di* is absorbed into the infinitive construction.
- *non potea parlarmi chiuso* ‘he could not speak-to-me obscurely’ → “he could not speak to me darkly”: the attached pronoun becomes a prepositional phrase, and the adjective *chiuso* (‘closed’), used adverbially, becomes an adverb with a different image.

> 88 A noi venia la creatura bella  
> 89 bianco vestita, e nella faccia quale  
> 90 par tremolando mattutina stella.  

To us came the beauteous creature, robed in white, and in his countenance, such as a tremulous star at morn appears.

- *venia* ‘was coming’ → “came”: the ongoing movement of the imperfect is given in the simple past.
- *la creatura bella* ‘the creature beautiful’ → “the beauteous creature”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *bianco vestita* ‘in white dressed’ → “robed in white”: the participle precedes the color, which is introduced by a preposition.
- *nella faccia* ‘in the face’ → “in his countenance”: the article becomes a possessive, and “countenance” renders *faccia*.
- *quale … par* ‘such as … appears’ → “such as a tremulous star at morn appears”: the verb, which immediately follows the comparative in Italian, is deferred until after its subject.
- *tremolando mattutina stella* ‘trembling, a morning star’ → “a tremulous star at morn”: the gerund describing how the star appears becomes an adjective modifying the star; the adjective of time becomes a phrase after the noun.

> 91 Le braccia aperse, ed indi aperse l' ale;  

His arms he opened and then outspread his wings;

- *Le braccia* ‘the arms’ → “His arms”: the definite article becomes a possessive.
- *ed indi aperse l' ale* ‘and then opened the wings’ → “and then outspread his wings”: the repetition of the first verb is varied, and the article again becomes a possessive.

> 92 disse: “Venite; qui son presso i gradi,  
> 93 ed agevolemente omai si sale.”  

he said: “Come; here nigh are the steps, and easily now is ascent made.”

- *qui son presso i gradi* ‘here are near the steps’ → “here nigh are the steps”: the adverb of proximity moves before the verb.
- *omai si sale* ‘by now one goes up’ → “now is ascent made”: *omai* is reduced to “now,” and the impersonal verb becomes a passive construction with the action expressed by a noun.

> 94 A questo invito vengon molto radi.  

To this announcement few be they who come.

- *A questo invito* ‘to this invitation’ → “To this announcement”: the invitation is termed an announcement.
- *vengon molto radi* ‘come very few’ → “few be they who come”: “few” becomes the predicate of a new main clause, the verb of motion moves into a relative clause, and the intensifier is omitted.

> 95 O gente umana per volar su nata,  
> 96 perchè a poco vento così cadi?  

O human folk, born to fly upward, why at a breath of wind thus fall ye down?

- *gente umana* ‘folk human’ → “human folk”: the adjective moves before the noun.
- *per volar su nata* ‘to fly upward born’ → “born to fly upward”: the participle moves before the phrase of purpose.
- *a poco vento* ‘at a little wind’ → “at a breath of wind”: an expression of quantity becomes a noun phrase naming a slight movement of air.
- *così cadi* ‘thus thou fallest’ → “thus fall ye down”: the singular address to the collective becomes plural, and the direction implicit in falling is made explicit.

> 97 Menocci ove la roccia era tagliata;  
> 98 quivi mi battèo l' ale per la fronte,  
> 99 poi mi promise sicura l' andata.  

He led us where the rock was cut; there he beat his wings upon my forehead, then did promise me my journey secure.

- *Menocci* ‘led-us’ → “He led us”: the attached object becomes a separate pronoun after the verb.
- *mi battèo l' ale per la fronte* ‘against me he beat the wings across the forehead’ → “he beat his wings upon my forehead”: the pronoun denoting the person touched becomes a possessive on “forehead”; the wings too receive a possessive, and *per* becomes “upon.”
- *mi promise* ‘to me he promised’ → “did promise me”: an auxiliary carries the past tense, and the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *sicura l' andata* ‘safe the going’ → “my journey secure”: the predicative adjective follows the noun, and the article-marked noun of action becomes a journey specified as the speaker’s.

> 100 Come a man destra, per salire al monte,  
> 101 dove siede la chiesa che soggioga  
> 102 la ben guidata sopra Rubaconte,  

As on the right hand, to ascend the mount where stands the church which, over Rubaconte, dominates the well-guided city,

- *salire al monte* ‘go up to the mount’ → “ascend the mount”: the destination becomes the direct object of a transitive verb.
- *siede* ‘sits’ → “stands”: the verb of posture changes in describing a building’s location.
- *soggioga … sopra Rubaconte* ‘holds in subjection … above Rubaconte’ → “over Rubaconte, dominates”: the locative phrase is moved ahead of the verb and its object; “dominates” renders the verb of subjection.
- *la ben guidata* ‘the well-guided [one]’ → “the well-guided city”: a noun is supplied for the substantivized participle.

> 103 si rompe del montar l' ardita foga,  
> 104 per le scalee, che si fero ad etade  
> 105 ch' era sicuro il quaderno e la doga:  

the bold scarp of the ascent is broken by the steps, which were made in the times when the records and the measure were safe.

- *si rompe del montar l' ardita foga* ‘is broken, of the climbing, the bold rush’ → “the bold scarp of the ascent is broken”: the subject moves before the passive, its dependent phrase moves after it, and the image of headlong motion becomes a physical “scarp”; the nominalized infinitive becomes “the ascent.”
- *che si fero* ‘which made themselves’ → “which were made”: the Italian passive with *si* is expressed by a passive auxiliary and participle.
- *ad etade ch'* ‘in an age in which’ → “in the times when”: singular becomes plural, and the relative is explicitly temporal.
- *era sicuro il quaderno e la doga* ‘was safe the ledger and the stave’ → “the records and the measure were safe”: the compound subject comes first and takes plural agreement; the ledger is rendered by its contents, in the plural, and the stave by the measure it represents.

> 106 così s' allenta la ripa che cade  
> 107 quivi ben ratta dall' altro girone;  

even so is the bank made easier, which here right steeply falls from the other cornice,

- *così s' allenta la ripa* ‘so eases the bank’ → “even so is the bank made easier”: “even” reinforces the comparison, and the verb of abatement becomes a passive with a comparative adjective.
- *che cade quivi ben ratta* ‘which falls here very steep’ → “which here right steeply falls”: the place and degree expressions precede the verb, and the adjective agreeing with the bank becomes an adverb modifying its fall.
- *dall' altro girone* ‘from the other circuit’ → “from the other cornice”: a term for the encircling course is rendered by one for the projecting ledge.

> 108 ma quinci e quindi l' alta pietra rade.  

but on this side and on that the high rock grazes.

- *quinci e quindi* ‘here and there’ → “on this side and on that”: the two adverbs are expanded into phrases specifying the sides.

> 109 Noi volgendo ivi le nostre persone,  
> 110 “*Beati pauperes spiritu*” voci  
> 111 cantaron sì che nol diria sermone.  

While we were turning there our persons, “*Beati pauperes spiritu*” voices so sweetly sang, that no speech would tell it.

- *Noi volgendo* ‘we turning’ → “While we were turning”: the gerund with its own subject becomes a finite progressive clause with an explicit temporal conjunction.
- *cantaron sì* ‘sang so’ → “so sweetly sang”: the modifier moves before the verb, and the quality of the singing, left implicit in *sì*, is supplied.
- *che nol diria sermone* ‘that not-it would tell speech’ → “that no speech would tell it”: negation moves from the verb to the noun, the subject precedes the verb, and the object pronoun follows it.

> 112 Ahi! quanto son diverse quelle foci  
> 113 dalle infernali: chè quivi per canti  
> 114 s' entra, e laggiù per lamenti feroci.  

Ah! how different are these openings from those in Hell! for here we enter through songs, and down there through fierce wailings.

- *son diverse* ‘are different’ → “different are”: the adjective moves ahead of the verb to follow the exclamative “how.”
- *quelle foci* ‘those mouths’ → “these openings”: the demonstrative changes from distant to near, and the image of mouths is rendered as openings.
- *dalle infernali* ‘from the infernal [ones]’ → “from those in Hell”: the substantivized adjective becomes a pronoun with a locative phrase.
- *quivi per canti s' entra* ‘there through songs one enters’ → “here we enter through songs”: the viewpoint changes from ‘there’ to “here,” the impersonal verb becomes first-person plural, and the phrase of means follows the verb.
- *lamenti feroci* ‘laments fierce’ → “fierce wailings”: the adjective precedes the noun, and “wailings” emphasizes the audible lament.

Now were we mounting up by the sacred steps,

- *Già montavam* ‘already we were ascending’ → “Now were we mounting”: the adverb marks the moment rather than saying ‘already’; the imperfect becomes a progressive, with the subject after the auxiliary.
- *li scaglion santi* ‘the steps sacred’ → “the sacred steps”: the adjective precedes the noun.

> 116 ed esser mi parea troppo più lieve,  
> 117 che per lo pian non mi parea davanti;  

and meseemed I was exceeding lighter, than meseemed before on the flat;

- *ed esser mi parea troppo più lieve* ‘and to be seemed to me very much lighter’ → “and meseemed I was exceeding lighter”: the infinitive, placed first in Italian, becomes a finite clause after “meseemed”; “exceeding” intensifies the English comparative.
- *che per lo pian non mi parea davanti* ‘than along the level ground it seemed to me before’ → “than meseemed before on the flat”: the phrase describing the ground moves to the end, and ‘along’ becomes “on”; the non-negative *non* of the comparison has no English counterpart.

> 118 ond' io: “Maestro, di', qual cosa greve  
> 119 levata s' è da me, che nulla quasi

- *levata s’è da me* ‘lifted has itself from me’ → “has been lifted from me”: the pronominal construction becomes an English passive, with the auxiliaries before the participle.
- *nulla quasi per me fatica andando si riceve* ‘no, almost, by me, toil, going, is received’ → “scarce any toil is perceived by me in journeying”: “scarce any” brings the two qualifications together before the noun; ‘receive’ becomes “perceived,” the agent follows the passive verb, and the gerund moves to the end with “in” supplied.

> 121 Rispose: “Quando i *P*, che son rimasi

- *ancor nel volto tuo presso ch’ estinti* ‘still in the face of thine almost extinguished’ → “still nearly extinguished on thy face”: the phrase of place follows the participle, ‘in’ becomes “on,” and the possessive precedes the noun.
- *saranno, come l’un, del tutto rasi* ‘will be, like the one, wholly scraped away’ → “shall, like the one, be wholly rased out”: the comparison comes between the future auxiliary and “be,” rather than after the future form of ‘be.’

> 124 fien li tuoi piè dal buon voler sì vinti,  
> 125 che non pur non fatica sentiranno,

thy feet shall be so vanquished by goodwill, that not only will they feel it no toil, but it shall be a delight to them to be urged upward.”

- *fien li tuoi piè dal buon voler sì vinti* ‘shall be thy feet by the good will so vanquished’ → “thy feet shall be so vanquished by goodwill”: the subject precedes the verb, the agent follows the participle, and the article before ‘good will’ is dropped.
- *che non pur non fatica sentiranno* ‘that not only no toil will they feel’ → “that not only will they feel it no toil”: the verb comes before “no toil,” and “it” is supplied as the object of “feel,” with “no toil” as its complement.
- *ma fia diletto loro esser su pinti* ‘but will be a delight to them to be upward pushed’ → “but it shall be a delight to them to be urged upward”: anticipatory “it” stands for the infinitive subject; “urged” renders ‘pushed,’ and the direction follows the participle.

> 127 Allor fec' io, come color che vanno  
> 128 con cosa in capo non da lor saputa,  
> 129 se non che i cenni altrui sospicar fanno:  

Then did I, like those who go with something on their head unknown to them, save that another’s signs make them suspect;

- *con cosa in capo non da lor saputa* ‘with a thing on the head not by them known’ → “with something on their head unknown to them”: the indefinite “something” replaces the noun, the possessive is supplied, and a negated passive with its agent before the participle becomes an adjective with “to them” after it.
- *i cenni altrui* ‘the signs of another’ → “another’s signs”: the possessive precedes the noun.
- *sospicar fanno* ‘suspect make’ → “make them suspect”: the causative verb comes first, and the object “them” is supplied.

> 130 per che la mano ad accertar s' aiuta,  
> 131 e cerca e trova, e quell' officio adempie  
> 132 che non si può fornir per la veduta;  

wherefore the hand lends its aid to make certain, and searches, and finds, and fulfils that office which cannot be furnished by the sight;

- *la mano ad accertar s’aiuta* ‘the hand to make certain is brought into use’ → “the hand lends its aid to make certain”: where the Italian speaks of having recourse to the hand, the English makes the hand the giver of aid; the purpose infinitive follows the main verb.
- *quell’officio adempie* ‘that office fulfils’ → “fulfils that office”: the object follows the verb.
- *non si può fornir* ‘one cannot accomplish’ → “cannot be furnished”: the *si* construction becomes an English passive, and “furnished” renders ‘accomplish’ or ‘perform.’
- *per la veduta* ‘through sight’ → “by the sight”: the means is introduced by “by” rather than ‘through.’

> 133 e con le dita della destra scempie  
> 134 trovai pur sei le lettere, che incise  
> 135 quel dalle chiavi a me sopra le tempie:  

and with the fingers of my right hand outspread, I found but six the letters, which he with the keys had cut upon me over the temples:

- *della destra scempie* ‘of the right [hand], separated’ → “of my right hand outspread”: “my” is supplied, “hand” is made explicit, and “outspread” renders the adjective describing the fingers as apart.
- *incise quel dalle chiavi a me* ‘cut that one of the keys on me’ → “he with the keys had cut upon me”: the subject moves before the verb, the demonstrative becomes “he,” and “with” expresses the identifying attribute; the Italian simple past becomes a pluperfect.

> 136 a che guardando il mio duca sorrise.  

whereat my Leader looking did smile.

- *a che guardando il mio duca sorrise* ‘at which looking my leader smiled’ → “whereat my Leader looking did smile”: “whereat” combines preposition and relative, the subject precedes the participle, and the simple past is expanded with “did.”

## Summary — Italian Order in English Prose

Okey often brings a subject forward, places an adjective before its noun, or draws together words separated in Italian: in *le membra de’ giganti sparte*, the final participle moves next to its noun in “the scattered limbs of the giants”. Yet he keeps an Italian inversion in “so saw I thee”, and “between seven and seven” preserves the wording of *tra sette e sette* rather than giving the total. The repeated *Vedea* and *Mostrava* likewise remain recognizable in “I saw” and “It showed”.

Changes of construction often accompany these shifts of order. After a verb of seeing, the infinitives *giacer* and *mirar* become the participles “lying” and “gazing on”. The imperfect may become a simple past, as in “I saw”, or a progressive, as in “was following”. An impersonal construction need not stay impersonal: *se ne piagne* becomes “men weep for them”, whereas *si sale* becomes “is ascent made”, with the act of ascending expressed by a noun. More generally, English supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending suffices, and drops the article before a possessive, as in *li tuoi piè*, “thy feet”.

Okey sometimes makes a word more specific: *segnata* (‘marked’) becomes “graven”, and *spenti* (‘extinguished’) becomes “slain”. He also supplies words to bring out a sense left implicit: *leggieri* becomes “light of foot”, and *cantaron sì* becomes “so sweetly sang”. Elsewhere a distinction falls away: *molto* has no counterpart in “few be they who come”, and *non è più tempo* loses the explicit sense of ‘no longer’ in “this is no time”. Such departures sit beside remarkably close turns such as “I had moved me”, which retains the pronominal form of *Io m’ era mosso* even where English could readily do without it.
