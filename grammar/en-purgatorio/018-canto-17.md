# Purgatorio 17

## Lines 1–9 — The Sun Seen Again Through Mist

> 1 Ricorditi, lettor, se mai nell' alpe  
> 2 ti colse nebbia, per la qual vedessi  
> 3 non altrimenti che per pelle talpe,  

Reader, if ever in the mountains a mist hath caught thee, through which thou sawest not otherwise than moles do through the skin, remember

- *Ricorditi, lettor* ‘remember, reader’ → “Reader” … “remember”: the address comes first, and the imperative is postponed until after the conditional clause; the Italian pronominal form is rendered by a simple English verb.
- *nell' alpe* ‘in the mountain country’ → “in the mountains”: a collective singular becomes a plural.
- *ti colse nebbia* ‘thee caught mist’ → “a mist hath caught thee”: the subject moves before the verb and takes an indefinite article, the object moves after the verb, and the simple past becomes a present perfect.
- *vedessi* ‘thou mightest see’ → “thou sawest”: the imperfect subjunctive becomes a simple past indicative.
- *che per pelle talpe* ‘than through skin moles’ → “than moles do through the skin”: the subject precedes the prepositional phrase, “do” stands for the verb left understood in Italian, and “the” is supplied before “skin”.

> 4 come, quando i vapori umidi e spessi  
> 5 a diradar cominciansi, la spera  
> 6 del sol debilemente entra per essi:  

how, when the damp and dense vapours begin to melt away, the sphere of the sun enters feebly through them:

- *i vapori umidi e spessi* ‘the vapours damp and dense’ → “the damp and dense vapours”: the adjectives precede the noun.
- *a diradar cominciansi* ‘to thin they begin’ → “begin to melt away”: the finite verb precedes its infinitive, and “melt away” renders the thinning expressed by *diradar* with the pronominal *-si* attached to *comincian-*.
- *debilemente entra* ‘feebly enters’ → “enters feebly”: the adverb follows the verb.

> 7 e fia la tua imagine leggiera  
> 8 in giugnere a veder, com' io rividi  
> 9 lo sole in pria, che già nel corcare era.  

and thy fancy will lightly come to see how first I beheld the sun again, that now was at the setting.

- *fia la tua imagine leggiera* ‘will be thy imagination light’ → “thy fancy will lightly”: the subject moves before the verb; “fancy” renders the imaginative faculty, and the predicate adjective becomes an adverb modifying the following verb, so the copula drops out.
- *in giugnere a veder* ‘in coming to see’ → “come to see”: the preposition drops out, and its infinitive becomes the main verb after “will”.
- *io rividi lo sole in pria* ‘I saw again the sun at first’ → “first I beheld the sun again”: the time phrase becomes an adverb placed before the subject, and the sense of the prefix in *rividi* is expressed by “again” after the object.
- *già nel corcare era* ‘already at the setting was’ → “now was at the setting”: “now” renders *già* rather than ‘already’, and the verb comes before the prepositional phrase.

## Lines 10–42 — Imagination and the Visions of Wrath

> 10 Sì, pareggiando i miei co' passi fidi  
> 11 del mio maestro, uscii fuor di tal nube,  
> 12 ai raggi, morti già nei bassi lidi.  

So, measuring mine with the trusty steps of my Master, I issued forth from such a cloud, to the rays already dead on the low shores.

- *pareggiando i miei* ‘matching mine’ → “measuring mine”: matching one set of steps with the other is expressed as measuring one with the other.
- *co' passi fidi* ‘with the steps trusty’ → “with the trusty steps”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *tal nube* ‘such cloud’ → “such a cloud”: English supplies the indefinite article.
- *morti già* ‘dead already’ → “already dead”: the adverb precedes the participle.
- *nei bassi lidi* ‘in the low shores’ → “on the low shores”: the preposition changes.

> 13 O immaginativa, che ne rube  
> 14 tal volta sì di fuor, ch' uom non s' accorge,  
> 15 perchè d' intorno suonin mille tube,  
> 16 chi move te, se il senso non ti porge?  

O fantasy, that at times dost so snatch us out of ourselves that we are conscious of naught, even though a thousand trumpets sound about us, who moves thee, if the senses set naught before thee?

- *immaginativa* ‘imaginative faculty’ → “fantasy”: a single noun names the faculty.
- *che ne rube / tal volta sì di fuor* ‘that dost rob us at times so of the outside’ → “that at times dost so snatch us out of ourselves”: the adverbs move forward and the object follows the verb; being deprived of awareness of the outside becomes being snatched out of oneself.
- *ch' uom non s' accorge* ‘that one does not notice’ → “that we are conscious of naught”: the indefinite singular becomes the first-person plural; the negative verb becomes an affirmative expression with a negative complement.
- *perchè d' intorno suonin mille tube* ‘though round about should sound a thousand trumpets’ → “even though a thousand trumpets sound about us”: the conjunction is taken concessively and reinforced by “even”; the subject comes before the verb, the Italian subjunctive is not separately marked, and the phrase of place comes last with “us” supplied.
- *il senso non ti porge* ‘sense does not offer to thee’ → “the senses set naught before thee”: the singular faculty becomes plural; the negation becomes the object “naught”, and offering to someone becomes setting something before them.

> 17 Moveti lume, che nel ciel s' informa  
> 18 per sè, o per voler che giù lo scorge.  

A light moves thee which takes its form in heaven, of itself, or by a will that sendeth it down.

- *Moveti lume* ‘moves thee light’ → “A light moves thee”: the subject comes first and receives an indefinite article.
- *s' informa* ‘is formed’ → “takes its form”: the pronominal verb is unpacked into an active verb with a possessive and a noun.
- *nel ciel* ‘in the heaven’ → “in heaven”: English uses no article.
- *per voler* ‘by will’ → “by a will”: an indefinite article is supplied.
- *giù lo scorge* ‘downward it guides’ → “sendeth it down”: guiding becomes sending, and the order changes to verb, object, direction.

> 19 Dell' empiezza di lei, che mutò forma  
> 20 nell' uccel che a cantar più si diletta,  
> 21 nell' imagine mia apparve l' orma;  

The traces of her impiety, who changed her form into the bird that most delights to sing, appeared in my fancy;

- *Dell' empiezza di lei … l' orma* ‘of the impiety of her … the trace’ → “The traces of her impiety”: the postponed subject comes first and becomes plural; its dependent phrase follows it, with *di lei* turned into a possessive before the noun.
- *mutò forma* ‘changed form’ → “changed her form”: the possessive is supplied.
- *che a cantar più si diletta* ‘that in singing most delights’ → “that most delights to sing”: the complement moves after the verb and becomes an infinitive rather than a phrase introduced by a preposition; the Italian pronominal verb needs no reflexive in English.
- *nell' imagine mia apparve* ‘in the imagination of mine appeared’ → “appeared in my fancy”: the verb comes before the phrase of place, “fancy” renders the inward faculty, and the possessive precedes its noun.

> 22 e qui fu la mia mente sì ristretta  
> 23 dentro da sè, che di fuor non venia  
> 24 cosa che fosse allor da lei recetta.  

and here my mind was so restrained within itself, that from outside came naught which was then received by it.

- *fu la mia mente* ‘was my mind’ → “my mind was”: the subject precedes the verb.
- *di fuor non venia / cosa* ‘from outside there did not come a thing’ → “from outside came naught”: the negation and the noun are combined in a negative pronoun.
- *che fosse allor da lei recetta* ‘which might be then by it received’ → “which was then received by it”: the subjunctive after the negated antecedent becomes an indicative, and the agent follows the participle.

> 25 Poi piovve dentro all' alta fantasia  
> 26 un crocifisso, dispettoso e fiero  
> 27 nella sua vista, e cotal si moria.  

Then fell within my lofty fantasy one crucified, scornful and fierce in mien, and even so was he dying.

- *piovve* ‘rained’ → “fell”: the motion is retained without the image of rain.
- *all' alta fantasia* ‘the lofty fantasy’ → “my lofty fantasy”: the definite article is replaced by a possessive.
- *un crocifisso* ‘a crucified one’ → “one crucified”: the participle follows the pronoun.
- *nella sua vista* ‘in his appearance’ → “in mien”: the possessive is omitted and “mien” specifies outward bearing.
- *cotal si moria* ‘such he was dying’ → “even so was he dying”: the adjective becomes an adverbial phrase reinforced by “even”; the pronominal imperfect becomes an English progressive with the auxiliary before the subject.

> 28 Intorno ad esso era il grande Assuero,  
> 29 Ester sua sposa e il giusto Mardocheo,  
> 30 che fu al dire ed al far così intero.  

Round about him were the great Ahasuerus, Esther his wife, and the just Mordecai, who in speech and deed was so sincere.

- *Intorno ad esso* ‘around him’ → “Round about him”: the Italian prepositional expression is rendered by the fuller English “Round about”.
- *era* ‘was’ → “were”: the Italian verb is singular before the list; English uses the plural for the subjects taken together.
- *che fu al dire ed al far così intero* ‘who was in speaking and in doing so upright’ → “who in speech and deed was so sincere”: the complements move before the verb, the two substantivized infinitives become nouns under a single preposition, and integrity is expressed as sincerity.

> 31 E come questa imagine rompeo  
> 32 sè per sè stessa, a guisa d' una bulla  
> 33 cui manca l' acqua sotto qual si feo,  

And as this fancy broke of itself, after the fashion of a bubble to which the water fails wherein it was made,

- *questa imagine* ‘this image’ → “this fancy”: the image is called a fancy.
- *rompeo / sè per sè stessa* ‘broke itself by itself’ → “broke of itself”: English uses “broke” intransitively, without the separate reflexive object.
- *cui manca l' acqua* ‘to which fails the water’ → “to which the water fails”: the subject precedes the verb.
- *sotto qual si feo* ‘under which it formed’ → “wherein it was made”: ‘under’ becomes ‘in’, and the pronominal verb becomes an explicit passive.

> 34 surse in mia visione una fanciulla,  
> 35 piangendo forte, e diceva: “O regina,  
> 36 perchè per ira hai voluto esser nulla?  

there arose in my vision a maiden weeping sorely, and she was saying: “O Queen, wherefore through wrath hast thou willed to be naught?

- *surse* ‘arose’ → “there arose”: introductory “there” is supplied before the verb with its postponed subject.
- *piangendo forte* ‘weeping loudly’ → “weeping sorely”: the adverb expresses intensity of distress rather than loudness.
- *diceva* ‘said’ → “she was saying”: the progressive brings out the continuing action of the imperfect.
- *hai voluto esser nulla* ‘thou hast willed to be nothing’ → “hast thou willed to be naught”: the auxiliary precedes the subject in the English question.

> 37 Ancisa t' hai per non perder Lavina;  
> 38 or m' hai perduta; io son essa che lutto,  
> 39 madre, alla tua pria ch' all' altrui ruina.”  

Thou hast slain thee not to lose Lavinia; now me hast thou lost; I am she that mourn, mother, for thy ruin rather than for another’s.”

- *Ancisa t' hai* ‘slain thyself thou hast’ → “Thou hast slain thee”: the auxiliary comes before the participle and the reflexive object follows it, in the simple pronoun form “thee”.
- *per non perder Lavina* ‘in order not to lose Lavinia’ → “not to lose Lavinia”: the infinitive alone conveys purpose.
- *or m' hai perduta* ‘now me thou hast lost’ → “now me hast thou lost”: the object stays in front, but the auxiliary precedes the English subject; the participle does not mark the feminine gender of the object.
- *io son essa* ‘I am she herself’ → “I am she”: the identification is retained without a separate intensive.
- *alla tua pria ch' all' altrui ruina* ‘at thy, before at another's, ruin’ → “for thy ruin rather than for another’s”: “for” introduces the object of mourning; the single noun is placed with the first possessive instead of after the second, and priority is interpreted as preference.

> 40 Come si frange il sonno, ove di butto  
> 41 nuova luce percote il viso chiuso,  
> 42 che fratto guizza pria che moia tutto:  

As sleep is broken when on a sudden new light strikes on the closed eyes, and being broken, quivers ere it wholly dies away;

- *si frange il sonno* ‘is broken sleep’ → “sleep is broken”: the pronominal construction becomes an English passive and its subject comes first.
- *ove* ‘where’ → “when”: the conjunction is taken temporally.
- *di butto* ‘suddenly’ → “on a sudden”: one adverbial idiom replaces another.
- *percote il viso chiuso* ‘strikes the face closed’ → “strikes on the closed eyes”: English supplies “on”, takes the singular *viso* as the eyes rather than the whole face, and puts the adjective first.
- *che fratto guizza* ‘which, broken, quivers’ → “and being broken, quivers”: coordination replaces the relative clause, and “being” is supplied with the participle.
- *pria che moia tutto* ‘before it die altogether’ → “ere it wholly dies away”: the subjunctive becomes an indicative, the expression of completeness precedes the verb, and “away” is supplied.

## Lines 43–75 — The Angel’s Invitation and the Failing Light

> 43 così l' imaginar mio cadde giuso,  
> 44 tosto ch' un lume il volto mi percosse,  
> 45 maggiore assai che quello ch' è in nostr' uso.  

so my imagination fell down soon as a light smote on my face, greater far than that which is in our use.

- *l' imaginar mio* ‘the imagining of mine’ → “my imagination”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun, and the possessive moves before it.
- *il volto mi percosse* ‘the face it smote me’ → “smote on my face”: the verb comes first; the face, its direct object in Italian, becomes the object of “on”, and the pronoun denoting the person struck becomes a possessive.

> 46 Io mi volgea per vedere ov' io fosse,  
> 47 quand' una voce disse: “Qui si monta,”  
> 48 che da ogni altro intento mi rimosse;  

I turned me to see where I was, when a voice which removed me from every other intent, said: “Here one ascends”;

- *Io mi volgea* ‘I was turning myself’ → “I turned me”: the imperfect becomes a simple past, and the reflexive pronoun follows the verb.
- *ov' io fosse* ‘where I might be’ → “where I was”: the imperfect subjunctive of the indirect question becomes indicative.
- *disse: Qui si monta* ‘said: Here one ascends’ → “said: “Here one ascends””: the verb and the words spoken move after the relative clause that Italian postpones until after the speech.
- *che da ogni altro intento mi rimosse* ‘which from every other intent me removed’ → “which removed me from every other intent”: the verb precedes its object, and the prepositional phrase comes last.

> 49 e fece la mia voglia tanto pronta  
> 50 di riguardar chi era che parlava,  
> 51 che mai non posa, se non si raffronta.  

and it gave my desire to behold who it was that spake, such eagerness as never rests until it sees face to face.

- *fece la mia voglia tanto pronta* ‘made my desire so eager’ → “gave my desire to behold who it was that spake, such eagerness”: making the desire eager becomes giving it eagerness; the adjective becomes a noun, and the degree phrase moves after the description of the desire.
- *di riguardar* ‘of looking at’ → “to behold”: the preposition and infinitive become an English infinitive attached directly to “desire”.
- *chi era che parlava* ‘who it was that was speaking’ → “who it was that spake”: the imperfect of the verb of speaking becomes a simple past.
- *che mai non posa* ‘that it never rests’ → “as never rests”: the result clause becomes a relative construction after “such eagerness”, and Italian negative concord is expressed by “never” alone.
- *se non si raffronta* ‘unless it comes face to face’ → “until it sees face to face”: a negative condition becomes a temporal limit, and the pronominal verb is expressed by a verb of seeing and an adverbial phrase.

> 52 Ma come al sol, che nostra vista grava,  
> 53 e per soperchio sua figura vela,  
> 54 così la mia virtù quivi mancava.  

But, as at the sun which oppresses our sight, and veils his form by excess, so my virtue there was failing me.

- *nostra vista grava* ‘our sight oppresses’ → “oppresses our sight”: the verb moves before its object.
- *per soperchio sua figura vela* ‘by excess his form veils’ → “veils his form by excess”: the verb comes first and the phrase of cause last.
- *la mia virtù quivi mancava* ‘my power there was failing’ → “my virtue there was failing me”: “virtue” renders the faculty or power just exercised, the progressive expresses the imperfect, and “me” is supplied.

> 55 “Questi è divino spirito, che ne la  
> 56 via d' andar su ne drizza senza prego,  
> 57 e col suo lume sè medesmo cela.  

“This is a divine spirit, that directs us to the way of ascent without our prayer, and conceals itself with its own light.

- *divino spirito* ‘divine spirit’ → “a divine spirit”: English supplies an indefinite article for the predicate noun.
- *ne la via d' andar su ne drizza* ‘in the way of going up us directs’ → “directs us to the way of ascent”: verb and object pronoun come first; “to” replaces ‘in’, and the infinitive with its adverb becomes the noun “ascent”.
- *senza prego* ‘without request’ → “without our prayer”: “prayer” renders a request, and the possessive specifies whose.
- *col suo lume sè medesmo cela* ‘with its light its very self conceals’ → “conceals itself with its own light”: the verb precedes the reflexive object and the instrumental phrase follows it; the separate emphasis on the reflexive is lost, while “own” emphasizes the possessive.

> 58 Sì fa con noi, come l' uom si fa sego:  
> 59 chè quale aspetta prego, e l' uopo vede,  
> 60 malignamente già si mette al nego.  

It doeth unto us as a man doth unto himself; for he who awaits the prayer and sees the need, already sets him unkindly towards denial.

- *Sì fa con noi* ‘thus it does with us’ → “It doeth unto us”: the initial ‘thus’, which anticipates the comparison, is omitted, and “unto” replaces ‘with’.
- *l' uom si fa sego* ‘the man does for himself with himself’ → “a man doth unto himself”: the generic definite becomes indefinite, and the reflexive pronoun and ‘with himself’ are represented by a single phrase.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *quale* ‘whoever’ → “he who”: the indefinite relative is expanded into antecedent and relative.
- *aspetta prego* ‘awaits request’ → “awaits the prayer”: “prayer” renders the request, and the article is supplied.
- *l' uopo vede* ‘the need sees’ → “sees the need”: the verb precedes its object.
- *malignamente già si mette al nego* ‘unkindly already himself sets to refusal’ → “already sets him unkindly towards denial”: “unkindly” moves after verb and pronoun; the reflexive is rendered by postverbal “him”, and “towards” gives the setting a direction.

> 61 Ora accordiamo a tanto invito il piede:  

Now accord we our feet to such an invitation;

- *accordiamo a tanto invito il piede* ‘let us accord to so great an invitation the foot’ → “accord we our feet to such an invitation”: an inverted exhortation renders the hortatory verb; the object moves forward, the singular becomes plural with a possessive, and “such” renders the degree expressed by *tanto*.

> 62 procacciam di salir pria che s' abbui,  
> 63 chè poi non si poria, se il dì non riede.”  

strive we to ascend ere the night cometh, for then we could not until the day return.”

- *procacciam di salir* ‘let us strive to go up’ → “strive we to ascend”: the exhortation again takes the inverted form, and a single verb renders ‘go up’.
- *pria che s' abbui* ‘before it grows dark’ → “ere the night cometh”: the impersonal darkening becomes the coming of an explicit subject; the Italian subjunctive is rendered by an indicative.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *poi non si poria* ‘afterwards one could not’ → “then we could not”: “then” renders ‘afterwards’, and the impersonal construction becomes first-person plural.
- *se il dì non riede* ‘if the day does not return’ → “until the day return”: a negative condition becomes an affirmative temporal clause, with English subjunctive “return” for the Italian indicative.

> 64 Così disse il mio duca, ed io con lui  
> 65 volgemmo i nostri passi ad una scala;  

Thus spake my Leader, and I with him did turn our footsteps to a stairway;

- *volgemmo i nostri passi* ‘we turned our steps’ → “did turn our footsteps”: the simple past is rendered with auxiliary “did”, and “footsteps” renders ‘steps’.

> 66 e tosto ch' io al primo grado fui,  
> 67 senti' mi presso quasi un mover d' ala,  
> 68 e ventarmi nel viso, e dir: “*Beati*  
> 69 *pacifici*, che son senza ira mala.”  

and soon as I was at the first step, near me I felt as ’twere the stroke of a wing, and my face fanned, and heard one say: “*Beati pacifici* who are without evil wrath.”

- *al primo grado fui* ‘at the first step I was’ → “I was at the first step”: the verb moves before the phrase of place.
- *senti' mi presso* ‘I felt close to me’ → “near me I felt”: the phrase of place moves before the verb.
- *quasi un mover d' ala* ‘as it were a moving of wing’ → “as ’twere the stroke of a wing”: the substantivized infinitive becomes the more specific “stroke”; its indefinite article becomes definite, and an indefinite article is supplied for the wing.
- *ventarmi nel viso* ‘to fan me in the face’ → “my face fanned”: the active infinitive dependent on the verb of perception becomes a passive participle; the face becomes the thing fanned, and the personal pronoun becomes possessive.
- *e dir* ‘and to say’ → “and heard one say”: where Italian still depends on the earlier verb of perception, English supplies a specifically auditory verb and an indefinite speaker.
- *ira mala* ‘wrath evil’ → “evil wrath”: the adjective precedes the noun.

> 70 Già eran sopra noi tanto levati  
> 71 gli ultimi raggi che la notte segue,  
> 72 che le stelle apparivan da più lati.  

Now were the last rays whereafter night followeth so far risen above us that the stars were appearing on many sides.

- *Già* ‘already’ → “Now”: a marker of what has already come about becomes one of present narrative time.
- *eran sopra noi tanto levati gli ultimi raggi* ‘were above us so raised the last rays’ → “were the last rays whereafter night followeth so far risen above us”: the subject, with its relative clause, moves between auxiliary and participle; “risen” presents the rays as rising, “far” makes the degree explicit, and “above us” comes after the participle.
- *che la notte segue* ‘which the night follows’ → “whereafter night followeth”: the direct-object relative becomes an adverbial relative expressing succession, and the article before ‘night’ is omitted.
- *le stelle apparivan* ‘the stars appeared’ → “the stars were appearing”: the progressive brings out the imperfect aspect.
- *da più lati* ‘from several sides’ → “on many sides”: location replaces the viewpoint expressed by ‘from’, and “many” renders the indefinite plurality.

> 73 “O virtù mia, perchè sì ti dilegue?”  

“O my virtue, wherefore dost thou pass away from me thus?”

- *virtù mia* ‘power of mine’ → “my virtue”: “virtue” renders ‘power’, and the possessive moves before the noun.
- *perchè sì ti dilegue* ‘why thus dost thou fade away’ → “wherefore dost thou pass away from me thus”: the pronominal verb is rendered by “pass away”, “from me” makes the loss personal, and the adverb of manner moves to the end.

> 74 fra me stesso dicea, chè mi sentiva  
> 75 la possa delle gambe posta in tregue  

I said within me, for I felt the power of my legs put in truce.

- *fra me stesso dicea* ‘within myself I was saying’ → “I said within me”: the verb comes first, the simple past replaces the imperfect, and “me” lacks the explicit emphasis of ‘myself’.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: a subordinating conjunction becomes coordinating.
- *mi sentiva* ‘I was feeling in myself’ → “I felt”: the personal reference of the clitic is not separately expressed, and the imperfect becomes a simple past.
- *delle gambe* ‘of the legs’ → “of my legs”: the possessive replaces the definite article.
- *posta in tregue* ‘put in truces’ → “put in truce”: the plural becomes singular.

## Lines 76–108 — Love as the Seed of Every Deed

> 76 Noi eravam dove più non saliva  
> 77 la scala su, ed eravamo affissi,  
> 78 pur come nave ch' alla piaggia arriva;  

We stood where the stairway ascended no higher, and were fixed even as a ship which arrives on the shore:

- *Noi eravam* ‘we were’ → “We stood”: the verb of being becomes one of posture.
- *più non saliva la scala su* ‘no further rose the stairway up’ → “the stairway ascended no higher”: the subject comes before the verb, and the separate indications of limit and upward direction are combined in “no higher”.
- *nave* ‘ship’ → “a ship”: the indefinite article is supplied.
- *ch' alla piaggia arriva* ‘which to the shore arrives’ → “which arrives on the shore”: the verb precedes the phrase of place, and “on” replaces the preposition of motion towards.

> 79 ed io attesi un poco s' io udissi  
> 80 alcuna cosa nel nuovo girone;  

and I gave heed awhile if I might hear aught in the new circle;

- *un poco* ‘a little’ → “awhile”: the expression of quantity is made explicitly temporal.
- *s' io udissi* ‘if I heard’ → “if I might hear”: the English modal expresses the possibility conveyed by the Italian subjunctive.
- *alcuna cosa* ‘anything’ → “aught”: one pronoun replaces adjective and noun.

> 81 poi mi volsi al maestro mio e dissi:  

then did turn me to my Master and said:

- *mi volsi* ‘I turned myself’ → “did turn me”: the simple past is expanded with an auxiliary, the reflexive follows the verb as “me”, and the subject is carried over from the preceding clause.
- *maestro mio* ‘master my’ → “my Master”: the possessive comes before the noun.

> 82 “Dolce mio padre, di', quale offensione  
> 83 si purga qui nel giro, dove semo?  

“Sweet my Father, tell, what offence is purged here in the circle where we are?

- *si purga* ‘purges itself’ → “is purged”: the Italian passive construction with *si* becomes an English passive with an auxiliary.

> 84 Se i piè si stanno, non stea tuo sermone.”  

If our feet are stayed, stay not thy discourse.”

- *i piè si stanno* ‘the feet remain still’ → “our feet are stayed”: the definite article gives way to a possessive, and the pronominal verb of remaining is rendered by “are stayed”.
- *non stea tuo sermone* ‘let not thy discourse stand still’ → “stay not thy discourse”: the third-person subjunctive with ‘discourse’ as subject becomes an imperative addressed to the speaker, with “thy discourse” as object.

> 85 Ed egli a me: “L' amor del bene, scemo  
> 86 di suo dover, quiritta si ristora,  

And he to me: “The love of good scant of its duty, just here restores itself;

- *scemo di suo dover* ‘deficient in its duty’ → “scant of its duty”: the adjective of deficiency is rendered by “scant”, with “of” for the respect in which the love falls short.
- *quiritta* ‘right here’ → “just here”: the emphatic adverb is expressed in two words.
- *si ristora* ‘itself restores’ → “restores itself”: the reflexive follows the verb.

> 87 qui si ribatte il mal tardato remo.  

here is plied again the ill-slackened oar.

- *si ribatte* ‘is struck again’ → “is plied again”: the *si* passive becomes an auxiliary passive, the iterative prefix becomes a separate adverb, and the verb is adapted to the action of an oar.
- *il mal tardato remo* ‘the ill-delayed oar’ → “the ill-slackened oar”: delay is expressed as a slackening of the stroke.

> 88 Ma perchè più aperto intendi ancora,  
> 89 volgi la mente a me, e prenderai  
> 90 alcun buon frutto di nostra dimora.”  

But that thou mayest understand yet more plainly, turn thy mind to me, and thou shalt take some good fruit from our tarrying.”

- *perchè più aperto intendi ancora* ‘in order that more plainly thou understand yet’ → “that thou mayest understand yet more plainly”: the purpose subjunctive is expressed with “mayest”; the adjective used adverbially becomes an English adverb, and the modifiers are brought together after the verb.
- *la mente* ‘the mind’ → “thy mind”: the possessive replaces the article.
- *prenderai* ‘thou wilt take’ → “thou shalt take”: the future is expressed with “shalt”.
- *di nostra dimora* ‘of our stay’ → “from our tarrying”: “from” makes the relation one of source, and a verbal noun replaces the noun for a stay.

> 91 “Nè creator nè creatura mai,”  
> 92 cominciò ei, “figliuol, fu senza amore,  
> 93 o naturale o d' animo; e tu il sai.  

He began: “Nor Creator, nor creature, my son, was ever without love, either natural or rational; and this thou knowest.

- *cominciò ei* ‘began he’ → “He began”: the reporting clause is moved from within the speech to its beginning, and subject and verb exchange places.
- *mai … fu* ‘ever … was’ → “was ever”: the adverb is brought down from before the reporting clause and vocative to follow the verb.
- *figliuol* ‘son’ → “my son”: the possessive is supplied.
- *o naturale o d' animo* ‘either natural or of the mind’ → “either natural or rational”: “rational” interprets the prepositional phrase and makes it parallel in form to “natural”.
- *tu il sai* ‘thou it knowest’ → “this thou knowest”: a demonstrative replaces the unstressed object pronoun and is moved before the subject.

> 94 Lo natural è sempre senza errore,  
> 95 ma l' altro puote errar per malo obbietto,  
> 96 o per poco o per troppo di vigore.  

The natural is always without error; but the other may err through an evil object, or through too little or too much vigour.

- *per malo obbietto* ‘through evil object’ → “through an evil object”: the indefinite article is supplied.
- *per poco o per troppo di vigore* ‘through little or through excess of vigour’ → “through too little or too much vigour”: “too” makes the deficiency explicit; a single “through” governs both alternatives, and the quantities modify “vigour” directly rather than taking an ‘of’ phrase.

> 97 Mentre ch' egli è ne' primi ben diretto,  
> 98 e ne' secondi sè stesso misura,  
> 99 esser non può cagion di mal diletto;  

While it is directed to the primal goods, and in the secondary, moderates itself, it cannot be the cause of sinful delight;

- *egli è ne' primi ben diretto* ‘it is to the first goods directed’ → “it is directed to the primal goods”: the participle is brought next to the auxiliary, and the ordinal is rendered by “primal”.
- *ne' secondi* ‘in the second’ → “in the secondary”: the ordinal becomes an adjective of rank, still with the noun understood.
- *sè stesso misura* ‘itself measures’ → “moderates itself”: measuring is interpreted as keeping within measure, and the reflexive object follows the verb.
- *esser non può cagion* ‘be it cannot cause’ → “it cannot be the cause”: the infinitive follows the modal, and the predicate noun gains a definite article.
- *mal diletto* ‘evil delight’ → “sinful delight”: the adjective is given a specifically moral rendering.

> 100 ma, quando al mal si torce, o con più cura  
> 101 o con men che non dee corre nel bene,  
> 102 contra il fattore adopra sua fattura.  

but when it is turned awry to evil, or speeds towards the good with more or less care than it ought, against the Creator his creature works.

- *al mal si torce* ‘to evil it twists itself’ → “it is turned awry to evil”: the pronominal verb becomes a passive with “awry” expressing the twist, and the goal follows the verb.
- *con più cura o con men che non dee* ‘with more care or with less than it ought’ → “with more or less care than it ought”: one preposition and one noun serve both comparatives, the Italian comparative negation is not reproduced, and the phrase is moved after the verb of motion.
- *corre nel bene* ‘runs in the good’ → “speeds towards the good”: “speeds” replaces ‘runs’, and “towards” makes the direction explicit.
- *il fattore* ‘the maker’ → “the Creator”: the more specific noun replaces ‘maker’.
- *adopra sua fattura* ‘works his handiwork’ → “his creature works”: the postposed subject comes before the verb, and what is made is called a “creature”, corresponding to “Creator”.

> 103 Quinci comprender puoi ch' esser conviene  
> 104 amor sementa in voi d' ogni virtute,  
> 105 e d' ogni operazion che merta pene.  

Hence thou mayst understand that love must be the seed of every virtue in you, and of every deed that deserves punishment.

- *comprender puoi* ‘understand thou canst’ → “thou mayst understand”: the modal is rendered by “mayst” and precedes its infinitive.
- *esser conviene amor sementa* ‘it is necessary for love to be seed’ → “love must be the seed”: the impersonal expression of necessity becomes a modal with “love” as subject, and “seed” gains an article.
- *in voi d' ogni virtute* ‘in you of every virtue’ → “of every virtue in you”: the phrase “in you” is postponed until after “virtue”.
- *operazion* ‘action’ → “deed”: the noun is rendered by a term for an act done.
- *merta pene* ‘merits punishments’ → “deserves punishment”: “deserves” replaces ‘merits’, and a plural becomes an uncounted singular.

> 106 Or, perchè mai non può dalla salute  
> 107 amor del suo suggetto torcer viso,  
> 108 dall' odio proprio son le cose tute;  

Now inasmuch as love can never turn its face from the weal of its subject, all things are safe from self-hatred;

- *perchè* ‘because’ → “inasmuch as”: a conjunctive phrase replaces the simple causal conjunction.
- *mai non può … amor* ‘never can … love’ → “love can never”: the delayed subject is brought forward, and “never” alone conveys the Italian negation.
- *dalla salute … del suo suggetto* ‘from the well-being … of its subject’ → “from the weal of its subject”: the interrupted phrase is brought together and moved after the expression for turning away.
- *torcer viso* ‘turn face’ → “turn its face”: the possessive is supplied.
- *dall' odio proprio* ‘from hatred of self’ → “from self-hatred”: the noun and its qualifier become a compound, and the phrase moves from the beginning of the clause to the end.
- *son le cose tute* ‘are the things safe’ → “all things are safe”: the subject precedes the verb, and the generic definite article is rendered by “all”.

## Lines 109–139 — Love of Evil and Disordered Love of Good

> 109 e perchè intender non si può diviso,  
> 110 e per sè stante, alcuno esser dal primo,  

and because no being can be conceived as existing alone in isolation from the Prime Being,

- *intender non si può … alcuno esser* ‘one cannot conceive … any being’ → “no being can be conceived”: the object, delayed in Italian, comes first as the subject of a passive verb; the negative passes from the verb to “no being”.
- *diviso, e per sè stante* ‘separate, and standing by itself’ → “as existing alone in isolation”: the two descriptions are taken in reverse order and combined without a conjunction; “as existing alone” renders the second, “in isolation” the first.
- *dal primo* ‘from the first’ → “from the Prime Being”: the substantive understood with the ordinal is made explicit.

> 111 da quello odiare ogni affetto è deciso.  

every affection is cut off from hate of him.

- *da quello odiare* ‘from hating that one’ → “from hate of him”: the infinitive with its preceding object becomes a noun followed by an *of*-phrase; a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative, and the whole phrase moves to the end.
- *è deciso* ‘is severed’ → “is cut off”: a phrasal verb renders the participle.

> 112 Resta, se dividendo bene estimo,  
> 113 che il mal che s' ama è del prossimo, ed esso  
> 114 amor nasce in tre modi in vostro limo.  

It follows, if I judge well in my division, that the evil we love is our neighbour’s, and this love arises in three ways in your clay.

- *Resta* ‘it remains’ → “It follows”: what remains after division is phrased as an inference.
- *dividendo bene estimo* ‘in dividing I judge well’ → “I judge well in my division”: the gerund becomes a noun with a possessive, and its phrase follows the verb; the adverb likewise follows rather than precedes the verb.
- *che s' ama* ‘that is loved’ → “we love”: the passive construction becomes active, with a first-person plural subject and no expressed relative pronoun.
- *del prossimo* ‘the neighbour’s’ → “our neighbour’s”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *esso amor nasce* ‘that same love is born’ → “this love arises”: a simple demonstrative renders the reference back, and a verb of arising replaces the verb of birth.

> 115 È chi per esser suo vicin soppresso  
> 116 spera eccellenza, e sol per questo brama  
> 117 ch' e' sia di sua grandezza in basso messo;  

There is he who through his neighbour’s abasement hopes to excel, and solely for this desires that he be cast down from his greatness;

- *È chi* ‘there is one who’ → “There is he who”: a personal rather than an indefinite pronoun serves as antecedent.
- *per esser suo vicin soppresso* ‘through his neighbour being brought low’ → “through his neighbour’s abasement”: the passive infinitive, whose subject stands between auxiliary and participle, becomes a noun with a possessive.
- *spera eccellenza* ‘hopes for eminence’ → “hopes to excel”: a verbal infinitive replaces the noun.
- *di sua grandezza in basso messo* ‘from his greatness put down low’ → “cast down from his greatness”: “cast down” renders the participle and its directional phrase together, before rather than after the phrase of separation.

> 118 è chi podere, grazia, onore e fama  
> 119 teme di perder perch' altri sormonti,  
> 120 onde s' attrista sì che il contrario ama;  

there is he who fears to lose power, favour, honour and fame because another is exalted, wherefore he groweth sad so that he loves the contrary;

- *è chi* ‘there is one who’ → “there is he who”: the indefinite antecedent is rendered by a personal pronoun.
- *podere, grazia, onore e fama teme di perder* ‘power, favour, honour and fame he fears to lose’ → “fears to lose power, favour, honour and fame”: the list of objects follows the infinitive instead of preceding the governing verb.
- *perch' altri sormonti* ‘because another should rise above’ → “because another is exalted”: the active subjunctive becomes an indicative passive.
- *s' attrista sì che* ‘becomes so sad that’ → “he groweth sad so that”: the pronominal verb becomes a verb with a predicate adjective; the degree word is placed with “that” rather than with “sad”.
- *il contrario ama* ‘the contrary he loves’ → “he loves the contrary”: the object follows the verb.

> 121 ed è chi per ingiuria par ch' adonti  
> 122 sì che si fa della vendetta ghiotto,  
> 123 e tal convien che il male altrui impronti.  

and there is he who seems to be so shamed through being wronged, that he becomes greedy of vengeance, and such must needs seek another’s hurt.

- *è chi* ‘there is one who’ → “there is he who”: the indefinite antecedent is rendered by a personal pronoun.
- *per ingiuria* ‘on account of a wrong’ → “through being wronged”: the noun becomes a passive gerund, and the cause follows rather than precedes the description of its effect.
- *par ch' adonti sì* ‘it seems that he takes such offence’ → “seems to be so shamed”: the man becomes the subject of “seems”; an infinitive replaces the subjunctive clause, taking the verb in the sense of feeling shame rather than taking offence. The degree word precedes “shamed”.
- *si fa della vendetta ghiotto* ‘makes himself greedy for vengeance’ → “he becomes greedy of vengeance”: the reflexive is rendered by a verb of becoming, and the complement follows the adjective.
- *tal convien che* ‘for such a one it is necessary that’ → “such must needs”: the person becomes the subject of a modal verb rather than of a clause dependent on an impersonal expression.
- *il male altrui impronti* ‘should inflict the hurt of another’ → “seek another’s hurt”: Okey takes the verb in the sense of seeking rather than impressing or inflicting; it follows the modal in the plain form and precedes its object. The possessive precedes “hurt”.

> 124 Questo triforme amor quaggiù di sotto  
> 125 si piange; or vo' che tu dell' altro intende,  
> 126 che corre al ben con ordine corrotto.  

This threefold love down below is mourned for: now I desire that thou understand of the other, which hastes toward good in faulty degree.

- *amor … triforme* ‘love … threefold’ → “threefold love”: the Italian order is actually *triforme amor*, already the English order; the departure is in the construction of the following verb.
- *quaggiù di sotto* ‘down here below’ → “down below”: the explicit reference to ‘here’ drops out.
- *si piange* ‘is lamented’ → “is mourned for”: the passive expressed with *si* becomes an auxiliary passive with a prepositional verb.
- *dell' altro intende* ‘of the other understand’ → “understand of the other”: the complement moves after the verb.
- *corre al ben* ‘runs to the good’ → “hastes toward good”: running becomes hastening, the preposition expresses direction, and the article drops out.
- *con ordine corrotto* ‘with order corrupted’ → “in faulty degree”: “in” replaces ‘with’; order is interpreted as degree, and the participle is rendered by an adjective placed before the noun.

> 127 Ciascun confusamente un bene apprende,  
> 128 nel qual si queti l' animo, e disira:  
> 129 per che di giugner lui ciascun contende.  

Each one apprehends vaguely a good wherein the mind may find rest, and desires it; wherefore each one strives to attain thereto.

- *confusamente un bene apprende* ‘indistinctly a good apprehends’ → “apprehends vaguely a good”: the verb moves before both adverb and object.
- *si queti l' animo* ‘may come to rest the mind’ → “the mind may find rest”: the postposed subject comes first, the subjunctive is marked by “may”, and the pronominal verb becomes a verb with a noun object.
- *e disira* ‘and desires’ → “and desires it”: the object understood from the preceding clause is expressed.
- *di giugner lui ciascun contende* ‘to reach it each one strives’ → “each one strives to attain thereto”: the infinitive phrase moves after the governing verb, and its pronoun object becomes the directional adverb “thereto”.

> 130 Se lento amore in lui veder vi tira,  
> 131 o a lui acquistar, questa cornice,  
> 132 dopo giusto penter, ve ne martira.  

If lukewarm love draw you towards the vision of it or the gaining of it, this cornice, after due penitence, torments you for it.

- *lento amore* ‘slow love’ → “lukewarm love”: a description of pace becomes one of temperature.
- *in lui veder … o a lui acquistar* ‘to see it … or to acquire it’ → “towards the vision of it or the gaining of it”: one directional preposition governs both English expressions; the infinitives become nouns with articles and following *of*-phrases in place of the preceding pronoun objects.
- *vi tira* ‘you draws’ → “draw you”: the verb precedes its object, and both come before the two ends toward which love draws, rather than between them.
- *dopo giusto penter* ‘after due repenting’ → “after due penitence”: an abstract noun renders the substantival infinitive.
- *ve ne martira* ‘you for it torments’ → “torments you for it”: both pronominal complements move after the verb.

> 133 Altro ben è che non fa l' uom felice;  
> 134 non è felicità, non è la buona  
> 135 essenza, d' ogni ben frutto e radice.  

Another good there is, which maketh not men happy; ’tis not happiness, ’tis not the good essence, the fruit and root of all good.

- *Altro ben è* ‘another good is’ → “Another good there is”: existential “there” is supplied.
- *non fa l' uom felice* ‘does not make man happy’ → “maketh not men happy”: the negative follows the verb, and the generic singular with an article becomes a bare plural.
- *d' ogni ben frutto e radice* ‘of every good fruit and root’ → “the fruit and root of all good”: the dependent phrase follows both nouns, an article is supplied before “fruit”, and distributive ‘every good’ becomes collective “all good”.

> 136 L' amor, ch' ad esso troppo s' abbandona,  
> 137 di sopra noi si piange per tre cerchi;  

The love that abandons itself too much to this, is mourned for above us in three circles:

- *ad esso troppo s' abbandona* ‘to it too much itself abandons’ → “abandons itself too much to this”: verb and reflexive come first, in English order, followed by the degree phrase and then the prepositional complement; “this” points back to the good just mentioned.
- *di sopra noi si piange* ‘above us is lamented’ → “is mourned for above us”: the place phrase moves after the verb, and the *si*-passive becomes an auxiliary passive with a prepositional verb.

> 138 ma come tripartito si ragiona,  
> 139 tacciolo, acciocchè tu per te ne cerchi.”  

but how it is distinguished in three divisions, I do not say, in order that thou search for it of thyself.”

- *tripartito si ragiona* ‘it is accounted threefold’ → “it is distinguished in three divisions”: the notion of distinguishing is put into the verb, and the predicate participle becomes a prepositional phrase following it.
- *tacciolo* ‘I pass it over in silence’ → “I do not say”: an affirmative verb of keeping silent becomes a negated verb of speaking; the attached object pronoun is not separately rendered.
- *tu per te ne cerchi* ‘thou for thyself inquire into it’ → “thou search for it of thyself”: the verb precedes both complements, “for it” renders the pronominal *ne* with “search”, and “of thyself” renders the phrase expressing independent action.

## Summary — Close in Imagery, Freer in Syntax

Okey often keeps Dante’s images while changing the order in which their parts appear. In *Moveti lume*, ‘moves thee a light’, the subject comes last; in “A light moves thee” it comes first. Longer phrases are reordered in much the same way: *d’ ogni ben*, ‘of every good’, precedes *frutto e radice*, but follows both nouns in “the fruit and root of all good”. Yet Okey does not invariably straighten an inversion. In “here is plied again the ill-slackened oar” he keeps both the image and the delayed subject of *qui si ribatte il mal tardato remo*.

A change in wording often goes with a change in construction. Where *imagine* is described as *leggiera*, ‘light’, Okey has “thy fancy will lightly come to see”: an adjective describing the faculty becomes an adverb describing what it does. *Spera eccellenza*, ‘hopes for eminence’, becomes “hopes to excel”; conversely, *per esser suo vicin soppresso*, ‘through his neighbour being brought low’, becomes “through his neighbour’s abasement”. Sometimes the construction stays close but the image shifts: *piovve*, ‘rained’, loses its suggestion of rain in “fell”, while *lento amore*, ‘slow love’, exchanges pace for warmth in “lukewarm love”.

Okey supplies words where Italian leaves a relation understood: *senza prego*, ‘without request’, becomes “without our prayer”, and *dal primo*, ‘from the first’, becomes “from the Prime Being”. Elsewhere he leaves something unexpressed: *rompeo sè per sè stessa*, ‘broke itself by itself’, needs no reflexive object in “broke of itself”. Two routine adjustments generally pass unmarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending identifies the subject, and omits the Italian definite article before a possessive. Thus *la tua imagine* is simply “thy fancy”.
