# Purgatorio 9

## Lines 1–15 — Night’s Steps and Dante’s Sleep

> 1 La concubina di Titone antico  
> 2 già s' imbiancava al balco d' oriente,  
> 3 fuor delle braccia del suo dolce amico;  

Now was the concubine of ancient Tithonus at eastern terrace growing white, forth from her sweet lover’s arms;

- *di Titone antico* ‘of Tithonus ancient’ → “of ancient Tithonus”: the adjective precedes the name.
- *già s' imbiancava* ‘already was whitening’ → “Now was” … “growing white”: the adverb moves to the opening and marks the time as “Now” rather than ‘already’. The inchoative verb becomes “growing” with an adjective; its auxiliary precedes the subject, and “growing white” follows the phrase of place.
- *al balco d' oriente* ‘at the balcony of the east’ → “at eastern terrace”: “terrace” replaces ‘balcony’, its article drops out, and the phrase indicating direction becomes a preceding adjective.
- *delle braccia del suo dolce amico* ‘from the arms of her sweet friend’ → “from her sweet lover’s arms”: “lover” makes the relationship more specific, and the possessive comes before “arms” in place of the following *di*-phrase.

> 4 di gemme la sua fronte era lucente,  
> 5 poste in figura del freddo animale,  
> 6 che con la coda percote la gente;  

with gems her forehead was glittering, set in the form of the cold animal that strikes folk with its tail;

- *era lucente* ‘was shining’ → “was glittering”: the Italian predicate adjective becomes a progressive verb, with “glittering” suggesting points of light.
- *in figura* ‘in form’ → “in the form”: the definite article is supplied.
- *con la coda percote la gente* ‘with the tail strikes the people’ → “strikes folk with its tail”: the phrase of instrument moves after the verb and object; a possessive replaces the article with the body part, and the collective “folk” takes no article.

> 7 e la notte de' passi, con che sale,  
> 8 fatti avea due nel loco ov' eravamo,  
> 9 e il terzo già chinava in giuso l' ale;  

and Night, in the place where we were, had made two of the steps wherewith she climbs, and the third was already down-stooping its wings;

- *de' passi, con che sale, fatti avea due nel loco ov' eravamo* ‘of the steps, with which she climbs, made had two in the place where we were’ → “in the place where we were, had made two of the steps wherewith she climbs”: the phrase of place moves from after the number to immediately after the subject; the auxiliary comes before the participle, and the number comes before the partitive phrase and its relative clause.
- *già chinava in giuso l' ale* ‘already bent downwards the wings’ → “was already down-stooping its wings”: the imperfect becomes a progressive, with “already” after the auxiliary; the direction is incorporated into “down-stooping”, and a possessive replaces the article.

> 10 quand' io, che meco avea di quel d' Adamo,  
> 11 vinto dal sonno, in su l' erba inchinai  
> 12 ove già tutti e cinque sedevamo.  

when I, who with me had somewhat of Adam, vanquished by sleep, sank down on the grass where already all we five were seated.

- *di quel d' Adamo* ‘of that of Adam’ → “somewhat of Adam”: the partitive construction with a demonstrative becomes an indefinite pronoun.
- *dal sonno* ‘by the sleep’ → “by sleep”: the abstract noun loses its article.
- *in su l' erba inchinai* ‘upon the grass I bent’ → “sank down on the grass”: “sank down” renders the intransitive verb as a downward movement, and the phrase of place follows it.
- *tutti e cinque* ‘all and five’ → “all we five”: the Italian idiom joining *tutti* and the number loses its conjunction; the subject pronoun is placed between the two.
- *sedevamo* ‘we sat’ → “were seated”: a form of ‘be’ with a participle renders the state expressed by the Italian imperfect.

> 13 Nell' ora che comincia i tristi lai  
> 14 la rondinella presso alla mattina,  
> 15 forse a memoria de' suoi primi guai,  

At the hour when the swallow begins her sad lays nigh unto the morn, perchance in memory of her former woes,

- *Nell' ora che* ‘in the hour that’ → “At the hour when”: the preposition changes, and a relative adverb makes the temporal relation explicit.
- *comincia i tristi lai la rondinella* ‘begins the sad lays the little swallow’ → “the swallow begins her sad lays”: the subject moves before the verb, the diminutive is not expressed, and a possessive replaces the article before the object.
- *presso alla mattina* ‘near to the morning’ → “nigh unto the morn”: the English uses more poetic diction for both proximity and time.
- *a memoria* ‘to memory’ → “in memory”: the preposition changes to suit the English idiom.
- *de' suoi primi guai* ‘of her first woes’ → “of her former woes”: the adjective marks the woes as earlier rather than explicitly first.

## Lines 16–48 — The Eagle Dream and the Awakening

> 16 e che la mente nostra, peregrina  
> 17 più dalla carne e men da' pensier presa,  
> 18 alle sue vision quasi è divina:  

and when our mind, more of a wanderer from the flesh and less prisoned by thoughts, in its visions is almost prophetic;

- *e che* ‘and that’ → “and when”: the connective is rendered explicitly as temporal.
- *la mente nostra* ‘the mind of ours’ → “our mind”: the possessive precedes the noun.
- *peregrina più dalla carne* ‘wandering farther from the flesh’ → “more of a wanderer from the flesh”: the predicate adjective becomes a noun phrase, and the comparative comes first.
- *men da' pensier presa* ‘less by the thoughts taken’ → “less prisoned by thoughts”: “prisoned” strengthens the image of being held, precedes the agent phrase, and the definite article before ‘thoughts’ drops out.
- *alle sue vision* ‘as regards its visions’ → “in its visions”: the prepositional phrase is recast with “in”.
- *quasi è divina* ‘almost is divine’ → “is almost prophetic”: the adverb follows the verb, and “prophetic” gives an interpretation of ‘divine’ rather than its direct equivalent.

> 19 in sogno mi parea veder sospesa  
> 20 un' aquila nel ciel con penne d' oro  
> 21 con l' ali aperte, ed a calare intesa.  

in a dream methought I saw an eagle poised in the sky, with plumes of gold, with wings outspread, and intent to swoop.

- *in sogno* ‘in dream’ → “in a dream”: English supplies the indefinite article.
- *mi parea veder* ‘it seemed to me to see’ → “methought I saw”: the infinitive becomes a finite clause after “methought”.
- *sospesa un' aquila nel ciel* ‘suspended an eagle in the sky’ → “an eagle poised in the sky”: the participle moves after the noun, and “poised” replaces ‘suspended’.
- *con l' ali aperte* ‘with the wings open’ → “with wings outspread”: the article drops out, and “outspread” specifies how the wings are open.
- *ed a calare intesa* ‘and on descending intent’ → “and intent to swoop”: the adjective comes first, and “swoop” is more specific than ‘descend’.

> 22 Ed esser mi parea là dove foro  
> 23 abbandonati i suoi da Ganimede,  
> 24 quando fu ratto al sommo consistoro.  

And meseemed to be there where his own people were abandoned by Ganymede, when he was snatched to the high consistory.

- *Ed esser mi parea* ‘and to be it seemed to me’ → “And meseemed to be”: the infinitive follows the expression of seeming.
- *foro abbandonati i suoi* ‘were abandoned his own’ → “his own people were abandoned”: the subject comes before the passive verb, and “people” makes the substantival possessive explicit.
- *al sommo consistoro* ‘to the highest consistory’ → “to the high consistory”: the superlative is rendered by a positive adjective.

> 25 Fra me pensava: “Forse questa fiede  
> 26 pur qui per uso, e forse d' altro loco  
> 27 disdegna di portarne suso in piede.”  

I thought within me: “Haply he strikes only here through custom, and perchance scorneth to bear aught upward from other place in his talons.”

- *Fra me pensava* ‘within myself I was thinking’ → “I thought within me”: the phrase for the inward thought follows the verb, and the imperfect is rendered by a simple past.
- *questa fiede* ‘this one strikes’ → “he strikes”: a personal pronoun replaces the feminine demonstrative referring to the eagle.
- *d' altro loco* ‘from another place’ → “from other place”: the phrase moves from before the verb of scorning to after the expression of upward motion.
- *di portarne suso* ‘to carry some up’ → “to bear aught upward”: “aught” renders the indefinite object expressed by the enclitic *ne*.
- *in piede* ‘in foot’ → “in his talons”: the singular is replaced by a more specific plural, with a possessive supplied.

> 28 Poi mi parea che, roteata un poco,  
> 29 terribil come folgor discendesse,  
> 30 e me rapisse suso infino al foco.  

Then meseemed that, having wheeled awhile, terrible as lightning, he descended and snatched me up far as the fiery sphere.

- *roteata un poco* ‘wheeled a little’ → “having wheeled awhile”: the past participle becomes an explicitly anterior participial phrase, and “awhile” takes ‘a little’ as duration.
- *discendesse, e me rapisse* ‘should descend, and me should snatch’ → “he descended and snatched me”: the imperfect subjunctives dependent on the expression of seeming become simple past indicatives, and the object follows its verb.
- *suso infino al foco* ‘up as far as the fire’ → “up far as the fiery sphere”: the first ‘as’ is absent, and the noun ‘fire’ becomes an adjective modifying the supplied “sphere”.

> 31 Ivi pareva ch' ella ed io ardesse,  
> 32 e sì l' incendio imaginato cosse,  
> 33 che convenne che il sonno si rompesse.  

There it seemed that he and I did burn, and the visionary flame so scorched that needs was my slumber broken.

- *ella ed io ardesse* ‘she and I should burn’ → “he and I did burn”: the eagle takes a masculine pronoun in English, and the singular imperfect subjunctive becomes a past indicative with auxiliary “did”.
- *l' incendio imaginato* ‘the conflagration imagined’ → “the visionary flame”: the modifier comes first, “visionary” replaces the participle, and “flame” replaces the noun for a blaze.
- *convenne che il sonno si rompesse* ‘it was necessary that sleep should break’ → “needs was my slumber broken”: the impersonal verb of necessity becomes an adverb, the inchoative reflexive subjunctive becomes a passive indicative, and a possessive replaces the definite article.

> 34 Non altrimenti Achille si riscosse,  
> 35 gli occhi svegliati rivolgendo in giro,  
> 36 e non sapendo là dove si fosse,  

Not otherwise Achilles startled, turning his awakened eyes around, and knowing not where he might be,

- *si riscosse* ‘roused himself’ → “startled”: a reflexive verb of waking or coming to oneself becomes an intransitive verb of sudden reaction.
- *gli occhi svegliati rivolgendo in giro* ‘the eyes awakened turning about’ → “turning his awakened eyes around”: the participle of turning precedes its object, a possessive replaces the article, and the modifier of ‘eyes’ precedes the noun.
- *non sapendo* ‘not knowing’ → “knowing not”: the negative follows the participle.
- *là dove si fosse* ‘there where he was’ → “where he might be”: the separate locative ‘there’ drops out, and a modal auxiliary renders the subjunctive in the indirect question.

> 37 quando la madre da Chiron a Schiro  
> 38 trafugò lui dormendo in le sue braccia,  
> 39 là onde poi li Greci il dipartiro;  

when his mother carried him away sleeping in her arms from Chiron to Scyros, there whence the Greeks afterwards made him depart,

- *la madre* ‘the mother’ → “his mother”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *da Chiron a Schiro trafugò lui dormendo in le sue braccia* ‘from Chiron to Scyros stole him away sleeping in the arms of hers’ → “carried him away sleeping in her arms from Chiron to Scyros”: the route moves from before the verb to after the phrase about the mother's arms. The verb loses its implication of secrecy, the object comes between “carried” and “away”, and English puts the possessive before ‘arms’.
- *poi li Greci il dipartiro* ‘afterwards the Greeks took him away’ → “the Greeks afterwards made him depart”: the adverb follows the subject, and the transitive verb with a preceding object pronoun becomes an explicit causative with the pronoun after “made”.

> 40 che mi scoss' io sì come dalla faccia  
> 41 mi fuggì il sonno, e diventai ismorto,  
> 42 come fa l' uom che spaventato agghiaccia.  

than I startled, soon as sleep fled from my face, and I grew pale even as a man who freezes with terror.

- *mi scoss' io* ‘roused myself I’ → “I startled”: the emphatically postposed subject comes first, and the reflexive verb becomes intransitive.
- *sì come* ‘just as’ → “soon as”: the conjunction is rendered as marking immediate succession.
- *dalla faccia mi fuggì il sonno* ‘from the face fled from me the sleep’ → “sleep fled from my face”: the subject comes first and loses its article; the phrase of departure comes last, with the personal pronoun recast as a possessive.
- *come fa l' uom* ‘as does the man’ → “even as a man”: “even” is supplied, the substitute verb ‘does’ drops out, and the generic definite article becomes indefinite.
- *spaventato agghiaccia* ‘frightened, freezes’ → “freezes with terror”: the participle becomes a phrase of cause following the verb.

> 43 Da lato m' era solo il mio conforto,  

Alone beside me was my Comfort,

- *Da lato m' era solo* ‘at my side was alone’ → “Alone beside me was”: “Alone” moves to the front, and the relation expressed by ‘at the side’ and the personal pronoun becomes “beside me”.

> 44 e il sole er' alto già più che due ore,  

and the sun was already more than two hours high,

- *alto già più che due ore* ‘high already more than two hours’ → “already more than two hours high”: the adjective moves after the adverb and measure phrase.

> 45 e il viso m' era alla marina torto.  

and mine eyes were turned to the sea.

- *il viso m' era alla marina torto* ‘the face was to me toward the seashore turned’ → “mine eyes were turned to the sea”: “eyes” interprets ‘face’ in terms of the direction of sight, the personal pronoun becomes a possessive, and the verb becomes plural. The directional phrase follows the participle, with ‘seashore’ rendered as “sea”.

> 46 “Non aver tema,” disse il mio signore;  

“Have no fear,” said my Lord,

- *Non aver tema* ‘do not have fear’ → “Have no fear”: the negative infinitival imperative becomes an English imperative with the negation attached to the noun.

> 47 “fatti sicur, chè noi siamo a buon punto;  

“make thee secure, for we are at a good spot;

- *fatti sicur* ‘make thyself secure’ → “make thee secure”: “thee” has reflexive force without an explicit reflexive form.
- *a buon punto* ‘at a good point’ → “at a good spot”: “spot” takes ‘point’ spatially, and English supplies an article.

> 48 non stringer, ma rallarga ogni vigore.  

hold not back, but put out all thy strength.

- *non stringer* ‘do not draw tight’ → “hold not back”: the negative infinitival imperative becomes an English imperative, and the image of constriction gives way to an idiom for restraint.
- *rallarga ogni vigore* ‘let out every strength’ → “put out all thy strength”: the verb of loosening or expanding becomes an idiom of exertion; “all” replaces distributive ‘every’, and “thy” is supplied.

## Lines 49–81 — Lucia’s Ascent and the Gate of Purgatory

> 49 Tu se' omai al Purgatorio giunto:  

Thou art now arrived at Purgatory;

- *al Purgatorio giunto* ‘at Purgatory arrived’ → “arrived at Purgatory”: the participle precedes the phrase of place.

> 50 vedi là il balzo che il chiude d' intorno;  

see there the rampart that compasseth it around;

- *il balzo* ‘the ledge’ → “the rampart”: a word for a rise or shelf of rock is rendered by a word for an enclosing wall.
- *il chiude d' intorno* ‘it encloses all about’ → “compasseth it around”: the object pronoun follows the verb, and enclosing is expressed as encircling.

> 51 vedi l' entrata là 've par disgiunto.  

see the entrance there where it seems cleft.

- *par disgiunto* ‘seems parted’ → “it seems cleft”: the participle is rendered by one suggesting a split.

> 52 Dianzi, nell' alba che precede al giorno,  
> 53 quando l' anima tua dentro dormia  
> 54 sopra li fiori, onde laggiù è adorno,  

Erewhile, in the dawn which precedes the day, when thy soul was sleeping within thee upon the flowers wherewith down below is adorned,

- *l' anima tua* ‘the soul of thine’ → “thy soul”: the possessive precedes the noun.
- *dentro dormia* ‘within slept’ → “was sleeping within thee”: the imperfect becomes a past progressive, the adverb follows the verb, and its complement is made explicit.
- *onde* ‘of which’ → “wherewith”: the relation to the flowers is expressed as ‘with which’.
- *è adorno* ‘is decked’ → “is adorned”: the adjective of condition is rendered by a past participle.

> 55 venne una donna, e disse: ‘Io son Lucia;  

came a lady and said: ‘I am Lucy,

- Literal.

> 56 lasciatemi pigliar costui che dorme,  
> 57 sì l' agevolerò per la sua via.’  

let me take this man who sleepeth, so will I prosper him on his way.’

- *costui* ‘this one’ → “this man”: the masculine demonstrative pronoun is expanded to a demonstrative and noun.
- *sì l' agevolerò* ‘thus him I shall assist’ → “so will I prosper him”: the object follows the verb; the future is expressed by an auxiliary, placed before the subject after “so”. The verb of making things easier is rendered by transitive “prosper”.
- *per la sua via* ‘along his way’ → “on his way”: English uses “on” rather than a preposition marking movement along a path.

> 58 Sordel rimase, e l' altre gentil forme.  

Sordello remained and the other noble forms.

- Literal.

> 59 Ella ti tolse, e come il dì fu chiaro  
> 60 sen venne suso, ed io per le sue orme.  

She took thee, and as day was bright, came on upward, and I followed in her track.

- *ti tolse* ‘thee took’ → “took thee”: the object follows the verb.
- *come il dì fu chiaro* ‘as soon as the day became bright’ → “as day was bright”: the article is omitted, and the onset of daylight is expressed as a state.
- *sen venne suso* ‘came her way up’ → “came on upward”: the pronominal turn of motion is rendered by “came on”.
- *ed io per le sue orme* ‘and I along her footprints’ → “and I followed in her track”: a verb is supplied in the elliptical clause, and the plural footprints become a singular track, with “in” for ‘along’.

> 61 Qui ti posò; e pria mi dimostraro  
> 62 gli occhi suoi belli quell' entrata aperta;  

Here she placed thee; and first her fair eyes did show to me that open entrance;

- *ti posò* ‘thee set down’ → “she placed thee”: the object follows the verb, and “placed” does not separately express the downward motion.
- *mi dimostraro gli occhi suoi belli* ‘to me showed the eyes of hers fair’ → “her fair eyes did show to me”: the subject moves before the verb, its possessive and adjective before the noun, and the indirect object after the verb. The simple past becomes a periphrasis with “did”.
- *quell' entrata aperta* ‘that entrance open’ → “that open entrance”: the adjective precedes the noun.

> 63 poi ella e il sonno ad una se n' andaro.”  

then she and sleep together went away.”

- *il sonno* ‘the sleep’ → “sleep”: the article is omitted.
- *se n' andaro* ‘went their way from there’ → “went away”: the pronominal expression of departure is condensed into a verb and adverb.

> 64 A guisa d' uom che in dubbio si raccerta  
> 65 e che muta in conforto sua paura,  
> 66 poi che la verità gli è discoperta,  

As doth a man who in dread is reassured, and who changes his fear to comfort after the truth is revealed to him,

- *A guisa d' uom* ‘in the manner of a man’ → “As doth a man”: the prepositional comparison becomes a clause with a supplied verb.
- *in dubbio* ‘in doubt’ → “in dread”: uncertainty is rendered as fear.
- *si raccerta* ‘reassures himself’ → “is reassured”: the reflexive becomes passive.
- *muta in conforto sua paura* ‘changes into comfort his fear’ → “changes his fear to comfort”: the object comes before the phrase of result, and “to” replaces ‘into’.
- *poi che* ‘after that’ → “after”: a single conjunction serves for the two-word connective.
- *gli è discoperta* ‘to him is uncovered’ → “is revealed to him”: the recipient comes after the verb, and uncovering is rendered as revelation.

> 67 mi cambia' io; e come senza cura  
> 68 videmi il duca mio, su per lo balzo  
> 69 si mosse, ed io di retro in ver l' altura.  

I changed me; and when my Leader saw me freed from care, he moved up by the rampart, and I following, towards the height.

- *mi cambia' io* ‘myself changed I’ → “I changed me”: the emphatic postposed subject comes first and the reflexive object last.
- *senza cura videmi il duca mio* ‘without care saw me the leader of mine’ → “my Leader saw me freed from care”: the subject moves before the verb and its possessive before the noun; the initial phrase describing the object moves after it and becomes a participial phrase suggesting release from care.
- *su per lo balzo si mosse* ‘up along the ledge he moved himself’ → “he moved up by the rampart”: the verb comes before the path phrase and loses its reflexive form; “by” renders ‘along’, and “rampart” again renders the rocky ledge.
- *di retro* ‘behind’ → “following”: an adverb of position becomes a participle of motion.

> 70 Lettor, tu vedi ben com' io innalzo  
> 71 la mia materia, e però con più arte  
> 72 non ti maravigliar s' io la rincalzo.  

Reader, well thou seest how I exalt my subject, therefore marvel thou not if with greater art I sustain it.

- *tu vedi ben* ‘thou seest well’ → “well thou seest”: the adverb is brought forward.
- *innalzo la mia materia* ‘raise my matter’ → “exalt my subject”: the verb of height is rendered as one of elevation in dignity, and ‘matter’ as the subject treated.
- *e però* ‘and therefore’ → “therefore”: the coordinating conjunction is omitted.
- *con più arte non ti maravigliar s' io la rincalzo* ‘with more art do not marvel if I shore it up’ → “marvel thou not if with greater art I sustain it”: the phrase qualifying the final verb moves inside the conditional clause. The negative reflexive infinitive used as a command becomes a finite imperative with postposed subject; ‘more’ becomes “greater”, and the image of propping up becomes “sustain”, with the object after the verb.

> 73 Noi ci appressammo, ed eravamo in parte,  
> 74 che là dove pareami in prima un rotto,  
> 75 pur come un fesso che muro diparte,  

We drew nigh, and were at a place, whence there where first appeared to me a break just like a fissure which divides a wall,

- *ci appressammo* ‘we brought ourselves near’ → “We drew nigh”: the reflexive becomes an intransitive expression.
- *in parte* ‘in a place’ → “at a place”: the preposition changes and English supplies an article.
- *che* ‘that’ → “whence”: the connective is interpreted as marking the place from which the speaker sees.
- *pareami in prima* ‘appeared to me at first’ → “first appeared to me”: the adverbial phrase becomes a single adverb and moves before the verb.
- *un rotto* ‘a broken spot’ → “a break”: a substantivized participle becomes a noun.
- *che muro diparte* ‘which a wall divides’ → “which divides a wall”: the object moves after the verb and takes an article.

> 76 vidi una porta, e tre gradi di sotto,  
> 77 per gire ad essa, di color diversi,  
> 78 ed un portier che ancor non facea motto.  

I espied a gate, and three steps beneath to go to it, of divers colours, and a warder who as yet spake no word.

- *vidi* ‘I saw’ → “I espied”: the translation uses a verb of catching sight.
- *non facea motto* ‘did not make an utterance’ → “spake no word”: the negative shifts from the verb to its object, and a construction with ‘make’ becomes one with “spake”.

> 79 E come l' occhio più e più v' apersi,  
> 80 vidil seder sopra il grado soprano,  
> 81 tal nella faccia, ch' io non lo soffersi;  

And as more I opened mine eyes there, I saw him seated upon the topmost step, such in his countenance that I endured him not;

- *l' occhio più e più v' apersi* ‘the eye more and more there I opened’ → “more I opened mine eyes there”: the repetition marking progressive increase is lost; the singular with definite article becomes a plural with possessive. The object moves after the verb and the adverb of place to the end.
- *vidil seder* ‘I saw him sit’ → “I saw him seated”: the infinitive becomes a participle describing posture.
- *il grado soprano* ‘the step uppermost’ → “the topmost step”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *nella faccia* ‘in the face’ → “in his countenance”: the possessive replaces the definite article, and “countenance” renders ‘face’.
- *non lo soffersi* ‘not him I endured’ → “I endured him not”: the object and the negative both move after the verb.

## Lines 82–114 — At the Gate of Purgatory

> 82 ed una spada nuda aveva in mano,  
> 83 che rifletteva i raggi sì ver noi,  
> 84 ch' io dirizzava spesso il viso in vano,  

and in his hand he held a naked sword which reflected the rays so towards us, that I directed mine eyes to it oft in vain.

- *una spada nuda aveva in mano* ‘a sword naked he had in hand’ → “in his hand he held a naked sword”: the hand comes first and is given a possessive; ‘had’ becomes “held”, and the object moves after the verb, with its adjective before the noun.
- *dirizzava spesso il viso in vano* ‘turned often my face in vain’ → “directed mine eyes to it oft in vain”: Okey takes the face as the eyes, supplies what they are directed toward, and moves the adverb after “to it”.

> 85 “Dite costinci, che volete voi?”  
> 86 cominciò egli a dire: “ov' è la scorta?  
> 87 Guardate che il venir su non vi noi!”  

“Tell, there where ye stand, what would ye?” he began to say; “where is the escort? Beware lest coming upward be to your hurt!”

- *costinci* ‘from there’ → “there where ye stand”: the place adverb becomes a phrase specifying the hearers’ position, without the sense of ‘from’.
- *che volete voi* ‘what want ye’ → “what would ye”: “would” expresses the wish in place of ‘want’.
- *cominciò egli a dire* ‘began he to say’ → “he began to say”: the expressed subject moves before the verb.
- *Guardate che il venir su non vi noi* ‘take care that the coming up not harm you’ → “Beware lest coming upward be to your hurt”: “lest” carries the negative force of the warning; the substantivized infinitive loses its article, and the verb of harming with a personal object becomes “be” with a phrase containing a possessive.

> 88 “Donna del ciel, di queste cose accorta,”  
> 89 rispose il mio maestro a lui, “pur dianzi  
> 90 ne disse: ‘Andate là, quivi è la porta.’”  

“A heavenly lady who well knows these things,” my Master answered him, “even now did say to us: ‘Go ye thither, there is the gate.’”

- *Donna del ciel* ‘lady of heaven’ → “A heavenly lady”: an article is supplied and the phrase after the noun becomes an adjective before it.
- *di queste cose accorta* ‘of these things aware’ → “who well knows these things”: the adjective becomes a relative clause, with the things known placed after the verb as its direct object; “well” conveys the force of *accorta*.
- *rispose il mio maestro a lui* ‘answered my master to him’ → “my Master answered him”: the subject moves before the verb and the prepositional phrase becomes a direct object.
- *pur dianzi* ‘only a little while ago’ → “even now”: Okey uses “now” in the sense of ‘just now’.
- *ne disse* ‘to us said’ → “did say to us”: the pronoun moves after the verb in a prepositional phrase, and the simple past is expanded with “did”.

> 91 “Ed ella i passi vostri in bene avanzi,”  
> 92 ricominciò il cortese portinaio;  
> 93 “venite dunque a' nostri gradi innanzi.”  

“And may she speed your steps to good,” again began the courteous door-keeper; “come then forward to our stairs.”

- *ella i passi vostri in bene avanzi* ‘she your steps toward good advance’ → “may she speed your steps to good”: “may” expresses the wish marked by the subjunctive; the main verb moves before its object and the possessive before its noun.
- *ricominciò* ‘began again’ → “again began”: the prefix becomes a separate adverb before the verb.
- *a' nostri gradi innanzi* ‘to our steps forward’ → “forward to our stairs”: the directional adverb moves before the phrase of destination, and the steps are called “stairs”.

> 94 Là 've venimmo, allo scaglion primaio,  
> 95 bianco marmo era sì pulito e terso,  
> 96 ch' io mi specchiai in esso quale io paio.  

There where we came, at the first step, was white marble so polished and smooth that I mirrored me therein as I appear.

- *allo scaglion primaio* ‘at the step first’ → “at the first step”: the ordinal moves before the noun.
- *bianco marmo era* ‘white marble was’ → “was white marble”: the verb moves before its subject.
- *terso* ‘gleaming’ → “smooth”: the English stresses the finish of the surface rather than its lustre.
- *mi specchiai* ‘myself I mirrored’ → “I mirrored me”: the reflexive pronoun moves after the verb.

> 97 Era il secondo tinto più che perso,  
> 98 d' una petrina ruvida ed arsiccia,  
> 99 crepata per lo lungo e per traverso.  

The second darker was than perse, of a stone, rugged and calcined, cracked in its length and in its breadth.

- *Era il secondo tinto più che perso* ‘was the second coloured more than perse’ → “The second darker was than perse”: the subject moves first, and the participle of colouring and its degree word become the comparative adjective “darker”, placed before “was”.
- *petrina* ‘little stone’ → “stone”: the diminutive form is not reproduced.
- *arsiccia* ‘scorched’ → “calcined”: the English uses a more specific term for the effect of heat.
- *per lo lungo e per traverso* ‘lengthwise and crosswise’ → “in its length and in its breadth”: the two directions become parallel noun phrases with possessives.

> 100 Lo terzo, che di sopra s' ammassiccia,  
> 101 porfido mi parea sì fiammeggiante,  
> 102 come sangue che fuor di vena spiccia.  

The third, which is massy above, seemed to me of porphyry so flaming red as blood that spurts from a vein.

- *di sopra s' ammassiccia* ‘above forms itself into a mass’ → “is massy above”: the pronominal verb becomes “is” with an adjective, and the place adverb moves to the end.
- *porfido mi parea* ‘porphyry to me it seemed’ → “seemed to me of porphyry”: the verb moves first, the personal pronoun follows in a prepositional phrase, and “of” presents the porphyry as the material.
- *fiammeggiante* ‘flaming’ → “flaming red”: the colour is made explicit.
- *fuor di vena spiccia* ‘out of a vein spurts’ → “spurts from a vein”: the verb moves before the phrase of origin, “from” renders ‘out of’, and an article is supplied.

> 103 Sopra questo teneva ambo le piante  
> 104 l' angel di Dio, sedendo in su la soglia,  
> 105 che mi sembiava pietra di diamante.  

Upon this God’s angel held both his feet, sitting upon the threshold, which seemed to me adamantine stone.

- *teneva ambo le piante l' angel di Dio* ‘held both the soles the angel of God’ → “God’s angel held both his feet”: the subject moves from last to first, with a possessive before “angel”; the soles become the feet, and the article before them becomes a possessive.
- *in su la soglia* ‘in upon the threshold’ → “upon the threshold”: one English preposition does the work of the Italian combination.
- *mi sembiava* ‘to me seemed’ → “seemed to me”: the personal pronoun follows the verb in a prepositional phrase.
- *pietra di diamante* ‘stone of diamond’ → “adamantine stone”: the phrase of material becomes an adjective before the noun.

> 106 Per li tre gradi su di buona voglia  
> 107 mi trasse il duca mio, dicendo: “Chiedi  
> 108 umilemente che il serrame scioglia.”  

Up by the three steps, with my good will, my Leader brought me, saying: “Humbly ask that the bolt be loosed.”

- *Per li tre gradi su* ‘by the three steps up’ → “Up by the three steps”: the directional adverb moves first.
- *di buona voglia* ‘of good will’ → “with my good will”: the preposition changes and the possessive makes explicit whose willingness is meant.
- *mi trasse il duca mio* ‘me drew the leader mine’ → “my Leader brought me”: the subject moves before the verb and the object after it; the possessive moves before its noun, and “brought” replaces ‘drew’.
- *Chiedi umilemente* ‘ask humbly’ → “Humbly ask”: the adverb moves before the imperative.
- *che il serrame scioglia* ‘that the fastening he loose’ → “that the bolt be loosed”: the active subjunctive becomes passive, leaving the agent unexpressed; “bolt” specifies the fastening.

> 109 Divoto mi gittai a' santi piedi;  
> 110 misericordia chiesi che m' aprisse;  
> 111 ma pria nel petto tre fiate mi diedi.  

Devoutly I flung me at the holy feet; for mercy I craved that he would open to me; but first on my breast thrice I smote me.

- *Divoto* ‘devout’ → “Devoutly”: an adjective describing the speaker becomes an adverb of manner.
- *mi gittai* ‘myself I flung’ → “I flung me”: the reflexive pronoun moves after the verb.
- *misericordia chiesi* ‘mercy I asked’ → “for mercy I craved”: the direct object becomes a prepositional phrase, and “craved” is stronger than ‘asked’.
- *che m' aprisse* ‘that to me he might open’ → “that he would open to me”: “would” renders the imperfect subjunctive, and the personal pronoun follows the verb in a prepositional phrase.
- *nel petto* ‘in the breast’ → “on my breast”: the preposition changes and the possessor replaces the article.
- *tre fiate* ‘three times’ → “thrice”: numeral and noun become one adverb.
- *mi diedi* ‘myself I struck’ → “I smote me”: the reflexive pronoun moves after the verb; “smote” gives the sense of *diedi* rather than its usual ‘gave’.

> 112 Sette *P* nella fronte mi descrisse  
> 113 col punton della spada, e: “Fa' che lavi,  
> 114 quando se' dentro, queste piaghe,” disse.  

Seven P’s upon my forehead he described with the point of his sword and: “Do thou wash these wounds when thou art within,” he said.

- *nella fronte* ‘in the forehead’ → “upon my forehead”: the preposition changes and a possessive replaces the article.
- *mi descrisse* ‘for me he inscribed’ → “he described”: the personal pronoun has no separate equivalent here, its force having passed into the possessive; “described” is used in the sense of tracing a figure.
- *col punton della spada* ‘with the point of the sword’ → “with the point of his sword”: a possessive replaces the article before the weapon.
- *Fa' che lavi* ‘see to it that thou wash’ → “Do thou wash”: the imperative governing a subjunctive clause becomes a single emphatic command.
- *quando se' dentro, queste piaghe* ‘when thou art within, these wounds’ → “these wounds when thou art within”: the object moves before the temporal clause, directly after the verb it belongs to.

## Lines 115–145 — The Two Keys and the Opening of the Gate

> 115 Cenere o terra che secca si cavi  
> 116 d' un color fora col suo vestimento,  

Ashes, or earth which is dug out dry, would be of one colour with his vesture,

- *Cenere* ‘ash’ → “Ashes”: the Italian mass noun is singular.
- *che secca si cavi* ‘which dry one might dig out’ → “which is dug out dry”: the indefinite *si* construction with the subjunctive becomes an indicative passive; the adjective moves after the verb.
- *d' un color fora* ‘of one colour would be’ → “would be of one colour”: the verb comes before its complement.

> 117 e di sotto da quel trasse due chiavi.  

and from beneath it he drew forth two keys.

- *di sotto da quel* ‘from under that’ → “from beneath it”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.

> 118 L' una era d' oro e l' altra era d' argento;  

One was of gold and the other was of silver;

- *L' una* ‘the one’ → “One”: the article is dropped.
- *e l' altra era d' argento* ‘and the other of silver was’ → “and the other was of silver”: the verb moves before the phrase naming the metal.

> 119 pria con la bianca, e poscia con la gialla  
> 120 fece alla porta sì ch' io fui contento.  

first with the white and then with the yellow he did so to the gate that I was satisfied.

- *e poscia con la gialla* ‘and afterwards with the yellow’ → “and then with the yellow”: “then” marks the second step.
- *fece alla porta sì* ‘he did to the gate so’ → “he did so to the gate”: “so” moves next to the verb.
- *ch' io fui contento* ‘that I was content’ → “that I was satisfied”: “satisfied” replaces the cognate adjective.

> 121 “Quandunque l' una d' este chiavi falla,  
> 122 che non si volga dritta per la toppa,”  
> 123 diss' egli a noi, “non s' apre questa calla.  

“Whensoever one of these keys fails so that it turns not aright in the lock,” said he to us, “this passage opens not.

- *l' una d' este chiavi* ‘the one of these keys’ → “one of these keys”: the article is dropped.
- *che non si volga dritta per la toppa* ‘that it not turn straight through the keyhole’ → “so that it turns not aright in the lock”: “so” makes the connection with “fails” explicit; the pronominal subjunctive becomes a simple intransitive indicative, with the negative after the verb. The adjective agreeing with the key becomes an adverb, and a phrase of position in the lock replaces one of movement through the keyhole.
- *non s' apre questa calla* ‘does not open itself this passage’ → “this passage opens not”: the subject moves before the verb, the pronominal form becomes simply intransitive, and the negative follows.

> 124 Più cara è l' una; ma l' altra vuol troppa  
> 125 d' arte e d' ingegno avanti che disserri,  
> 126 perch' ell' è quella che il nodo disgroppa.  

More precious is one, but the other requires exceeding art and wit ere it unlocks, because it is the one which unties the knot.

- *l' una* ‘the one’ → “one”: the article is dropped.
- *vuol troppa d' arte e d' ingegno* ‘wants very much of art and of ingenuity’ → “requires exceeding art and wit”: wanting is expressed as requiring; “exceeding” qualifies the nouns directly, without the repeated partitive preposition. “Wit” renders ingenuity in the sense of mental skill.
- *avanti che disserri* ‘before it should unlock’ → “ere it unlocks”: the English temporal conjunction takes an indicative where Italian uses the subjunctive.
- *quella che il nodo disgroppa* ‘that one which the knot unties’ → “the one which unties the knot”: the demonstrative becomes “the one”, and the object moves after the verb.

> 127 Da Pier le tengo; e dissemi ch' io erri  
> 128 anzi ad aprir, che a tenerla serrata,  
> 129 pur che la gente a' piedi mi s' atterri.”  

From Peter I hold them; and he told me to err rather in opening, than in keeping it locked, if only the people fell prostrate at my feet.”

- *le tengo* ‘them I hold’ → “I hold them”: the object follows the verb.
- *dissemi ch' io erri* ‘he said to me that I should err’ → “he told me to err”: the indirect object becomes the object of “told”, and the finite subjunctive clause becomes an infinitive.
- *anzi ad aprir, che a tenerla serrata* ‘rather to open than to keep it shut’ → “rather in opening, than in keeping it locked”: preposition and gerund replace the infinitives; “locked” specifies the manner of closure.
- *pur che la gente a' piedi mi s' atterri* ‘provided that the people at the feet to me prostrate themselves’ → “if only the people fell prostrate at my feet”: the proviso becomes an “if only” clause, with a past form in place of the present subjunctive. The singular collective subject is construed as plural; the reflexive verb becomes verb and adjective, and the phrase denoting position moves to the end, with the dative of the possessor expressed by “my”.

> 130 Poi pinse l' uscio alla porta sacrata,  

Then he pushed the door of the sacred portal,

- *l' uscio alla porta sacrata* ‘the door at the portal sacred’ → “the door of the sacred portal”: “of” makes the door part of the portal rather than locating it there; the adjective precedes its noun.

> 131 dicendo: “Entrate; ma facciovi accorti  
> 132 che di fuor torna chi 'ndietro si guata.”  

saying: “Enter, but I make you ware that he who looketh behind returns outside again.”

- *facciovi accorti* ‘I make-you aware’ → “I make you ware”: the attached object pronoun becomes a separate word; the archaic adjective does not mark the plural of those addressed.
- *di fuor torna chi 'ndietro si guata* ‘outside returns whoever turns to look back’ → “he who looketh behind returns outside again”: the relative clause serving as subject moves to the front. “He who” expands the single relative pronoun, the looking is expressed without the reflexive, and “again” reinforces the sense of return.

> 133 E quando fur ne' cardini distorti  
> 134 gli spigoli di quella regge sacra  
> 135 che di metallo son sonanti e forti,  

And when in their sockets were turned the pivots of that sacred portal, which are of metal ringing and strong,

- *fur ne' cardini distorti* ‘were in the sockets turned’ → “in their sockets were turned”: the phrase locating the movement moves before the auxiliary, bringing auxiliary and participle together; a possessive replaces the article.
- *gli spigoli* ‘the edges’ → “the pivots”: the translation names the projecting parts by their function in the hinge.
- *di quella regge sacra* ‘of that portal sacred’ → “of that sacred portal”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *che di metallo son* ‘which of metal are’ → “which are of metal”: the verb precedes its complement.
- *sonanti e forti* ‘resounding and strong’ → “ringing and strong”: “ringing” specifies the sound. In Italian both adjectives are plural and describe the pivots, an agreement not marked in English.

> 136 non rugghiò sì, nè si mostrò sì acra  
> 137 Tarpeia, come tolto le fu il buono  
> 138 Metello, per che poi rimase macra.  

Tarpeia roared not so, nor showed her so harsh, when good Metellus was taken from her, whereby she after remained poor.

- *non rugghiò sì, nè si mostrò sì acra Tarpeia* ‘roared not so, nor showed herself so harsh, Tarpeia’ → “Tarpeia roared not so, nor showed her so harsh”: the shared subject moves from after both verbs to before them; the reflexive is rendered by the older use of “her”, following the verb.
- *come* ‘as’ → “when”: the comparative link after *sì* is expressed as a temporal one.
- *tolto le fu il buono Metello* ‘taken from her was the good Metellus’ → “good Metellus was taken from her”: the subject moves first and loses its article; auxiliary and participle come together, followed by the phrase rendering the dative.
- *per che* ‘through which’ → “whereby”: the preposition and relative are combined in one adverb.
- *macra* ‘lean’ → “poor”: the figurative adjective is rendered by its sense rather than its image.

> 139 Io mi rivolsi attento al primo tuono,  
> 140 e *Te Deum laudamus* mi parea  
> 141 udir in voce mista al dolce suono.  

I turned me intent for the first sound, and *Te Deum laudamus* meseemed to hear in a voice mingled with sweet music.

- *mi rivolsi* ‘myself I turned’ → “I turned me”: the reflexive moves after the verb and takes the form “me”.
- *al primo tuono* ‘at the first thunder’ → “for the first sound”: “for” makes the sound the object of attention, and “sound” is less specific than ‘thunder’.
- *mi parea udir* ‘to me it seemed to hear’ → “meseemed to hear”: the dative and the impersonal verb are expressed by the single archaic form “meseemed”.
- *in voce* ‘in voice’ → “in a voice”: the indefinite article is supplied.
- *mista al dolce suono* ‘mixed with the sweet sound’ → “mingled with sweet music”: “mingled” renders the participle, the article disappears, and “sound” is interpreted more specifically as music.

> 142 Tale imagine appunto mi rendea  
> 143 ciò ch' io udiva, qual prender si suole  
> 144 quando a cantar con organi si stea:  
> 145 che or sì or no s' intendon le parole.  

Just such impression gave me that which I heard, as we are wont to receive when people are singing with an organ, and now the words are clear, and now are not.

- *Tale imagine appunto* ‘such an image precisely’ → “Just such impression”: “Just” moves before “such”, and the image becomes an impression.
- *mi rendea* ‘to me rendered’ → “gave me”: the verb of giving replaces that of rendering, with its object after it.
- *qual prender si suole* ‘as to take one is accustomed’ → “as we are wont to receive”: the impersonal construction becomes first-person plural, the infinitive follows the expression of custom, and taking is expressed as receiving.
- *quando a cantar con organi si stea* ‘when at singing with organs one should be’ → “when people are singing with an organ”: “people” supplies a subject for impersonal *si*; the subjunctive with *a* and an infinitive becomes an indicative progressive, and the plural instrument becomes indefinite singular.
- *che or sì or no s' intendon le parole* ‘that now yes, now no, the words are understood’ → “and now the words are clear, and now are not”: coordination replaces the explanatory *che*. The words move from final position into the first alternative; being understood becomes being clear, and the compact affirmative and negative alternatives are expanded into two clauses.

## Summary — Italian Order and English Idiom

Okey keeps much of the Italian sequence, even where the order is unusual in English: *di gemme* remains at the head of its clause as “with gems”, and the subject still follows the verb in “came a lady”. Elsewhere he brings the subject before the verb, as when the swallow is named before “begins” rather than after its object. Object pronouns generally move after their verbs, and modifiers often move before their nouns: *quell' entrata aperta* becomes “that open entrance”. Two routine adjustments are largely left unmarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending identifies the subject, and drops the article that Italian uses before a possessive, as in *il mio conforto*, “my Comfort”.

The choice of words sometimes narrows an image or interprets it. Thus *calare*, ‘descend’, becomes “swoop”, while *divina*, ‘divine’, becomes “prophetic”. Elsewhere a distinction is lost: “the swallow” does not convey the diminutive of *rondinella*, and “more” does not repeat the comparative as *più e più* does. Additions can make an understood relation explicit: “within thee” gives *dentro* a complement, and “I followed in her track” supplies the verb that *ed io per le sue orme* leaves unstated.

Changes of construction range from the Italian imperfect rendered by an English progressive, as in *dormia*, “was sleeping”, to a change of voice: *che il serrame scioglia*, ‘that he loose the fastening’, becomes “that the bolt be loosed”. Yet Okey does not consistently smooth the prose into ordinary English idiom. Reflexives such as *mi gittai* and *mi rivolsi* remain audible in “I flung me” and “I turned me”; even where the object changes position, the construction stays close to Dante’s.
