# Purgatorio 33

## Lines 1–9 — The Ladies’ Psalm and Beatrice’s Reply

> 1 “*Deus, venerunt gentes*” alternando,  
> 2 or tre or quattro, dolce salmodia  
> 3 le donne incominciaro, e lagrimando;  

“*Deus, venerunt gentes*”: now three, now four, alternately and weeping, a sweet psalmody the ladies began;

- *alternando, or tre or quattro* ‘alternating, now three, now four’ → “now three, now four, alternately”: the gerund becomes an adverb and moves after “now three, now four”.
- *dolce salmodia le donne incominciaro, e lagrimando* ‘sweet psalmody the ladies began, and weeping’ → “and weeping, a sweet psalmody the ladies began”: the final phrase moves before the object, and the indefinite article is supplied.

> 4 e Beatrice sospirosa e pia  
> 5 quelle ascoltava sì fatta, che poco  
> 6 più alla croce si cambiò Maria.  

and Beatrice sighing and compassionate was hearkening to them so altered, that little more did Mary change at the cross.

- *sospirosa* ‘sighful’ → “sighing”: the adjective becomes a present participle.
- *pia* ‘pious, tender-hearted’ → “compassionate”: the translation takes the adjective in its sense of pity.
- *quelle ascoltava* ‘those she listened to’ → “was hearkening to them”: the demonstrative direct object becomes a personal pronoun after a preposition, and the imperfect is expressed by the past progressive.
- *sì fatta* ‘so made’ → “so altered”: the Italian describes the state of Beatrice; the English makes the change in her explicit.
- *alla croce si cambiò Maria* ‘at the cross changed herself Mary’ → “did Mary change at the cross”: the phrase of place moves to the end, the reflexive becomes an intransitive verb, and with “little more” placed first, English puts the auxiliary before the subject and the main verb after it.

> 7 Ma poi che l' altre vergini dier loco  
> 8 a lei di dir, levata dritta in piè  
> 9 rispose, colorata come foco:  

But when the other virgins gave place to her to speak, uprisen erect on her feet, she answered in hue of fire:

- *poi che* ‘after that’ → “when”: the temporal conjunction marks the occasion rather than expressly marking succession.
- *di dir* ‘of speaking’ → “to speak”: the preposition and infinitive give way to an English to-infinitive.
- *in piè* ‘on foot’ → “on her feet”: the noun becomes plural and takes a possessive.
- *colorata come foco* ‘coloured like fire’ → “in hue of fire”: the participial adjective becomes a prepositional phrase with a noun, and the explicit comparison becomes an of-phrase.

## Lines 10–42 — Beatrice Invites Questions and Foretells Vengeance

> 10 “*Modicum, et non videbitis me,*  
> 11 *et iterum,* sorelle mie dilette,  
> 12 *modicum, et vos videbitis me.*”  

“*Modicum, et non videbitis me, et iterum,* my beloved sisters, *modicum, et vos videbitis me.*”

- *sorelle mie dilette* ‘sisters my beloved’ → “my beloved sisters”: both the possessive and the adjective move before the noun.

> 13 Poi le si mise innanzi tutte e sette,  
> 14 e dopo sè, solo accennando, mosse  
> 15 me e la donna e il savio che ristette.  

Then she placed them all seven in front of her, and, merely by her nod, motioned behind her, me and the Lady and the Sage who had stayed.

- *le si mise innanzi tutte e sette* ‘them before herself she placed, all seven’ → “she placed them all seven in front of her”: the object pronoun follows the verb, its apposition “all seven” comes immediately after it, and the reflexive construction becomes a prepositional phrase ending in “her”.
- *dopo sè* ‘after herself’ → “behind her”: the spatial relation is made explicit, the pronoun is not reflexive in form, and the phrase moves after the verb.
- *solo accennando* ‘only making a sign’ → “merely by her nod”: the gerund becomes a phrase of means, with a possessive and a more specific noun for the sign.
- *mosse* ‘she moved’ → “motioned”: English names the act of directing the others rather than the movement she caused.
- *che ristette* ‘who stayed’ → “who had stayed”: the simple past becomes a past perfect.

> 16 Così sen giva, e non credo che fosse  
> 17 lo decimo suo passo in terra posto,  
> 18 quando con gli occhi gli occhi mi percosse;  

Thus she went on, and I believe not that her tenth step was put on the ground, when with her eyes mine eyes she smote;

- *sen giva* ‘she went her way’ → “she went on”: “on” conveys the continuing motion of the Italian pronominal expression.
- *non credo* ‘I do not believe’ → “I believe not”: the negative follows the verb instead of preceding it.
- *che fosse lo decimo suo passo in terra posto* ‘that was her tenth step on the ground set’ → “that her tenth step was put on the ground”: the subject moves before the passive auxiliary, the possessive before the ordinal, and the participle before the phrase of place; English does not distinguish the Italian subjunctive here.
- *con gli occhi gli occhi mi percosse* ‘with the eyes the eyes she smote for me’ → “with her eyes mine eyes she smote”: the first possessor is understood in Italian, and the second is indicated by the dative *mi*; English makes both possessors explicit before their nouns.

> 19 e con tranquillo aspetto: “Vien più tosto,”  
> 20 mi disse, “tanto che s' io parlo teco,  
> 21 ad ascoltarmi tu sie ben disposto.”  

and with tranquil mien did say to me: “Come more quickly so that if I speak with thee, thou be well placed to listen to me.”

- *Vien più tosto, mi disse* ‘come more quickly, to me she said’ → “did say to me: “Come more quickly”: the reporting clause moves before the command, the indirect object follows the verb, and “did say” renders a simple past.
- *tanto che* ‘so much that’ → “so that”: the expression of degree becomes a conjunction of purpose.
- *ad ascoltarmi tu sie ben disposto* ‘to listen to me thou be well disposed’ → “thou be well placed to listen to me”: the infinitive moves to the end, and “placed” gives *disposto* a specifically positional sense.

> 22 Sì com' io fui, com' io doveva, seco,  
> 23 dissemi: “Frate, perchè non ti attenti  
> 24 a domandarmi omai venendo meco?”  

Soon as I was with her, as ’twas my duty to be, she said to me: “Brother, wherefore coming now with me, venturest thou not to ask of me?”

- *Sì com' io fui, com' io doveva, seco* ‘as soon as I was, as I ought, with her’ → “Soon as I was with her, as ’twas my duty to be”: “with her” is brought forward to complete the first clause; the personal modal verb becomes an impersonal expression with “my duty”, and the understood infinitive is supplied.
- *dissemi* ‘said to me’ → “she said to me”: the attached indirect-object pronoun becomes a separate prepositional phrase.
- *perchè non ti attenti* ‘why do you not dare’ → “wherefore coming now with me, venturest thou not”: the question is interrupted before its verb; the reflexive Italian verb becomes nonreflexive “venturest”, with the subject inverted and the negative following it.
- *a domandarmi* ‘to ask me’ → “to ask of me”: the attached object pronoun becomes a prepositional complement.
- *omai venendo meco* ‘by now coming with me’ → “coming now with me”: the phrase moves forward in the question, and “now” is placed after “coming”, attaching the time more closely to the coming than to the asking.

> 25 Come a color, che troppo reverenti  
> 26 dinanzi a' suoi maggior parlando sono,  
> 27 che non traggon la voce viva ai denti,  

As to those, who in presence of their betters are too lowly in speech so that they bring not their voice whole to the lips,

- *troppo reverenti … parlando sono* ‘too reverent … in speaking are’ → “are too lowly in speech”: the verb moves before its complement, reverence is rendered as lowliness, and the gerund becomes a prepositional phrase with a noun.
- *dinanzi a' suoi maggior* ‘before their superiors’ → “in presence of their betters”: the preposition is expanded into a phrase, which moves before the description of how they speak.
- *che non traggon* ‘that they do not draw’ → “so that they bring not”: “so” marks the clause explicitly as a result, “bring” replaces ‘draw’, and the negative follows the verb.
- *la voce viva* ‘the voice alive’ → “their voice whole”: English supplies the possessive and takes *viva* in the sense of undiminished strength rather than literally ‘alive’.
- *ai denti* ‘to the teeth’ → “to the lips”: one part of the mouth is substituted for another.

> 28 avvenne a me, che senza intero suono  
> 29 incominciai: “Madonna, mia bisogna  
> 30 voi conoscete, e ciò ch' ad essa è buono.”  

it happened to me and without full utterance I began: “My Lady, my need you know, and that which is good for it.”

- *che* ‘that’ → “and”: coordination replaces the clause introduced by *che* after ‘it happened to me’.
- *senza intero suono* ‘without full sound’ → “without full utterance”: “utterance” specifies the sound as speech.
- *ciò ch' ad essa è buono* ‘that which for it is good’ → “that which is good for it”: the prepositional complement moves after the adjective.

> 31 Ed ella a me: “Da tema e da vergogna  
> 32 voglio che tu omai ti disviluppe,  
> 33 sì che non parli più com' uom che sogna.  

And she to me: “From fear and from shame I would that now thou unbind thee, so that thou speak no more like one that is dreaming.

- *voglio* ‘I will’ → “I would”: the direct present expression of will becomes a modal expression of desire.
- *tu omai ti disviluppe* ‘thou by now thyself disentangle’ → “now thou unbind thee”: the adverb moves before the subject, and the reflexive pronoun follows the verb; ‘disentangle’ is rendered as “unbind”.
- *non parli più* ‘thou speak not any more’ → “thou speak no more”: the separated elements of the Italian negative become the single postverbal phrase “no more”.
- *com' uom che sogna* ‘like a man who dreams’ → “like one that is dreaming”: the noun becomes the indefinite pronoun “one”, and a simple present becomes a progressive.

> 34 Sappi che il vaso, che il serpente ruppe,  
> 35 fu e non è, ma chi n' ha colpa creda  
> 36 che vendetta di Dio non teme suppe.  

Know that the vessel which the serpent broke, was, and is not; but let him whose fault it is, believe that God’s vengeance fears no sops.

- *chi n' ha colpa creda* ‘whoever has blame for it, let him believe’ → “let him whose fault it is, believe”: “let” expresses the third-person imperative force of the subjunctive; having blame for something becomes its being someone’s fault.
- *vendetta di Dio* ‘vengeance of God’ → “God’s vengeance”: the possessive moves before the noun.
- *non teme suppe* ‘does not fear sops’ → “fears no sops”: the negation shifts from the verb to its object.

> 37 Non sarà tutto tempo senza ereda  
> 38 l' aquila che lasciò le penne al carro,  
> 39 per che divenne mostro e poscia preda:  

Not for all time shall be without heir the eagle that left the plumage on the car, whereby it became a monster and then a prey;

- *Non sarà tutto tempo* ‘it shall not be all the time’ → “Not for all time shall be”: the expression of duration is put first with the negative, and “for” is supplied.
- *le penne* ‘the feathers’ → “the plumage”: a collective singular replaces the plural.
- *al carro* ‘to the car’ → “on the car”: English gives the place where the plumage was left rather than using ‘to’.
- *per che* ‘through which’ → “whereby”: a single relative adverb replaces preposition and pronoun.
- *divenne mostro e poscia preda* ‘it became monster and then prey’ → “it became a monster and then a prey”: English supplies an indefinite article before each predicate noun.

> 40 ch' io veggio certamente, e però il narro,  
> 41 a darne tempo già stelle propinque,  
> 42 sicure d' ogni intoppo e d' ogni sbarro,  

for of a surety I see, and therefore do tell it, stars already nigh, secure from all impediment and from all hindrance, that shall bring us times

- *io veggio certamente* ‘I see certainly’ → “of a surety I see”: the adverb becomes a prepositional phrase and moves before the subject.
- *e però il narro* ‘and therefore it I tell’ → “and therefore do tell it”: “do” is supplied, and the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *a darne tempo* ‘to give us time’ → “that shall bring us times”: the infinitive becomes a relative clause with an explicit future, ‘give’ becomes “bring”, and singular *tempo* becomes plural “times”; the whole phrase moves from before “stars” to the end.
- *già stelle propinque* ‘already stars near’ → “stars already nigh”: “already” moves after the noun to stand next to the adjective.
- *sicure d' ogni intoppo e d' ogni sbarro* ‘secure from every obstacle and from every barrier’ → “secure from all impediment and from all hindrance”: each distributive ‘every’ becomes collective “all”, with abstract nouns in place of the individual obstacles and barriers.

## Lines 43–75 — The Prophecy and the Despoiled Tree

> 43 nel quale un cinquecento diece e cinque,  
> 44 messo da Dio, anciderà la fuia  
> 45 con quel gigante che con lei delinque.  

wherein a five hundred ten and five, sent by God, shall slay the thief, with that giant who sins with her.

- *nel quale* ‘in which’ → “wherein”: the preposition and relative pronoun become a single relative adverb.
- *la fuia* ‘the she-thief’ → “the thief”: the English noun does not mark the thief as female.
- *che con lei delinque* ‘who with her sins’ → “who sins with her”: the prepositional phrase follows the verb.

> 46 E forse che la mia narrazion, buia  
> 47 qual Temi e Sfinge, men ti persuade,  
> 48 perch' a lor modo lo intelletto attuia;  

And perchance my prophecy, obscure as Themis and Sphinx, doth less persuade thee, because after their fashion it darkens thy mind;

- *forse che* ‘perhaps’ → “perchance”: the introductory *che* after the adverb has no separate equivalent.
- *la mia narrazion* ‘my account’ → “my prophecy”: the English specifies the kind of account.
- *buia qual* ‘dark as’ → “obscure as”: the adjective is rendered in its figurative sense.
- *men ti persuade* ‘less thee persuades’ → “doth less persuade thee”: the auxiliary precedes the adverb, and the object pronoun follows the main verb.
- *a lor modo* ‘in their manner’ → “after their fashion”: the preposition and noun are rendered by an English idiom.
- *lo intelletto attuia* ‘the understanding it darkens’ → “it darkens thy mind”: the object follows the verb, and the definite article becomes a possessive identifying whose understanding is meant.

> 49 ma tosto fien li fatti le Naiade,  
> 50 che solveranno questo enigma forte,  
> 51 senza danno di pecore o di biade.  

but soon the facts shall be the Naiades that will solve this hard riddle without loss of flocks or of corn.

- *fien li fatti le Naiade* ‘shall be the facts the Naiads’ → “the facts shall be the Naiades”: the subject precedes the verb.
- *questo enigma forte* ‘this riddle hard’ → “this hard riddle”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *senza danno di pecore o di biade* ‘without harm to sheep or to crops’ → “without loss of flocks or of corn”: harm to something becomes loss of it; the animals are named collectively, and the crops are specified as grain.

> 52 Tu nota; e, sì come da me son porte,  
> 53 così queste parole segna ai vivi  
> 54 del viver ch' è un correre alla morte;  

Note thou; and even as these words from me are borne, so do thou signify them to those who live that life which is a race unto death;

- *Tu nota* ‘thou note’ → “Note thou”: the emphatic subject follows the imperative.
- *sì come da me son porte* ‘even as by me they are proffered’ → “even as these words from me are borne”: the subject, implicit here in Italian, is named in anticipation of the next clause; the agent becomes the source, and the participle expresses conveyance rather than presentation.
- *così queste parole segna* ‘so these words mark’ → “so do thou signify them”: the imperative takes an auxiliary and an explicit subject; the object, already named in English, becomes a pronoun after the verb. ‘Mark’ is rendered in the sense of making known.
- *ai vivi del viver* ‘to the living of the living’ → “to those who live that life”: the substantivized adjective becomes a relative clause, and the substantivized infinitive governed by *del* becomes the direct object of “live”, with a demonstrative.
- *un correre* ‘a running’ → “a race”: a noun replaces the substantivized infinitive.

> 55 ed abbi a mente, quando tu le scrivi,  
> 56 di non celar qual hai vista la pianta,  
> 57 ch' è or due volte dirubata quivi.  

and bear in mind when thou writest them, not to conceal how thou hast seen the tree which now twice hath been despoiled here.

- *abbi a mente* ‘have in mind’ → “bear in mind”: the verb changes in the English idiom.
- *quando tu le scrivi* ‘when thou them writest’ → “when thou writest them”: the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *di non celar* ‘of not concealing’ → “not to conceal”: the preposition introducing the Italian infinitive has no separate equivalent.
- *qual hai vista la pianta* ‘of what sort thou hast seen the plant’ → “how thou hast seen the tree”: the predicative *qual*, referring to the condition of the plant, becomes an adverb, and the more general noun becomes “tree”.
- *è or due volte dirubata* ‘is now two times despoiled’ → “now twice hath been despoiled”: the present passive describing the resulting state becomes a present perfect passive; both adverbials precede it, and the count is expressed by one adverb.

> 58 Qualunque ruba quella o quella schianta,  
> 59 con bestemmia di fatto offende a Dio,  
> 60 che solo all' uso suo la creò santa.  

Whoso robs that or that doth rend, with blasphemy in act offendeth God, who alone for his service did create it holy.

- *quella schianta* ‘that rends’ → “that doth rend”: the English retains the object before the verb but adds an auxiliary.
- *con bestemmia di fatto* ‘with blasphemy of deed’ → “with blasphemy in act”: the relation expressed by *di* is recast with “in”.
- *offende a Dio* ‘gives offence to God’ → “offendeth God”: the English verb takes a direct object instead of a prepositional complement.
- *che solo all’ uso suo* ‘who only for the use of his own’ → “who alone for his service”: the English attaches “alone” to “who”, rather than restricting the purpose to his use only; “service” interprets ‘use’, and the possessive precedes the noun.
- *la creò santa* ‘it created holy’ → “did create it holy”: the simple past is expressed with an auxiliary, and the object pronoun follows the main verb.

> 61 Per morder quella, in pena ed in disio  
> 62 cinquemili' anni e più l' anima prima  
> 63 bramò Colui che il morso in sè punio.  

For eating of that, in torment and in desire, five thousand years and more the first soul did yearn for him who punished the bite in himself.

- *Per morder quella* ‘for biting that’ → “For eating of that”: the infinitive becomes a gerund, and biting a direct object becomes eating of something.
- *in pena ed in disio* ‘in pain and in desire’ → “in torment and in desire”: the first noun is rendered more strongly.
- *l’ anima prima* ‘the soul first’ → “the first soul”: the ordinal precedes the noun.
- *bramò Colui* ‘longed for that one’ → “did yearn for him”: the simple past takes an auxiliary, and the emphatic demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun.
- *che il morso in sè punio* ‘who the bite in himself punished’ → “who punished the bite in himself”: the verb precedes both its object and the prepositional phrase.

> 64 Dorme lo ingegno tuo, se non estima  
> 65 per singular cagione essere eccelsa  
> 66 lei tanto, e sì travolta nella cima.  

Thy wit sleepeth if it judge not that tree to be for special cause thus lofty and thus transposed at the top.

- *Dorme lo ingegno tuo* ‘sleeps the intellect of thine’ → “Thy wit sleepeth”: the subject precedes the verb, the possessive precedes the noun, and the faculty of understanding is called “wit”.
- *se non estima* ‘if it does not judge’ → “if it judge not”: the Italian present indicative becomes an English subjunctive, with the negative after the verb.
- *per singular cagione essere eccelsa lei tanto* ‘for a special cause to be lofty she so much’ → “that tree to be for special cause thus lofty”: the pronoun is replaced by an explicit noun and placed before the infinitive; the phrase of cause follows the infinitive, without an article, and the degree word precedes the adjective.
- *travolta nella cima* ‘turned about in the crown’ → “transposed at the top”: the participle is interpreted as a change of position, and “at” replaces the Italian preposition of inclusion.

> 67 E, se stati non fossero acqua d' Elsa  
> 68 li pensier vani intorno alla tua mente,  
> 69 e il piacer loro un Piramo alla gelsa,  

And if thy idle thoughts had not been Elsan waters about thy mind, and their pleasantness a Pyramus to the mulberry,

- *se stati non fossero acqua d’ Elsa li pensier vani* ‘if been they had not water of Elsa the vain thoughts’ → “if thy idle thoughts had not been Elsan waters”: the subject moves before the verb, the auxiliary before the participle, and the negative between them. A possessive replaces the definite article, “idle” precedes its noun, and singular water with a place-name complement becomes plural “waters” with an adjective.
- *il piacer loro* ‘the pleasure of them’ → “their pleasantness”: pleasure is expressed as a quality, and the possessive precedes the noun.

> 70 per tante circostanze solamente  
> 71 la giustizia di Dio, nello interdetto,  
> 72 conosceresti all' arbor moralmente.  

by so many circumstances alone thou wouldst recognise in the tree morally, God’s justice in the ban.

- *la giustizia di Dio, nello interdetto, conosceresti* ‘the justice of God, in the prohibition, thou wouldst recognise’ → “thou wouldst recognise in the tree morally, God’s justice in the ban”: the verb moves ahead of its object and the phrase about the prohibition; the possessive replaces the *di* phrase, and “ban” renders the noun for the prohibition.
- *all’ arbor moralmente* ‘at the tree morally’ → “in the tree morally”: the preposition becomes “in”, and the phrase now stands between the verb and its object rather than at the end of the clause.

> 73 Ma, perch' io veggio te nello intelletto  
> 74 fatto di pietra ed, impietrato, tinto  
> 75 sì che t' abbaglia il lume del mio detto,  

But because I see thy mind turned to stone and, stonelike, such in hue that the light of my word dazes thee,

- *io veggio te nello intelletto fatto di pietra* ‘I see thee in the understanding made of stone’ → “I see thy mind turned to stone”: the person ceases to be the direct object; the faculty becomes the object and takes a possessive. Being made of a material is expressed as a transformation into it.
- *impietrato, tinto sì* ‘petrified, coloured so’ → “stonelike, such in hue”: the first participle becomes an adjective of likeness; the second participle and its degree adverb become a phrase describing the resulting colour.
- *t’ abbaglia il lume del mio detto* ‘thee dazzles the light of my saying’ → “the light of my word dazes thee”: the subject moves before the verb and the object after it; the effect of dazzling is rendered as dazing, and ‘saying’ as “word”.

## Lines 76–108 — The Imprint of Beatrice’s Words

> 76 voglio anche, e se non scritto, almen dipinto,  
> 77 che il te ne porti dentro a te, per quello  
> 78 che si reca il bordon di palma cinto.”  

I also will that thou bear it away within thee, and if not written at least outlined, for the reason that the pilgrim’s staff is brought back wreathed with palm.”

- *voglio anche* ‘I will also’ → “I also will”: the adverb precedes the verb.
- *e se non scritto, almen dipinto* ‘and if not written, at least painted’ → “and if not written at least outlined”: the qualification comes after the clause about bearing it away, rather than before it; painting becomes outlining.
- *che il te ne porti* ‘that it thou carry away with thee’ → “that thou bear it away”: the object follows the verb; the sense of carrying off for oneself in *te ne porti* is not separately expressed.
- *dentro a te* ‘inside of thee’ → “within thee”: one preposition does the work of two.
- *per quello che* ‘for that for which’ → “for the reason that”: the demonstrative becomes the explicit noun “reason”; the English reads as giving a cause rather than referring to the purpose for which something is done.
- *si reca il bordon* ‘one brings the staff’ → “the pilgrim’s staff is brought back”: the *si* construction becomes a passive with the staff as subject before the verb; “pilgrim’s” makes the kind of staff explicit, and “back” gives the direction.
- *di palma cinto* ‘with palm girded’ → “wreathed with palm”: the participle comes first, and girding becomes wreathing.

> 79 Ed io: “Sì come cera da suggello,  
> 80 che la figura impressa non trasmuta,  
> 81 segnato è or da voi lo mio cervello.  

And I: “Even as wax under the seal, that the imprinted figure changeth not, my brain is now stamped by you.

- *cera da suggello* ‘wax for a seal’ → “wax under the seal”: suitability for sealing becomes position under a particular seal.
- *che la figura impressa non trasmuta* ‘which alters not the figure impressed’ → “that the imprinted figure changeth not”: in Italian the wax is the subject and the figure the object; in the natural English reading the figure is the subject of an intransitive verb. The participle precedes its noun, and the negative follows the verb.
- *segnato è or da voi lo mio cervello* ‘marked is now by you my brain’ → “my brain is now stamped by you”: the subject moves from last to first, the participle follows the auxiliary and adverb, and “stamped” is more specific than ‘marked’.

> 82 Ma perchè tanto sopra mia veduta  
> 83 vostra parola disiata vola,  
> 84 che più la perde quanto più s' aiuta?”  

But why doth your longed-for word soar so far beyond my sight, that the more it straineth the more it loses it?”

- *tanto sopra mia veduta vostra parola disiata vola* ‘so much above my sight your word longed for flies’ → “doth your longed-for word soar so far beyond my sight”: the question takes an auxiliary; the subject precedes the main verb, the adjective precedes its noun, and the phrase of distance comes last. Flying ‘above’ becomes soaring “beyond”.
- *più la perde quanto più s’ aiuta* ‘more it loses it the more it helps itself’ → “the more it straineth the more it loses it”: the two halves of the comparison exchange places; helping oneself becomes making an effort, and the object of losing follows its verb.

> 85 “Perchè conoschi,” disse, “quella scuola  
> 86 ch' hai seguitata, e veggi sua dottrina  
> 87 come può seguitar la mia parola;  

“That thou mayst know,” she said, “that School which thou hast followed, and see how its teaching can keep pace with my word;

- *Perchè conoschi* ‘in order that thou know’ → “That thou mayst know”: the purpose is expressed with a modal auxiliary rather than an inflected subjunctive.
- *e veggi sua dottrina come può* ‘and see its teaching how it can’ → “and see how its teaching can”: the teaching is placed directly inside the clause introduced by “how”, as its subject.
- *seguitar la mia parola* ‘follow my word’ → “keep pace with my word”: following is rendered as matching a pace.

> 88 e veggi vostra via dalla divina  
> 89 distar cotanto, quanto si discorda  
> 90 da terra il ciel che più alto festina.”  

and mayst see your way so far distant from the divine way, as the heaven which highest speeds is removed from earth.”

- *e veggi* ‘and see’ → “and mayst see”: the English renews the modal auxiliary for the subjunctive.
- *vostra via dalla divina distar cotanto* ‘your way from the divine to stand so far apart’ → “your way so far distant from the divine way”: the infinitive becomes an adjective with the degree phrase before it; the phrase beginning with “from” moves to the end, and “way” is repeated where Italian leaves the noun understood.
- *quanto si discorda da terra il ciel* ‘as differs from earth the heaven’ → “as the heaven which highest speeds is removed from earth”: the subject, with its relative clause, comes before the predicate; differing or standing apart becomes the passive expression “is removed”.
- *che più alto festina* ‘which highest hastens’ → “which highest speeds”: “speeds” renders the verb of hastening.

> 91 Ond' io risposi lei: “Non mi ricorda  
> 92 ch' io straniassi me giammai da voi  
> 93 nè honne coscienza che rimorda.”  

Wherefore I answered her: “I remember not that I e’er estranged me from you, nor have I conscience thereof that gnaws me.”

- *Non mi ricorda* ‘it comes not to mind for me’ → “I remember not”: the impersonal construction becomes a personal verb, with the negative after it.
- *straniassi me giammai* ‘should estrange myself ever’ → “e’er estranged me”: the imperfect subjunctive becomes a simple past, and the adverb moves before the verb.
- *nè honne coscienza* ‘nor have I of it conscience’ → “nor have I conscience thereof”: the equivalent of the attached *ne* follows the noun.
- *che rimorda* ‘that should gnaw’ → “that gnaws me”: the subjunctive becomes indicative, and the object is made explicit.

> 94 “E se tu ricordar non te ne puoi,”  
> 95 sorridendo rispose, “or ti rammenta  
> 96 come bevesti di Letè ancoi;  

“And if thou canst not remember it,” smiling she answered, “now bethink thee how thou didst drink of Lethe this very day;

- *ricordar non te ne puoi* ‘remember not thyself of it canst’ → “canst not remember it”: the modal moves before the negative and infinitive; reflexive remembering with *ne* becomes a verb with a direct object.
- *ti rammenta* ‘thyself bethink’ → “bethink thee”: the reflexive pronoun follows the imperative.
- *bevesti* ‘thou drankest’ → “thou didst drink”: auxiliary and infinitive replace the simple past.
- *ancoi* ‘today’ → “this very day”: a phrase with emphatic “very” replaces the adverb.

> 97 e se dal fummo foco s' argomenta,  
> 98 cotesta oblivion chiaro conchiude  
> 99 colpa nella tua voglia altrove attenta.  

and if from smoke fire is argued, this forgetfulness clearly proves fault in thy desire otherwhere intent.

- *dal fummo* ‘from the smoke’ → “from smoke”: the article is omitted.
- *foco s’ argomenta* ‘fire one infers’ → “fire is argued”: the inference expressed with *si* becomes an English passive; “argued” has the sense ‘inferred’.
- *cotesta oblivion* ‘that forgetfulness of thine’ → “this forgetfulness”: the demonstrative pointing to the addressee becomes “this”.
- *chiaro conchiude* ‘plainly concludes’ → “clearly proves”: drawing a conclusion is expressed as proving.

> 100 Veramente oramai saranno nude  
> 101 le mie parole, quanto converrassi  
> 102 quelle scoprire alla tua vista rude.”  

But now my words shall be naked, so far as shall be meet to discover them to thy rude vision.”

- *Veramente* ‘truly’ → “But”: the adverb is taken in its adversative sense rather than as an affirmation.
- *oramai saranno nude le mie parole* ‘henceforth shall be naked my words’ → “now my words shall be naked”: “now” does not expressly carry the sense ‘from now on’; the subject comes before the verb and adjective.
- *quanto converrassi* ‘as much as shall be fitting’ → “so far as shall be meet”: degree is expressed by a phrase of extent, and the impersonal verb by a copula and adjective.
- *quelle scoprire* ‘those to uncover’ → “to discover them”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun after the infinitive; “discover” is used in the sense ‘lay bare’.
- *alla tua vista rude* ‘to thy vision rude’ → “to thy rude vision”: the adjective precedes the noun.

> 103 E più corrusco, e con più lenti passi,  
> 104 teneva il sole il cerchio di merigge,  
> 105 che qua e là, come gli aspetti, fassi,  

Both more refulgent, and with slower steps, the sun was holding the meridian circle, which varies hither and thither as positions vary,

- *E più corrusco* ‘and more flashing’ → “Both more refulgent”: the initial conjunction is read as the first half of a paired construction; flashing brightness becomes refulgence.
- *più lenti passi* ‘more slow steps’ → “slower steps”: the comparative is expressed by an ending rather than a separate adverb.
- *teneva il sole* ‘was holding the sun’ (verb followed by its subject) → “the sun was holding”: the subject moves before the verb, and the progressive expresses the imperfect.
- *il cerchio di merigge* ‘the circle of midday’ → “the meridian circle”: the phrase following the noun becomes a modifier before it.
- *che qua e là, come gli aspetti, fassi* ‘which here and there, according to the aspects, forms itself’ → “which varies hither and thither as positions vary”: the final reflexive verb becomes “varies” near the beginning; words of place become directional, and the compressed comparison is expanded into a clause with a second “vary”, taking the aspects as positions.

> 106 quando s' affisser, sì come s' affigge  
> 107 chi va dinanzi a gente per iscorta,  
> 108 se trova novitate o sue vestigge,  

when did halt, even as he halts who goes for escort before folk, if he finds aught that is strange or the traces thereof,

- *s’ affisser* ‘they stopped themselves’ → “did halt”: the reflexive preterite becomes an intransitive verb with a past auxiliary; the English verb does not mark the plural.
- *sì come s’ affigge chi va* ‘just as stops himself whoever goes’ → “even as he halts who goes”: the reflexive becomes intransitive, and the single *chi* construction is divided between an anticipatory “he” and a relative “who”.
- *dinanzi a gente per iscorta* ‘before folk for escort’ → “for escort before folk”: the two phrases exchange places.
- *novitate* ‘a novelty’ → “aught that is strange”: a noun becomes an indefinite pronoun qualified by a relative clause.
- *sue vestigge* ‘its traces’ → “the traces thereof”: the possessive before the noun becomes “thereof” after it, and an article is supplied.

## Lines 109–141 — The Two Streams and the Sweet Draught

> 109 le sette donne al fin d' un' ombra smorta,  
> 110 qual sotto foglie verdi e rami nigri  
> 111 sopra suoi freddi rivi l' Alpe porta.  

those seven ladies at the margin of a pale shadow, such as beneath green leaves and dark boughs, the Alp casts over its cool streams.

- *le sette donne* ‘the seven women’ → “those seven ladies”: the definite article becomes a demonstrative, and the noun takes a more courtly rendering.
- *al fin* ‘at the end’ → “at the margin”: an end is rendered as an edge.
- *d' un' ombra smorta* ‘of a shadow pale’ → “of a pale shadow”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *sotto foglie verdi e rami nigri* ‘under leaves green and branches black’ → “beneath green leaves and dark boughs”: both adjectives precede their nouns; “dark” is less specific than ‘black’.
- *sopra suoi freddi rivi l' Alpe porta* ‘over its cold streams the Alp bears’ → “the Alp casts over its cool streams”: subject and verb come before the prepositional phrase; English casts rather than bears a shadow, and “cool” softens ‘cold’.

> 112 Dinanzi ad esse Eufrates e Tigri  
> 113 veder mi parve uscir d' una fontana,  
> 114 e quasi amici dipartirsi pigri.  

In front of them I seemed to behold Euphrates and Tigris welling up from one spring, and parting like friends that linger.

- *Eufrates e Tigri veder mi parve* ‘Euphrates and Tigris to see it seemed to me’ → “I seemed to behold Euphrates and Tigris”: the experiencer becomes the subject of “seemed”; the infinitive follows it, and the names follow “behold” as its object.
- *uscir d' una fontana* ‘to issue from a spring’ → “welling up from one spring”: the infinitive after the verb of perception becomes an *-ing* form; “welling up” specifies the manner of issuing, and “one” stresses the singular.
- *e quasi amici dipartirsi pigri* ‘and like friends to part from one another slow’ → “and parting like friends that linger”: the reflexive infinitive becomes an *-ing* form and precedes the comparison; the predicative adjective becomes a relative clause describing the friends.

> 115 “O luce, o gloria della gente umana,  
> 116 che acqua è questa che qui si dispiega  
> 117 da un principio, e sè da sè lontana?”  

“O light, O glory of human kind, what water is this that here pours forth from one source, and self from self doth wend away?”

- *della gente umana* ‘of the race human’ → “of human kind”: the definite article drops out and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *si dispiega* ‘unfolds itself’ → “pours forth”: an intransitive expression specific to flowing water replaces the reflexive.
- *da un principio* ‘from a beginning’ → “from one source”: “source” makes the beginning spatial, and “one” emphasizes its singleness.
- *e sè da sè lontana* ‘and itself from itself distances’ → “and self from self doth wend away”: the repeated pronouns become bare “self”; instead of a verb acting on the first pronoun, English gives the first “self” a verb of motion of its own.

> 118 Per cotal prego detto mi fu: “Prega  
> 119 Matelda che il ti dica”; e qui rispose,  

At such prayer was said to me: “Pray Matilda that she tell it thee;” and here made answer,

- *Per cotal prego* ‘in response to such a prayer’ → “At such prayer”: “At” compresses the relation, and the indefinite article is omitted.
- *detto mi fu* ‘said to me was’ → “was said to me”: the auxiliary moves before the participle.
- *Prega Matelda che il ti dica* ‘pray Matilda that it to thee she tell’ → “Pray Matilda that she tell it thee”: the two objects precede the Italian verb as clitics but follow the English verb.
- *rispose* ‘answered’ → “made answer”: a verb becomes a verb–noun phrase.

> 120 come fa chi da colpa si dislega,  
> 121 la bella donna: “Questo, ed altre cose  
> 122 dette gli son per me; e son sicura  
> 123 che l' acqua di Letè non gliel nascose.”  

as he doth who frees him from blame, the fair Lady: “This and other things have been told him by me, and sure am I that Lethe’s water hid them not from him.”

- *come fa chi* ‘as does whoever’ → “as he doth who”: the single relative pronoun becomes an antecedent before the verb and a relative after it.
- *da colpa si dislega* ‘from blame unbinds himself’ → “frees him from blame”: the prepositional phrase moves after the verb and its object; the metaphor of unbinding becomes “frees”, with “him” used reflexively.
- *dette gli son per me* ‘told to him are by me’ → “have been told him by me”: the present passive becomes a present perfect passive, and the recipient follows the participle.
- *e son sicura* ‘and I am sure’ → “and sure am I”: the predicate adjective comes first, followed by verb and subject.
- *l' acqua di Letè* ‘the water of Lethe’ → “Lethe’s water”: a preceding possessive replaces the article and following *di*-phrase.
- *non gliel nascose* ‘not from him it hid’ → “hid them not from him”: the verb moves first, the combined pronouns are separated, and the singular object becomes plural, taking up the things told.

> 124 E Beatrice: “Forse maggior cura,  
> 125 che spesse volte la memoria priva,  
> 126 fatto ha la mente sua negli occhi oscura.  

And Beatrice: “Haply a greater care that oft bereaves of memory hath dimmed his mind’s eyes.

- *Forse maggior cura* ‘perhaps greater care’ → “Haply a greater care”: the indefinite article makes the care a countable concern.
- *spesse volte* ‘many times’ → “oft”: adjective and noun become an adverb.
- *la memoria priva* ‘memory takes away’ → “bereaves of memory”: the direct object preceding the verb becomes an *of*-complement following it.
- *fatto ha la mente sua negli occhi oscura* ‘made has his mind in the eyes dark’ → “hath dimmed his mind’s eyes”: the auxiliary comes first; ‘made … dark’ becomes one verb, and the mind, darkened in respect of its eyes, becomes the possessor of the eyes directly affected.

> 127 Ma vedi Eunoè che là deriva:  
> 128 menalo ad esso, e, come tu sei usa  
> 129 la tramortita sua virtù ravviva.”  

But behold Eunoe, which there flows on; lead him to it, and as thou art wont, requicken his fainting virtue.”

- *che là deriva* ‘which there flows’ → “which there flows on”: “on” gives the flow a continuing direction.
- *la tramortita sua virtù ravviva* ‘the stunned virtue of his revive’ → “requicken his fainting virtue”: the imperative moves before its object and the possessive before the adjective; “fainting” presents an ongoing weakening rather than the resulting state expressed by the past participle.

> 130 Com' anima gentil che non fa scusa,  
> 131 ma fa sua voglia della voglia altrui,  
> 132 tosto ch' ell' è per segno fuor dischiusa:  

As a gentle soul that maketh no excuse, but makes her will of the will of another, soon as it is disclosed by outward sign,

- *anima gentil* ‘a soul gentle’ → “a gentle soul”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *non fa scusa* ‘does not make excuse’ → “maketh no excuse”: the negative shifts from the verb to the noun.
- *fa sua voglia della voglia altrui* ‘makes her own will of another’s will’ → “makes her will of the will of another”: the possessive *altrui* is expanded into an *of*-phrase.
- *tosto ch'* ‘as soon as’ → “soon as”: English omits the initial ‘as’.
- *ell' è per segno fuor dischiusa* ‘it is by a sign outwardly disclosed’ → “it is disclosed by outward sign”: the participle moves next to the auxiliary, and the adverb of disclosure becomes an adjective qualifying the sign.

> 133 così, poi che da essa preso fui,  
> 134 la bella donna mossesi, ed a Stazio  
> 135 donnescamente disse: “Vien con lui.”  

so the fair Lady, after I was taken by her, set forth, and to Statius with queenly mien did say: “Come with him.”

- *poi che da essa preso fui* ‘after by her taken I was’ → “after I was taken by her”: the auxiliary precedes the participle and the agent follows it; the entire clause now follows the main subject instead of preceding it.
- *mossesi* ‘moved herself’ → “set forth”: an intransitive phrasal verb replaces the reflexive.
- *donnescamente disse* ‘in lady-like fashion said’ → “with queenly mien did say”: the adverb becomes a phrase describing bearing, with the stronger “queenly”; the simple past is expanded with “did”.

> 136 S' io avessi, lettor, più lungo spazio  
> 137 da scrivere, io pur canterei in parte  
> 138 lo dolce ber che mai non m' avria sazio;  

If, reader, I had greater space for writing, I would sing, at least in part, of the sweet draught which never would have sated me;

- *S' io avessi, lettor* ‘if I had, reader’ → “If, reader, I had”: the address moves between the conjunction and the subject.
- *più lungo spazio* ‘longer space’ → “greater space”: the comparative expresses extent rather than length.
- *da scrivere* ‘to write’ → “for writing”: the infinitive becomes a preposition and gerund.
- *io pur canterei in parte* ‘I would indeed sing in part’ → “I would sing, at least in part”: the force of *pur* is attached to the qualification “in part” rather than to the verb.
- *lo dolce ber* ‘the sweet drinking’ → “of the sweet draught”: the substantivized infinitive becomes the thing drunk, and the direct object becomes the topic sung “of”.
- *mai non m' avria sazio* ‘never me would have sated’ → “never would have sated me”: English uses one negative for Italian negative concord and places the object after the participle.

> 139 ma perchè piene son tutte le carte  
> 140 ordite a questa Cantica seconda,  
> 141 non mi lascia più ir lo fren dell' arte.  

but forasmuch as all the pages ordained for this second canticle are filled, the curb of art no further lets me go.

- *perchè* ‘because’ → “forasmuch as”: a longer causal connective replaces the conjunction.
- *piene son tutte le carte* ‘full are all the sheets’ → “all the pages ordained for this second canticle are filled”: the subject, together with its modifier, precedes the predicate; “pages” renders the sheets as writing space, and “filled” renders the adjective of state by a participle.
- *ordite a questa Cantica seconda* ‘laid out for this canticle second’ → “ordained for this second canticle”: the image of laying out or planning becomes one of designation, and the ordinal precedes the noun.
- *non mi lascia più ir lo fren dell' arte* ‘does not let me farther go the rein of art’ → “the curb of art no further lets me go”: the subject moves first, “curb” replaces ‘rein’, the negative and comparative are brought together before the verb, and the object follows “lets”.

## Lines 142–145 — Renewed and Ready to Ascend

> 142 Io ritornai dalla santissim' onda  

I came back from the most holy waves,

- *ritornai* ‘returned’ → “came back”: a verb and an adverb replace the single verb.
- *onda* ‘wave’ → “waves”: the singular becomes plural.

> 143 rifatto sì, come piante novelle  
> 144 rinnovellate di novella fronda,  

born again, even as new trees renewed with new foliage,

- *rifatto* ‘remade’ → “born again”: Okey uses a phrase of rebirth rather than a participle of making.
- *sì, come* ‘so, as’ → “even as”: instead of qualifying the preceding participle with ‘so’, Okey uses “even” to emphasize the comparison.
- *piante novelle* ‘plants new’ → “new trees”: the noun is made more specific, and the adjective moves before it.
- *di* ‘of’ → “with”: English uses “with” to introduce the foliage with which the trees are renewed.

> 145 puro e disposto a salire alle stelle.  

pure and ready to mount to the stars.

- *disposto* ‘disposed’ → “ready”: Okey uses an adjective of readiness rather than the cognate participle.

## Summary — Italian Order and English Idiom

Okey often brings forward a subject that Dante holds back, places a verb before its object, or moves a modifier closer to the word it qualifies. In *segnato è or da voi lo mio cervello*, the subject comes last and the participle first; in “my brain is now stamped by you” their positions are reversed. Yet Okey does not make every sequence idiomatic. In “with her eyes mine eyes she smote” he keeps both the repeated noun and the delayed verb of *con gli occhi gli occhi mi percosse*, even as he makes both possessors explicit.

Changes of construction can also narrow or shift the sense. The gerund *solo accennando* (‘only making a sign’) becomes “merely by her nod”: the action becomes a means, and the gesture is specified. The impersonal *non mi ricorda* (‘it does not come to mind for me’) becomes the personal “I remember not”. At times the change lies in a single noun: *narrazion* (‘account’) becomes “prophecy”, while *ai denti* (‘to the teeth’) becomes “to the lips”.

Okey regularly supplies subject pronouns where the Italian verb ending suffices and leaves out the Italian article before a possessive, as in *le mie parole*, “my words”. Other additions give a detail that Dante leaves implicit: “the pilgrim’s staff” identifies the bearer of *il bordon*, and “brought back” specifies the direction of *si reca*. Yet Okey leaves the Latin phrases unchanged, and in “new trees renewed with new foliage” he preserves the verbal kinship of *novelle*, *rinnovellate*, and *novella*. Even where the syntax changes, the pattern of the Italian words can remain distinct.
