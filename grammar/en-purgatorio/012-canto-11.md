# Purgatorio 11

## Lines 1–12 — A Prayer to Our Father

> 1 “O Padre nostro, che nei cieli stai,  
> 2 non circonscritto, ma per più amore  
> 3 che ai primi effetti di lassù tu hai,  

“O our Father who art in heaven, not circumscribed, but through the greater love thou hast for thy first works on high,

- *Padre nostro* ‘Father our’ → “our Father”: the possessive comes before the noun.
- *che nei cieli stai* ‘who in the heavens dwellest’ → “who art in heaven”: the verb comes before the phrase of place; dwelling is expressed as being, and the definite plural becomes a singular without an article.
- *per più amore* ‘through more love’ → “through the greater love”: the comparison is expressed by “greater” with a definite article.
- *che ai primi effetti di lassù tu hai* ‘that for the first effects up there thou hast’ → “thou hast for thy first works on high”: the object relative is left unexpressed, and the phrase naming the objects of love follows the verb. “Thy” replaces the definite article, “works” renders ‘effects’ as things made, and “on high” renders the expression of place.

> 4 laudato sia il tuo nome e il tuo valore  
> 5 da ogni creatura, com' è degno  
> 6 di render grazie al tuo dolce vapore.  

praised be thy name and thy worth by every creature, as ’tis meet to give thanks to thy sweet effluence.

- *com' è degno di render grazie* ‘as it is fitting to render thanks’ → “as ’tis meet to give thanks”: English uses a dummy subject, takes the infinitive directly after “meet” without a counterpart to *di*, and uses “give” for ‘render’.
- *dolce vapore* ‘sweet vapour’ → “sweet effluence”: “effluence” expresses something flowing forth rather than using the literal noun ‘vapour’.

> 7 Vegna ver noi la pace del tuo regno  

May the peace of thy kingdom come upon us,

- *Vegna ver noi la pace del tuo regno* ‘come toward us the peace of thy kingdom’ → “May the peace of thy kingdom come upon us”: the subjunctive of wish becomes “May” with an infinitive; the subject stands between them instead of after the phrase of direction, and “upon” replaces ‘toward’.

> 8 chè noi ad essa non potem da noi,  
> 9 s' ella non vien, con tutto nostro ingegno.  

for we cannot of ourselves attain to it with all our wit, if it come not.

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the subordinating conjunction becomes a coordinating one.
- *noi ad essa non potem da noi* ‘we to it cannot of ourselves’ → “we cannot of ourselves attain to it”: the phrase ‘to it’ moves after “of ourselves”, and “attain” supplies the infinitive left understood with the Italian verb of ability.
- *s' ella non vien, con tutto nostro ingegno* ‘if it does not come, with all our ingenuity’ → “with all our wit, if it come not”: the condition moves from before the phrase of means to the end; the Italian present indicative becomes an English subjunctive, with the negative after the verb. “wit” renders the mental capacity denoted by *ingegno*.

> 10 Come del suo voler gli angeli tuoi  
> 11 fan sacrificio a te, cantando *Osanna*,  
> 12 così facciano gli uomini de' suoi.  

As of their will thine angels make sacrifice to thee, singing *Hosanna*, so may men make of theirs.

- *del suo voler* ‘of their willing’ → “of their will”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun.
- *gli angeli tuoi* ‘the angels thine’ → “thine angels”: the possessive moves before the noun, displacing the article.
- *così facciano gli uomini* ‘so let men make’ → “so may men make”: the subjunctive of wish becomes “may” with an infinitive; the subject moves before “make”, and the generic plural loses its article.

## Lines 13–45 — Daily Manna, Mutual Prayer, and the Way Up

> 13 Dà oggi a noi la cotidiana manna,  

Give us this day our daily manna,

- *Dà oggi a noi* ‘give today to us’ → “Give us this day”: the recipient comes immediately after the verb, without a preposition, and the adverb becomes a noun phrase.
- *la cotidiana manna* ‘the daily manna’ → “our daily manna”: a possessive replaces the definite article.

> 14 senza la qual per questo aspro diserto  
> 15 a retro va chi più di gir s' affanna.  

without which he backward goes through this rough desert, who most toileth to advance.

- *per questo aspro diserto / a retro va* ‘through this rough desert backward goes’ → “backward goes through this rough desert”: the phrase of passage comes after the verb.
- *chi più di gir s' affanna* ‘whoever most to go strives’ → “he” … “who most toileth to advance”: the single relative is divided into an antecedent before the main verb and a relative after it; the reflexive verb becomes intransitive, and the infinitive follows it, with ‘go’ made more specifically “advance”.

> 16 E come noi lo mal che avem sofferto  
> 17 perdoniamo a ciascuno, e tu perdona  
> 18 benigno, e non guardare al nostro merto.  

And as we forgive every one the evil we have suffered, do thou forgive in loving-kindness and regard not our desert.

- *lo mal che avem sofferto / perdoniamo a ciascuno* ‘the evil that we have suffered we forgive to each one’ → “forgive every one the evil we have suffered”: the verb and recipient precede the thing forgiven; the recipient loses its preposition, and the object relative is left unexpressed.
- *e tu perdona / benigno* ‘and thou forgive, gracious’ → “do thou forgive in loving-kindness”: the conjunction that resumes the comparison is dropped, the imperative gains an emphatic auxiliary, and the adjective describing the one addressed becomes a phrase of manner.
- *non guardare al nostro merto* ‘do not look to our merit’ → “regard not our desert”: a transitive verb replaces the verb with a preposition, the negative follows it, and “desert” expresses what is deserved.

> 19 Nostra virtù che di leggier s' adona,  
> 20 non spermentar con l' antico avversaro,  
> 21 ma libera da lui, che sì la sprona.  

Put not our virtue, which lightly is subdued, to trial with the old adversary, but deliver us from him who so pricks it.

- *Nostra virtù che di leggier s' adona, / non spermentar* ‘our virtue that readily gives way, do not test’ → “Put not our virtue, which lightly is subdued, to trial”: the prohibition comes first, with the object between the verb and the noun “trial”; the reflexive verb of yielding is rendered as a passive.
- *libera da lui* ‘deliver from him’ → “deliver us from him”: the object, unexpressed in Italian, is supplied.
- *che sì la sprona* ‘who so spurs it’ → “who so pricks it”: the verb expresses the spur’s action as a prick, and the object pronoun follows it.

> 22 Quest' ultima preghiera, Signor caro,  
> 23 già non si fa per noi, chè non bisogna,  
> 24 ma per color, che retro a noi restaro.”  

This last prayer, dear Lord, is not made for us, for need is not, but for those who have remained behind us.”

- *Signor caro* ‘Lord dear’ → “dear Lord”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *già non si fa* ‘indeed is not made’ → “is not made”: the reinforcing adverb is dropped, and the passive construction with *si* becomes an English passive with ‘be’.
- *chè non bisogna* ‘for there is no need’ → “for need is not”: the impersonal verb becomes a noun with a negated verb of being.
- *che retro a noi restaro* ‘who behind us remained’ → “who have remained behind us”: the simple past becomes a present perfect, and the phrase of place follows the verb.

> 25 Così a sè e noi buona ramogna  
> 26 quell' ombre orando, andavan sotto il pondo,  
> 27 simile a quel che talvolta si sogna,  

Thus those shades, praying good speed for them and for us, were going under their burden, like that whereof we sometimes dream,

- *a sè e noi buona ramogna / quell' ombre orando* ‘for themselves and us good speed those shades praying’ → “those shades, praying good speed for them and for us”: the subject comes first, followed by the participle, its object, and the beneficiaries; the reflexive becomes a personal pronoun, and the preposition is repeated.
- *andavan sotto il pondo* ‘went under the burden’ → “were going under their burden”: the progressive brings out the imperfect, and a possessive replaces the article.
- *simile a quel che talvolta si sogna* ‘like that which one sometimes dreams’ → “like that whereof we sometimes dream”: dreaming takes a prepositional relative rather than a direct object, and impersonal *si* becomes an inclusive first-person plural.

> 28 disparmente angosciate tutte a tondo,  
> 29 e lasse su per la prima cornice,  
> 30 purgando le caligini del mondo.  

unequal all in anguish around and weary, along the first cornice, purging away the foul mists of the world.

- *disparmente angosciate tutte* ‘unequally anguished all’ → “unequal all in anguish”: the adverb becomes an adjective, the participle becomes a prepositional phrase, and “all” stands between them.
- *a tondo* ‘in a circle’ → “around”: one adverb replaces the phrase.
- *su per la prima cornice* ‘up along the first cornice’ → “along the first cornice”: the separate indication of position or direction in *su* is not expressed.
- *purgando le caligini* ‘purging the mists’ → “purging away the foul mists”: “away” makes removal explicit, and “foul” qualifies the mists without a separate Italian adjective.

> 31 Se di là sempre ben per noi si dice,  
> 32 di qua che dire e far per lor si puote  
> 33 da quei ch' hanno al voler buona radice?  

If there ever a good word for us is said, what can be said and done for them here, by those who have their will rooted in good?

- *di là* ‘over there’ → “there”: the expression of place is reduced to a single adverb.
- *sempre ben per noi si dice* ‘always well for us one speaks’ → “ever a good word for us is said”: “ever” has the sense of ‘always’; the adverb of speaking becomes a noun phrase, which is the subject of a passive.
- *di qua che dire e far per lor si puote* ‘over here what to say and do for them is possible’ → “what can be said and done for them here”: the indication of place moves to the end; the impersonal modal construction with active infinitives becomes a modal with passive infinitives.
- *ch' hanno al voler buona radice* ‘who have for their will a good root’ → “who have their will rooted in good”: the will becomes the object of “have”, the noun for the root becomes a participle, and its adjective becomes the substantive “good” after a preposition.

> 34 Ben si dee loro aitar lavar le note,  
> 35 che portar quinci, sì che mondi e lievi  
> 36 possano uscire alle stellate rote.  

Truly we ought to help them to wash away their stains, which they have borne hence, so that pure and light they may go forth to the starry spheres.

- *si dee loro aitar lavar* ‘one ought them to help wash’ → “we ought to help them to wash away”: the impersonal construction becomes first-person plural, the object pronoun follows “help”, and the second infinitive gains “to” and the particle “away”.
- *le note* ‘the marks’ → “their stains”: the definite article becomes a possessive, and the marks are specified as stains.
- *che portar quinci* ‘which they bore hence’ → “which they have borne hence”: the simple past becomes a present perfect.
- *possano uscire* ‘they may go out’ → “they may go forth”: “forth” gives an onward turn to the verb of exit.
- *stellate rote* ‘starred wheels’ → “starry spheres”: the image of wheels is rendered as spheres, and the participial adjective becomes “starry”.

> 37 “Deh! se giustizia e pietà vi disgrevi  
> 38 tosto, sì che possiate mover l' ala,  
> 39 che secondo il disio vostro vi levi,  

“Pray!—so may justice and pity soon unload you, that ye may spread the wing which may uplift you according to your desire,—

- *Deh!* ‘ah!’ → “Pray!”: a verb of entreaty serves for the interjection.
- *se giustizia e pietà vi disgrevi / tosto* ‘if justice and pity may unburden you soon’ → “so may justice and pity soon unload you”: the conditional form of the wish becomes an English optative; the adverb precedes the main verb rather than following it, and the object pronoun follows the verb.
- *mover l' ala* ‘move the wing’ → “spread the wing”: the general verb of motion becomes a particular wing movement.
- *che secondo il disio vostro vi levi* ‘which according to the desire of yours may lift you’ → “which may uplift you according to your desire”: the phrase of accordance follows the verb, the possessive precedes its noun, and the object pronoun follows the verb.

> 40 mostrate da qual mano in ver la scala  
> 41 si va più corto; e se c' è più d' un varco,  
> 42 quel ne insegnate che men erto cala:  

show us on which hand we go quickest towards the stairway; and if more than one passage there be, tell us that which least steeply ascends;

- *mostrate* ‘show’ → “show us”: the recipient is made explicit.
- *da qual mano* ‘by which hand’ → “on which hand”: English changes the preposition in the expression of direction.
- *in ver la scala / si va più corto* ‘towards the stairway one goes by a shorter way’ → “we go quickest towards the stairway”: the destination follows the verb, impersonal *si* becomes “we”, and comparative shortness of route becomes superlative quickness.
- *se c' è più d' un varco* ‘if there is more than one passage’ → “if more than one passage there be”: the quantified noun phrase comes first, and indicative ‘is’ becomes subjunctive “be”.
- *quel ne insegnate* ‘that one to us point out’ → “tell us that”: the demonstrative follows the verb and recipient, and pointing out a way is expressed as telling.
- *che men erto cala* ‘which less steep comes down’ → “which least steeply ascends”: the comparative becomes a superlative, the adjective becomes an adverb, and the slope is described in the direction of ascent rather than descent.

> 43 chè questi che vien meco, per l' incarco  
> 44 della carne d' Adamo ond' ei si veste,  
> 45 al montar su, contra sua voglia, è parco.  

for he who cometh with me, because of the weight of Adam’s flesh wherewith he is clad, at climbing up is slow against his will.”

- *questi che* ‘this one who’ → “he who”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *per l' incarco* ‘for the burden’ → “because of the weight”: the causal force of the preposition is made explicit, and the burden is expressed as weight.
- *della carne d' Adamo* ‘of the flesh of Adam’ → “of Adam’s flesh”: the possessor precedes the noun in the English genitive.
- *ond' ei si veste* ‘with which he clothes himself’ → “wherewith he is clad”: the reflexive action becomes a description of his clothed state.
- *contra sua voglia, è parco* ‘against his will, is sparing’ → “is slow against his will”: the verb and adjective precede the phrase of unwillingness, and being sparing in climbing is rendered as being slow at it.

## Lines 46–78 — Omberto Confesses His Pride

> 46 Le lor parole, che rendero a queste,  
> 47 che dette avea colui cu' io seguiva,  
> 48 non fur da cui venisser manifeste;  

From whom came the words which were returned to those which he whom I was following had said, was not manifest,

- *Le lor parole* ‘their words’ → “the words”: the possessive is omitted, and the noun phrase is moved after “came”.
- *che rendero* ‘which they returned’ → “which were returned”: the active becomes passive.
- *a queste* ‘to these’ → “to those”: the near demonstrative becomes a far demonstrative.
- *che dette avea colui cu' io seguiva* ‘which had said he whom I followed’ → “which he whom I was following had said”: the subject and its relative clause precede the verb instead of following it; the auxiliary precedes the participle, and the imperfect is rendered by a progressive.
- *non fur da cui venisser manifeste* ‘were not, from whom they came, manifest’ → “From whom came” … “was not manifest”: in Italian the words are the plural subject of ‘were not manifest’; in English the question of their source becomes the subject of singular “was not manifest”, with “the words” made the subject of “came”. The imperfect subjunctive of the indirect question becomes a simple past.

> 49 ma fu detto: “A man destra per la riva  
> 50 con noi venite, e troverete il passo  
> 51 possibile a salir persona viva.  

but it was said: “To the right hand along the bank come with us, and ye shall find the pass possible for a living person to ascend.

- *A man destra* ‘to hand right’ → “To the right hand”: the article is added and the adjective precedes the noun.
- *con noi venite* ‘with us come’ → “come with us”: the imperative precedes the prepositional phrase.
- *a salir persona viva* ‘for a person living to ascend’ → “for a living person to ascend”: Italian places the subject after the infinitive and the adjective after the noun; English puts the subject before the infinitive, introduces it with “for”, and puts the adjective first.

> 52 E s' io non fossi impedito dal sasso,  
> 53 che la cervice mia superba doma,  
> 54 onde portar convienmi il viso basso,  

And if I were not impeded by the stone which subdues my proud neck, wherefore needs must I carry my visage low,

- *che la cervice mia superba doma* ‘which the neck of mine, proud, subdues’ → “which subdues my proud neck”: the verb moves before its object, and both possessive and adjective move before the noun.
- *portar convienmi* ‘to carry it behoves me’ → “needs must I carry”: the impersonal verb with its attached personal pronoun becomes a personal modal construction, with “carry” after “must I”.

> 55 cotesti che ancor vive, e non si noma,  
> 56 guardere' io, per veder s' io 'l conosco,  
> 57 e per farlo pietoso a questa soma.  

him who is yet alive, and names not himself, would I look at, to see if I know him, and to make him pitiful to this burden.

- *cotesti* ‘that man there’ → “him”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *ancor vive* ‘still lives’ → “is yet alive”: the verb of living becomes a copula and adjective, with the adverb between them.
- *non si noma* ‘does not name himself’ → “names not himself”: the verb precedes both negation and reflexive instead of following them.
- *guardere' io* ‘would look at, I’ → “would I look at”: the conditional ending is rendered by an auxiliary, with the subject between auxiliary and main verb.
- *'l conosco* ‘him I know’ → “I know him”: the object pronoun follows the verb.

> 58 Io fui Latino, e nato d' un gran Tosco:  
> 59 Guglielmo Aldobrandesco fu mio padre:  
> 60 non so se il nome suo giammai fu vosco.  

I was Italian and son of a great Tuscan: Guglielmo Aldobrandesco was my father; I know not if his name was ever with you.

- *Latino* ‘Latin’ → “Italian”: the geographical sense is given rather than the cognate.
- *nato d' un gran Tosco* ‘born of a great Tuscan’ → “son of a great Tuscan”: a noun of kinship replaces the participle.
- *non so* ‘I do not know’ → “I know not”: the negative follows the verb.
- *il nome suo giammai fu vosco* ‘the name of his ever was with you’ → “his name was ever with you”: the possessive precedes the noun, and the adverb follows the verb.

> 61 L' antico sangue e l' opere leggiadre  
> 62 de' miei maggior mi fer sì arrogante,  
> 63 che, non pensando alla comune madre,  

The ancient blood and gallant deeds of my ancestors made me so insolent that, thinking not of our common mother,

- *l' opere leggiadre* ‘the deeds gallant’ → “gallant deeds”: the repeated article is dropped and the adjective put first.
- *miei maggior* ‘my elders’ → “my ancestors”: the substantive comparative is rendered by its genealogical sense.
- *mi fer sì arrogante* ‘me made so arrogant’ → “made me so insolent”: the object follows the verb, and “insolent” replaces ‘arrogant’.
- *non pensando alla comune madre* ‘not thinking of the common mother’ → “thinking not of our common mother”: the negative follows the participle, and a possessive replaces the definite article.

> 64 ogni uomo ebbi in dispetto tanto avante  
> 65 ch' io ne mori', come i Sanesi sanno,  
> 66 e sallo in Campagnatico ogni fante.  

all men I held in such exceeding scorn that it was the death of me, as the Sienese know, and every child knows in Campagnatico.

- *ogni uomo* ‘every man’ → “all men”: the distributive singular becomes a collective plural.
- *ebbi in dispetto tanto avante* ‘I held in scorn to such an extent’ → “I held in such exceeding scorn”: the expression of degree after the noun becomes a modifier of “scorn”.
- *ch' io ne mori'* ‘that I died of it’ → “that it was the death of me”: the cause becomes the subject, and a noun with a copula replaces the verb of dying.
- *sallo in Campagnatico ogni fante* ‘knows it in Campagnatico every child’ → “every child knows in Campagnatico”: the attached object pronoun is dropped, and the subject moves from the end to the beginning.

> 67 Io sono Omberto: e non pure a me danno  
> 68 superbia fa, chè tutti i miei consorti  
> 69 ha ella tratti seco nel malanno.  

I am Humbert; and not to me alone pride works ill, for all my fellows hath it dragged with it to mishap.

- *non pure a me* ‘not only to me’ → “not to me alone”: the restrictive adverb moves after the pronoun.
- *danno superbia fa* ‘harm pride does’ → “pride works ill”: object–subject–verb becomes subject–verb–object, with ‘does harm’ rendered as “works ill”.
- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the causal link is made with a coordinating conjunction.
- *consorti* ‘kinsmen’ → “fellows”: the English term for associates does not specify kinship.
- *ha ella tratti* ‘has she dragged’ → “hath it dragged”: the feminine pronoun referring to pride becomes neuter.
- *seco* ‘with itself’ → “with it”: the reflexive is rendered by a personal pronoun.
- *nel malanno* ‘into calamity’ → “to mishap”: the preposition of entry becomes “to”, and ‘calamity’ is rendered by “mishap”.

> 70 E qui convien ch' io questo peso porti  
> 71 per lei, tanto che a Dio si satisfaccia,  
> 72 poi ch' io nol fei tra' vivi, qui tra' morti.”  

And here must I therefore bear this load among the dead, until God be satisfied, since I did it not among the living.”

- *convien ch' io questo peso porti* ‘it is necessary that I this weight bear’ → “must I” … “bear this load”: the impersonal main verb and subjunctive clause become a modal and infinitive; the object follows “bear”.
- *per lei* ‘on account of her’ → “therefore”: an adverb replaces the phrase referring back to pride and moves before “bear”.
- *tanto che* ‘so long that’ → “until”: the duration and its endpoint are expressed by one conjunction.
- *a Dio si satisfaccia* ‘satisfaction be made to God’ → “God be satisfied”: the recipient of satisfaction becomes the subject of a personal passive.
- *io nol fei* ‘I did not do it’ → “I did it not”: the object and negative both follow the verb, in the opposite order.
- *qui tra' morti* ‘here among the dead’ → “among the dead”: the repeated ‘here’ is omitted, and the phrase is brought forward to follow “this load”.

> 73 Ascoltando, chinai in giù la faccia;  

Listening I bent down my face;

- *chinai in giù la faccia* ‘I bent down the face’ → “I bent down my face”: English uses a possessive where Italian uses the definite article with a part of the body.

> 74 ed un di lor, non questi che parlava,  
> 75 si torse sotto il peso che lo impaccia;  

and one of them, not he who was speaking, twisted himself beneath the weight which encumbers him;

- *non questi che parlava* ‘not this one who spoke’ → “not he who was speaking”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative, and a progressive renders the imperfect.
- *si torse* ‘himself he twisted’ → “twisted himself”: the reflexive follows the verb.
- *lo impaccia* ‘him encumbers’ → “encumbers him”: the object pronoun follows the verb.

> 76 e videmi e conobbemi e chiamava,  
> 77 tenendo gli occhi con fatica fisi  
> 78 a me, che tutto chin con loro andava.  

and saw me and knew me and was calling out, keeping his eyes with difficulty fixed upon me, who all bent was going with them.

- *chiamava* ‘called’ → “was calling out”: the progressive marks the imperfect, and “out” is supplied.
- *gli occhi* ‘the eyes’ → “his eyes”: the possessive replaces the definite article.
- *fisi a me* ‘fixed on me’ → “fixed upon me”: “upon” renders the preposition linking the gaze to its object.
- *con loro andava* ‘with them I went’ → “was going with them”: a progressive renders the imperfect, and the phrase of accompaniment follows the verb.

## Lines 79–111 — Oderisi on Fleeting Fame

> 79 “O,” dissi lui, “non sei tu Oderisi,  
> 80 l' onor d' Agobbio, e l' onor di quell' arte  
> 81 che ‘alluminare’ è chiamata in Parisi?”  

“Oh,” said I to him, “art thou not Oderisi, the honour of Gubbio, and the honour of that art which in Paris is called ‘illuminating’?”

- *non sei tu* ‘not art thou’ → “art thou not”: the negative moves after the subject in the English question.
- *‘alluminare’ è chiamata in Parisi* ‘to illuminate is called in Paris’ → “in Paris is called ‘illuminating’”: the place moves before the verb, the name of the art after it; the infinitive becomes an *-ing* form.

> 82 “Frate,” diss' egli, “più ridon le carte  
> 83 che pennelleggia Franco Bolognese:  
> 84 l' onore è tutto or suo, e mio in parte.  

“Brother,” said he, “more pleasing are the leaves which Franco Bolognese paints; the honour now is all his and mine in part.

- *più ridon le carte* ‘more smile the leaves’ → “more pleasing are the leaves”: the leaves’ figurative action becomes a quality attributed to them.
- *pennelleggia Franco Bolognese* ‘paints with the brush Franco Bolognese’ → “Franco Bolognese paints”: the subject moves before the verb, which no longer specifies the brush.
- *è tutto or suo* ‘is all now his’ → “now is all his”: the adverb of time moves before the verb.

> 85 Ben non sare' io stato sì cortese  
> 86 mentre ch' io vissi, per lo gran disio  
> 87 dell' eccellenza, ove mio core intese.  

Truly I should not have been so courteous while I lived, because of the great desire of excelling whereon my heart was bent.

- *Ben non sare' io stato* ‘indeed not should I have been’ → “Truly I should not have been”: the subject moves before the auxiliary and the negative follows it.
- *dell' eccellenza* ‘of excellence’ → “of excelling”: the noun becomes a verbal form.
- *ove mio core intese* ‘whither my heart directed itself’ → “whereon my heart was bent”: direction towards an object becomes fixed attention upon it; an active past verb becomes an expression of state.

> 88 Di tal superbia qui si paga il fio;  
> 89 ed ancor non sarei qui, se non fosse,  
> 90 che, possendo peccar, mi volsi a Dio.  

For such pride here the fine is paid; and I should not yet be here, were it not that having power to sin, I turned me to God.

- *Di tal superbia* ‘of such pride’ → “For such pride”: English marks what the penalty is paid for rather than what it is the penalty of.
- *si paga il fio* ‘is paid the penalty’ → “the fine is paid”: the Italian *si* construction becomes an English passive with its subject first; “fine” gives the penalty a monetary sense.
- *ancor non sarei qui* ‘yet not should I be here’ → “I should not yet be here”: the negative and the adverb move between the auxiliary and the infinitive.
- *se non fosse, che* ‘if it were not that’ → “were it not that”: inversion replaces the conditional conjunction.
- *possendo peccar* ‘being able to sin’ → “having power to sin”: ability expressed by a verb becomes the object of “having”.

> 91 O vanagloria dell' umane posse,  
> 92 com' poco verde in su la cima dura,  
> 93 se non è giunta dall' etati grosse!  

O empty glory of human powers! How short the time its green endures upon the top, if it be not overtaken by rude ages!

- *vanagloria* ‘vainglory’ → “empty glory”: the single noun is unfolded into an adjective and a noun.
- *dell' umane posse* ‘of the human powers’ → “of human powers”: the definite article drops out in the English generalization.
- *com' poco verde in su la cima dura* ‘how little it lasts green upon the summit’ → “How short the time its green endures upon the top”: “the time” makes the duration explicit; the adjective describing the glory becomes the noun “green”, with “its” supplied. The verb moves before the phrase of place.
- *dall' etati grosse* ‘by the coarse ages’ → “by rude ages”: “rude” takes the adjective in the sense of uncultivated; it precedes the noun, and the article drops out.

> 94 Credette Cimabue nella pittura  
> 95 tener lo campo, ed ora ha Giotto il grido,  
> 96 sì che la fama di colui è oscura.  

Cimabue thought to hold the field in painting, and now Giotto hath the cry, so that the fame of the other is obscured.

- *Credette Cimabue nella pittura tener lo campo* ‘thought Cimabue in painting to hold the field’ → “Cimabue thought to hold the field in painting”: the subject moves before the finite verb and the phrase specifying the art moves after the object of the infinitive.
- *ha Giotto* ‘hath Giotto’ → “Giotto hath”: the subject precedes the verb.
- *di colui* ‘of that man’ → “of the other”: the demonstrative becomes a term distinguishing one of the two painters from the other.
- *è oscura* ‘is obscure’ → “is obscured”: a predicate adjective becomes a passive participle.

> 97 Così ha tolto l' uno all' altro Guido  
> 98 la gloria della lingua; e forse è nato  
> 99 chi l' uno e l' altro caccerà di nido.  

Even so one Guido hath taken from the other the glory of our tongue; and perchance one is born who shall chase both from the nest.

- *Così* ‘so’ → “Even so”: “Even” reinforces the comparison.
- *ha tolto l' uno all' altro Guido* ‘has taken the one from the other Guido’ → “one Guido hath taken from the other”: the subject precedes the verb, “Guido” is attached explicitly to “one”, and the first article drops out.
- *della lingua* ‘of the tongue’ → “of our tongue”: a possessive replaces the definite article.
- *è nato chi* ‘is born he who’ → “one is born who”: the English supplies a separate antecedent for the relative and places it before the verb.
- *l' uno e l' altro caccerà di nido* ‘the one and the other will drive out of nest’ → “shall chase both from the nest”: the paired object is compressed into “both” and follows the verb; English supplies an article before “nest”.

> 100 Non è il mondan romore altro che un fiato  
> 101 di vento, che or vien quinci ed or vien quindi,  
> 102 e muta nome, perchè muta lato.  

Earthly fame is naught but a breath of wind, which now cometh hence and now thence, and changes name because it changes direction.

- *Non è il mondan romore altro che* ‘is not the worldly noise other than’ → “Earthly fame is naught but”: the subject comes first, without the article; “fame” gives the sense of the figurative noise, and “naught but” recasts the negative comparison.
- *or vien quindi* ‘now comes thence’ → “now thence”: the repeated verb is omitted.
- *lato* ‘side’ → “direction”: English names the wind’s direction rather than the side from which it comes.

> 103 Che fama avrai tu più, se vecchia scindi  
> 104 da te la carne, che se fossi morto  
> 105 innanzi che lasciassi il pappo e il dindi,  

What greater fame shalt thou have, if thou strip thee of thy flesh when old, than if thou hadst died ere thou wert done with pap and chink,

- *Che fama avrai tu più* ‘what fame shalt thou have more’ → “What greater fame shalt thou have”: the comparative moves forward and becomes an adjective modifying “fame”.
- *se vecchia scindi da te la carne* ‘if old thou sever from thyself the flesh’ → “if thou strip thee of thy flesh when old”: the flesh, the Italian direct object, becomes the object of “of”, and the person becomes the direct object. “Thy” replaces the article; “when old” moves to the end and applies to the person, whereas *vecchia* agrees with *carne*.
- *innanzi che lasciassi* ‘before thou shouldst leave’ → “ere thou wert done with”: the act of leaving is recast as being finished with something.
- *il pappo e il dindi* ‘the food and the money’ → “pap and chink”: the articles drop out; “pap” and the sound-word “chink” answer to the Italian nursery words.

> 106 pria che passin mill' anni? ch' è più corto  
> 107 spazio all' eterno, che un mover di ciglia  
> 108 al cerchio che più tardi in cielo è torto.  

before a thousand years are passed? which is shorter space to eternity than the twinkling of an eye to the circle which slowest is turned in heaven.

- *pria che passin mill' anni* ‘before there pass a thousand years’ → “before a thousand years are passed”: the subject moves before the verb, and the Italian present subjunctive becomes a construction expressing completed passage.
- *più corto spazio all' eterno* ‘shorter space in relation to the eternal’ → “shorter space to eternity”: the substantivized adjective becomes an abstract noun.
- *un mover di ciglia* ‘a moving of eyelashes’ → “the twinkling of an eye”: the idiom changes the movement and substitutes one eye for plural lashes; the indefinite article becomes definite.
- *più tardi in cielo è torto* ‘more slowly in heaven is turned’ → “slowest is turned in heaven”: the comparative is taken as a superlative, and the phrase of place moves after the verb.

> 109 Colui, che del cammin sì poco piglia  
> 110 dinanzi a me, Toscana sonò tutta,  
> 111 ed ora a pena in Siena sen pispiglia,  

All Tuscany rang with the sound of him who moves so slowly along the way in front of me, and now hardly is a whisper of him in Siena,

- *Colui, che del cammin sì poco piglia dinanzi a me, Toscana sonò tutta* ‘he who takes so little of the way before me made all Tuscany resound’ → “All Tuscany rang with the sound of him who moves so slowly along the way in front of me”: Tuscany moves to the front as the subject of an intransitive verb; the man is introduced through the supplied phrase “with the sound of”. The small amount of ground he covers becomes his slow rate of movement.
- *a pena in Siena sen pispiglia* ‘scarcely in Siena does one whisper of him’ → “hardly is a whisper of him in Siena”: the impersonal verb becomes the noun “a whisper” in an existential construction, and the place moves to the end.

## Lines 112–142 — Fleeting Fame and Provenzan’s Humility

> 112 ond' era sire, quando fu distrutta  
> 113 la rabbia fiorentina, che superba  
> 114 fu a quel tempo, sì com' ora è putta.  

whereof he was lord, when the rage of Florence was destroyed who at that time was proud even as now she is degraded.

- *fu distrutta la rabbia fiorentina* ‘was destroyed the Florentine rage’ → “the rage of Florence was destroyed”: the subject moves before the passive verb, and the adjective becomes an “of” phrase.
- *che superba fu a quel tempo* ‘which proud was at that time’ → “who at that time was proud”: “who” attaches the relative clause to Florence herself rather than to her rage; the time phrase moves before the verb, and the predicate adjective follows it.
- *è putta* ‘is a whore’ → “she is degraded”: the insulting noun becomes a less specific description of Florence’s condition.

> 115 La vostra nominanza è color d' erba,  
> 116 che viene e va, e quei la discolora,  
> 117 per cui ell' esce della terra acerba.”  

Your repute is as the hue of grass which cometh and goeth, and he discolours it through whom it springeth green from the ground.”

- *è color d’ erba* ‘is the hue of grass’ → “is as the hue of grass”: “as” makes the identification a comparison.
- *e quei la discolora* ‘and that one it discolours’ → “and he discolours it”: the demonstrative becomes a personal pronoun, and the object moves after the verb.
- *per cui ell’ esce della terra acerba* ‘through whom it comes forth from the earth unripe’ → “through whom it springeth green from the ground”: “springeth” gives the verb a sense of sprouting; “green” takes *acerba* as describing the emerging grass and moves it before the phrase of origin.

> 118 Ed io a lui: “Lo tuo ver dir m' incora  
> 119 buona umiltà, e gran tumor m' appiani;  
> 120 ma chi è quei di cui tu parlavi ora?”  

And I to him: “Thy true saying fills my heart with holy humility, and lowers my swollen pride, but who is he of whom but now thou wast speaking?”

- *Lo tuo ver dir* ‘thy true speaking’ → “Thy true saying”: the substantivized infinitive becomes a noun for what is said.
- *m’ incora buona umiltà* ‘puts into my heart good humility’ → “fills my heart with holy humility”: the heart implicit in the verb becomes the object of “fills,” humility is introduced by “with,” and “holy” is more specific than *buona*.
- *gran tumor m’ appiani* ‘a great swelling thou flattenest for me’ → “lowers my swollen pride”: the second-person verb addressed to the speaker becomes a third-person verb governed by “Thy true saying.” The swelling is interpreted as “pride,” with the image retained in “swollen”; its magnitude is not separately expressed, and the personal pronoun becomes possessive.
- *quei* ‘that one’ → “he”: a personal pronoun replaces the demonstrative.
- *tu parlavi ora* ‘thou wast speaking just now’ → “but now thou wast speaking”: the time expression moves before the subject and verb.

> 121 “Quegli è,” rispose, “Provenzan Salvani;  
> 122 ed è qui, perchè fu presuntuoso  
> 123 a recar Siena tutta alle sue mani.  

“That,” he answered, “is Provenzan Salvani; and he is here because in his presumption he thought to bring all Siena in his grasp.

- *Quegli è, rispose* ‘that one is, he answered’ → “That,” he answered, “is”: the reporting clause is moved between subject and verb.
- *fu presuntuoso a recar* ‘he was presumptuous in bringing’ → “in his presumption he thought to bring”: the predicate adjective becomes a noun in a prepositional phrase, and “thought to” supplies a verb of intention.
- *Siena tutta alle sue mani* ‘Siena all into his hands’ → “all Siena in his grasp”: “all” moves before the name, and the plural ‘hands’ becomes the singular figure of a “grasp,” with “in” rather than a preposition of motion.

> 124 Ito è così, e va senza riposo,  
> 125 poi che morì: cotal moneta rende  
> 126 a satisfar chi è di là tropp' oso.”  

Thus he hath gone and goes without rest since he died; such coin he pays back in satisfaction who yonder is too daring.”

- *Ito è così* ‘gone is he thus’ → “Thus he hath gone”: the adverb moves to the front, and the perfect with *essere* becomes an English perfect with “hath” before the participle.
- *rende a satisfar chi* ‘renders, to make satisfaction, whoever’ → “he pays back in satisfaction who”: “pays back” makes the sense of restitution explicit; the infinitive of purpose becomes a prepositional phrase, and the postposed subject *chi* is divided into anticipatory “he” and relative “who.”
- *è di là tropp’ oso* ‘is over there too bold’ → “yonder is too daring”: the place expression becomes a single adverb and precedes the verb; “daring” renders the adjective of boldness.

> 127 Ed io: “Se quello spirito che attende,  
> 128 pria che si penta, l' orlo della vita,  
> 129 laggiù dimora e quassù non ascende,  

And I: “If that spirit who awaits the brink of life, ere he repents, abides there below, and mounts not up hither,

- *pria che si penta, l’ orlo della vita* ‘before he repents, the brink of life’ → “the brink of life, ere he repents”: the object of “awaits” is brought forward from beyond the intervening time clause.
- *laggiù dimora* ‘there below abides’ → “abides there below”: the place expression follows the verb.
- *quassù non ascende* ‘up here does not ascend’ → “mounts not up hither”: the verb comes first, the negative follows it, and the place adverb is rendered directionally.

> 130 se buona orazion lui non aita,  
> 131 prima che passi tempo quanto visse,  
> 132 come fu la venuta a lui largita?”  

unless holy prayers aid him, until so much time be passed as he hath lived, how has the coming here been vouchsafed to him?”

- *se buona orazion lui non aita* ‘if good prayer him does not aid’ → “unless holy prayers aid him”: “unless” absorbs the negation; singular prayer becomes plural, ‘good’ becomes “holy,” and the object follows the verb.
- *prima che passi tempo quanto visse* ‘before time passes as much as he lived’ → “until so much time be passed as he hath lived”: after the preceding negative, “until” expresses the limit given by ‘before’; the measure “so much” moves before “time,” passing is presented as completed, and the simple past becomes a present perfect.
- *fu la venuta a lui largita* ‘was the coming to him granted’ → “has the coming here been vouchsafed to him”: the simple past passive becomes a present perfect passive, “here” supplies a destination, and the recipient follows the participle.

> 133 “Quando vivea più glorioso,” disse,  
> 134 “liberamente nel campo di Siena,  
> 135 ogni vergogna deposta, s' affisse;  

“When he lived in highest glory,” said he, “in the market-place of Siena he stationed himself of his free will and put away all shame;

- *vivea più glorioso* ‘he lived most glorious’ → “he lived in highest glory”: the adjective predicated of the man becomes a noun in a prepositional phrase; *più* is taken in a superlative sense.
- *liberamente* ‘freely’ → “of his free will”: the adverb becomes a phrase and moves after the main verb.
- *nel campo di Siena* ‘in the square of Siena’ → “in the market-place of Siena”: “market-place” specifies the kind of public space.
- *ogni vergogna deposta, s’ affisse* ‘all shame laid aside, he fixed himself’ → “he stationed himself of his free will and put away all shame”: the main verb comes first, with the reflexive after it, and the preceding absolute participial construction becomes a second finite verb coordinated by “and.”

> 136 e lì, per trar l' amico suo di pena  
> 137 che sostenea nella prigion di Carlo,  
> 138 si condusse a tremar per ogni vena.  

and there, to deliver his friend from the pains he was suffering in Charles’s prison, he brought himself to tremble in every vein.

- *per trar l’ amico suo di pena* ‘to draw the friend of his out of suffering’ → “to deliver his friend from the pains”: “deliver … from” replaces the image of drawing out, the possessive precedes the noun, and singular *pena* becomes a definite plural.
- *che sostenea* ‘which he was enduring’ → “he was suffering”: the object relative is unexpressed, and the imperfect is rendered by a progressive.
- *nella prigion di Carlo* ‘in the prison of Charles’ → “in Charles’s prison”: a possessive before the noun replaces the following *di* phrase.
- *si condusse* ‘himself he brought’ → “he brought himself”: the reflexive follows the verb.
- *per ogni vena* ‘through every vein’ → “in every vein”: a preposition of location replaces one of extension.

> 139 Più non dirò, e scuro so che parlo;  
> 140 ma poco tempo andrà che i tuoi vicini  
> 141 faranno sì che tu potrai chiosarlo.  

No more will I tell, and darkly I know that I speak, but short time will pass ere thy neighbours will act so, that thou shalt be able to interpret it.

- *Più non dirò* ‘more I shall not say’ → “No more will I tell”: the negative is joined to “more,” the auxiliary precedes the subject, and “tell” renders *dire*.
- *scuro* ‘obscure’ → “darkly”: the Italian adjective used adverbially becomes an English adverb.
- *poco tempo andrà che* ‘little time will go before’ → “short time will pass ere”: the quantity of time becomes its duration, “pass” replaces ‘go,’ and “ere” makes the temporal relation explicit.
- *faranno sì* ‘will do so’ → “will act so”: the verb of doing is rendered as one of acting.
- *tu potrai chiosarlo* ‘thou wilt be able to gloss it’ → “thou shalt be able to interpret it”: “interpret” gives the sense of glossing without the suggestion of supplying a gloss; the attached object pronoun becomes a separate word.

> 142 Quest' opera gli tolse quei confini.”  

This deed released him from those confines.”

- *Quest’ opera* ‘this work’ → “This deed”: “deed” specifies an action.
- *gli tolse quei confini* ‘took those confines away from him’ → “released him from those confines”: the person affected becomes the direct object, and the former direct object becomes the complement of “from.”

## Summary — Italian Order and English Idiom

Okey often keeps Dante’s words but changes the order in which they reach the reader. In *Credette Cimabue*, “Cimabue thought” puts the subject first; “in painting” comes after “to hold the field” rather than before it. Yet he does not consistently smooth away an unusual order: “of their will” still precedes “thine angels”, and “all men” retains the emphatic position of *ogni uomo* before the verb. Two routine adjustments generally go unremarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where Italian indicates the subject by the verb ending alone, and omits the article before a possessive, as when *il tuo nome* becomes “thy name”.

Changes of construction often preserve the force rather than the form of the Italian. A subjunctive of wish such as *Vegna* becomes “May” followed by the subject and “come”; the impersonal *si va* becomes the personal “we go”. In *più ridon le carte*, the leaves themselves ‘smile’, whereas “more pleasing are the leaves” gives the effect on the observer. Elsewhere the wording stays close even to a distinctive image: *un fiato di vento* is “a breath of wind”, and *cotal moneta* is “such coin”.

Words are supplied as readily for grammatical clarity as for emphasis. “Attain” gives *non potem* the infinitive that Italian leaves understood, while “foul” gives *caligini* a quality not separately named. Conversely, the reinforcing *già* in *già non si fa* has no distinct English equivalent, and the second *vien* disappears from “now thence”. Okey’s choice of words can also shift the emphasis without adding or omitting a phrase: “starry spheres” gives the reference of *stellate rote* rather than its image of ‘starred wheels’, while “she is degraded” softens the bluntness of *è putta*.
