# Purgatorio 18

## Lines 1–6 — Fear of Asking Too Much

> 1 Posto avea fine al suo ragionamento  
> 2 l' alto dottore, ed attento guardava  
> 3 nella mia vista, s' io parea contento;  

The lofty Teacher had put an end to his argument, and was looking intent in my face, if I seemed satisfied;

- *Posto avea fine al suo ragionamento l' alto dottore* ‘put had an end to his discourse the lofty teacher’ → “The lofty Teacher had put an end to his argument”: the subject moves from the end to the beginning, the auxiliary comes before the participle, and ‘discourse’ is rendered more specifically as “argument”.
- *attento guardava* ‘intent he looked’ → “was looking intent”: the imperfect takes the progressive form, and the adjective describing the subject moves after the verb.
- *nella mia vista* ‘in my countenance’ → “in my face”: “face” gives a concrete rendering of the noun denoting outward appearance.

> 4 ed io, cui nuova sete ancor frugava,  
> 5 di fuor taceva e dentro dicea: “Forse  
> 6 lo troppo domandar, ch' io fo, gli grava.”  

and I, whom a new thirst was yet tormenting, was silent outwardly, and within said; “Perchance the too great questioning which I make irks him.”

- *cui nuova sete ancor frugava* ‘whom new thirst still goaded’ → “whom a new thirst was yet tormenting”: an indefinite article is supplied, and the imperfect is rendered by a progressive, with “yet” between auxiliary and participle. “Tormenting” expresses distress rather than the image of prodding.
- *di fuor taceva* ‘outwardly I kept silent’ → “was silent outwardly”: the verb becomes a form of ‘be’ with an adjective, and the adverbial phrase becomes a single adverb placed last.
- *lo troppo domandar* ‘the asking too much’ → “the too great questioning”: the substantival infinitive becomes the verbal noun “questioning”, with excess expressed by the preceding adjectival phrase “too great”.
- *gli grava* ‘to him weighs’ → “irks him”: the verb of weighing or burdening becomes one of vexing, and the preceding indirect object becomes a following direct object.

## Lines 7–39 — Love as Inclination and Desire

> 7 Ma quel padre verace, che s' accorse  
> 8 del timido voler che non s' apriva,  
> 9 parlando di parlare ardir mi porse.  

But that true Father, who perceived the shrinking desire which disclosed not itself, by speaking put courage in me to speak.

- *padre verace* ‘father truthful’ → “true Father”: the adjective precedes the noun; “true” renders ‘truthful’.
- *s' accorse del* ‘became aware of the’ → “perceived the”: the reflexive verb with a prepositional complement becomes a transitive verb.
- *timido voler* ‘timid wish’ → “shrinking desire”: a participle replaces the adjective, and “desire” renders the substantivized infinitive ‘to wish’.
- *non s' apriva* ‘did not open itself’ → “disclosed not itself”: disclosure renders the figurative opening; the negative and reflexive follow the verb rather than preceding it.
- *di parlare ardir mi porse* ‘to speak daring to me he offered’ → “put courage in me to speak”: the infinitive moves to the end, and offering something to someone becomes putting something in him.

> 10 Ond' io: “Maestro, il mio veder s'avviva  
> 11 sì nel tuo lume, ch' io discerno chiaro  
> 12 quanto la tua ragion porti o descriva;  

Wherefore I: “Master, my vision is so quickened in thy light, that I discern clearly all that thy discourse imports or describes;

- *il mio veder* ‘my seeing’ → “my vision”: a noun replaces the substantivized infinitive.
- *s'avviva sì* ‘grows lively so’ → “is so quickened”: the reflexive verb of becoming is rendered by a passive, with the adverb before the participle.
- *discerno chiaro* ‘discern clear’ → “discern clearly”: English uses an adverb where Italian uses an adjective adverbially.
- *quanto* ‘as much as’ → “all that”: the relative of quantity becomes a pronoun of totality followed by a relative.
- *la tua ragion porti o descriva* ‘thy reasoning may carry or describe’ → “thy discourse imports or describes”: the reasoning is called a discourse, ‘carry’ is rendered in the sense of conveying meaning, and the Italian subjunctives become English indicatives.

> 13 però ti prego, dolce padre caro,  
> 14 che mi dimostri amore, a cui riduci  
> 15 ogni buono operare e il suo contraro.”  

therefore I pray thee, sweet Father dear, that thou define love to me, to which thou dost reduce every good work and its opposite.”

- *ti prego* ‘thee I pray’ → “I pray thee”: the object follows the verb.
- *mi dimostri amore* ‘to me thou show love’ → “thou define love to me”: showing is understood as defining, and the indirect object comes last.
- *a cui riduci* ‘to which thou reducest’ → “to which thou dost reduce”: the simple present is expressed with an auxiliary.
- *ogni buono operare* ‘every good acting’ → “every good work”: a count noun replaces the substantivized infinitive.

> 16 “Drizza,” disse, “ver me l' acute luci  
> 17 dello intelletto, e fieti manifesto  
> 18 l' error dei ciechi che si fanno duci.  

“Direct,” said he, “towards me the keen eyes of the understanding, and the error of the blind who make them guides shall be manifest to thee.

- *l' acute luci* ‘the keen lights’ → “the keen eyes”: the metaphorical ‘lights’ are named as eyes.
- *fieti manifesto l' error dei ciechi* ‘will become to thee manifest the error of the blind’ → “the error of the blind” … “shall be manifest to thee”: the subject, together with its relative clause, precedes the predicate; becoming is rendered by being, and the attached pronoun becomes a final prepositional phrase.
- *si fanno duci* ‘make themselves leaders’ → “make them guides”: “them” stands for the reflexive ‘themselves’, and the leaders are called guides.

> 19 L' animo, ch' è creato ad amar presto,  
> 20 ad ogni cosa è mobile che piace,  
> 21 tosto che dal piacere in atto è desto.  

The mind which is created quick to love, is responsive to everything that is pleasing, soon as by pleasure it is awakened into activity.

- *ad amar presto* ‘to love ready’ → “quick to love”: the adjective moves before its infinitive, with “quick” for ‘ready’.
- *ad ogni cosa è mobile che piace* ‘towards every thing is movable that pleases’ → “is responsive to everything that is pleasing”: the predicate moves before its complement, bringing noun and relative clause together; susceptibility to movement becomes responsiveness, and the active verb of the relative becomes a predicate adjective.
- *in atto è desto* ‘into act is awakened’ → “it is awakened into activity”: the phrase expressing the resulting state follows the passive verb, and ‘act’ becomes “activity”.

> 22 Vostra apprensiva da esser verace  
> 23 tragge intenzione, e dentro a voi la spiega,  
> 24 sì che l' animo ad essa volger face.  

Your apprehensive faculty draws an impression from a real object, and unfolds it within you, so that it makes the mind turn thereto.

- *Vostra apprensiva* ‘your apprehensive [power]’ → “Your apprehensive faculty”: English supplies a noun for the substantivized adjective.
- *da esser verace tragge intenzione* ‘from a being real draws an intentional representation’ → “draws an impression from a real object”: the source phrase follows the verb and its object; ‘being’ becomes “object”, the adjective moves before it, and the technical noun becomes “an impression”.
- *dentro a voi la spiega* ‘within you it unfolds’ → “unfolds it within you”: the object pronoun follows the verb, and the phrase of place comes last.
- *l' animo ad essa volger face* ‘the mind towards it turn makes’ → “it makes the mind turn thereto”: the causative verb comes first rather than last, and the phrase of direction becomes an adverb following “turn”.

> 25 E se, rivolto, in ver di lei si piega,  
> 26 quel piegare è amor, quello è natura  
> 27 che per piacer di nuovo in voi si lega.  

And if, being turned, it inclines towards it, that inclination is love; that is nature, which through pleasure is bound anew within you.

- *rivolto* ‘turned’ → “being turned”: English expands the participle with “being”.
- *in ver di lei si piega* ‘towards it bends itself’ → “it inclines towards it”: an intransitive verb replaces the reflexive, and the directional phrase follows it.
- *quel piegare* ‘that bending’ → “that inclination”: the substantivized infinitive becomes an abstract noun, repeating the preceding verb in noun form.
- *di nuovo in voi si lega* ‘anew in you binds itself’ → “is bound anew within you”: the reflexive is read as a passive, the verb precedes both modifiers, and “within” renders ‘in’.

> 28 Poi come il foco movesi in altura,  
> 29 per la sua forma, ch' è nata a salire  
> 30 là dove più in sua materia dura:  

Then, even as fire moves upward by reason of its form, whose nature it is to ascend, there where it endures longest in its material;

- *come il foco* ‘as the fire’ → “even as fire”: “even” reinforces the comparison, and English omits the generic article.
- *movesi in altura* ‘moves itself on high’ → “moves upward”: the verb is intransitive rather than reflexive, and an adverb replaces the prepositional phrase.
- *per la sua forma* ‘through its form’ → “by reason of its form”: the preposition is expanded to make its causal force explicit.
- *ch' è nata a salire* ‘which is born to ascend’ → “whose nature it is to ascend”: a possessive relative and an explicit noun express what Italian predicates of the form as an inborn disposition.
- *più in sua materia dura* ‘more in its matter it lasts’ → “it endures longest in its material”: the verb moves first, ‘more’ is interpreted as a superlative of duration, and “material” renders ‘matter’.

> 31 così l' animo preso entra in disire,  
> 32 ch' è moto spiritale, e mai non posa  
> 33 fin che la cosa amata il fa gioire.  

so the enamoured mind falls to desire, which is a spiritual movement, and never rests until the object of its love makes it rejoice.

- *l' animo preso* ‘the mind taken’ → “the enamoured mind”: “enamoured” specifies in what sense the mind is taken, and the modifier precedes the noun.
- *entra in disire* ‘enters into desire’ → “falls to desire”: a different verb and preposition express the onset of desire.
- *moto spiritale* ‘movement spiritual’ → “a spiritual movement”: English supplies an indefinite article and puts the adjective first.
- *mai non posa* ‘never not rests’ → “never rests”: a single English negative renders Italian negative concord.
- *la cosa amata* ‘the thing loved’ → “the object of its love”: a noun with a possessive replaces the participle, and “object” renders ‘thing’.
- *il fa gioire* ‘it makes rejoice’ → “makes it rejoice”: the object pronoun follows the causative verb.

> 34 Or ti puote apparer quant' è nascosa  
> 35 la veritade alla gente, ch' avvera  
> 36 ciascuno amore in sè laudabil cosa;  

Now may be apparent to thee, how deeply the truth is hidden from the folk who aver that every act of love is in itself a laudable thing,

- *ti puote apparer* ‘to thee may appear’ → “may be apparent to thee”: a copula and adjective replace the infinitive ‘appear’, and the personal complement moves to the end.
- *quant' è nascosa la veritade* ‘how much is hidden the truth’ → “how deeply the truth is hidden”: degree is expressed as depth, and the subject precedes the verb.
- *alla gente* ‘to the folk’ → “from the folk”: English uses ‘from’ for those to whom something is hidden.
- *ch' avvera* ‘which affirms as true’ → “who aver”: the relative is personal, and the verb agrees with “folk” as a plural rather than a singular collective.
- *ciascuno amore in sè laudabil cosa* ‘each love in itself a praiseworthy thing’ → “that every act of love is in itself a laudable thing”: the object and its predicate become a finite clause with “that” and “is”; “act of” is supplied, interpreting each love as an instance of loving.

> 37 però che forse appar la sua matera  
> 38 sempr'esser buona; ma non ciascun segno  
> 39 è buono, ancor che buona sia la cera.”  

because, forsooth, its material may seem always to be good; but not every imprint is good, albeit the wax may be good.”

- *forse appar la sua matera sempr'esser buona* ‘perhaps appears its matter always to be good’ → “forsooth, its material may seem always to be good”: “forsooth” gives the opening an emphatic rather than a conjectural sound; the uncertainty of ‘perhaps’ is carried by “may”. The subject moves before the verb, with “material” for ‘matter’.
- *ciascun segno* ‘each mark’ → “every imprint”: “imprint” specifies the kind of mark.
- *ancor che buona sia la cera* ‘although good be the wax’ → “albeit the wax may be good”: the subject moves first and the adjective last; the modal “may” renders the concessive subjunctive.

## Lines 40–72 — Love and the Freedom of Assent

> 40 “Le tue parole e il mio seguace ingegno,”  
> 41 risposi lui, “m' hanno amor discoperto;  
> 42 ma ciò m' ha fatto di dubbiar più pregno:  

“Thy words and my attendant wit,” I answered him, “have made love plain to me, but that has made me more teeming with doubt;

- *seguace ingegno* ‘following intellect’ → “attendant wit”: following is expressed as attendance, and *ingegno* by “wit” in the sense of intellectual faculty.
- *m' hanno amor discoperto* ‘to me have love uncovered’ → “have made love plain to me”: the participle becomes a verb and a predicative adjective; the recipient moves to the end.
- *ciò m' ha fatto di dubbiar più pregno* ‘this me has made of doubting more pregnant’ → “that has made me more teeming with doubt”: the demonstrative changes, the object follows the verb, and the complement of the adjective comes last. The infinitive becomes a noun, and pregnancy is rendered as teeming.

> 43 chè, s' amore è di fuori a noi offerto  
> 44 e l' anima non va con altro piede,  
> 45 se dritta o torta va, non è suo merto.”  

for if love is offered to us from without, and the soul walks with no other foot, it is no merit of hers whether she go straight or crooked.”

- *chè* ‘because’ → “for”: the reason is introduced by a coordinating rather than a subordinating conjunction.
- *è di fuori a noi offerto* ‘is from without to us offered’ → “is offered to us from without”: the participle comes next to its auxiliary, and the recipient precedes the source.
- *non va con altro piede* ‘does not go with another foot’ → “walks with no other foot”: the verb is made specific to going on foot, and the negation shifts to the determiner.
- *se dritta o torta va* ‘whether straight or crooked she goes’ → “whether she go straight or crooked”: the whole clause is postponed, the verb precedes the alternatives, and its indicative becomes an English subjunctive.
- *non è suo merto* ‘is not her merit’ → “it is no merit of hers”: an anticipatory “it” stands for the postponed clause; the negation attaches to the noun, and the possessive follows it.

> 46 Ed egli a me: “Quanto ragion qui vede  
> 47 dirti poss' io; da indi in là t' aspetta  
> 48 pure a Beatrice, ch' opera è di fede.  

And he to me: “So far as reason sees here, I can tell thee; from beyond that point, ever await Beatrice, for ’tis a matter of faith.

- *Quanto ragion qui vede* ‘as much as reason here sees’ → “So far as reason sees here”: the extent is expressed in terms of distance, and the adverb follows the verb.
- *dirti poss' io* ‘tell thee can I’ → “I can tell thee”: the explicit subject comes first, followed by the modal and infinitive.
- *da indi in là* ‘from there onward’ → “from beyond that point”: the adverbs of place become a phrase with an explicit point of reference.
- *t' aspetta pure a Beatrice* ‘wait thou only for Beatrice’ → “ever await Beatrice”: the pronominal imperative with *a* becomes a transitive verb; “ever” gives *pure* a continuing rather than an exclusive force.
- *ch' opera è di fede* ‘because a work it is of faith’ → “for ’tis a matter of faith”: the causal clause becomes coordinate, the copula precedes its complement, and a ‘work’ becomes a ‘matter’.

> 49 Ogni forma sustanzial, che setta  
> 50 è da materia ed è con lei unita,  
> 51 specifica virtude ha in sè colletta,  

Every substantial form, which is distinct from matter and is in union with it, has a specific virtue contained within itself

- *forma sustanzial* ‘form substantial’ → “substantial form”: the adjective precedes the noun.
- *setta è da materia* ‘separated is from matter’ → “is distinct from matter”: the copula comes first, and an adjective of distinction replaces the participle of separation.
- *con lei unita* ‘with it united’ → “is in union with it”: the shared copula is repeated and the participle becomes a prepositional phrase preceding “with it”.
- *specifica virtude ha in sè colletta* ‘specific virtue has in itself gathered’ → “has a specific virtue contained within itself”: the verb precedes its object, an indefinite article is supplied, and the participle precedes the phrase of place. ‘Gathered’ becomes “contained” and ‘in’ becomes “within”.

> 52 la qual senza operar non è sentita,  
> 53 nè si dimostra ma' che per effetto,  
> 54 come per verdi fronde in pianta vita.  

which is not perceived save in operation, nor is manifested except by its effects, just as life in a plant by the green leaves.

- *senza operar non è sentita* ‘without operating is not felt’ → “is not perceived save in operation”: the negative predicate comes first; felt experience becomes perception, and the infinitive after ‘without’ becomes a noun in an exceptive phrase.
- *nè si dimostra ma' che per effetto* ‘nor shows itself otherwise than by effect’ → “nor is manifested except by its effects”: the pronominal verb becomes passive, the restriction is expressed by “except”, and the singular becomes a plural with a possessive.
- *come per verdi fronde in pianta vita* ‘as by green leaves in a plant life’ → “just as life in a plant by the green leaves”: “just” strengthens the comparison; life comes first, then its location, then its sign. Articles are supplied before plant and leaves.

> 55 Però là onde vegna lo intelletto  
> 56 delle prime notizie, uomo non sape,  
> 57 nè de' primi appetibili l' affetto,  

Therefore man knows not whence the understanding of the first cognitions may come, nor the inclination to the prime objects of appetite,

- *là onde vegna lo intelletto delle prime notizie, uomo non sape* ‘there whence may come the understanding of the first cognitions, man knows not’ → “man knows not whence the understanding of the first cognitions may come”: the governing clause moves before the indirect question; “whence” alone gives the source, the subject precedes the verb, and “may” represents the subjunctive.
- *de' primi appetibili l' affetto* ‘for the first appetibles the affection’ → “the inclination to the prime objects of appetite”: the governing noun comes first; ‘affection’ becomes “inclination”, and the substantivized adjective is expanded into “objects of appetite”.

> 58 che sono in voi, sì come studio in ape  
> 59 di far lo mele; e questa prima voglia  
> 60 merto di lode o di biasmo non cape.  

which are in you, even as the instinct in bees to make honey; and this prime will admits no desert of praise or of blame.

- *sì come studio in ape* ‘just as diligence in a bee’ → “even as the instinct in bees”: *studio* is interpreted as an instinct, receives a definite article, and the generic singular becomes plural.
- *di far lo mele* ‘of making the honey’ → “to make honey”: an English infinitive expresses what the instinct is to do, and the mass noun loses its article.
- *questa prima voglia* ‘this first desire’ → “this prime will”: “prime” renders priority, and desire is expressed as will.
- *merto di lode o di biasmo non cape* ‘merit of praise or of blame does not contain’ → “admits no desert of praise or of blame”: the verb moves before its object and becomes “admits”; negation passes to the noun, rendered as “desert”.

> 61 Or, perchè a questa ogni altra si raccoglia,  
> 62 innata v' è la virtù che consiglia,  
> 63 e dell' assenso de' tener la soglia.  

Now in order that to this will every other may be related, innate with you is the virtue which giveth counsel, and ought to guard the threshold of assent.

- *perchè a questa ogni altra si raccoglia* ‘so that to this every other may gather’ → “in order that to this will every other may be related”: the purpose conjunction is expanded, the noun understood with *questa* is supplied, and pronominal gathering becomes a passive expression of relation.
- *innata v' è* ‘innate in you is’ → “innate with you is”: “with” replaces the locative force of the pronoun.
- *che consiglia* ‘which counsels’ → “which giveth counsel”: a single verb becomes a verb and its object.
- *dell' assenso de' tener la soglia* ‘of assent ought to keep the threshold’ → “ought to guard the threshold of assent”: the dependent phrase moves after its noun, and ‘keep’ is interpreted as guarding.

> 64 Questo è il principio, là onde si piglia  
> 65 ragion di meritare in voi, secondo  
> 66 che buoni e rei amori accoglie e viglia.  

This is the principle whence is derived the reason of desert in you, according as it garners and winnows good and evil loves.

- *là onde si piglia* ‘there whence one takes’ → “whence is derived”: “whence” absorbs the locative antecedent, and the pronominal construction becomes an explicit passive of derivation.
- *ragion di meritare* ‘reason for meriting’ → “the reason of desert”: an article is supplied and the infinitive becomes an abstract noun.
- *buoni e rei amori accoglie e viglia* ‘good and bad loves it gathers and sifts’ → “it garners and winnows good and evil loves”: the object follows both verbs; “evil” renders *rei*, and the verbs are given specifically harvest-related senses.

> 67 Color che ragionando andaro al fondo  
> 68 s' accorser d' esta innata libertate,  
> 69 però moralità lasciaro al mondo.  

Those who in their reasoning went to the foundation, perceived this innate freedom, therefore they left ethics to the world.

- *ragionando* ‘reasoning’ → “in their reasoning”: the gerund becomes a prepositional phrase with a possessive.
- *al fondo* ‘to the bottom’ → “to the foundation”: the noun is interpreted as an underlying basis.
- *s' accorser d' esta innata libertate* ‘became aware of this innate freedom’ → “perceived this innate freedom”: the pronominal verb with *di* becomes a transitive verb with a direct object.
- *moralità lasciaro* ‘morality they left’ → “they left ethics”: the object follows the verb, and *moralità* is rendered as the discipline of ethics.

> 70 Onde, pognam che di necessitate  
> 71 surga ogni amor che dentro a voi s' accende,  
> 72 di ritenerlo è in voi la potestate.  

Wherefore suppose that every love which is kindled within you arise of necessity, the power to arrest it is within you.

- *pognam che* ‘let us suppose that’ → “suppose that”: the first-person plural exhortation becomes a bare imperative.
- *di necessitate surga* ‘of necessity arise’ → “arise of necessity”: the adverbial phrase follows the verb, and both move after the subject and its relative clause.
- *dentro a voi s' accende* ‘within you kindles itself’ → “is kindled within you”: the pronominal verb becomes passive and precedes the phrase of place.
- *di ritenerlo è in voi la potestate* ‘to hold it back is in you the power’ → “the power to arrest it is within you”: the subject comes first with its infinitive immediately following; holding back becomes arresting, and ‘in’ becomes “within”.

## Lines 73–105 — Free Will and the Haste of Love

> 73 La nobile virtù Beatrice intende  
> 74 per lo libero arbitrio, e però guarda  
> 75 che l' abbi a mente, s' a parlar ten prende.”  

By the noble virtue Beatrice understands Freewill, and therefore, look that thou have this in mind, if she betake her to speak with thee thereof.”

- *La nobile virtù Beatrice intende per lo libero arbitrio* ‘the noble virtue Beatrice means for free will’ → “By the noble virtue Beatrice understands Freewill”: Okey puts the preposition before the expression to be explained, rather than before its equivalent; he makes “Freewill” the direct object of “understands” and drops its article.
- *l' abbi a mente* ‘thou have it in mind’ → “thou have this in mind”: the object pronoun becomes a demonstrative.
- *s' a parlar ten prende* ‘if to speak to thee of it she takes it upon herself’ → “if she betake her to speak with thee thereof”: the finite verb moves before the infinitive and becomes reflexive in English; the two pronouns compressed into *ten* are unpacked after “speak,” with “with” for ‘to’.

> 76 La luna, quasi a mezza notte tarda,  
> 77 facea le stelle a noi parer più rade,  
> 78 fatta com' un secchione che tutto arda;  

The moon, almost retarded to midnight, made the stars appear more thin to us, fashioned like a bucket all burning;

- *quasi a mezza notte tarda* ‘almost till midnight late’ → “almost retarded to midnight”: the adjective of lateness becomes a participle of delay and moves before the time phrase.
- *facea le stelle a noi parer più rade* ‘made the stars to us appear sparser’ → “made the stars appear more thin to us”: the phrase naming the observers moves to the end; ‘sparser’ becomes “more thin.”
- *fatta com' un secchione che tutto arda* ‘shaped like a large bucket that should burn all over’ → “fashioned like a bucket all burning”: the augmentative force of *secchione* is lost, and the relative clause with its subjunctive becomes a participial phrase.

> 79 e correa contra il ciel, per quelle strade  
> 80 che il sole infiamma allor che quel da Roma  
> 81 tra i Sardi e i Corsi il vede quando cade.  

and her course against the heavens was on those paths which the sun inflames, when they in Rome see him between the Sardinians and the Corsicans at his setting.

- *e correa contra il ciel* ‘and she ran against the sky’ → “and her course against the heavens was”: the action of running becomes a noun with a possessive and a form of ‘to be’; the singular sky becomes plural.
- *per quelle strade* ‘along those roads’ → “on those paths”: a phrase of motion along a route becomes one locating the course.
- *allor che quel da Roma tra i Sardi e i Corsi il vede* ‘at the time when the one from Rome between the Sardinians and the Corsicans sees him’ → “when they in Rome see him between the Sardinians and the Corsicans”: “when” absorbs the separate indication of time; the singular observer becomes an indefinite plural, ‘from’ becomes “in,” and the verb and its object precede the phrase beginning “between.”
- *quando cade* ‘when he falls’ → “at his setting”: the temporal clause becomes a prepositional phrase with a noun specific to the sun's descent.

> 82 E quell' ombra gentil, per cui si noma  
> 83 Pietola più che villa Mantovana,  
> 84 del mio carcar deposto avea la soma:  

And that noble shade through whom Pietola is more renowned than any Mantuan town, had put off the burden I had laid upon him;

- *per cui si noma Pietola* ‘through whom is named Pietola’ → “through whom Pietola is more renowned”: the subject moves before the verb, and the pronominal passive meaning ‘is named’ or ‘is spoken of’ becomes an expression of renown.
- *più che villa Mantovana* ‘more than Mantuan town’ → “than any Mantuan town”: the comparative degree has been placed before “renowned”; “any” makes the scope of the comparison explicit.
- *del mio carcar deposto avea la soma* ‘of my loading put down had the burden’ → “had put off the burden I had laid upon him”: auxiliary and participle change order, and putting a burden down becomes putting it “off.” The preceding phrase with a possessive and a substantivized infinitive becomes a following relative clause with its own past perfect and an explicit recipient.

> 85 per ch' io, che la ragione aperta e piana  
> 86 sopra le mie questioni avea ricolta,  
> 87 stava com' uom che sonnolento vana.  

wherefore I, who had garnered clear and plain reasons to my questionings, stood like one who is rambling drowsily.

- *che la ragione aperta e piana sopra le mie questioni avea ricolta* ‘who the explanation open and plain concerning my questions had gathered’ → “who had garnered clear and plain reasons to my questionings”: the verb moves ahead of its object, the singular definite explanation becomes plural “reasons” without an article, and its adjectives move before it, with “clear” for ‘open.’ The explanation concerning questions becomes reasons “to” questionings.
- *com' uom* ‘like a man’ → “like one”: the noun becomes an indefinite pronoun.
- *che sonnolento vana* ‘who, drowsy, wanders in mind’ → “who is rambling drowsily”: the simple present becomes progressive, and the adjective describing the man becomes an adverb following the verb.

> 88 Ma questa sonnolenza mi fu tolta  
> 89 subitamente da gente, che dopo  
> 90 le nostre spalle a noi era già volta.  

But this drowsiness was taken from me on a sudden, by people who behind our backs had already come round to us.

- *mi fu tolta* ‘from me was taken’ → “was taken from me”: the pronoun indicating the person deprived becomes a prepositional phrase after the verb.
- *subitamente* ‘suddenly’ → “on a sudden”: the adverb becomes a phrase.
- *da gente, che dopo le nostre spalle a noi era già volta* ‘by a throng that behind our shoulders toward us was already turned’ → “by people who behind our backs had already come round to us”: the singular collective becomes plural, and “backs” stands for ‘shoulders.’ Being or having turned toward someone becomes having “come round”; the destination follows the verb, and “already” precedes its participle.

> 91 E quale Ismeno già vide ed Asopo  
> 92 lungo di sè di notte furia e calca,  
> 93 pur che i Teban di Bacco avesser uopo:  

And even as Ismenus and Asopus saw of old a fury and a rout along their banks by night, if but the Thebans had need of Bacchus,

- *E quale* ‘and as’ → “And even as”: “even” reinforces the comparison.
- *Ismeno già vide ed Asopo* ‘Ismenus once saw and Asopus’ → “Ismenus and Asopus saw of old”: the two subjects are brought together before the verb, and the adverb becomes a phrase following it.
- *lungo di sè di notte furia e calca* ‘along themselves by night frenzy and press’ → “a fury and a rout along their banks by night”: the objects move before the phrases of place and time and acquire articles. “Their banks” specifies what the reflexive leaves implicit, and “rout” renders the press of people.
- *pur che i Teban di Bacco avesser uopo* ‘provided only that the Thebans of Bacchus had need’ → “if but the Thebans had need of Bacchus”: the condition is recast with “if but,” the Italian imperfect subjunctive is not separately marked, and the complement of ‘need’ follows it.

> 94 cotal per quel giron suo passo falca,  
> 95 per quel ch' io vidi di color, venendo,  
> 96 cui buon volere e giusto amor cavalca.  

suchwise along that circle, quickening their pace, were coming, by what I saw of them, those whom good will and just love bestride.

- *cotal* ‘such’ → “suchwise”: the correlative is made explicitly adverbial.
- *per quel giron suo passo falca* ‘along that circle it sweeps its stride’ → “along that circle, quickening their pace”: the figurative finite verb of sweeping or scything a stride becomes a participle denoting speed; the singular possessive becomes plural.
- *per quel ch' io vidi di color, venendo* ‘according to what I saw of those, coming’ → “were coming, by what I saw of them, those”: the participle becomes the main progressive verb and moves before the qualification. ‘Of those’ is split into “of them” and a separate “those” serving as subject.
- *cui buon volere e giusto amor cavalca* ‘whom good willing and just love rides’ → “whom good will and just love bestride”: the substantivized infinitive becomes the noun “will”; the singular verb becomes plural in agreement with the paired subjects, and riding is rendered as sitting astride.

> 97 Tosto fur sopra noi, perchè correndo  
> 98 si movea tutta quella turba magna;  
> 99 e due dinanzi gridavan piangendo:  

Soon were they upon us, because all that great throng was moving at a run; and two in front were shouting in tears:

- *perchè correndo si movea tutta quella turba magna* ‘because running moved all that throng great’ → “because all that great throng was moving at a run”: the subject moves before the verb and the adjective before the noun; the imperfect becomes progressive, and the preceding gerund becomes a following phrase of manner.
- *gridavan piangendo* ‘shouted weeping’ → “were shouting in tears”: the imperfect becomes progressive, and the accompanying action becomes a prepositional phrase.

> 100 “Maria corse con fretta alla montagna,”  

“Mary ran with haste to the hill country,”

- *alla montagna* ‘to the mountain’ → “to the hill country”: a singular landform is rendered as a region.

> 101 e: “Cesare, per soggiogare Ilerda,  
> 102 punse Marsilia e poi corse in Ispagna.”  

and “Cæsar to subdue Ilerda, stabbed Marseilles and then raced to Spain.”

- *punse* ‘pricked’ → “stabbed”: the English verb makes the thrust more forceful.
- *corse in Ispagna* ‘ran into Spain’ → “raced to Spain”: the verb stresses speed, and the preposition marks a destination rather than entry.

> 103 “Ratto, ratto, che il tempo non si perda  
> 104 per poco amor,” gridavan gli altri appresso,  
> 105 “che studio di ben far grazia rinverda.”  

“Haste! Haste! let no time be lost through little love,” cried the others afterwards, “that striving to do well may renew grace.”

- *Ratto, ratto* ‘quickly, quickly’ → “Haste! Haste!”: the repeated adverb becomes a repeated exhortation.
- *che il tempo non si perda* ‘that the time not be lost’ → “let no time be lost”: the subordinate subjunctive becomes an independent exhortation with “let,” and the negation shifts from the verb to the noun, displacing the definite article.
- *gridavan gli altri appresso* ‘were crying the others following behind’ → “cried the others afterwards”: the imperfect becomes a simple past, and “afterwards” gives a temporal sense to what can indicate position behind.
- *che studio di ben far grazia rinverda* ‘that zeal for well doing grace may make green again’ → “that striving to do well may renew grace”: the noun of application or zeal becomes “striving,” its complement becomes an infinitive with “well” after the verb, and the verb moves before its object. “Renew” drops the image of greening again.

## Lines 106–138 — The Runners and Their Rebuke of Sloth

> 106 “O gente, in cui fervore acuto adesso  
> 107 ricompie forse negligenza e indugio,  
> 108 da voi per tepidezza in ben far messo,  

“O people, in whom keen fervour now perchance doth make good negligence and delay used by you through lukewarmness in well-doing,

- *fervore acuto* ‘fervour keen’ → “keen fervour”: the adjective comes before the noun.
- *ricompie forse* ‘makes good perhaps’ → “perchance doth make good”: the adverb comes before the verb, which is expanded with “doth”.
- *da voi per tepidezza in ben far messo* ‘by you through lukewarmness into doing well put’ → “used by you through lukewarmness in well-doing”: the final participle moves to the front and becomes “used” rather than ‘put’; the infinitive phrase becomes the compound noun “well-doing”, and “in” no longer expresses the direction implied by ‘put’.

> 109 questi che vive, e certo io non vi bugio,  
> 110 vuole andar su, pur che il sol ne riluca;  

this one who lives, and surely I lie not to you, desires to ascend, if but the sun shine to us again;

- *e certo io non vi bugio* ‘and surely I not to you lie’ → “and surely I lie not to you”: both the negative and the indirect object follow the verb.
- *vuole andar su* ‘wants to go up’ → “desires to ascend”: “desires” renders the verb of wishing, and one verb expresses both the motion and its direction.
- *pur che il sol ne riluca* ‘provided that the sun on us shine again’ → “if but the sun shine to us again”: “if but” recasts the conditional conjunction; “to us” follows the verb, and the sense of the prefix *ri-* is expressed separately by “again”.

> 111 però ne dite ov' è presso il pertugio.”  

therefore tell us where the opening is near.”

- *ne dite* ‘to us tell’ → “tell us”: the indirect object follows the imperative.
- *ov' è presso il pertugio* ‘where is near the opening’ → “where the opening is near”: the subject moves before the verb in the indirect question.

> 112 Parole furon queste del mio duca;  

These were my Leader’s words;

- *Parole furon queste del mio duca* ‘words were these of my leader’ → “These were my Leader’s words”: “These” moves to the front; the predicate noun moves to the end, with the possessive before it in place of the phrase with ‘of’.

> 113 ed un di quegli spirti disse: “Vieni  
> 114 di retro a noi, e troverai la buca.  

and one of those spirits said: “Come behind us, and thou shalt find the cleft.

- *la buca* ‘the hole’ → “the cleft”: a more specific word for the opening.

> 115 Noi siam di voglia a moverci sì pieni,  
> 116 che ristar non potem; però perdona,  

We are so filled with desire to speed us, that stay we cannot;

- *di voglia a moverci sì pieni* ‘of desire to move ourselves so full’ → “so filled with desire to speed us”: the degree phrase moves before its complement, and the adjective ‘full’ becomes the participle “filled” with “with”; “speed” adds haste to the movement, and “us” has reflexive force.
- *ristar non potem* ‘stay not can we’ → “stay we cannot”: the infinitive remains first, but the negative is joined to the modal rather than preceding it.

> 117 se villania nostra giustizia tieni.  

therefore forgive, if thou hold our penance for rudeness.

- *se villania nostra giustizia tieni* ‘if for rudeness our justice you take’ → “if thou hold our penance for rudeness”: the verb comes before its object, and the judgement “for rudeness” comes last; “penance” interprets ‘justice’ as the penalty undergone.

> 118 Io fui abate in San Zeno a Verona,  

I was Abbot of San Zeno at Verona,

- *in San Zeno* ‘in San Zeno’ → “of San Zeno”: the place of office becomes a complement of the title.

> 119 sotto lo imperio del buon Barbarossa,  
> 120 di cui dolente ancor Milan ragiona.  

under the rule of the good Barbarossa, of whom Milan yet discourses with sorrow.

- *lo imperio* ‘the imperial sway’ → “the rule”: the English uses the more general term.
- *dolente ancor Milan ragiona* ‘sorrowing still Milan discourses’ → “Milan yet discourses with sorrow”: the subject comes first, before the adverb; the adjective describing Milan becomes a final phrase modifying the verb.

> 121 E tale ha già l' un piè dentro la fossa,  
> 122 che tosto piangerà quel monastero,  
> 123 e tristo fia d' averne avuto possa:  

And one I know has already a foot in the grave, who soon shall mourn because of that monastery, and sad will be for having had power there;

- *tale* ‘a certain one’ → “one I know”: the indefinite pronoun is expanded by an explicit claim of knowledge.
- *l' un piè dentro la fossa* ‘the one foot inside the pit’ → “a foot in the grave”: the English idiom uses an indefinite article rather than singling out one foot, shortens ‘inside’ to “in”, and takes the pit specifically as a grave.
- *piangerà quel monastero* ‘will weep for that monastery’ → “shall mourn because of that monastery”: the Italian makes the monastery the object of the verb; the English makes it the cause of the mourning.
- *d' averne avuto possa* ‘of having had power over it’ → “for having had power there”: “for” gives the ground of the sadness, and the pronominal reference to the monastery becomes the locative “there”, placed after “power”.

> 124 perchè suo figlio, mal del corpo intero,  
> 125 e della mente peggio, e che mal nacque,  
> 126 ha posto in loco di suo pastor vero.”  

because his son, deformed in his whole body and worse in mind, and who was born in shame, he has put there in place of its true shepherd.”

- *mal del corpo intero* ‘ill-whole of body’ → “deformed in his whole body”: the Italian qualifies bodily soundness with ‘ill’; the English puts the defect into “deformed” and makes “whole” qualify the extent of the body, with “his” supplied.
- *e della mente peggio* ‘and of mind worse’ → “and worse in mind”: the comparison comes first, with “in” specifying the respect in which it holds.
- *e che mal nacque* ‘and who ill was born’ → “and who was born in shame”: the adverb becomes a phrase after the verb, interpreting the ill as attaching to the circumstances of birth.
- *ha posto* ‘has put’ → “he has put there”: “there” supplies an explicit reference back to the monastery.
- *suo pastor vero* ‘its shepherd true’ → “its true shepherd”: the adjective precedes the noun.

> 127 Io non so se più disse, o s' ei si tacque,  
> 128 tant' era già di là da noi trascorso;  

If more he said, or if he was silent, I know not, so far already had he raced beyond us;

- *Io non so* ‘I do not know’ → “I know not”: the governing clause moves after both alternatives, with the negative after the verb.
- *o s' ei si tacque* ‘or if he fell silent’ → “or if he was silent”: the reflexive preterite becomes a description of a state rather than a change into it.
- *tant' era già di là da noi trascorso* ‘so far was he already beyond us passed’ → “so far already had he raced beyond us”: “already” precedes the auxiliary; English uses “had” for the perfect of motion, gives the motion the more specific sense “raced”, and puts “beyond us” after the participle.

> 129 ma questo intesi, e ritener mi piacque.  

but this I heard and was pleased to retain.

- *intesi* ‘I understood’ → “I heard”: the English takes the verb in its auditory sense.
- *ritener mi piacque* ‘to retain pleased me’ → “was pleased to retain”: the experiencer becomes the subject, shared with “heard”, and the infinitive follows rather than precedes the expression of pleasure.

> 130 E quei che m' era ad ogni uopo soccorso  
> 131 disse: “Volgiti in qua, vedine due  
> 132 venire, dando all' accidia di morso.”  

And he who was my succour in every need, said: “Turn thee hither, see two of them that come biting at sloth.”

- *m' era ad ogni uopo soccorso* ‘to me was at every need succour’ → “was my succour in every need”: the personal relation becomes possessive, and “in every need” follows the predicate noun instead of separating it from the copula.
- *vedine due venire* ‘see of them two come’ → “see two of them that come”: “of them” follows the numeral, and the dependent infinitive becomes a finite relative clause.
- *dando all' accidia di morso* ‘giving sloth a bite’ → “biting at sloth”: the construction with ‘give’ and a noun becomes a single participle, with “at” introducing its target.

> 133 Di retro a tutti dicean: “Prima fue  
> 134 morta la gente, a cui il mar s' aperse,  
> 135 che vedesse Jordan le erede sue”;  

Last of them all they said: “The people for whom the sea opened, were dead ere Jordan saw its heirs”;

- *Di retro a tutti* ‘behind all’ → “Last of them all”: position behind the others becomes rank in the sequence, with “of them” making the comparison explicit.
- *dicean* ‘were saying’ → “they said”: the imperfect becomes a simple past.
- *Prima fue morta la gente … che* ‘sooner was dead the people … than’ → “The people … were dead ere”: the subject and its relative clause move before the predicate; English treats the collective as plural, and the separated terms of the time comparison become “ere”.
- *a cui il mar s' aperse* ‘to whom the sea opened itself’ → “for whom the sea opened”: “for” expresses the beneficiary, and the Italian pronominal verb becomes an intransitive verb without a reflexive.
- *vedesse Jordan le erede sue* ‘should see Jordan the heirs its’ → “Jordan saw its heirs”: the subject precedes the verb, the temporal subjunctive becomes a simple past, and the possessive precedes the noun.

> 136 e: “Quella, che l' affanno non sofferse  
> 137 fino alla fine col figliuol d' Anchise,  
> 138 sè stessa a vita senza gloria offerse.”  

and: “That folk who endured not the toil to the end with Anchises’ son, gave them up to a life inglorious.”

- *Quella* ‘that [one]’ → “That folk”: the noun understood from the preceding utterance is supplied.
- *l' affanno non sofferse* ‘the toil did not endure’ → “endured not the toil”: the object moves after the verb, as does the negative.
- *col figliuol d' Anchise* ‘with the son of Anchises’ → “with Anchises’ son”: the possessive before the noun replaces the following phrase with ‘of’.
- *sè stessa … offerse* ‘itself … offered’ → “gave them up”: the pronominal object follows the verb, and “them” refers back to the members of the collective rather than using an emphatic singular reflexive; “gave … up” replaces ‘offered’.
- *a vita senza gloria* ‘to life without glory’ → “to a life inglorious”: the phrase follows the verb instead of preceding it, an indefinite article is supplied, and the phrase of privation becomes an adjective.

## Lines 139–145 — Thought Turns Into Dream

> 139 Poi quando fur da noi tanto divise  
> 140 quell' ombre che veder più non potersi,  

Then, when those shades were so far parted from us, that they could be seen no more,

- *fur da noi tanto divise quell' ombre* ‘were from us so parted those shades’ → “those shades were so far parted from us”: the subject moves before the verb, “from us” moves after the participle, and “far” makes the degree of separation explicit.
- *veder più non potersi* ‘be seen any longer they could not’ → “they could be seen no more”: the reflexive clitic gives ‘see’ passive force; English uses a passive infinitive after the modal and puts the negative in “no more”. The Italian modal is already finite and plural.

> 141 nuovo pensiero dentro a me si mise,  

a new thought was set within me,

- *nuovo pensiero dentro a me si mise* ‘new thought within me placed itself’ → “a new thought was set within me”: English adds the indefinite article, turns the pronominal verb into a passive, and moves the phrase of place after the verb.

> 142 del qual più altri nacquero e diversi;  

wherefrom many and divers others sprang;

- *del qual* ‘from which’ → “wherefrom”: a relative adverb replaces the preposition and relative pronoun.
- *più altri nacquero e diversi* ‘several others were born and divers’ → “many and divers others sprang”: “many” gives the indefinite quantity more emphasis; “divers” moves from after the verb to stand beside “many”, both now modifying “others”. The verb of birth becomes “sprang”.

> 143 e tanto d' uno in altro vaneggiai,  

and so from one to another I rambled,

- *d' uno in altro* ‘from one into another’ → “from one to another”: “to” replaces ‘into’.
- *vaneggiai* ‘I wandered idly in thought’ → “I rambled”: the English verb conveys the wandering without expressly marking it as idle or fanciful.

> 144 che gli occhi per vaghezza ricopersi,  

that I closed mine eyes for very wandering,

- *gli occhi per vaghezza ricopersi* ‘the eyes through wandering I covered over’ → “I closed mine eyes for very wandering”: the verb moves before its object and the phrase of cause; covering the eyes becomes closing them, and the definite article becomes a possessive. “Wandering” takes the abstract noun in the sense of mental roving; “very” is added for emphasis.

> 145 e il pensamento in sogno trasmutai.  

and thought I transmuted into dream.

- *il pensamento in sogno trasmutai* ‘the thought into dream I transmuted’ → “thought I transmuted into dream”: the object remains first but loses its article, making “thought” less determinate; the verb moves before the phrase of result.

## Summary — English Syntax, Italian Echoes

Okey’s most frequent departure is to rearrange rather than omit. In the opening sentence, the delayed subject *l' alto dottore* becomes “The lofty Teacher” and precedes the verb; the auxiliary and participle in *posto avea* likewise change places in “had put”. Elsewhere, objects and their modifiers move together: *dell' assenso de' tener la soglia* becomes “ought to guard the threshold of assent”, with the threshold no longer separated from the phrase that qualifies it. Yet Okey does not regularize every inversion. In “thought I transmuted into dream”, *il pensamento* keeps its emphatic place before subject and verb.

Changes of construction often go hand in hand with changes of diction. The substantivized infinitive *quel piegare*, ‘that bending’, becomes “that inclination”, preserving its link to the preceding “inclines”. The imperfect *gridavan* becomes the progressive “were shouting”, while the accompanying gerund *piangendo* becomes “in tears”. Sometimes a choice of word sharpens an image: “imprint” for *segno*, ‘mark’, is particularly apt beside “wax”. Elsewhere it removes one: “renew” conveys the effect of *rinverda* without its image of growing green again.

Some additions name what the Italian leaves implicit: *Vostra apprensiva* becomes “Your apprehensive faculty”, and *lungo di sè*, ‘along themselves’, becomes “along their banks”. Conversely, “bucket” leaves out the suggestion of size in *secchione*. Two routine adjustments generally pass unremarked in the comparison: English supplies subject pronouns where Italian gives the person through the verb ending, as in *discerno* rendered “I discern”, and drops the article before a possessive, as in *il mio veder* rendered “my vision”.

Okey stays especially close where a turn of phrase can carry over without explanation. “Sweet Father dear” retains the unusual placement of the two adjectives in *dolce padre caro*, and “the soul walks with no other foot” keeps the concrete figure of *l' anima non va con altro piede*. Such correspondences are not always word-for-word: the motion in *va* becomes the more specific “walks”, but the image and its emphasis survive.
