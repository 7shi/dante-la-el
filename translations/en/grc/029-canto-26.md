# Canto XXVI

The crafty counsellors.—The moving flames.—Ulysses and Diomed.—The last voyage of Ulysses.

Rejoice, O Florence; for your renown\
Flies about over both land and sea,\
And stretches even to the depths of Hell.\
Among robbers I found five citizens\
Of yours, which wraps me in shame, 5\
Nor attaches great honour to you.\
But if morning dreams speak true,\
You shall know after the passing of no long time,\
What Prato wishes for you, and others besides.\
And if it is fated to happen, better with speed: 10\
For what is the use, if it is destined to come to pass?\
To me grown older, harder to bear is the burden.\
So we departed, and up along the steps,\
By which we had first descended the crags,

The Master climbed up and drew me up. 15\
And as we walked along the solitary path,\
Amid the crags and rocks of the bridge,\
The foot without the hand could not make good way.\
Then I grieved, and I still grieve even now\
When I reflect on what things I saw. 20\
And more than ever I curbed my mind, so that\
Without the bridle of virtue it might not run astray.\
So that if some kindly star, or something higher,\
Has given me good gifts, I may not begrudge them to myself.\
As many as the peasant resting on the hill, 25\

I stood upright, looking from the bridge\

And my Guide, seeing me gazing intently,\
Said: "Within every flame there is a spirit,\
Each wearing the flame as a garment."\
—"Master," I answered, "since you say it,\
I am wholly convinced; yet that this was so 50\
Already I thought; still I wished to ask you,\
Who is in the flame that is cleft in two at the top,\
Which is approaching, and seems to issue\

He said: "Within, Ulysses is punished 55\
And Diomed, going together thus\
In their punishment now, as while living in their hatreds.\
Within their flame they now lament\
The stratagem of the horse, which opened the gate,\
Whence went forth the noble seed of the Romans. 60\
They bewail the deceit, through which even in death\
Deidamia still blames Achilles;\
And they pay the penalty for the theft of the Palladium."—\
—"If they can from the midst of those flames\
Speak," I said, "Master, I beseech you, 65\
And know that I beseech you a thousand times,\
That you consent for us to remain here,\
Until the two-horned flame draws near.\
See how longing drives me toward it."\
He said: "Your entreaty is worthy 70\
Of much praise; therefore I grant it to you;\
But do you yourself restrain your tongue.\
Let me speak, since I have already perceived\
