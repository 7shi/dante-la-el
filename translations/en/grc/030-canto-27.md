# Canto XXVII

Continuation.—Guido da Montefeltro.

The flame, upright and no longer swaying,\
Ceased speaking, and was already departing\
With the consent of the sweet poet,\
When another coming behind it\
Drew our eyes toward its own peak 5\
By a certain confused sound issuing forth.\
As the Sicilian bull, whose first\
Bellowing was the weeping (with just reason)\
Of him who had fashioned it with the file,\
Bellowed with the sufferer's own voice, 10\
So that, although wholly of bronze, yet\
It seemed to be pierced by pain,

<!-- p. 202 (222) -->

So, having no passage of exit\
In the flame at first, when it was speaking,\
The sorrowful words were confounded. 15\
But when they found the passage clear\
Out of the peak because of the vibrations,\
Which the movement of the tongue produced,\
Then we heard: "You, toward whom I direct\
My voice, who in Lombard speech just now 20\
Said, 'Depart now, I urge you no more,'\
Even though perhaps I have come a little late,\
I pray you, stay to converse with me.\
You see that I indeed stay, even while burning.\
If you just now into this blind world 25\
Have fallen from sweet Latian land,\
That land, whence I bear all my guilt,\
Tell me: do the Romagnols peace or war\
Keep? for I was from the mountains of Urbino\
And from the ridge whence the Thymbris springs." 30\
Intent and leaning forward I was still listening,\
When on my side my Master touched me,

<!-- p. 203 (223) -->

Saying: "Speak, this man is a Latian."\
And I, having my answer ready,\
Began straightway speaking thus: 35\
"O soul, who are now hidden down below,\
Never is, nor ever was, your Romagna\
Without war in the counsel of its tyrants;\
Yet open war I left none there just now.\
Ravenna is just as it was in days of old. 40\
Brooding there, the eagle of Polenta\
Covers Ficocle with its wings;\
And the land that already endured long struggles,\
And of the French a bloody heap\
Made, still lies beneath the green talons. 45\
And the old and the young hound of Verrucchio,\
Who cruelly ill-treated Montagna,\
Bite with their teeth there, as is their wont.\
The cities by the Lamone and Vatrenus\
Are ruled by the lioncel of the white lair, 50\
Who changes his party from summer to winter;\
And the city whose flank the Sapis washes,

<!-- p. 204 (224) -->

Standing midway between mountain and plain, midway\
Between freedom and tyranny now lives.—\
Now you, tell me who you are, I pray you; 55\
Be not, I beg, harder than the others;\
So may you be of great renown in the world."\
After the flame had hissed softly a little\
In its own fashion, it shook its sharp\
Tip to and fro, breathing forth these words: 60\
"If I thought I were being questioned by a man\
Who was about to return into the world,\
This flame would stand still without shaking.\
But knowing that no one ever from this depth\
Has gone up thither, if I hear the truth, 65\
Without fear of shame I will tell you all.\
I was a warrior, and afterward a Cordelier,\
Thinking, thus girded, to make amends.\
And my hope would assuredly have been fulfilled,\
Had not the High Priest cast me back again 70\
(May he perish miserably!) into my former sins.\
And how and why, I wish you now to learn.

<!-- p. 205 (225) -->

While I had the form of bones and flesh,\
Which my mother gave me, every deed of mine\
Was not of the lion, but the works of the fox. 75\
The devices and secret ways\
I knew, all of them; and so cunningly did I contrive,\
That my fame went forth to the ends of the earth.\
But when, however, I came to that season\
Of life, where everyone ought 80\
To lower the sails and gather in the ropes,\
What pleased me before, seemed hateful to me then.\
To conversion and repentance I came;\
That would have saved me; but alas, wretched me!\
The Prince of the new Pharisees, 85\
Waging war near the Lateran,\
And not against Saracens, nor against Jews,\
(For Christians only he had as enemies,\
None of whom had hastened to defend Acre,\
Nor yet a merchant in the Sultan's land), 90\
Respected neither the high dignity\
Himself, nor the sacred orders, nor my cord,

<!-- p. 206 (226) -->

Which is wont to make lean those who are girded with it;\
But as Constantine asked Silvestro\
Within Soracte to cure his leprosy, 95\
So he asked of me instruction\
To cure his glory-mad pride.\
He sought counsel of me; but I kept silent,\
Since his words seemed those of a drunken man.\
Then he said: 'Be free from all doubt; 100\
I absolve you henceforth, if you teach me\
How I can cast down Praenestino.\
Heaven it is in my power to lock and unlock,\
As you know; therefore the keys are two,\
Which my predecessor neglected.' 105\
Then such weighty words persuaded me,\
That silence was for me worse than speech;\
So I said: 'Father, since you wash me clean\
Of the sin of which I am now about to be the doer,\
Promising much, and fulfilling little, 110\
May you triumph upon the sacred throne.'\
And when I died, Francis came

<!-- p. 207 (227) -->

To claim me, but one of the black Cherubim\
Said to him: 'Do not carry him off, do not wrong me;\
He must come down into my place; 115\
For he gave counsel craftily;\
And ever since then I have held him by the hair;\
There is no absolution for one who does not repent;\
Repenting and willing cannot coexist;\
For contradiction does not permit this.' 120\
Ah, wretched me! what trembling I felt,\
When he seized me, saying: 'Perhaps\
You did not think me to be a logician.'—\
He led me away to Minos; and he, coiling\
His tail eight times around his loins, 125\
Bit it with great fury,\
Saying: 'This one is of the concealing fire.'\
And so now here I perish, as you see,\
And suffer as I walk in this garment."\
Having thus brought its speech to an end, 130\
The flame resumed its former course,\
Twisting itself and shaking its sharp horn;

<!-- p. 208 (228) -->

And my Guide and I went onward\
Upon the crag as far as another arch,\
Covering the ditch, where those suffer who, 135\
Sowing discord, reap ruin.
