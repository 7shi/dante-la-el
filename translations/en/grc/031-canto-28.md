# Canto XXVIII

Those who stir up political discords.—Wounds and mutilations.—The Schismatics.—Curio.—Mosca.—Bertran de Born.

Who could ever tell, even in unrhythmed words,\
What streams of blood and wounds I now beheld,\
And recount them one after another?\
Assuredly it is likely every tongue must fail,\
Since power in no wise suffices 5\
For us to speak and to comprehend with the mind.\
Even if all were gathered into one place, of whom\
Upon the wretched land of Apulia\
Long ago the grievous blood was poured forth\
By the Romans, and in the long war, 10\
Where there were spoils of countless rings\
(As Livy the truth-loving writes),\
Together with all those who were grievously wounded\
While resisting Robert Guiscard

With the others, whose bones still lie heaped up 15\
In Fregellae, where a liar was every\
Apulian, and at Tagliacozzo,\
Where aged Alardo conquered without arms,\
If all their mutilated and pierced limbs\
They showed,—nothing could ever be likened 20\
To the horrors of the ninth enclosure.\
No cask that has lost a stave or bottom-piece\
Suffered such a breach as one I saw\
Cleft from the chin down to the seat,\
Having his entrails hanging upon his legs, 25\
His inward parts laid bare, and the loathsome sack\
Which turns what is swallowed into dung.\
While I was fixing my eyes upon him,\
He saw me, and opening his breast with his hands\
Said: "Behold now how I suffer being rent apart; 30\
See how maimed Arius is;\
Another walks weeping before me,\
Cleft in the face from chin to crown;\
And all the rest whom you see here,

<!-- p. 211 (231) -->

Were, while living, sowers of scandals and of schisms; 35\
And therefore they now suffer cleft in pieces.\
There is behind us yonder a demon who smites us\
So cruelly, and with the edge of the sword\
Striking each one of this company,\
When we complete the circuit of this doleful road; 40\
For our wounds close up again,\
Before we pass in front of him anew.\
But who are you, standing upon the bridge,\
And lingering perhaps to go forward to the punishment\
Imposed upon you for your deeds?" 45\
—"He is not yet dead, nor does guilt bring him\
To torment," said my Protector;\
"But that he may gain full experience of all,\
I, who am dead, deem it fitting to lead\
Him into all the circles within Hell; 50\
This is as true as that I speak to you."—\
More than a hundred then, hearing these words,\
Stood still in the ditch gazing at me,\
Through amazement forgetting their torments.

—"Tell Fra Dolcino to lay in provisions 55\
For himself—you who shall soon see the sun—\
If he does not wish speedily to follow me hither,\
Lest scarcity, when the snow comes on,\
Grant the victory to the Navarrese;\
For otherwise he cannot be saved."— 60\
Having lifted his foot to walk onward,\
Arius spoke these words to me;\
Then he stretched out his foot and departed.\
Another, having his throat pierced through,\
His nose cut off up to the eyebrows, 65\
And only one ear, stood in wonder\
Gazing intently at us with the others,\
In front of whom he opened his windpipe, which\
Was all red on the outside with blood,\
Saying: "O you, who are without guilt, 70\
Whom upon the Latian land I saw before,\
Unless a very close likeness deceives me,\
Remember Pier da Medicina,\
If ever you behold again the sweet plain,

Which slopes from Vercelli down to Marcabò. 75\
And make known to the two worthiest men of Fano,\
To Messer Guido and to Angiolello,\
That, unless foresight deceives here,\
They shall be cast headlong out of their ship,\
And drowned near Cattolica 80\
By the treachery of a faithless tyrant\
Between the islands of Cyprus and Majorca.\
Neptune never saw so great an impiety,\
Either from pirates or from men of Argolis.\
That traitor who sees with a single eye, 85\
The ruler of the land which one of those with me\
Would wish never to have known before,\
Will summon them to a parley with himself,\
And will bring it to pass that against the winds of Focara\
They will have no need of vows nor of prayer."— 90\
And I to him: "Tell me and make known to me,\
If you wish me to carry tidings of you above,\
Who is he that has bitter memory of that region."\
Then laying his hand upon the jaw

Of one of his companions, he opened his mouth 95\
Crying: "This is the very man; and he has no speech.\
Being an exile, he dispelled the doubts\
Of Caesar, declaring: 'Whoever is\
Prepared always suffers loss by waiting.'"\
Oh, how dismayed he now appeared to me, 100\
Having his tongue cut off in his throat,\
Curio, who dared to speak such words!\
Another, maimed of both his hands,\
Raising the stumps in the gloomy darkness,\
And having his face defiled with blood, 105\
Cried out: "Remember also Mosca,\
Who, alas! said, 'What is done has an end,'\
Which became a fountain of evils to the Tuscans."\
And I added: "And of death to your kindred."\
And he, heaping grief upon grief, 110\
Departed like a man maddened by suffering.\
And I, remaining and gazing intently at the throng,\
Suddenly saw a thing which I would not dare

To recount alone without proof,\
Did not my conscience embolden me, 115\
Which is a faithful companion preserving men\
Beneath the breastplate of pure feelings.\
I saw assuredly, and I seem to see it still,\
A body walking without a head, just as\
The rest of the doleful herd walk. 120\
And by the hair it held the severed head\
Swinging in its hand after the fashion of a lantern.\
And that head, looking at us, said: "Woe is me!"\
Of itself it made a torch for itself,\
And they were two in one, and moreover one in two. 125\
How this may be, only He who ordained it knows.\
And straight to the foot of the bridge it came,\
Raising its hand on high together with the head,\
So that we might hear its words close by,\
As it said: "Behold now the hideous punishment, 130\
You who are still living and visit the dead.\
See whether any other punishment is greater than this.\
But that you may carry tidings of me,

Know that I am the son of Bertran de Born,\
Who gave evil counsel to the Young King. 135\
I made father and son enemies to one another;\
Toward Absalom and David, Ahithophel\
Did no worse with treacherous counsels.\
And because I parted two so closely joined,\
Alas! I carry my brain parted 140\
From its source which is in this trunk;\
And thus I endure the law of retribution."
