# Canto XIV

The violent against God.—The burning sand and the rain of fire.—Capaneus.—The source of the four rivers of Hell.

Out of compassion for the dear fatherland,\
Having gathered the leaves that had fallen scattered about,\
I gave them back to him who was already speaking hoarsely.\
Thence we came to a place which separates\
The second enclosure from the third, 5\
Where dreadful works of justice were to be seen.\
I say, in explanation of these new\
Things, that we came unto a plain\
Forever deprived of every plant.\
This is encircled, after the manner of a garland, 10\
By the mournful wood, as that is by a mournful trench.\
We halted there on foot at the very edge;\
The ground was of dry and fine sand,\
Of no other nature than such as once

<!-- p. 101 (121) -->

The feet of Cato trod upon. 15\
O Divine Justice, how ought to fear Thee\
Every man who may ever chance to read\
The things manifested to my eyes!\
For of naked souls I saw many herds,\
All lamenting most grievously, 20\
And a different law was set for each.\
Some lay supine upon their backs, while others\
Sat huddled together, all crouching;\
And others walked about unceasingly.\
Those walking round about were more numerous, 25\
And fewer were they who lay in the torments;\
But they had a tongue more vocal in grief.\
Over all the sandy expanse slowly\
Broad flakes of flame were falling down,\
Like snowflakes in a windless calm upon the Alps. 30\
Even such as Alexander in the hot regions\
Of India saw upon his armies,\
Flames falling still kindled even to the ground,\
So that he commanded his army to trample

<!-- p. 102 (122) -->

The ground, that the fire might be extinguished 35\
More easily, while it still remained alone:\
Such was the falling of the eternal flames.\
And the sand kindled, just like tinder\
Beneath the steel, doubling the torment.\
Without ceasing, their wretched hands 40\
They shook out now on this side, now on that,\
Shaking off each fresh flame.\
And I began: "Protector, you who all things\
Have conquered, except the daring demons\
Who withstood us before the gate, 45\
Who is that mighty one, who seems not to care\
About the fire, but lies raging,\
So that the rain of flames has not softened him?"\
And he himself, as it seemed, perceiving\
That I was asking my Guide about him, 50\
Cried out: "I am now, dead, such as I was living.\
Though Jove press his own smith hard,\
From whom in wrath he took the sharp thunderbolt\
And struck me on the last day of my life;

And though in relays the black workmen 55\
He drive hard at Etna's furnace,\
As once he did in the battle of Phlegra,\
Crying out: 'Vulcan, help now, help!'\
And though he shoot at me with all his bolts,\
He would not have the gladness of vengeance." 60\
Then spoke my Guide with vehemence,\
Such as I had never heard him speaking:\
"O Capaneus, through this very unbending\
Arrogance of yours you now suffer so harshly;\
For there is no other torment, save your own rage, 65\
More worthy of your madness."\
Then turning toward me mildly,\
He said: "This was one of the seven kings\
Against Thebes, having and seeming to have\
Impious disdain toward the Deity; 70\
But, as I just told him, his own rage\
He has here now as a sufficient punishment.\
Now follow me, and take care that your feet\
You chance not to set upon the burning sand;

<!-- p. 104 (124) -->

But keep them always close to the wood." 75\
In silence we came to where there gushes forth\
Out of the forest a little stream,\
Whose redness still makes me shudder.\
And like as the stream flows from Bulicame,\
Which the harlots divide among themselves, 80\
So did this flow across the sand.\
Its bed and both its banks\
And the outer margin were wholly of stone;\
Whereby I perceived that we must cross by that way.\
"Of all other things which I have shown you, 85\
Since we entered through the gate\
Through which entry is forbidden to no one,\
Nothing has been to your eyes of such account\
As this little stream,\
Within which all flames are quenched." 90\
Such were the words of my Protector.\
And when I besought him to satisfy\
The desire which by saying these things he had awakened in me,\
"In the midst of the sea there is a wasted"

He said to me then, "country, named Crete, 95\
Under whose king the world was chaste.\
There is a mountain there, of old full\
Of waters and greenery, whose name is Ida,\
But now deserted, like an outworn thing.\
As a trusty cradle Rhea chose it 100\
For her child, and the more easily to hide him\
When weeping, she caused loud cries to be sent up.\
Within the mountain stands a great Old Man,\
Having his back turned toward Damietta,\
And looking toward Rome as in a mirror. 105\
He has his head of pure gold,\
And of silver his arms and breast;\
Then he is of bronze as far as the waist,\
And of choice iron from thence downward,\
Save the right foot, which is of clay, 110\
Upon which he leans more than on the other.\
Every part except the gold is cleft\
By a fissure, from which a tear drips,\
Which, gathering together, bores through the mountain.

And from these streams, in the valley, 115\
Are formed Acheron, Styx, and Phlegethon.\
Then going downward through a narrow channel,\
The streams as far as the depth of the abyss,\
Make Cocytus; and what that lake\
Is, you shall see; for now I will tell you nothing." 120\
And I to him: "If this stream,\
As you say, takes its source from our world,\
Why does it not appear except at this border?"\
And he to me: "You know that circular\
Is this place; and in your descent, 125\
Although you have walked far toward the left,\
You have not yet traversed the entire circle.\
Therefore, if anything new appears to you,\
It ought not to make you seem amazed thereat."\
And I again: "Protector, where is Phlegethon 130\
And Lethe? for concerning the latter you have said nothing,\
And you say from what streams the former springs."\
—"Truly your questions please me,"\
He answered; "yet the boiling of the red

Water resolves one of your doubts. 135\
You shall see Lethe, but outside this trench,\
Where the souls come to bathe themselves,\
When repentance blots out their fault."\
Then he said: "It is time to go forth from the forest;\
Let it now be your care to follow behind me. 140\
The margin is passable, since it does not burn;\
For every vapour falling upon it is quenched."
