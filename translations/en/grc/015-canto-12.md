# Canto XII

The seventh circle, having three divisions for the violent.—Minotaur.—The Centaurs.—Nessus.—Chiron.—The violent against others.—Phlegethon.

The place where we came to descend\
The bank, and the parts there, were so rugged,\
That the spectacle turned away one's eyes.\
As in the landslide on this side of Trent\
Which befell one of the flanks of the Adige 5\
Either through an earthquake, or from the earth giving way beneath,\
The rock from the mountain's summit to the plain\
Broken off, so precipitous\
Is, that from above one cannot descend,\
Such was the descent of that precipice. 10\
And upon the top of the rock lay stretched out\
The infamy of Crete, a monster, which was conceived

<!-- p. 85 (105) -->

In the shameful belly of the false cow.\
And when he saw us, he began biting himself,\
Like one whom wrath inflames within. 15\
Toward him my Sage cried out: "Perhaps\
You suppose the hero of Athens to be here,\
Who in the world gave death to you.\
Begone, beast; this man did not come hither\
According to the instruction of your sister, 20\
But he comes to behold your torments."\
Like the bull that breaks its bonds to pieces\
At the moment when it is struck mortally,\
Dizzy, leaps hither and thither,\
So I saw the Minotaur doing. 25\
Then the Protector cried out: "Cross swiftly;\
While he rages, it is good for you to descend."\
Thus we went down over heaps\
Of round stones beneath our feet\
Rolling under my weight. 30\
And I was pondering; and he said: "You ponder\
Perhaps about the rock which is guarded

<!-- p. 86 (106) -->

By the raging one, whom I just now quelled;\
I would have you know, then, that the first time\
When I descended into this depth of Hell, 35\
This crag had not yet collapsed.\
But, if I am not mistaken, shortly before he came\
Who the great spoil of the first circle\
Snatched from the hands of Dis, this cleft rock\
Was shaken on every side so violently, 40\
That I thought the universe felt\
That love through which, as the opinion is,\
The world has oftentimes been turned into chaos.\
And at that time this ancient crag\
Fell down here, and elsewhere besides. 45\
But look into the valley; for near at hand is\
The stream of blood, where those who others\
By violence harm, all boil dreadfully."\
O passion of greed, of senseless wrath,\
That goads us in this brief life, 50\
Only to plunge us eternally into blood!\
A broad, curved trench then

I saw, encircling all the plain,\
Just as my guide had foretold to me.\
And between this and the cliff, in a troop 55\
Centaurs were running to and fro, bearing bows,\
As they used to do in the world when hunting.\
And when they saw us descending, all\
Halted, and three detached themselves from the troop\
With bows and arrows ready-fitted. 60\
And one cried out from afar: "To torment\
Of what kind do you come descending thither?\
Tell from where; otherwise, I shoot the arrow."\
And my Protector said: "We will tell this\
To Chiron near at hand ourselves forthwith; 65\
An evil will you always show."\
Then he touched me, saying: "This is Nessus,\
Who died for the sake of fair Dejanira,\
And himself cruelly avenged himself.\
And he in the midst, gazing at his own breast, 70\
Is Chiron, the rearer of Achilles.\
And the other is Pholus, who was full of wrath.

<!-- p. 88 (108) -->

Around the trench they go in myriads,\
Shooting with arrows the souls that in the blood's\
Stream have transgressed the bounds of their punishment." 75\
And when we were near the swift monsters,\
Chiron took an arrow, and with the notch\
Drew back his beard behind his jaws,\
And opening wide his mouth, he says to the others:\
"Have you observed, then, how the one behind 80\
Who comes moves everything that he touches?\
Yet the feet of the dead do not act thus."\
And my Master, when he was near the breast,\
Where the two natures are joined together,\
Answered him: "Indeed, this man is alive; 85\
Yet I must show him this valley.\
Necessity constrains us to this, and not pleasure.\
Such-a-one, having left off singing alleluia,\
Imposed this new duty upon me.\
He is no robber, nor I a wicked soul. 90\
But come, by that virtue through which I direct\
My steps along this savage way,

<!-- p. 89 (109) -->

Give us one of yours, who shall guide us,\
And show where the ford of the stream is,\
And carry this man upon his back; 95\
For he is not a spirit walking through the air."\
Then Chiron turned upon his right side,\
And said to Nessus: "Turn back, guide them,\
And against other bands defend them."\
We move forward now with our trusty escort 100\
Along the brink of the crimson boiling,\
Where those who boiled cried out shrilly,\
And were plunged up to the eyebrows.\
And the Centaur said: "These bloodthirsty\
And rapacious are tyrants all, 105\
Lamenting their own pitiless deeds.\
Here is Alexander, Dionysius yonder,\
Who afflicted Sicily for a long time;\
And he who has a black-haired brow\
Is Azzolino; the other, fair-haired, 110\
Is Obizzo of Este, whom, as true report says,\
His own son murdered in the world."

<!-- p. 90 (110) -->

And the Poet, toward whom I turned, said:\
"Now let this one be first for you, and I second."\
And the Centaur looked a little further on 115\
At those who from the boiling bloody stream\
Seemed to emerge as far as the throat.\
He showed us a shade on one side alone,\
Saying: "This one in God's temple a heart\
Pierced, a man's, which is now revered upon the Thames." 120\
Then I saw others, who outside the stream\
Held their head, and besides their whole chest too;\
And I recognized many of these.\
And the boiling blood grew lower\
By degrees, until it covered the feet; 125\
And at that place we crossed over the stream.\
"As on this side you have now seen\
The stream growing ever shallower step by step,"\
Said the Centaur, "I wish you to know,\
How on the other side downward the depth 130\
Of it increases yet more until it reaches\
Where it is fitting for tyranny to groan.

<!-- p. 91 (111) -->

There Divine Justice has plunged\
Attila, who was a scourge upon the earth,\
Pyrrhus and Sextus; and there for evermore 135\
It wrings forth tears flowing into the boiling blood\
From Rinier of Corneto, from Rinier Pazzo,\
Who on the highways wrought long ravages."\
Then turning back, he crossed the ford again.
