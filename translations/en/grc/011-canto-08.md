# Canto VIII

Phlegyas.—Filippo Argenti.—The sixth circle.—The burning city of Dis.—The fallen angels.

I say, continuing, that, before we arrived\
At the foot of that high tower,\
Raising our eyes toward its summit,\
We saw two small flames shining,\
And another answering to the signal, 5\
From so far off that it was impossible to discern it.\
And turning toward the fount of all wisdom,\
I said: "What does this signify? And what does the other\
Fire reply? And who are they that do this?"\
And he said: "Upon the filthy waves 10\
What is about to happen, you can see straightway,\
Unless the smoke of the marsh conceal these things."\
Never did a bow shoot forth an arrow\
Cleaving the air so swiftly,

<!-- p. 56 (076) -->

As a small skiff, which I then saw 15\
Coming toward us upon the waves,\
Steered by one single sailor\
Crying: "Now you have come hither, wicked soul!"\
—"O Phlegyas, Phlegyas, in vain you shout\
As for the present," said my Protector; 20\
"You shall not have us except as far as across the lake."—\
Like one who learns that he has been deceived\
Cruelly, and then deeply frets,\
Such was Phlegyas, having curbed his wrath.\
And my Guide stepped down into the skiff, 25\
And then brought me in beside himself;\
And only after me was the bark laden.\
And when we had both entered within the skiff,\
The ancient ferry-boat went ploughing\
Furrows deeper than at other times. 30\
While we were speeding over the dead sea,\
There stood before us one full of mire\
Saying: "Who are you? And how come you before the hour?"\
And I to him: "I come indeed, yet I do not stay;

<!-- p. 57 (077) -->

And who are you? And how have you become so hideous?" 35\
And he answered: "You see that I am one who weeps."\
And I to him: "In anguish and weeping\
May you ever remain, O accursed spirit;\
For I know you, even though full of mire."\
Then with both hands he clung to the skiff; 40\
But the prudent Protector thrust him back,\
Saying: "Begone from here with the other dogs."\
Then, putting his arms around my neck,\
And kissing me, he said: "Noble soul,\
Blessed is the mother who conceived you! 45\
This man was an arrogant boaster in the world;\
There is no goodness adorning his memory;\
Therefore his shade is furious.\
How many up above now deem themselves kings,\
Who shall remain in this mud like swine, 50\
Leaving behind after death a dreadful disgrace!"\
And I: "Protector, I greatly longed to see\
Him plunged deep in this mire,\
Before we yet depart out of the lake."

And he to me: "You shall not cease looking 55\
Upon the shore, and you shall have the delight which you desire;\
For it is fitting that you rejoice in this desire."\
And after a little while I saw him torn to pieces\
By those wallowing in the mire,\
And God from that time forth I bless and glorify. 60\
All cried out: "Against Filippo Argenti!"\
And that furious Florentine spirit\
Turned its own teeth against itself.\
There we left him, and I shall say no more.\
But straightway a wailing struck my ears, 65\
And with intent eyes I looked ahead.\
And the good Protector said: "Now, child,\
Near to us is the city named Dis,\
Thronged with a multitude and with suffering citizens."\
And I: "Protector, I see already its mosques 70\
Glowing red within the valley,\
As if just rescued from the fire."\
And he said: "The eternal fire burns\
Within, giving them a red hue

In this lower Hell, as you see." 75\
Into deep moats we now arrived,\
Surrounding that wretched city;\
And its walls seemed to be of iron.\
And scarcely after a long circuit\
We came where the ferryman with a loud voice, 80\
"Go forth," cried out, "here is the entrance."\
I saw above the gates more than a thousand\
Who had fallen from Heaven, and furiously\
Crying out: "Who is this who, not yet having died,\
Passes through the realm of the dead?" 85\
And my Guide made a sign,\
Wishing to speak with them secretly.\
Then slightly abating their great fury,\
They said: "Come you alone; and let this one depart,\
Who has dared to come into this realm. 90\
Let him return by his own foolish path,\
By the ways he knows; but you shall remain here,\
Who led this man into the wretched region."\
Reader, imagine what distress

Words so dreadful wrought in me; 95\
For I was convinced that I should never return.\
"Dear Guide, who more than seven times\
Have afforded me protection, and from perils\
Dreadful, in which I was found, have led me forth,\
Forsake me not now," I said, "so 100\
Wretched; but if to go onward for us\
Is not possible, let us swiftly turn back."\
And the Protector who had led me thither\
Said: "Fear not; our passage to hinder\
No one is able, by the will of Such-a-one: 105\
But await me here, and have courage\
In your spirit, and nourish good hope;\
For I will not leave you within the nether world."\
Thus he departs, leaving me there,\
The sweet father; and I in doubt 110\
Remain; for Yes and No toss my mind to and fro.\
What he said to them I could not hear.\
Yet not long did he stay with them; but they\
Retreated hastily within.

And our adversaries shut the gates 115\
In the face of him who remained outside,\
And with slow step turned back toward me.\
With his eyes toward the ground, and his brows devoid\
Of cheerful bearing, he spoke with sighs:\
"Who has closed to me the woeful halls?" 120\
Then to me: "Although angered\
You see me, take courage; I shall win the struggle,\
Even if they do anything for defence within.\
This arrogance of theirs is not new;\
They showed it at a less secret door, 125\
Which still now remains without bolts.\
Above it you saw the inscription of death;\
From it already descends the steep,\
Traversing the circles without attendants,\
Such-a-one, through whom the city shall be accessible." 130
