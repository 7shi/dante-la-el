# Canto XVIII

Evil-pit (Malebolge), or the Eighth Circle, having ten divisions for the fraudulent.—The seducers of women.—The scourges.—The flatterers.—The dunghill.

A place in Hell, Evil-pit by name,\
There is, all of stone and iron-coloured,\
Just as is the circular wall around it.\
Right in the middle of the dismal plain\
There is a wide and deepest well, 5\
Whose form I shall describe elsewhere.\
Circular, then, is the remaining space\
From the well to the sheer cliff-wall;\
And its area is divided into ten valleys.\
Even as, where for the defence of walls very many 10\
Moats encircle the heights of towers,\
The part in which they lie presents a figure,

<!-- p. 132 (152) -->

Such an image did those make there.\
And as from their foundations unto the farther\
Embankment the towers have their bridges, 15\
So from the foot of the iron-coloured wall\
Crags ran across through the pits\
As far as the well, around which they united.\
Into this place, shaken off from the back\
Of Geryon, we came; and my Guide 20\
Walked on the left, and I followed.\
And on the right I saw new woes,\
New torments and new tormentors,\
With which the first pit was wholly filled.\
And at the bottom I saw the sinners naked, 25\
Some coming toward us from that side,\
And others on this side with us at greater speed,\
Just as the Romans because of the great throng\
In the year of Jubilee in two files\
Cross over the bridge, in such a way 30\
That on this side some have their faces\
Toward the castle and Saint Peter's,

<!-- p. 133 (153) -->

And those on the other side walk toward the mount.\
On this side and on that of the iron-coloured rock\
I saw horned demons with scourges 35\
Cruelly striking them from behind.\
Ah! how at the first blow they lifted their shanks,\
The wretched ones, none of them now\
Waiting for a second or a third.\
And as I walked, my gaze upon one 40\
Chanced to fall; and straightway I said:\
"Not for the first time now have I beheld him."\
Then looking intently at him I stopped;\
And my sweet Guide, doing likewise,\
Consented that I stay behind a little. 45\
Yet that scourged wretch, wishing to hide himself,\
Bowed down his face, but in vain.\
For I said to him: "Why do you bend your gaze to the ground?\
If the features of your form do not deceive me,\
You are Venedico Caccianimico. 50\
But who has brought you to such bitter brines?"\
And he to me: "It is painful for me to tell;

<!-- p. 134 (154) -->

But your clear speech urges me,\
Which reminds me of the former world.\
It was I who the fair Ghisola 55\
Persuaded to yield to the Marquis's desires,\
Even though the foul story be told otherwise;\
Nor am I the only Bolognese weeping here,\
But the place is full of so many,\
That not so many tongues have now learned to say 60\
'Sipa' between the Savena and the Reno.\
And if you wish proof or testimony of this,\
Call to mind our avarice."\
As he was saying this, a Demon struck him\
With his all-painful scourge, saying: "Begone! 65\
Here, pander, women are not for sale."\
Then I returned to where my Guide was;\
And then after a few steps we came\
Where a crag jutted forth from the bank,\
And easily we climbed it, and thence 70\
Turning to the right along the crag\
We moved away from these eternal circles.

And when we came where underneath there is a passage\
Giving a thoroughfare to the scourged wretches,\
My Guide said: "Stay and look 75\
Intently at those ill-fated souls,\
Whose face you have not yet beheld,\
Inasmuch as they were advancing together with us."\
From the ancient bridge we watched\
The file coming toward us from the other side 80\
Struck in like manner by the scourge.\
And my Guide, before I could ask him,\
Said to me: "Look at that great one, who\
Comes seeming to shed no tear of grief.\
How royal is his aspect still! 85\
This is Jason, who by daring and wit\
Carried off the golden fleece from Colchis.\
He, passing through the island of Lemnos,\
Where the bold and pitiless women\
Ruthlessly slew all the males, 90\
With wiles and honeyed words\
Deceived the young Hypsipyle,

<!-- p. 136 (156) -->

Who had before deceived all the others,\
Leaving her there pregnant and alone.\
Such a transgression brings him such 95\
Punishment, in vengeance also for Medea;\
With him goes everyone who has deceived in this way.\
We have had enough now of the first valley\
And of those whom it encloses."\
Already we were where the narrow path intersects 100\
The second valley, and makes of it\
The abutment of another bridge.\
Thence we heard souls in the other\
Pit lamenting, dreadfully snorting\
With their mouths, and beating one another with their hands. 105\
And it had its sides coated with mould,\
Issuing from the foul-smelling depth,\
Most loathsome both to sight and to smell.\
Such is its depth that from nowhere\
Can it be seen, except from the bridge 110\
Above, where the crag overhangs most.\
We came there, and thence into the pit

Bending down I saw people plunged in dung,\
That had flowed together from human privies.\
And while I was searching below with my eyes, 115\
I saw one who had a dung-caked head;\
And I was at a loss whether he was a layman or of the clergy.\
And he cried out to me: "Why do you so greedily\
Gaze at me, even more than at the other filthy ones?"\
And I to him: "Because I saw you formerly, 120\
If I remember rightly, with dry hair.\
You are Alessio Interminei of Lucca;\
Therefore I gaze intently at you more than at the others."\
Then striking his own head he said:\
"Down here the flatteries have plunged me, 125\
Of which my tongue was ever unwearied."\
And to me then my Guide said:\
"Stretch forward your eyes a little farther,\
That you may clearly see the face of that\
Filthy, dishevelled wench, 130\
Who scratches herself with dung-filled nails,\
Now crouching down, now standing upright.

<!-- p. 138 (158) -->

It is the harlot Thais, who to her paramour\
Answered when he said: 'Great indeed to me\
Are the thanks you owe?'—'Yes, and marvellous ones!'— 135\
But now our eyes have seen enough."
