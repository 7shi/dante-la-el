# Canto XXI

The barrators.—The boiling pitch.—The Evil-claws (Malebranche).

Going from bridge to bridge,\
Speaking of other things, of which my Comedy\
Cares not to sing, we ascended the summit.\
Here we stood to look upon the other\
Lamentations and the next chasm of Evil-pit, 5\
Which I beheld exceedingly dark.\
As in the shipyard of the Venetians\
Viscous pitch boils in the winter season\
For caulking the damaged ships\
And those unseaworthy; and at the same time one a new 10\
Vessel builds, while another stops up\
The ribs of that which has made many voyages;

<!-- p. 154 (174) -->

This one hammers the prow, that one the stern,\
Another hews oars, or twists ropes,\
And another patches sail-cloth and foresail: 15\
So, not by fire, but by divine art,\
Down there was boiling a thick pitch,\
Smearing on every side the surrounding bank.\
I saw it; yet I saw nothing within it,\
Save the bubbles which the boiling raised up, 20\
Swelling it and letting it sink back again.\
While I was gazing intently down below,\
My Guide, saying: "Look out, take care!"\
Drew me away from the spot where I was standing.\
Then I turned round, like one who eagerly longs 25\
To see that which it would be best for him to flee,\
Yet, seized by a sudden fear,\
While still gazing, hastens his flight.\
And behind us upon the summit I saw\
A black demon coming at a run. 30\
Alas! how savage he was in aspect!\
And how fierce he seemed to me in bearing,

<!-- p. 155 (175) -->

Having outspread wings and swift feet!\
And upon a shoulder long and angular\
He bore a sinner by the loins, 35\
And held him tightly gripped by the sinews of his feet.\
"O Evil-claws of this bridge," he said,\
"Behold one of the elders of Santa Zita.\
Thrust him beneath the depths; for I am going\
Back for others, of whom that land is full. 40\
All there are cheats, except Bonturo;\
For they turn No into Yes for the sake of money."\
And hurling him down, toward the precipitous\
Crag he turned back; and never more swiftly\
Did an unleashed hound pursue the thief. 45\
And the other, having plunged in, rose up doubled over;\
But the demons who were beneath the bridge\
Shouted: "Here there is no Sacred Image.\
Here men do not swim as in the streams of Aesar.\
If then you wish to escape our grappling-hooks, 50\
Take care not to float above the pitch."\
Then piercing him with a hundred hooks

<!-- p. 156 (176) -->

They said: "Now you must dance under cover\
And traffic in secret, if you can."\
Not otherwise the cooks to their helpers 55\
Give orders into the middle of the cauldrons\
To thrust down with flesh-hooks the floating pieces of meat.\
And the good Protector: "Lest you here,"\
He said, "be perceived present, stand down now\
Behind the rocks, so that you may be safe. 60\
Concerning any harm that might befall me,\
Do not fear; for I know all things here,\
Having met with such a struggle before."\
Then from there he crossed over the bridge;\
And when he reached the sixth bank, 65\
He needed to show a stout heart.\
With the fury and frenzy with which dogs\
Rush out upon a wandering beggar\
Who suddenly stops and asks for alms,\
Out they came from beneath the bridge, 70\
Turning all their grappling-hooks against him.\
But he cried out: "Let none of you dare!

<!-- p. 157 (177) -->

Before your hook touches me,\
Let one of you come forward here to hear me,\
And then let there be counsel about striking me." 75\
—"Go you, Evil-tail," they all shout.\
While the rest stood still, toward the Protector\
One of them came and asked: "What do you want?"—\
—"O Evil-tail, do you think you see here\
Me arrived," then said my Protector, 80\
"In safety from your weapons,\
Without divine counsel and favouring destiny?\
Let me go on; for it is willed above,\
That I should show this rugged way to someone."—\
So then was his arrogance cast down, 85\
That he let his hook fall at his feet;\
And to the others he said: "Let no one strike him."\
And my Protector to me: "O you now, who\
Sit crouching in the recesses of the bridge,\
Come back to me safely without fear." 90\
Then moving forward I came to him swiftly;\
And all the demons rushed forward against me,

<!-- p. 158 (178) -->

So that I feared they would break their pledged faith.\
Thus I saw trembling those who under truce\
Once marched out of the garrison of Caprona, 95\
When they saw themselves in the midst of enemies.\
I clung close with my whole body\
To my Protector, not turning aside\
My eyes from their suspicious aspect.\
They lowered their hooks, and—"Do you want me to strike him," 100\
They said to one another, "upon the back?"\
And they answered: "Yes, let him be pierced through."—\
But that demon, who with my Protector\
Was conversing, turning straightway said:\
"Hold still, still, Scarmiglione." 105\
Then he said to us: "Over this bridge\
It is not possible to go farther across, since the sixth\
Arch has fallen wholly into the depths.\
But if indeed you wish to go farther on,\
Go up through this cavern: 110\
Near at hand is another bridge that gives a way.\
Yesterday, five hours later than this hour,

<!-- p. 159 (179) -->

Years one thousand two hundred\
And sixty-six passed since every passage\
Here was closed. Some of my band I am sending 115\
Thither, to see if any one is cooling himself.\
Go with them; they will do no harm."\
—"Step forward now, Alichino and Calcabrina,"\
He began saying, "and you, Cagnazzo;\
Lead the band of ten, Barbariccia; 120\
Let Libicocco come and Draghignazzo,\
Tusked Ciriatto and Graffiacane,\
And Farfarello and mad Rubicante.\
Search round about the boiling pitch;\
And let these be safe as far as the bridge, 125\
Another one, which is unbroken over the chasms."—\
"Alas, Protector! what is this that I see?"\
I said: "Let us go alone without escorts;\
You know the way; I seek no others for myself.\
If, as is your wont, you are watchful, 130\
Do you not see their gnashing of teeth\
And the threats and beckonings of their eyes?"

And he to me: "I do not wish you to fear;\
Let them gnash their teeth as much as they will;\
They do this at the pitch-boiled ones." 135\
Along the left of the bank they turned;\
Yet each one first pressed between his teeth\
His tongue, as a signal toward their Decurion;\
And he used his rump like a trumpet.
