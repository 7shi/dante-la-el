# Canto XXXII

Cocytus, or the lake of ice.—Caina.—Antenora.—The Traitors.

If I had verses hoarse and harsh,\
As befits such a mournful pit,\
Upon which all the other precipices rest,\
I would declare my thought\
More fully; but since I am lacking in this, 5\
Not without fear do I now begin the tale;\
For it is no easy task or any child's play\
To describe all the bottom of the Universe\
With a tongue babbling Mamma and Pappa.\
Yet may the Maidens be helpers to me, 10\
As they were to Amphion, wall-builder of Thebes,\
So that my words may not prove inferior to the deed.—\
O accursed race above all others,\
That lies in this unspeakable place;

Better had it been for you to be sheep and goats.— 15\
Down into the gloomy well we then descended,\
Beneath the Giant's feet and further below;\
And I, still gazing at the height of the wall,\
Heard someone saying: "Watch how you walk;\
Take heed now lest with your feet you crush 20\
The heads of much-enduring brothers."\
Turning round, I saw before me and beneath\
My feet a lake, which through the frost to glass\
Rather than to water bore a likeness.\
Never did a thicker veil of winter 25\
Cover the stream of the Danube in Austria,\
Nor the Don under its icy sky,\
Than was here; for if there fell upon it\
Tabernicum, or Petra Apuana,\
Not even the edge of the sheet would have cracked. 30\
And just as the croaking frog its snout\
Holds out of the pond, when in a dream\
The peasant woman imagines she is gleaning,\
So the shades, livid and mournful,

Up to their cheeks were within the ice, 35\
Clattering their teeth like storks.\
Each one held its face turned downward;\
By the mouth the cold, and from the eyes\
The grief of the heart was testified.\
Having stretched my gaze a little round about, then 40\
Toward my feet, I saw two so close together,\
That they had their hair mingled one with another.\
"You who press your breasts together, tell me,"\
I cried out, "who are you?" Then they, bending\
Their necks, lifted up their gaze toward me; 45\
And their eyes, which before were wet only within,\
Dripped from their eyelids; and then the frost\
Congealed the tears and locked them shut again.\
Never did clamp bind wood with wood\
So violently; wherefore, like two he-goats, 50\
They clashed together; so fiercely did they rage!\
And one, who had been deprived of both his ears\
By the cold, bending his face downward\
Said: "Why do you gaze at us as in a mirror?

Then I saw a thousand faces turned livid 70\
From the cold, all of them; and a shudder brings upon me,\
And ever will bring me, the memory of this lake.\

And I was trembling in the eternal gloom, 75\
Whether it was will, or chance, or fortune,\
I know not; but advancing amidst the heads\
With my foot I struck someone's face;\
And he weeping cried out: "Why do you strike me?\
If you have not come to increase the vengeance 80\
Of Montaperti, why do you torment me?"—\
And I: "Master, wait for me now here,\
So that I may resolve my doubt concerning this one;\
And afterwards you shall hasten me on as you please."\
The Master stood still; and I then 85\
Said to him who was still bitterly reviling me:\
"Who then are you, who thus rail at others?"\
—"And who are you, walking through Antenora,\
And striking," he replied, "the cheeks of others,\
So that, even if you were alive, it would be too much?"— 90\
—"I am alive, and it may be pleasing to you,"\
I answered, "if you love fame, that\
In my songs I should make mention of your name."

And he to me: "The opposite I longed for;\
Depart from here, and hurt me no more; 95\
Ill do you know how to flatter in this pit."—\
Then I seized him by the hair of his head,\
Saying: "You must needs tell me your name;\
Otherwise, not a hair shall remain upon your head."\
And he said: "Even though you pluck out my hair, 100\
I will not tell you who I am, nor show you anything,\
Even if a thousand times you strike my head."—\
Already I had his hairs coiled in my hand,\
And not a few tufts torn away,\
While he was barking with rolling eyes, 105\
When another cried out: "What ails you, Bocca?\
Is it not enough for you to clatter your jaws\
Without barking? What Demon torments you?"\
—"Now," I answered, "I do not want you to speak any more,\
Accursed traitor; for to your shame 110\
I will bear back true tidings of you."\
