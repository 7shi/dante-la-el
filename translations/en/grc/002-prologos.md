# Prologue

Having resolved to publish in print this translation of Dante's poem on Hell, I consider it superfluous to extol in a preface the merit of the poet or to discourse on his life and his entire epic, all these things being known to everyone. As for the translation itself, I leave it to the benevolent readers to judge, maintaining only this, that it is faithful in other respects, though wholly lacking the beauty of the original.

I have used a regular Greek language, yet easily intelligible, and a paroxytone twelve-syllable metre, similar to the iambic, though lacking quantitative rhythm; which would be imperceptible to those who speak Greek today, since the ancient pronunciation of long and short syllables has, so to speak, been lost, just as this very thing has happened to the Latin language as well. At the end of the translation I have added historical and geographical notes, and in places philological ones as well, for an easier comprehension of the thought of the Poet, who is moreover formidable and delights in allegories. Most of these notes I have gleaned from those of the Englishman Frederick Pollock, distinguished among scholars, an excellent and successful translator of the poems of Dante.
