*Most Reverend Fathers in Christ, Lord* AMADEUS, *Cardinal Deacon of the most holy and universal Church, of the title of Santa Maria Nuova, commonly styled of Saluzzo; and Lord* NICHOLAS BUBWITH, *by the grace of God and of the Apostolic See Bishop of Bath and Wells; and also Lord* ROBERT HALLUM, *by the grace of the said Apostolic See Bishop of Salisbury; Brother* GIOVANNI DA SERRAVALLE, *by the like grace Bishop and Prince of Fermo, taken from the Order of Friars Minor, sends due and devout reverence, with all readiness to obey*.

Since the interpretation of discourses, or the explanation and exposition of books, and also the writing of glosses, provided it be truthful and faithful, is deservedly reckoned among the divisions of divine graces; and is rendered the more praiseworthy, commendable, and acceptable, the more useful, true, and profitable it is found to be; for this reason I might perhaps have deemed myself worthy of some praise or favour in this work written below, had there not stood in the way the loss of the sweetness, charm, and beauty of the rhymes of the book of the *Comedy* of Dante Alighieri degli Alighieri of Florence, the theologian-poet, which had perforce to be suffered in the translation to be made at your command, while the book itself was translated by me from the Italian vernacular idiom into that rough and clumsy prose, such as it is, which it pleased you and which you strictly commanded to be made thus. I think the cause of your commands was your anxiety over the shortness of the time during which we are to remain here at Constance. Considering, namely, that once the Council is ended we shall all depart hence and be separated from one another, and some of us will go to France, some to England, some to Spain, some will be scattered through Germany, and the rest will return to Italy, of whom I shall be one, under God's guidance; therefore, anxious about the extreme shortness of the time that would be required to perform such a work well, you decreed that it should be done in this way: namely, that I should hasten to compose this little work, not caring about its rustic Latinity and its unadorned and clumsy translation, which had necessarily to be made thus on account of the shortness of time; since there does not seem to be a greater space than one year, whereas at least two years would be required to translate the said book becomingly; which I would have done in literary rhymes, since the book was composed in vernacular rhymes of the Italian idiom, so that they might have sounded less ill. Let your commands and the shortness of time therefore excuse me; for I do not think it possible in so short a time for any man, at least for one so ignorant as I am, to make such a becoming translation in good and praiseworthy rhetoric, while unwilling to depart from the author's text, or to add to it, or to take away from it. For I know, and well I know, and already perceive, that I shall be defamed by rivals, and in manifold ways and by many means, with detracting words and various slanders, proclaimed everywhere as ignorant in this work, inept, and presumptuous. Yet because I learned from boyhood that the requests or entreaties of princes and lords, such as you are, hold a high rank in the class of commands, and how obedience holds the primacy among services, I therefore judged and resolved to endure with patience the words of rivals, who have never been lacking to a praiseworthy work, and to obey your biddings humbly and diligently. A great work, and one that exceeds my powers, I shall undertake, under God's guidance: first to translate, or render, the said book from the said Italian idiom into the literary language itself; then I shall proceed to the exposition of the same book, translated after a fashion by me, and to the commentary upon it, according as divine grace shall grant me, in which all my hope abides; which translation and which exposition, or commentary, I transmit to your Most Reverend Paternities, that you may deign to examine, correct, and abridge or expand, praise or blame, reject or retain it. And in these things, if—because you will find something useless, inept, and unadorned—you should marvel, let it be ascribed to me and to my ignorance. If, however, anything good, or commendable and useful, shall be found in them, to Him be praise and glory who disposed all things in weight, number, and measure. Amen.
