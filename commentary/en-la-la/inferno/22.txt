I already saw knights move the camp,
And begin a tumult, and make their muster,
And sometimes retreat to escape;
I saw riders across your land,
O Aretines, and saw bands go about,
Strike in tourneys and run at the joust,
Now with trumpets, now with bells,
With drums and signals from castles,
With things of our own and with foreign things;
Never with such varied pipes
Have I seen knights or foot soldiers move,
Nor a ship at a signal from land or star.
We went with those ten demons:
What a savage company! Yet in church
With the saints, and in the tavern with gluttons.
My attention was always fixed on the pitch,
To see every feature of the pouch
And of the people burning inside it.
As dolphins, when they signal
To sailors with the arch of their backs,
That they should try to save their ship;
So sometimes, to ease their pain,
Some sinner showed his back,
And hid it faster than lightning flashes.
And as at the edge of a ditch's water
Frogs stay with only their snouts above,
So that they hide their feet and all the rest;
So the sinners stood on every side:
But as soon as Barbariccia drew near,
At once they withdrew beneath the boiling pitch.
And I saw, and still my heart recoils,
One waiting like this, as happens
When one frog stays and another darts away.
And Graffiacane, who stood closest to him,
Hooked his pitch-matted hair
And hauled him up; he looked to me like an otter.
I already knew all their names:
So well had I noted them when they were chosen,
And after they had called one another, I marked how.
O Rubicante, make sure you set
Your claws on his back so that you flay him!
All those cursed ones shouted together.
And I said: My Master, see to it, if you can,
That you find out who this wretched man is,
Fallen into the hands of his enemies.
My leader went up beside him,
Asked where he came from, and he replied:
I was born in the kingdom of Navarre.
My mother placed me in a lord's service,
Though she had conceived me by a scoundrel
Who destroyed himself and all he owned.
Later I became a member of good King Thibault's household;
There I set about practising barratry,
For which I give account in this heat.
And Ciriatto, whose mouth showed
A tusk on each side, like a boar's,
Made him feel how one of them tore.
A mouse had fallen among wicked cats;
But Barbariccia closed him in his arms
And said: Stand back while I hook him.
Then he turned his face toward my Master:
Ask him more, he said, if there is more you wish
To learn from him before another tears him apart.
My leader said: Now then, tell me of the other guilty ones:
Do you know any Italian
Under the pitch? He replied: I parted
Not long ago from one who came from near those parts;
If only I were still covered with him,
For then I would fear neither claw nor hook.
And Libicocco: We have endured too much,
He said; and he seized his arm with his hook,
And tore away a piece as he dragged it.
Draghignazzo too reached to seize him
Down by the legs; whereupon their decurion
Turned all about with an angry look.
When they had calmed down a little,
The one still looking at his wound
My leader questioned without delay:
Who was he from whom a bad escape
You say you made to reach the bank?
And he replied: It was Friar Gomita,
The man of Gallura, a vessel of every fraud,
Who had his lord's enemies in his hands
And treated them so that each praises him:
He took their money and let them go free,
As he himself says; and in other offices too
He was no minor barrator, but supreme.
He keeps company with Don Michele Zanche
Of Logodoro; and when they speak of Sardinia,
Their tongues never grow tired.
Alas! Look at that other one baring his teeth:
I would say more, but I fear he may
Prepare to scratch my scalp.
And the great captain, turning toward Farfarello,
Who rolled his eyes to strike,
Said: Get back, you wicked bird.
If you want to see or hear
The frightened man continued at once,
Tuscans or Lombards, I will bring some here.
But let those wicked claws stand back a little,
So I need not fear their revenge;
And I, seated here in this very place,
For one such as I am, will bring seven,
When I whistle, as is our custom
To do whenever someone lets one out.
At this remark Cagnazzo raised his snout,
Shaking his head, and said: Listen to the trick
He has devised, so he can throw himself down.
But the man who had traps in great abundance
Replied: I would be far too malicious
If I sought to make my own distress worse.
Alichino could not hold back, and before
The others he said to him: If you dive,
I will not follow you at a gallop,
But will beat my wings over the pitch.
Let us leave the hill; let the bank serve as his shield,
And see whether you alone are worth more than us.
O you who read, you will hear of a new game.
Each turned his eyes toward the opposite bank,
The first, who was readiest to do this.
The Navarrese chose his moment well.
He planted his feet on the ground, and in an instant
He leapt and broke free of their plan.
At this, each was stung by guilt,
But most of all the one who caused the failure;
So he moved and shouted: You are caught!
But it did him little good, for his wings the fugitive
Could not overtake. The latter went below,
While the other flew upward, chest first.
No differently a duck at once,
When a falcon draws near, plunges down,
And the falcon rises again, angry and exhausted.
Calcabrina was angry at the trick.
Flying, he kept after him, eagerly hoping
That the man would escape, so he could have a fight.
As soon as the barrator had disappeared,
He turned his claws against his companion
And grappled with him over the ditch.
But the other was a fine griffon hawk,
Ready to claw him well, and both
Fell into the middle of the boiling pool.
The fighters suddenly found themselves in the heat;
Yet there was no chance of rising,
For their wings were smeared with pitch.
Barbariccia, grieving with his other men,
Sent four of them flying to the other bank
With all their hooks, and very swiftly
They came down, one here and one there, to their posts;
They thrust their hooks toward the pitch-coated pair,
Who were already cooked beneath the crust;
And we left them there, entangled.
