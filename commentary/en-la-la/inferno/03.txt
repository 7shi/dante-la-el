Through me one goes into the city of sorrow,
Through me one goes into eternal pain,
Through me one goes among the lost people.
Justice moved my high Maker;
Divine power made me,
Supreme wisdom and primal love.
Before me no created things existed,
Except eternal ones; and I endure eternally.
Abandon every hope, you who enter.
These words, in a dark color,
I saw written at the top of a gate;
So I: Master, their meaning is hard for me.
And he to me, like a prudent person:
Here one must abandon every doubt;
All cowardice must die here.
We have come to the place I told you of,
Where you will see the sorrowing people
Who have lost the good of the intellect.
And after he had placed his hand in mine,
With a cheerful face, which heartened me,
He led me into hidden lamentations.
There sighs, laments, and loud cries
Resounded through the starless air,
So that at the beginning I wept for them.
Diverse tongues, dreadful speech,
Words of pain, accents of anger,
Loud and hoarse voices, and the sound of hands with them,
Made a tumult that whirled
Always in that air, stained with no time,
Like sand when a whirlwind blows.
And I, whose head was encircled by error,
Said: Master, what is it that I hear?
And what people are these, who seem so bound by grief?
And he to me: This wretched condition
Is borne by the sad souls of those
Who lived without fame and without praise.
They are mingled with that wretched host
Of angels who were not rebels
And were not faithful to God, but existed for themselves.
The heavens cast them out, lest they be less beautiful;
Nor does deep Hell receive them,
For the rebel angels would gain some glory from them.
And I: Master, what is so grievous
To them that makes them lament so bitterly?
He answered: I shall tell you very briefly:
These have no hope of death,
And their blind life is so low
That they envy every other lot.
The world allows no fame of them to remain.
Mercy and justice disdain them.
Let us not speak of them, but look and pass on.
As I looked, I saw a banner
Whirling and racing so fast
That it seemed to me unworthy of any rest.
And behind it came so long a train
Of people that I would not have believed
Death had undone so many.
After I recognized some of them,
I saw and knew the shade of the one
Who, through cowardice, made the great refusal.
At once I understood and was certain
That this was the sect of wretches
Displeasing to God and to his enemies.
These wretches, who were never alive,
Were naked and stung severely
By the flies and wasps there.
They streaked their faces with blood,
Which, mixed with tears, at their feet
Was gathered by disgusting worms.
Then, when I set myself to look farther,
I saw people at the bank of a great river;
So I said: Master, now grant me
To learn who they are, and what custom
Makes them seem so eager to cross,
As I discern by the dim light.
And he to me: These things will be made clear to you
When we stop our steps
Beside the sad bank of Acheron.
Then, with eyes downcast and ashamed,
Fearing that my words might trouble him,
I drew back from speaking as far as the riverbank.
And behold, there came toward us in a boat
An old man, white with ancient hair,
Shouting: Woe to you, wicked souls!
Never hope to see Heaven;
I come to take you to the other bank,
Into eternal darkness, heat and cold.
And you, who are here, living soul,
Leave those who are dead.
But when he saw that I did not leave,
He said: By other ways and other harbors
You will come to shore, not here to cross;
A lighter vessel must carry you.
And the guide to him: Charon, do not be troubled;
He wills it so there, where he can do
Whatever he wills; ask no more.
Then grew still the shaggy cheeks
Of the ferryman of the livid marsh;
Around his eyes were wheels of flame.
But those souls, tired and naked,
Changed color and gnashed their teeth,
As soon as they heard the harsh words.
They cursed God and their parents,
The human race, the place, the time, the seed
Of their ancestry and their birth.
Then they all drew together,
Weeping bitterly, at the evil bank
That awaits everyone who does not fear God.
Charon the demon, with eyes like embers,
Signaled to them and gathered them all;
He struck with his oar anyone who lingered.
As in autumn the leaves fall,
One close after another, until the branch
Sees all its spoils upon the ground,
So the evil seed of Adam:
They cast themselves from that shore, one by one,
At a signal, like birds at their recall.
So they go over the dark wave;
And before they have landed on the other side,
A new band already gathers on this side.
My son, said the gracious master,
Those who die in God's wrath
All come here from every land,
And are eager to cross the river,
For divine justice spurs them on,
So that fear turns into desire.
No good soul has ever crossed this way;
So if Charon complains about you,
You can now well know what his words mean.
When this was finished, the dark plain
Trembled so violently that, from terror,
My mind is still bathed in sweat.
The tearful earth gave off a wind,
From which a red light flashed,
Overcoming all my senses;
And I fell like a man overcome by sleep.
