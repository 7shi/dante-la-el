Day was declining, and the dark air
Drew the animals that are on earth
From their labors; and I, all alone,
Was preparing to endure the struggle
Of the journey and of pity alike,
Which the unwavering mind will recount.
O Muses, O lofty genius, help me now;
O mind, that wrote down all that I saw,
Here your nobility will be made plain.
I began: Poet, who guides me,
Look at my strength, whether it is powerful enough,
Before you entrust me with the high passage.
You say that the father of Silvius,
Still mortal, to the immortal
Realm went, and was there in the flesh.
Therefore, if the adversary of all evil
Was gracious to him, considering the great outcome
That was to issue from him—who and what it would be—
It does not seem unworthy to a discerning man,
For he was, of holy Rome and its Empire,
In the empyrean Heaven chosen to be the father.
Which, and of what kind, if one wishes to speak the truth,
Was established as the holy place
Where the successor of great Peter sits.
Through this journey, for which you give him praise,
He learned things that were the cause
Of his victory and the papal mantle.
Then the chosen vessel went there,
To bring back strength for that faith
Which is the beginning of the way of salvation.
But why should I go there? Or who permits it?
I am neither Æneas nor Paul;
Neither I nor anyone else thinks me worthy of it.
For if I set out to go,
I fear my coming may be foolish.
If you are wise, you understand better than I can explain.
And like one who rejects what he once willed,
And changes his purpose for new thoughts,
So that he wholly abandons what he began,
So I became on that dark slope;
For, thinking it over, I spent my resolve,
Which at the outset had been so eager.
If I have rightly understood your words,
Replied that magnanimous shade,
Your soul is wounded by cowardice.
It often overwhelms a man,
So that it turns him back from an honorable undertaking,
As a false vision does a beast when it takes fright.
Therefore, so that you may free yourself from this fear,
I will tell you why I came and what I heard
At the first moment when I grieved for you.
I was among those who are suspended,
When a lady called me blessed and beautiful,
So much so that I asked her to give me her command.
Her eyes shone brighter than the stars;
And she began speaking to me gently and plainly,
In her speech, with an angelic voice:
O courteous spirit of Mantua,
Whose fame still endures in the world
And will endure as long as the world lasts,
My friend, and not a friend of Fortune,
Is hindered on the desolate slope,
Thus on his way that he has turned back out of fear.
And I fear that he may be so lost
That I have risen too late to help him,
Because of what I have heard of him in Heaven.
Now go, and with your eloquent speech
And with all that is needed for his rescue,
Help him so that I may be comforted concerning him.
I am Beatrice, who sends you on your way;
I come from the place to which I long to return;
Love moved me, and makes me speak.
When I am before my Lord,
I shall often speak well of you to Him.
Then she fell silent; afterward I began:
O lady of virtue, you alone, through whom
The human kind surpasses all that is contained
In that heaven which has its smallest circles:
Your command is so pleasing to me
That, even if I had already obeyed, it would seem too late;
I do not ask you to explain your wish to me further.
But tell me the reason why you
Do not fear to descend here below, into this center,
From the spacious place to which you burn to return.
Since you wish to know so much within,
I will tell you briefly, she answered me,
Why I am not afraid to come in here.
One must fear only those things
That have the power to harm others;
Not of the others, which are not fearful.
I was made by God, through his grace, such
That your misery does not touch me,
Nor does the flame of this fire assail me.
There is a gracious lady in Heaven who grieves
At this hindrance to which I send you,
So that there a stern judgment is broken.
She called on Lucia in her request
And said: Now your faithful one needs
You, and I commend him to you.
Lucia, enemy of every cruel person,
Moved and came to the place where I was,
Where I sat with ancient Rachel.
And she said: Beatrice, true praise of God,
Why do you not help the one who loved you so much
That for your sake he left the common crowd?
Do you not hear the pity of his weeping?
Do you not see the death that fights him
On the river where the sea has no dominion?
Never in the world were people so quick
To pursue their own good or flee their harm
As I, after these words had been spoken,
Came here from my blessed seat,
Trusting in your honorable speech,
Which honors you and those who have heard it.
After she had reasoned this with me,
She turned her shining eyes in tears;
In such a way that it made me come more swiftly.
And I came to you just as she herself wished;
I rescued you from in front of that beast
That had robbed you of the short way up the lovely mountain.
So what do you say? Why do you stop?
Why do you welcome such cowardice in your heart?
Why have you no courage and boldness?
Since these three blessed ladies
Care for you in the court of Heaven,
And my words promise you such good,
Like little flowers by the night frost
Bent and closed, when the sun brightens them
They all rise, open, upon their stems;
So I recovered, my strength exhausted;
And such good courage ran to my heart
That I began like a bold man:
O compassionate one who helped me,
And you, courteous one, who promptly obeyed
The true words she brought to you.
With desire you have filled my heart
So to go, with your words,
That I have returned to my first resolve.
Now go, for the two of us have but one will:
You the guide, you the lord, and you the master.
So I said to him; and when he had moved,
I entered upon the high and wild path.
