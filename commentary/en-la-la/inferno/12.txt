It was a place where, to descend the bank, we came,
A rugged place, because of what was there as well,
Such that every sight would shun it.
Like that landslide which struck the slope
This side of Trent, where the Adige flows,
Whether from earthquake or failing support;
Where from the mountain top, whence it broke loose,
Down to the plain the rock is so steeply broken
That it would afford a path to one above;
Such was the descent of that ravine.
And at the edge of the broken slope
The shame of Crete lay stretched out,
Who was conceived in the counterfeit cow:
And when he saw us, he bit himself,
Like one whom anger gnaws within.
My master shouted toward him: Perhaps
You think this is the duke of Athens,
Who gave you death in the world above?
Go away, beast, for this man does not come
Taught by your sister,
But comes to see your punishments.
As a bull that breaks its tether,
After it has received its mortal wound,
Can no longer walk, but leaps here and there,
So I saw the Minotaur behave;
And that wary man cried: Run to the passage;
While he is in a rage, it is best to hurry down.
So we began our way down over the broken slope
Of those stones, which often shifted
Beneath my feet under my unfamiliar weight.
I was already thinking, and he said: You think
Perhaps of that ruin guarded
By the bestial rage I have just quelled.
Now I want you to know that the other time
When I descended here into the depths of Hell,
This rock had not yet fallen.
But surely a little earlier, if I judge rightly,
Before he came who carried off the great spoil
From Dis, from the highest circle,
The foul deep valley shook on every side
So that I thought the universe felt
That love by which, as some believe,
The world has more than once turned to chaos;
And at that moment this ancient rock
Here and elsewhere made such a collapse.
But fix your eyes below, for there approaches
The river of blood, in which boils
Whoever harms another by violence.
O blind, wicked, and foolish greed,
That spurs us so through our short life,
Then so evilly immerses us in the eternal one!
I saw a broad pit curved in an arc,
Like one that embraces all the plain,
As my guide had told me;
And between the bank's foot and it, in a file,
Ran Centaurs armed with arrows,
As they used to hunt in the world.
Seeing us descend, each one stopped,
And three left the troop,
With bows and carefully chosen arrows.
And one called from afar: To what torment
Are you coming, you who descend the slope?
Tell us from there, or I will shoot.
My master said: We will answer Chiron
Up close, there beside you;
Your will was always all too quick for evil.
Then he touched me and said: That is Nessus,
Who died for the fair Dejanira
And avenged himself upon himself;
And he in the middle, who looks at his chest,
Is great Chiron, who brought up Achilles;
The other is Pholus, who was so full of rage.
Around the pit they go by thousands,
Shooting every soul that rises
Out of the blood farther than its guilt allotted.
We drew near those swift beasts:
Chiron took an arrow and with its notch
Pushed back his beard against his jaws.
When he had uncovered his great mouth,
He said to his companions: Have you noticed
That the one behind moves whatever he touches?
The feet of the dead do not usually do that.
And my good guide, who was already at his chest,
Where the two natures are joined,
Replied: He is indeed alive, and so alone
I must show him the dark valley;
Necessity brings him here, not pleasure.
One who has left the singing of Alleluia
Entrusted me with this new duty;
He is no robber, nor am I a thieving soul.
But by that power by which I move
My steps along so wild a road,
Give us one of your company, to guide us,
So that he may show us where to ford,
And carry this man on his back,
For he is no spirit who travels through the air.
Chiron turned toward his right breast
And said to Nessus: Turn back and guide them,
And make way if another troop should meet you.
Then we set out with our faithful guide
Along the edge of the boiling red stream,
Where the boiled souls raised loud cries.
I saw people submerged up to their brows;
And the great Centaur said: They are tyrants,
Who laid hands on blood and possessions.
Here the pitiless harms are bewailed;
Here is Alexander, and fierce Dionysius,
Who made Sicily endure grievous years;
And that brow with hair so black
Is Azzolino; that other, with fair hair,
Is Obizzo d'Este, who in truth
Was killed by his stepson in the world above.
Then I turned to the Poet, and he said:
Let this one go first, and I will follow.
A little farther on the Centaur stopped
Above a group of people who seemed to rise
Out of the boiling pool up to their throats.
He showed us a solitary shade off to one side,
Saying: That man split, in God's own house,
The heart that is still honored above the Thames.
Then I saw people who held their heads
And even their whole chests above the stream;
And I recognized many of them.
The blood grew shallower and shallower,
Until it scalded only their feet;
And there we crossed the pit.
Just as you see on this side
The boiling spring continually grow shallower,
The Centaur said, so I want you to believe
That on the other side its bed descends
Deeper and deeper, until it joins
The place where tyrants must groan.
Divine justice here torments
Attila, who was the scourge of the earth,
And Pyrrhus and Sextus; forever it makes them bellow
The tears the boiling blood forces out
For Rinier of Corneto and Rinier Pazzo,
Who made the roads suffer such violence.
Then he turned and crossed the ford again.
