That pallor which cowardice had painted on my face,
when I saw my guide turn back,
quickly drew in again his new color.
He stood alert, like one who listens,
for his eyes could not carry him far
through the black air and through the dense fog.
Still, we must win the fight,
he began: if not... one such has offered himself to us.
Oh, how long it seems until another arrives here!
I clearly saw how he covered over
one beginning with another that came after,
for the later words differed from the first.
Yet his words gave me fear,
because I took his broken phrase
perhaps to mean something worse than he intended.
Into this depth of the sorrowful hollow,
has anyone ever descended from the first circle,
whose sole punishment is hope cut short?
I asked this question, and he: Rarely,
does it happen to me, he said, that of us
anyone makes the journey I am taking.
It is true that once before I came down here,
summoned by that cruel Erichtho,
who called shades back to their bodies.
Not long had my flesh been stripped from me
when she made me enter within that wall,
to draw a spirit from Judas's circle.
That is the lowest and darkest place,
and farthest from the heaven that turns all things.
I know the way well, so be reassured.
This swamp, which breathes its great stench,
encircles the sorrowful city,
where we can no longer enter without anger.
And he said more, but I do not remember it,
for my eyes had drawn me wholly
toward the high tower, toward the city glowing red,
where in an instant, quickly upright, stood
three infernal Furies stained with blood,
with the limbs and bearing of women.
And they were girt with very green hydras;
they had little snakes and horned serpents for hair,
with which their fierce temples were bound.
And he, who knew well the wretched servants
of the queen of eternal lament,
said: Look at the fierce Erinnys.
That one in the left corner is Megæra;
the one who weeps on the right is Alecto;
Tisiphone is in the middle. And at that he fell silent.
Each tore at her own breast with her claws;
they beat themselves with their palms and cried so loudly
that, in fear, I pressed close to the poet.
Let Medusa come, and thus we shall turn him to stone!
they all cried, looking down:
But we did not take revenge for the attack on Theseus!
Turn back and keep your face covered;
for if the Gorgon appears and you see her,
there will be no hope of ever returning above.
So said the master, and he himself
turned me around; he did not trust my hands
without covering my eyes with his as well.
O you who have sound understanding,
consider the teaching hidden
beneath the veil of strange verses.
And now there came over the filthy waves
a crashing sound full of dread,
at which both banks trembled.
It was made no differently than by a wind
blowing fiercely through opposing fires,
striking the forest without restraint,
breaking branches, felling them, and sweeping them away.
It goes proudly before, clouded with dust,
and makes wild beasts and shepherds flee.
He freed my eyes and said: Now direct the sharpness
of your sight over that ancient foam,
toward the place where the smoke is thickest.
As frogs before their enemy,
the serpent, all scatter through the water,
until each reaches land,
I saw more than a thousand damned souls
fleeing thus before one who at a stride
crossed the Styx with his feet dry.
He was clearing the thick air from his face,
often moving his left hand before him;
and only from that distress did he seem weary.
I clearly realized that he had been sent from heaven,
I turned to my master, and he signaled
that I should be silent and bow to him.
Ah, how full of disdain he seemed to me!
He came to the gate and with a little wand
opened it, meeting no resistance.
O you expelled from heaven, despicable race,
he began on the dreadful threshold,
where does this arrogance in you take root?
Why do you resist that will
whose end can never be thwarted,
and which has so often increased your pain?
What good is it to butt your heads against fate?
Your Cerberus, if you remember well,
still bears a shorn chin and throat.
Then he turned along the foul road
and said nothing to us; but he gave the impression
of a man pressed and troubled by some other concern,
than that of the one before him.
And we set out toward the city,
made secure by those sacred words.
We entered within without a fight;
and I, who longed to see
the condition enclosed by that fortress,
as soon as I was inside, turned my eyes around;
and on every side I saw a great company,
full of sorrow and bitter torment.
As at Arles, where the Rhône forms a lake,
as at Pola near the Quarnaro,
which bounds Italy and bathes its borders,
tombs make the whole place mottled,
so they stood there on every side,
though the sight there was more bitter.
For among the tombs flames were scattered,
and they were so wholly set alight by them
that no craft asks for hotter iron.
All their lids were raised,
and such harsh lamentations came out
that they plainly seemed the cries of wretched, tormented souls.
And I said: Master, who are those people
who, buried inside those chests,
make themselves heard with sorrowful sighs?
And he said to me: Here are the founders of heresies
with their followers of every sect; far
more tombs are filled than you believe.
Like is buried here with like;
and the tombs are hotter or cooler.
And after he turned right,
we passed between the torments and the high walls.
