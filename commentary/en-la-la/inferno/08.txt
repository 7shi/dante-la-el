I say, continuing, that long before
We were at the foot of the lofty tower,
Our eyes had climbed all the way to its summit;
Because of two little flames we saw placed there,
And another far away giving a signal,
So far that the eye could scarcely make it out.
I turned to the sea of all wisdom:
What does this one, I said, say? And what does
That other fire answer? Who made them?
And he replied to me: On the filthy waves
You can already make out what is awaited,
If the marsh's smoke does not hide it from you.
No bowstring ever sent an arrow
That sped so lightly through the air,
As I saw a tiny boat
Come toward us over the water just then,
Guided by a single boatman,
Who cried: Now you are caught, wretched soul!
Phlegyas, Phlegyas, your cry is in vain,
Said my lord, for this time you will have us
Only while we cross the mire.
As one who hears of a great deception
Practised on him and then laments it,
So Phlegyas grew angry in his wrath.
My guide stepped down into the boat,
Then made me enter beside him;
Only when I was inside did it seem laden.
As soon as my guide and I were in the boat,
The ancient prow went on its way,
Cutting through the water more than it usually did.
While we sped across the dead marsh,
A figure full of mud appeared before me
And said: Who are you, coming before your time?
And I to him: If I come, I do not stay;
But who are you, so foully disfigured?
He replied: You see, I am one who weeps.
Then I to him: Stay with your weeping and grief,
Accursed spirit;
For I know you, though you are all filthy.
Then he stretched both hands toward the boat;
But the alert master pushed him away,
Saying: Go over there with the other dogs.
Then he put his arms around my neck,
Kissed my face, and said: Indignant soul,
Blessed is she who conceived you.
He was a proud person in the world;
There is no goodness that adorns his memory;
So his shade is furious here.
Many who now consider themselves great kings up there
Will stand here like pigs in muck,
Leaving behind a horrible disgrace of themselves.
Then I said: Master, I would very much like
To see him plunged into this broth
Before we leave the lake.
And he said to me: Before the prow
Lets itself be seen by you, you will be satisfied;
You should take pleasure in such a desire.
Shortly afterward, I saw the torment
The muddy people inflicted on him,
For which I still praise and thank God.
They all cried: Ah, Filippo Argenti!
That irritable Florentine spirit
Was biting himself with his own teeth.
There we left him; I tell no more of him.
But a sound struck my ears,
And I opened my eyes wide and looked ahead.
And the good master said: Now, my son,
The city called Dis is drawing near,
With its grave citizens and great host.
And I: Master, already I can see its mosques
Down there, surely, in the valley,
Red, as if they had emerged from fire.
And he said to me: The eternal fire
That kindles them within makes them appear red,
As you see in this deep Hell.
At last we reached the deep ditches
That surround that desolate land;
Its walls seemed to me to be iron.
After first making a great circuit,
We came to a place where the boatman cried out loudly:
Get out here; this is the entrance.
I saw more than a thousand above the gates,
Rained down from Heaven, who angrily
Said: Who is this who, without dying,
Goes through the kingdom of the dead?
And my wise master signalled
That he wished to speak with them in private.
Then they restrained their great disdain a little
And said: You come alone, and let that one go away
Who entered this realm so confidently.
Let him alone return by the foolish road;
Let him try, if he can; for you will stay here,
You who guided him through so dark a region.
Think, reader, how disheartened I was
At the sound of their accursed words;
I thought I would never return.
O dear guide of mine, more than seven
Times you have given me confidence and drawn me
From another danger that stood in my way;
Do not leave me so undone, I said,
And if we are forbidden to go farther,
Let us quickly retrace our steps together.
And that lord who had led me there
Said to me: Do not fear, for no one can
Take our passage from us; it is granted by One such as this.
But wait for me here; strengthen and nourish
Your weary spirit with good hope,
For I will not leave you in the lower world.
So he went away and left me there,
My sweet father, while I remained uncertain;
Yes and no contended in my mind.
I could not hear what he said to them;
But he did not stay there long,
For each of them hurried back inside.
Those adversaries shut the gates
In my lord's face, leaving him outside;
He returned to me with slow steps.
His eyes were lowered to the ground, his brows stripped
Of all boldness; and with sighs he said:
Who has denied me the sorrowful houses?
And he said to me: You, though I am angry,
Do not be dismayed, for I will win the contest,
Whatever may be gathering inside to resist us.
Their arrogance is nothing new;
They have used it before at a less secret gate,
Which still stands without a lock.
Above it you saw the dead inscription;
And already someone has come down this side of it,
Passing through the circles without a guide,
Through whom the land will be opened to us.
