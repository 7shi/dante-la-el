Thus from bridge to bridge, speaking of other things
That my Comedy does not care to sing,
We came and held the summit, when
We stopped to see another cleft
Of Malebolge, and the other vain lamentations;
And I saw it wondrously dark.
As in the Venetian arsenal
Sticky pitch boils in winter
To recaulk their unseaworthy ships,
Which cannot sail; and in their place
One builds his own new vessel, and another caulks
The sides of one that has made many voyages;
One works at the prow, and another at the stern;
Some make oars, and others twist ropes;
One patches the mizzen and the mainsail:
So, not by fire but by divine art,
Down there boiled a thick pitch,
Which made the bank sticky on every side.
I saw it, but did not yet see in it
Anything other than the bubbles the boiling raised,
And swelling and then sinking back, all compressed.
While I gazed fixedly down there,
My leader, saying, Look, look,
Pulled me toward him from where I stood.
Then I turned like someone who delays
Seeing what he must flee,
And whom sudden fear has weakened,
Who, for the sake of seeing, does not delay his flight;
And I saw behind us a black devil
Coming at a run along the crag.
Ah, how fierce he was in appearance!
And how savage he seemed to me in his manner,
With wings spread wide and light upon his feet!
On his shoulder, which was sharp and proud,
He bore a sinner across both thighs,
And he gripped the sinews of his feet.
From our bridge, he said: O Malebranche,
Here is one of the elders of Santa Zita;
Put him below, for I'll return for more
From that land, which is well supplied with them:
Everyone there is a barrator, except Bonturo;
For money, No there becomes Yes.
He threw him down there, and along the hard crag
Turned, and no mastiff ever broke loose
So fast in pursuit of a thief.
The sinner plunged in and came up again, face first;
But the demons who sheltered beneath the bridge
Shouted: The Holy Face has no place here!
Here one swims differently than in the Serchio;
So, if you do not want any of our hooks,
Do not come up above the pitch.
Then they jabbed him with more than a hundred hooks;
They said: You must dance covered over here,
So that, if you can, you may steal unseen.
Just as cooks, by their kitchen boys,
Have pushed down into the middle of a cauldron
Meat with hooks, so it does not float up,
The good Master: So that it may not appear
That you are here, crouch down below,
Behind a rock, so that no harm may befall you.
And whatever offense may be done to me,
Do not fear, for I know the situation,
Having faced such a skirmish before.
Then he crossed over the bridge's head;
But as soon as he reached the sixth bank,
He had to put on a bold face.
With the fury and the storm
With which dogs rush at a poor man
Who suddenly begs where he may stop,
Those demons came out from the little bridge
And all turned their hooks toward him.
But he cried: Let none of you be reckless.
Before your hook catches me,
Let one of you step forward to hear me,
And then decide whether to hook me.
They all cried: Let Malacoda go!
So one moved while the others stood still;
And he came to him saying: What do you want?
Do you think, Malacoda, that here you see me
Come, my master said,
Already safe from all your assaults,
Without the divine will? It is not permitted.
Let us go, for it is willed in Heaven
That I show another this wild way.
Then his arrogance fell so low
That he let his hook fall at his feet,
And said to the others: From now on, let him not be struck.
And my master to me: O you who sit
Hidden among the rocks,
Now safely come back to me.
So I moved and came to him in haste;
And all the devils came forward,
So I feared they would not keep their pact.
And so once I saw the foot soldiers fear
As they came out of Caprona on terms already agreed,
Seeing themselves among so many enemies.
I pressed my whole body
Close to my master, and did not take my eyes
From their ugly faces.
They lowered their hooks, and: Shall I touch him
On the back? one asked another;
They answered: Yes, give it to him!
But the demon who was talking
With my master quickly turned his whole body around
And said: Stand down, stand down, Scarmiglione!
Then he said to us: Going farther along this
Crag is impossible, because there lies
Completely shattered at the bottom the sixth arch.
And if you still wish to go farther,
Take this ravine up
Beside another crag that makes a way.
Yesterday, five hours later than this hour,
One thousand two hundred and sixty-six
Years were completed since the way here was broken.
I am sending some of these men of mine over there
To see again if anyone climbs out;
Go with them, for they will do you no harm.
Step forward, Alichino and Calcabrina,
He began to say, and you, Cagnazzo;
And let Barbariccia lead the ten.
Libicocco come forward, and Draghignazzo,
Tusked Ciriatto and Graffiacane,
And Farfarello, and mad Rubicante.
Search around the boiling pans;
Let these men be safe as far as the next crag,
Which runs entirely intact above the dens.
Oh me! Master, what is it I see?
I said. Come, let us go alone without an escort,
If you know the way, for I, for my part, ask for none.
If you are as wary as you usually are,
Can you not see them bare their teeth
And with their brows threaten us with pain?
And he to me: I do not want you to be afraid;
Just let them bare their teeth as they please,
For they do it on account of the wretched who suffer.
They turned left along the bank;
But first each had clamped his tongue
Between his teeth as a signal to their leader,
And he had made a trumpet of his backside.
