Rejoice, Florence, since you are so great,
That over sea and land you beat your wings,
And your name spreads through Hell.
Among the thieves I found five such men,
Your citizens, at whom I feel ashamed,
Yet you rise to no great honor through them.
But if truth is dreamed near dawn,
You will soon feel what Prato,
And others besides, wish upon you.
And if it were already so, it would not be too soon;
Would that it were, since it must surely come;
For it will grieve me more the older I grow.
We left, and up the stairways
By which the demons had made us descend before,
My master climbed again and drew me after him.
And following the lonely path
Between the broken rocks and rough crags,
My foot could not make its way without my hand.
Then I grieved, and I grieve again now,
When I turn my mind to what I saw;
And I restrain my ingenuity more than I used to,
So that it may not run unless virtue guides it;
Thus, if a good star or something better
Gave me the good, I must not begrudge it to myself.
Like a peasant resting on a hill,
At the time when he who lights the world
Shows us his face less hidden,
When the fly gives way to the gnat,
He sees glowworms in the valley,
Perhaps there where he harvests grapes or ploughs;
The whole eighth bolgia shone
With so many flames, as I perceived
As soon as I came where the bottom appeared.
And as the man who avenged himself with bears
Saw Elijah's chariot depart,
When its horses rose high to heaven;
For he could not follow it with his eyes
So as to see anything but the flame alone,
Like a little cloud, ascending upward;
So each flame moved along the hollow
Of the ditch, so that none displayed its theft,
And every flame stole away a sinner.
I stood upon the bridge, leaning out to see,
So that if I had not seized a crag,
I would have fallen down without being struck.
And my guide, who saw me so intent,
Said: The spirits are inside the fires;
Each one is wrapped in what burns him.
My master, I replied, by hearing you
I am more certain; yet I already thought
That this was so, and was about to ask you:
Who is in that fire, which seems so divided
At its summit, and seems to rise from the pyre
Where Eteocles was laid with his brother?
He replied to me: Within it are tormented
Ulysses and Diomed, and together
They go to vengeance as once they went to anger;
And within their flame is lamented
The ambush of the horse that made the gate
Through which Rome's noble seed emerged.
There the art is mourned, through which, now dead,
Deidamia still grieves for Achilles,
And there the penalty for the Palladium is borne.
If they can speak within those flames,
I said, master, I beg you greatly,
And beg again, that one request may count for a thousand,
That you do not deny me the chance to wait
Until the horned flame comes here;
See how desire bends me toward it.
And he replied: Your request deserves
Much praise, and therefore I accept it;
But see that your tongue holds back.
Let me speak, for I have understood
What you want; they might be reluctant,
Because they were Greeks, to hear your words.
When the flame had come there,
Where my guide thought the time and place were right,
I heard him speak in this way:
O you who are two within one fire,
If I earned anything from you while I lived,
If I earned much or little from you,
When I wrote my lofty verses in the world,
Do not move away; let one of you tell
Where he went, and was lost, to die.
The greater horn of the ancient flame
Began to sway, murmuring,
Like one troubled by the wind.
Then, moving its tip this way and that,
As if it were the tongue that spoke,
It sent forth its voice and said: When
I left Circe, who kept me
For more than a year there near Gaeta,
Before Æneas gave it that name,
Neither the sweetness of my son, nor pity
For my old father, nor the due love
That should have made Penelope glad,
Could overcome within me the ardor
I had to become experienced in the world,
And in human vices and worth.
But I set out upon the open sea,
Alone in one ship, with that small company
By whom I had not been abandoned.
I saw both shores as far as Spain,
As far as Morocco and the island of Sardinia,
And the others bathed by that sea.
My companions and I were old and slow
When we reached that narrow strait
Where Hercules set his boundary signs,
So that no one might sail farther;
On my right I left Seville,
And on the other side I had left Ceuta.
O brothers, I said, who through a hundred thousand
Dangers have reached the west,
For this brief remaining watch
Of your senses, do not deny yourselves
Experience of the world without people,
Beyond the sun's course.
Consider your origin: you were not made
To live like beasts,
But to pursue virtue and knowledge.
With this brief speech I made my companions
So eager for the journey
That afterward I could scarcely have held them back.
With our stern turned toward the morning,
We made wings of our oars for our mad flight,
Always gaining ground on the left.
At night I saw all the stars of the other pole,
And our own so low
That it did not rise above the sea's surface.
Five times the light below the moon
Had been rekindled and just as often extinguished,
Since we entered the deep passage,
When a dark mountain appeared to us,
Dark with distance, and seeming higher
Than any mountain I had ever seen.
We rejoiced, but soon our joy turned to weeping,
For a whirlwind rose from the new land
And struck the forepart of our ship;
Three times it made the ship whirl with all the waters;
At the fourth, it lifted the stern upward
And drove the prow down, as another willed,
Until the sea closed over us.
