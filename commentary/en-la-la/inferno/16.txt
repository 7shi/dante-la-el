I was now in a place where the rumbling could be heard
Of the water falling into the next ring,
Like the rumbling made by beehives;
When three shades broke away together,
Running from a band that was passing
Under the rain of harsh torment.
They came toward us, and each cried:
Stop, you who by your clothing seem
To be someone from our wicked land.
Alas, what wounds I saw on their limbs,
Fresh and old, seared by the flames!
It still pains me, whenever I remember them.
My master attended to their cries,
Turned his face toward me, and said: Wait,
For one must be courteous to these men;
And if it were not for the fire that shoots
The nature of this place, I would say
That haste suited you better than them.
They began again, when we had stopped,
Their ancient refrain; and when they reached us,
All three made a wheel of themselves.
As champions, naked and oiled, are wont to do,
Sizing up their grip and their advantage,
Before they strike and grapple with one another;
So, wheeling, each one directed his face
Toward me, so that his neck turned backward
While his feet kept moving forward.
And if the misery of this soft place
Makes us and our prayers contemptible,
One began, with his sad, scorched face;
Let our fame bend your mind
To tell us who you are, who with living feet
So securely tread through Hell.
This man, whose footprints you see me trampling,
Though he goes naked and stripped of hair,
Was of higher rank than you believe.
He was grandson of the noble Gualdrada:
His name was Guido Guerra, and in his life
He did much with wisdom and with the sword.
The other, who treads the sand beside me,
Is Tegghiaio Aldobrandi, whose voice
Should be welcomed in the world above.
I, who am crucified with them,
Was Jacopo Rusticucci; and indeed
My savage wife harms me more than anything.
If I had been protected from the fire,
I would have thrown myself among them below,
And I believe my master would have allowed it.
But since I would have been burned and scorched,
Fear overcame my generous wish,
Which made me eager to embrace them.
Then I began: Not contempt, but grief
For your condition pierced me within,
So that it will take long to leave me entirely,
As soon as my lord said words
That made me think to myself
That people like you would come,
I am from your land, and always
I have recalled your deeds and honored names
With affection, and listened to them.
I leave the gall and go for the sweet fruit
Promised me by my truthful guide;
But first I must descend to the center of the earth.
If your soul may long guide
Your limbs, he answered then,
And if your fame may shine after you,
Does courtesy and valor, he said, remain
In our city as they once did,
Or have they entirely departed?
For Guglielmo Borsiere, who grieves
With us only a little while ago, and goes on with his companions,
Angers us greatly with his words.
New people and sudden gains
Have bred pride and excess
In you, Florence, so that you already weep for it.
So I cried, my face raised;
And the three, who understood this as an answer,
Looked at one another as one looks at the truth.
If it costs you so little on other occasions,
They all replied, to satisfy another,
You are fortunate to speak as you please.
Therefore, if you escape these dark places
And return to see the lovely stars,
When it will please you to say, I was,
Do us the favor of speaking of us to the people.
Then they broke their circle, and their legs
Seemed wings as they hurried to flee.
An Amen could not have been said
So quickly as they vanished,
So quickly did my master depart from them.
I followed him, and we had gone a little way
When the sound of water was so near us
That we could scarcely hear one another speak.
As that river, which has its own course,
First from Monte Veso eastward,
Along the left side of the Apennines,
Which above is called Acquacheta, before
It plunges down into its low bed,
And near Forli is emptied of that name,
Rumbles there above San Benedetto
In the Alps, as it falls at a single descent,
Where it ought to provide a refuge for a thousand;
Thus, down from a steep bank,
We found that blood-dark water resounding,
So that in a short time it would have hurt our ears.
I had a cord girded around me,
And with it I had once thought
To catch the leopard with the painted hide.
After I had entirely loosened it from myself,
As my guide had commanded me,
I handed it to him knotted and wound up.
Then he turned toward the right side,
And some distance away from the bank
He cast it down into that deep ravine.
Surely something new must answer,
I said to myself, the new signal
That my master follows with his eye.
Ah, how cautious people must be
Near those who not only see the deed,
But also wisely look into one's thoughts!
He said to me: Soon what I await
And what your mind dreams of will rise above;
Soon it must be revealed to your sight.
One should always keep one's lips closed
As long as one can, about a truth that wears the face of a lie,
For it brings shame without fault;
But here I cannot be silent; by the verses
Of this Comedy, reader, I swear to you,
May they not be deprived of lasting favor,
That through that dense and dark air
I saw a figure swimming upward,
Marvelous to every unafraid heart,
Like one returning who sometimes goes down
To free an anchor caught
On a reef or something else hidden in the sea,
Who stretches upward, then draws in his feet.
