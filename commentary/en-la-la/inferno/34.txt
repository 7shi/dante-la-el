The banners of the King of Hell advance
Toward us; so look ahead,
My master said, if you can make him out.
As when a thick fog breathes forth,
Or when our hemisphere grows dark,
A mill appears far off, turning in the wind:
Such a structure seemed to me to appear there;
Then, because of the wind, I drew back
Behind my guide, for no other shelter was there.
I was already there (and with fear I put it into verse),
Where all the shades were covered,
And showed through like a straw in glass.
Some lie down, others stand upright,
One with its head up, another with its feet;
Another, like a bow, bends its feet back toward its face.
When we had advanced so far
That my master was pleased to show me
The creature that once had a beautiful appearance,
He moved away from in front of me and made me stop:
There is Dis, he said, and there is the place
Where you must arm yourself with courage.
How I became then frozen and hoarse,
Do not ask, reader, for I do not write it,
Since every word would fall short.
I did not die, and I did not remain alive:
Think now for yourself, if you have any wit,
What I became, deprived of both.
The emperor of the sorrowful realm
Rose out of the ice from mid-chest upward;
I am closer in size to a giant,
Than giants are with their arms:
Think now how large the whole must be,
To match such a part.
If he was as beautiful as he is now ugly,
And raised his brows against his Maker,
All sorrow must rightly come from him.
Oh, how great a wonder it seemed to me
When I saw three faces on his head!
One was in front, and it was red;
The other two joined this one
Above the middle of each shoulder,
And met at the crown of his head;
The right seemed to me between white and yellow;
The left looked like those
Who come from where the Nile descends into its valley.
Beneath each face came out two great wings,
As befitted so great a bird;
I never saw sails at sea like them.
They had no feathers, but were like a bat's;
He raised them upward,
So that three winds moved from him.
From them all Cocytus was frozen:
With six eyes he wept, and from three chins
Dripped tears and bloody foam.
From each mouth, with his teeth, he crushed
A sinner, like a flax-breaking tool,
So that he made three suffer in this way.
The biting was nothing
Compared with the clawing, which sometimes left the back
Completely stripped of skin.
That soul above, who suffers most,
My master said, is Judas Iscariot;
His head is inside, and his legs hang out.
Of the other two with their heads below,
The one hanging from the black face is Brutus;
See how he twists, and makes no sound.
The other is Cassius, who looks so muscular.
But night rises again, and now
We must leave, for we have seen everything.
As he wished, I clasped his neck;
And he waited for the right time and place;
When the wings were opened wide enough,
He grasped the shaggy sides;
From tuft to tuft he climbed down,
Between the thick hair and the frozen crusts.
When we had reached the place where the thigh
Turns at the hip's thickest point,
My guide, with effort and distress,
He turned his head to where he had his legs,
And grasped the fur like a man climbing,
So that I thought I was returning to Hell.
Hold tight, for by such stairs,
My master said, panting like a tired man,
One must leave every evil behind.
Then he came out through a hole in a rock
And sat me on its edge;
Then he offered me his careful step.
I raised my eyes and thought I saw
Lucifer as I had left him,
And saw his legs held upward.
Let ordinary people, who cannot see
The point I had passed, imagine
How bewildered I became then.
Get up on your feet, my master said;
The way is long and the road is hard,
And the sun is already returning to midmorning.
Where we were was no palace passage,
But a natural tunnel
With rough ground and little light.
Before I tear myself from the abyss,
My master, I said when I stood upright,
Speak a little to draw me out of error.
Where is the ice? And how is this one fixed
Upside down? And how, in so little time,
Has the sun made its passage from evening to morning?
He answered me: You still imagine
You are beyond the center, where I grasped
The fur of the evil worm that pierces the world.
You were on that side as long as I descended;
When I turned, you passed the point
Toward which weights are drawn from every side;
And now you have reached the hemisphere
Opposite the one covered by the great dry land,
Beneath whose highest point was consumed
The man who was born and lived without sin.
You have your feet upon a small sphere
That forms the other face of Judecca.
Here it is morning when there it is evening;
And this one who made us stairs with his fur
Is still fixed, just as he was before.
On this side he fell down from Heaven;
And the earth that first rose up here,
For fear of him made a veil of the sea,
And came to our hemisphere; and perhaps,
To flee from him, the part that appears here
Left this empty space and rushed upward.
There is a place down there, as far from Beelzebub
As the tomb extends,
Known not by sight but by sound,
From a little stream that descends there
Through a rock's mouth, which it has worn away
With its winding course, and flows gently downward.
My guide and I entered that hidden path
To return to the bright world,
And without concern for any rest
We climbed upward, he first and I second,
Until I saw the beautiful things
The heavens carry, through a round opening;
Then we came out again to see the stars.
