Behold the beast with the sharp tail,
That pierces mountains and breaks walls and armor;
Behold her who fouls the whole world:
Thus my guide began to speak to me,
And signaled to her to come to the bank,
Near the end of the marble steps.
And that foul image of fraud
Came forward, and brought her head and trunk to shore,
But did not draw her tail up onto the bank.
Her face was the face of a just man;
So kindly was her outer skin,
While all the rest was a serpent's body.
She had two hairy forelegs reaching to the armpits;
Her back, breast, and both flanks
Were painted with knots and little wheels.
With so many colors woven and layered
No Tartars or Turks ever made cloth,
Nor did Arachne ever weave such webs.
As sometimes boats lie by the bank,
They lie partly in water and partly on land,
And as among the German gluttons—
The beaver settles down to wage his war;
So the worst beast was standing
On the stone rim that encloses the sand.
In the empty space its whole tail kept flicking,
Twisting its venomous fork,
Which armed its point like a scorpion's sting.
My guide said: Now there must be a bend
In our path, a little way to that
Evil beast that lies over there.
So we descended along its right side
And walked ten paces on the edge,
To keep well clear of the sand and little flames.
And when we had come to her,
A little farther on, there on the sand
I saw people sitting near the edge of the pit.
Here my master: So that a full and complete
Experience of this ring you may carry away,
He told me, go, and see how they fare.
Keep your talk there brief;
While you return, I will speak with her,
So that she grants us her strong shoulders.
So, still along the outer edge
Of that seventh circle, quite alone,
I went where the sorrowful people sat.
Their grief burst out through their eyes;
They hurried their hands here and there,
Now at the flames, now at the heated ground.
No differently do dogs in summer,
Now with their snouts, now with their paws, when bitten
By fleas, flies, or horseflies.
When I turned my eyes toward some of them,
On whom the painful fire falls,
I recognized none; but I noticed
That from each one's neck hung a purse
With its own color and its own device,
And their eyes seemed to feed on it.
As I looked among them and drew near,
On a yellow purse I saw blue,
In the form and bearing of a lion.
Then, as I followed the course of my gaze,
I saw another, red as blood,
Displaying a goose as white as butter.
And one who with a large blue sow
Had his white purse marked
Said to me: What are you doing in this pit?
Now be off; and since you are still alive,
Know that my neighbor Vitaliano
Will sit here at my left side.
I am a Paduan among these Florentines;
They often make my ears ring,
Shouting: May the supreme knight come,
Who will bring a purse with three goats!
Then he twisted his face and stuck out
His tongue, like an ox licking its nose.
And I, fearing that my staying longer would vex
The one who had warned me to stay only briefly,
Turned back from those weary souls.
I found my guide already mounted
On the back of the fierce beast;
He said to me: Now be strong and bold.
From now on we descend by such steps;
Mount in front, for I want to sit in the middle,
So the tail cannot harm you.
Like someone nearing the chill
Of a quartan fever, whose nails are already pale,
Who trembles all over just seeing the first chill,
So I became at the words he spoke;
But his rebukes brought me shame,
For they make a servant brave before a good master.
I settled myself on those broad shoulders;
I wanted to say, but my voice failed
As I thought it would: Make sure you embrace me.
But he, who had helped me at other times
In other dangerous moments, as soon as I mounted,
Put his arms around me and held me up.
And he said: Geryon, move now;
Make wide circles, and descend slowly;
Remember the new burden you carry.
As a little boat leaves its place,
Backing away, so he drew back;
And when he felt himself free,
He turned his tail where his chest had been,
And stretched it out, moving like an eel,
While with his paws he gathered air toward himself.
I do not think there was greater fear
When Phaeton let go of the reins,
For the sky, as it seems, still bears the scorch marks.
Nor when wretched Icarus' back
Felt the feathers being stripped off by the heated wax,
As his father cried to him: You take the wrong path!
Than mine was, when I saw that I was
In the air on every side, and saw extinguished
All sight except that of the beast.
She went swimming very slowly;
She circled and descended, but I could not tell,
Except by the wind against my face and from below.
I already heard, on my right, the whirlpool
Making a terrible roar beneath us;
So I leaned my head and eyes down.
Then I was more afraid of the descent,
For I saw fires and heard cries;
So, trembling, I drew myself all together.
And then I saw what I had not seen before,
The descent and the circling amid the great torments
That came near from every side.
Like a falcon that has been long on the wing,
And, seeing neither lure nor bird,
Makes the falconer cry: Alas, you are coming down!
It descends, weary, from where it set out lightly,
Through a hundred circles, and settles far away
From its master, disdaining and sullen;
So Geryon set us down at the bottom,
Very close by the jagged cliff,
And, once he had unloaded us,
Vanished like an arrow from the bowstring.
