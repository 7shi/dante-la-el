If I had harsh and hoarse rhymes,
As would be fitting for the grievous pit,
Above which all the other circles rest,
I would press out the juice of my thought
More fully; but since I do not have it,
I approach the telling not without fear.
For this is no task to be taken lightly,
To write of the bottom of the whole universe,
Nor for a tongue that says mamma and papa.
But may those ladies aid my verse,
Who helped Amphion enclose Thebes,
So that my words may match the deed.
O people, more ill-created than all others,
Who dwell in a place where speech is difficult,
Better for you had you been sheep or goats.
When we were down in the dark well,
Far below, beneath the giant's feet,
I was still looking at the high wall,
I heard someone say to me: Watch how you walk;
Proceed so that you do not trample with your feet
The heads of the wretched, weary brothers.
Then I turned and saw before me,
Beneath my feet, a lake which, from the frost,
Had the appearance of glass, not water.
Never made so thick a covering for its course
In winter the Danube in Austria,
Nor did the Tanais there beneath the cold sky,
As it was here: for if Tambernic
Had fallen upon it, or Pietrapana,
It would not even have made a crack.
And as a frog sits croaking
With its snout out of the water, when there dreams
Of gleaning ears of corn often the countrywoman;
Livid, down to where shame appears,
The sorrowing shades were in the ice,
Their teeth chattering like a stork's.
Each held its face turned downward;
The cold from their mouths and the sorrow of their hearts from their eyes
Bear witness among them.
When I had looked around for a while,
I turned toward my feet and saw two so tightly joined
That the hair of their heads was mingled together.
Tell me, you who thus press your breasts together,
I said; who are you? And they bowed their necks;
And after they had raised their faces toward me,
Their eyes, once soft within,
First trickled over their lips, and the frost sealed
Their tears between the lids and closed them in.
Never did a clamp bind wood to wood
So tightly; then they, like two he-goats,
Butted one another: such anger overcame them.
And one who had lost both ears
To the cold, then with his face down,
Said: Why do you stare so intently at us?
If you wish to know who these two are,
The valley from which the Bisenzio descends
Belonged to their father Alberto and to them.
They came from one body; and all Caïna
You may search, and you will find no shade
More deserving of being fixed in the ice-jelly.
Not the one whose breast and shadow were pierced
With one blow by Arthur's hand;
Not Focaccia; nor this man who blocks my way,
With his head so that I can see no farther,
And was called Sassol Mascheroni.
If you are Tuscan, you now know well who he was.
And so that you need not ask me more,
Know that I was Camicion de' Pazzi,
And I await Carlino, who will excuse me.
Afterward I saw a thousand faces, dog-like,
Made so by the cold; and I shudder
And shall always shudder at frozen pools.
And as we went toward the centre,
To which all weight is drawn,
And I trembled in the everlasting shade;
whether it was by will, destiny, or fortune,
I do not know; but, walking among the heads,
I struck one of them hard in the face with my foot.
Weeping, he cried out to me: Why do you trample me?
If you do not come to increase the vengeance
For Montaperti, why do you trouble me?
And I: Master, now wait here for me,
Just long enough for me to resolve a doubt through this man;
Afterward you may hurry me as much as you wish.
The guide stopped; and I said to the one
Who was still cursing fiercely:
Who are you, to reproach others so?
Now who are you, going through Antenora
Striking other people's faces, he replied,
so that, if you were alive, it would be too much?
I am alive, and it may be dear to you,
was my reply, if you seek fame,
that I place your name among the other notes.
And he to me: I desire the opposite.
Get away from here, and do not trouble me further;
For you do not know how to flatter in this pit.
Then I seized him by the nape
And said: You will have to name yourself,
Or not a hair will remain here on your head.
And he to me: Though you tear out my hair,
I will not tell you who I am or show it to you,
Even if you tumble onto my head a thousand times.
I already had his hair wound around my hand
And had pulled more than one lock from him,
While he barked with his eyes cast downward;
when another cried: What is the matter with you, Bocca?
Is it not enough for you to chatter your jaws
Without barking too? What devil is troubling you?
From now on, I said, I do not want you to speak,
Wicked traitor, for to your shame
I shall carry true news about you.
Go on, he replied, and tell whatever you wish;
But when you leave here, do not be silent
About the one who just now had such a ready tongue.
He is weeping for the Frenchmen's silver.
You may say: I saw that man from Duera
There where the sinners stand fresh.
If you ask who else was there,
You have beside you that man of Beccaria,
Whose throat Florence cut.
I believe Gianni del Soldanier is
Farther on with Ganellone and Tribaldello,
Who opened Faenza while its people slept.
We had already left him,
When I saw two frozen in one hole,
So that one head was a cap over the other;
and as bread is eaten from hunger,
So the one set his teeth on the other
Where the brain joins the nape of the neck.
No more fiercely did Tydeus gnaw
The temples of Menalippus in anger
Than this one gnawed the skull and what lay within.
O you, who show by so bestial a sign
Hatred for the one you are eating,
Tell me why, I said, under this agreement,
for if you rightly grieve over him,
Knowing who you both are and his sin,
In the upper world I may yet repay you,
If that with which I speak does not dry up.
