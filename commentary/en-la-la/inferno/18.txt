The place in Hell called Malebolge,
All of stone and iron-gray in color,
Like the circuit that turns around it.
In the very middle of the evil field
There turns a pit, very wide and deep,
Whose structure I shall describe in its place.
The circle that thus remains, rounded,
Between the pit and the foot of the hard high bank,
Was divided at its bottom into ten valleys.
As, for the defense of walls,
More and more ditches surround castles,
On the side where they are less secure;
Such an image those valleys made there:
And, as from the thresholds of such fortresses
Little bridges lead out to the outer bank,
So, from the base of the bank, rocky ridges
Stretched across the ramparts and ditches
To the pit that cuts them off and gathers them together.
At this place, shaken from the back
Of Geryon, we found ourselves; and the Poet
Kept to the left, and I followed behind.
On the right I saw a new kind of suffering:
Newly scourged souls and new scourgers,
With whom the first bolgia was filled.
At the bottom were naked sinners:
Those on this side came toward us,
Those on the other went with us, but at quicker pace.
As the Romans, because of the great crowds,
In the Jubilee year, on the bridge,
Devised a way to have people cross:
So that on one side all face
Toward the Castle and go to St. Peter's;
On the other bank they go toward the hill.
Here and there, along the gloomy stone,
I saw horned demons with great whips,
Cruelly striking them from behind.
Ah, how they made them lift their heels
At the first blows! No one now
Waited for a second or a third.
As I went, my eyes met those
Of one man; at once I said:
I am not new to seeing this man.
To make sure, I planted my feet;
And my gentle guide stopped,
And agreed that I should go back a little.
And that scourged soul thought to hide himself
By lowering his face, but it did him little good;
For I said: You who cast your eyes to the ground,
If the features you bear are not false,
You are Venedico Caccianemico;
But what brings you to these stinging Salse?
And he said to me: I am loath to say it;
But your clear speech compels me,
For it makes me remember the world I knew.
I am the one who led Ghisola
To do the Marquis's will,
As the shameful story tells.
And I am not the only Bolognese weeping here:
This place is so full of us
That there are not as many tongues now ready
To say sipa between Savena and Reno.
If you want proof or witness of this,
Remember our greedy nature.
As he spoke, a demon struck him
With his lash and said: Move on,
Pimp! There are no women here to buy.
I rejoined my guide;
After a few steps we came
To where a ridge jutted out from the bank.
We climbed it quite easily,
And, turning right, went up its rocky ledge;
We left those eternal circles behind.
When we reached the place where the narrow path
Joins the second bank, to make way below
For the scourged, my guide said: Wait, and fix
Your gaze on these other wretches,
Whose faces you have not yet seen,
Though they have been traveling along with us.
I was watching the line from the old bridge
Coming toward us from the other side,
Driven along by the whip as well.
The good Master, without my asking,
Said to me: Look at that tall man coming;
His pain does not seem to bring him to tears.
How much of a kingly bearing he still retains!
That is Jason, who by courage and good sense
Robbed the Colchians of the ram's fleece.
He passed through the island of Lemnos
After its bold and pitiless women
Had put all their men to death.
There with signs and ornate words
He deceived Hypsipyle, the young girl,
Who had first deceived all the others.
He left her there, pregnant and alone:
Such a fault condemns him to such torment;
And vengeance is also taken for Medea.
With him goes whoever deceives in that way:
This is enough to know about the first valley
And those who suffer within it.
We were already where the narrow path
Joins the second bank,
And makes it a support for another arch.
There we heard people groaning
In the next bolgia, snorting through their snouts,
And striking themselves with their palms.
The banks were crusted with a mold
From the breath below that settled on them,
Which assailed both eyes and nose.
The bottom is so deep that our eyes
Could not see it without climbing onto the back
Of the arch, where the ridge stands highest.
We came there, and down in the ditch
I saw people submerged in excrement
That seemed to have come from human privies.
And while I searched down there with my eyes,
I saw one with his head so caked in shit
That I could not tell if he was layman or cleric.
He shouted at me: Why are you so eager
To look at me more than at the other filthy ones?
And I said to him: Because, if I remember rightly,
I have seen you before with dry hair,
And you are Alessio Interminei of Lucca;
That is why I look at you more than all the others.
Then he, striking his own head, said:
The flatteries have submerged me down here,
For my tongue was never tired of them.
After this, my guide said: Make your gaze
Reach a little farther ahead,
So that your eyes clearly take in the face
Of that foul, disheveled servant,
Who scratches herself with shit-covered nails,
Now crouching, now standing on her feet.
Thais is the whore who replied
To her lover when he asked: Do I have great favor
With you? Indeed, an astonishing amount.
And let our eyes be satisfied with this.
