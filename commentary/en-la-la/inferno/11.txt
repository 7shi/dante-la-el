Above the edge of a high bank,
Made of great stones broken into a circle,
We came upon a crueler enclosure:
And there, because of the dreadful excess
Of the stench cast up by the deep abyss,
We drew back close behind a cover.
Of a great tomb, where I saw an inscription
That said: I guard Pope Anastasius,
Whom Photinus drew from the right path.
Our descent must be slow,
So that our senses first grow a little accustomed
To the foul air, and then it will no longer trouble us.
Thus spoke the master. And I: Find some diversion,
I said to him, so that time does not pass
Wasted. And he: See, I am thinking of that.
My son, behind those rocks,
He then began to say, there are three little circles,
Descending tier by tier, like those you leave behind.
They are all full of accursed spirits;
But, so that your sight may later suffice,
Understand how and why they are confined.
Every wickedness that earns hatred in Heaven
Does injury to its end, and every such end
Grieves another by force or by fraud.
But since fraud is man's own evil,
It displeases God more; therefore the fraudulent stand lower,
And greater pain assails them.
The first circle is wholly for the violent;
But since violence is done to three kinds of persons,
It is divided and arranged into three rings.
Violence can be done to God, oneself, or one's neighbor,
I mean to them and to what belongs to them,
As you will hear with clear explanation.
Violent death and painful wounds
Are inflicted on one's neighbor, and on his property
Ruin, fires, and harmful levies.
Hatred, murder, and everyone who wounds evilly,
Devastators and robbers: all are tormented
In the first ring, in separate companies.
A man can turn a violent hand against himself
And against his possessions; therefore in the second
Ring he must repent to no avail,
Whoever deprives himself of your world,
Squanders and dissipates his wealth,
And weeps where he ought to be joyful.
Violence can be done to the Godhead
By denying and blaspheming it in one's heart,
And by scorning nature and its goodness.
Therefore the smallest ring stamps
Its mark on Sodom and Cahors,
And on whoever speaks in his heart, scorning God.
Fraud, by which every conscience is stung,
A man can practice against one who trusts him,
And against one who places no trust in him.
This second way seems to destroy
Only the bond of love created by nature;
Therefore it nests in the second circle.
Hypocrisy, flattery, and sorcery,
Forgery, theft, and simony,
Pimps, barrators, and similar filth.
Through this way the other love is forgotten,
The love created by nature, and that which is added later,
From which special faith is formed.
Therefore in the smallest circle, where the point
Of the universe lies, on which Dis sits,
Whoever betrays is consumed forever.
And I: Master, your reasoning proceeds
Quite clearly, and distinguishes very well
This pit and the people it contains.
But tell me: those in the foul marsh,
Whom the wind drives and the rain beats,
And those who clash with such harsh tongues,
Why are they not punished inside the red city,
If God is angry with them?
And if He is not, why are they treated this way?
And he said to me: Why does your intellect,
He said, stray so far from its usual course?
Or where else is your mind looking?
Do you not remember those words
In which your Ethics treats
The three dispositions Heaven rejects:
Incontinence, malice, and foolish
Bestiality? And how incontinence
Offends God less and earns less blame?
If you consider this teaching carefully,
And recall to mind who those are
Who endure punishment above, outside,
You will clearly see why they are separated
From these wicked ones, and why divine justice,
Less angered, torments them less.
O sun who heals every troubled sight,
You make me so content when you resolve my doubts
That I delight in doubting no less than in knowing.
Turn back a little farther,
I said, to where you say that usury offends
The divine goodness, and untie the knot.
Philosophy, he said to me, for one who understands it,
Shows, in more than one place,
How nature takes its course
From the divine intellect and its art;
And if you study your Physics carefully,
You will find, after only a few pages,
That your art, as far as it can,
Follows nature, as a pupil follows his master,
So that your art is almost God's granddaughter.
From these things, if you recall
The beginning of Genesis, you must derive
Your life and multiply your people.
But since the usurer follows another path,
He scorns nature in itself and its follower,
For he puts his hope in something else.
But follow me now, for I wish to go:
The Fishes are leaping above the horizon,
And the whole Wain lies above the South Wind,
And the ledge farther on descends.
