One and the same tongue first bit me,
So that it stained both my cheeks,
And then offered me the remedy.
So I hear that the spear was wont to do,
That of Achilles and his father: to be the cause
First of a bitter, then of a welcome gift.
We turned our backs on the wretched valley,
Up along the bank that circles it,
Crossing without a word.
There it was less than night and less than day,
So that my sight reached only a little way ahead;
But I heard a loud horn sound,
So loud it would have made any thunder faint,
And I, following its course against itself,
Direct all my eyes to one place.
After the painful rout, when
Charlemain lost the holy host,
Roland did not sound so terribly.
I had held my head turned that way only a little while
When I seemed to see many tall towers;
So I said: Master, tell me, what land is this?
And he: Because you look
Through the darkness from too far away,
It happens that your imagination then goes astray.
You will see clearly, when you draw near that place,
How much the senses are deceived at a distance;
So spur yourself a little more.
Then he kindly took me by the hand
And said: Before we go farther,
So that what you see may seem less strange to you,
Know that these are not towers, but Giants,
And they stand in the pit around its rim,
All of them from the navel down.
As, when the mist disperses,
The eye little by little makes out
What the vapor hides and envelops,
So, piercing the thick, dark air
And drawing nearer and nearer to the bank,
My error fled, and my fear grew.
For just as on its round circuit
Montereggione is crowned with towers,
So on the rim that circles the pit
There towered from the waist up
The horrid Giants, to whom threats are made
By Jove from heaven still, whenever it thunders.
And I could already make out one face,
His shoulders and chest, and a great part of his belly,
And both arms hanging down along his sides.
Nature certainly, when she ceased the making
Of such creatures, did quite well,
To take such agents away from Mars;
And if, as for elephants and whales, she
Felt no regret, one who looks closely
Will judge her more just and more wise:
For where the mind's capacity
Is joined to evil will and strength,
No people there can provide a defense.
His face seemed long and massive to me,
Like the pinecone of Saint Peter's in Rome;
And the other bones were in proportion to it.
So that the bank, which was his apron
From the waist down, showed us fully so much
Above it that, to reach his hair,
Three Frisians would scarcely have sufficed;
For I saw thirty great palms
Down from the place where a man fastens his cloak.
Raphel malbi ameth zabi almy,
The savage mouth began to shout,
For whom no sweeter hymns were fitting.
And my guide said to him: Foolish soul,
Keep to your horn, and vent yourself with it
Whenever anger or another passion touches you.
Search your neck and you will find the cord
That keeps you bound, confused soul;
And look at it: it adorns your great chest.
Then he said to me: He himself accuses himself.
This is Nimrod, by whose evil purpose
The world does not use just one language.
Let us leave him there and not speak in vain;
For every language is so to him
As his is to others, known to no one.
So we made a longer journey,
Turning to the left; and within a crossbow's range
We found another, much fiercer and larger.
Whoever the master was that bound him,
I cannot say; but he held tied
In front one arm, and behind him the right arm,
With one chain that held him bound
From his neck down, so that on the exposed part
It wound around him to the fifth coil.
This proud one wished to test
His strength against highest Jove,
My guide said, and this is his just reward.
His name is Ephialtes; he performed great deeds
When the Giants made the gods afraid;
The arms he once wielded he never moves.
And I said to him: If it can be, I would like
That of the immense Briareus
My eyes might have experience.
At that he answered: You will see Antæus
Near here. He can speak and is unbound;
He will place us at the bottom of all evil.
The one you wish to see is much farther on,
And is bound and made like this one,
Except that he looks fiercer in the face.
Never was an earthquake so violent
As to shake a tower so forcefully
As Ephialtes was so quick to shake himself.
Then I feared death more than ever,
And no more was needed there than a moment,
Had I not seen the twisted chains.
We then proceeded farther on,
And came to Antæus, who a full five ells
Rose out of the cave without his head.
O you, who in the fortunate valley
That made Scipio heir to glory,
When Hannibal and his men turned their backs,
Once carried off a thousand lions as prey,
And if you had been in the great war
Of your brothers, it still seems believable
That the sons of Earth would have won;
Set us down (do not disdain to do so)
Where Cocytus is locked in ice.
Do not send us to Tityos or Typhon;
This one can give some of what is desired here.
So bend down, and do not turn your face away.
He can still restore your fame in the world,
For he lives and still expects a long life,
Unless grace calls him to itself before his time.
So my guide spoke, and that one at once
Stretched out his hand and seized my guide,
By whom Hercules felt a powerful grip.
When Virgil felt himself being seized,
He said to me: Come close, so I can hold you.
Then he did so, and he and I became one burden.
As the Carisenda seems to one who looks
From beneath its leaning side, when a cloud passes
Above it, so that it seems to lean the other way,
So Antæus seemed to me as I stood watching
To see him bend; and there was such an hour
When I would have wished to take another road.
But gently, at the bottom that devours
Lucifer with Judas, he set us down;
Nor did he linger thus bent there,
But rose like a mast in a ship.
