Now one of the hard banks carries us,
And the stream's smoke above casts its shade,
So that it saves the water and the banks from the fire.
As the Flemings between Wissant and Bruges,
Fearing the tide that surges toward them,
Make their barrier to drive the sea back;
And as the Paduans along the Brenta,
To defend their villages and their castles,
Before Chiarentana feels the heat;
In that likeness those banks had been made,
Though neither so high nor so thick;
Whatever the master was who made them.
We had already moved from the wood
So far that I could not have seen where it was,
Even if I had turned back;
When we encountered a band of souls,
Who came along near the bank, and each one
Looked at us, as is usual at evening
For one to look at another beneath the new moon;
And so they sharpened their brows toward us,
As an old tailor does at the eye of a needle.
So scrutinized by such a company,
I was recognized by one, who seized me
By the hem and cried: What a marvel!
And I, when he stretched his arm toward me,
Fixed my eyes on his scorched face,
So that his burned appearance did not hide
His identity from my understanding;
And, lowering my face to his,
I replied: Are you here, Ser Brunetto?
And he to me: O my son, do not disdain
Ser Brunetto Latini a little with you:
Turn back and let the line go on.
I said to him: As far as I can, I beg you;
And if you wish me to sit with you,
I shall, if this one approves, for I go with him.
O son, he said, whoever in this flock
Stops, thereafter lies for a hundred years
Without shielding himself when the fire strikes him.
So go on; I will come at your hem,
And then rejoin my company,
Which goes lamenting its eternal losses.
I did not dare to descend from the path
To walk level with him; but my head bowed
I kept, as a reverent man would walk.
He began: What fortune or destiny
Brings you here before your final day?
And who is this who shows you the way?
Up there, in the serene life,
I replied to him, I lost my way in a valley
Before my life had reached its full span.
Only yesterday morning I turned my back on it;
This one appeared to me as I was returning to it,
And is leading me home along this path.
And he said to me: If you follow your star,
You cannot fail to reach the glorious harbor,
If I read you rightly in the fair life;
And if I had not died so soon,
Seeing Heaven so favorable to you,
I would have given you encouragement in your work.
But that ungrateful, wicked people,
Who long ago came down from Fiesole,
And still retain something of the mountain and rock,
Will become your enemy for your good deeds;
And rightly so, for among bitter sorb trees
It is unfitting to cultivate the sweet fig.
An old saying in the world calls them blind,
A people greedy, envious, and proud:
See that you keep yourself clean of their ways.
Your fortune preserves such great honor for you
That both factions will hunger
For you: but the grass will be far from their beaks.
Let the Fiesolan beasts make straw
Of one another, and not touch the plant,
If any still sprouts in their dung,
In which the holy seed may revive
Of those Romans who remained there, when
The nest of such wickedness was made.
If my request were wholly fulfilled,
I replied to him, you would not yet have been
Cast out from human nature;
For fixed in my mind is, and now it grieves me,
The dear, good paternal image
Of you, when in the world, from time to time,
You taught me how a man makes himself eternal;
And how grateful I held that teaching, while I live,
Must be clear in my words.
Whatever you tell me of my course, I write down,
And keep it to be explained with another text
By a lady who will know, if I reach her.
This much I wish you to know,
Provided my conscience does not reproach me,
That I am ready for fortune, however she wills.
Such a pledge is not new to my ears:
So let Fortune turn her wheel
As she pleases, and the peasant his hoe.
My master then over his cheek,
The right one, turned back and looked at me;
Then he said: He listens well who takes note of it.
Nevertheless, as I went on speaking, I walked
With Ser Brunetto, and asked who were
His best-known and most eminent companions.
And he said to me: It is good to know some;
Of the others it will be praiseworthy for us to say nothing,
For there would be too little time for so much speaking.
In short, know that they were all clerics,
And great scholars and of great fame,
Defiled in the world by one and the same sin.
Priscian goes with that wretched band,
And Francesco d'Accorso too; and see there,
If you had wished for such a thing,
You could, the one who by the Servant of Servants
Was transferred from the Arno to the Bacchiglione,
Where he left his badly stretched sinews.
I would speak of more of them, but my coming and speech
Can last no longer, for I see
New smoke already rising from the sand.
People are coming with whom I must not be.
I commend my Treasury to you,
In which I still live; and I ask no more.
Then he turned away, and seemed like one of those
Who race at Verona for the green cloth
Across the fields; and he seemed among them
The winner, not the loser.
