Now he goes along a secret path
Between the walls of the city and the torments
My master, and I behind him.
O supreme power, who through impious circles
Turn me as you please, I began,
Speak to me, and satisfy my desires.
The people lying in the tombs,
Can they be seen? Their lids have all been lifted,
And no one keeps watch.
And he said to me: They will all be shut
When they return here from Jehosaphat
With the bodies they left above.
On this side they have their cemetery
With Epicurus, all his followers,
Who make the soul die with the body.
Therefore your question
Will soon be answered here within,
And the desire you still keep silent.
And I: Good guide, I do not keep
My heart concealed from you, except to speak little;
And you have trained me to this more than just now.
O Tuscan, who go alive through the city of fire,
Speaking so nobly,
Please stop here for a while.
Your speech makes it clear
That you are a native of that noble homeland
To which perhaps I was too troublesome.
Suddenly this voice came
From one of the tombs; so I drew
A little closer, in fear, to my guide.
And he said to me: Turn around. What are you doing?
Look there at Farinata, who has risen;
From the waist up you will see all of him.
I had already fixed my gaze on his;
And he rose with chest and brow,
As if he held Hell in great contempt.
My guide's eager, ready hands
Pushed me between the tombs toward him,
Saying: Let your words be fitting.
As soon as I was at the foot of his tomb,
He looked at me a little, then, almost disdainfully,
Asked me: Who were your ancestors?
I, eager to obey,
Did not hide it from him, but disclosed it all;
Whereupon he raised his brows a little.
Then he said: They were fiercely opposed
To me, my forebears, and my party,
So that twice I drove them out.
If they were driven out, they returned from every side,
I answered him, both times;
But yours never learned that art.
Then, beside the uncovered tomb,
A shade rose up near this one, as far as the chin;
I think he had risen on his knees.
He looked around me as if he wanted
To see whether someone else was with me;
But when his suspicion had wholly faded,
He said in tears: If through this blind
Prison you travel by the power of genius,
Where is my son? Why is he not with you?
And I said to him: I do not come by myself;
The one waiting there leads me through this place,
Perhaps the one your Guido held in disdain.
His words and the kind of punishment
Had already revealed his name to me;
That is why my answer was so full.
Suddenly he sprang upright and cried: What
Did you say: He had? Is he not still alive?
Does the sweet light not strike his eyes?
When he realized I was hesitating
Before answering,
He fell back supine and appeared no more.
But that other noble spirit, at whose request
I had stopped, did not change his expression,
Nor move his neck, nor bend his side.
And, continuing his first remark,
If they learned that art badly, he said,
That torments me more than this bed.
But before the face of the lady who rules here
Has been rekindled fifty times,
You will learn how heavy that art is.
And if you ever return to the sweet world,
Tell me: Why is that people so hostile
To mine in every law it makes?
And I said to him: The slaughter and great carnage
That made the Arbia run red
Cause such prayers to be made in our temple.
After he had sighed and shaken his head,
He said: I was not alone in that, nor certainly
Would I have joined the others without cause;
But I alone was there, when it was agreed
By all that Florence should be wiped away,
Who defended her with open face.
Ah, if your descendants ever find rest,
I begged him, untie that knot for me
That has entangled my thought here.
It seems, if I hear you rightly, that you see
What present time brings with it,
But have another way of knowing the present.
We see, he said, like those with poor sight,
The things that are far from us;
The supreme Leader still grants us so much.
When they draw near or are here, all our
Understanding is useless; if someone else does not tell us,
We know nothing of your human state.
Therefore you can understand that all our knowledge
Will be utterly dead from the moment
When the door to the future is closed.
Then, stung by guilt,
I said: Now tell that fallen one
That his son is still among the living.
And if I was mute before your answer,
Tell him I was so because I was thinking
About the question you have resolved for me.
My master was already calling me back;
So I asked the spirit to tell me more quickly
Who was lying there with him.
He said to me: Here I lie with more than a thousand.
Here within is the second Frederick,
And the Cardinal; I will say no more of the others.
Then he hid himself, and I turned my steps
Toward the ancient poet, thinking
About that speech, which seemed hostile to me.
He set out; and as we went along,
He said: Why are you so bewildered?
And I answered his question.
Let your mind keep what you have heard
Against you, that wise one began,
And now wait here; and he pointed upward.
When you stand before the sweet ray
Of her whose beautiful eye sees all,
From her you will learn the path of your life.
Then he turned his foot to the left,
We left the wall and went toward the center
Along a path that cut through a valley,
Whose stench made it loathsome even up there.
