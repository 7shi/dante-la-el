A marvel, Satan, a marvel, Satan, Aleph,
Pluto began with a hoarse voice.
And that noble sage who knew all things,
He said to comfort me: May it not harm you,
Your fear, for whatever power he has
Will not prevent us from descending this crag.
Then he turned toward those swollen lips
And said: Be silent, accursed wolf;
Consume yourself within with your rage.
This journey to the depths is not without cause:
Thus it is willed on high, where Michael
Avenged the proud outrage.
As sails, swollen by the wind,
Fall furled when the mast is broken,
So the cruel beast fell to the ground.
Thus we descended to the fourth ditch,
Taking more of the sorrowful bank,
Which gathers all the evil of the universe.
Ah, how great is the justice of God, which heaps up
New torments and pains, as many as I saw!
And why does our guilt destroy us?
As does the wave there above Charybdis,
Which breaks against the one it meets,
So must those people dance in a ring.
Here I saw more people than elsewhere, an immense crowd,
On both sides, with great howls,
Rolling weights by the force of their breasts.
They struck against each other, and then right there
Each turned thus, rolling backward,
Shouting: Why do you keep and why do you squander?
Thus they ran around the gloomy circle,
From each side toward the opposite point,
Shouting this insulting verse of theirs:
Then each turned when he had reached
By way of his half-circle, to the other joust;
And I, whose heart was almost pierced,
Said: My master, now explain to me
What people these are, and whether all were clerics,
These tonsured ones on the left.
And he said to me: All of them were cross-eyed
So much so in their minds during their first life,
That none of their spending there was measured.
Their voices bark this clearly enough
When they come to the two points of the circle,
Where opposing guilt separates them.
These were clerics who had no covering
Of hair on their heads, popes and cardinals,
In whom avarice has its excess.
Then I said: Master, among these people
I should surely recognize some
Who were stained by these sins.
And he said to me: You gather vain thoughts;
Their unknowing life, which made them foul,
Now makes them dark to every recognition.
Forever they will come to the two collisions;
And these will rise from the grave
With clenched fists, and those with shorn hair.
Through bad giving and bad keeping, the fair world
Was taken from them, and they were set to this brawl.
What this brawl is, it is no use to tell.
Now, son, you can see the brief folly
Of goods entrusted to Fortune,
For which humankind quarrels bitterly.
For all the gold beneath the moon,
And all that once belonged to these weary souls,
Could not make one of them rest.
And I said to him: Master, now tell me further:
This Fortune of whom you speak to me,
What is she, who thus holds the world's goods in her claws?
And he said to me: O foolish creatures,
How great is the ignorance that harms you!
Now I would have you take in my meaning:
He whose wisdom transcends all things
Made the heavens and gave them one who guides them,
So that every part shines on every other,
Distributing the light equally.
Likewise, for worldly splendors,
He appointed a general minister and guide,
To change in due time vain goods
From people to people, from one bloodline to another,
Beyond the defense of human reason.
For one people rules and another languishes,
Following her judgment,
Which lies hidden from us like a snake in the grass.
Your wisdom cannot contend with her.
She provides, judges, and carries out
Her realm, as the other gods do theirs.
Her changes never cease.
Necessity makes her swift;
So often one comes to take another's place.
This is she who is so often put on the cross
Only by those who ought to praise her,
Who unjustly slander and malign her.
But she is blessed and does not hear them.
Joyful with the other primal creatures,
She turns her sphere and blissfully rejoices in herself.
Now let us descend from here on to greater grief.
Already every star is falling that was rising
When I set out, and we may not stay too long.
We crossed the circle to the farther bank,
Above a spring that boils and pours
Through a channel flowing from it.
The water was much darker than perse.
And we, in company with the gray waves,
Entered within by a different path.
It forms a marsh named Styx,
This sad stream, when it has descended
To the foot of the malign gray banks.
And I, who stood intent on looking,
Saw muddy people in that marsh,
All naked, with an angry look.
They struck one another, not only with their hands,
But with their heads, chests, and feet,
Tearing one another limb from limb with their teeth.
The good master then said: Now see
The souls of those whom wrath overcame.
And I would also have you firmly believe,
That beneath the water there are people who sigh
And make the water bubble at the surface,
As your eye tells you wherever it turns.
Fixed in the slime, they say: We were sad
In the sweet air gladdened by the sun,
Carrying slothful smoke within us.
Now we grieve in the black mire.
They gurgle thus in their throats,
For they cannot speak in whole words.
Thus we circled the foul pool
In a great arc between the dry bank and the middle,
Our eyes turned toward those who gulp the mud,
We finally came to the foot of a tower.
