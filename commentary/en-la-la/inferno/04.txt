My deep sleep in my head was broken
By a heavy thunderclap, so that I jolted awake,
Like someone roused by force:
And I moved my rested eye around,
Rising upright, I gazed intently
To learn the place where I was.
It is true that I found myself on the brink
Of the valley of the sorrowful abyss,
Which gathers the thunder of countless woes.
It was dark, deep, and shadowy,
So that, though I fixed my gaze on the bottom,
I could make out nothing there.
Now let us go down into the blind world below,
The poet began, all pale;
I shall be first, and you shall be second.
And I, noticing his color,
Said: How shall I come, if you are afraid,
You who are wont to comfort me when I doubt?
And he said to me: The anguish of the people
Who are down here paints on my face
The pity that you feel as fear.
Let us go, for the long road urges us on.
So he set out, and so made me enter
The first circle that girds the abyss.
There, as I could tell by listening,
There was no weeping, but only sighs
That made the eternal air tremble:
This came from pain without torment,
Which the very great crowds suffered,
Of children, women, and men.
The good master said to me: Do you not ask
Who these spirits are whom you see?
Now I want you to know, before you go farther,
That they did not sin; and if they have merit,
It is not enough, because they had no baptism,
Which is part of the faith you believe:
And if they lived before Christianity,
They did not worship God as they ought;
And I myself am one of these.
Because of this lack, and no other crime,
We are condemned, and afflicted only in this,
That without hope we live in desire.
Great grief seized my heart when I heard him;
For I recognized people of great worth
Who were suspended in that Limbo.
Tell me, my master, tell me, lord,
I began, wishing to be certain
Of that faith which conquers every error.
Has anyone ever left here through his own merit,
Or another's, and afterward become blessed?
And he, who understood my veiled words,
replied: I was new to this state
When I saw a mighty one come here,
Crowned with the sign of victory.
He drew out the shade of the first parent,
Of his son Abel, and that of Noah,
Of Moses, lawgiver and obedient man;
Abraham the patriarch, and David the king,
Israel with his father and his children,
And Rachel, for whom he labored so long,
And many others, and made them blessed;
But I want you to know that before them
No human spirits had been saved.
We did not stop walking while he spoke;
We kept passing through the forest,
The forest, I mean, of crowded spirits.
Our way on this side of sleep
Was not yet long when I saw a fire
That overcame a hemisphere of darkness.
We were still some little distance away,
But not so far that I could not partly discern
That this place was occupied by honorable people.
O you who honor every science and art,
Who are these, so greatly honored
That they are set apart from the rest?
And he said to me: Their honored renown,
Which rings out above in your lifetime,
Wins grace in Heaven and raises them so high.
Meanwhile a voice was heard by us:
Honor the highest poet;
His shade returns, which had departed.
After the voice had ceased and fallen silent,
I saw four great shades coming toward us;
They looked neither sad nor glad.
The good master began to say to me:
Look at the one with a sword in his hand,
Who comes before the three like a lord.
That is Homer, the sovereign poet;
The next is Horace, the satirist;
Ovid is third, and Lucan last.
For each of them shares with me
The name spoken by that single voice;
They honor me, and rightly so.
So I saw the beautiful company gather,
Those lords of the highest song,
Who fly above the rest like an eagle.
After they had spoken together for a while,
They turned toward me with a friendly greeting,
And my master smiled at this.
They honored me still further
By making me one of their company;
So I was sixth among such wisdom.
Thus we went as far as the light,
Speaking of things it is good to keep silent about,
As it was good to speak there where I was.
We came to the foot of a noble castle,
Encircled seven times by high walls,
Defended all around by a lovely little river.
We crossed it as if it were solid ground;
Through seven gates I entered with these wise men;
We came to a meadow of fresh green.
People were there with eyes slow and grave,
Of great authority in their appearance;
They spoke seldom, in gentle voices.
We drew ourselves away to one side,
Into an open, bright, and elevated place,
So that they could all be seen.
There, directly above the green enamel,
The great spirits were shown to me,
And I exalt myself in seeing them.
I saw Electra with many companions,
Among whom I recognized Hector and Æneas,
And Cæsar in arms, with keen eyes.
I saw Camilla and Penthesilea;
On the other side I saw King Latinus,
Seated with his daughter Lavinia.
I saw Brutus, who drove out Tarquin,
Lucretia, Martia, Julia, and Cornelia,
And, apart from them, I saw Saladin.
After I lifted my brows a little higher,
I saw the master of those who know,
Seated among the philosophical family.
All of them gaze at him; all honor him.
There I saw Socrates and Plato,
Who stand closest to him before the others:
Democritus, who attributes the world to chance,
Diogenes, Anaxagoras, and Thales,
Empedocles, Heraclitus, and Zeno;
And I saw the good gatherer of qualities,
I mean Dioscorides; and I saw Orpheus,
Tully, Linus, and moral Seneca;
Euclid the geometer and Ptolemæus,
Hippocrates, Avicenna, and Galen,
Averrhoës, who wrote the great commentary.
I cannot recount all of them fully,
For the long subject presses me on,
And many times my words fall short of the deed.
The company of six is reduced to two;
By another path my wise guide leads me,
Out of the quiet [Limbo], into the trembling air;
And I come to a place where nothing shines.
