Who could ever, even in plain prose,
Tell fully of the blood and wounds
That I saw now, though I tell of them again and again?
Every tongue would surely fail,
For our speech and our minds,
Have little room to contain so much.
If all the people were gathered together,
Who once lay upon the fortunate land
Of Apulia, grieving for their own blood
Shed by the Trojans, or in the long war
That made such rich spoils of rings,
As Livy writes, who does not err;
With those who suffered painful blows
In resisting Robert Guiscard;
And the other people whose bones still gather there
At Ceperano, where every Apulian
Was false, and there near Tagliacozzo,
Where old Alardo won without arms;
If one showed a pierced limb, another a severed one,
It would count for nothing beside
The foul manner of the ninth bolgia.
A cask does not gape so wide
When it loses its middle stave or end stave
As I saw one split from chin to where he breaks wind:
His entrails hung between his legs;
His organs showed, and the sad sack
That turns what is swallowed into dung.
While I fixed my whole attention on him,
He looked at me and opened his chest with his hands,
Saying: Now see how I am split open:
See how Mahomet is mutilated.
Ahead of me goes Ali, weeping,
His face split from chin to crown:
And all the others you see here
Were in life sowers of scandal and schism;
That is why they are split like this.
A devil is back here who splits us
So cruelly with the edge of his sword,
Sending each one of this company back,
When we have completed our sorrowful road;
For our wounds close again
Before anyone comes back before him.
But who are you, who pause upon the rock?
Perhaps to delay your journey to the punishment
That is decreed for your accusations?
Neither death has reached him yet, nor guilt leads him,
My master replied, to suffer torment;
He is here to gain full experience,
And I, who am dead, must lead him
Through Hell down here, from circle to circle;
This is true, just as I speak to you.
More than a hundred, when they heard him,
Stopped in the ditch to stare at me,
For wonder, forgetting their torment.
Now tell Friar Dolcino to arm himself,
You who may soon see the sun again,
If he does not wish to follow me here so soon,
And to stock up on food, so the pressure of the snow
Does not bring victory to the Novarese,
For otherwise victory would not be easy to win.
After he raised one foot to leave,
Mahomet spoke these words to me,
Then set it down again to depart.
Another, whose throat was pierced,
Whose nose was cut off below the eyebrows,
And who had only one ear,
Had stopped with the others to stare in wonder;
He opened his windpipe before the rest,
Which was red on every side,
And said: You, whom guilt does not condemn,
And whom I saw in the Latin land,
If likeness from one to another does not deceive me,
Remember Pier da Medicina,
If ever you return to see the sweet plain
That slopes from Vercelli to Marcabò.
And tell the two best men of Fano,
Sir Guido and Angiolello too,
That, if my foresight here is not false,
They will be cast out of their vessel
And drowned near Cattolica,
Through the treachery of a wicked tyrant.
Between Cyprus and Majorca
Neptune never saw so great a crime,
Not by pirates, nor by the Argive people.
That traitor, who sees only with one eye
And rules the land of Rimini, who is here with me,
Would wish he had never seen it,
Will summon them to a meeting with him;
Then, in the wind of Focara,
They will have no need of vows or prayers.
And I said to him: Show me and explain,
If you want me to carry news of you above,
Who is the one of the bitter vision?
Then he put his hand on the jaw
Of one companion and opened his mouth,
Shouting: This is the man, and he cannot speak:
This man, driven out of Rome, dispelled
Caesar's doubt, affirming that delay
Always brings harm to those prepared.
How terrified Curio seemed to me,
His tongue cut off in his throat,
He who had been so bold in speech!
And one who had both hands cut off,
Raising his stumps into the dark air,
So that his own blood stained his face,
Cried: Will you remember Mosca still,
Who said, alas, A deed once done is finished,
Which was evil seed for the Tuscan people.
And I added: And the death of your kin.
Then he, piling grief upon grief,
Went away like a sad and foolish man.
But I remained to gaze upon the crowd,
And saw a thing I would fear
To tell alone without further proof,
Were it not that conscience reassures me,
And good company emboldens a person
Beneath the armor of feeling pure.
I truly saw, and still seem to see,
A headless trunk walking, as
The others of the sad flock walked.
The severed head he held by its hair,
Dangling in his hand like a lantern,
And it looked at us and said: Alas!
It made a lamp of itself for itself;
They were two in one and one in two;
How this can be, He who rules above knows.
When he was directly below the bridge,
He raised his arm, holding his whole head,
To bring his words closer to us,
Which were: Now look at this painful punishment,
You who, still breathing, go and see the dead;
See if any is as great as this.
And so that you may carry news of me,
Know that I am Bertran de Born,
The one who gave King John wicked counsel.
I set father and son against each other;
Ahithophel did no more to Absalom
And David with his malicious devices.
Because I divided such close relations,
I carry my brain divided, alas,
From its source, which is in this trunk.
Thus the law of retaliation is served on me.
