To the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit
All Paradise began to give glory,
So that the sweet song intoxicated me.
Whatever I saw seemed to be one laughter
Of the universe; and so my delight
Entered through hearing and through sight.
O joy! O ineffable gladness!
O whole life of love and peace!
O secure wealth without desire!
Before my eyes the four torches
Stood alight, and the one that came first
Began to grow more vivid;
And it became in its appearance
As Jupiter would become if he and Mars
Were birds and changed their plumage.
Providence, which assigns here
Their turns and duties in the blessed choir,
Had set silence everywhere,
When I heard: If I change color,
Do not marvel; for as I speak,
You will see all these change color.
The one who usurps on earth my place,
My place, my place, which is vacant
In the presence of the Son of God,
Has made my cemetery a sewer
Of blood and filth, which soothes the perverse one
Who fell from here above, down below.
In that color with which, from the opposing sun,
Clouds are painted at evening and morning,
I then saw the whole sky stained;
And as an honorable woman, confident
In herself, yet timidly listens
Because of another's fault,
So Beatrice changed her appearance;
And such an eclipse, I believe, was in the sky
When supreme Power suffered below.
Then his words continued
With a voice so changed from its own
That his appearance changed no further;
Christ's bride was not raised
By my blood, by Linus's and Cletus's,
To be used for the acquisition of gold;
But to gain this joyful life.
Pius, with Sixtus, Calixtus, and Urban,
Shed their blood after much weeping.
It was not our intention that on the right hand
Some of our successors should sit,
And others on the left, among the Christian people;
Nor that the keys entrusted to me
Should become a device on a banner
Used to fight against the baptized;
Nor that I should be the image on a seal
For privileges sold and false,
For which I often blush and blaze.
Ravening wolves in shepherd's clothing
Are seen from here in every pasture:
O God's defense, why do you lie so still?
Cahorsines and Gascons prepare
To drink our blood: O good beginning,
What shameful end you must fall to!
The high Providence that with Scipio
Defended Rome, the glory of the world,
Will soon come to its aid, as I foresee.
And you, my son, who under mortal weight
Will return below, open your mouth,
And do not hide what I do not hide.
As frozen vapors snow down
In our air when the Goat's horn
In the sky touches the Sun,
So upward I saw the adorned ether
Form and snow with triumphant vapors,
Who had made their dwelling there with us.
My sight followed their appearance
And followed it until the distance
Took away its passage farther on.
Then the lady, who saw me released
From looking upward, said: Lower
Your sight, and see how you have turned.
From the hour when I had first looked,
I saw that I had moved through the whole arc
That runs from the middle to the end of the first clime;
So I saw beyond Gades the passage
Of foolish Ulysses, and on this side, near the shore
Where Europa became a sweet burden.
I would have seen more clearly the site
Of this little plot of earth; but the Sun advanced,
One sign and more, divided beneath my feet.
The enamored mind, which always holds sway
With my lady, burned more than ever
To turn its eyes back to her.
And if nature or art made pastures
To capture the eyes and thereby seize the mind,
In human flesh or in its images,
All gathered together would seem nothing
Beside the divine pleasure that shone on me
When I turned toward her smiling face.
And the power that granted me the gaze
Uprooted me from Leda's lovely nest
And launched me into the swiftest heaven.
Its swift and lofty regions
Are so uniform that I cannot say
Which part Beatrice chose for me.
But she, who saw my desire,
Began to speak, smiling, so joyful
That God seemed to rejoice in her face:
The nature of the motion that keeps
The center still and moves all else around
Begins here, as from its starting point.
And this heaven has no other place
Than the divine mind, in which are kindled
The love that turns it and the power that pours down.
Light and love enclose it in one circle,
As this heaven encloses the others, and that enclosure;
Only the One who encloses understands it.
Its motion is not measured by another motion;
But the others are measured by this one,
As ten is measured by half and by fifth.
And how time holds its roots
In such a vessel, and its branches in the others,
Can now be clear to you.
O greed, which sinks mortals
So far beneath you that none can draw
Their eyes from your waves!
In people the will blossoms well;
But continual rain turns
True plums into misshapen fruit.
Faith and innocence are found
Only in little children; then each flees
Before their cheeks are covered.
One, while still babbling, fasts,
Then, when his tongue is loosed, devours
Any food in any season of the moon;
And another, while babbling, loves
And listens to his mother, but with full speech
Later longs to see her buried.
Thus white skin turns black
At first sight, in the beautiful daughter
Of him who brings morning and leaves evening.
You, so that this may not surprise you,
Know that on earth there is no one to govern;
That is why the human family strays.
But before January has left winter entirely,
Because of the hundredth part neglected below,
These upper spheres will roar so loudly
That Fortune, so long awaited,
Will turn the sterns where the prows are,
So the fleet will sail on a straight course;
And true fruit will come after the flower.
