Post Constantine turned the eagle
Against the course of heaven, which it followed
Behind that ancient one who took Lavinia,
For two hundred years and more, the bird of God
Stayed at Europe's farthest edge,
Near the mountains from which it first emerged;
And beneath the shade of its sacred wings
It governed the world there, passing from hand to hand,
And, changing hands thus, came into mine.
I was Caesar, and I am Justinian,
Who, by the will of the first Love I feel,
Removed the excess and the useless from the laws;
And before I turned my attention to that work,
I believed there was one nature in Christ, not more,
And was content with that belief;
But blessed Agapetus, who was
The supreme shepherd, directed me
To the true faith with his words.
I believed him, and what was contained in his faith
I now see clearly, as clearly as you see
That every contradiction has one truth and one falsehood.
As soon as I moved my feet with the Church,
God, in his grace, was pleased to inspire me
With the great work, and I gave myself wholly to it;
And I entrusted my arms to my Belisarius,
Whom Heaven's right hand so favored
That this was a sign I should lay them aside.
Now my answer addresses the first question,
But the condition compels me to add something further;
So that you may see how reasonably
They act against the sacred standard,
Both those who claim it for themselves
And those who oppose it.
See how many virtues made it worthy
Of reverence; its story began when
Pallas died to give it a kingdom.
You know what it achieved while dwelling in Alba
For more than three hundred years, until the end
When three fought three for it once more.
You know what it achieved, from the Sabine women's wrong
To Lucretia's grief, under seven kings,
Conquering the neighboring peoples all around.
You know what it achieved, borne by the illustrious
Romans against Brennus and against Pyrrhus,
And against other princes and allied peoples:
Thus Torquatus and Quintius, who was named
For his neglected curl, and the Decii and Fabii
Won a fame I gladly preserve.
It crushed the pride of the Arabs,
Who followed Hannibal across
The Alpine rocks, O Po, from which you flow.
Beneath it the youthful Scipio and Pompey
Triumphed, and to the hill beneath which you were born
It seemed bitter.
Then, near the time when all heaven willed
To bring the world back to its serene order,
Caesar took it by Rome's will;
And what he did from the Var to the Rhine
Was seen by the Isère, Arar, and Seine,
And every valley filled by the Rhône.
What he did after leaving Ravenna
And crossing the Rubicon was such a flight
That neither tongue nor pen could follow it.
Toward Spain he turned his host,
Then struck toward Durazzo and Pharsalia
So that the hot Nile felt the pain.
Antandros and the Simois, from which it had moved,
He saw again, and the place where Hector lies,
Then he shook himself cruelly over Ptolemy;
From there it flashed down upon Juba;
Then it turned toward your West,
Where it heard Pompey's trumpet.
What it did with the next bearer
Brutus and Cassius bark about in Hell,
And Mutina and Perugia grieved for it.
Sad Cleopatra still weeps for it,
For, fleeing before it, by the serpent
She met a sudden, bitter death.
With him it ran as far as the Red Sea shore;
With him it brought the world such peace
That Janus's temple was shut.
But all that the standard that makes me speak
Had done before, and would do afterward,
Throughout the mortal realm subject to it,
Seems little and dim by comparison,
If one looks at it in the hand of the third Caesar
With clear eye and pure regard;
For the living justice that inspires me
Granted it, in the hand of the one I name,
The glory of avenging its anger.
Now attend to what I repeat to you:
Afterward it ran with Titus to avenge
The vengeance for the ancient sin.
And when the Lombard tooth bit
The holy Church, beneath its wings
Charlemagne came to its aid by victory.
Now you can judge those people
Whom I accused above, and their faults,
The cause of all your evils.
One sets golden lilies against the public standard,
The other claims it for his faction,
So it is hard to see which errs more.
Let the Ghibellines practice their craft
Under another standard; for he follows this one badly
Who always separates it from justice;
And let this new Charles not overthrow it
With his Guelfs, but fear the talons
That have already plucked a greater lion's fleece.
Many times sons have wept
For their father's fault; let no one think
God will change his arms for their lilies.
This little star is adorned
With good spirits, active in their lives
So that honor and fame might follow them;
When desires are fixed there,
They stray, and the rays
Of true love must rise less brightly.
Yet in measuring our rewards
Against our merit lies part of our joy,
For we see them neither less nor greater.
Hence living justice sweetens
Our affections so that they can never
Be twisted toward any wickedness.
Different voices make sweet notes;
So different ranks in our life
Make sweet harmony in these spheres.
And within this present pearl
Shines the light of Romeo, whose great
And noble work was poorly rewarded.
But the Provençals who acted against him
Did not prosper; so fares ill
Who harms himself because another does good.
Raymond Berengar had four daughters, each a queen,
And this was made possible for him
By Romeo, a humble pilgrim.
Then crooked words moved him
To demand an account from this just man,
Who made seven and five equal ten.
Then he left, poor and old;
If the world knew the heart he had,
Begging his life from door to door,
It praises him much and would praise him more.
