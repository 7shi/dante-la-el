O you who are in a little boat,
Eager to hear, following
Behind my ship, which crosses singing,
Turn back to see your shores again,
Do not venture into the deep; for perhaps,
If you lose me, you would be left astray.
The waters I take have never been sailed;
Minerva breathes, and Apollo leads me,
And new Muses show me the Bears.
You other few, who early raised your necks
Toward the bread of angels, on which
One lives here, but is never fully sated,
You may well put your vessel on the deep sea,
Keeping to my wake, ahead of the water
That returns to an even surface.
Those glorious ones who went to Colchis
Were no more amazed than you will be
When they saw Jason turned plowman.
The innate and everlasting thirst
For the Godlike Kingdom carried us
Swiftly, almost as fast as you see the heavens.
Beatrice looked upward, and I looked at her;
And perhaps in the time it takes an arrow to rest,
Then fly, and spring free from the bow's notch,
I found myself arrived where a wondrous thing
Drew my gaze toward itself; and therefore she,
From whom my thoughts could not be hidden,
Turned toward me, as joyful as she was beautiful:
Direct your mind in gratitude to God, she said,
who has joined us with the first star.
It seemed to me that a cloud enveloped us,
Bright, dense, solid, and polished,
Like a diamond struck by the sun.
The eternal pearl received me
Within itself, as water receives
A ray of light, remaining undivided.
If I was a body, and here we cannot conceive
How one dimension could endure another's entry,
As must happen when one body enters another,
This should kindle in us more desire
To see that Essence in which one sees
How our nature was united with God.
There we shall see what we now hold by faith,
Not demonstrated, but self-evident,
Like the first truth that people know.
I answered then: My Lady, as devoutly
As I can be, I thank Him
Who has removed me from the mortal world.
But tell me, what are the dark marks
On this body, which down there on earth
Make people tell tales of Cain?
She smiled a little, then said: If the opinion
Of mortals errs, she told me, where the key
Of sense does not unlock,
Surely the arrows of wonder should no longer
Wound you; look beyond the senses
And see that reason has short wings.
But tell me what you yourself think of it.
And I: What appears different up here,
I believe is caused by bodies rare and dense.
And she: Indeed, your belief will prove
Deeply submerged in error, if you listen well
To the argument I shall make against it.
The eighth sphere shows you many lights,
In which differences of kind and size
May be observed in many forms.
If rarity and density alone caused this,
One power would exist in all bodies,
Distributed more or less equally.
Different powers must be the fruits
Of formal principles; and, by your reasoning,
All but one would be destroyed.
Moreover, if rarity were the cause
Of that darkness you ask about, or if in part
This planet were so starved of its matter,
Then, as fat and lean divide a body,
So this one would change its pages
Throughout its volume.
If the first were true, it would be plain
In a solar eclipse, as the light shone through,
As through another transparent rare body.
This is not so; therefore we must examine
The other case; and if I disprove that one too,
Your view will be shown false.
If this rarity does not pass through,
There must be a boundary beyond which
Its opposite no longer lets it pass.
From there the other ray is reflected,
As color returns through glass
With lead hidden behind it.
Now you will say the ray appears darker
There, more than elsewhere, because it is reflected
Farther back within.
Experience can free you from this objection,
If you ever test it; experience is the source
From which the streams of your arts flow.
Take three mirrors, and set two
At equal distances from you, and the third farther away;
Place your eyes between the first two.
Turn toward them, and place a light
Behind your back, to illuminate all three mirrors;
Let its reflection from each return to you.
Though the farthest image does not spread
As much in quantity, you will see
That it must shine equally in quality.
Now, just as under the blows of hot rays
The underlying matter of snow is left bare,
Stripped of its first whiteness and cold,
So, with your understanding left bare,
I now wish to shape it with a light so true
That it will shimmer before your sight.
Within the heaven of divine peace
A body revolves, in whose power
Lies the being of all that it contains.
The next heaven, which holds so many sights,
Divides that being among diverse essences,
Distinct from it and contained within it.
The other spheres, through their various differences,
Arrange the dispositions they hold within
Toward their ends and their seeds.
These instruments of the world proceed
As you now see, from level to level,
Receiving from above and acting below.
Look carefully now at how I proceed
Through this passage toward the truth you desire,
So that afterward you can cross the ford alone.
The motion and power of the holy spheres,
Like the hammer's craft coming from the smith,
Must breathe forth from the blessed movers.
The heaven made beautiful by so many lights
Takes its image from the profound mind
That turns it, and makes itself its seal.
And as the soul within your dust
Unfolds through diverse limbs, formed
For diverse powers,
So its intelligence multiplies its goodness
Through the stars and spreads it abroad,
Revolving upon its own unity.
A diverse power forms a different bond
With the precious body it enlivens,
And there, like life, it is joined to it.
From the joyful nature whence it comes,
The mingled power shines through the body,
As joy shines through a living eye.
From it comes what appears different
From one light to another, not from dense or rare;
It is the formal principle that produces,
According to its goodness, dark and bright.
