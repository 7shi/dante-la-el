O you who are in a little boat,
Eager to hear, following
Behind my ship, which crosses singing,
Turn back to see your shores again,
Do not venture into the deep, in which perhaps,
If you lose me, you would be left astray.
The waters I take no one has ever sailed;
Minerva breathes, and Apollo leads me,
And new Muses show me the Bears.
You other few, who raised your necks
Early toward the bread of angels, on which
One lives here, but is never fully sated,
You may well launch upon the deep sea
Your vessel, keeping to my wake,
Ahead of the water that returns to an even surface.
Those glorious ones who went to Colchis
Were no more amazed than you
When they saw Jason as a plowman.
The innate and everlasting thirst
For the Godlike Kingdom carried us
Swiftly, almost as fast as you see the heavens.
Beatrice looked upward, and I looked at her;
And perhaps in the time it takes an arrow to rest,
Then fly, and spring free from the bow's notch,
I found myself arrived where a wondrous thing
Drew my gaze toward itself; and therefore she,
From whom my thoughts could not be hidden,
Turned toward me, as joyful as she was beautiful:
Direct your mind to God, she said to me,
who has joined us with the first star.
It seemed to me that a cloud enveloped us,
Bright, dense, solid, and polished,
Like a diamond struck by the sun.
Within itself the eternal pearl
Received me, as water receives
A ray of light, remaining undivided.
I was a body; here we cannot conceive
How one dimension penetrates another,
As must happen when one body enters another,
This should rekindle in us more desire
To see that Essence in which one sees
How our nature was united with God.
There we shall see what we hold by faith,
Not demonstrated; but it will be self-evident,
Like the first truth that people know.
I answered: O Lady, now devout
To the utmost that I can be, I thank Him
Who has removed me from the mortal world.
But tell me, what are the dark marks
On this body, since down there on earth
They make others tell tales of Cain?
She smiled a little, then: If it errs,
The opinion of mortals, she told me,
Where the key of sense does not unlock,
Surely there should not wound you the arrows
Of wonder any longer; then beyond the senses
See that reason has short wings.
But tell me what you yourself think of it.
And I: What appears different up here,
I believe that bodies rare and dense cause it.
And she: Indeed, well shall you see submerged
In error your belief, if you listen well
To the argument I shall make against it.
The eighth sphere shows many
Lights, in which, in kind and size,
May be observed different forms.
If rarity and density alone caused this,
Only one single power would exist in all,
Distributed in greater and lesser measure, or at least equally.
Different powers must be the fruits
Of formal principles; and all but one
Would consequently, by your reasoning, be destroyed.
Moreover, if rarity were of that darkness
The cause you ask about, either in some part all the way through
So starved of its matter would be
This planet; or, just as there is a division
Of fat and lean in a body, so this one
Throughout its volume would change its pages.
If the first were true, it would be plain
In a solar eclipse, from the shining through
Of light, as through another rare body interposed.
This is not so; therefore we must examine
The other case; and if it happens that I disprove that one,
Your view will be shown false.
If it is the case that this rarity does not pass through,
There must be a boundary from which
Its opposite does not let it pass farther.
And from there the other ray is reflected,
As color returns through glass
With lead hidden behind it.
Now you will say that it appears dark
There, the ray, more than elsewhere,
Because it is reflected farther back there.
From this objection there can free you
Experience, if you ever test it,
Which is wont to be the source of the streams of your arts.
Take three mirrors, and set two
Thus at equal distances, and the third farther away;
Place your eyes between the first two.
Turn toward them, and arrange that at the back
Of you stands a light to illuminate all three mirrors;
And let its reflection from each return to you.
Though in quantity it does not spread as much
To your sight far off, there you will see
That it must shine equally.
Now, just as under the blows of hot rays
The underlying matter of snow is left bare
Of both its first whiteness and its cold;
So it will remain in your understanding:
I wish to shape it with a light so true
That it will shimmer for you in its appearance.
Within the heaven of divine peace
A body revolves, in whose power
Lies the being of all that it contains.
The next heaven, which holds so many sights,
Divides that being among diverse essences,
Distinct from it and contained within it.
The other spheres, through their various differences,
The dispositions they hold within
Arrange toward their ends and their seeds.
These instruments of the world proceed in such a way,
As you now see, from level to level,
That they receive from above and act below.
Look carefully at me as I proceed
Through this passage toward the truth you desire,
So that afterward you can cross the ford alone.
The motion and power of the holy spheres,
Like the hammer's craft from the smith,
Must breathe forth from the blessed movers.
The heaven made beautiful by so many lights
From the profound mind that turns it
Takes its image, and makes itself its seal.
And as the soul within your dust
Through limbs diverse and formed
For diverse powers, unfolds;
So the intelligence its goodness,
By multiplying it through the stars, spreads abroad,
Revolving upon its own unity.
A diverse power forms a different bond
With the precious body it enlivens,
And there, like life, it is joined to it.
From the joyful nature whence it comes,
The mingled power shines through the body,
As joy through a living eye.
From it comes what from one light to another
Appears different, not from dense or rare;
It is the formal principle that produces,
According to its goodness, dark and bright.
