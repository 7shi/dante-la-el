Hosanna, Holy God of Hosts,
You who shine forth in your brightness
Upon the blessed fires of these kingdoms!
Thus, turning to its own song,
That substance seemed to me to sing,
Upon which a double light was doubled:
And it and the others moved to their dance,
And, like swiftest sparks,
They vanished from me in sudden distance.
I doubted, and said: Tell, tell them,
Within myself: tell, I said to my lady,
Who quenches my thirst with sweet drops;
But that reverence which takes possession
Of all of me, through B, through I and through Ice,
Bowed me down as a drowsy man.
For a little while Beatrice bore with me in that state,
And began speaking to me, with such a smile,
That it would make a man happy in fire:
According to my ineffable vision,
How a just vengeance justly
Was punished has entered your thoughts;
But I shall soon untie your mind:
And you, listen, for my words
Will bring you a great truth as a gift.
Because he would not endure the power that wills
A bridle for its good, that man who was not born,
Condemning himself, condemned all his offspring;
Thus the human race lay sick
On earth for many centuries, in great error,
Until it pleased the divine Word to descend,
Where nature, which from its Maker
Had strayed far, united itself to him in person
By the sole act of his eternal love.
Now turn your sight to what is now reasoned:
This nature, united to its Maker,
Was pure and good as when created;
But by itself it was banished
From Paradise, because it turned away
From the way of truth and from its life.
The penalty, then, which the Cross imposed,
If measured against the nature assumed,
No other was ever inflicted so justly;
And so no injury was ever so great,
Considering the person who suffered,
In whom such nature was joined.
Thus from one act diverse things issued;
For one death pleased God and the Jews:
Through it the earth shook and heaven opened.
Henceforth it should no longer seem difficult to you,
When it is said that just vengeance
Was afterward avenged by a just court.
But now I see your mind drawn tight
From thought to thought within one knot,
From which it waits with great longing to be freed.
You say: I understand well what I hear;
But it is hidden from me why God willed,
For our redemption, this one way alone.
This decree, brother, lies concealed
From the eyes of everyone whose mind
Has not matured in love's flame.
Truly, since this question
Is much considered and little understood,
I shall tell you why this way was most worthy.
Divine goodness, which casts away from itself
All envy, burns and shines within itself
So that it unfolds eternal beauties.
Whatever flows from it without mediation
Has no end thereafter, for unchanged remains
Its imprint when it seals it.
Whatever rains from it without mediation
Is wholly free, for it is not subject
To the power of the heavenly bodies.
It is more like God, and so pleases him more;
For ardor, which illumines all things,
Is most alive in what most resembles it.
Endowed with all these things is
The human creature; and if one is lacking,
From its nobility it must fall.
Sin alone deprives it of freedom
And makes it unlike the highest good,
For it is little whitened by his light;
And it never returns to its dignity
Unless it fills what guilt made empty,
With just penalties against sinful pleasure.
Your nature, when all of it sinned
In its seed, from these dignities,
As from Paradise, was removed;
Nor could these be regained, if you consider
The matter very carefully, by any way
Without passing through one of these steps:
Either God, solely by his generosity,
Had pardoned it, or humanity by itself
Had made satisfaction for its folly.
Now fix your eyes within the abyss
Of eternal counsel, as far as you can,
Firmly intent upon my words.
Humanity could not, within its bounds,
Ever make satisfaction, since it could not descend
In humility, obeying afterward,
As far as through disobedience it had sought to rise;
And this is why humanity was
Barred from being able to make satisfaction by itself.
Therefore God had to, by his ways,
Restore humanity to its full life,
I mean by one or by both.
For a deed is that much more welcome
From its doer, the more it reveals
The goodness of the heart from which it came;
Divine goodness, which stamps the world,
To proceed by all its ways
To raise us up again, was pleased.
Nor between the final turning and the first day
So exalted and so magnificent a work,
By either way, has there been or will there be.
For God was more generous in giving himself
To make humanity able to rise again,
Than if he himself had pardoned it on his own;
And every other way fell short
Of justice, if the Son of God
Had not humbled himself to become incarnate.
Now, to fulfill your every desire well,
I return to explain one point,
So that you may see there as I do.
You say: I see water, I see fire,
Air and earth and all their mixtures
Decay and last only a little while;
Yet these things too were created:
So, if what I said is true,
They ought to be safe from decay.
Angels, brother, and the pure region
In which you are may be called created,
As they are, in their complete being;
But the elements you have named,
And the things made from them,
Are given form by created power.
The matter they possess was created;
The power that gives it form was created
In these stars that circle around them.
The souls of all animals and plants
Are drawn from potential mixtures
By the ray and motion of the holy lights.
But your life is breathed directly
By supreme goodness, which draws it lovingly
To itself, so that it always desires it.
And from this you may also reason
Toward your resurrection, if you recall
How human flesh was made then,
When both first parents were formed.
