Let whoever wishes to understand well imagine
What I saw just now, and hold the image
Firmly while I speak, like an unshaken rock.
Fifteen stars that in different regions
Enliven the heavens with such brightness
That they surpass every constellation of the air;
Imagine the Wain, whose enclosure
Suffices for our heaven by night and day,
So that it never fails as its pole revolves;
Imagine the mouth of that horn
That begins at the tip of the axle,
Around which the first wheel turns;
Imagine these making two signs in heaven,
As the daughter of Minos made one,
When she felt the chill of death;
Let one send its rays into the other,
And let both revolve in such a way
That one goes first and the other follows;
And you will have something like the shadow
Of a true constellation and a double dance,
Circling the point where I stood.
Since it is so far beyond our custom,
As the sky that surpasses all the others
Moves beyond the slow flow of the Chiana.
There they sang not of Bacchus or Paean,
But of three persons in the divine nature,
And of the human and divine in one person.
They completed song and turning in their measure,
And those holy lights turned their attention to us,
Rejoicing from one task to the next.
Then the light that had told me
The wondrous life of God's Poor One
Broke the silence of those harmonious spirits,
And said: When the straw of one threshing floor
Has been beaten and its grain stored away,
Sweet love invites me to thresh another.
You think that in the breast from which
The rib was taken to form the fair cheek,
The taste of whose food costs the whole world;
And in him who was pierced by the lance,
And before and after gave such satisfaction
That he outweighed every sin;
Whatever light human nature may rightly
Possess was infused in both
By the power that made them;
And so you wonder at what I said above,
When I told you that the fifth light held
The good for which no second arose.
Now open your eyes to my answer,
And you will see your belief and my words
Meet in truth, as a center lies in a circle.
All that does not die and all that can die
Is only a radiance of that Idea
Which our Lord, in love, brings forth.
For that true light which flows
From its source, never divided
From him or from the Love entwined in him,
By its goodness gathers its ray,
As if reflected, in new substances,
Yet eternally remains one;
From there it descends to the lowest powers,
From act to act, descending so far
That it makes only brief contingencies.
By contingencies I mean generated things,
Produced with or without seed
As the heavens move.
Their wax, and that which guides it,
Are not always uniform; therefore beneath
The ideal stamp shines more or less;
Hence one and the same kind of tree
Bears better or worse fruit,
And you are born with differing talents.
If the wax were perfectly shaped
And heaven had its highest power,
The seal's light would appear in full.
But nature always gives it imperfect,
Working like an artist
Whose practiced hand trembles.
Yet if the warm love and clear vision
Of the first power dispose and stamp it,
All perfection is attained there.
Thus earth was once made fit
For every animal perfection;
Thus the Virgin was made pregnant.
Therefore I affirm your belief: human nature
Never was, nor will be,
As it was in those two persons.
Now, if I did not go further,
How was this man without an equal?
Your words would take that to be the answer.
But so that what is hidden may become clear,
Think who he was, and what moved him
When he was told, Ask, to make his request.
I have spoken so that you can see clearly
He was a king who asked for wisdom,
So that he might be a capable king;
Not to learn the number of the movers above,
Or whether the necessary ever makes
A necessity with the contingent;
Not whether there was a first motion,
Or whether a triangle can be made
From a semicircle with no right angle;
If you note what I said and this,
You will learn the royal prudence
At which my argument's arrow aims.
And if you look clearly at arose,
You will see the reference is only
To kings: they are many, the good are rare.
With this distinction, take my statement,
And it can agree with what you believe
Of our first father and our Beloved.
Let this always be lead upon your feet,
To make you move slowly, like a weary man,
Toward yes or no when you cannot see;
For he is the lowest among fools
Who affirms or denies without distinction,
In either case, at every turn;
For it often happens that common opinion
Bends toward the false,
And then emotion binds the intellect.
Far worse than leaving the shore in vain
And not returning as one set out
Is fishing for truth without skill.
The world has clear examples of this:
Parmenides, Melissus, Bryson, and many
Who went on, not knowing where.
So did Sabellius and Arius, and those fools
Who were like swords against Scripture,
Twisting straight meanings into crooked ones.
Let people not be too sure
In judging, like one who estimates grain
In the field before it is ripe;
For all winter I have seen a thorn bush
Look stiff and fierce,
Then bear a rose at its crown;
And I have seen a ship, straight and swift,
Sail the sea on its whole course,
Then perish at the river mouth.
Let Lady Bertha and Sir Martin not think,
Because they see one steal and the other give,
That they see them in God's counsel;
For the thief may rise, and the other may fall.
