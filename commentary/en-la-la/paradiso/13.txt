Let whoever wishes to understand well imagine
What I saw just now, and hold the image
While I speak, like an unshaken rock.
Fifteen stars that in different regions
Enliven the heavens with such brightness
That they surpass every constellation of the air;
Imagine the Wain, whose enclosure
Suffices for our heaven by night and day,
So that it never fails as its pole revolves;
Imagine the mouth of that horn
That begins at the tip of the axle,
Around which the first wheel turns;
To have made of themselves two signs in heaven,
As the daughter of Minos made one,
Then, when she felt the chill of death;
And let one send its rays into the other,
And let both revolve in such a way
That one goes first and the other follows;
And you will have something like the shadow of a true
Constellation and a double dance,
Circling the point where I stood.
Since it is so far beyond our custom,
As beyond the flow of the Chiana
Moves the sky that surpasses all the others.
There they sang not of Bacchus or Paean,
But of three persons in the divine nature,
And of the human and divine in one person.
They completed song and turning in their measure,
And those holy lights turned their attention to us,
Rejoicing from one task to the next.
The silence among those harmonious spirits was broken
Then by the light in which the wondrous life
Of God's Poor One had been told to me,
And said: When one lot of straw has been beaten,
When its grain has already been stored away,
Sweet love invites me to thresh another.
You think that in the breast from which the rib
Was taken to form the fair cheek,
Whose taste costs the whole world;
And in him who was pierced by the lance,
And before and after gave such satisfaction
That he outweighed the scale pan of every sin;
However much human nature may rightly
Possess of light, all was infused
By the power that made them both:
And so you wonder at what I said above,
When I told you that it had no second,
The good that is held in the fifth light.
Now open your eyes to my answer to you,
And you will see your belief and my words
Meet in truth, as a center lies in a circle.
All that does not die and all that can die
Is only a radiance of that Idea
Which our Lord, in love, brings forth.
For that true light which flows
From its source, never divided
From him or from his Love entwined in him,
By its goodness gathers its ray,
As if reflected, in new substances,
Eternally remaining one;
From there it descends to the lowest powers,
From act to act, descending so far
That it makes only brief contingencies.
And by these contingencies I mean
Generated things, which are produced
With or without seed by the moving heavens.
Their wax, and that which guides it,
Are not always uniform; therefore beneath the stamp
Ideal, it shines more or less;
Hence it happens that one and the same tree,
In respect of kind, bears better or worse fruit,
And you are born with differing and unequal talents.
If the wax were perfectly shaped
And heaven had its highest power,
The seal's light would appear in full.
But nature always gives it imperfect,
Working like an artist
Who has command of the art and a hand that trembles.
Yet if the clear vision of warm love
Of the first power disposes and stamps it,
All perfection is attained there.
Thus earth was once made fit
For every animal perfection;
Thus the Virgin was made pregnant.
Therefore I affirm your belief,
That human nature never was,
Nor will be, as it was in those two persons.
Now, if I did not go further,
How was this man without an equal?
Your words would argue.
But so that what is hidden may become quite clear,
Think who he was, and what moved him
When he was told, Ask, to make his request.
I have not spoken in such a way that you cannot
Clearly see that he was a king who asked for wisdom,
So that he might be a capable king;
Not to learn how many there are
Of the movers above, or whether the necessary
With the contingent ever makes a necessity;
Not whether one can grant that there was a first motion,
Or whether from a semicircle there can be made
A triangle with no right angle;
Hence, if you note what I said and this,
Royal prudence it is, and you will learn that seeing
At which my argument's arrow aims.
And if you look clearly at arose,
You will see the reference is only
To kings: they are many, the good are rare.
With this distinction, take my statement,
And thus it can agree with what you believe
Of our father and our Beloved.
And let this always be lead upon your feet,
To make you move slowly, like a weary man,
Toward yes or no when you cannot see;
For he is the very lowest among fools
Who affirms or denies without distinction,
As much at one turn as at the other;
From this it happens that often there is bent
An opinion running in a false direction,
And then emotion binds the intellect.
Far worse than in vain does he leave the shore,
Who does not return as he set out,
Who fishes for truth without skill.
And the world has clear examples of this:
Parmenides, Melissus, Bryson, and many
Who went on, not knowing how they went.
So did Sabellius and Arius, and those fools
Who were like swords against Scripture,
Twisting straight meanings into crooked ones.
Let people not be too sure
In judging, like one who estimates
Grain in the field before it is ripe;
For all winter I have seen, first,
A thorn bush look stiff and fierce,
Then bear a rose at its crown;
And I have once seen a ship, straight and swift,
Sail the sea on its whole course,
Perish at the end and at the entrance to the river mouth.
Let Lady Bertha and Sir Martin not think,
Because they see one steal and the other give,
That they see them in God's counsel;
For the thief may rise, and the other may fall.
