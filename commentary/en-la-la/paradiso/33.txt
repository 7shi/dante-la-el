Virgin Mother, daughter of your Son,
Humble and exalted above every creature,
Fixed limit of the eternal counsel,
You are she who so ennobled human nature
That its Maker did not disdain
To become its handiwork.
In your womb love was kindled anew,
By whose warmth in eternal peace
This flower thus blossomed.
Here in heaven you are for us the noonday torch
Of charity, and below, among mortals,
You are the living fountain of hope.
Lady, you are so great and so powerful
That whoever seeks grace and does not turn to you
Would have desire fly without wings.
Your kindness not only helps
Those who ask, but many times
Freely anticipates the request.
In you are mercy and compassion;
In you is magnificence; in you is gathered
All the goodness found in any creature.
Now this man, who from the deepest pit
Of the universe up to here has seen
The spiritual lives, one by one,
Humbly begs you for the grace of strength
To raise his eyes still higher
Toward the final salvation.
And I, who have never burned with desire for my own vision
As much as I do for his, offer you
All my prayers, and beg they not be refused,
So that with your prayers you may dissolve every cloud
Of his mortality, and let the highest joy
Unfold itself to him.
I also beg you, O Queen, who can do
Whatever you will, to keep his feelings sound
After so great a vision.
May your protection overcome human change;
See Beatrice and all the blessed
Join their hands in prayer for me.
The eyes beloved and revered by God,
Fixed upon the one praying, showed us
How welcome to her are devout prayers.
Then toward the eternal light they turned
Into which no creature's eye, one must believe,
Can penetrate so clearly.
And I, who was drawing near the end
Of all my desires, as I ought,
Brought the fire of longing in me to its end.
Bernard nodded to me and smiled,
So that I would look upward; but I was already
Of myself as he wished me to be,
For my sight, becoming clear,
Entered more and more through the ray
Of the high light, which is true in itself.
From this point onward, my seeing was greater
Than words can show, for speech yields to such a sight,
And memory yields to such excess.
As one who sees in a dream,
And after the dream the impression remains,
While the rest does not return to mind,
So am I: almost all my vision fades,
Yet still there drips into my heart
The sweetness born from it.
So snow melts beneath the sun,
So in the wind among light leaves
The Sibyl's words were lost.
O highest light, so far above
Mortal thoughts, restore to my mind
A little of what you once appeared to be;
And make my tongue so powerful
That I may leave one spark of your glory
For generations yet to come;
For if I return a little to my memory
And sound a little in these verses,
More of your victory will be understood.
I believe that, because of the sharpness I endured
From the living ray, I would have been overwhelmed
Had my eyes turned away from it.
And I remember that I grew bolder
Through this, to endure so much, that I joined
My sight to the infinite power.
O abundant grace, through which I dared
To fix my gaze through the eternal light,
Until my sight was spent!
In its depth I saw all things held
Together by love in one volume,
All that is scattered throughout the universe;
Substance and accident, and their qualities,
As if fused together in such a way
That what I say is only a simple gleam.
I believe I saw the universal form
Of this bond; as I speak of it more fully,
I feel my joy grow.
A single instant brings me more forgetfulness
Than twenty-five centuries brought to Neptune
When he first gazed at the shadow of Argo.
So my mind, wholly suspended,
Gazed fixed, motionless, and intent,
And was always kindled to gaze again.
Whoever sees that light becomes such
That turning from it to another sight
Can never be consented to;
For all the good that is the object of the will
Is gathered in it, and outside it
Whatever is perfect there is deficient.
From now on my speech will be shorter
Than that of an infant who still
Wets its tongue at the breast, even for what I remember.
Not because more than a single appearance
Was in the living light I gazed upon,
For it is always as it was before;
But because my sight grew stronger
As I gazed, one single appearance,
While I changed, seemed to change for me:
In the deep and clear substance
Of the high light, three circles appeared to me,
Of three colors and one dimension;
One seemed reflected from another, like rainbow from rainbow,
And the third seemed fire,
Breathing equally from either side.
O how short is speech, and how faint
Beside my thought! And what I saw
Is so great that even a little cannot be told.
O eternal light, dwelling only in yourself,
Only understanding yourself, and understood by yourself,
And understanding yourself, you smile upon me!
That circular form, which thus conceived
Appeared within you as reflected light,
When my eyes had looked upon it a little,
Seemed painted within itself, in its own color,
With our human likeness;
For all my sight was fixed upon it.
Like a geometer who devotes himself entirely
To measuring the circle, but cannot find,
By thinking, the principle he needs,
So was I before that new sight:
I wished to see how the image fit
The circle, and how it found its place there;
But my own wings were not sufficient;
Then my mind was struck
By a flash in which its desire came.
Here power failed my high imagination;
But now my will and desire were turned,
As a wheel moved evenly, by the Love
That moves the sun and the other stars.
