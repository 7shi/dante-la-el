If ever it should happen that the sacred poem,
To which Heaven and earth have set their hands,
So that it has made me lean for many years,
Overcome the cruelty that keeps me outside
The fair sheepfold where I slept as a lamb,
An enemy to the wolves that wage war on it;
With another voice, and with another fleece,
I shall return as a poet, and at the font
Of my baptism I shall take the crown;
For into the Faith that makes known
Souls to God I entered there, and afterward
Peter for it turned my brow around.
Then a light moved toward us
From that sphere whence came the first prince
Whom Christ left as one of His vicars.
And my lady, full of joy,
Said to me: Look, look, behold the Baron,
Because of whom Galicia is visited below.
As when a dove settles
Beside its mate, and each reveals,
Circling and cooing, its affection,
So I saw one by the other great
And glorious prince welcomed,
Praising the food that satisfies them at their feast above.
But when their greeting had ended,
Each stood silent before me,
So aflame that they overcame my sight.
Then Beatrice, smiling, said:
Illustrious spirit, through whose generosity
Our basilica was written of,
Make Hope resound in this height;
You know how often you portray it,
As often as Jesus gave greater light to the three.
Raise your head, and make yourself secure;
For whatever comes here from the mortal world
Must ripen in our rays.
This encouragement from the second flame
Came to me; then I raised my eyes to the heights,
Which had bent them before with their great weight.
Since, by grace, that you should meet is the will
Of our Emperor, before death,
In the most secret chamber of His court, with His companions,
So that, having seen the truth of this court,
The Hope that below draws so strongly into love
You may thereby encourage in yourself and others;
Say what it is; say how it flowers
In your mind, and tell where it came to you.
So the second light spoke again.
And that gracious lady, who guided the feathers
Of my wings to so high a flight,
Anticipated my answer in this way.
Any son the Church militant
Does not have with greater hope, as is written
In the Sun that shines upon our whole host;
Therefore it has been granted him that from Egypt
He may come to Jerusalem to see,
Before his time of service is ended.
The other two questions, which, not to learn,
Are asked, but so that he may report
How much this virtue pleases you;
I leave to him, for they will not be too difficult for him
Nor boastful; and let him answer you on this,
And may God's grace give him strength for this.
Like a student following his teacher,
Ready and willing in what he has mastered,
So that his skill may not stay hidden:
Hope, I said, is a sure expectation
Of that glory, produced by
Divine grace and prior merit.
This light comes to me from many stars;
But first it was poured into my heart
By him who was the foremost singer of the first Leader.
May they hope in you, in his Theodia,
He says to those who know your name;
And who does not know it, if he has my faith?
You poured into me with your rain
In your Epistle afterward, so that I am full,
And rain your shower back upon others.
While I was speaking, within the living heart
Of that flame, a flash trembled,
Sudden and dense, like lightning.
Then it breathed: The Love that sets me aflame
Still for the virtue that followed me
To the palm and to the end of the field
Makes me look to you, who delight
In it; and it pleases me that you tell
What Hope promises you.
And I: The new and ancient Scriptures
Set forth the sign, and themselves point it out to me with a finger,
Of souls God has made His friends.
Isaiah says each soul, clothed
In its own land, shall wear a double robe,
And its land is this sweet life.
Your brother, in much fuller detail,
Where he speaks of the white robes,
Reveals this vision to us.
And first, and near the end of those words,
May they hope in you was heard above us,
And all the circles answered it;
Then a light shone bright among them,
So that if Cancer had such a crystal,
Winter would have a month made of one day.
And as there rises, goes, and enters the dance
A joyful maiden, only to honor
The bride, not because of any fault,
So I saw the brightened splendor
Come to the two who turned in a circle,
As befitted their ardent love.
It joined their song and melody,
And my lady kept her gaze on them,
Only like a silent, motionless bride.
This is he who lay upon the breast
Of our Pelican, and this man was
Chosen from the Cross for a great office.
So spoke my lady; yet for this she moved no more
Her gaze, remaining attentive,
After than before her words.
As one who watches and strains
To see the sun eclipsed a little,
And by looking becomes unable to see,
So I became before that final flame,
While it was said: Why are you dazzled
By looking at what has no place here?
On earth my body is earth, and there it shall remain
With the others until our number
Matches the eternal plan.
With their two robes into the blessed cloister
Have ascended only two lights;
And you shall carry this to your world.
At this voice the flaming circle
Fell silent, along with the sweet mingling
That arose from the sound of the three breaths,
As, to end labor or avert danger,
Oars, which had beaten the water before,
All rest at the sound of one whistle.
Ah, how deeply, how deeply I was shaken in my mind
When I turned to look at Beatrice,
Because I could not see, though I was
Near her, and in the blessed world!
