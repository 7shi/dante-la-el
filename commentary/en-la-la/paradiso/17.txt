As he came to Clymene, seeking assurance
About what he had heard said against him,
He who still makes fathers sparing toward their sons,
So was I, and so perceived
Was I by Beatrice, and by the holy flame
That earlier had changed its place for my sake.
So my lady: Send out the flame
Of your desire, she told me, so that it may emerge
Clearly marked by the eternal seal;
Not so that our knowledge may thereby grow
Through your speech, but so that you may grow used
To voicing your thirst, so that someone may give you drink.
O dear root of mine, who rise so high
That, as earthly minds can see
That two obtuse angles cannot fit in a triangle,
So you see contingent things
Before they exist in themselves, by gazing on the point
To which all times are present.
[While I was with Virgil
Up the mountain that heals the souls,
And descending into the world of the dead,
There were spoken to me about my future life
Grave words; though I feel myself
Well squared against the blows of fortune.
So my will would be content
To learn what fortune draws near to me,
For a foreseen arrow flies more slowly.]
Thus I spoke to that same light
That had spoken to me before; and, as was wished
By Beatrice, I made my desire known.
Not by riddles, in which foolish people
Were already ensnared before he was slain,
The Lamb of God who takes away sins,
But in clear words and precise
Latin, that fatherly love answered,
Enclosed in, yet visible through, its own smile.
Contingency, which beyond the book
Of your matter does not extend,
Is all painted in the eternal sight.
Yet it does not for that reason take necessity from there,
Any more than from the gaze in which is reflected
A ship descending a stream.
From that point, as there reaches the ear
Sweet harmony from an organ, there comes to me
Into sight the time prepared for you.
As Hippolytus left Athens
Because of his cruel and treacherous stepmother,
So you must leave Florence.
This is willed, and this is already being sought,
And soon it will be done by those who plan it,
Where Christ is traded every day.
The injured party will be blamed
By public outcry, as is usual; but vengeance
Will bear witness to the truth that grants it.
You will leave everything you love
Most dearly; and this is that arrow
Which exile's bow shoots first.
You will learn how salty it tastes,
Another's bread, and how hard a road it is
To go up and down another's stairs.
And what will weigh most heavily on your shoulders
Will be the wicked, foolish company
With whom you will fall into this valley;
They, all ungrateful, all foolish and wicked,
Will turn against you; but soon
They, not you, will blush.
Of their brutishness their conduct
Will give proof, so that you will be glad
To have made for yourself a party of yourself alone.
Your first refuge and first lodging
Will be the courtesy of the great Lombard,
Who bears the holy bird upon his ladder;
He will look on you so kindly
That between you two, in giving and asking,
What is slower among others will come first.
With him you will see one who was stamped,
At birth, so strongly by this star
That his deeds will be remarkable.
People have not yet noticed him,
Because of his youth; for only nine years
Have these wheels revolved around him.
But before the Gascon deceives the lofty Henry,
Sparks of his virtue will appear
In his disregard for silver and hardship.
Recognition of his magnificence
Will yet be such that his enemies
Will not be able to keep silent about it.
Look to him and to his benefits;
Through him many people will be changed,
The rich and the poor changing places.
And you will carry it written in your mind
Concerning him, but will not speak of it. He also told things
Incredible to those who are in the present.
Then he added: My son, these are the explanations
Of what was told you; here are the traps
Hidden within a few turns.
I do not want you on this account to envy your neighbors,
For your life will continue in the future
Longer than the punishment of their treachery.
After, in silence, it showed itself ready,
The holy soul, to put the weft
Into the cloth whose warp I had given it,
I began, like one eager,
Uncertain, to seek counsel from someone
Who sees, wills rightly, and loves.
My father, I clearly see how it spurs,
Time, toward me to strike me a blow
In such a way that it is heavier for one who wholly abandons himself.
Therefore it is wise that I arm myself with foresight,
So that if my dearest place is taken from me,
I do not lose the rest because of my poems.
Down through the world of endless bitterness,
And over the mountain and over the fair summit
My lady's eyes lifted me from,
And then through heaven, from light to light,
I learned what, if I repeat it,
Will taste sharply bitter to many;
And if I am a timid friend to truth,
I fear losing life among those
Who will call this time the past.
The light in which my treasure smiled,
The one I found there, grew brighter,
Like a golden mirror in a sunbeam.
Then it replied: A dark conscience,
Whether from its own or another's shame,
Will surely feel itself in your harsh words.
Nevertheless, remove all falsehood
Make your whole vision known,
And let them scratch there where the itch is.
For if your voice is unpleasant
At first taste, vital nourishment
It will leave afterward, once it has been digested.
This cry of yours will be like the wind
That strikes the highest towers hardest;
And this will be no small proof of honor.
Therefore you were shown in these spheres,
On the mountain, and in the sorrowful valley,
Only souls known for their fame;
For the mind of one who hears does not rest
Nor believe firmly on the strength of an example that has
Its root unknown and hidden,
Or any other argument that is not clear.
