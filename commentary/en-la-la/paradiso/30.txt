Perhaps six thousand miles away,
Here the sixth hour burns, and this world
Already inclines its shadow to the level ground,
When heaven's midpoint, deep to us,
Begins to become such that some star
Loses its appearance down to this earth;
And as there comes the brightest handmaid
Of the sun farther on, so the sky closes
From sight to sight, down to the fairest;
No differently the triumph, which plays
Always around the point that conquered me,
Seemed enclosed by those whom it encloses,
Little by little vanished from my sight;
Wherefore to turn my eyes back to Beatrice
And see nothing love compelled me.
If what is said of her up to now
Were all gathered into a single praise,
It would be too little to lend support on this occasion.
The beauty I saw exceeds all measure,
Not only beyond us, but indeed, I believe
Only its Maker wholly enjoys it.
At this passage I admit myself defeated,
More than ever by a point in his theme
Was any comic or tragic poet overcome.
As the sun does to eyes that tremble most,
So the memory of her sweet smile
Makes my mind forget itself.
From the first day I saw her face
In this life until this vision,
My song in following her would not have been cut short;
But now I must cease to follow
Any farther after her beauty in verse,
As every artist must at the limit of his power.
Such as I leave her, with greater praise
Than my trumpet can give, which brings
Its difficult theme to its end,
With the voice and bearing of a ready guide
She began again: We have come out
From the greatest sphere into the heaven of pure light;
Light intellectual, full of love;
Love of the true good, full of joy;
Joy that surpasses every sweetness.
Here you will see both companies
Of Paradise, and one in the forms
You will see at the final judgment.
As a sudden flash that confounds
The visual spirits, so that it deprives
The eye of the act of seeing stronger objects,
So a living light surrounded me,
And left me wrapped in such a veil
Of its brightness that nothing appeared to me.
Ever the love that dwells in this heaven
Gathers such goodness within itself,
To ready the candle for its flame.
No sooner had there entered me
These brief words than I understood
That I had risen above my own power;
And I was rekindled with new sight,
So that no light is so pure
That my eyes could not withstand it.
And I saw a light in the form of a river,
Flowing with radiance, between two banks
Painted with a wondrous early spring.
From that river issued living sparks,
And on every side they settled among the flowers,
Like rubies encircled by gold.
Then, as if drunk with fragrance,
They plunged back into the wondrous whirlpool;
When one entered, another came out.
The great desire that now inflames and urges you
To know all that you are seeing
Pleases me more the more it swells.
But you must drink from this water
Before such thirst in you is satisfied.
So spoke the sun of my eyes to me.
And she also added: The river and the topazes
That enter and leave, and the smiling grass,
Are shadowy foretastes of their true selves.
Not that these things are imperfect in themselves;
But the defect lies on your side,
For your sight is not yet so exalted.
No child rushes so swiftly
With his face toward the milk, if he wakes
Long delayed beyond his usual time,
As I did, to make better mirrors
Still of my eyes, bending toward the water
That flows, so that sight may improve there.
And as soon as there drank of it the rims
Of my eyelids, just as swiftly it seemed to me
To have changed from its length into roundness.
Then, like people who were beneath masks,
Who look unlike their former selves when they remove
The false appearance in which they were hidden,
So changed for me into greater brightness
The flowers and sparks, so that I saw
Both courts of heaven revealed.
O divine splendor, through whom I saw
The high triumph of the true kingdom,
Grant me the power to tell it as I saw it.
There is a light there that makes visible
The Creator to the creature
Who finds its peace only in seeing him;
And it extends in a circular form
So far that its circumference
Would be too wide a girdle for the sun.
All its appearance comes from a ray
Reflected from the summit of the Primum Mobile,
Which draws life and power from it.
And as a hillside in the water at its foot
Mirrors itself, seeming to see itself adorned,
When every flower is rich with green,
So, above, all around the light,
I saw reflected in more than a thousand tiers
Those who from among us had returned above.
And if the lowest tier gathers within itself
So great a light, how wide is
This rose at its outermost leaves?
My sight across its breadth and height
Did not wander, but grasped it all,
The quality and quantity of that joy.
Nearness or distance there adds nothing and takes nothing away;
For where God governs without an intermediary,
Natural law has no force.
In the gold of the eternal rose,
Which widens by tiers and is fragrant,
A fragrance rises to the Sun who ever blooms,
As one who is silent yet longs to speak,
Beatrice drew me and said: Look
How great is the assembly of white-robed souls!
See how far our city extends!
See how full our seats are,
So that few more people are desired here.
And in that great seat on which your eyes rest,
Because a crown is already placed upon it,
Before you sit at this wedding feast,
There will sit the soul, to be august below,
Of the great Henry, who, to set Italy straight,
Will come first, before it is ready.
Blind greed, which bewitches you,
Has made you like a little child
Who dies of hunger and drives his nurse away.
And in the divine court a prelate will be
Then such that, openly and in secret,
He will not walk there on the same path as Henry.
But soon God will tolerate him no longer
In his holy office; for he will be thrust down
Where Simon Magus is for his deserts,
And he will send the man of Anagni still lower.
