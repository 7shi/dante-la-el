There appeared before me with outspread wings
the beautiful image, which in sweet fruition
made joyful the souls joined together.
Each appeared a ruby, in which
a ray of the sun burned so brightly
that it reflected it into my eyes.
And what I must now relate
was never carried by a voice, nor written in ink,
nor ever grasped by imagination;
for I saw, and also heard, the beak speak,
and its voice sound I and Mine
when its thought was We and Ours.
And it began: Because I was just and merciful,
I have been exalted here to this glory,
which does not allow itself to be conquered by desire;
and on earth I left my memory
such that wicked people there
praise it, though they do not follow its example.
Thus one heat from many coals
makes itself felt, just as from many loves
only one sound came from that image.
Then I came near: O everlasting flowers
of eternal joy, who as only one
make all your fragrances appear to me,
Breathe and release me from the great hunger
that has kept me famished for so long,
finding no food for it there on earth.
I know well that if in the high kingdom of Heaven
divine justice makes itself a mirror,
your sight does not receive it through a veil.
You know how attentively I prepare
to listen; you know what this is,
the doubt that has kept me hungry so long.
Like a falcon that leaves its hood,
moves its head, and claps its wings,
showing its eagerness and preening,
I saw that sign do the same, which from the praise
of divine grace was woven,
with songs known to those who rejoice there.
Then it began: He who turned the sixth sphere
to the farthest edge of the world, and within it
distinguished so much that is hidden and manifest,
could not impress his power so fully
upon the whole universe that his Word
would not remain infinitely beyond it.
This makes certain that the first Proud One,
who was the highest of all creatures,
fell unripe because he would not wait for light;
and from this it appears that every creature
is a narrow vessel for that Good
which has no end and measures itself by itself.
Therefore your sight, which must
be one of the rays of the Mind
with which all things are filled,
cannot by its nature be so powerful
that it fails to discern its source
far beyond what appears to it.
Therefore into eternal justice
the sight that your world receives,
as an eye into the sea, is plunged;
for though from shore it sees the bottom,
in the open sea it cannot; yet
the bottom is there, but the depth hides it.
Light does not exist unless it comes from the clear
that is never clouded; otherwise it is darkness,
or the shadow of the flesh, or its poison.
Quite open to you is the hiding place
that concealed from you the living justice,
about which you so often questioned;
for you said: A man is born on the bank
of the Nile, where no one speaks
of Christ, or reads or writes about him.
All his desires and good actions,
are, as far as human reason can see,
without sin in life or speech.
He dies unbaptized and without faith:
where is the justice that condemns him?
Where is his fault, if he does not believe?
Who are you, then, who wish to sit in judgment
and judge from a thousand miles away
with a sight only a handspan long?
Indeed, anyone who would reason with me,
if Scripture were not above you,
would have cause to doubt and wonder.
O creatures of earth, O coarse minds!
The first will, which is good in itself,
has never moved away from itself, the highest Good.
A thing is just only insofar as it accords with that will.
No created good draws it to itself;
rather, by shining forth, it causes that good.
As above its nest circles,
after feeding its chicks, the stork,
and as the chick looks up at the one that fed it,
Such did it become, and so I raised my brows,
the blessed image, which its wings
had stirred toward me at such questions.
Circling, it sang and said: As
my notes are to you, who do not understand them,
so eternal judgment is to you mortals.
Then continued those shining fires
of the Holy Spirit, still arranged in the sign
that made the Romans revered in the world.
It began again: To this kingdom
no one has ever ascended without believing in Christ,
whether before or after he was nailed to the wood.
But see: many cry Christ, Christ,
yet on Judgment Day they will be much farther
from him than one who never knew Christ.
And such a Christian will be condemned by an Ethiopian
when the two companies part,
one eternally rich, the other poor.
What will the Persians be able to say to your kings
when they see the book opened,
in which all their contempt is written?
There will be seen among Albert's deeds
the one that will soon move the pen,
by which the kingdom of Prague will be deserted;
there will be seen the fraud which upon the Seine
is brought, by falsifying the coin,
by him who will die from a boar's tusk.
There will be seen the pride that makes thirsty,
and makes the Scot and the Englishman foolish,
so that neither can stay within his own bounds.
There will be seen the lust and soft living
of the man from Spain and the man from Bohemia,
who never knew or sought virtue.
There will be seen, for the Lame One of Jerusalem,
marked with an I, his goodness,
while the opposite will be marked with an M.
The greed and cowardice will be seen
of the one who rules the Island of Fire,
where Anchises ended his long life;
and to show how slight is
his writing, shortened letters will be made,
which will not say much in little space.
Everyone will see the foul deeds
of the uncle and brother, which so noble
a nation and two crowns have stained.
The man from Portugal and the man from Norway
will be known there, and the one from Rascia,
who dealt falsely with the coin of Venice.
Blessed be Hungary, if it does not let itself
be mistreated any longer! Blessed be Navarre,
if it arms itself with the mountains that enclose it!
And everyone should believe that already, as a pledge
of this, Nicosia and Famagusta
lament and cry out because of their beast,
who does not keep apart from the others.
