O chosen company for the great feast
Of the blessed Lamb, who feeds you
So that your desire is ever full;
If by God's grace this man tastes beforehand
Of what falls from your table,
Before death's time claims him,
Turn your attention to the boundless longing,
And sprinkle him a little: you drink
Always from the source whence comes what he thinks.
So spoke Beatrice; and those joyful souls
Became spheres upon fixed poles,
Blazing, wheeling like comets.
And as circles in a clock's mechanism
Turn so that the first, to one who watches,
Seems still, and the outermost seems to fly,
So those circling dances, at different speeds
Moving, by their wealth
Made me judge them swift or slow.
From the one I marked as most beautiful
I saw a fire emerge, so blessed
That it left none there that shone more brightly;
And three times it turned around Beatrice
With a song so divine
That my imagination cannot bring it back to me;
So my pen leaps over it, and I do not write it,
For our imagination, faced with such folds,
Much less our speech, has too vivid a color.
O holy sister mine, who so pray for me
Devoutly, for your ardent love
You release me from that fair sphere.
Then the blessed fire came to rest
And turned its breath toward my lady,
Which spoke thus, as I have related.
And she: O eternal light of the great man,
To whom our Lord left the keys
He brought down from this wondrous joy,
Test this man on easy and difficult points,
As you wish, concerning the faith
For which you walked upon the sea.
Whether he loves well, hopes well, and believes,
Is not hidden from you, since your sight is there,
Where everything is seen as it is painted.
But since this kingdom has made you citizens
Through the true faith, to glorify it,
It is good that you come to him to speak of it.
As a bachelor arms himself but does not speak
Until the master poses the question,
To test him, not to settle it,
So I armed myself with every argument
While she spoke, to be ready
For such a question and such a profession.
Speak, good Christian, make it plain:
What is faith? Then I raised my face
Toward that light from which this was breathed.
Then I turned to Beatrice, and she prompt
Encouragement gave me, so I might pour
The water from my inner spring.
The grace that grants me confession,
I began, from the highest first pole
May it make my thoughts clearly expressed.
And I continued: As the truthful pen
Wrote for us, Father, of your dear brother,
Who with you set Rome on the right path,
Faith is the substance of things hoped for,
And evidence of things not seen;
And this seems to me its very essence.
Then I heard: You think rightly,
If you understand well why it is placed
Among substances, and then among evidences.
And I replied at once: The deep things
That grant me their appearance here
Are so concealed from eyes below,
That there their being lies in belief alone,
On which the lofty hope is founded;
Thus it takes its meaning from substance.
From this belief we must
Reason, without having any other vision;
Thus it bears the force of evidence.
Then I heard: If everything learned
Below through teaching were understood so,
There'd be no room there for a sophist's wit.
Thus spoke that ardent love;
Then he added: Quite well tested are
This coin's alloy and weight by now.
But tell me if you have it in your purse.
I answered: Yes, I have it, so bright and so round
That I have no doubt about its stamp.
Then there came from that deep light
That shone there: This precious jewel,
On which every virtue rests,
Whence came it to you? And I replied: The ample rain
Of the Holy Spirit, poured down
Upon the old and new parchment,
Is the syllogism that proved it to me
So sharply that beside it
Every demonstration would seem blunt to me.
Then I heard: The old and new
Testament that draws this conclusion for you,
Why do you hold it to be divine speech?
And I answered: The proof that reveals the truth to me
Is the works that followed, for which nature did not
Ever heat iron or strike an anvil.
He replied to me: Tell me, what assures you
That those works were real? The very one
That proves it, if no one else swears it to you.
If the world turned to Christianity,
I said, without miracles, this is one
And such a one that all the rest are not its hundredth;
For you entered, poor and hungry,
Into the field, to sow the good plant,
Which once was a vine and now has become a thornbush.
When this was done, the high and holy court
Resounded through the spheres: We praise one God,
In the melody sung there above.
And that Baron, who so far from branch to branch,
Examining me, had already drawn me
That we were nearing the final leaves,
He began again: The grace that rules
With your mind has opened your mouth
Until now, as it should be opened;
So I approve what has come forth;
But now you must express what you believe,
And whence this came to your belief.
O holy father and spirit, who see here
What you believed so firmly that you outstripped,
Toward the Sepulcher, younger feet,
You wish me, I began, to state
The form of my ready belief here,
And you have also asked its cause.
And I answer that I believe in one God,
Alone and eternal, who moves all heaven,
Himself unmoved, with love and desire;
And for this belief I have not only proofs,
Physical and metaphysical, but it is given me
Also by the truth that rains down from here,
Through Moses, through the Psalms, and through the Prophets,
Through the Gospel, and through you who wrote
After the ardent Spirit made you wise;
And I believe in three eternal Persons, and these
I believe to be one essence, so one and so three
That together they admit of both Are and Is.
Of the deep divine nature
That I share with you, my mind is sealed
Many times by the Gospel teaching.
This is the beginning, this is the spark
That later spreads into a living flame,
And shines within me like a star in heaven.
As a lord who hears what pleases him
Then embraces his servant, rejoicing
At the news, as soon as he falls silent,
So, blessing me in song,
Three times it encircled me, as soon as I fell silent,
The apostolic light, at whose command
I had spoken; thus by speaking I pleased him.
