Oppressed by stupor, I turned to my guide
I turned, like a child who runs
Always to the one in whom he trusts most.
And she, like a mother who comes to the aid
At once of her pale and breathless child
With her voice, which soothes him well,
Said to me: Do you not know that you are in Heaven?
And do you not know that Heaven is wholly holy,
And that whatever is done here comes from good zeal?
How would song have transformed you,
And I, laughing? Now you can think on it,
Since the cry moved you so greatly;
In which, had you understood their prayers,
The vengeance would already be known to you,
Which you will see before you die.
The sword from above does not strike in haste,
Nor is it ever slow, except in the eyes
Of one who awaits it in desire or fear.
But turn now toward the others,
For you will see many illustrious spirits,
If you turn your gaze as I say.
As she wished, I turned my eyes,
And saw a hundred little spheres, which together
Grew more beautiful through their mutual rays.
I stood like one who restrains within himself
The first point of desire, and dares not
To ask, so much does he fear excess.
And the greatest and brightest
Of those pearls came forward,
To satisfy my wish through himself.
Then within it I heard: If you could see,
As I do, the charity that burns among us,
Your thoughts would be revealed;
But so that you, while waiting, may not delay
Your journey to the highest end, I shall answer
Only the thought that you are so reluctant to reveal.
That mountain, on whose slope lies Cassino,
Was once frequented on its summit
By people deceived and ill disposed.
And I am he who first carried up there
The name of Him who brought to earth
The truth that so exalts us;
And such grace shone upon me
That I drew the surrounding villages
Away from the impious worship that misled the world.
These other lights were all contemplative men,
Kindled by that warmth
Which makes holy flowers and fruits grow.
Here is Macarius, here is Romuald;
Here are my brothers, who within cloisters
Planted their feet and kept their hearts steadfast.
And I to him: The affection you show
In speaking with me, and the gracious appearance
That I see and note in all your flames,
Has enlarged my confidence
As the sun makes a rose expand, when opened
Until it reaches all its natural power.
Therefore I beg you, father, assure me
Whether I may receive so great a grace
As to see you with your image unveiled.
Then he said: Brother, your ardent desire
Will be fulfilled in the last sphere,
Where all others, and mine, are fulfilled.
There perfect nature is, and every desire
Is complete; in that sphere alone
Every part is always where it is:
For it is not in a place, nor turns upon poles;
Our ladder reaches up to it,
Which is why it is hidden from your sight.
The patriarch Jacob saw it reach
Its highest part up there,
When he saw it laden with angels.
But now no one sets foot on Earth
To climb it, and my rule
Has been left below, to the harm of its pages.
The walls that used to be an abbey
Have become dens, and the cowls
Are sacks filled with foul flour.
Yet so much grave usury is not taken
Against God's pleasure as is that fruit
Which makes monks' hearts so foolish.
For whatever the Church keeps
Belongs to those who ask for God's sake,
Not to relatives, nor to anything fouler.
Mortal flesh is so weak
That a good beginning below does not last
From the oak's birth until it bears an acorn.
Peter began without gold or silver,
I with prayers and fasting,
And Francis humbly with his order.
If you look at each one's beginning,
Then consider where it has ended,
You will see white turned to black.
Truly, the Jordan turning back
And the sea fleeing, when God willed it,
Were more wondrous to see than this help.
So he spoke to me, then withdrew
To his company; the company drew together,
Then, like a whirlwind, all gathered upward.
The sweet lady then pressed me from behind,
With a single gesture, up that ladder;
So her power overcame my nature:
Never down here, where we climb and descend,
Has nature made a movement so swift
That it could be compared with my flight.
If I ever return, reader, to that devout
Triumph, for which I often weep
For my sins and beat my breast,
You would not have passed and put
Your finger in fire in so short a time
As I saw the sign after Taurus and entered it.
O glorious stars, O light filled
With great power, from which I acknowledge
All that is in my intellect, whatever it may be;
With you he was born and set
When I first breathed the Tuscan air,
He who is father of every mortal life;
And later, when grace was granted me
To enter the high wheel that turns you,
Your region was allotted to me.
To you my soul now sighs devoutly,
To gain the strength for the hard passage
That draws it toward itself.
You are so near the final salvation,
Beatrice began, that you must have
Your eyes clear and keen.
Therefore, before you draw nearer to it,
Look down again, and see how much of the world
I have already placed beneath your feet,
So that your heart, as joyful as it can be,
May present itself to the triumphant host
That comes gladly through this rounded heaven.
With my sight I returned through all
The seven spheres and saw this globe
So small that I smiled at its lowly appearance;
And I approve as best the counsel
That considers it least; whoever thinks otherwise
Can truly be called upright.
I saw Latona's daughter ablaze,
Without the shadow that once made me think
She was rare and dense.
I endured the sight of your son, Hyperion,
There, and saw how Maia and Dione
Move around him, close beside him.
From there I saw Jove tempering his course
Between the father and the son; and from there
I understood their changing places;
All seven showed themselves to me,
How great they are, how swift,
And how far apart they rest.
The little plot of earth that makes us so fierce,
As I revolved with the eternal Twins,
Appeared all to me, from hill to shore;
Then I turned my eyes to those beautiful eyes.
