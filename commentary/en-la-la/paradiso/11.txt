O foolish concern of mortals,
How faulty are the arguments
Those that make you beat your wings downward!
Some after the law, and some after aphorisms,
Went, and some following the priesthood,
And some to rule by force or sophistry;
And some to rob, and some to civil business;
And some, wrapped in fleshly delight,
Wore themselves out, and some gave themselves to idleness;
While, released from all these things,
I was with Beatrice above in heaven,
So gloriously received.
After each had returned to that
Point of the circle where he had been before,
He stood still like a candle on its stand.
And I felt within that flame,
Which had spoken to me before, smiling,
Begin, growing brighter:
Just as I shine in its ray,
So, gazing into the eternal light,
I understand your thoughts and their cause.
You doubt, and wish to have restated
In so clear and so distinct a tongue
My words, so that your understanding may discern them,
Where I said before, where one is well nourished,
And where I said, a second did not arise;
The distinction must be made clear.
Divine Providence, which governs the world
With that counsel in which all sight
Created is overcome before it can fathom the depth,
So that toward her Beloved might go
The Bride of him who amid loud cries
Wed her with his blessed blood,
Secure in herself and also more faithful to him,
Appointed two princes for her aid,
To guide her on either side.
One burned wholly with seraphic ardor;
The other, through wisdom on earth, was
A radiance of cherubic light.
I shall speak of one, for one speaks of both
In praising one, whichever a person chooses,
Since their works aimed at one end.
Between the Tupino and the stream that descends
From the hill chosen by blessed Ubaldo,
A fertile slope hangs from a high mountain,
From which Perugia feels cold and heat
At Porta Sole, while behind it grieve,
Under the heavy ridge, Nocera and Gualdo.
On this slope, there where it breaks
Its steepest descent, a sun was born to the world,
As our sun sometimes rises from the Ganges.
So whoever speaks of that place
Should not call it Assisi, which says too little,
But East, if he would speak truly.
He was not yet far advanced from birth
When he began to make the earth feel
Some comfort from his great virtue;
Because for such a lady, while still young, into war
Against his father he went; to her, as to death,
No one opens the door of pleasure;
And before his spiritual court,
And in his father's presence, he joined himself to her;
Then day by day he loved her more.
She, bereft of her first husband,
For more than eleven hundred years despised and obscure,
Had waited without invitation until him.
Nor did it help to say that she was found secure
With Amyclas, at the sound of his voice,
By him who made the whole world fear;
Nor did her constancy and courage help,
So that, while Mary remained below,
She wept with Christ upon the cross.
But so that I may not speak too obscurely,
Francis and Poverty as these lovers
Understand from now on in my extended speech.
Their accord and their happy looks,
Love, wonder, and tender gaze
Gave cause for holy thoughts;
So much so that venerable Bernard
First took off his shoes and after such peace
Ran, and, as he ran, thought himself too late.
O unknown riches, O fruitful good!
Egidio took off his shoes, and so did Silvester,
They followed the bridegroom; such was the bride's appeal.
Then that father, that teacher, set out
With his lady and with his family
Already bound by the humble halter;
The lowliness of heart did not weigh down his brow,
Though he was Pietro Bernadone's son,
Nor because he seemed contemptible in the eyes of onlookers;
But his firm resolve he plainly
Disclosed to Innocent, and from him received
The first seal for his Order.
After the poor little band had grown
Behind this man, whose wondrous life
Would be better sung in the glory of heaven,
With a second crown enwreathed
Was, by Honorius, at the eternal Spirit's prompting,
The holy will of this shepherd-prince.
And after, thirsting for martyrdom,
Before the proud Sultan,
He preached Christ, and others who followed him;
And because he found hardened against conversion
To excess the people, and so as not to remain in vain,
He returned to the fruit of Italian soil;
On the rugged rock between the Tiber and Arno,
He received from Christ the final seal,
Which his limbs bore for two years.
When he who had chosen him for so great a good
Was pleased to draw him up to the reward
He earned by making himself so small,
To his brothers, as rightful heirs,
He entrusted his dearest lady,
And charged them to love her faithfully;
And from his embrace the noble soul
Longed to depart, returning to its kingdom,
And wanted no other bier for its body.
Now consider who it was who a worthy
Companion was in steering the boat
Of Peter across the high sea by the true course!
And this was our Patriarch;
Therefore, whoever follows him as he commands,
You can see that he carries good merchandise.
But his flock, for new food,
Has grown greedy, so that it cannot be
But that it scatters by many leaps;
And in measure as his sheep, distant
And wandering, go farther from him,
The emptier of milk they return to the fold.
Some do fear harm
And cling to the shepherd; but they are so few
That little cloth would furnish their cloaks.
Now, if my words are not faint,
And if you have listened attentively,
If you recall all that I have said,
In part your wish will be fulfilled,
For you will see the root from which it branches;
You will see the correction that explains
Where one is well nourished, if one does not go astray.
