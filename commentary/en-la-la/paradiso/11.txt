O foolish concern of mortals,
How faulty are the arguments
That make you beat your wings downward!
Some pursued the law, some aphorisms,
Some followed the priesthood,
Some sought to rule by force or sophistry;
Some to rob, some to civil business;
Some, wrapped in fleshly delight,
Wore themselves out; some gave themselves to idleness;
While I, released from all these things,
Was with Beatrice above in heaven,
So gloriously received.
After each had returned
To the point of the circle where he had been,
He stood still like a candle on its stand.
And I felt within that flame,
Which had spoken to me before, smiling,
Begin, growing brighter:
As I shine in its ray,
So, gazing into the eternal light,
I understand your thoughts and their cause.
You doubt, and wish my words
To be restated clearly and fully,
So that your understanding may discern them,
Where I said before, where one is well nourished,
And where I said, a second did not arise;
Here the distinction must be made clear.
Providence, which governs the world
With counsel beyond all created sight
Before it can fathom the depth,
So that his Bride might go to her Beloved,
The one who amid loud cries
Wed her with his blessed blood,
Secure in herself and more faithful to him,
Appointed two princes for her aid,
To guide her on either side.
One burned wholly with seraphic ardor;
The other, through wisdom on earth,
Shone with cherubic light.
I shall speak of one, for in praising either
A person praises both, whichever he chooses,
Since their works aimed at one end.
Between the Tupino and the stream
That descends from the hill chosen by blessed Ubaldo,
A fertile slope hangs from a high mountain,
From which Perugia feels cold and heat
At Porta Sole, while behind it grieve,
Under the heavy ridge, Nocera and Gualdo.
On this slope, where it breaks
Its steepest descent, a sun was born to the world,
As our sun sometimes rises from the Ganges.
So whoever speaks of that place
Should not call it Assisi, which says too little,
But East, if he would speak truly.
He was not far advanced from birth
When he began to make the earth feel
Some comfort from his great virtue;
For such a lady, while still young, he went
To war against his father, who, like death,
Opens no door to pleasure;
And before his spiritual court,
In his father's presence, he joined himself to her;
Then day by day he loved her more.
Bereft of her first husband,
For more than eleven hundred years despised and obscure,
She had waited without invitation until him.
Nor did it help that he found her secure
With Amyclas, at the sound of his voice,
He who made the whole world fear;
Nor did her constancy and courage help,
Though Mary remained below,
While she wept with Christ upon the cross.
But so that I may not speak too obscurely,
From now on in my extended speech
Understand Francis and Poverty as these lovers.
Their joy and happy looks,
Love, wonder, and tender gaze
Gave cause for holy thoughts;
So that venerable Bernard
First took off his shoes and ran after such peace,
And, as he ran, thought himself too late.
O unknown riches, O fruitful good!
Egidio took off his shoes, and so did Silvester,
They followed the bridegroom; such was the bride's appeal.
Then that father and teacher set out
With his lady and the family
Already bound by the humble halter;
The lowliness of heart did not weigh down his brow,
Though he was Pietro Bernadone's son,
Nor did he shrink from seeming contemptible;
But he plainly disclosed his firm resolve
To Innocent, and from him received
The first seal for his Order.
After the poor little band had grown
Behind this man, whose wondrous life
Would be sung more gloriously in heaven,
The holy will of this shepherd-prince
Was crowned a second time by Honorius,
Inspired by the eternal Spirit.
Then, thirsting for martyrdom,
He preached Christ before the proud Sultan,
And those who followed him;
Finding the people too hardened to convert,
And unwilling to remain there in vain,
He returned to the fruit of Italian soil;
On the rugged rock between the Tiber and Arno,
He received from Christ the final seal,
Which his limbs bore for two years.
When he who had chosen him for so great a good
Was pleased to draw him up to the reward
He earned by making himself so small,
He entrusted to his brothers, as rightful heirs,
His dearest lady,
And charged them to love her faithfully;
And from his embrace the noble soul
Longed to depart, returning to its kingdom,
And wanted no other bier for its body.
Now consider who it was, so worthy
To be his companion in steering Peter's boat
Across the high sea by the true course!
This was our Patriarch. Whoever follows him
As he commands, can see
That he carries good merchandise.
But his flock has grown greedy
For new food, so that it cannot help
Scattering by many leaps;
The farther his sheep stray
And wander from him,
The emptier of milk they return to the fold.
Some do fear harm
And cling to the shepherd; but they are so few
That little cloth would furnish their cloaks.
Now, if my words are not faint,
And you have listened attentively,
If you recall what I have said,
In part your wish will be fulfilled,
For you will see the root from which it branches;
You will see the correction that explains
Where one is well nourished, if one does not go astray.
