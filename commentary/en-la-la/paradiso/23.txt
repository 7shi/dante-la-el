Like a bird among beloved leaves,
Quiet by the nest of its dear young,
In the night that hides things from us,
Which, to see the faces it longs for,
And to find food with which to feed them,
In which its hard labors are sweet to it,
It comes before the time upon the open bough,
And with ardent longing watches for the sun,
Gazing fixedly, only to see whether dawn will rise;
So my Lady stood upright
And attentive, turned toward the quarter
Where the sun shows less haste;
So that, when I saw her intent and eager,
I became like one who, while longing
Would wish for something else, and finds comfort in hope.
But little time passed between one and the other,
I mean between my waiting and my seeing
I saw the heavens grow brighter and brighter.
And Beatrice said: Behold the ranks
Of Christ's triumph, and all the fruit
Gathered from the revolutions of these spheres.
It seemed to me that her face was all aflame,
And her eyes were so full of joy
That I must pass on without description.
As, in clear full moons,
Trivia smiles among the eternal nymphs,
Who adorn the heavens in every quarter,
I saw, above thousands of lamps,
One sun that kindled them all,
As our sun kindles the sights above;
And through the living light there shone
A shining substance so bright
To my sight that it could not endure it.
O Beatrice, sweet and dear guide!
And she said to me: He who surpasses you
Is a power against which nothing can defend itself.
There are wisdom and might there,
Which opened the roads from Heaven to earth,
For which there was such long yearning.
As fire is released from a cloud
To spread, because the cloud cannot contain it,
And by its nature is driven downward;
So my mind, among those feasts,
Made greater, went out of itself,
And cannot remember what became of it then.
Open your eyes, and look at what I am;
You have seen things through which strong enough
You have become to endure my smile.
I was like one who wakes
From a vision forgotten, and strives
In vain to bring it back to mind,
When I heard this offer, worthy
Of such honor that it will never fade
From the book that records the past.
If now all the tongues were sounding
That Polyhymnia, with her sisters, made
Fatter with their sweetest milk,
To help me, to a thousandth part of the truth
They would not attain, singing of the holy smile
And how clearly the holy face made it shine.
And so, depicting Paradise,
The sacred poem must leap,
Like one who finds his path cut off.
But whoever considered the weighty theme
And the mortal shoulder that bears its load
Would not blame it for trembling beneath it.
This is no passage for a little boat,
Where the daring prow cleaves its way,
Nor for a helmsman who spares himself.
Why does my face so draw you to love
That you do not turn toward the fair garden
Blooming beneath Christ's rays?
There is the rose in which the divine Word
Was made flesh; there are the lilies,
By whose fragrance the good path was taken.
So Beatrice spoke. And I, who for her counsel
Was wholly ready, again returned
To the battle of my weak eyes.
As when a ray of sunlight passes pure
Through a broken cloud upon a flowered meadow,
So I saw my eyes veiled in shadow;
So I saw companies of splendors
Lit from above by burning rays,
Without seeing where the brightness began.
O gracious power, who impresses them so,
So high did you rise, to grant me a place
For eyes there that were not strong enough for me.
The name of the fair flower, which I always invoke
At morning and at evening, wholly drew my
Mind to gaze upon the greatest flame.
And as soon as it had shown my eyes
The quality and greatness of the living star,
Who there above conquers as she conquered below,
Through the heavens descended a little flame,
Shaped in a circle like a crown,
And encircled her, revolving around her.
Any melody that sounds sweeter
Here below, and draws the soul to itself more strongly,
Would seem a cloud that thunders as it bursts,
Compared with the sound of that lyre
That crowned the fair sapphire,
By which the heavens are made more sapphire-bright.
I am angelic love, circling
The lofty joy that breathes from the womb
That was the dwelling of our desire;
And I shall circle, Lady of Heaven, up to the time
When you follow your Son and make divine
To a still greater degree the highest sphere, as he enters it.
So the circling melody
Was sealed, and all the other lights
Made the name of Mary resound.
The royal mantle of all the spheres
Of the world, which moves most swiftly and is most enlivened
By God's breath and by nature,
Had its inner rim above us
So far away that its appearance
Was still invisible where I stood.
Therefore my eyes had no power
To follow the crowned flame,
Which rose near its offspring.
And as a little child, who toward its mother
Stretches out its arms after taking milk,
Because its spirit is kindled all the way to the right place,
So each of those bright ones stretched out
With its crown, so that the great love
They bore for Mary was made clear to me.
Then they remained there before my eyes,
Singing Queen of Heaven so sweetly
That the delight never left me.
O how great is the abundance stored
In those richest chests, who were
For sowing here below good plowmen!
There one lives and rejoices in the treasure
Won with tears in the exile
Of Babylon, where they left their gold.
There triumphs, beneath the high Son
Of God and Mary, for his victory,
Both with the old and with the new council,
He who holds the keys to such glory.
