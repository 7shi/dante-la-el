When he who illumines the whole world
Descends from our hemisphere,
Whereby day is spent on every side,
Heaven, which first is lit only by him,
Suddenly appears again
Through many lights that shine within it.
And this act of heaven came to my mind,
As soon as the sign of the world and its rulers
Fell silent in its blessed beak;
For all those living lights,
Much brighter, began songs
Fragile and fleeting in my memory.
O sweet love, who cloak yourself in joy,
How ardent you appeared in those flames
That held only the spirit of holy thoughts!
After the bright and shining stones,
From which I saw the sixth light adorned with gems,
Silenced their angelic notes,
I seemed to hear the murmur of a river
That descends clear, down from stone to stone,
Revealing the abundance of its source.
And as a sound at the neck of a lyre
Takes shape, and as in the opening
Of a pipe does the wind that enters,
So, far from waiting for any delay,
That murmur of the Eagle rose
Up through its neck, as if it were hollow.
A voice was formed there, and from there it came forth
Through its beak in the form of words,
Such as the heart awaited, where I wrote them.
The part in me that sees and endures the sun
Through mortal eyes began to tell me:
Now you must gaze intently;
For among the spirits from whom my figure is formed,
Those who make the eye sparkle in my head
Are the highest of their whole order.
He who shines in the middle as the pupil
Was the singer of the Holy Spirit,
Who carried the Ark from town to town:
Now he knows the merit of his song,
How great was the effect of his purpose,
By the reward, which is equal to it.
Of the five who form a circle around my brow,
The one who is nearest my beak
Comforted the widow for her son:
Now he knows the great price of
Not following Christ, through his experience
Of this sweet life and its opposite.
And he who follows along the circumference,
The one of whom I speak, along the upper arc,
Delayed death through true repentance:
Now he knows that eternal judgment
Is not changed when worthy prayers
Turn today into tomorrow.
The next, who follows, with the laws, with me,
Under a good intention that bore bad fruit,
Became Greek to yield to the Shepherd:
Now he knows how the evil that arose
From his good deeds does him no harm,
Although the world was thereby destroyed.
And he whom you see on the descending arc
Was William, whom that land mourns,
Though it weeps for Charles and Frederick alive:
Now he knows how love takes hold
Of heaven for a just king, and by the sign
Of his radiance he makes it seen as well.
Who would believe in the wandering world below
That Ripheus the Trojan in this circle
Was the fifth of the holy lights?
Now he knows much of what the world
Cannot see of divine grace,
Though its sight cannot fathom the depths.
Like a little lark that ranges in the air,
First singing, then falling silent, content
With the final joy that satisfies it;
Such seemed to me the image of the imprint
Of eternal delight, by whose desire
Each thing becomes what it is.
And although my doubt was
Like clear glass to the color that clothes it,
It could not bear to await the time in silence;
But from the mouth: What are these things?
It thrust forth for me with all the force of its weight;
For I had seen a great display of flashes.
Then, close by, with its eye more aflame,
The blessed sign answered me,
Not to leave me suspended in wonder:
I see that you believe these things
Because I say them, but do not see how;
So, though believed, they remain hidden.
You are like one who a thing by its name
Grasps well; but its nature
He cannot see unless another reveals it.
The kingdom of heaven suffers force
From ardent love and living hope,
Which overcome the divine will;
Not as one man overcomes another,
But it overcomes her because she wills to be overcome,
And, overcome, she overcomes through her goodness.
The first life of the brow and the fifth
Make you marvel, because you do not see
The realm of angels depicted.
They did not leave their bodies, as you believe,
As pagans, but as Christians, firm in faith:
One believed in the feet yet to suffer, the other in feet that had suffered.
For one soul from Hell, whence no one returns
Ever to good will, returned to its bones;
And this was the reward of living hope;
Of living hope, which placed its power
In the prayers made to God to revive her,
So that her will might be moved.
The glorious soul of whom we speak,
Returned to the flesh in which it had been briefly,
Believed in him who could help it;
And by believing it was kindled in such a fire
Of true love that, at its second death,
It was worthy to come to this place.
The other, through grace that from so deep
A fountain drips that ever any creature
Has not plunged its eye to the first wave,
Set all his love below on righteousness;
Wherefore, from grace to grace, God opened
His eye to our future redemption:
And he believed in it, and could not bear
From then on the stench of paganism any longer,
And rebuked its perverse peoples.
Those three ladies were his baptism,
Whom you saw at the right wheel,
More than a thousand years before baptism.
O predestination, how far removed
Is your root from those eyes
That do not see the first cause entire!
And you, mortals, restrain yourselves
From judging; for we, who see God,
Do not yet know all the elect;
And such a lack is sweet to us,
For our good is refined in this good,
Since what God wills, we also will.
Thus from that divine image,
To make my short sight clear to me,
A gentle remedy was given to me.
And as, for a good singer, a good lyrist
Makes the pluck of the string follow,
In which the song gains most delight,
So, as it spoke, I remember
That I saw the two blessed lights there,
In the way the blinking of eyes moves in harmony,
Move their flames with the words.
