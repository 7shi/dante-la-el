From center to circle and from circle to center,
Water moves in a round vessel,
According as it is struck from outside or within.
This that I say made a sudden impression
Upon my mind, just as the glorious life
Of Thomas fell silent,
Because of the likeness that arose
Between his speech and that of Beatrice,
Whom it pleased to begin after him:
This man needs, though he does not tell you,
Either in speech or even in thought yet,
To reach the root of another truth.
Tell him whether the light from which
Your substance flowers will remain with you
Forever, just as it is now;
And if it remains, tell how, after
You have been made visible again,
It can be that sight will not harm you.
As those who dance in a ring are sometimes
Moved and drawn by greater joy,
They raise their voices and brighten their gestures;
So the holy circles, prompt and devout
In their response, showed new delight
In circling round and in wondrous song.
Whoever laments that people die here,
Because they would live up there, has not seen
The refreshment of the eternal rain.
That One, Two, and Three who ever lives,
And reigns forever in Three, Two, and One,
Unbounded, yet bounding all,
Was sung three times by each
Of those spirits, in such melody
That it would be a just reward for every merit.
And I heard in the more divine light
A modest voice from the smaller circle,
Perhaps like the angel's voice to Mary,
Answer: As long as the feast of Paradise
Will last, so long our love
Will radiate around this garment.
Its brightness will follow ardor,
Ardor, vision; and vision is as great
As the grace that exceeds one's capacity.
When our glorious, holy flesh
Is clothed again, our person
Will be more pleasing because it is whole.
For what the Supreme Good gives us
Of gratuitous light will increase,
The light that enables us to see him:
Therefore vision must increase,
And ardor that is kindled from it must increase,
And the ray that comes from ardor must increase.
But as a coal that gives out flame,
And by its living glow outshines it,
Yet keeps its own appearance visible,
So this radiance that now surrounds us
Will be surpassed in appearance by the flesh
That earth still covers all around;
Nor will such light be able to weary us,
For the body's organs will be strong
For all that can delight us.
So ready and prompt, it seemed to me,
Were both choirs in saying Amen,
That they showed their desire for dead bodies;
Perhaps not only for themselves, but for their mothers,
Their fathers, and the others they had loved,
Before they became eternal flames.
And then around them, of equal brightness,
A new radiance arose above the former,
Like a horizon growing bright.
And as new sights begin to appear
Across the sky at the start of evening,
So that what one sees seems real and unreal,
New beings seemed to me
To begin to appear and form a ring
Outside the other two circles.
O true flashing of the Holy Spirit,
How sudden and glowing it became
To my eyes, which, overcome, could not endure it!
But Beatrice, so beautiful and smiling,
Appeared to me that among those visions
I must leave off, for my mind could not follow.
Then my eyes recovered their power
To rise again, and I saw myself transported
Alone with my lady to a higher blessedness.
I well perceived I had been raised higher,
By the fiery smile of the star,
Which seemed redder to me than usual.
With all my heart and with that speech
That is one in all, I made a holocaust to God,
As was fitting for this new grace;
The ardor of the sacrifice had not yet
Left my breast when I knew
That the offering had been accepted and welcomed;
For with such brightness and such redness
Shining souls appeared to me within two rays,
That I said: O Helios, who adorns them so!
Like the Milky Way, distinguished by greater and lesser
Lights, shines white between the world's poles,
And makes even the wise uncertain,
So, arranged in constellations, those rays
Made the venerable sign deep within Mars,
Which the four quarters form in a circle.
Here my memory defeats my ingenuity,
For that cross flashed forth Christ,
And I cannot find an adequate example.
But he who takes up his cross and follows Christ
Will still excuse me for what I leave unsaid,
Seeing Christ flash in that whiteness.
From arm to arm, and between top and bottom,
Lights moved, flashing brightly
As they joined together and passed through.
As here one sees straight and crooked,
Fast and slow, renewing the sight,
Particles of bodies, long and short,
Moving through the ray that sometimes marks
The shadow which people acquire
For their defense through skill and craft.
And as the gyga and harp, with many
Sweet strings stretched in tune, make a sweet ringing
For one who does not understand the melody,
So from the lights that appeared to me
A melody gathered across the Cross,
Carrying me away without my understanding the hymn.
I well perceived it was high praise,
For I heard Rise and conquer,
As one who does not understand, yet hears.
I fell so deeply in love with it
That until then nothing had bound me
With bonds so sweet.
Perhaps my words seem too bold,
Putting the pleasure of those lovely eyes second,
In which my desire finds rest when I gaze.
But whoever understands that the first seals
Of all beauty grow more refined higher up,
And that I had not yet turned toward them,
Can excuse me for what I accuse myself of,
In order to excuse myself, and see that I speak truly:
For holy pleasure was not excluded here,
Since it grows purer as one ascends.
