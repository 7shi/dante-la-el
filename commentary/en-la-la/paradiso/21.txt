My eyes were already fixed upon the face
Of my lady, and my mind with them
Had turned away from every other thought;
And she did not smile. But if I smiled,
She began, you would become as Semele was
When she was turned to ash;
For my beauty, which grows more aflame
As it climbs the stairs of the eternal palace,
As you have seen, the higher one ascends,
If it were not tempered, would shine so brightly
That your mortal strength, before its splendor,
Would be a branch struck down by thunder.
We have been raised to the seventh splendor,
Which now beneath the breast of the ardent Lion
Sends down its mingled influence.
Fix your mind behind your eyes,
And make them mirrors for the image
That will appear to you in this mirror.
Whoever knew what nourishment my gaze
Found in that blessed sight,
When I turned from my former concern,
Would know how glad I was
To obey my heavenly guide,
Weighing one side against the other.
Within the crystal that bears the name
Of its beloved leader as it circles the world,
Under whom all malice lay dead,
I saw a ladder standing upright,
Golden in color, through which a ray shone,
So high that my sight could not follow it.
I also saw so many splendors descend
Down its steps that I thought every light
Seen in heaven was spread from there.
And as, by their natural habit,
Jackdaws move together at the start of day
To warm their cold wings,
Then some fly off and do not return,
Others turn back to where they came from,
And others wheel about and linger,
So it seemed to me those spirits did
In the sparks that gathered together,
As soon as they struck a certain step.
And the one that stayed nearest to me
Grew so bright that I thought to myself:
I see well, O love, the sign you give me.
But she, from whom I await the how and when
Of speaking or keeping silent, remains still; so,
Against my wish, I do well not to ask.
Since she, who saw my silence
In the sight of Him who sees all,
Said to me: Give voice to your ardent wish.
And I began: My merit
Does not make me worthy of your answer;
But for her sake, who permits me to ask,
Blessed life, hidden
Within your joy, make known to me
The reason you have drawn so near to me;
And tell me why, in this sphere,
The sweet harmony of Paradise is silent,
Though it sounds so devoutly in the others below.
You have mortal hearing, as you have mortal sight,
It answered me; the reason no song is heard here
Is the same reason Beatrice did not smile.
Down the steps of the holy ladder
I descended only to delight you
With my words and the light that surrounds me;
No greater love made me quicker,
For love as great and greater burns here above,
As my flaming shows you.
But high charity, which makes us servants,
Ready to the counsel that governs the world,
Allots us places here, as you can see.
I see well, I said, sacred lamp,
How free love in this court
Suffices to follow eternal providence;
But this is what I find hard to discern:
Why were you alone predestined
For this task among your companions?
I had not yet reached the last word
When that light made its center its own middle,
Turning like a swift wheel.
Then the love within it replied:
Divine light is focused upon me,
Passing through the light that clothes me;
Its power, joined with my sight,
Raises me above myself so far that I see
The supreme essence from which it comes.
From this comes the joy that makes me flame,
And as far as my vision is clear,
I match the brightness of my flame.
But neither the soul in heaven that shines most brightly,
Nor the seraph who fixes his gaze most on God,
Could answer your question;
For what you ask lies so deeply
In the abyss of the eternal decree
That it is cut off from every created sight.
When you return to the mortal world,
Report this, so that no one presumes
To set foot farther toward so hidden a goal.
The mind that shines here smokes on earth;
So consider how it can see down there
What it cannot, even when heaven raises it.
His words so stung me
That I left the question and turned
To ask him humbly who he was.
Between Italy's two shores rise rocky heights,
Not very far from my homeland,
So high that thunder sounds far below,
And form a ridge called Catria,
Beneath which a hermitage is consecrated,
Set apart for worship of God alone.
Thus he began his third speech again;
Then, continuing, he said: There
I devoted myself so firmly to God's service
That, with food made only from olive oil,
I passed easily through heat and cold,
Content with contemplative thoughts.
That cloister used to bear fruit abundantly
For these heavens, but now it has become barren,
So it must soon be revealed.
In that place I was Peter of Damian;
And I was Peter the Sinner in the house
Of Our Lady on the Adriatic shore.
Little of my mortal life remained
When I was summoned and drawn to that hat
That keeps passing from bad to worse.
Cephas came, and the great vessel
Of the Holy Spirit came, lean and barefoot,
Taking food from any lodging.
Now the modern shepherds want attendants
At every side to support and lead them,
So heavy are they, and someone to lift their train.
They cover their palfreys with their mantles,
So that two beasts go beneath one skin.
O patience, how much you endure!
At these words I saw many little flames
Descend from step to step and turn;
Each turn made them more beautiful.
They gathered and stood around this spirit,
And raised a cry of such great sound
That none here could be compared with it;
I did not understand it, so thunder overcame me.
