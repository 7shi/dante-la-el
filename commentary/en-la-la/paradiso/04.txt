Between two foods, distant and enticing
in equal measure, one would die of hunger first,
before a free man brought one to his teeth.
So would a lamb stand between two desires,
equally fearing the fierce wolves,
so would a dog stand between two does.
Therefore, if I kept silent, I do not blame myself,
equally driven by my doubts,
since silence was necessary; I commend myself.
I kept silent, and my desire was painted
on my face, and with it my question,
far clearer than distinct speech could make it.
Then Beatrice acted as Daniel did,
when he lifted Nebuchadnezzar from the anger
that had made him unjustly fierce.
And she said: I see well how you are pulled
by one desire and the other, so that your concern
binds itself so that it cannot breathe outward.
You reason: If good will endures,
why does another person's violence
lessen my measure of merit?
Another reason for doubt is given to you:
to see souls return to the stars,
according to Plato's teaching.
These are the questions that in your will
prick equally; so first
I shall discuss the one with the sharper sting.
Of the Seraphim, he who is most divine,
Moses, Samuel, and that John
whom you would choose, I say the same of Mary,
do not have their seats in another heaven
than the spirits who have just appeared to you,
nor do they have more or fewer years of being;
but all adorn the highest sphere,
and their sweet life differs in degree,
as they feel more or less of the eternal breath.
They appeared here, not because assigned
was this sphere to them, but to give a sign
of the heavenly realm that has a lower ascent.
Speaking thus suits your understanding,
since only from what the senses perceive does it grasp
what it afterward makes fit for the intellect.
Therefore Scripture accommodates itself
to your capacity, and feet and hands
it attributes to God while meaning something else;
and Holy Church in human form
shows Gabriel and Michael to you,
and the other one who restored Tobias to health.
What the Timaeus argues about the soul
is unlike what is seen here,
since, as it says, it seems to hold that view.
He says the soul returns to its star,
believing it was cut from there
when nature gave it form.
And perhaps his teaching means something else
than its words seem to say, and may be such
with an intention not to be mocked.
If he means that to these spheres return
honor and blame for influence, perhaps
his bow strikes somewhere upon the truth.
This principle, misunderstood, has twisted
by now almost the whole world, so that to Jupiter,
Mercury, and Mars it ran in worship.
The other doubt that troubles you
has less poison, since its falsehood
cannot lead you away from me elsewhere.
For our justice to appear unjust
in mortal eyes is an argument
of faith, not heretical wickedness.
But since your understanding can
well penetrate this truth,
as you desire, I shall satisfy you.
If violence is when the one who suffers
contributes nothing to the one using force,
these souls would not be excused by it;
for the will, if it does not consent, is never quenched,
but acts as fire naturally does,
though violence twist it a thousand times;
for if it yields, much or little,
it follows force; and these women did so,
though they could return to the holy place.
If their will had been whole,
like the one Lawrence held upon the gridiron,
and the one that made Mucius severe toward his hand,
so it would have driven them back along the road
from which they were taken, once they were free;
but such a steadfast will is far too rare.
And on account of these words, if you have gathered them
well as you should, the argument is empty
that would have troubled you again and again.
But now another obstacle crosses your path,
before your eyes, so that alone
you could not get past it before growing weary.
I have firmly placed in your mind
that a blessed soul cannot lie,
because it is always near the first truth;
and then you could hear from Piccarda
that Constance kept her affection for the veil,
so that she seems here to contradict me.
It has often happened already, brother,
that to escape danger someone unwillingly
does what ought not to be done;
Like Alcmæon, who, asked to do this
by his father, killed his own mother,
and to keep his piety became impious.
At this point I want you to consider
how force mingles with the will, and they make it
so that the offenses cannot be excused.
Absolute will does not consent to harm,
but consents to it only as far as it fears
that withdrawing would bring greater distress.
So when Piccarda speaks of this,
she means qualified will, while I mean
the other kind; thus together we tell the truth.
Such was the current of the holy stream
that flowed from the source from which all truth comes;
it brought both desires to peace.
O beloved of the first Lover, O goddess,
I said then, whose speech floods me
and warms me, making me more and more alive,
my affection is not deep enough
to repay you with gratitude for gratitude;
but may the One who sees and can do so answer for it.
I see clearly that never is it satisfied,
our intellect, unless the truth enlightens it,
beyond which no truth can range.
It rests in it like a beast in its lair
as soon as it reaches it, and it can reach it;
otherwise every desire would be in vain.
From this is born, like a young shoot,
beside the truth, doubt; and it has a nature
that drives us from height to height toward the summit.
This invites me, this reassures me,
with reverence, lady, to ask you
about another truth that is obscure to me.
I want to know whether a person can satisfy you
for unfulfilled vows in such a way with other good deeds
that they are not too light on your scales.
Beatrice looked at me with eyes full
of sparks of love, so divine
that my strength overcome, I turned away,
and nearly lost myself, eyes lowered.
