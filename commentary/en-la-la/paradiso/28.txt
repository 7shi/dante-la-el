After, against the present life
Of wretched mortals, truth had been spoken by
She who holds my mind in Paradise;
As a candle's flame in a mirror
Is seen by one who lights the way for those behind,
Before it comes within sight or thought,
And turns to see whether the glass
Tells him true, and finds it agrees
With it, as note agrees with measure;
So my memory recalls
What I did, gazing into those lovely eyes,
Where Love had made the cord to capture me.
And as soon as I turned, there were struck
My eyes by what appears in that volume,
However steadily I gazed around it,
I saw a point that sent out light,
So sharp that the sight it burns
Must close against its piercing brilliance;
And the smallest star seen here
Would seem a moon placed beside it,
As one star is set beside another.
Perhaps by as much as there seems nearer
The halo to the light that paints it,
When the vapor carrying it is thickest,
Distant around the point, a circle of fire
Revolved so swiftly that it would outstrip
The motion that most swiftly girds the world.
This circle was enclosed by another,
That one by a third, and the third by a fourth,
The fourth by a fifth, and then the fifth by a sixth.
Above them followed the seventh, so wide
Already in breadth, that Juno's messenger
In its entirety would be too narrow to contain it.
So too the eighth and ninth; and each
Moved more slowly according as it was
Farther in number from the One:
And that one had the purest flame
Which was nearest to the pure spark;
Because, I think, it held more of its truth.
My lady, seeing me in concern
So deep, held in suspense, said: From that point
Heaven and all nature depend.
Look at the circle most closely joined to it;
And know that its motion is so swift
Because burning love has pierced it.
And I said to her: If the world were arranged
In the order I see in those wheels,
It would satisfy what has been proposed to me.
But in the sensible world it is possible
To see revolutions so much more divine,
The farther they are removed from the center.
So, if my desire is to be fulfilled
In this wondrous, angelic temple,
Bounded only by love and light,
I must hear how what is patterned
And the pattern do not follow the same rule;
For by myself I ponder this in vain.
If your fingers are not, for such a knot,
Strong enough, it is no wonder;
So firm has it grown from being left untried.
So spoke my lady; then she said: Take
What I shall tell you, if you wish to be satisfied,
And ponder it closely.
The circles that appear are wide or narrow
According to the greater or lesser power
That extends through all their parts.
Greater goodness wills to make greater blessedness;
Greater blessedness contains a greater body,
If its parts are equally complete.
Therefore to this one, which draws the whole
Of the rest of the universe with it, corresponds
The circle that loves and knows most.
For if to power you apply
Your measure, not to the appearance
Of substances that look round to you,
You will see a wondrous correspondence,
Greater to greater, lesser to lesser,
In every heaven with its intelligence.
As bright and clear remains
The hemisphere of air, when there blows
Boreas from the cheek where he is gentlest,
Because he cleans and dissolves the haze
That first clouded it, so that heaven smiles
With the beauties of its whole domain;
So did I, after I was provided by
My lady with her clear answer;
And truth appeared like a star in heaven.
And after her words had ceased,
No otherwise does iron throw off sparks
When it glows than did the circles sparkle.
Each spark was followed by its flame;
They were so many that their number
Mounts into thousands beyond the doubling of the chessboard.
I heard Hosanna sung from choir to choir
To the fixed point, by those who rejoice in their places,
Forever eternal, where they always were.
And she, who saw the doubts
In my mind, said: The first circles
Have shown you Seraphim and Cherubim.
Thus swiftly they follow their bonds,
To make themselves like the point as far as they can;
And their power is measured by the height of their vision.
Those other loves that circle around it
Are called Thrones of the divine aspect,
For they complete the first triad.
And know that all have delight
In proportion to how deeply their vision
Plunges into the Truth where every intellect finds rest.
Hence one can see how the foundation
Of blessedness lies in the act of seeing,
Not in the act of loving, which follows;
And the reward is the measure of vision,
Which God's grace gives birth to, with good will;
Thus we proceed from rank to rank.
The other triad, flowering so
In this everlasting first spring,
Which nocturnal Aries does not strip bare,
Sings Hosanna forever, sweetly,
With three melodies sounding in three
Orders of joy, which grow within.
In this hierarchy are the other two orders:
First Dominations, then Virtues;
The third order is the Powers.
Then in the two final dances
Principalities and Archangels revolve;
The last is all angelic play.
All these orders look upward,
And below they prevail, so that toward God
All are drawn, and all draw others.
And Dionysius with such eagerness
Devoted himself to contemplating these orders
That he named and distinguished them as I do.
But Gregory later differed from him;
And as soon as he opened his eyes
In this heaven, he laughed at himself.
And if such truth was spoken so precisely
By a mortal on earth, I do not want you to wonder;
For he who saw it here above revealed it to him,
Along with much more truth about these circles.
