In the form, then, of a pure white rose
The holy host appeared to me,
Whom Christ made his bride in his own blood.
But the other host, which in its flight sees and sings
The glory of Him who draws it to love,
And the goodness that makes it so great,
Like a swarm of bees that goes among the flowers,
One time going out, another returning
To where its labor becomes sweet,
It descended into the great flower, adorned
With so many petals, and from there rose again
To where its love always dwells.
Their faces were all of living flame,
Their wings of gold, and all the rest was white,
Whiter than any snow can be.
When they descended into the flower, from white to white
They brought peace and ardor,
Which they gained by beating their wings.
Nor did so great a multitude in flight,
Coming between the upper part and the flower,
Obstruct the sight or the radiance;
For divine light penetrates
Through the universe according to its worth,
So that nothing can obstruct it.
This secure and joyful realm,
Crowded with people, ancient and new,
Had sight and love fixed all on one goal.
O threefold light, which in one star
Shining, satisfies them at the sight of it,
Look down upon our storm below.
If barbarians, coming from a region
That every day is covered by Helice,
Turning with her son, whom she gazes upon,
Saw Rome and its towering works,
They were astonished when the Lateran
Rose above mortal things;
I, Dante, who had come from the human to the divine,
From time to eternity,
And from Florence to a people just and whole,
How great an astonishment must have filled me!
Indeed, between it and my joy, I felt
A wish to hear nothing and remain silent.
And like a pilgrim who finds refreshment
In the temple of his vow, looking around,
And already hopes to tell how it is,
So, walking through the living light,
I led my eyes over the tiers,
Now up, now down, now circling around.
I saw faces whose looks inspired loving-kindness,
Adorned by another's light and by their own smiles,
And gestures adorned with every virtue.
My whole gaze had already taken in
The general form of Paradise,
Though fixed firmly on no one part;
And I turned with renewed desire
To ask my lady about things
That had left my mind in suspense.
I was intending one thing, and another answered me:
I thought I would see Beatrice, but saw an old man
Clothed among the glorious people.
Kind joy was spread across his eyes
And cheeks, in a loving manner,
As befits an eternal Father.
And where is she? I said at once.
Then he: To fulfill your desire,
Beatrice has sent me from my place;
And if you look upward to the third circle
From the highest tier, you will see her again
On the throne her merits won for her.
Without answering, I raised my eyes
And saw her, making a crown for herself
By reflecting the eternal rays from her.
From that region where thunder sounds highest,
No mortal eye is as far away,
However deep it sinks beneath the sea,
As my sight was from Beatrice;
Yet this did not trouble me, for her image
Did not come to me through an obstructing medium.
O lady, in whom my hope is alive,
Who for my salvation endured
Leaving your footprints in Hell;
For all the things I have seen,
I acknowledge the grace and power
As coming from your might and goodness.
You drew me from slavery into freedom,
By every path and every means
That lay within your power to use.
Preserve your greatness in me,
So that my soul, which you have made whole,
May leave my body pleasing to you.
So I prayed; and she, as far away
As she seemed, smiled and looked at me;
Then turned again to the eternal fountain.
And the holy old man: So that you may bring
Your journey to its perfect end, he said,
The end for which prayer and holy love sent me,
Fly with your eyes through this garden;
Seeing it will sharpen your sight
To rise farther through the divine ray.
The Queen of Heaven, for whom I burn
With all my love, will grant us every grace,
For I am her faithful Bernard.
Like one who perhaps comes from Croatia
To see our Veronica,
And is not sated by its ancient fame,
But says in his thoughts, while it is shown: My Lord
Jesus Christ, true God, was your likeness
Really like this?
So I was, gazing in wonder at the living
Love of the one who in this world,
Through contemplation, tasted that peace.
Son of grace, this joyful being,
He began, will not be known to you
If you keep your eyes fixed only down here;
But look around the circles, to the farthest,
Until you see the Queen seated,
To whom this realm is subject and devoted.
I raised my eyes; and as in the morning
The eastern part of the horizon
Outshines the place where the sun declines,
So, as if going from valley to mountain
With my eyes, I saw a part at the far edge
Outshine the whole other face with light.
And as, where the chariot is awaited
That Phaëton drove badly, the light burns brightest,
While on either side it grows dimmer,
So that peaceful golden flame
Grew brighter in the middle, and on every side
The flame faded evenly.
And in that middle, with their wings spread,
I saw more than a thousand angels rejoicing,
Each distinct in brightness and in art.
I saw amid their games and songs
A beauty smiling, who was joy
In the eyes of all the other saints.
If I had as much richness in speech
As I have in imagination, I would not dare
To attempt the least part of her delight.
When Bernard saw my eyes
Fixed and intent in his warm affection,
He turned his own toward her with such love
That mine grew more intent on looking.
