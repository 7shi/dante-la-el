The sun that first warmed my heart with love
had shown me a beautiful truth,
proving and disproving, with a gracious look;
and I, to confess myself corrected and certain,
as far as was fitting,
raised my head to speak more boldly.
But a vision appeared and held
me so tightly to itself, that I might see it,
that I could no longer remember my confession.
As through clear or polished glass,
or through bright, calm water,
not so deep that the bottom is lost,
the outlines of our faces return,
so faintly that against a white brow
they do not strike our eyes strongly,
so I saw many faces ready to speak;
and so I fell into the opposite error
of him who kindled love between a man and a spring.
As soon as I noticed them,
thinking they were mirrored images,
I turned my eyes to see whose they were;
but seeing nothing there, I turned them forward,
back toward the light of my sweet guide,
who smiled, her holy eyes aflame.
Do not wonder, even if I smile,
she said to me; lay bare your childish thought,
which still does not trust its footing in truth;
but turn yourself, as you usually do, toward your wish.
What you see are real substances,
confined here because their vows were incomplete.
Therefore speak with them, listen, and believe
that the true light which gives them peace
does not let them turn their feet away from it.
And I, toward the shade that seemed to me most eager
to speak, turned and began,
like someone overcome by too great a desire:
O well-created spirit, who by the rays
of eternal life feel its sweetness,
which no one can understand without tasting it,
it will please me if you grant my request
and tell me your name and your condition.
Then she, ready and with shining eyes:
Our charity closes no doors
to a just request, like the one
who wills her whole court to be like herself.
I was a virgin sister in the world;
and if your memory looks carefully,
my greater beauty will not hide me from you;
but you will recognize that I am Piccarda,
placed here with these other blessed souls,
blessed in the slowest sphere.
Our affections, kindled
only by the joy of the Holy Spirit,
rejoice, shaped by its order.
And this lot, which seems so low,
was given to us because neglected were
our vows, and left incomplete in part.
Then I said to her: In those wondrous faces
of yours something divine shines forth,
transforming you from your former selves.
That is why I was not quick to recognize you,
but what you tell me now helps me,
so that I can picture you more easily.
But tell me: you who are happy here,
do you desire a higher place,
to see more or to be more loved?
She smiled a little first with the other shades;
then she answered me so joyfully
that she seemed to burn with the first fire of love:
Brother, what quiets our will is
the virtue of charity, which makes us desire
only what we have, and thirst for nothing else.
If we desired to be higher,
our desires would disagree
with the will of Him who assigns our places here;
for you will see that this cannot happen in these circles,
if it is necessary to live in charity here
and if you consider its nature well.
Rather, the essential condition of this blessed life
is to remain within the divine will,
so that our wills become one with it.
And as we are placed from sphere to sphere,
throughout this realm, the whole realm pleases us,
as it pleases the King, who makes us will as he wills.
And his will is our peace;
it is the sea toward which everything moves,
both what he creates and what nature makes.
Then it became clear to me that every place
in Heaven is Paradise, though the grace
of the highest Good does not rain there in one measure.
But just as it happens that one food satisfies,
while one still has an appetite for another,
and one asks for the latter and gives thanks for the former,
so I did, in deed and word,
to learn from her what the cloth was
from which she had not drawn the shuttle to the end.
A perfect life and great merit place in Heaven
a lady higher up, she said to me, by whose rule
your world below dresses and veils itself.
So that one may watch and sleep until death
with that spouse who accepts every vow,
and makes charity conform to his pleasure.
From the world, to follow her, as a young girl
I fled, and enclosed myself in her habit,
promising to follow her order's way.
Afterward men, more accustomed to evil than good,
dragged me out of the sweet cloister.
And God knows what my life became afterward.
And that other brightness you see,
to my right, and which is kindled
by all the light of our sphere,
understands of herself what I say of me:
she was a sister, and so what was taken from her,
from her head, was the shadow of the sacred veil.
Though she was only returned to the world,
against her will and against good custom,
she was never freed from the veil of her heart.
This is the great light of Constance,
who from the second wind of Swabia
gave birth to the third and last power.
Thus she spoke to me, then began: Hail,
Mary, singing; and as she sang, she vanished,
like a heavy object sinking through deep water.
My sight, which followed her as far
as it could, after losing her
turned toward the object of its greatest desire,
and turned wholly to Beatrice;
but she flashed brightly in my gaze,
so much so that at first my eyes could not endure it.
And this made me slower to ask her questions.
