Quam cito ultimum verbum
Benedicta flamma per dicere tolsit,
Rotare cepit sancta mola;
Et in suo gyro totam non se volvit
Priusquam alia de gyro illam clausit,
Et motum a motu, et cantum a cantu, colsit;
Cantum, qui tantum vincit nostras musas,
Nostras syrenes, in illis dulcibus tubis,
Quantum primus splendor illum qui refulsit.
Sicut volvuntur per teneram nubem
Duo arcus paralelli cum coloribus,
Quando Iuno sue ancille iubet,
Nascendo de illo quod est intus illud quod est exterius,
Ad instar loquele illius vage,
Quam amor consumpsit sicut sol vapores;
Et faciunt hic gentem esse presagam,
Propter fedus quod Deus cum Noe posuit,
De mundo qui nunquam plus allagatur;
Sic ille sempiterne rose
Volvebantur circa nos duo serta,
Et sic extrema ultime respondit.
Postquam tripudium et aliud festum grande,
Tum cantus et tum flammigiare
Lux cum luce gaudiosa et blanda,
Simul in puncto et in velle quieverunt,
Ac sicut oculi quibus ad complacentiam que ipsos movet
Convenit simul claudere et se levare,
From the heart of one of the new lights
a voice moved, making the needle
seem to turn toward its star;
and it began: The love that makes me beautiful
draws me to speak of the other leader,
because of whom my own is so well praised here.
It is fitting that where one is named, the other should shine,
so that, as they fought for one cause,
their glory may shine together.
Christ's army, which cost so dearly
to reclaim, moved slowly behind its banner,
wary and in sparse ranks;
when the Emperor who reigns forever
provided for the army in uncertainty,
by grace alone, not because it deserved it.
And, as I have said, he aided his bride
with two champions; by their deeds and words
the straying people found their way again.
In that region where gentle Zephyr rises
to open the new leaves
with which Europe is seen to clothe herself,
not far from the crashing of the waves,
beyond which, in their long sweep,
the sun sometimes hides from everyone,
lies fortunate Calahorra,
under the protection of the great shield
where the lion is subject and rules.
Within it was born the ardent lover
of the Christian faith, the great athlete,
kind to his own, harsh to his foes;
and as soon as his soul was created,
it was filled with divine power,
which made his mother a prophet.
After the betrothal was sealed
at the holy font between him and the faith,
where they endowed each other with salvation,
The woman who answered for him
saw in a dream the wondrous fruit
that would come from him and his heirs;
and, so that his name might fit
the nature he possessed, a spirit moved
to name him by the possessive of what he wholly was.
He was called Dominic; and I speak of him
as of the farmer whom Christ
chose to help tend his garden.
He truly seemed Christ's messenger and friend,
for his first love, made clear in him,
was for Christ's first counsel.
Often he was found silent and awake
on the ground by his nurse,
as if to say: I have come for this.
O truly blessed was his father Felix!
O truly gracious was his mother Giovanna,
if her name means what it is said to mean!
Not for the world, which people now pursue
by following the Bishop of Ostia and Thaddeus,
but for love of the true manna,
in a short time he became a great teacher,
so that he set out to circle the vineyard,
which soon grows pale if its vinedresser is wicked.
He went to the See, once kinder
to the poor and just—not because of the See,
but because of the one who sits there and strays—
not to exchange two or three for six,
not to seek the fortune of the first vacancy,
not to ask for tithes belonging to God's poor;
he asked instead for leave to fight
against the wandering world, for the seed
that clothes twenty-four plants.
Then, with learning and will together,
he set out with apostolic authority,
like a torrent forced down from a height;
and his force struck the heretics' brambles
most vigorously where
resistance was strongest.
Many streams then flowed from him,
watering the Catholic garden,
so its young trees grew more vigorous.
If one wheel of the chariot was such,
on which the holy Church defended herself
and won her civil war in the field,
You should already know clearly
the excellence of the other wheel, which Thomas
praised so graciously before I came.
But the track its outer rim made
has been abandoned,
so that there is mold where once was good lees.
His family, which once set out straight
following his footsteps, has turned so far
that the one ahead casts at the one behind;
but soon the harvest of bad cultivation
will be seen, when the tares complain
that their barn has been taken from them.
I say truly: leaf by leaf,
if one searched our book, one would still find a page
that reads: I am still what I used to be.
But it will not come from Casale or Acquasparta,
where such men come to Scripture,
one fleeing it, the other constraining it.
I am the soul of Bonaventure
of Bagnoregio, who in high offices
always put lesser concerns last.
Illuminato and Augustine are here,
among the first barefoot poor ones,
who made themselves God's friends with a cord.
Hugh of St Victor is here with them,
and Pietro Mangiadore and Pietro Ispano,
who shines below in twelve little books;
the prophet Nathan, and the metropolitan
Chrysostom, Anselm, and that Donatus
who deigned to lay a hand on the first art;
Rabanus is here, and beside me shines
the Calabrian Abbot Joachim,
endowed with prophetic spirit.
Saint Thomas's burning courtesy
and his discerning Latin moved me
to praise so great a champion,
and this company moved with me.
