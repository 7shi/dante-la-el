The glory of Him who moves all things
penetrates the universe and shines
more in one part and less elsewhere.
In the Heaven that receives His light most fully
I was, and saw things which to recount
he knows not, nor can, who descends from there;
for as it draws near its desire,
our intellect sinks so deeply
that memory cannot follow afterward.
Yet whatever from the holy realm
I could gather as treasure in my mind
will now become the subject of my song.
O good Apollo, at this final labor
make me such a vessel of your power,
as you ask in order to grant the beloved laurel.
Until now one peak of Parnassus
was enough for me, but now with both
I must enter the contest that remains.
Enter my breast and do you breathe,
thus, as when you drew Marsyas
from the sheath of his limbs.
O divine power, if you so lend yourself to me
so fully that the shadow of the blessed realm,
imprinted in my mind, I can reveal,
you will see me to the foot of your beloved tree
come and crown myself with its leaves,
of which the subject and you will make me worthy.
So rarely, father, are they gathered
for either Caesar or poet to triumph,
it is the fault and shame of human desires,
that to bring joy to the joyful
Delphic godhead the frond should,
Peneian, when it makes someone thirst for itself.
A small spark follows a great flame:
perhaps after me, with better voices,
people will pray for Cyrrha to answer.
There rises for mortals through different openings
the lamp of the world; but from that one
which joins four circles with three crosses,
with a better course and with a better star
it joined itself to the world's wax;
therefore in its own manner it shapes and stamps it.
Morning on the other side and evening on this side had made
almost such an opening; and all white there was
that hemisphere, while the other part was dark,
when Beatrice, on her left side,
I saw turn and gaze into the sun:
never did an eagle fix its eyes on it so steadily.
And as a second ray is wont
to leave the first and rise upward again,
just like a pilgrim who wishes to return,
so from her eyes, through the act infused
into my image, mine was formed,
and I fixed my gaze on the sun beyond our custom.
Much is permitted there that here is not permitted
to our powers, thanks to the place
made expressly for human nature.
I did not endure it long, nor so briefly
that I failed to see it sparkle all around,
as red-hot iron comes out of the fire;
and at once it seemed that one day to another
had been added, as if the One who can
had adorned the sky with another sun.
Beatrice, wholly on the eternal spheres
fixed with her eyes, stood; and I, on her
fixed my eyes, withdrawing them from what lay farther above.
Within her appearance I became inwardly such
as Glaucus made himself when he tasted the herb
that made him a companion of the other sea gods.
To express in words a transformation beyond humanity
would not be possible; so let the example suffice
for those whose experience grants them grace.
Whether I was alone in myself, whom You created
anew, the love by which You govern Heaven,
this You know, for You raised me with Your light.
When the wheel You make eternal
turned my eager attention toward itself,
with the harmony You tune and distinguish,
Then it seemed to me that so much of the sky was kindled
by the sun's flame that rain and river
never made a lake so broad.
The newness of the sound and the great light
from their causes kindled in me a desire
the like of which in sharpness I had never felt before.
Therefore she, who saw me as I saw myself,
to calm my troubled mind,
before I could ask, opened her mouth.
And she began: You yourself make yourself dull
with false imagining, so that you do not see
what you would see if you shook it off.
You are not on earth, as you believe;
but lightning, fleeing its proper place,
never moved as swiftly as you now return.
If I was released from my first doubt
by her brief words, spoken with a smile,
I was caught all the more in a new one.
And I said: Now, content, I have rested
from my great wonder; but now I wonder
how I can rise through these light bodies.
Then she, after a gentle sigh,
turned her eyes toward me with the look
a mother gives her delirious child,
and began: All things
have an order among themselves; and this is the form
that makes the universe resemble God.
The highest creatures see in this order the trace
of eternal power, which is the end
for which the rule just mentioned was made.
In the order I describe are inclined
all natures, each according to its kind,
some nearer and some farther from their source.
Therefore they move toward different ports
across the great sea of being, and each
with an instinct given it, to carry it.
One instinct carries fire toward the moon;
another stirs the hearts of mortals;
another draws the earth together and binds it.
Not only at creatures that are without
intelligence does this bow shoot,
but also at those who possess intellect and love.
Providence, which orders so well,
by its light keeps the Heaven forever still,
where that which turns most swiftly moves.
And now there, as to an appointed place,
the power of that bow carries us,
which directs whatever it sends toward a joyful end.
True, just as a form fails to conform
many times to the artist's intention,
because the material is deaf to the answer,
so from this course there strays
sometimes a creature that has the power
to bend elsewhere, though thus driven,
and, as one may see falling
fire from a cloud, so from its first impulse
it is turned toward earth by false pleasure.
You should no more wonder, if I judge rightly,
at your ascent than at a stream
that flows down from a high mountain to the bottom.
It would be a wonder in you if, free
of hindrance, you had settled below,
as it would be if living fire rested quietly on earth.
Then she turned her gaze back toward Heaven.
