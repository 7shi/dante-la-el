The glory of Him who moves all things
penetrates the universe and shines
more in one part and less elsewhere.
In the Heaven that receives most fully
His light, I was, and saw things that no one
who descends from there knows or can recount;
for as our intellect draws near its desire,
it sinks so deeply into it
that memory cannot follow afterward.
Yet whatever treasure I could gather
in my mind from the holy realm
will now become the subject of my song.
O good Apollo, at this final labor
make me a vessel of your power,
as the beloved laurel asks of one who grants it.
Until now one peak of Parnassus
was enough for me, but now I must enter
the contest that remains with both.
Enter my breast and breathe inspiration,
as when you drew Marsyas
from the sheath of his limbs.
O divine power, if you lend yourself to me
so fully that I can reveal the shadow
of the blessed realm imprinted in my mind,
you will see me come to the foot of your beloved tree
and crown myself with its leaves,
which the subject and you will make me worthy to receive.
So rarely, father, are the leaves gathered
that Caesar or poet may triumph with them—
the fault and shame of human desires—
that the laurel should bring joy to the joyful
Delphic godhead, when it makes someone
thirst for itself, the Peneian crown.
A small spark follows a great flame:
perhaps after me, with better voices,
people will pray for Cyrrha to answer.
The lamp of the world rises for mortals
through different openings; but from that one
which joins four circles with three crosses,
it comes forth joined to a better course
and a better star, and shapes and stamps
the world's wax more in its own manner.
On one side it had made morning, and on this side evening,
at nearly the same opening; and that hemisphere
was all white, while the other part was dark,
when I saw Beatrice turn on her left side
and gaze into the sun: never did an eagle
fix its eyes on it so steadily.
And as a second ray is wont
to leave the first and rise upward,
like a pilgrim who wishes to return,
so, through the act that entered my image
from her eyes, mine was formed,
and I fixed my gaze on the sun beyond our custom.
Much is permitted there that our powers
do not permit here, thanks to the place
made as the proper home of human nature.
I did not endure it long, nor so briefly
that I failed to see it sparkle all around,
as red-hot iron comes out of the fire;
and at once one day seemed added to another,
as if the One who can had adorned the sky
with another sun.
Beatrice stood wholly fixed on the eternal spheres,
and I fixed my eyes on her, withdrawing them
from what lay farther above.
Within her appearance I was changed
as Glaucus was when he tasted the herb
that made him a companion of the other sea gods.
To express in words a transformation beyond humanity
would not be possible; so let the example suffice
for those whose experience grants them grace.
Whether I was alone in myself, whom You created anew,
Love that governs Heaven, You know,
for You raised me with Your light.
When the wheel You make eternal, desired by me,
turned my attention toward itself,
with the harmony You tune and distinguish,
Then it seemed to me that so much of the sky
was kindled by the sun's flame that no rain
or river ever made a lake so broad.
The newness of the sound and the great light
from their causes kindled in me a desire
whose sharpness I had never felt before.
She, who saw me as I saw myself,
opened her mouth to calm my troubled mind
before I could ask.
She began: You make yourself dull
with false imagining, so that you do not see
what you would see if you shook it off.
You are not on earth, as you believe;
but lightning, fleeing its proper place,
does not move as swiftly as you are now.
If I was released from my first doubt
by her brief words, spoken with a smile,
I was caught all the more in a new one.
I said: Now, content, I have rested
from my great wonder; but now I wonder
how I can rise through these light bodies.
Then she, after a gentle sigh,
turned her eyes toward me with the look
a mother gives her delirious child,
and began: All things, whatever they are,
have an order among themselves; and this is the form
that makes the universe resemble God.
The highest creatures see in this order
the trace of eternal power, which is the end
for which this rule was made.
In the order I describe, all natures
are inclined, each according to its kind,
some nearer and some farther from their source.
Therefore they move toward different ports
across the great sea of being, each
with an instinct given to carry it.
One instinct carries fire toward the moon;
another stirs the hearts of mortals;
another draws the earth together and binds it.
Nor does this bow shoot only at creatures
without intelligence, but also at those
who possess intellect and love.
Providence, which orders all things so well,
by its light keeps the Heaven forever still,
where that which turns most swiftly moves.
And now, like an arrow at its appointed mark,
the power of that bow carries us there,
which directs whatever it sends toward a joyful end.
True, just as a form often fails
to conform to the artist's intention,
because the material is deaf to the answer,
so a creature sometimes strays from this course,
though it has the power to bend elsewhere,
being thus driven in another direction,
as fire may be seen falling from a cloud,
if its first impulse is turned toward earth
by false pleasure.
You should no more wonder, if I judge rightly,
at your ascent than at a stream
that flows down from a high mountain.
It would be a wonder in you if, free
of every hindrance, you had settled below,
as it would be if living fire
rested quietly on earth.
