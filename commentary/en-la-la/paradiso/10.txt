Gazing upon his Son with Love,
Whom one and the other eternally breathe forth,
The first and ineffable Power,
Whatever revolves through mind or space
He made in such order that it cannot exist
Without tasting of him who contemplates it.
Lift up your sight with me, reader, to the high wheels,
Straight toward that region
Where one motion strikes against the other;
And there begin to contemplate the craft
Of that Maker who loves it within himself
So much that he never takes his eye from it.
See how from there branches forth
The oblique circle that carries the planets,
To satisfy the world that calls for them;
And if their path were not winding,
Much of their power would be wasted,
And nearly every power below would be dead.
And if its departure from the straight line
Were nearer or farther, the order of the world
Would be greatly diminished, above and below.
Now remain, reader, at your desk,
Thinking over what has been offered,
If you would be glad long before you tire.
I have set it before you; now feed yourself from it,
For all my care is drawn toward that subject
Of which I have become the scribe.
Nature's greatest minister,
Who impresses the world with heaven's power,
And measures time for us with his light,
Joined with that region recalled above,
Revolved through the spirals
In which each hour returns most swiftly:
I was with him, but I did not notice the ascent,
Any more than one notices, before the first thought,
That it has arrived.
O Beatrice, she who is seen
To pass so suddenly from good to better
That her act unfolds beyond time,
How radiant she must have been of herself
When she was within the Sun, where I entered,
Appearing not by color but by light!
Though I call upon my skill, my art, and my practice,
I could not describe her so that anyone might imagine it;
Yet it may be believed, and longed for in sight.
If our imagination falls short
Of such a height, it is no wonder,
For no eye has gone above the Sun.
Such was there the fourth family
Of the exalted Father, who always nourishes it,
Showing how he breathes forth and how he begets.
And Beatrice began: Give thanks,
Give thanks to the Sun of the angels, who by his grace
Has lifted you to this visible sun.
No mortal heart was ever so prepared
For devotion and surrender to God,
With all its grateful joy, so quickly,
As I became at those words;
And all my love entered into him so completely
That Beatrice vanished from my memory.
She was not displeased, but smiled at it,
And the radiance of her smiling eyes
Divided my unified mind among many things.
I saw many living, victorious splendors
Make a center of us and a crown of themselves,
Sweeter in voice than bright in appearance.
So we sometimes see the daughter of Latona
Girdled, when the air is so full
That it holds the thread which makes her halo.
In the court of heaven, whence I return,
There are many precious, beautiful jewels,
So many that they cannot be drawn from that realm;
And the song of those lights was one of them.
Those who do not grow wings to fly up there
May wait for news from the mute.
Then, singing thus, those blazing suns
Circled us three times,
Like stars near the unmoving poles;
They seemed to me like women who do not leave the dance,
But pause in silence, listening
Until they have gathered the new notes.
And within one I heard it begin: When
The ray of divine grace, from which true love is kindled
And then grows by loving,
Has multiplied and shines so brightly in you
That it leads you up that stair
From which no one descends without climbing back;
Whoever denied you the wine of his flask
For your thirst would lack generosity,
Like water that does not flow down to the sea.
You wish to know which plants make this garland bloom
Around which the beautiful lady
Who gives you strength in heaven gazes.
I belonged to the lambs of the holy flock
That Dominic leads along the way
Where they grow well nourished, if they do not stray.
This one, nearest me on my right,
Was my brother and teacher: he was Albert
Of Cologne, and I was Thomas Aquinas.
If you wish to be certain of all the others,
Follow my speech with your gaze,
Turning upward around the blessed garland.
That other flame comes from the smile
Of Gratian, who helped both courts
So that he pleased in Paradise.
The other, who adorns our choir nearby,
Was Peter, who, with the poor woman,
Offered his treasure to Holy Church.
The fifth light, fairest among us,
Breathes such love that the whole world below
Longs to hear news of him.
In his lofty mind such profound knowledge
Was placed that, if truth be true,
No second arose to see so deeply.
Nearby, see the light of that candle
Which, while below in the flesh, saw most deeply
The angelic nature and its ministry.
In another little light smiles
The defender of Christian temples,
Whose Latin Augustine drew upon.
Now, if you draw your mind's eye
From light to light, following my praises,
You will already thirst for the eighth.
There, in seeing every good, the holy soul rejoices
And reveals the deceptive world
To whoever hears well of it.
The body from which it was driven lies
Below in Cieldauro; through martyrdom
And exile it came to this peace.
Beyond, see the ardent spirit of Isidore,
Bede, and Richard,
Who in contemplation were more than men.
This one, toward whom your gaze returns to me,
Is the light of a spirit whose grave thoughts
Made dying seem to come too slowly.
It is the eternal light of Siger,
Who, reading in the Street of Straw,
Syllogized envious truths.
Then, like a clock that calls us
At the hour when God's bride rises
To sing matins to her bridegroom, that he may love her,
With one part pulling and urging the other,
Ting, ting, ringing with so sweet a note
That a well-disposed spirit swells with love,
So I saw the glorious wheel
Move and answer voice to voice in harmony
And sweetness that cannot be known
Except where joy is made eternal.
