We were at the summit of the stairs,
Where the mountain is girded for the second time,
The mountain that, as one climbs, frees others from evil:
There a ledge girds the hill in this way,
All around its base, like the first,
Except that its curve bends more sharply.
There is no shade, nor any sign to be seen;
The bank appears, and the narrow path appears,
In the livid color of the stony ground.
If we wait here for people to ask,
The Poet was reasoning, I fear our choice
Will be delayed for far too long.
Then he fixed his eyes upon the sun;
He made his right side the pivot for turning,
And turned his left side around.
O sweet light, in whose trust I enter
Upon the new path, you guide us,
He said, as one ought to be guided in here.
You warm the world, you shine above it;
Unless some other reason works against it,
Your rays should always be our guides.
As far as a mile is reckoned from here,
So far we had already gone on that side,
In little time, because our desire was eager.
Spirits were heard flying toward us,
Though unseen, speaking
Invitations, courteous, to love's table.
The first voice that flew past
Said loudly, They have no wine,
And went on behind us, repeating it.
Before it had altogether faded
As it receded, another passed shouting, I am Orestes,
And it too did not stop.
O, I said, Father, what voices are these?
And as I asked, behold a third,
Saying, Love those from whom you have suffered harm.
And the good Teacher said: This circle lashes
The sin of envy, and therefore its lash's cords
Are drawn from love.
The bridle must be of the opposite sound;
I believe you will hear it, as I judge,
Before you reach the step of forgiveness.
But fix your gaze through the air,
And you will see people sitting before us,
Each seated along the rock wall.
Then I opened my eyes more than before;
I looked ahead and saw shades in cloaks
No different in color from the stone.
And when we had gone a little farther,
I heard them cry, Mary, pray for us,
And cry, Michael, Peter, and all the Saints.
I do not believe that anyone on earth today
Is so hard-hearted as not to be moved
With pity at what I saw next:
For when I came so close to them
That their features became clear to me,
My eyes were wrung with deep sorrow.
They seemed covered in coarse haircloth,
One supporting the other with a shoulder,
And all supported by the cliff.
So the blind, whose livelihood has failed,
Stand at jubilees begging what they need,
One lowering his head onto another's,
So that pity may quickly arise in people,
Not only from the sound of words,
But from a sight that longs no less.
And as the sun does not draw near the blind,
So to the shades, where I speak now,
Heaven's light will not give itself;
For an iron thread pierces everyone's eyelid,
And sews it, as is done to a wild hawk,
Because it will not stay still.
As I went, it seemed to me an injury
To see others without being seen;
So I turned to my wise counselor.
He knew well what the mute man meant;
So he did not wait for my question,
But said: Speak, and be brief and pointed.
Virgil came beside me on that side
Of the ledge where one could fall,
For it is walled on neither side.
On the other side beside me were the devout
Shades, who because of the horrible stitching
Pressed so hard that their cheeks were wet.
I turned toward them and began: O secure people,
Who will see the highest light,
The sole object of your desire,
If grace soon dissolves the scum
Of your conscience, so that clearly
The river of the mind may flow through it,
Tell me, for it will be a kindness and a pleasure,
If there is a soul among you who is Italian;
Perhaps it will be good for her if I learn of it.
O my brother, each of us is a citizen
Of a true city; but you mean
One who lived as a stranger in Italy.
This seemed to me to be the answer I heard
A little farther from where I stood;
So I moved farther toward the voice.
Among the others I saw a shade waiting
In appearance; and if someone asked, How?
She raised her chin like a blind person.
Spirit, I said, who humble yourself in order to climb,
If you are the one who answered me,
Make yourself known to me by place or name.
I was from Siena, she replied, and with these
Others I cleanse here my guilty life,
Weeping to him who grants himself to us.
I was not wise, although I was called Sapia,
And I was much happier at others' misfortunes
Than at my own good fortune.
And so you will not think I deceive you,
Hear whether I was, as I tell you, a fool.
The arc of my years was already descending,
My fellow citizens were near Colle,
Gathered on the field with their adversaries,
And I prayed to God for what he willed.
There they were broken and turned toward
The bitter steps of flight; seeing the rout,
I seized a joy beyond all others;
So much so that I raised my bold face upward,
Shouting to God: Now I fear you no more,
As a blackbird does in a brief spell of fair weather.
At the end of my life I sought peace with God;
And still my debt would not have been lessened
By my penance,
If this had not happened: Peter Pettinaio remembered me
In his holy prayers, and out of charity
He felt compassion for me.
But who are you, asking about our condition
As you go, and carrying your eyes unstitched,
As I believe, and speaking while you breathe?
My eyes, I said, will also be taken from me here,
But for a short time, since small is the offense
I committed by turning them toward envy.
Much greater is the fear that weighs down
My soul because of the torment below,
For the burden down there already weighs on me.
And she said to me: Who then brought you
Up here among us, if you expect to return below?
And I: This one with me, who says not a word.
I am alive; therefore ask me,
Elect spirit, if you wish me to carry
My mortal feet from here back to the world.
Oh, this is such a new thing to hear,
She replied, that it is a great sign God loves you;
So sometimes help me with your prayer.
I ask you, by what you desire most,
If ever you tread the soil of Tuscany,
To restore my good name among my kin.
You will see them among that vain people
Who hope in Talamone, and will lose there
More hope than when seeking Diana;
But the admirals will lose even more.
