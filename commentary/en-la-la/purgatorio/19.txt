At the hour when the warmth of day cannot
warm the coldness of the moon any further,
overcome by Earth and at times by Saturn;
when geomancers their greatest fortune
see in the east, before dawn,
rise along a path that remains dark for only a little while;
a stammering woman came to me in a dream,
cross-eyed, and twisted above her feet,
with stunted hands and a pallid complexion.
I gazed at her; and as the sun revives
the cold limbs weighed down by night,
so my gaze made nimble
her tongue, and then straightened her all
in a little while, and her face, which had been made fearful,
as love desires, so it colored for her.
After she had loosened her speech,
she began to sing so that only with difficulty
could I turn my attention away from her.
I am, she sang, I am the sweet siren
who bewilders sailors in mid-sea;
I am so full of pleasure to hear.
I turned Ulysses from his wandering course
to my song; and whoever grows used to me
rarely leaves, so fully do I content him.
Her mouth was not yet closed
when a holy, swift lady appeared
nearby, to shame her.
O Virgil, Virgil, who is this?
I cried aloud, while he came
with his eyes fixed only on that honorable woman.
She seized the other, and tore open her front,
ripping her garments, and showing me her belly;
that woke me with the stench that came from it.
I turned my eyes to good Virgil;
and meanwhile a voice called, as if it said: Rise and come;
let us find the gate through which you may enter.
I rose, and all were already full
of the high daylight, the circles of the sacred mountain,
and we walked with the new sun at our backs.
Following him, I carried my brow
like one who has it weighed down by thought,
who makes of himself a half-arch of a bridge;
when I heard: Come, this is the passage;
spoken in a gentle, gracious way
such as is not heard in this mortal realm.
With wings spread, which seemed those of a swan,
he who had spoken thus to us turned us upward
between two walls of hard rock.
Then he moved his feathers and fanned,
Those who mourn, affirming, are blessed,
who shall have souls that are mistresses of consolation.
What is wrong, that you keep gazing only at the ground?
my guide began to say to me,
after the angel had lifted us both a little.
And I replied: With such apprehension am I made to go
by a new vision that draws me to itself,
that I cannot turn my thoughts away.
Have you seen, he said, that ancient witch
who now bewails all alone above us?
Have you seen how a man frees himself from her?
Let that suffice for you, and stamp your heels upon the ground;
turn your eyes to the spheres that are turned
by the eternal King with the great wheels.
Like a falcon that first looks down at its feet,
then turns at the call and stretches forward,
drawn there by its desire for food,
so I became; and so, when split was
the rock to make a path for those going upward,
I went on to the place where the circle begins.
When I was admitted into the fifth ring,
I saw people throughout it weeping,
lying on the ground, all face down.
My soul clings to the pavement,
I heard them say with such deep sighs
that their words could scarcely be understood.
O chosen of God, whose sufferings
are made less harsh by both justice and hope,
show us the way toward the higher ascent.
If you come free from lying down,
and wish to find the way more quickly,
keep always to your right, on the outside.
So the poet asked, and this answer
came to us from a little ahead of us; from this,
in his speech I noticed another hidden there.
And I turned my eyes to my master's eyes,
and he signaled his assent to me with a cheerful nod
to what my eager gaze was asking.
Once I could do as I wished,
I drew near to that creature
whose words had first caught my attention,
saying: Spirit, in whom weeping brings to completion
that without which one cannot return to God,
pause a little for me, your chief concern.
Who you were, and why you have your backs turned
upward, tell me; and if you wish me to obtain for you
something there whence I set out to come.
And he answered me: Why our backs
heaven turns toward itself, you shall learn; but first,
know that I was Peter's successor.
Between Sestri and Chiavari descends
a lovely stream; and from its name
my family's title had its eminence.
For a month or a little more I learned how
heavy the great mantle is for one who guards it from the mud;
it makes all other burdens seem feathers to me.
Alas for me, my conversion came late;
but as soon as I became Rome's shepherd,
so I saw through the lying life.
I saw that there the heart found no rest,
nor could one rise higher in that life;
so love for this other life awoke in me.
Until that moment, wretched and separated
from God, I was a soul wholly avaricious;
now, as you see, I am punished here for it.
What avarice does is shown here
in the purification of converted souls;
and no punishment on the mountain is more bitter.
Just as our eyes did not lift
upward, fixed on earthly things,
so justice here has sunk us to the ground.
As avarice extinguished, for every good,
our love, so that we ceased to act,
so justice keeps us bound here
hand and foot, tied and captive;
and as long as the supreme Lord wills,
so long shall we lie motionless and stretched out.
I had already knelt down and wanted to speak;
but as I began and he noticed in me,
just by listening, my reverence.
What cause, he said, bent you so low?
And I replied to him: Because of your dignity,
my conscience rightly rebuked me.
Raise your legs, stand up, brother, he answered.
Do not err, for I am a fellow servant
with you and the others, under one power.
If ever that holy Gospel saying,
which says, They shall neither marry, you understood,
you can well understand why I speak so.
Go now; I do not want you to linger longer,
for your staying disrupts my weeping,
by which I bring to completion what you said.
I have a niece in the world named Alagia,
good in herself, provided our house
does not make her wicked by example;
and she alone is left to me there.
