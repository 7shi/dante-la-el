After we were within the threshold of the gate,
Which makes the soul abandon evil love,
Because it makes the straight road seem crooked,
I heard it close with a clang:
And had I turned my eyes toward it,
What worthy excuse could there have been for my fault?
We were climbing over a split rock,
That shifted from one side to the other,
Like a wave that ebbs and draws near.
Here one must use a little skill,
My guide began, in keeping close,
Now here, now there, along the dividing side.
And this made our steps slow
So much so that the moon's waning part sooner
Returned to its bed to rest,
Than we got out of that cleft.
But when we were free and open
Above, where the mountain gathers itself behind,
I was tired, and both of us unsure
Of our way; we stopped on a level place,
More lonely than paths through deserts.
From its edge, where the empty space begins,
To the foot of the other bank, which still climbs,
A human body could measure three times;
And as far as my eye could stretch its wings
Now to the left and now to the right,
This terrace seemed to me so wide.
Our feet had not yet moved up there,
When I saw that the encircling bank,
Which had no straight ascent,
Was of bright marble, and adorned
With carvings such that not only Polycletus,
But nature herself would there be put to shame.
The angel who came to earth with the decree
Of the peace wept for through so many years,
Who opened heaven after its long prohibition,
Appeared before us so true to life,
There carved in a gentle action,
That it did not seem a silent image.
One could have sworn that he said, Hail;
For there was pictured she
Who turned the key to open the highest Love.
And this speech was impressed in her posture:
Behold the handmaid of God; just
As a figure is sealed into wax.
Do not keep your mind fixed in one place alone,
Said the gentle master, who had me
On the side where people have their hearts;
So I turned my face; and I saw,
Behind Mary, on that side
Where the one who guided me stood beside me,
Another story carved into the rock;
So I passed Virgil and drew near,
So that it would be clear to my eyes.
There was carved in the very marble
The cart and oxen drawing the holy Ark,
Because of which one fears an office not entrusted.
Before it appeared a crowd, all of them
Divided into seven choirs; to my two senses
It gave cause to say, one no, the other yes, it sings.
Likewise, at the smoke of the incense
Pictured there, my eyes and nose
Disagreed over yes and no.
There the blessed vessel went first,
The humble Psalmist leaping with his robe gathered up,
And in that moment he was more and less than a king.
Across from him stood, pictured in a window
Of a great palace, Michal; she watched,
Like a disdainful and sorrowful lady.
I moved from where I stood
To look more closely at another story
That gleamed white to me behind Michal.
There was pictured the high glory
Of Roman rule, whose worth
Moved Gregory to his great victory:
I mean the emperor Trajan;
And a little widow stood at his bridle,
Marked by tears and grief.
Around him the space seemed crowded and full
Of knights, and the eagles on the gold
Above them seemed to move in the wind.
The poor little woman among them all
Seemed to say: Lord, avenge me
For my son who is dead, and for whom I grieve.
And he answered her: Now wait
Until I return. And she: O my lord,
Like a person on whom grief presses,
What if you do not return? And he: Someone will take my place;
He will do it for you. And she: Another's good deed,
What will it do for you, if you forget it?
Then he: Now take heart, for it is necessary
That I fulfill my duty before I leave:
Justice wills it, and compassion holds me back.
He who never saw anything new
Created this visible speech,
New to us, for none is found here.
While I delighted in looking
At the images of so much humility,
And dear to behold for their maker,
Look over here, but they move slowly,
The Poet murmured, many people;
They will guide us to the higher steps.
My eyes, content to gaze
Through seeing novelties for which they are eager,
Were not slow to turn toward him.
Yet I do not want you, reader, to lose
Your good resolve on hearing
How God wills the debt to be paid.
Do not dwell on the form of the torment:
Think of what follows; think that, at worst,
One cannot go beyond the great judgment.
I began: Master, those I see
Moving toward us do not look like people to me,
And I cannot tell what they are, so deceived am I in seeing.
And he replied to me: The heavy condition
Of their torment bends them to the ground,
So that my eyes were once confused.
But look closely, and untangle
With your sight the one who comes beneath those stones:
You can already see how each one is bowed.
O proud Christians, O wretched and weary,
Whose mind's sight is weakened,
You put your trust in backward steps;
Do you not see that we are worms,
Born to form the angelic butterfly
That flies to justice without a shield?
Why does your spirit soar so high?
Then you are like incomplete insects,
Like a worm whose formation fails.
As, to support a ceiling or a roof,
A figure sometimes, as a bracket,
Is seen to draw its knees up to its chest,
Which from what is not real makes true anguish
Arise in those who see them; so fashioned
I saw these when I looked closely.
It is true that they were more or less contracted,
According to how much they bore on their backs.
And one who was more patient in his deeds,
Weeping, seemed to say: I can bear no more.
