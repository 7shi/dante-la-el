To run upon better water, he raises the sails
Now the little ship of my intellect,
Which leaves behind it a sea so cruel.
And I shall sing of that second realm,
Where the human spirit is purged,
And becomes worthy to ascend to heaven.
But here let dead poetry rise again,
O holy Muses, since I am yours,
And here let Calliope rise a little,
Following my song with that sound
Of which the wretched Pierides felt
So deadly a blow that they despaired of pardon.
The sweet color of eastern sapphire,
Gathering in the serene aspect
Of the pure air as far as the first circle,
Began again to delight my eyes,
As soon as I came out of the dead air
That had saddened my eyes and breast.
The fair planet that encourages love
Made all the East rejoice,
Veiling the Fishes that were in its train.
I turned to my right and fixed my attention
On the other pole, and saw four stars
Never seen except by the first people,
Heaven seemed to rejoice in their little flames.
O northern, widowed region,
Since you are deprived of seeing them!
As soon as I had left their gaze,
Turning a little toward the other pole,
Where the Wain had already disappeared,
I saw near me an old man, alone,
So worthy of reverence in appearance
That no son owes more to any father.
He wore a long beard mixed with white hairs,
Like his own hair,
Which fell in two locks upon his breast.
The rays of the four holy lights
Adorned his face with such brightness
That I saw him as if the sun were before me.
Who are you, who against the blind river
Have fled the eternal prison?
He said, moving those honorable locks.
Who guided you? What light served you
As you came out of the profound night
That always darkens the infernal valley?
Are the laws of the abyss so broken?
Or has a new decree been made in heaven,
That the damned come to my caves?
Then my guide took hold of me,
And by words, hands, and gestures
Made my legs and brows show reverence.
Then he answered him: I did not come on my own.
A lady descended from heaven, at whose prayers
I helped this man with my company.
Since it is your wish that more be explained
Of our condition, as it truly is,
I cannot deny you what I wish to tell.
This man has never seen his final evening,
But through his folly he came so near it
That little time remained for him to turn.
As I told you, I was sent to him
For his salvation, and there was no other way
Than this one on which I have embarked.
I have shown him all the guilty people;
Now I intend to show him the spirits
Who purge themselves under your authority.
How I brought him here would take too long to tell you;
A power descends from on high and helps me
Lead him to see and hear you.
Now may it please you to welcome his coming:
He seeks freedom, so precious,
As he knows who rejects life for its sake.
You know that death was not bitter to you for it
At Utica, where you left the garment
That will be so precious on the great day.
The eternal decrees are not broken by us:
For this man lives, and Minos does not bind me;
Rather, I am from the circle of chaste eyes,
Of your Marcia, who in appearance still prays to you,
O sacred heart, that you hold her as your own:
For love of her, then, bend toward us.
Let us go through your seven realms;
I shall carry her your thanks,
If you care to be remembered down there.
Marcia pleased my eyes so much,
While I was on that side, he said then,
That I did for her whatever favors she wished.
Now that she dwells beyond the evil river,
She can no longer move me by that law
Which was made when I went out from there.
But if a lady of heaven moves and guides you,
As you say, there is no need for flattery: enough
That you ask me for her sake.
Go, then, and gird this man
With a smooth rush, and wash his face,
So that every stain is removed from it;
For it would not be fitting that an eye clouded
By any mist should go before the first
Minister, who belongs to those of Paradise.
This little island, all around at its lowest edge,
Down there, where the waves beat against it,
Bears rushes upon the soft mud.
No other plant that puts forth leaves
Or grows hard can live there,
For it does not yield to the blows.
Afterward, do not return this way;
The sun, which is now rising, will show you
Where to take the mountain by an easier ascent.
Thus he vanished; and I rose up
Without speaking, and drew myself close
To my guide, fixing my eyes on him.
He began: Son, follow my steps;
Let us turn back, for on this side
The plain slopes down to its lowest edge.
Dawn was overcoming the morning hour
That had fled before, so that from afar
I recognized the trembling of the sea.
We walked across the lonely plain
Like one returning to a lost road,
Who thinks the journey to it has been in vain.
When we reached the place where the dew
Fights with the sun, because it is in a spot
Where, sheltered, it thins only a little,
My master gently laid both hands
Upon the grass; and I, who understood
His purpose,
Presented my tearful cheeks to him;
There he uncovered for me all
The color that Inferno had hidden.
Then we came to the deserted shore
Whose waters no man ever sailed
Who afterward had experience of returning.
There he girded me as another had wished.
O wonder! The humble plant he chose
Sprang up again at once, just there
Where he had torn it out.
