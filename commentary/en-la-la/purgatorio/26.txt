As we went along the edge, one before another,
We walked, and often the good master
Said: Take care; may my warning help you.
The sun struck my right shoulder,
And, shining, was already turning all the west
To a pale azure appearance;
And with my shadow I made the flame seem more burning;
Yet at such a sign
I saw many shades take notice as they went.
This was the cause that began
Their talk of me; and they began
To say: That one does not seem an unreal body.
Then some moved toward me as far as they could,
Always taking care
Not to leave where they would not be burned.
O you who walk, perhaps not because you are slow,
But perhaps out of respect, behind the others,
Answer me, for I burn in thirst and fire.
Your answer is needed not by me alone,
For all these thirst for it more
Than an Indian or Ethiopian for cold water.
Tell us how you make yourself a wall
Against the sun, as if you had not yet
Entered the nets of death.
So one of them spoke to me, and I would already
Have made myself known, had I not been intent
On another new sight then appearing;
For through the middle of the fiery path
A company came facing this one,
And held me fixed in wonder as I watched.
There I saw each shade hurry from every side,
And kiss one another, without stopping,
Content with a brief greeting.
So within their dark ranks
One ant meets another, perhaps
To learn their way and fortune.
As soon as they ended their friendly greeting,
Before the first step had passed,
Each company strove to cry aloud;
The new company: Sodom and Gomorrah!
And the other: Pasiphaë entered the cow,
So that the bull might run to her lust.
Then, like cranes flying toward the Rhipean mountains,
Some toward the mountains, others toward the sands,
These shunning cold, those the sun, they flew;
One company goes, another comes,
And weeping they return to their first cries,
And to the cry that suits them best:
And they drew near me again, as before,
Those same ones who had questioned me,
Their faces intent on listening.
I, who had twice seen them pleased,
Began: O souls, secure
That one day you will be at peace,
My limbs have not remained behind in the world,
Neither immature nor aged; they are here with me,
With their blood and their joints.
I go upward so that I may no longer be blind;
A lady above obtains grace for me,
And for her I carry my mortal body through your world.
But may your greatest desire soon be fulfilled,
So that Heaven may receive you,
Heaven, full of love and wider in its expanse;
Tell me, so that I may still write of you,
Who you are, and who is that company
Going behind your backs.
No more was the mountaineer amazed and troubled,
Gazing in silence,
When the rustic enters the city,
Than each shade appeared;
But when they were relieved of wonder,
Which soon changes in noble hearts,
Blessed are you, who in our regions
Begin again, said the one who first questioned me,
To gather experience so that you may die wiser!
The company that does not come with us offended
God in that for which Cæsar, triumphing,
Heard Queen called against himself;
Therefore they depart crying Sodom,
Reproaching themselves, as you have heard,
And by shame they help the burning.
Our sin was that of hermaphrodites;
But because we did not keep human law,
Following appetite like beasts,
In our disgrace, when we depart,
The name of her is read by us,
Who made herself a beast in the beastly wood.
Now you know our deeds and what we were guilty of;
If you would know our names,
There is no time to tell, and I could not.
I will satisfy your wish about me:
I am Guido Guinicelli, and I am purifying myself,
For I repented well before the end.
As in Lycurgus' grief
Two sons came to see their mother again,
So I became, but I did not rise to such joy,
When I hear myself named father
Of my betters, who ever used
Sweet and graceful love rhymes:
Without hearing or speaking, I went thoughtfully
For a long while, gazing at him,
Nor did I move farther through the fire.
When I had feasted my gaze,
I offered myself wholly, ready to serve him,
With an oath that makes others believe.
And he said to me: From what I hear, you leave
So clear a trace in me
That Lethe cannot take it or make it dim.
But if your words have sworn truly,
Tell me, what is the reason you show
By speech and gaze that you hold me dear?
And I said to him: Your sweet writings,
As long as the modern usage lasts,
Will keep their ink precious.
O brother, he said, the one I point out to you
With my finger, and showed the spirit ahead,
Was the finest craftsman of the mother tongue.
In love verse and prose romances
He surpassed all; let fools say
That the man from Limoges surpasses him.
They lifted their faces more toward reputation than truth,
And so fixed their opinion
Before hearing art or reason;
So many elders did with Guittone,
From shout to shout giving him praise,
Until truth prevailed among more people.
Now, if you have so great a privilege
That you may go to the cloister
Where Christ is abbot of the company,,
Say one Our Father for me,
As much as we need of it in this world,
Where the power to sin is no longer ours.
Then, perhaps to make room for another beside him,
He vanished into the fire,
Like a fish sinking through water.
I moved a little toward the one shown ahead,
And said that my desire had prepared
A gracious place for his name.
He began freely to say:
Your courteous request pleases me so much,
That I cannot and will not conceal my name.
I am Arnaut, who weeps and goes singing,
As I did for my past sins,
And gladly go toward the day I hope for.
Now I beg you, by that power
That guides you to the summit of the stair,
Remember my pain in due time.
Afterward he hid in the purifying fire.
