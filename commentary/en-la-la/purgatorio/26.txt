As thus along the edge, one before another,
We walked, and often the good master
Said to me: Take care; may my warning help you.
The sun struck my right shoulder,
Which already, shining, all the west
Was turning to a pale azure appearance;
And with my shadow I made more burning
The appearance of the flame; yet at such a sign
I saw many shades take notice as they went.
This was the cause that began
Their mouths speaking of me; and they began
To say: That one does not seem an unreal body.
Then toward me, as far as they could move,
Some moved, always taking care
Not to leave where they would not be burned.
O you who walk, not because you are slower,
But perhaps out of respect, behind the others,
Answer me, for I burn in thirst and fire.
Your answer is needed not by me alone,
For all these thirst for it more
Than an Indian or Ethiopian for cold water.
Tell us how it is that you make yourself a wall
Against the sun, as if you had not yet
Entered the nets of death.
So one of them spoke to me, and I would have made myself
Already known, had I not been intent
On another new sight then appearing;
For through the middle of the fiery path
A company came facing this one,
And held me fixed in wonder as I watched.
There I saw hastening from every side
Each shade, and one kissing another,
Without stopping, content with a brief greeting.
So within and through their dark ranks
One ant meets another,
Perhaps to learn their way and fortune.
As soon as they ended their friendly greeting,
Before the first step had passed them,
Each company strove to cry aloud;
The new company: Sodom and Gomorrah!
And the other: Pasiphaë entered the cow,
So that the bull might run to her lust.
Then, like cranes that toward the Rhipean mountains
Would fly, some that way and others toward the sands,
These shunning cold, those the sun;
One company goes, another comes,
And weeping they return to their first cries,
And to the cry that suits them best:
And they drew near me, as before,
Those same ones who had questioned me,
Their faces intent on listening.
Whereupon I, who had twice seen them pleased,
Began: O troubled souls, secure
That one day you will be at peace,
Neither immature nor aged have they remained behind,
My limbs, in the world; but they are here with me,
With their blood and also with their joints.
I go upward this way so that I may no longer be blind;
There is a lady above who obtains grace for me,
And for her I carry my mortal body through your world.
But may fulfillment of your greatest desire
Soon come, so that Heaven may receive you,
Heaven, full of love and wider in its expanse;
Tell me, so that I may still mark the pages,
Who you are, and who is that company
Going behind your backs.
No more amazed and troubled was
The mountaineer, and as he gazed he fell silent,
When the untutored rustic enters the city,
Than each shade appeared;
But when they were relieved of wonder,
Which soon changes in noble hearts,
Blessed are you, who in our regions
Resumed the one who first questioned me,
So that you may die wiser, gather experience!
The company that does not come with us offended
God in that for which Cæsar, triumphing,
Heard Queen called against himself;
Therefore they depart crying Sodom,
Reproaching themselves, as you have heard,
And by shame they help the burning.
Our sin was that of hermaphrodites;
But because we did not keep human law,
Following appetite like beasts,
In our disgrace we read,
When we depart, the name of her
Who made herself a beast in the beastly wood.
Now you know our deeds and what we were guilty of;
If perhaps you would know by name who we were,
There is no time to tell, and I could not.
I will indeed satisfy your wish about me:
I am Guido Guinicelli, and already I am purifying myself,
For I repented well before the end.
As in Lycurgus' grief
Two sons came to see their mother again,
So I became, but I did not rise so far,
When I hear myself named father
Of mine and of others better than I, who ever
Used sweet and graceful love rhymes:
And without hearing or speaking, I went
For a long while, gazing at him,
Nor did I move farther through the fire.
When I had feasted my gaze,
I offered myself wholly, ready to serve him,
With an oath that makes others believe.
And he said to me: You leave such a trace,
From what I hear, in me, and one so clear
That Lethe cannot take it or make it dim.
But if your words have now sworn truly,
Tell me, what is the reason you show
By speech and gaze that you hold me dear?
And I said to him: Your sweet writings,
Which, as long as the modern usage lasts,
Will keep their ink precious.
O brother, he said, the one I now point out to you
With my finger, and showed the spirit ahead,
Was the finest craftsman of the mother tongue.
In love verse and prose romances
He surpassed all; and let fools say
That I believe the man from Limoges surpasses him.
They lifted their faces more toward reputation than truth,
And so fixed their opinion
Before hearing art or reason;
So many elders did with Guittone,
From shout to shout alone giving him praise,
Until truth prevailed over him among more people.
Now, if you have so great a privilege
That you may go to the cloister
Where Christ is abbot of the company,,
Have one Our Father said for me,
As much as we need of it in this world,
Where the power to sin is no longer ours.
Then, perhaps to make room in turn for another,
Whom he had beside him, he vanished into the fire,
Like a fish sinking through water to the bottom.
I moved toward the one who had shown himself a little before,
And said that for his name my desire
Had prepared a gracious place.
He began freely to say:
Your courteous request pleases me so much,
That I cannot and will not dissemble.
I am Arnaut, who weeps and goes singing,
As I already did for my past sins,
And gladly go rejoicing toward the day ahead that I hope for.
Now I beg you, by that power
That guides you to the summit of the stair, that
You remember my pain in due time.
Afterward he hid in the fire that purifies them.
