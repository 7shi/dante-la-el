Who is this who circles our mountain,
before death has given him flight,
and opens and closes his eyes at will?
I do not know who he is, but I know he is not alone;
you ask him, since you are nearer to him,
and gently, in such a way that you speak to him.
Thus two spirits, leaning toward each other,
were talking of me there on my right;
then they raised their faces to speak to me.
And one said: O soul, who, fixed
in a body still, are journeying toward heaven,
for charity, comfort us and tell us:
where you come from and who you are; for you make
us wonder so much at your grace,
as much as something never seen before calls for.
And I: Through Tuscany's midst flows
a little river born on Falterona;
and a hundred miles of course do not sate it.
Above that river I bring this person back.
To tell you who I am would be to speak in vain,
for my name is not yet widely known.
If I grasp your meaning rightly
with my understanding, then replied to me
the one who had spoken first, you are speaking of the Arno.
And the other said to him: Why hides
this man the name of that river,
as people do with shameful things?
And the spirit who had been asked about this
answered thus: I do not know, but it is indeed fitting
that the name of such a valley should perish,
for from its source, where so high is
the Apennine ridge of which Pelorus is a spur
that in few places does it rise beyond that height,
down to the place where it repays
some of what heaven draws up from the sea,
from which rivers receive all that goes along with them.
Virtue thus flees as from enemies,
from all of them, like a snake, whether through the ill fortune
of the place or the bad habits that goad them.
Hence they have changed their nature,
these inhabitants of the wretched valley,
that Circe seems to have kept them as beasts.
Among the filthy pigs, more fit for acorns
than for any other food made for human use,
it first directs its wretched course.
Next, as it descends, it finds small dogs,
more snarling than their strength warrants;
and disdainfully it turns its snout away from them.
It goes on descending; and as it grows fuller,
the more dogs it finds turning into wolves,
the accursed and ill-fated valley.
Descending then through several deep channels,
it finds foxes so full of cunning
that they fear no trick that might ensnare them.
Nor will I stop speaking, though another hears me;
and this man will do well if he still remembers
what a true spirit reveals to me.
I see your nephew becoming
a hunter of wolves on the bank
of the fierce river, terrifying them all.
He sells their flesh while they are still alive,
then kills them like some ancient beast;
he deprives many of life, and himself of honour.
Bloodied, he leaves the sorrowful wood;
he leaves it so that for a thousand years hence
it will not grow back into its former wooded state.
As the announcement of sorrowful losses
clouds the face of one who hears it,
from whatever quarter danger assails him,
so I saw the other soul, who stood turned
to see, grow troubled and become sad
once it had taken the words as referring to itself.
The speech of one and the other's expression
made me eager to learn their names;
and I asked them, mixing my request with pleas.
Then the spirit who had spoken to me first
began again: You want me to bring myself
to do for you what you were unwilling to do for me.
But since God wills that through you should shine
so greatly his grace, I will not be sparing with you;
know then that I am Guido del Duca.
My blood was so inflamed with envy
that, had I seen a man grow joyful,
you would have seen me blotched with spite.
Such chaff I reap from my own seed.
O human race, why set your heart
there where sharing must be forbidden?
This is Rinier, this is the worth and honour
of the house; he is of the Calboli, where no one
has since inherited his virtue.
And not only his family has been stripped
between the Po, the mountains, the sea, and Reno,
of the goodness sought for truth and delight;
for within these borders it is filled
with poisonous brambles, so that only slowly
would cultivation from now on clear them away.
Where are good Lizio, and Arrigo Mainardi,
Pier Traversaro, and Guido di Carpigna?
O people of Romagna, turned to bastards!
When will a Fabbro be reborn in Bologna?
When in Faenza will there be a Bernardin di Fosco,
a noble branch from a humble stock?
Do not wonder, Tuscan, if I weep
when I remember, with Guido da Prata,
Ugolin d'Azzo, when he lived among us;
Federico Tignoso and his company,
the houses of Traversara and Anastagi;
and both families have been dispossessed.
The ladies and knights, the labours and delights
that stirred us to honour and courtesy,
there where hearts have become so wicked.
O Brettinoro, why do you not flee along the road,
since your family has departed,
and many others, lest they grow wicked?
Bagnacavallo does well to have no children;
and Castrocaro does ill, and Conio worse,
in not striving to beget such sons.
The Pagani will do well when the demon
of theirs has departed; yet nothing pure
will ever remain as a record of them.
O Ugolin de' Fantolin, secure
is your name, since no one is now expected
who could darken it by degenerating.
But now go your way, Tuscan, from this point on; for now I delight
too much in weeping rather than speaking;
your account has so tightened my heart.
We knew those dear souls
could hear us walking; so, by keeping silent,
they let us feel confident on our way.
Afterwards, as we went on, we were left alone;
it seemed like lightning when it splits the air,
the voice that came toward us, saying:
Whoever finds me will kill me.
And it fled like thunder that vanishes
when it suddenly splits a cloud.
As our hearing had ceased from that voice,
and behold, another with a great crash,
which was like thunder following close behind:
I am Aglauros, who became stone.
And then, to draw closer to the Poet,
I stepped back, not forward.
The air was now quiet all around,
and he said to me: That was the hard bridle
that should keep a person within their own bounds.
But you take the bait, so that the hook
of the ancient adversary draws you to itself;
and therefore bridle or warning does little good.
Heaven calls you, circling around you,
showing you its eternal beauties;
yet your eyes keep gazing at the earth.
Therefore the One who sees all chastises you.
