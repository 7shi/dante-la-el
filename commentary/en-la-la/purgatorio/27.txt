As when it sends forth its first rays
Where its Maker shed his blood,
As the Ebro sets beneath the other Balance,
And once again lights the waves of Ganges,
Whence day was passing away, so stood the sun,
When God's angel appeared to us, joyful.
Outside the flame, on the bank, he stood,
And sang: Blessed are the pure in heart,
In a voice far more alive than ours.
Then: You cannot go farther unless it first bites,
Holy souls, the fire: enter it,
And do not be deaf to the song being sung beyond.
He spoke thus as we came near him;
At his words I became such, when I understood him,
As one who has been laid in a grave.
I held myself over my clasped hands,
Looking at the fire and vividly imagining
Human bodies I had already seen burning.
The good guides turned toward me,
And Virgil said to me: My son,
There may be torment here, but no death.
Remember, remember: if I
Across Geryon led you safely,
What shall I do now, so much nearer to God?
Believe for certain that if inside the belly
Of this flame you stayed for a full thousand years,
It could not make one hair of yours fall.
And if you think I might deceive you,
Go toward it and test it for yourself
With your hands at the hem of your clothes.
Put down, now, put down all fear;
Turn this way and come in safely.
Yet I stood firmer still, and against my own conviction.
When he saw me still standing firm and stubborn,
A little troubled, he said: Now look, son,
This wall stands between you and Beatrice.
As at the name of Thisbe he opened his eyes,
Pyramus, as he lay dying, and looked at her,
Then when the mulberry turned red,
So, my stubbornness softened,
I turned toward the wise guide, hearing the name
That always sprouts in my mind.
Then he shook his brow and said: What?
Are we going to stay here? Then he smiled,
As one does to a child won over by an apple.
Then he went into the fire ahead of me,
Asking Statius to come behind,
For before, on the long road, he had kept us apart.
Once we were inside, into a mass of boiling glass
I would have thrown myself to cool myself;
So measureless was the blaze there.
My gentle father, to comfort me,
Kept speaking only of Beatrice,
Saying: I seem already to see her eyes.
A voice guided us, singing
From beyond; and we, intent only on it,
Came out where the ascent began.
Come, blessed of my Father,
Sounded within a light that was there,
So overwhelming me that I could not look.
The sun is setting, he added, and evening comes;
Do not stop, but hasten your steps
While the west has not yet darkened.
The path rose straight inward through the rock,
In such a direction that it took away the rays
Of the sun before me, already low.
And we tried a few steps,
That the sun was setting, by the disappearance of the shadow,
Both I and my wise guides felt it behind us.
And before in all its vast parts
The horizon had become of one appearance,
And night had spread everywhere,
Each of us made a bed on one step,
For the mountain's nature broke our
Strength to climb more than our desire.
Like those that grow tame as they chew the cud,
Goats that earlier were quick and unruly
On the heights before they had eaten,
Silent in the shade while the sun burns,
Watched by the shepherd who on his staff
Leans, and leaning there serves them;
And like the herdsman who sleeps outdoors,
Quietly spending the night beside his flock,
Keeping watch so no wild beast scatters it,
So all three of us were then,
I like a goat, and they like shepherds,
Sheltered on both sides by the cave.
Little daylight showed there;
Yet in that little I could see the stars,
Brighter and larger than was usual for them.
As I mused thus and gazed thus at them,
Sleep took me, which often,
Before an event, knows the news.
At the hour, I think, when from the east
Venus first shone upon the mountain,
She who seems always to burn with love's fire,
Young and beautiful, in a dream I seemed
To see a woman across a meadow,
Gathering flowers; and singing, she said:
Let whoever asks my name know
That I am Leah, and I move around
My lovely hands to make myself a garland.
To please myself I adorn myself here before the mirror;
But my sister Rachel never turns away
From her mirror, and sits there all day.
She is eager to gaze with her lovely eyes,
As I am to adorn myself with my hands;
For her, seeing; for me, adorning, brings contentment.
And now, in the pre-dawn brightness,
Which seems all the dearer to travelers
The farther from home they lodge on their return,
Darkness fled on every side,
And my sleep fled with it; so I rose,
Seeing the great masters already risen.
That sweet fruit which through so many branches
Mortal care goes searching for
Will today quiet your hunger.
To me Virgil just such
Words addressed; and never were there New Year's gifts
That were as pleasing as these words.
Such desire upon desire came over me
To be above, that with every step afterward
I felt my wings for flight growing.
When the whole stairway beneath us
Had been climbed and we stood on the topmost step,
Virgil fixed his eyes on me,
And said: Temporal fire and eternal
You have seen, my son, and have come to a place
Beyond which I cannot see for myself.
I have brought you here with skill and craft;
From now on let your own pleasure guide you.
You are beyond the steep ways, and the narrow you are beyond.
See there the sun that shines upon your brow;
See the grass, flowers, and little trees
That this earth produces solely of itself.
While the joyful, beautiful eyes arrive,
Those that made me come to you in tears,
You may sit and may walk among them.
Expect no more words or sign from me.
Your will is free, upright, and sound;
And it would be wrong not to follow its judgment.
For I crown and mitre you over yourself.
