As when the sun sends forth its first rays
Where its Maker shed his blood,
As the Ebro sets beneath the other Balance,
And once again lights the waves of Ganges,
So stood the sun, and day was passing away,
When God's angel appeared to us, joyful.
Outside the flame, on the bank, he stood,
And sang: Blessed are the pure in heart,
In a voice far more alive than ours.
Then he said: You cannot go farther unless fire first bites you;
Holy souls, enter the fire,
And do not be deaf to the song beyond.
He spoke thus as we came near him;
At his words I became like one
Who has been laid in a grave.
I clasped my hands together,
Looking at the fire and vividly imagining
Human bodies I had seen burning.
My good guides turned toward me,
And Virgil said: My son,
There may be torment here, but no death.
Remember, remember: if I led you safely
Across Geryon, what shall I do now,
So much nearer to God?
Believe for certain that if you stayed inside
The belly of this flame for a thousand years,
It could not make one hair of yours fall.
And if you think I might deceive you,
Go toward it and test it with your hands,
At the hem of your clothes.
Put down, now, put down all fear;
Turn this way and come in safely.
Yet I stood firm, against my own conviction.
When he saw me still standing firm and stubborn,
A little troubled, he said: Now look, my son,
This wall stands between you and Beatrice.
As at the name of Thisbe Pyramus opened his eyes
As he lay dying and looked at her,
When the mulberry turned red,
So, my stubbornness softened,
I turned toward the wise guide, hearing the name
That always sprouts in my mind.
Then he shook his brow and said: What?
Are we going to stay here? Then he smiled,
As one does to a child won over by an apple.
Then he went into the fire ahead of me,
Asking Statius to come behind,
For before, on the long road, he had kept us apart.
Once I was inside, I would have thrown myself
Into boiling glass to cool myself;
So measureless was the blaze there.
My gentle father, to comfort me,
Kept speaking only of Beatrice,
Saying: I seem already to see her eyes.
A voice guided us, singing
From beyond; intent only on it,
We came out where the ascent began.
Come, blessed of my Father,
Sounded within a light that was there,
So overwhelming me that I could not look.
The sun is setting, he added, and evening comes;
Do not stop, but hasten your steps
While the west has not yet darkened us.
The path rose straight through the rock,
Toward a point that took away the rays
Of the sun before me, already low.
We tried a few steps,
Then, as the sun sank and its light faded,
I and my wise guides felt it behind us.
Before the whole vast horizon
Had become one dark appearance,
And night had spread everywhere,
Each of us made a bed on one step,
For the mountain's nature broke our strength
To climb more than our desire urged us.
Like goats, now tame as they chew the cud,
Though earlier they were quick and unruly
On the heights before they had eaten,
Silent in the shade while the sun burns,
Watched by the shepherd leaning on his staff,
Who leans there and serves them,
And like the herdsman who sleeps outdoors,
Quietly keeping watch all night beside his flock
So no wild beast scatters it,
So all three of us were then,
I like a goat, they like shepherds,
Sheltered on both sides by the cave.
Little daylight showed there outside;
Yet in that little I could see the stars,
Brighter and larger than usual.
As I mused and gazed at them,
Sleep took me; sleep that often,
Before an event, knows the news.
At the hour, I think, when from the east
Venus first shone upon Mount Purgatory,
She who seems always to burn with love's fire,
A young and beautiful woman seemed to me
In a dream, walking across a meadow,
Gathering flowers; and singing, she said:
Let whoever asks my name know
That I am Leah, and I move my lovely hands
Around to make myself a garland.
To please myself I adorn myself before the mirror;
But my sister Rachel never turns away
From her mirror, and sits there all day.
She is eager to gaze with her lovely eyes,
As I am to adorn myself with my hands;
For her, seeing; for me, adorning, brings contentment.
And now, in the pre-dawn brightness,
Which seems all the dearer to travelers
The farther they have journeyed from home,
Darkness fled on every side,
And my sleep fled with it; I rose,
Seeing the great masters already risen.
That sweet fruit for which mortal care
Goes searching through so many branches
Will today quiet your hunger.
Virgil spoke these words to me;
No New Year's gift was ever
As pleasing as they were.
Such a desire to be above came over me
That with every step I took afterward
I felt my wings for flight growing.
When the whole stairway beneath us
Had been climbed and we stood on the topmost step,
Virgil fixed his eyes on me,
And said: You have seen temporal fire
And eternal fire, my son, and have come to a place
Beyond which I cannot see for myself.
I have brought you here with skill and craft;
From now on let your own pleasure guide you.
You are beyond the steep, narrow ways.
See how the sun shines upon your brow;
See the grass, flowers, and little trees
That this earth alone produces.
While the joyful, beautiful eyes arrive,
Those that made me come to you in tears,
You may sit and walk among them.
Expect no more words or sign from me.
Your will is free, upright, and sound;
It would be wrong not to follow its judgment.
For I crown and mitre you over yourself.
