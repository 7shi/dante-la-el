And you, who are beyond the sacred stream,
she began again, turning her words directly to me,
who had found them sharp even when spoken edge-on;
and she continued without delay:
Tell me, tell me whether this is true; to so many charges
your confession must be joined.
My power was so bewildered
that my voice stirred, then died
before it could leave its organs.
She waited a little, then said: What are you thinking?
Answer me, for the sad memories
have not yet been washed from you.
Shame and fear mingled together
forced such a yes from my mouth
that eyes were needed to understand it.
As a crossbow breaks when it shoots,
its cord and bow snapped by excess tension,
and the shaft strikes the target with less force,
so I broke beneath the heavy burden,
pouring out tears and sighs,
and my voice slackened on its way out.
Then she said to me: Among my desires,
which led you to love the highest good,
beyond which nothing can be desired,
what ditches or chains did you find
that made you strip away your hope
of going farther onward?
And what advantages and profits
appeared in the faces of others,
that you should give them precedence?
After releasing a bitter sigh,
I could barely find a voice to answer,
and my lips formed it with effort.
Weeping, I said: The things of this world,
with their false delight, turned my steps away
as soon as your face was hidden.
She said: If you were silent or denied
what you confess, your guilt would be no less known;
such a judge knows it.
But when the charge of sin bursts
from one's own mouth, in our court
the wheel turns against its cutting edge.
Still, so that you may feel less shame
for your error, and next time,
when you hear the sirens, be stronger,
put down the seed of weeping and listen;
then you will hear how my buried flesh
should have moved you in the opposite direction.
Neither nature nor art ever showed you
such delight as the lovely limbs
in which I was enclosed, now scattered in earth.
If the highest delight failed you
because of my death, what mortal thing
should then draw you into its desire?
You should, at the first arrow
from deceptive things, have risen up
and followed me, who was no longer such.
Your wings should not have weighed you down
to await more blows, whether Pargolecta
or another fleeting vanity.
A young bird waits for two or three signs,
but before the eyes of experienced birds
the net is spread or the arrow shot in vain.
Like children, silent with shame,
eyes lowered, standing to listen,
recognizing themselves and repenting,
so I stood. Then she said: Since
you grieve at hearing this, lift your chin,
and you will feel greater grief as you look.
A sturdy oak is uprooted with less resistance,
whether by our wind
or by the wind from Iarbas's land,
than I raised my chin at her command;
and when she asked my face for my beard,
I knew well the poison of her argument.
And when my face was lifted,
my eye perceived those first creatures
ceasing their work of scattering flowers.
My eyes, still a little unsure,
saw Beatrice turned toward the beast
that is one person in two natures.
Beneath her veil, beyond the riverbank,
she seemed to surpass her former self
more than all the women here she once surpassed.
The nettle of repentance stung me there so deeply
that every thing which had turned me
most toward its love became my greatest enemy.
Such recognition bit my heart
that I fell overcome; what I became then
is known to her who gave me the cause.
Then, when my heart restored my strength,
I saw above me the lady I had left alone,
and I said: Hold me, hold me.
She had drawn me into the river up to my throat,
and pulling me behind her, she went
lightly over the water like a little boat.
When I was near the blessed bank,
Asperges me sounded so sweetly
that I cannot recall it, let alone write it.
The lovely lady opened her arms,
embraced my head and plunged me under,
so that I had to swallow the water.
Then she drew me out and, washed, presented me
within the dance of the four lovely ladies,
each covering me with her arm.
We are nymphs here, and stars in heaven;
before Beatrice descended to the world,
we were appointed her handmaids.
We will lead you to her eyes; but the three beyond,
who gaze more deeply, will sharpen yours
in the joyful light within.
Thus singing, they began; then
they led me with them to the griffin's breast,
where Beatrice stood turned toward us.
They said: Do not spare your sight;
we have set you before the emeralds
from which Love once drew his weapons for you.
A thousand desires, hotter than flame,
fixed my eyes on those shining eyes
that remained steady on the griffin.
As the sun shines in a mirror,
so the twofold beast shone within them,
now with one aspect, now another.
Think, reader, whether I marveled
when I saw the thing itself remain still
while its image changed.
While my soul, full of wonder and joy,
tasted that food which, satisfying itself,
makes others thirst for it,
the other three, showing their higher rank
in their bearing, came forward,
dancing in their angelic circle.
Turn, Beatrice, turn your holy eyes,
was their song, toward your faithful one,
who has traveled so far to see you.
For grace, grant us the grace to unveil
your face to him, so that he may discern
the second beauty you conceal.
O divine splendor, O eternal light,
whoever grew pale beneath Parnassus's shade
or drank from its spring,
would seem to have an overwhelmed mind
if trying to portray you as you appeared
where heaven's harmony overshadows you,
when you unveiled yourself in the open air.
