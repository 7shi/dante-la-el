Neither speech our journey, nor the journey our speech, any slower
made; but reasoning, we went swiftly,
like a ship driven by a fair wind.
And the shades, who seemed squalid things,
through the hollows of their eyes their wonder
drew from me, noticing that I was alive.
And I, continuing my discourse,
said: She perhaps goes more slowly
than she otherwise would, for another's sake.
But tell me, if you know, where Piccarda is;
tell me whether I see anyone worthy of note
among these people gazing at me so.
My sister, in whom, between beauty and goodness,
I cannot tell which was greater, triumphs joyfully
on high Olympus, now crowned.
So he said to me first, and then: Here it is not forbidden
to name anyone, since so shrivelled is
our appearance now through abstinence.
This one—and he pointed with his finger—is Bonagiunta;
Bonagiunta of Lucca; and that face
beyond him, more wasted than the rest,
held Holy Church in its arms;
he was from Tours, and by fasting purges
the eels of Bolsena in Vernaccia wine.
Many others he named one by one;
and as he named them, they all seemed content,
so I saw not one face darkened on that account.
I saw gnawing at air from hunger
Ubaldino della Pila, and Boniface,
who with his crozier shepherded many peoples.
I saw Messer Marchese, who had time
once to drink at Forlì with less dryness,
yet was such that he never felt sated.
But as one does who looks and then values
one person more than another, so I did with the man from Lucca,
who seemed most to want to know me.
He murmured: I know not what Gentucca;
I heard it where he felt the wound
of justice that so gnaws at them.
O soul, I said, who seems so eager
to speak with me, see that I understand you,
and satisfy us both with your words.
A woman has been born, and does not yet wear a veil;
he said to me, who will make you like
my city, though people speak ill of it.
You will leave carrying this foreknowledge;
if you have misunderstood my murmur,
the truth of events will soon make it clear to you.
But tell me whether I see here the one who forth
brought the new rhymes, beginning:
Ladies who have understanding of love.
And I to him: I am one who, when
Love breathes on me, take note, and that manner
which he dictates within I give expression to.
O brother, now I see, he said, the knot
that held back the Notary, Guittone, and me
from the sweet new style, the key.
I see well how your pens
follow closely the one who dictates;
that surely did not happen with our own.
And whoever sets himself to look farther ahead
sees no more of the difference between one style and another.
And he fell silent, almost content.
As birds that winter toward the Nile
sometimes form a line in the air,
then fly faster,
so all the people there,
turning their faces, quickened their pace,
light both from leanness and from desire.
And as a man tired from hurrying
lets his companions go on and himself walks slowly
until the pounding in his chest subsides,
so did he let the holy flock pass,
Forese, and came behind with me,
saying: When shall I see you again?
I do not know, I replied, how long I shall live;
but my return will not now come so soon
that my desire has not reached the shore before me.
For the place where I was set to live,
day by day is stripped of more goodness
and seems set for a grievous ruin.
Now go, he said, for the one most to blame for this
I see dragged by the tail of a beast
toward the valley where he will never be absolved.
The beast goes faster at every step,
growing ever swifter until it strikes him,
and leaves his body vilely destroyed.
These wheels will not turn for long,
and he raised his eyes to Heaven, before there becomes clear to you
what my words cannot explain further.
For now, remain, for time is precious
in this realm, so much so that I lose too much
by keeping pace with you step for step.
As sometimes there rides out at a gallop
a knight from the troop in which he rides,
and goes to win himself honor in the first encounter,
so he left us, with longer strides;
and I remained on the road with those two
who were such great marshals of the world.
And when he had gone so far ahead of us,
so that my eyes followed after him,
as my mind had followed his words,
branches heavy and full of life appeared to me,
belonging to another tree, and not very far away,
for I had only then turned in that direction.
I saw people beneath it raising their hands,
and crying, I know not what, toward the leaves,
and like eager, foolish little children,
who beg, but the one begged does not answer;
yet, to make their desire very keen,
he holds what they long for on high and does not hide it.
Then the crowd went away, as if disappointed,
and we came to that great tree
that rejects so many prayers and tears.
Pass farther on without drawing near;
higher up is the tree bitten by Eve,
and this plant was taken from it.
So spoke I know not who among the branches;
and Virgil, Statius, and I, constrained by this,
went on along the side where the path rose.
Remember, the voice said, those accursed ones
formed in the clouds, whom, once they were full,
Theseus provoked to fight with their doubled ranks;
and the Hebrews, who proved weak at the drink,
so that Gideon did not take them as companions
when he descended toward Midian from the hills.
Thus, close against one edge of the path,
we passed on, hearing examples of gluttony,
which had once brought wretched gain.
Then, as we spread out on the deserted road,
a good thousand paces and more carried us onward,
each of us pondering in silence.
What are you three alone going along thinking about so deeply?
a sudden voice said; and I started,
as frightened, skittish animals do.
And I raised my head to see who it was,
and never had there been seen in a furnace
glass or metal so bright and red
as the one I saw, who said: If you wish
to go upward, you must turn here;
this is the way for anyone who wishes to go in search of peace.
His appearance had dazzled my sight,
so I turned back toward my teachers,
like someone who goes where he is guided by what he hears.
And as, herald of dawn,
the breeze in May moves and gives fragrance,
all filled with grass and flowers,
so I felt a wind pass across the middle of
my forehead, and clearly felt the movement of the feather
that brought me the scent of ambrosia.
And I heard it said: Blessed are those enlightened by
so much grace that the love of taste
does not kindle excessive desire in their hearts,
hungering always as much as is just.
