Neither speech slowed our journey,
nor did the journey slow our speech;
reasoning together, we went swiftly, like a ship driven by a fair wind.
The shades, who seemed twice dead,
looked at me in wonder through the hollows of their eyes,
noticing that I was alive.
And I, continuing my discourse,
said: She perhaps goes upward more slowly
than she otherwise would, for another's sake.
Tell me, if you know, where Piccarda is;
tell me whether among these people gazing at me
I see anyone worthy of note.
My sister, of whom I cannot tell
whether beauty or goodness was greater,
triumphs joyfully, crowned, on high Olympus.
So he said first, and then: Here no one
is forbidden to name, since by abstinence
our features have grown so shrivelled.
This one—and he pointed—is Bonagiunta;
and that face beyond him, more wasted than the rest,
once held Holy Church in its arms;
he was Pope Martin, from Tours in France,
and by fasting purges the eels of Bolsena
and the Vernaccia wine.
Many others he named one by one;
and as he named them, they all seemed content,
so I saw no darkened face.
I saw Ubaldino della Pila gnawing at air
from hunger, and Boniface, who with his crozier
shepherded many peoples.
I saw Messer Marchese, who once had time
to drink at Forlì with less dryness,
yet was so thirsty he never felt sated.
But as one looks and then values one person
more than another, so I did with the man from Lucca,
who seemed to know me best.
He murmured, and I heard something like Gentucca
where he felt the wound of justice
that so gnaws at them.
O soul, I said, who seems so eager
to speak with me, make yourself understood,
and satisfy us both with your words.
A woman has been born, and does not yet wear a veil;
she will make you like my city,
though people speak ill of it.
You will leave carrying this foreknowledge;
and if you have misunderstood my murmur,
the truth of events will soon explain it.
But tell me whether I see here the one
who brought forth the new rhymes,
beginning, Ladies who have understanding of love.
And I to him: I am one who, when Love breathes on me,
take note, and in that manner
give expression to what he dictates within.
O brother, now I see, he said, the knot
that held back the Notary, Guittone,
and me from the sweet new style.
I see well how your pens follow closely
the one who dictates; that surely did not happen
with our own.
Whoever looks farther ahead
sees no more of the difference between one style and another.
He fell silent, almost content.
As birds that winter toward the Nile
sometimes form a flock in the air,
then fly faster in a line,
so all the people there turned their faces,
quickening their pace,
light both from leanness and from desire.
And as a man tired from hurrying
lets his companions go on and walks slowly
until the pounding in his chest subsides,
so Forese let the holy flock pass
and came behind me, saying:
When shall I see you again?
I do not know, I replied, how long I shall live;
but my return will not come so soon
that my desire has not reached the shore before me.
For the place where I was set to live,
day by day is stripped of more goodness
and seems set for a ruinous end.
Now go, he said, for the one most to blame
I see dragged by the tail of a beast
down toward the valley of Hell, where no one is ever absolved.
The beast goes faster at every step,
growing ever swifter until it strikes him,
and leaves his body vilely destroyed.
These wheels will not turn for long,
and he raised his eyes to Heaven, before what I say
will become clear to you.
For now, remain; time is precious
in this realm, and I lose too much
by keeping pace with you step for step.
As a knight sometimes rides out at a gallop
from the troop in which he rides,
to win honor in the first encounter,
so he left us, with longer strides;
and I remained on the road with those two
who were such great marshals of the world.
And when he had gone ahead of us,
so that my eyes followed after him,
as my mind had followed his words,
branches heavy with living fruit appeared to me,
belonging to another tree, not far away,
for I had only then turned in that direction.
I saw people beneath it raising their hands,
and crying, I know not what, toward the leaves,
like eager, foolish little children,
who beg, but the one begged does not answer;
yet, to make their desire keen,
he keeps their longing raised on high.
Then the crowd went away, as if disappointed,
and we came to the great tree
that rejects so many prayers and tears.
Pass farther on without drawing near;
higher up is the tree bitten by Eve,
and this plant was taken from it.
So someone spoke among the branches;
and Virgil, Statius, and I, constrained by this,
went on along the side where the path rose.
Remember, the voice said, those accursed ones
formed in the clouds, whom Theseus, once they were full,
provoked to fight with their doubled ranks;
and remember the Hebrews, who proved weak at the drink,
so that Gideon did not take them as companions
when he descended toward Midian from the hills.
Thus, close against one edge of the path,
we passed on, hearing examples of gluttony,
which had once brought wretched gain.
Then, as we widened our way onto the open road,
we went on for a thousand paces and more,
each of us pondering in silence.
What are you three thinking about so deeply?
a sudden voice said; and I started,
as frightened, skittish animals do.
I raised my head to see who it was,
and never had glass or metal in a furnace
looked so bright and red
as the one I saw, who said: If you wish
to go upward, you must turn here;
this is the way for anyone who wishes to go up.
His appearance had dazzled my sight,
so I turned back toward my teachers,
like someone who goes where he is guided by what he hears.
As in May the breeze, herald of dawn,
moves and gives fragrance,
all filled with grass and flowers,
so I felt a wind pass across
the middle of my forehead, and clearly felt the feather
move, bringing the scent of ambrosia.
And I heard a voice say: Blessed are those whom grace
so enlightens that the love of taste
