I had already left those shades,
And followed in the footsteps of my guide,
When one behind me pointed a finger:
She cried: Look, the sunbeam seems not to shine
On the one below, to his left,
And he seems to move as though alive.
I turned my eyes at the sound of those words,
And saw her gaze in wonder
Only at me, only at me, and at the broken light.
Why is your mind so entangled,
My master said, that you slow your pace?
What does the murmuring here matter to you?
Come after me, and let the people talk;
Stand firm like a tower that never shakes
Its summit when the winds blow.
For a person whose thoughts keep sprouting
One above another always moves the goal farther away,
For one impulse weakens the next.
What could I say but, I am coming?
I said it, touched somewhat by the color
That sometimes makes a person worthy of pardon.
Meanwhile, crosswise along the slope,
Some people came a little way ahead of us,
Singing the Miserere verse by verse.
When they saw that I gave no way
For the rays to pass through my body,
They changed their song to one long, hoarse O.
Two of them, like messengers,
Ran toward us and asked: Tell us
About your condition.
My master said: You can go
And tell those who sent you
That this man's body is real flesh.
If they stopped to see his shadow,
As I suppose, that is answer enough.
Let them honor him; it may serve them well.
Never did I see fiery vapors so quickly
Split the clear sky in the middle of the night,
Nor, as the sun goes down, August clouds,
That those shades did not return upward sooner;
And when they reached the others, they turned toward us,
Like a troop rushing without a bridle.
These people coming toward us are many,
And they come to ask you, said the poet.
So go on, and listen as you ask.
O soul, who go to become joyful
With those limbs with which you were born,
They came shouting, Slow your pace a little!
Look, have you ever seen any of us,
So that you may carry news of him back there?
Tell us, why go? Why not stop?
We were all once killed by force,
And sinners until our final hour;
There the light of Heaven made us aware,
So that, repenting and forgiving, outside
Our life we departed reconciled with God,
Who fills us with longing to see Him.
And I: Though I look at your faces,
I recognize no one; but if it pleases you,
O blessed spirits, tell me something I can do,
And I will do it, by that peace
Which, following the feet of such a guide,
I am made to seek from world to world.
Then one began: Each of us trusts
Your kindness without your swearing,
Provided inability does not cut off your will.
Therefore I, who speak alone before the others,
Beg you: if ever you see that country
Which lies between Romagna and Charles's land,
Be kind to me with your prayers in Fano,
So that people may pray well for me there,
And I may cleanse the grave offenses.
I came from there; but the deep wounds
From which the blood flowed, on which I sat,
Were made for me in the lap of the Antenori,
There where I thought myself safest.
The man of Este had it done, who hated me
Far more than justice required.
But if I had fled toward Mira
When I was overtaken at Oriago,
I would still be where breath is drawn.
I ran to the marsh; the reeds and mud
Caught me so that I fell, and there I saw
A pool form on the ground from my veins.
Then another said: Ah, may the desire
That draws you to the high mountain be fulfilled;
With good compassion, help mine as well.
I was of Montefeltro; I was Buonconte.
Giovanna and the others do not care for me,
So I go among these with lowered brow.
And I said to him: What force or chance
Led you astray so far from Campaldino,
That no one ever learned your burial place?
Oh, he replied, at the foot of Casentino
A stream crosses the land, called the Archiano,
Which rises above the Hermitage in the Apennines.
Where its name is lost, I arrived
With my throat pierced, fleeing on foot
And staining the plain with blood.
There I lost sight and speech;
I ended with Mary's name, and there
I fell, and my flesh remained alone.
I shall tell the truth, and you will repeat it among the living;
God's angel took me, and the one from Hell cried:
O you from Heaven, why do you rob me?
You carry this man's eternal soul away
For one little tear that takes it from me;
But I shall deal differently with the other.'
You know well how in the air gathers
The moist vapor that turns to water
As soon as it rises where cold draws it together.
That evil will, which seeks only evil,
Joined with its understanding, stirred smoke and wind
By the power its nature gave it.
Then, when daylight had gone, it covered the valley
From Pratomagno to the great ridge
With mist, and made the sky above grow heavy,
So the pregnant air turned to water.
Rain fell, and to the ditches came
What the earth could not bear of it:
And as it gathered into larger streams,
It rushed toward the royal river so swiftly
That nothing could hold it back.
My frozen body at the river mouth
Was found by the raging Archiano; it pushed it
Into the Arno and loosened the cross on my chest,
Which I had made on myself when pain overcame me.
It rolled me along the banks and bottom,
Then covered and enclosed me in its spoils.
Ah, when you have returned to the world
And rested after the long journey,
The third spirit followed the second,
Saying: Remember me, for I am La Pia.
Siena made me, and Maremma unmade me.
He knows it, the man who first gave me his ring
And married me with his jewel.
