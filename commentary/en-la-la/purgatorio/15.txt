As much, from the ending of the third hour
To the beginning of day, appears of the sphere
Which always plays in the likeness of a child,
So much now seemed, as evening drew near,
To remain to the sun of its course;
It was evening there and midnight here.
And the rays struck us across the middle of the nose,
Because the mountain had so circled around us
That now we walked straight toward the west;
When I felt my forehead burdened
By a brightness much greater than before,
And I was astonished by what I did not know;
So I lifted my hands to the top
Of my eyebrows and made a shade for myself,
To soften the excess of what can be seen.
As when from water or from a mirror
A ray leaps toward the opposite side,
Rising upward in a way equal
From the descending ray, and so far departs
From the stone's fall along an equal track,
As experience and science show;
So it seemed to me that by reflected light
Something before me was struck,
At which my vision quickly turned away.
What is that, dear Father, that I cannot
Protect my sight enough to bear it?
I said, and it seems to be moving toward us.
Do not wonder if you are still blinded
By heaven's company, he replied to me;
It is a messenger who comes to invite you to ascend.
Soon it will be that to see these things
Will not be difficult for you, but will be a delight for you,
To the extent nature has disposed you to perceive.
After we reached the blessed angel,
He said with a glad voice: Enter this way,
By a stair much less steep than the others.
We climbed, having now departed from there,
And Blessed are the merciful was
Sung behind us, and Rejoice, you who overcome.
My master and I alone together
Went upward, and as we walked I thought
To learn something useful from his words;
And I turned toward him and asked thus:
What did the spirit of Romagna mean,
When he spoke of both lack and sharing?
Then he said to me: From his greatest fault
He knows the damage; and so do not wonder
If he reproves others, that there may be less grief.
For he well understood your desires:
Where a share is reduced by having companions,
Envy drives the bellows to sigh.
But if love for the highest sphere
Turned your longing upward,
That fear would not trouble your hearts;
For the more ours is said there,
The more good each person possesses,
And the more charity burns in that cloister.
I am more hungry to be satisfied,
I said, than if I had kept silent before,
And I gather more doubt in my mind.
How can one good, when shared out,
Into more possessions, make their holders richer
Than if it were kept by a few?
And he answered me: Because you fasten
Your mind only on earthly matters,
You take darkness from true light.
That infinite, unspeakable Good
That is there on high flows toward love just so,
As a ray falls on a bright body.
It gives itself as much as it finds of ardor;
Thus, as far as charity extends,
Eternal value grows above it.
And the more people are kindled here on high,
The more there is there to love well, and the more one loves there,
And like a mirror each reflects back to the other.
And if my explanation does not sate you,
You will see Beatrice, who will fully
Take away from you this and every other desire.
Take care, however, that soon they are extinguished,
As two already are, those five wounds
Which close through suffering.
As I was about to say, You satisfy me,
I saw we had reached the next terrace,
So that my wavering eyes made me silent.
Here it seemed to me that into a vision,
An ecstatic one, I was suddenly drawn,
And saw many people inside a temple;
And one woman at the entrance, with a manner
Gentle as a mother, said: My son,
Why have you done this to us?
Behold, your father and I, grieving,
Were searching for you. And when here she fell silent,
What had appeared before vanished.
Then another woman appeared to me, with those tears
Running down her cheeks, shed by sorrow
When it arises from great distress on account of others,
And said: If you are lord of the city
Over whose name there was so great a dispute among the gods,
And from which all knowledge flows,
Avenge yourself on those bold arms
That embraced our daughter, O Pisistratus!
And the lord, gentle and mild, seemed to me
To answer her with a composed face:
What shall we do to those who wish us harm,
If we condemn the one who loves us?
Then I saw a crowd aflame with anger,
Fiercely killing a young man with stones,
Shouting at him continually: Stone him, stone him!
And I saw him bow because of death,
Which was weighing him earthward,
Yet with his eyes he kept opening gates to heaven,
Praying to the Highest Lord in that great persecution,
That he forgive his persecutors,
With the look that releases compassion.
When my soul came back from beyond
To the real things that truly exist outside it,
I recognized my errors as not false.
My guide, who could see me
Become like one waking from sleep,
Said: What is wrong, that you cannot keep your balance?
But you have come more than half a league,
Eyes shut and legs tangled,
As one stooped by wine or sleep.
O my dear father, if you will listen to me,
I will tell you, I said, what appeared to me
When my legs were thus taken from me.
And he said: If you had a hundred masks
Over your face, they would not be concealed from me,
Your thoughts, however slight they were.
What you saw was meant to stop you refusing
To open your heart to the waters of peace
Poured forth from the eternal source.
I did not ask, What is wrong? for the reason that prompts
One who looks only with an eye that cannot see
When an unconscious body lies there;
But I asked in order to give strength to your feet.
Thus the lazy and slow must be urged
To use their wakefulness when it returns.
We walked through the evening, looking
Ahead, as far as our eyes could stretch,
Against the bright rays of the setting sun.
And behold, little by little a smoke formed
Toward us, dark as night,
And there was no place to avoid it;
It took away our sight and the pure air.
