Now eager to search within and all around
the divine forest, dense and green,
which tempered the new day's light for the eyes.
I left the bank without further delay
and slowly, slowly entered the open ground,
over soil that smelled on every side.
A gentle breeze, without any change
having within itself, struck me on the brow,
with no greater force than a soft wind;
At its breath the leaves, trembling readily,
all bent toward the side
where the holy mountain first casts its shadow.
Yet they did not stray from their upright stance
so far that little birds among the treetops
ceased to practice all their art.
But with full joy the early hours
they welcomed, singing among the leaves,
which kept time with their rhymes.
Such was it as gathers from branch to branch
through the pinewood on Chiassi's shore,
when Aeolus lets Eurus out.
By now my slow steps had carried me
so far into the ancient wood that I
could not see where I had entered.
And behold, a stream stopped me from going farther;
which toward the left with its little waves
bent the grass that grew along its bank.
All the clearest waters in the world
would seem to hold some mixture within themselves
beside that water which conceals nothing,
though it moved darkly, darkly
beneath perpetual shade, which never
lets the sun or moon send rays there.
With my feet I stood still and with my eyes I passed
across the little stream, to wonder at
the great variety of fresh trees.
And there appeared to me, as sometimes appears
something that suddenly drives away
all other thought through wonder,
a solitary lady walking
and singing, choosing flower by flower,
her whole path painted with them.
Ah, lovely lady, who in love's rays
warm yourself, if I may trust the signs
that usually bear witness to the heart,
may you feel the wish to come forward,
I said to her, toward this bank,
close enough for me to hear your song.
You make me remember where and what she was,
Proserpina, at the time when she was lost to
her mother, and the season of spring to her.
As she turns, with feet held close
to the ground and to each other, a lady dancing,
and scarcely places one foot before another,
so she turned among the red and tawny
little flowers toward me, no differently
than a modest maiden lowers her eyes.
And she granted my request,
coming so near that her sweet voice
reached me with its meaning.
As soon as she reached the place where the grass was
already bathed by the lovely river's waves,
she gave me the gift of raising her eyes.
I think such light never shone
beneath the brows of Venus, pierced
by her son, beyond all her customary ways.
She smiled, standing straight on the farther bank,
choosing many colors with her hands
which the high earth yields without seed.
The river kept us three paces apart;
not even the Hellespont, where Xerxes crossed,
still a check on all human pride,
brought Leander greater hatred
for its surging between Sestos and Abydos
than this stream brought me, because it was then closed.
You are new, and perhaps because I smile,
she began, in this place chosen
for human nature as its nest,
some doubt holds you in wonder;
but the psalm You have delighted gives light
and can clear your understanding's mist.
And you, who are in front and asked me,
say if you wish to hear anything else, for I came ready
for all your questions, as far as need be.
The water, I said, and the forest's sound
challenge in me a new belief
about what I heard that contradicts this.
Then she said: I will explain how it comes about
through its cause, this thing that makes you wonder,
and clear the cloud that troubles you.
The highest Good, who alone delights in himself,
made man good, and this good place
gave him as a pledge also of eternal peace.
Because of his fault he stayed here briefly;
because of his fault, to weeping and distress
he changed fair laughter and sweet play.
So that the turmoil below, which of themselves make
exhalations of water and earth,
which follow heat as far as they can,
would not harm man,
this mountain rose so high toward heaven
and freed you from it where it is closed.
But because the whole turns in a circle,
the air, with the first sphere,
unless its circle were broken by some corner;
at this height, which is wholly free
in the clear air, that motion strikes
and makes the dense forest sound.
And the struck plant has such power
that it fills the air with its virtue,
and then shakes it out as it revolves in a circle.
And the other earth, according as it is fit
by nature and by its heaven, conceives and brings forth
different trees of differing virtues.
It would not then seem a wonder in the world,
knowing this, when a plant
takes root there without visible seed.
And you must know that the holy plain
where you are is full of every seed,
and bears within itself a fruit never lost below.
The water you see does not rise from a spring
renewed by vapor transformed by frost,
like a river that ebbs and loses flow;
but it comes from a firm, certain source,
which draws from God's will as much
as it pours from its two open sides.
From this side it flows with power
that takes from others the memory of sin;
from the other it restores each good deed to memory.
Here Lethe, likewise on the other side
Eunoe it is called; and it does not work
unless both waters have first been tasted.
It surpasses every other taste.
And let us suppose it could be sufficiently satisfied,
your thirst, though I reveal no more,
I will give you one further corollary as a grace;
Nor do I think my words will be less dear to you
if I extend them to you beyond my promise.
Those who in ancient times sang
of the Golden Age and its happy state
perhaps dreamed of this place on Parnassus.
Here the human root was innocent;
here spring and every fruit endure forever;
this is the nectar of which all speak.
Then I turned wholly back
toward my poets, and saw that with a smile
they had heard the final words;
afterward I turned my face toward the lovely lady.
