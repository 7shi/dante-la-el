In step, as oxen under a yoke,
I went with that burdened soul,
As long as my sweet guide allowed.
But when he said, Leave him, and pass on,
For here each should drive his boat with sail and oars,
With all the strength he has, ahead:
I straightened, as one should walk,
My body upright, though my thoughts
Remained bowed and shrunken.
I had set out, gladly following
The steps of my master, and both of us
Already seemed so light;
When he said to me: Turn your eyes below;
It will help make the way more peaceful
To see the ground beneath your feet.
As, so their memory may remain,
Earthly tombs above the buried
Bear marks of what they once were;
And there one often weeps for them,
Stung by remembrance,
Which spurs only the compassionate to pity;
So I saw there, but more lifelike,
By art, portrayed all along
The path that winds outside the mountain.
I saw him nobly created,
More than any other creature, fall from heaven,
Flashing like lightning, on one side.
I saw Briareus, pierced by the dart
From heaven, lying on the other side,
Heavy on earth in deathly chill.
I saw Thymbræus, Pallas, and Mars,
Still armed, before their father,
Gazing at the Giants' scattered limbs.
I saw Nimrod at the foot of the great work,
As if bewildered, looking at the people
Who shared his pride in Shinar.
Niobe, with what grief-filled eyes
I saw you carved upon the road,
Among your seven sons and seven daughters, dead!
Saul, how there you seemed to lie dead
Upon your own sword at Gilboa,
Which afterward felt neither rain nor dew!
O foolish Arachne, so I saw you,
Already half a spider, grieving over
The torn work that brought you harm.
Rehoboam, your image no longer threatens;
A chariot bears it off in terror,
Before another need drive it away.
The hard pavement also showed
How Alcmæon made the unhappy ornament
Seem precious to his mother.
It showed how the sons rushed
Upon Sennacherib in the temple,
And left him dead there.
It showed the ruin and slaughter
Tomyris wrought when she said to Cyrus:
You thirsted for blood; I fill you with blood.
It showed how the Assyrians fled in rout
After Holofernes was killed,
And the remains of the slaughter.
I saw Troy in ash and ruin:
O Ilion, how low and vile
The carving showed you there!
What master's brush or sculptor's tool
Could have rendered the shadows and actions there?
Every subtle mind would marvel.
The dead looked dead, the living alive:
No one who saw the truth saw better
Than I, as far as I walked, bent low.
Be proud, then, and go with lofty faces,
Children of Eve, and do not bow your heads,
So you may see your evil path.
We had already gone farther around the mountain,
And the sun had traveled much farther
Than my unfreed mind supposed;
When he who always walked ahead, alert,
Began: Lift up your head;
It is no time to walk so absorbed.
Look there: an angel is ready
To come toward us; see, the sixth handmaid
Of the day returns from her service.
Adorn your face and bearing with reverence,
So he may gladly send us upward;
Remember, this day never returns.
I was well accustomed to his warning,
Especially not to waste time, so on this matter
He could speak plainly to me.
The beautiful creature came toward us,
Dressed in white, and in his face
Like the trembling morning star.
He opened his arms, then spread his wings;
He said: Come; the steps are near,
And now the climb is easy.
Few answer this invitation:
O human race, born to fly upward,
Why do you fall at so slight a breeze?
He led us where the rock was cleft;
There he brushed my forehead with his wings,
Then promised safe passage.
As on the right, climbing the hill
Where Saint Miniato's church overlooks
Well-governed Florence above Rubaconte,
The bold climb is broken
By stairs built in the age
Of honest ledgers and measures;
So the slope descending here
To the next circle grows gentler;
Yet high rock presses on either side.
Then we turned our steps,
And voices sang Blessed are the poor in spirit,
So well no speech could tell it.
Ah, how unlike those voices
Were the voices of Hell, where we entered to songs,
And below, to fierce lamentation!
We were climbing the holy steps,
And I felt much lighter
Than I had felt on the level path;
So I said: Master, tell me, what heavy thing
Has been lifted from me, that I feel
Almost no effort as I walk?
He answered: When the other P's
That remain, almost erased, upon your face
Are gone, as one already has been,
Your feet will be bound to good will
So that they will not merely feel no effort,
But take delight in climbing up.
Then I acted like those who walk
With something on their head, unknown to them,
Though others' signs make them suspect it;
So their hands reach up to check,
And search and find, performing the task
That sight alone cannot complete;
With the simple fingers of my right hand
I found only six letters, which the keeper of the keys
Had carved on my temples; my guide smiled.
And my guide smiled at what he saw.
