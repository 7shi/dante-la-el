He had brought his reasoning to an end,
the lofty teacher watched attentively
to see whether my face seemed content:
And I, whom a new thirst still gnawed,
was silent outwardly, while inwardly I said: Perhaps
my asking too much wearies him.
But that truthful father, who perceived
the timid wish that did not reveal itself,
by speaking gave me courage to speak.
Whereupon I said: Master, my sight is quickened
so greatly by your light that I clearly discern
whatever your reasoning bears or describes:
Therefore I beg you, dear beloved father,
to show me love, to which you trace
every good deed and its contrary.
Turn toward me, he said, the keen eyes
of your intellect, and the error of the blind
who make themselves guides will be plain to you.
The soul, created swift to love,
is moved toward every thing that pleases,
as soon as pleasure awakens it to action.
Your apprehensive faculty, from what is real,
draws an image and unfolds it within you,
so that it makes the soul turn toward it.
And if, once turned, it inclines toward it,
that inclination is love; it is a nature
that binds itself anew within you through pleasure.
Then, as fire moves upward,
because of its form, which is born to rise
to where its matter lasts longest,
so the captured soul enters desire,
which is a spiritual movement and never rests
until the loved thing makes it rejoice.
Now you can see how hidden the truth is
to those who claim that every love in itself
is a thing deserving praise:
for perhaps its matter always seems good;
but not every imprint is good,
even though the wax is good.
Your words and my following intellect,
I answered him, have revealed love to me;
and this has made me more full of doubt:
For if love is offered to us from outside,
and the soul walks with no other foot,
if it goes straight or crooked, that is not its merit.
He replied: What reason sees here,
I can tell you; beyond that, wait
only for Beatrice, whose work is faith.
Every substantial form that is separated
from matter and united with it
has a specific power gathered within it,
which is not perceived without acting,
nor shown except through its effect,
as green leaves show a living plant.
But where the intellect's first perceptions
come from, no one knows;
and the first affection is desirable,
which is in you like the bees' urge
to make honey; this first desire
admits no merit of praise or blame.
Now every other desire gathers upon this;
there is an inborn power that advises,
and gives assent to keeping it at the threshold.
This is the beginning from which
the ground for merit is taken in you,
according as it gathers and distinguishes good and bad loves.
Those who reasoned their way to the depths
recognized this inborn freedom;
therefore they left moral philosophy to the world.
So let us suppose that every love
that kindles within you arises necessarily;
the power to retain it is yours.
Beatrice understands this noble power
as free will; so take care
to remember it if she speaks to you.
The moon, already late near midnight,
made the stars seem sparser to us,
and was like a great burning brand;
it ran against the sky along those paths
the sun sets aflame when, from Rome,
one sees it sink between Sardinia and Corsica;
and that gentle shade, through whom
Pietola is more renowned than Mantua's town,
had lifted the load from my shoulders.
Since I had gathered the clear and plain
reasoning he had given in answer to my questions,
I stood like a man walking in a daze of sleep.
But this drowsiness was suddenly taken from me
by a people who, behind our backs,
were already turned toward us.
Just as Ismenus and Asopus once saw
near them at night a frenzy and a throng,
when the Thebans needed Bacchus,
so these people strode through that circle,
as I saw them coming, driven by good will
and righteous love.
They quickly came upon us, for, running,
that whole great crowd was moving,
and two ahead cried out in tears:
Mary ran in haste to the hill country;
and Cæsar, to subdue Ilerda,
attacked Marseilles, then ran on to Spain.
Quick, quick, so that time may not be lost
through scant love, the others cried nearby,
for zeal in doing good makes grace grow green.
O people, in whom sharp fervor now
perhaps makes up for the neglect and delay
you left behind through lukewarmness in doing good,
this man who lives—and I tell you no lie—
wishes to go up as long as the sun shines for us;
so tell us where the passage is nearby.
These were my leader's words;
and one of those spirits said: Come
behind us, and you will find the opening.
We are so full of the will to move
that we cannot stay; so forgive us
if our justice seems discourteous.
I was abbot at San Zeno in Verona,
under the rule of good Barbarossa,
of whom Milan still speaks in sorrow.
And one man already has a foot in the grave,
who will soon make that monastery weep,
and regret having had the power,
because he has put his son, unsound in body,
and worse in mind, and ill-born,
in the place of its true shepherd.
I do not know whether he said more or fell silent,
for he had already sped far beyond us;
but this I understood, and was glad to retain.
And he who helped me in every need
said: Turn this way; see two coming,
biting sloth with their words.
Behind all the others they cried: The people
to whom the sea opened had died first,
before their heirs saw the Jordan;
and that people who did not endure hardship
to the end with Anchises' son
deprived themselves of a life without glory.
When those shades had gone so far from us
that they could no longer be seen,
a new thought came into my mind,
from which many others arose, all different;
and as I wandered from one to another,
I closed my eyes in longing,
and my thought turned into a dream.
