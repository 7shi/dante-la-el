The hour was such that climbing allowed no hindrance,
For the sun had left the meridian circle
To Taurus, and the night to Scorpio.
For, as a man does who does not stop,
But goes along his way, whatever may appear to him,
If need's spur pricks him through;
So we entered through the narrow passage,
One before another, taking the stairway
That, because of its steepness, separates climbers.
And as a little stork lifts its wing
With the desire to fly, but does not dare
To leave the nest, and lowers it again;
So was I, with a desire kindled and quenched
To ask, reaching the very act
Of one who is preparing to speak.
My sweet father did not forgo it,
Though our journey was so swift, but said: Shoot
The bow of speech, which you have drawn to the iron point.
Then confidently I opened my mouth
And began: How can one become thin
Where the need to nourish does not reach?
If you remembered how Meleager
Consumed himself as a brand was consumed,
This would not seem so hard to you, he said.
And if you considered how your motion
Moves your image within the mirror,
What seems hard would seem soft to you.
But so that your mind may settle within your desire,
Here is Statius; I call him and ask
That he now heal your wounds.
If I unfold to him the eternal vision,
Statius replied, where you are,
May it excuse me if I cannot refuse you.
Then he began: If my words,
Son, your mind watches and receives,
They will shed light on what you ask.
Perfect blood, which is never drunk
By thirsty veins, and so remains
Like food left over from the table,
Takes in the heart the formative power
For all the human limbs, like that
Which can be made through the empty veins.
Once further digested, it descends where
It is better to be silent than to speak; then it drips
Upon other blood in nature's vessel.
There the two gather together,
One disposed to suffer, the other to act,
In the perfect place where it is pressed out;
When it has arrived, it begins to work,
First coagulating, then giving life
To what it made firm through its matter.
Once soul is made, the active power
Is like that of a plant, but differs
In that this one is on the way, the other at shore;
It then works so much that it moves and senses,
Like a sea fungus; and from there it undertakes
To organize the powers for which it is the seed.
Now the power from the begetter's heart
Is explained and extended, my son,
Where nature attends to every limb;
But how an animal becomes an infant,
You do not yet see. This is a point
That has made one wiser than you err;
So that, by his teaching, he separated
The possible intellect from the soul,
Because he saw no organ taken up by it.
Open your heart to the truth,
And know that as soon as the fetus's
Brain is fully formed,
The First Mover turns to it gladly,
Upon so great a work of nature, and breathes
A new spirit, filled with power,
Which draws into its substance
Whatever active power it finds there, and becomes one soul,
Which lives and senses and turns back upon itself.
And so that my words may seem less marvelous,
Look at the sun's heat becoming wine
When joined to the moisture flowing from the vine.
When Lachesis no longer has any thread
To spin, the soul parts from the flesh, and in its power
It carries what is human and divine.
All the other powers are then mute;
Memory, understanding, and will
Are much sharper in their activity than before.
Without pausing, it falls by itself
Miraculously to one of the banks;
There it first learns its paths.
As soon as the place surrounds it,
The formative power radiates around it,
Just as it did in the living limbs;
And as the air, when well saturated,
Through another's ray reflected within it,
Becomes adorned with many colors,
So the neighboring air takes on
The form that the soul, which remains there,
Imprints upon it virtually;
And just as a little flame
Follows the fire wherever it moves,
So the new form follows the spirit.
From this it later takes on its appearance
And is called a shade; and there it organizes
Every sense, as far as sight.
From it we speak, from it we laugh;
From it we shed tears and sighs,
As you have perceived on the mountain.
As desires and other feelings
Move us, the shade takes on different forms;
This is the cause of your wonder.
And already we had reached the final torment
And turned to the right,
While we were intent on another concern.
There the bank shoots flames outward,
And the ledge sends a breeze upward
That bends them back and keeps the path clear;
So we had to go along the open side,
One behind another; I feared the fire,
And on either side I feared falling below.
My Guide said: Along this path
The reins must be held tightly on the eyes,
For even a little could lead one astray.
Most merciful God, within
That great blaze I then heard them singing,
Which made me no less eager to turn;
And I saw spirits walking through the flame;
Since I watched them and my own steps,
I divided my gaze from time to time.
Near the end of that hymn,
They cried aloud: I know not a man;
Then they began the hymn again in low voices.
When it ended, they also cried: In the woods
Diana stayed, and drove Helice out,
For she had felt the poison of Venus.
Then they returned to singing; then women
And husbands who had been chaste cried out,
As virtue and marriage command.
I believe this way is enough for them
For all the time the fire burns them.
With such care and such steps
The final wound must be sewn closed.
