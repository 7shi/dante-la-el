My eyes were so fixed and intent
On sating their ten-year thirst,
That all my other senses were extinguished:
And on either side they had a wall
Of not caring; so the holy smile
Drew them to itself with its ancient net;
When by force my gaze was turned
Toward my left by those goddesses,
Because I heard one of them say: Too fixed.
The condition of sight
In eyes just now struck by the sun
Made me remain without sight for a while;
But after my vision somewhat recovered
(I say a little in relation to the great
Sensible object from which I forcibly withdrew),
I saw, turned on its right arm,
The glorious host, and saw it return,
With the sun and seven flames toward its face.
As beneath its shields, to save itself,
An army turns, and wheels about with its standard,
Before the whole of it can change direction,
That militia of the heavenly kingdom,
Which led the way, all passed by
Before the chariot bent its first pole.
Then the ladies returned to the wheels,
And the griffin moved the blessed burden,
So that afterward not a feather of it stirred.
The beautiful lady who led me to the ford,
And Statius and I followed the wheel
That made its track in a smaller arc.
Thus, as we walked through the high, empty wood,
Through the fault of her who believed the serpent,
An angelic song measured our steps.
Perhaps in three flights as much space was covered
By an unbridled arrow as we had
Moved when Beatrice descended.
I heard everyone murmur, Adam!
Then they circled a tree stripped
Of leaves and other foliage on every branch.
Its crown, which spreads so much
More the higher it rises, would by the Indians be
In their forests admired for its height.
Blessed are you, griffin, who do not tear
With your beak from this tree, sweet to the taste,
Since the belly is badly twisted by it.
Thus around the sturdy tree
The others cried out. And the two-natured creature:
So the seed of every just person is preserved.
And turning to the pole he had drawn,
He pulled it to the foot of the widowed branch,
And left it tied there by one of its branches.
As our plants, when there falls
Below the great light mingled with that one
Which shines behind the heavenly Fish,
Grow swollen, and then each is renewed
In its own color, before the sun
Yokes its horses beneath another star;
Less than roses' and more than violets'
Was the hue it took on; thus renewed itself the tree
Whose branches had previously been so bare.
I did not understand it; therefore not sung here is
The hymn that company sang then,
Nor could I grasp the whole melody.
If I could recall how there fell asleep
The pitiless eyes, hearing of Syrinx,
Those eyes for whom not keeping watch cost so dear;
Like a painter who paints from a model,
I would draw how I fell asleep;
But let whoever wishes portray sleep well.
So I pass on to when I awoke,
And say that a light tore through my veil
Of sleep, and a call: Rise, what are you doing?
As, to look upon the little blossoms of the apple tree,
Whose fruit makes angels eager,
And holds eternal weddings in heaven,
Peter, John, and James, led there,
And overcome, returned to the word
That broke greater sleeps,
And saw their company diminished,
Both Moses and Elias gone,
And their master's garment changed;
So I returned and saw that gentle lady
Standing above me, who the guide
Had been of my steps earlier along the river;
And entirely uncertain, I said: Where is Beatrice?
And she: See her beneath the foliage
New, sitting at its root.
See the company surrounding her;
The others have gone up after the griffin,
With a sweeter, deeper song.
And whether her speech went on at greater length
I do not know, for before my eyes appeared she
Who had closed me to other thoughts.
She sat alone on the true earth,
Like a guardian left there for the wagon
I had seen tied to the two-natured beast.
Around her they formed of themselves a circle,
The seven nymphs, with those lights in their hands
That are safe from north wind and south.
Here you will be a woodland dweller for a little while,
And without end you will be with me, a citizen
Of that Rome where Christ is Roman.
As a reproach to the world that lives ill,
Keep your eyes on the chariot now; and what you see,
When you return there, make sure you write down.
So spoke Beatrice; and I, who wholly at the feet
Of her commands was devoted,
Gave my mind and eyes wherever she wished.
Never did there descend with such swift motion
Fire from a dense cloud, when it rains
From that farthest boundary,
As I saw Jove's bird come down
Down along the tree, breaking its bark,
Let alone its flowers and new leaves;
And it struck the chariot with all its force,
So that it bent like a ship in a storm,
Now beaten by a wave from prow, now stern.
Then I saw spring into the cradle
Of the triumphal vehicle a fox,
Seeming starved of every good food.
But, rebuking her for her shameful faults,
My lady drove her away so far
That her bones could bear it without flesh.
Then, by the way it had come before,
I saw the Eagle descend into the chest
Of the chariot, leaving it feathered with itself.
And as from a grieving heart there comes,
Such a voice came from heaven and thus said:
O my little ship, how badly you are laden!
Then it seemed to me that the earth opened
Between the two wheels, and I saw a dragon come out of it,
Driving its tail up through the chariot;
And, like a wasp that draws back its sting,
Pulling its wicked tail toward itself,
It ripped from the bottom and went its way.
What remained, as with weeds
Fertile ground, with feathers offered
Perhaps with sound and kindly intent,
Covered itself, and covered were
Both wheels and the pole in so short a time
That a breath keeps the mouth open longer.
Thus transformed, the holy edifice
Put out heads from its parts:
Three above the pole, and one at each corner.
The first were horned like an ox,
But the four had a single horn on their brow:
No such monster had ever been seen.
Secure as a fortress on a high mountain,
A wanton harlot sitting on it
Appeared to me, with bold eyes all around.
And, so that she would not be taken from him,
I saw a giant sitting beside her,
And they kissed each other now and then;
But because her eager, wandering eye
She turned toward me, that fierce lover
Lashed her from head to foot.
Then, full of suspicion and savage anger,
He unyoked the monster and dragged it through the wood,
So far that its ground made a shield for me
Against the harlot and the new beast.
