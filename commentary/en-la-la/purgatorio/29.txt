Singing like a lady captured by love,
she continued with the end of her words:
Blessed are those whose sins are covered.
And like nymphs who went alone
through woodland shade, desiring
some to see, some to flee, the sun,
then she moved along against the stream,
up the bank, and I beside her,
following with small steps, step for step.
Not yet a hundred paces lay between
her steps and mine, when the banks
turned alike, so that I faced east.
Nor had our way yet gone very far,
when the lady turned fully toward me,
saying: My brother, look and listen.
And suddenly a brightness swept
from every side through the great forest,
so that I doubted it was lightning.
But lightning fades as soon as it comes,
while this, growing brighter and brighter,
made me think: What can this be?
And a sweet melody ran
through the luminous air; so good zeal
made me condemn Eve's boldness,
For she, though earth and heaven obeyed,
a woman just newly created,
could not bear to remain beneath a veil;
beneath which, had she been devout,
I would have known those ineffable delights
sooner, and for a longer time.
As I went among so many first fruits
of eternal joy, all rapt,
and still desiring greater delights,
before us the air, like a kindled fire,
glowed beneath the green branches,
and the sweet song could already be heard.
O holy virgins, if hunger,
thirst, cold, or vigils I ever endured for you,
may this move me to ask your reward.
Now Helicon must pour forth for me,
and Urania with her choir aid me
to think great things and set them in verse.
A little farther on, seven golden trees
seemed to falsify the long distance
of air still lying between us and them;
but when I came so close to them
that the common object which deceives the senses
no longer lost any power through distance,
the faculty that prepares reason's judgment
recognized them as candlesticks,
and heard the singers' voices cry Hosanna.
Above, the beautiful arrangement blazed
far brighter than the moon on a clear night,
at midnight in the middle of its month.
I turned, full of wonder,
toward good Virgil, and he answered me
with a look no less laden with amazement.
Then I turned my gaze to the other things
moving toward us so slowly
that new brides would have outpaced them.
The lady rebuked me: Why do you burn
so with your gaze upon the living lights,
and fail to look at what follows them?
Then I saw people approaching behind
their leaders, dressed in white;
no such whiteness was ever seen here.
The water lay along my left side
and gave back my left flank,
if I looked in it as in a mirror.
When I had reached a place on my bank
where only the river separated me from them,
I stopped to see better;
and I saw the little flames move ahead,
leaving the air painted behind them,
like strokes made by a brush;
above, the air was marked off
in seven stripes, all in the colors
of the rainbow and Diana's halo.
Those banners behind were larger
than my sight could encompass; as I judged,
the outer ones stood ten paces apart.
Beneath so lovely a sky, as I describe,
twenty-four lords came, two by two,
crowned with lilies.
They all sang: Blessed are you
among the daughters of Adam, and blessed
forever be your beauties.
When the flowers and fresh grasses
across from me on the farther bank
were freed of those chosen people,
like one light following another in heaven,
four creatures came close to them,
each crowned with green leaves.
Each had six feathered wings,
the feathers full of eyes; Argus's eyes,
if living, would be like these.
I will not spend more words, reader,
describing their form, for another task
presses me so that I cannot be lavish here.
But read Ezekiel, who depicts them
as he saw them coming from the north
with wind, cloud, and fire;
And as you find them in his pages,
so they were here, except that in the wings
John agrees with me and differs from him.
The space within the four contained
a triumphal chariot on two wheels,
drawn by the neck of a griffin.
It stretched both wings upward
between the middle stripe and the three on either side,
without harming anything as it split them.
They rose so high they could not be seen;
the griffin's birdlike limbs were gold,
the others white mixed with red.
Not only would Rome rejoice Africanus
or Augustus with a chariot so fine,
even the sun's would seem poor beside it;
the sun's chariot, which strayed and burned
because of Earth's devout prayer,
when Jove was secretly just.
Three ladies came dancing in a ring
at the right wheel: one so red
she could scarcely be seen within the fire;
another seemed to have flesh and bones
made of emerald; the third appeared
like newly fallen snow.
Now they seemed led by the white one,
now by the red; and by her song
the others took their pace, slow or swift.
On the left, four were making merry,
clad in purple, following the way
of one with three eyes in her head.
Beside the whole procession,
I saw two old men unlike in dress,
but equal in their worthy, firm conduct.
One looked like a member of the household
of that great Hippocrates, who made nature
for the creatures she holds most dear.
The other showed the opposite concern
with a bright, sharp sword,
so that on this side of the stream I feared.
Then I saw four in humble appearance,
and behind all the others one old man alone
coming asleep, with a keen face.
These seven, with the first company,
were dressed alike, but made no garland
of lilies around their heads;
instead, of roses and other red flowers.
From far away I would have sworn
they all burned above their brows.
When the chariot stood opposite me,
a thunderclap was heard; those worthy people
seemed forbidden to go farther,
and stopped there beside the first standards.
