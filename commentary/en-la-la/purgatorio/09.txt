Tithonus's ancient concubine
was already whitening on the eastern horizon,
outside the arms of her sweet beloved:
Her brow shone with gems
arranged in the shape of the cold creature
that strikes people with its tail:
And night, by the steps with which it ascends,
had made two in the place where we were,
and the third was already lowering its wings:
When I, who carried with me something of Adam,
overcome by sleep, bent down upon the grass
there where all five of us had been sitting;
at the hour when begins its mournful cries
the swallow, near morning,
perhaps remembering its first sorrows;
and our mind, wandering
more from the flesh, and less occupied by thought,
is almost prophetic in its visions;
In my dream I seemed to see suspended
an eagle in the sky with golden feathers,
its wings spread, intent on swooping down.
And I seemed to be there where there were
left behind his companions by Ganymede
when he was carried off to the highest council.
I thought to myself: Perhaps she strikes
only here out of habit, and perhaps from elsewhere
she disdains to carry up in her talons.
Then it seemed to me that, circling a little more,
terrible as lightning, she descended
and seized me, carrying me up to the fire.
There it seemed to me that she and I were burning,
and the imagined blaze burned so fiercely
that sleep had to break apart.
No differently did Achilles start awake,
turning his awakened eyes around
and not knowing where he was,
when his mother from Chiron to Scyros
carried him off, sleeping in her arms,
from where the Greeks later took him away;
than I started awake as soon as from that face
of mine sleep fled, and I turned pale,
as a man does when frightened on a hunt.
I had no one beside me but my Comforter,
and the sun had already been high for more than two hours,
while my gaze was turned toward the sea.
Have no fear, my lord said;
be reassured; we are at a good point:
do not constrain, but release, all your strength.
You have now reached Purgatory.
Look there at the bank that encloses it all around;
look at the entrance, where it seems to be divided.
A little while ago, at the dawn that precedes the day,
when your soul was sleeping within,
upon the flowers that adorn the place below,
a lady came and said: I am Lucy.
Let me take this sleeping man;
Thus I will make his way easier for him.
Sordello and the other people remained, except me.
She took you; and as soon as daylight grew clear,
she came up, and I followed in her tracks.
She set you here; and first there showed me
her lovely eyes that entrance standing open.
Afterward she and sleep departed together.
Like a man whose doubts have been settled,
and who turns his fear into reassurance
once the truth has been disclosed to him,
I changed; and when, free of care,
my guide saw me, up the slope
he moved, and I followed him toward the heights.
Reader, you see well how I elevate
my subject matter; and therefore, with greater art,
do not be surprised if I strengthen it.
We drew near and were in the place
from which, where I had first seemed to see a break,
and just like a crack that divides a wall,
I saw a gate, with three steps below it
leading to it, each of a different color,
and a gatekeeper who had not yet spoken.
And as I opened my eyes wider and wider toward it,
I saw him sitting on the topmost step,
with such a face that I could not bear it.
And he held a naked sword in his hand,
which reflected the rays toward us so brightly
that I often aimed my gaze in vain.
Say from there, what do you want?
he began to say. Where is the guide?
Take care that coming up does you no harm.
A lady of heaven, informed about these things,
my master replied to him, a little while ago
told us: Go there; the gate is there.
And may she advance your steps for good,
the courteous gatekeeper began again.
Come forward now to our steps.
We came there, and the first step
was white marble, so polished and smooth
that I saw my reflection in it, just as I am.
The second was darker than deep blue,
made of rough, scorched-looking stone,
cracked lengthwise and across.
The third step, set above the others,
seemed to me to be porphyry so aflame,
like blood spurting from a vein.
Upon it rested both feet
the angel of God, sitting on the threshold,
which seemed to me a stone of diamond.
Up the three steps willingly
my guide drew me, saying: Ask
humbly that he unlock the fastening.
Devoutly I threw myself at his holy feet.
I asked for mercy and for him to open to me,
but first I struck my breast three times.
He wrote seven P's on my forehead
with the point of his sword, and: See that you wash,
when you are inside, these wounds, he said.
Ash or earth, the kind dug up dry,
would be of the same color as his garment;
and from beneath it he drew two keys.
One was gold and the other silver.
First with the white one, and then with the yellow,
he opened the gate to my satisfaction.
If either of these keys fails,
so that it does not turn straight in the lock,
he told us, this passage will not open.
One is more precious, but the other requires great
skill and ingenuity before it can unlock,
for it is the one that loosens the knot.
I hold them from Peter; and he told me to err
rather in opening than in keeping the gate shut,
provided people bow at my feet.
Then he pushed the door of the sealed gate,
saying: Enter; but I warn you
that whoever looks back returns outside.
And when there were turned in their hinges
the pivots of that sacred gate,
which are of metal, strong and loud,
Not so did she bellow or show herself so fierce,
Tarpeia, as soon as there was taken from her good
Metellus, after which she was left impoverished.
I turned, attentive to the first sound,
and Te Deum laudamus I seemed
to hear in a voice mingled with sweet music.
Exactly such an image did it give me,
all that I heard, as one is wont to perceive
when one pauses to sing with instruments:
of which the words are sometimes heard, sometimes not.
