God, the nations have come, alternately,
Now three, now four, the sweet psalmody,
The ladies began, and weeping:
And Beatrice, sighing and compassionate,
Listened to them, so changed that a little more
Mary changed at the Cross.
But when the other virgins had given her room
To speak, rising straight to her feet,
She answered, colored like fire:
A little while, and you will not see me;
And again, my beloved sisters,
A little while, and you will see me.
Then she placed all seven before her,
And after her, signaling alone, she moved
Me and the lady, and the sage who had remained.
So she went on, and I believe her tenth step
Had scarcely been set upon the ground
When she struck my eyes with her eyes;
And in answer to that look: Come more quickly,
She said to me, so that if I speak with you,
You may be ready to listen well.
As I was, as I ought to be, beside her,
She said to me: Brother, why do you not dare
To ask me questions, now that you come with me?
As happens to those who are too reverent
When speaking before their superior,
So that they cannot bring a clear voice to their teeth,
It happened to me: without a full sound
I began: My Lady, my need
You know, and what is good for me.
And she to me: I want you now to free yourself
From fear and shame,
So that you no longer speak like a man who dreams.
Know that the vessel the serpent broke
Was, and is no more; but let whoever is guilty
Believe that God's vengeance does not fear soup.
The eagle that left its feathers on the chariot
Will not always be without an heir,
Since it became a monster and then prey;
For I see clearly, and therefore tell it,
That a time is coming, with stars already near,
Safe from every obstacle and every barrier;
In which a five hundred, ten, and five,
God's messenger, will slay the harlot,
Together with the giant who sins with her.
Perhaps my dark account,
Like those of Themis and the Sphinx, persuades you less,
For it clouds the intellect in their manner;
But soon the Fates and Naiads will come,
Who will solve this difficult riddle,
Without harm to sheep or grain.
Take note; and just as they are given by me,
So teach these words to the living: life
Is a single running toward death;
And remember, when you write them down,
Not to conceal what sort of plant you saw,
Now twice robbed there.
Whoever robs it or breaks it
Offends God in deed and blasphemy,
For he created it holy for his use alone.
For biting it, in pain and longing,
For five thousand years and more, the first soul
Longed for Him who bore the punishment for the bite.
Your intellect sleeps, if it does not judge
That for a special reason it is so lofty,
And so inverted at the top.
And if the waters of Elsa had not been
The vain thoughts around your mind,
And their delight a Pyramus beneath the mulberry,
From so many circumstances alone
You would recognize God's justice in the prohibition,
And the tree in its moral meaning.
But since I see you turned to stone in understanding,
And stained with stone,
So that the light of my words dazzles you,
I want you, if not written, at least pictured,
To carry it within you, for the reason
That a pilgrim carries a staff wreathed with palm.
And I: Like wax beneath a seal,
That does not change the impression made,
My brain is now stamped by you.
But why does your desired word fly
So far beyond my sight,
That the more it strives, the more it is lost?
So that you may know, she said, the school
You have followed, and see its teaching,
How far it can follow my word;
And see your way depart from the divine
As far as earth is distant from the heaven
That hastens highest.
Then I answered her: I do not remember
That I ever turned away from you,
Nor have I a conscience that bites me.
And if you cannot remember, she answered, smiling,
Now recall how you drank
From Lethe today;
For as fire is inferred from smoke,
So this forgetfulness clearly proves
A fault in your will, intent elsewhere.
From now on my words will be plain,
As far as it is fitting to uncover them
To your untrained sight.
And brighter, and with slower steps,
The sun held the circle of noon,
Which shifts here and there, as the horizon does,
When they halted, as one halts
Who goes before a company as its guide,
If he finds something new in his tracks.
The seven ladies at the edge of a pale shadow,
Such as the Alps cast above their cold streams
Beneath green leaves and dark branches.
Before them I seemed to see the Euphrates and Tigris
Flow out from a single spring,
Then part like two friends, reluctant to leave.
O light, O glory of the human race,
What water is this that spreads here
From one source, then moves away from itself?
In answer to this prayer I was told: Ask
Matilda to tell you. And here the fair lady answered,
As one who frees herself from blame:
This and other things were told to him by me,
And I am sure the river Lethe
Did not hide them from him.
And Beatrice: Perhaps a greater care,
Which often robs the memory,
Has darkened his mind before his eyes.
But see where Eunoe flows from there:
Lead him to it, and, as you are accustomed,
Revive his half-dead power.
Like a noble soul that makes no excuse,
But makes its own will the will of another,
As soon as it is revealed by signs,
So, after she had taken hold of me,
The fair lady moved and said graciously to Statius:
Come with him.
If I, reader, had more space
To write, I would still sing in part
Of the sweet drinking that would never have sated me;
But since all the pages prepared
For this second Cantica are full,
The rein of this art lets me go no farther.
I returned from the most holy wave
Renewed, as young plants are renewed
With fresh leaves,
Pure and ready to rise to the stars.
