Remember, reader, if ever in the Alps
a mist came over you, through which you saw
no more than through a mole's skin;
as when damp, dense vapors begin
to thin, the sun's sphere
enters faintly through them;
so your imagining will be light
when you come to see, as I saw again
the sun first, already near its setting.
So, matching my steps with the steady
steps of my master, I came out of that cloud,
to the dead rays already on the low shores.
O power of imagination, that sometimes steals us
so far from ourselves that one does not notice,
though a thousand trumpets sound around us,
who moves you, if the senses do not supply you?
Does a light move you, formed in heaven
by itself, or by a will that guides it below?
The trace of the anger of that woman
who changed into the bird that most delights
in singing appeared in my imagination;
and there my mind was so enclosed
within itself that nothing from outside came
that it might still receive.
Then into my lofty fantasy came
a man crucified, contemptible and fierce
in appearance, and he was dying so.
Around him were great Ahasuerus,
his wife Esther, and righteous Mordecai,
so upright in deed and word.
And as this image broke apart
by itself, like a bubble
whose supporting water beneath has gone;
a young woman rose in my vision,
weeping bitterly and saying: O queen,
why did you choose to become nothing in anger?
You killed yourself so as not to lose Lavinia;
now you have lost me. I am she who mourns,
mother, your ruin before another's.
As sleep breaks when suddenly
a new light strikes closed eyes,
and the broken sleep stirs before it wholly dies,
so my vision fell away
as soon as the light struck my face,
far brighter than any we commonly see.
I turned to see where I was,
when a voice said, This way is up,
and drew me from every other thought;
and made my desire so eager
to look at whoever was speaking
that it never rests until it sees him.
But as the sun burdens our sight
and veils its own shape through excess,
so my power failed there.
This is a righteous spirit, who on the
way upward directs us without being asked,
and hides himself with his own light.
He acts toward us as a person should toward himself:
when one sees a need and waits to be asked,
one is already turning cruelly toward refusal.
Let us therefore set our feet to this great invitation.
Let us hasten upward before it grows dark,
for afterward we could not, until daylight returns.
So spoke my guide, and with him I turned
our steps toward a stairway;
and as soon as I reached its first step,
I felt, close by, a movement of wings,
and a breeze upon my face, and heard: Blessed
are the peacemakers, who are free from evil anger.
The last rays had risen so far above us,
the rays followed by night,
that stars appeared on every side.
O my strength, why do you fade so?
I said within myself, for I felt
the power in my legs had come to rest.
We were where the stairway rose
no farther upward, and we sat there,
like a ship that reaches the shore.
I waited a little to see whether I could hear
anything in the new ring;
then I turned to my master and said:
Dear father, tell me, what offense
is purged here in the ring where we are?
If our feet are still, let your words not be.
He answered: Love of the good, deficient
in what it owes, is restored here;
here the badly delayed oar is struck again.
But so that you may understand more clearly,
turn your mind toward me, and you will gain
some good fruit from our stay.
Neither Creator nor creature ever,
he began, my son, was without love,
whether natural or of the mind; you know this.
Natural love is always without error;
the other can err through an evil object,
or through too much or too little strength.
While it is directed to the first good
and measures itself in the second,
it cannot cause evil delight;
but when it twists toward evil, or pursues the good
with greater or lesser care than it should,
the creature works against its Maker.
From this you can understand that love
must be the seed in you of every virtue
and every deed that deserves punishment.
And since love can never turn its face
from the well-being of its subject,
things are safe from self-hatred;
and since no being can be understood
as separate and self-subsisting from the First,
every affection is barred from hating Him.
It follows, if I judge rightly by dividing,
that the evil one loves is in one's neighbor;
and this love arises in three ways in your clay.
One hopes to excel by seeing
one's neighbor brought low, and for this alone
desires him cast down from his greatness;
another fears losing power, favor, honor, and fame
because someone else rises above him,
and grieves, loving the opposite;
another seems indignant at an injury,
and becomes greedy for revenge;
such a person must seek another's harm.
This threefold love below is mourned;
now I want you to understand the other kind,
which runs toward the good in a disordered way.
Everyone indistinctly apprehends a good
in which the soul may find rest, and desires it;
everyone strives to reach it.
If sluggish love draws you to see it
or to attain it, this cornice
torments you after due repentance.
Another good, which does not make a person happy,
is not happiness; it is not the good
essence, root, and fruit of every good.
The love that gives itself too much to it
is mourned above us in three circles.
But how it divides into three, I leave unsaid,
so you may discover it for yourself.
