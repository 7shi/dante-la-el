Remember, reader, if ever in the Alps
a mist came over you, through which you could see
no more than through a mole's skin;
as when damp, dense vapors
begin to thin, the sphere
of the sun enters faintly through them;
so your imagining will be light
when you come to see, as I saw again
the sun first, near its setting.
So, matching my steps with the steady steps
of my master, I came out of that cloud,
to the dead rays on the low shores.
O power of imagination, that steals us
sometimes so far from ourselves that one does not notice,
though a thousand trumpets sound around us,
who moves you, if the senses do not supply you?
Does a light move you, formed in heaven
by itself, or by a will that guides it below?
Of the anger of that woman who changed her form
into the bird that most delights in singing,
in my imagination appeared the trace;
and there my mind was so enclosed
within itself that from outside there came not
a thing that it might still receive.
Then into the lofty fantasy came
a man crucified, contemptible and fierce
in his appearance, and he was dying so.
Around him were great Ahasuerus,
his wife Esther, and righteous Mordecai,
who was so upright in deed and word.
And as soon as this image broke apart
by itself, like a bubble
which loses the water beneath which it was formed;
a young woman rose in my vision,
weeping bitterly and saying: O queen,
why did you choose to become nothing in anger?
You killed yourself so as not to lose Lavinia;
now you have lost me. I am she who mourns,
mother, your ruin before another's.
As sleep breaks when suddenly
a new light strikes closed eyes,
and the broken sleep stirs before it wholly dies,
so my vision fell away
as soon as the light struck my face,
far brighter than any we commonly see.
I turned to see where I was,
when a voice said, This way is up,
and drew me from every other thought;
and made my desire so eager
to look and see who it was that was speaking
that it never rests until it sees him.
But as the sun burdens our sight
and veils its own shape through excess,
so my power failed there.
This is a righteous spirit, who on the way
upward directs us without being asked,
and hides himself with his own light.
He acts toward us as a person does toward himself:
for when one sees a need and waits to be asked,
one is already turning cruelly toward refusal.
Let us therefore set our feet to this great invitation.
Let us hasten upward before it grows dark,
for afterward we could not, until daylight returns.
So spoke my guide, and I with him
turned our steps toward a stairway;
and as soon as I reached its first step,
I felt myself, close by, as if by a movement of wings,
fanned upon the face, and heard it said: Blessed
are the peacemakers, who are free from evil anger.
Already they had risen so far above us,
the last rays followed by night,
that stars appeared on every side.
O my strength, why do you fade so from me?
I said within myself, for in myself I felt
the power of the legs had come to rest.
We were where one climbed no farther
up the stairway, and we sat there,
like a ship that reaches the shore.
And I waited a little to see whether I could hear
anything in the new ring;
then I turned to my master and said:
My dear father, tell me, what offense
is purged here in the ring where we are?
If our feet are still, let your words not be.
And he answered me: Love, honor of the good, deficient
in what it owes, is restored here;
here the badly delayed oar is struck again.
But so that you may understand still more clearly,
turn your mind toward me, and you will gain
some good fruit from our stay.
Neither Creator nor creature ever,
he began, my son, was without love,
whether natural or of the mind; and you know this.
Natural love is always without error;
but the other can err through an evil object,
or through too much or too little strength.
While it is directed to the first good
and measures itself in the second,
it cannot cause evil delight;
but when it twists toward evil, or with greater care
or less than it should pursues the good,
the Maker's own creature works against its Maker.
From this you can understand that there must be
love as the seed in you of every virtue
and every deed that deserves punishment.
And since it is never possible from the well-being
of its subject for love to turn its face,
things are safe from self-hatred;
and since one cannot conceive as separate
and self-subsisting any being apart from the First,
every affection is barred from hating Him.
It follows, if I judge rightly by dividing,
that the evil one loves is in one's neighbor; and this
love arises in three ways in your clay.
And one, to be superior to one's neighbor,
hopes to excel, and for this alone desires
that he be cast down from his greatness;
and another, power, favor, honor, and fame
fears losing because someone else rises above him,
and therefore grieves so much that he loves the opposite;
and another seems indignant at an injury,
so much so that he becomes greedy for revenge;
and such a person must seek another's harm.
This threefold love here below
is mourned; now I want you to understand the other kind,
which runs toward the good in a disordered way.
Everyone indistinctly apprehends a good
in which the soul may find rest, and desires it;
for everyone strives to reach it.
If sluggish love draws you to see it
or to attain it, this cornice
torments you after due repentance.
There is another good which does not make a person happy;
it is not happiness; it is not the good
essence, root, and fruit of every good.
The love that gives itself too much to it
is mourned above us in three circles.
But how its division into three is reasoned out,
I leave unsaid, so you may discover it for yourself.
