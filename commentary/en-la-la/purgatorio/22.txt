Now the angel had remained behind us,
the angel who had turned us toward the sixth ring,
and had already erased one stroke from my face:
And those who hunger for justice
had said Blessed in their voices,
and finished it with I thirst, and nothing more.
And I, lighter than through other passages,
went on so that without any effort
I followed the swift spirits upward:
When Virgil began: Love,
kindled by virtue, always kindles another,
provided its flame appears outwardly.
So, from the hour when among us descended
into Hell's Limbo Juvenal,
and made your affection known to me,
my goodwill toward you was such
as never bound me more closely to anyone unseen,
so now these stairs will seem short to us.
But tell me, and forgive me as a friend,
if too much confidence loosens my reins;
and speak with me now as a friend:
how could there be found within your bosom
a place for avarice, amid all the wisdom
with which your studies had filled you?
These words moved Statius
to smile a little; then he replied:
Every word of yours is to me a dear sign of love.
Indeed, things often appear
which give false grounds for doubt,
because their true causes are hidden.
Your question confirms to me what your belief
is: that I was avaricious in my other life,
perhaps because of the circle where I was:
know now that avarice distant
too far from me was; and this excess
was punished by thousands of lunar months.
And had I not corrected my course
when I understood the place where you cry out,
almost in anger at human nature,
Why do you not govern, O sacred hunger
for gold, the appetites of mortals?
I would feel the grievous jousts as I turned.
Then I saw that to spread their wings too wide
was in my hands' power in spending, and I repented
of this sin as of my other evils.
How many will rise with hair cut short,
through ignorance that, concerning this fault,
keeps them from repenting in life or at the end!
And know that the fault which strikes back
by direct opposition against a sin
committed, dries up here together with its green growth.
So if I stayed among those people
who lament avarice, to purge myself,
it was its opposite that brought me there.
Now, when you sang of savage arms
and Jocasta's double grief,
the singer of pastoral songs said,
from what Clio touches on for you, with you,
it does not seem that what made you a believer was yet
faith, without which doing good is not enough.
If so, what sun or what candles
drew you from darkness, so that you set
afterward, behind the Fisherman, your sails?
And he then: You first directed me
toward Parnassus, to drink in its caves,
and first enlightened me toward God.
You did as one who walks by night,
carrying a light behind him that does not help himself,
but makes those behind him wise,
when you said: The age is renewed;
justice returns, and the first human age,
and a new offspring descends from Heaven.
Through you I was a poet, through you a Christian;
but so that you may see more clearly what I mean,
I shall extend my hand to add the colors.
The whole world was already pregnant
with the true faith, sown
by the messengers of the eternal kingdom;
and your word mentioned above
accorded with the new preachers,
so I made a habit of visiting them.
They then seemed so holy to me
that when Domitian persecuted them,
their grief was not without my tears.
And while I remained in that world,
I helped them, and their upright ways
made me despise every other sect.
And before I led the Greeks to the rivers
of Thebes in verse, I was baptized;
but through fear I remained a hidden Christian.
For a long time showing myself a pagan;
and this lukewarmness the fourth circle
made me seek for more than four hundred.
You, then, who lifted the lid from me
that hid from me how much good I speak of,
while we have more climbing to do,
tell me where our old Terence is,
Caecilius, Plautus, and Varro, if you know;
tell me if they are damned, and in what quarter.
These men, and Persius, and I, and many others,
my guide replied, are with that Greek
whom the Muses nursed more than they ever did any other,
in the first circle of the blind prison.
We often speak of the mountain
that keeps our nurses always with it.
Euripides is there with us, and Antiphon,
Simonides, Agathon, and many more
Greeks who once crowned their brows with laurel.
There are seen, from among your people,
Antigone, Deiphyle, and Argia,
and Ismene, as sorrowful as she was.
There is the woman who showed Langia;
the daughter of Tiresias is there, and Thetis,
and Deidamia with her sisters.
Both poets were silent,
again intent on looking around,
free of climbing and of walls;
and already four handmaids of the day
had fallen behind, while the fifth was at the pole,
still pointing its burning horn upward;
when my guide: I think at the far end
we must turn our right shoulders,
circling the mountain as we usually do.
So our custom served as our guide there,
and we took the road with less doubt,
because that worthy soul agreed.
They went ahead, and I alone behind them,
and I listened to their conversation,
which gave me insight for writing poetry.
But soon what broke their sweet conversation
was a tree we found midway along the road,
with apples fragrant and fine to smell.
And as a fir narrows upward,
from branch to branch, so this one narrowed downward,
I think so no one could climb it.
On the side where our path was blocked,
from the high cliff a clear liquid fell
and spread upward through the leaves.
The two poets approached the tree,
and a voice from within, through the leaves,
cried: You will be deprived of this food.
Then it said: Mary cared more that
the wedding be honorable and complete
than for her own mouth, which now answers for you.
And the Roman women of old, for their drink,
were content with water; and Daniel
scorned food and gained wisdom.
The first age, as good as gold, was lovely;
hunger made acorns taste sweet,
and thirst made every stream nectar.
Honey and locusts were the food
that nourished the Baptist in the desert;
therefore he is glorious and so great,
as the Gospel reveals to us.
