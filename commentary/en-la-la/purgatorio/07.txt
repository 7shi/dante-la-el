After the courteous and joyful greetings
Had been repeated three and four times,
Sordello drew back and said: Who are you?
Before toward this mountain had turned
Souls worthy to climb to God,
My bones were buried by Octavian.
I am Virgil; and for no other fault
Than lacking faith did I lose Heaven.
Thus my guide answered then.
Like one who, with something before him,
Suddenly sees it and is thereby astonished,
Believing and not, saying: It is, and it is not;
So he appeared; then he lowered his brows,
And humbly returned toward him
And embraced him where the lesser embraces.
O glory of the Latins, he said, through whom was shown
All that our language was capable of,
O eternal honor of the place where I was born,
What merit or what grace has shown you to me?
If I am worthy to hear your words,
Tell me if you come from Hell, and from which circle.
Through all the circles of the sorrowful realm,
He answered, I have come to this place;
Heaven's power moved me, and with it I come.
Not for doing, but for not doing, have I lost
The sight of the high Sun you desire,
And whom I came to know too late.
There below is a place not sad with torments,
But only with darkness, where laments
Sound not like cries of woe, but like sighs.
There I dwell with innocent little ones,
Bitten by the teeth of death sooner
Than they were freed from human sin.
There I am with those who in the three holy
Virtues did not clothe themselves, yet without fault
Knew the others and followed them all.
But if you know and can, some sign
Give us, by which we may come more quickly
To where Purgatory truly begins.
A fixed place is not assigned to us, he replied;
I am permitted to go up and around;
As far as I may go, I will stay by you to guide you.
But see how the day is already declining,
And one cannot climb by night;
So it is well to think of a pleasant lodging.
Souls are far off to the right here;
If you agree with me, I will lead you to them,
And not without delight will you come to know them.
As soon as this was answered: If anyone wished
To climb by night, would he be hindered
By someone else, or would it be only because he could not?
And good Sordello drew his finger across the ground,
Saying: See, this very line
You would not cross after the sun had set;
Not, however, that anything else would hinder you
From climbing, only the darkness of night:
This, together with impossibility, entangles the will.
With it he could indeed return downward,
And walk about the mountain, wandering,
While the horizon keeps the day shut in.
Then my guide, as if amazed:
Lead us, then, he said, there where you say
One may enjoy staying for a while.
We had gone a little way from there
When I noticed the mountain was hollow,
Like the valleys that are hollow here.
We shall go there, said that shade,
Where the slope forms a hollow in itself,
And there we shall wait for the new day.
Between steep ground and level ground ran a crooked path,
Which led us along the side of the hollow,
There where its rim extends beyond the middle.
Fine gold and silver, scarlet and white,
Indian wood, bright and clear,
Fresh emerald when it is split,
By the grass and flowers in that hollow,
If set there, each would be surpassed in color,
As the lesser is surpassed by the greater.
Nature had not only painted there,
But from the sweetness of a thousand scents
There made one unknown, indistinguishable blend.
Salve Regina, on the green and on the flowers,
I heard souls sitting there sing,
Who in the valley could not be seen from outside.
Before the little sun now settles into its nest,
The Mantuan who had turned us began to speak,
Do not ask me to guide you among them.
From this ledge better the deeds and faces
Will you know of all of them
Than when gathered below in the hollow among them.
He who sits highest, and seems to show
That he neglected what he ought to do,
And does not move his mouth for the others' song,
Was Emperor Rudolph, who could have
Healed the wounds that have killed Italy,
So that it is revived too late by others.
The other, who comforts him by his looks,
Ruled the land where the water rises
That flows into the Moldau, and the Moldau carries it into the sea:
His name was Ottocar; in the cradle
He was far better than his son Wenceslas,
The bearded one, whom lust and idleness feed.
And that small-nosed man, who, close in counsel,
Appears beside him whose face is so gracious,
Died fleeing, and caused the lily to fade:
Look there, how he strikes his breast.
See the other, who has made against his cheek
Of his palm, sighing, a pillow.
They are father and father-in-law of the evil of France:
They know their descendants' lives are corrupt and foul,
And from this comes the grief that so pierces them.
He who looks so robust and is in harmony
Singing with the man of the prominent nose
Wore the cord of every virtue girt about him.
And if after him there had remained as king
The young man seated behind him,
Virtue would indeed have passed from vessel to vessel;
But this cannot be said of the other heirs:
James and Frederick have kingdoms,
Yet neither possesses the better inheritance.
Rarely does there rise again through the branches
Human goodness; this is the will
Of Him who grants it, that it be named from Him.
My words also turn to the prominent-nosed man,
No less than to the other Peter who sings with him,
For whom Apulia and Provence now grieve within themselves.
By so much is the plant lesser than its seed,
As, more than Beatrice and Margaret,
Constance still boasts of her husband.
See the king of simple life,
Sitting alone there, Henry of England;
He has a better outcome in his branches.
He who lies lowest among them,
Looking upward, is Marquis William,
For whom Alessandria and its wars
Make Montferrato and Canavese weep.
