When through pleasures or through pains,
that seize any faculty of ours,
the soul gathers itself wholly to it,
it seems to attend to no other faculty;
and this goes against that error which believes
that one soul is kindled above another within us.
And so, when one hears or sees something
that holds the soul turned firmly toward it,
time passes and one is unaware of it;
for one faculty is that which heeds it,
and another that which holds the whole soul:
this one is as though bound, and that one free.
Of this I had true experience,
hearing that spirit and marveling;
for fully fifty degrees had risen
the sun, and I had not noticed, when
we came where those souls all together
cried to us: “Here is what you seek.”
A larger opening is often blocked
with a little forkful of his thorns
by the peasant when the grapes grow dark,
than was the passage by which ascended
my guide, and I behind him, alone,
when the company departed from us.
One goes up to Sanleo and descends to Noli,
one climbs up Bismantova and Cacume
with the feet alone; but here one must fly;
I mean with the swift wings and with the feathers
of great desire, behind that guide
who gave me hope and provided light.
We were climbing within the split rock,
and on every side its edges pressed us close,
and the ground beneath demanded feet and hands.
When we were upon the highest edge
of the lofty bank, on the open slope,
“My master,” I said, “which way shall we go?”.
And he to me: “Let none of your steps be wasted;
keep gaining ground up the mountain behind me,
until some wise guide appears to us.”
The summit was so high it surpassed our sight,
and the slope far steeper
than a line from a quadrant's midpoint to its center.
I was weary when I began:
“O sweet father, turn and look
how I am left alone, unless you stop.”
“My son,” he said, “pull yourself as far as here,”
pointing out to me a ledge a little higher
that circles the whole mountain on that side.
So did his words spur me on,
that I strained myself, crawling after him,
until the ledge was beneath my feet.
There we both sat down,
facing east, whence we had climbed,
for looking back usually heartens one.
First I directed my eyes toward the low shores;
then I raised them to the sun, and wondered
that we were being struck by it from the left.
The poet clearly perceived that I stood
wholly astonished at the chariot of light,
where it passed between us and the north.
Whereupon he said to me: “If Castor and Pollux
were in the company of that mirror
which leads its light above and below,
you would see the reddened Zodiac
revolving still closer to the Bears,
unless it strayed from its ancient path.
If you wish to understand how this can be,
gathered inwardly, imagine Zion
with this mountain standing upon the earth
so that both have a single horizon
and different hemispheres; hence the road
which Phaethon, to his ruin, knew not how to drive,
you will see how it must pass this mountain
on one side, while passing that on the other,
if your intellect attends quite clearly.”
“Certainly, my master,” I said, “never yet
have I seen as clearly as I now discern,
where my understanding seemed deficient,
that the middle circle of the heavenly motion,
which in a certain science is called the Equator,
and which always remains between the sun and winter,
for the reason you give, stretches away from here
toward the north, as far as the Hebrews
saw it toward the region of heat.
But if it pleases you, I would gladly know
how far we have to go; for the mountain rises
higher than my eyes can climb.”
And he to me: “This mountain is such,
that it is always hard at the beginning below;
and the higher one goes, the less it hurts.
Therefore, when it shall seem gentle to you
so much that going upward will be easy for you
as going downstream in a boat,
then you will be at the end of this path;
there expect to rest your weariness.
I answer no more, and this I know for truth.”
And when he had spoken his words,
a voice sounded close by: “Perhaps
you will need to sit down before then!”.
At its sound each of us turned,
and we saw on our left a great boulder,
which neither I nor he had noticed before.
We made our way there; and there were people
who stayed in the shade behind the rock
as one settles down through laziness.
And one of them, who seemed weary to me,
sat and clasped his knees,
keeping his face lowered between them.
“O my sweet lord,” I said, “look at
that one who shows himself more negligent
than if sloth were his sister.”
Then he turned toward us and took notice,
moving his face only up along his thigh,
and said: “Now go on up, you who are so capable!”.
Then I knew who he was, and that distress
which still quickened my breath a little,
did not prevent my going to him; and after
I had reached him, he barely raised his head,
saying: “Have you clearly seen how the sun
drives its chariot past your left shoulder?”.
His sluggish gestures and his brief words
moved my lips a little toward laughter;
then I began: “Belacqua, it no longer grieves me
about you now; but tell me: why seated
are you right here? are you waiting for a guide,
or have you merely resumed your accustomed ways?”.
And he: “O brother, what use is going up?
for he would not let me go to the torments,
the angel of God who sits upon the gate.
First the heavens must revolve around me for as long
outside it, as they did during life,
because I delayed until the end the good sighs,
unless prayer first comes to my aid
that rises from a heart living in grace;
what does the other avail, which is not heard in heaven?”.
And already the poet was climbing ahead of me,
and was saying: “Come now; see that there has been reached
the meridian by the sun, and at the shore
night already covers Morocco with its foot.”
