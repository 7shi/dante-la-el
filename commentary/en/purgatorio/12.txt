Side by side, like oxen that go under the yoke,
I was going with that burdened soul,
as long as the gentle teacher allowed it.
But when he said: “Leave him and pass on;
for here it is good, with wings and oars,
for each to drive his boat as best he can”;
Upright, as one ought to walk, I made myself again
in my body, although my thoughts
remained bowed down and diminished.
I had moved on, and willingly followed
my master's footsteps, and both of us
were already showing how light we were;
and he said to me: “Turn your eyes downward:
it will be good for you, to ease the way,
to see the bed beneath the soles of your feet.”
As, so that their memory may endure,
above the buried, tombs level with the ground
bear depicted what they were before,
whence there one often weeps anew
at the sting of remembrance,
which spurs only the compassionate;
so I saw there, but of finer appearance
in their workmanship, depicted
all that projects from the mountain as a path.
I saw him who was created noble
more than any other creature, down from heaven
descending like lightning, on one side.
I saw Briareus, pierced by the bolt
of heaven, lying on the other side,
heavy upon the earth with the chill of death.
I saw Thymbraeus, I saw Pallas and Mars,
still armed, around their father,
gazing at the scattered limbs of the Giants.
I saw Nimrod at the foot of the great work
as though bewildered, and looking at the peoples
who in Shinar were proud with him.
O Niobe, with what sorrowful eyes
I saw you depicted upon the road,
among your seven and seven children slain!
O Saul, how upon your own sword
you appeared there, dead on Gilboa,
which afterward felt neither rain nor dew!
O foolish Arachne, thus I saw you
already half spider, sorrowful upon the shreds
of the work that was made to your harm.
O Rehoboam, no longer does it seem to threaten,
your image there; but full of terror
a chariot bears it away, with no one pursuing it.
The hard pavement also showed
how Alcmaeon made his mother deem costly
the ill-fated ornament.
It showed how the sons hurled themselves
upon Sennacherib within the temple,
and how, once he was dead, they left him there.
It showed the destruction and the cruel slaughter
that Tomyris wrought, when she said to Cyrus:
“You thirsted for blood, and I fill you with blood.”
It showed how in rout they fled,
the Assyrians, after Holofernes was killed,
and also the remains of the slaughter.
I saw Troy in ashes and in hollow ruins;
O Ilium, how low and abject
the image discerned there showed you!
What master of brush or stylus was there
who could portray the shadows and the features that there
would make a subtle mind marvel?
The dead appeared dead, and the living alive:
one who saw the reality saw no better than I,
all that I trod upon while I went bent down.
Now be proud, and onward with haughty faces,
children of Eve, and do not lower your faces
so that you may see your evil path!
More of the mountain had already been rounded by us,
and far more of the sun's course spent,
than my absorbed mind reckoned,
when he who always went attentive
ahead began: “Raise your head;
it is no longer time to go so lost in thought.
See there an angel who makes ready
to come toward us; see that there returns
from the service of the day the sixth handmaid.
Adorn your face and gestures with reverence,
so that it may please him to send us upward;
Think that this day never dawns again!”
I was well accustomed to his admonition
always not to lose time, so that on that
subject he could not speak obscurely to me.
Toward us came the beautiful creature,
clothed in white, and in his face such
as the morning star appears, trembling.
He opened his arms, and then opened his wings;
he said: “Come: here the steps are near,
and easily now one climbs.
To this invitation very few come:
O human race, born to fly upward,
why do you fall so at a little wind?”
He led us where the rock was cut;
there he struck his wings against my forehead;
then he promised me a safe passage.
As on the right, to climb the hill
where stands the church that overlooks
the well-governed city above Rubaconte,
the bold steepness of the ascent is broken
by the stairways made in an age
when the ledger and the stave were safe;
so the slope is eased that falls
there very steeply from the next terrace;
but on either side the high rock grazes you.
As we turned our bodies that way,
“Blessed are the poor in spirit!” voices
sang so that speech could not express it.
Ah, how different are those entrances
from those of Hell! For here with songs
one enters, and down there with savage lamentations.
Already we were climbing up the holy steps,
and I seemed to be far lighter
than I had seemed before upon the level ground.
Whence I: “Master, tell me, what heavy thing
has been lifted from me, that almost no
weariness is felt by me as I walk?”
He answered: “When the P's that have remained
still upon your face, almost faded,
are, as one is, entirely erased,
your feet will be so mastered by good will,
that not only will they feel no weariness,
but it will be their delight to be urged upward.”
Then I did as those do who go
with something on their heads unknown to them,
except that others' gestures make them suspect it;
so the hand lends its help to make certain,
and searches and finds and performs that task
which cannot be accomplished by sight;
and with the fingers of my right hand spread apart
I found only six of the letters that he had carved,
the one with the keys, above my temples:
and watching this, my guide smiled.
