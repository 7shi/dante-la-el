To sail through better waters, raises its sails
now the little ship of my intellect,
which leaves behind itself so cruel a sea;
and I shall sing of that second kingdom
where the human spirit purges itself
and becomes worthy of ascending to heaven.
But here let dead poetry rise again,
O holy Muses, since I am yours;
and here let Calliope rise a little,
accompanying my song with that sound
of which the wretched magpies felt
such a blow that they despaired of pardon.
Sweet color of oriental sapphire,
that was gathering in the serene appearance
of the air, pure as far as the first sphere,
began once more to give delight to my eyes,
as soon as I came out of the dead air
that had saddened my eyes and breast.
The beautiful planet that encourages love
made all the east smile,
veiling the Fishes that were in its company.
I turned to the right, and gave heed
at the other pole, and I saw four stars
never seen except by the first people.
The heavens seemed to rejoice in their little flames:
O widowed northern region,
since you are deprived of looking upon them!
When I had turned away from gazing at them,
turning a little toward the other pole,
there where the Wain had already disappeared,
I saw near me an old man alone,
worthy in appearance of so much reverence,
that no son owes more to a father.
A long beard, mingled with white hair,
he wore, like the hair of his head,
from which a double tress fell to his breast.
The rays of the four holy lights
so adorned his face with light,
that I saw him as though the sun were before him.
“Who are you who against the blind river
have fled the eternal prison?”
he said, moving those venerable plumes.
“Who has guided you, or what served you as a lamp,
as you came out of the deep night
that forever blackens the infernal valley?
Are the laws of the abyss thus broken?
or has a new decree been made in heaven,
that, though damned, you come to my cliffs?”
My guide then took hold of me,
and with words and with hands and with signs
made my knees and brow reverent.
Then he answered him: “I did not come of myself:
a lady descended from heaven, through whose prayers
I aided this man with my companionship.
But since it is your will that more be explained
of our condition as it truly is,
my will cannot be to deny you.
This man has never seen his final evening;
but through his folly he came so near it,
that very little time remained to run.
As I said, I was sent to him
to save him; and there was no other way
than this upon which I have set out.
I have shown him all the wicked people;
and now I intend to show him those spirits
who purge themselves under your authority.
How I have brought him would be long to tell you;
from on high descends a power that helps me
lead him to see you and to hear you.
Now may it please you to welcome his coming:
he goes seeking freedom, which is so dear,
as one knows who refuses life for its sake.
You know it, for on its account there was no bitterness for you in
death at Utica, where you left
the garment that on the great day will be so bright.
The eternal decrees are not broken by us,
for this man lives, and Minos does not bind me;
but I am from the circle where are the chaste eyes
of your Marcia, who in her look still begs you,
O holy breast, to hold her as your own:
for her love, then, incline toward us.
Let us go through your seven kingdoms;
I shall carry back to her thanks for you,
if you deign to be mentioned down there.”
“Marcia so pleased my eyes
while I was on the other side,” he then said,
“that whatever favors she wished of me, I granted.
Now that she dwells beyond the evil river,
she can move me no longer, because of that law
that was made when I came out from there.
But if a lady from heaven moves and guides you,
as you say, there is no need of flattery:
let it suffice that you ask me on her behalf.
Go, then, and see that you gird this man
with a plain reed and that you wash his face,
so that you wipe away every stain from it;
for it would not be fitting, with his eye clouded
by any mist, to go before the first
minister, who is one of those of paradise.
This little island, all around its lowest edge,
down there where the waves beat upon it,
bears rushes upon the soft mud:
No other plant that put forth foliage
or grew hard can live there,
because it does not yield to the blows.
Then let your return not be this way;
the sun, which is now rising, will show you
how to take the mountain by an easier ascent.”
Thus he vanished; and I rose up
without speaking, and drew wholly back
to my guide, and turned my eyes toward him.
He began: “Son, follow my steps:
let us turn back, for on this side slopes down
this plain to its lowest bounds.”
The dawn was overcoming the morning hour
that fled before it, so that from afar
I discerned the trembling of the sea.
We were walking over the lonely plain
like one who returns to the road he has lost,
who until he reaches it seems to walk in vain.
When we were where the dew
struggles with the sun, through being in a place
where, in the shade, it scarcely disperses,
both his hands, spread out upon the little grass,
my master gently placed:
whereupon I, who understood his purpose,
I offered him my tearful cheeks;
there he made wholly uncovered for me
that color which Hell had hidden from me.
We came then to the deserted shore,
which never saw its waters sailed by
a man who afterward knew return.
Here he girded me as another had wished:
O wonder! for as he picked
the humble plant, so it sprang up again
at once where he had plucked it.
