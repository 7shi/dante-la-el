After the courteous and joyful greetings
had been repeated three and four times,
Sordello drew back, and said: “You, who are you?”.
“Before toward this mountain were turned
the souls worthy of ascending to God,
my bones were buried by Octavian.
I am Virgil; and for no other sin
did I lose heaven than for not having faith”.
Thus answered then my guide.
As one who before him something
suddenly sees at which he marvels,
who believes and does not, saying “It is . . . it is not . . . ”,
such that man appeared; and then he lowered his eyes,
and humbly turned back toward him,
and embraced him where the lesser person takes hold.
“O glory of the Latins”, he said, “through whom
showed what our language could do,
O eternal glory of the place from which I came,
what merit or what grace reveals you to me?
If I am worthy to hear your words,
tell me if you come from Hell, and from which cloister”.
“Through all the circles of the sorrowful kingdom”,
he answered him, “have I come here;
the power of heaven moved me, and with it I come.
Not through doing, but through not doing, I have lost
the sight of the high Sun that you desire
and that was known by me too late.
There is a place below saddened not by torments,
but by darkness alone, where the laments
sound not as cries of pain, but are sighs.
There I dwell with the innocent little children
bitten by the teeth of death before
they were freed from human guilt;
there I dwell with those who the three holy
virtues did not put on, and without vice
knew the others and followed them all.
But if you know and can, some indication
give us by which we may come more quickly
to where purgatory has its proper beginning”.
He answered: “No fixed place is assigned to us;
I am permitted to go upward and around;
as far as I can go, I join you as a guide.
But see already how the day declines,
and to go upward by night is not possible;
therefore it is well to think of a pleasant resting place.
There are souls apart here to the right;
if you consent, I shall lead you to them,
and not without delight will they become known to you”.
“How is this?”, came the answer. “Whoever wished
to climb by night, would he be prevented
by another, or would he not climb because he could not?”.
And good Sordello drew his finger on the ground,
saying: “See? Even this line alone
you would not cross after the sun had gone:
not, however, because anything else caused obstruction,
than the darkness of night, to going upward;
that entangles the will with inability.
One could indeed in that darkness return below
and walk about the slope, wandering around,
while the horizon keeps the daylight shut away”.
Then my lord, as if in wonder,
“Lead us”, he said, “then where you say
that one may find delight in staying”.
We had gone but a little distance from there,
when I noticed that the mountain was hollowed out,
in the way that valleys hollow them out here.
“Over there”, said that shade, “we shall go
where the slope makes a lap of itself;
and there we shall await the new day”.
Between steep and level was a slanting path,
which led us into the side of the hollow,
where its rim dwindles to less than half its height.
Gold and fine silver, scarlet and white lead,
indigo, wood bright and clear,
fresh emerald at the moment it is broken,
by the grass and flowers, within that hollow
placed, each would be surpassed in color,
as the lesser is surpassed by its greater.
Nature had not only painted there,
but from the sweetness of a thousand scents
she made there one unknown and indistinct.
‘Hail, Queen’ upon the greenery and upon the flowers
from there I saw souls sitting and singing,
who because of the valley were not visible from outside.
“Before the little sun remaining now nestles away”,
began the Mantuan who had turned us aside,
“do not wish me to guide you among those souls.
From this ledge better the gestures and faces
you will discern of every one of them,
than down in the hollow gathered among them.
The one who sits highest and shows signs
of having neglected what he ought to have done,
and who does not move his mouth to the others' songs,
was Emperor Rudolf, who could
heal the wounds that have killed Italy,
so that only slowly through others is she revived.
The other, who comforts him by his look,
ruled the land where the water rises
that the Vltava bears into the Elbe, and the Elbe into the sea:
Ottokar was his name, and in swaddling clothes
he was far better than Wenceslaus his son
bearded, whom lust and idleness feed.
And that small-nosed man who in close counsel
seems to be with the one who has so kindly an appearance,
died fleeing and stripping the lily of its petals:
look there how he beats his breast!
See the other who has made for his cheek
a bed of his palm, sighing.
They are father and father-in-law of the evil of France:
they know his vicious and filthy life,
and from this comes the grief that so pierces them.
The one who appears so strongly built and joins in harmony,
singing, with the one of the manly nose,
wore the cord girded with every kind of worth;
and if as king after him had remained
the young man who sits behind him,
truly worth would have passed from vessel to vessel,
which cannot be said of the other heirs;
James and Frederick possess the kingdoms;
neither possesses the better inheritance.
Rarely does there rise again through the branches
human uprightness; and this is willed by
the one who gives it, so that it may be asked of him.
To the large-nosed man also my words apply
no less than to the other, Peter, who sings with him,
for which Apulia and Provence already grieve.
So much inferior to its seed is the plant,
as, more than Beatrice and Margaret,
Constance still boasts of her husband.
See the king of the simple life
sitting there alone, Henry of England:
he has in his branches a better outcome.
The one who sits lowest on the ground among these,
looking upward, is Marquis William,
because of whom both Alessandria and its war
make Montferrat and Canavese weep.”
