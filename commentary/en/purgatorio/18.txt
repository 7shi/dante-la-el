An end had been put to his discourse
by the lofty teacher, and he looked attentively
into my face to see if I seemed content;
and I, whom a new thirst still spurred,
was silent outwardly, and inwardly said: ‘Perhaps
the many questions I ask weigh on him’.
But that true father, who perceived
the timid wish that did not reveal itself,
by speaking, gave me courage to speak.
Then I: “Master, my sight grows so keen
in your light, that I clearly discern
whatever your reasoning distinguishes or describes.
Therefore I beg you, sweet dear father,
to explain to me love, to which you trace
every good action and its contrary.”
“Turn,” he said, “toward me the keen eyes
of your intellect, and manifest to you shall be
the error of the blind who make themselves guides.
The soul, which is created ready to love,
is moved toward everything that pleases,
as soon as pleasure awakens it to action.
Your apprehensive faculty, from a real being,
draws a representation, and unfolds it within you,
so that it makes the mind turn toward it;
and if, having turned, it inclines toward it,
that inclination is love, that is nature
which through pleasure binds itself anew within you.
Then, as fire moves upward
through its form, which is born to rise
to where it endures longest in its own substance,
so the captivated soul enters into desire,
which is a spiritual movement, and never rests
until the beloved thing makes it rejoice.
Now it may be clear to you how hidden
the truth is from those who declare
every love to be in itself praiseworthy;
because perhaps its matter appears
always to be good, but not every impression
is good, although the wax may be good.”
“Your words and my understanding, following them,”
I answered him, “have revealed love to me,
but this has made me more laden with doubt;
for, if love is offered to us from outside
and the soul walks with no other foot,
whether it goes straight or crooked is no merit of its own.”
And he to me: “As much as reason sees here,
I can tell you; beyond that, look
only to Beatrice, for it is a matter of faith.
Every substantial form, which separate
is from matter and yet united with it,
has a specific power gathered within itself,
which is not perceived unless it acts,
nor ever reveals itself except through its effect,
as life in a plant through green leaves.
Therefore, whence comes the understanding
of first notions, man does not know,
nor the affection for the first objects of desire,
which are in you just as diligence in a bee
to make honey; and this first desire
admits no desert of praise or blame.
Now, so that every other desire may conform to this one,
innate in you is the faculty that deliberates,
and it must guard the threshold of assent.
This is the principle from which derives
the ground of merit in you, according
as it receives and sifts good and evil loves.
Those who through reasoning reached the bottom
became aware of this innate freedom;
therefore they left moral teaching to the world.
Therefore, let us suppose that of necessity
every love arises that is kindled within you,
within you is the power to restrain it.
This noble power Beatrice understands
as free will, and therefore take care
to keep it in mind, if she begins to speak of it to you.”
The moon, late by almost midnight,
made the stars appear fewer to us,
shaped like a bucket that glows all over;
and it ran against the heavens along those paths
that the sun sets aflame when those in Rome
see it between Sardinia and Corsica as it sets.
And that gentle shade through whom
Pietola is more renowned than the city of Mantua,
had laid down the burden with which I had laden him;
so that I, who the clear and plain reasoning
upon my questions had gathered in,
stood like a man who wanders drowsily in thought.
But this drowsiness was taken from me
suddenly by people who, from behind
our backs, had already turned toward us.
And as Ismenus and Asopus once saw
frenzy and thronging along their banks at night,
whenever the Thebans had need of Bacchus,
so around that terrace it curves its stride,
from what I saw of those approaching,
whom good will and righteous love ride.
Soon they were upon us, because at a run
that whole great crowd was moving;
and two in front were crying out in tears:
“Mary ran with haste to the mountain country;
and Caesar, to subdue Ilerda,
struck at Marseille and then ran into Spain.”
“Quickly, quickly, so that time may not be lost
through too little love,” cried the others behind,
“so that zeal in doing good may make grace green again.”
“O people in whom keen fervor now
perhaps makes amends for negligence and delay
shown by you through lukewarmness in doing good,
this man who lives, and surely I do not lie to you,
wishes to go up, provided the sun shines on us;
therefore tell us where the passage lies nearby.”
These were the words of my guide;
and one of those spirits said: “Come
behind us, and you will find the opening.
We are so full of desire to move
that we cannot stay; therefore forgive us,
if you take our just penance for discourtesy.
I was abbot at San Zeno in Verona
under the rule of the good Barbarossa,
of whom Milan still speaks sorrowfully.
And someone has already one foot in the grave,
who will soon mourn on account of that monastery,
and will be sorry to have had power;
because his son, unsound in body,
and worse in mind, and who was born illegitimate,
he has put in the place of its true shepherd.”
I do not know if he said more or fell silent,
so far beyond us had he already run;
but this I heard, and was pleased to keep in mind.
And he who had helped me in every need
said: “Turn this way: see two of them
coming, biting at sloth.”
Behind all the others they were saying: “Earlier was
dead the people for whom the sea opened,
than Jordan saw its heirs.
And that people which did not endure the hardship
to the end with the son of Anchises,
gave itself over to a life without glory.”
Then when so far removed from us were
those shades that they could no longer be seen,
a new thought entered into me,
from which many other and different thoughts were born;
and so far I wandered from one to another,
that through wandering thought I closed my eyes,
and I transformed thought into a dream.
