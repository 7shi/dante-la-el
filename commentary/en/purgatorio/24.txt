Neither speech walking, nor walking it any slower
made, but talking we went swiftly,
just as a ship driven by a good wind;
and the shades, who seemed things dead a second time,
through the hollows of their eyes amazement
drew from me, having become aware that I was alive.
And I, continuing my discourse,
said: “She goes upward perhaps more slowly
than she otherwise would, for another's sake.
But tell me, if you know, where Piccarda is;
tell me if I see any person worth noting
among these people who gaze at me so.”
“My sister, who between beautiful and good
I do not know which she was more, triumphs joyfully
in high Olympus already in her crown.”
So he first spoke; and then: “Here it is not forbidden
to name each person, since so drained
away is our appearance by the diet.
This one,” and he pointed with his finger, “is Bonagiunta,
Bonagiunta of Lucca; and that face
beyond him, more deeply pitted than the others,
held Holy Church in his arms:
he was from Tours, and purges through fasting
the eels of Bolsena and the vernaccia wine.”
Many others he named to me one by one;
and all seemed pleased at being named,
so that I saw no dark look on that account.
I saw, from hunger, working their teeth on emptiness,
Ubaldin della Pila and Boniface,
who shepherded many people with his crozier.
I saw Messer Marchese, who once had leisure
to drink at Forlì with less dryness,
and yet was such that he never felt sated.
But as one does who looks and then takes account
of one more than another, so I did with him of Lucca,
who seemed to have more knowledge of me.
He was murmuring; and something like “Gentucca”
I heard there, where he felt the wound
of the justice that so strips them bare.
“O soul,” I said, “who seem so eager
to speak with me, make yourself understood to me,
and satisfy both yourself and me with your speech.”
“A woman has been born, and does not yet wear a headcloth,”
he began, “who will make pleasing to you
my city, however people may reproach it.
You will go away with this foreknowledge:
if you took my murmuring amiss,
the actual events will yet make it clear to you.
But tell me if I see here the one who brought forth
the new rhymes, beginning
‘Ladies who have understanding of love’.”
And I to him: “I am one who, when
Love inspires me, takes note, and in that manner
which he dictates within I go on expressing.”
“O brother, now I see,” he said, “the knot
that held the Notary and Guittone and me
on this side of the sweet new style that I hear!
I see clearly how your pens
follow closely behind the dictator,
which certainly did not happen with ours;
and whoever sets himself to go any further
sees no other difference between the one style and the other”;
and, as though satisfied, he fell silent.
As the birds that winter along the Nile
sometimes form a flock in the air,
then fly more swiftly and go in single file,
so all the people who were there,
turning their faces, quickened their pace,
light both through leanness and through will.
And as a man who is weary of trotting
lets his companions go on, and so walks
until the panting of his chest subsides,
so he let the holy flock pass on,
Forese, and came along behind with me,
saying: “When shall I see you again?”
“I do not know,” I answered him, “how long I shall live;
but my return will certainly not be so soon,
that I shall not first be at the shore in desire;
because the place where I was set to live,
from day to day strips itself more of goodness,
and seems appointed for grievous ruin.”
“Now go,” he said; “for the one most to blame for it,
I see dragged at the tail of a beast
toward the valley where guilt is never purged.
The beast at every step goes faster,
ever increasing, until it strikes him,
and leaves the body shamefully mangled.
Not much longer have those wheels to turn,”
and he raised his eyes to heaven, “before it becomes clear to you
what my words cannot make clearer.
Now remain behind; for time is precious
in this kingdom, so that I lose too much
by coming alongside you thus at an equal pace.”
As sometimes at a gallop there comes forth
a knight from a troop riding onward,
and goes to win himself honor in the first encounter,
so he departed from us with longer strides;
and I remained upon the way with those two
who were such great marshals of the world.
And when he had gone ahead of us,
so that my eyes became his followers,
as my mind did his words,
there appeared to me the laden and living branches
of another fruit tree, and not far away,
for only then had I turned in that direction.
I saw people beneath it raise their hands
and cry I know not what toward the leaves,
like eager and unsuccessful little children
who beg, and the one begged does not answer,
but, to make their desire thoroughly keen,
holds high what they desire and does not hide it.
Then they departed as though disillusioned;
and we came at once to the great tree,
which refuses so many prayers and tears.
“Pass beyond without drawing near:
higher up is a tree that was bitten by Eve,
and this plant sprang from it.”
Thus among the branches someone spoke, I know not who;
at which Virgil and Statius and I, close together,
went onward along the side that rises.
“Remember,” it said, “the accursed ones
formed in the clouds, who, sated,
fought Theseus with their double breasts;
and the Hebrews who showed themselves soft in drinking,
for which Gideon did not want them as companions,
when toward Midian he descended the hills.”
Thus close to one of the two edges
we passed, hearing of sins of gluttony
followed once by wretched gains.
Then, spreading out along the empty road,
a good thousand steps and more carried us onward,
each contemplating without a word.
“What are you thinking as you go thus, you three alone?”
a sudden voice said; at which I started
as frightened and sluggish beasts do.
I raised my head to see who it was;
and never in a furnace were seen
glass or metals so shining and red,
as one I saw who said: “If you wish
to climb upward, here you must turn;
this way goes whoever wishes to go toward peace.”
His appearance had taken away my sight;
so I turned behind my teachers,
like a man who walks according to what he hears.
And as, heralding the dawn,
the breeze of May stirs and gives forth fragrance,
all steeped in the grass and flowers;
such a wind I felt strike across the middle of
my forehead, and clearly felt the feather move,
which made the breeze smell of ambrosia.
And I heard it said: “Blessed are those whom it illumines
with so much grace that the love of taste
does not kindle excessive desire in their breast,
always hungering as much as is right!”
