Darkness of hell and of a night deprived
of every planet, beneath a barren sky,
as darkened by clouds as it can be,
never made so thick a veil before my sight
as that smoke which covered us there,
nor to the touch of so harsh a texture,
that the eye could not endure staying open;
wherefore my knowledgeable and faithful escort
came close to me and offered me his shoulder.
Just as a blind man follows his guide
so as not to lose his way and not to strike
against something that might hurt him, or perhaps kill him,
I went through the bitter and foul air,
listening to my leader, who kept saying
only: “Take care that you are not cut off from me.”
I heard voices, and each one seemed
to pray for peace and for mercy
to the Lamb of God who takes away sins.
Only ‘Lamb of God’ were their beginnings;
one wording was in them all, and one manner,
so that every harmony seemed to exist among them.
“Are those spirits, master, that I hear?”
I said. And he to me: “You apprehend truly,
and they go loosening the knot of wrath.”
“Now who are you who cleave our smoke,
and speak of us just as though you
still divided time by the calendar?”
Thus was it spoken by a voice;
wherefore my master said: “Reply,
and ask whether this way leads upward.”
And I: “O creature who cleanse yourself
to return beautiful to him who made you,
you shall hear a marvel, if you accompany me.”
“I shall follow you as far as I am allowed,”
he replied; “and if smoke does not let us see,
hearing will keep us joined instead.”
Then I began: “With that covering
which death dissolves I am going upward,
and I came here through infernal anguish.
And if God has enclosed me in his grace,
so much that he wills that I should see his court
in a manner wholly outside modern custom,
do not conceal from me who you were before death,
but tell me, and tell me if I go rightly toward the passage;
and your words shall be our guides.”
“I was a Lombard, and was called Marco;
I knew the world, and loved that worth
toward which everyone now has unstrung his bow.
You are going straight toward the ascent.”
Thus he replied, and added: “I beg you
to pray for me when you are above.”
And I to him: “By my faith I bind myself to you
to do what you ask me; but I shall burst
inwardly with a doubt, unless I free myself of it.
Before it was single, and now it is made double
by your judgment, which makes certain for me
here what I couple with it from elsewhere.
The world is indeed thus wholly barren
of every virtue, as you tell me,
and pregnant and covered with malice;
but I pray you to point out the cause,
so that I may see it and show it to others;
for one places it in heaven, and another here below.”
A deep sigh, which grief compressed into “Ah!”
he first let out; and then began: “Brother,
the world is blind, and you indeed come from it.
You who are living attribute every cause
only to heaven above, just as if everything
it moved along with itself by necessity.
If this were so, in you would be destroyed
free choice, and there would be no justice
in having joy for good, and grief for evil.
Heaven initiates your impulses;
I do not say all, but, even supposing I said so,
light is given you to discern good and evil,
and free will; which, if hardship
in its first battles with heaven it endures,
afterward conquers all, if it is well nourished.
To a greater power and a better nature
you are subject in freedom; and that creates
the mind within you, which heaven has not in its charge.
Therefore, if the present world goes astray,
in you is the cause, in you let it be sought;
and I shall now be your true informant on this.
From the hand of him who lovingly beholds it
before it exists, in the likeness of a little girl
who behaves childishly, weeping and laughing, comes
the little simple soul that knows nothing,
except that, set in motion by a joyful maker,
it gladly turns toward what delights it.
At first it tastes a little good;
there it deceives itself, and runs after it,
unless a guide or rein redirects its love.
Hence law was needed to impose a rein;
a ruler was needed, who could discern
of the true city at least the tower.
The laws exist, but who puts a hand to them?
No one, because the shepherd who goes before,
can chew the cud, but does not have divided hooves;
therefore the people, who see their guide
strike only at that good for which they hunger,
feed on that, and seek nothing further.
You can clearly see that bad guidance
is the cause that has made the world wicked,
and not nature corrupted within you.
Rome, which made the world good, used
to have two suns, which the one and the other road
made visible, both of the world and of God.
One has extinguished the other; and the sword is joined
with the pastoral staff, and the one together with the other
by sheer necessity must go badly;
because, joined, neither fears the other:
if you do not believe me, consider the ear of grain,
for every plant is known by its seed.
In the land that the Adige and Po water,
worth and courtesy used to be found,
before Frederick encountered strife;
now there may safely pass through it
anyone who refrained, for shame,
from speaking with the good or approaching them.
Yet there are still three old men in whom rebukes
the ancient age the new, and it seems long to them
before God restores them to a better life:
Conrad da Palazzo and the good Gherardo
and Guido da Castel, who is better named,
in the French manner, the straightforward Lombard.
Say henceforth that the Church of Rome,
by confusing within itself two governments,
falls into the mud, and soils itself and its burden.”
“O my Marco,” I said, “you reason well;
and now I discern why from the inheritance
the sons of Levi were excluded.
But which Gherardo is he whom, as a sample,
you say remains of the vanished people,
in reproach of this savage age?”
“Either your speech deceives me, or it tests me,”
he replied to me; “for, speaking Tuscan to me,
you seem to know nothing of the good Gherardo.
By any other byname I do not know him,
unless I were to take it from his daughter Gaia.
God be with you, for I come no farther with you.
See the dawnlike light that shines through the smoke
already growing white, and I must depart
(the angel is there) before I appear to him.”
Thus he turned back, and would hear me no more.
