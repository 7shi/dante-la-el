Already eager to explore within and all around
the divine forest, dense and living,
which tempered the new daylight for my eyes,
without waiting longer, I left the bank,
taking my way across the countryside slowly, slowly
over the ground that was fragrant on every side.
A gentle breeze, without alteration
having any within itself, struck me upon the forehead
with no stronger stroke than a mild wind;
at which the leaves, trembling, ready
all of them bent toward the side
where the holy mountain casts its first shadow;
yet not so far bent from their upright bearing
that the little birds among the treetops
ceased to practise all their arts;
but with full gladness the first hours,
singing, they welcomed among the leaves,
which kept a drone to their rhymes,
such as from branch to branch gathers
through the pinewood upon the shore of Classe,
when Aeolus releases the sirocco.
Already my slow steps had carried me
so far within the ancient wood that I
could no longer see where I had entered;
and behold, a stream prevented my going farther,
which toward the left with its little waves
bent the grass that sprang upon its bank.
All the waters that here are purest,
would seem to contain some admixture
beside that one, which conceals nothing,
although it flows dark, dark
beneath the perpetual shade, which never
allows sun or moon to cast their rays there.
With my feet I stopped, and with my eyes I passed
beyond the little river, to gaze upon
the great variety of fresh flowering boughs;
and there appeared to me, just as there appears
suddenly something that turns aside
through wonder every other thought,
a woman all alone who went
both singing and choosing flower from flower
with which her whole path was painted.
“Ah, beautiful woman, who in the rays of love
warm yourself, if I am to believe the looks
that are wont to bear witness to the heart,
may it please you to come forward,”
I said to her, “toward this riverbank,
so that I may understand what you sing.
You make me remember where and what she was,
Proserpina at the time when she lost
her mother her, and she springtime.”
As she turns, with the soles held close
to the ground and to each other, a woman dancing,
and scarcely sets one foot before the other,
she turned upon the red and upon the yellow
little flowers toward me, no differently
than a maiden who lowers her modest eyes;
and she brought my prayers to fulfilment,
drawing so near that the sweet sound
came to me with its meaning.
As soon as she was where the grasses are
already bathed by the waves of the lovely river,
she granted me the gift of raising her eyes.
I do not believe that so much light shone
beneath the eyelashes of Venus, pierced
by her son quite contrary to his usual practice.
She smiled, standing on the other bank,
handling many colours with her hands,
which the high land brings forth without seed.
The river kept us three steps apart;
but the Hellespont, where Xerxes crossed,
still a curb upon all human pride,
did not suffer more hatred from Leander
for surging between Sestos and Abydos,
than that stream from me because it did not then open.
“You are new here, and perhaps because I smile,”
she began, “in this place chosen
for human nature as its nest,
some doubt holds you in wonder;
but the psalm Delectasti gives light,
which can clear the mist from your understanding.
And you who are in front and entreated me,
say if you wish to hear more; for I came ready
for every question of yours, until you are satisfied.”
“The water,” I said, “and the sound of the forest
challenge within me a new belief
concerning something I heard contrary to this.”
Then she: “I shall tell how there proceeds
from its cause what makes you wonder,
and clear away the mist that assails you.
The supreme Good, which alone is pleasing to itself,
made humankind good and for good, and this place
gave to it as a pledge of eternal peace.
Through its own fault it dwelt here but a little;
through its own fault, for tears and anguish
it exchanged innocent laughter and sweet play.
So that the disturbance produced below of themselves
by the exhalations of water and earth,
which follow heat as far as they can,
might wage no war upon humankind,
this mountain rose so high toward heaven,
and is free of it from where it is locked.
Now because in a circuit all
the air revolves with the first revolution,
unless its circle is interrupted somewhere,
at this height, which stands wholly free
in the living air, that motion strikes,
and makes the forest sound because it is dense;
and the plant thus struck has such power,
that it impregnates the breeze with its virtue
and that breeze then, revolving, scatters it around;
and the other earth, according as it is fit
in itself and in its sky, conceives and brings forth
different trees of different powers.
It would then seem no wonder over there,
once this is heard, when some plant
takes root there without visible seed.
And you must know that the holy countryside
where you are is full of every seed,
and holds fruit that is not plucked over there.
The water that you see does not rise from a vein
that vapor replenishes when cold condenses it,
like a river that gains and loses strength;
but issues from a fountain constant and sure,
which receives again from the will of God as much
as it pours out, open on two sides.
On this side it descends with a power
that takes away one's memory of sin;
on the other it restores it of every good deed.
Here Lethe; so on the other side
it is called Eunoë, and does not work
unless first tasted on this side and on that:
this surpasses all other flavours.
And although sufficiently satisfied may be
your thirst without my revealing more to you,
I shall give you a corollary still, as a gift;
nor do I think my words will be less dear to you,
if they range with you beyond my promise.
Those who in ancient times sang in poetry
the Golden Age and its happy condition,
perhaps dreamed of this place upon Parnassus.
Here the human root was innocent;
here spring is everlasting and every fruit;
this is the nectar of which each one speaks.”
I then turned completely around
toward my poets, and saw that with a smile
they had heard the final statement;
then toward the beautiful woman I turned my face again.
