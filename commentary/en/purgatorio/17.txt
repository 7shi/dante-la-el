Remember, reader, if ever in the mountains
a mist overtook you through which you could see
no differently than moles through skin,
how, when the damp and dense vapors
begin to thin, the sphere
of the sun enters faintly through them;
and your imagination will be quick
in coming to see how I saw again
the sun at first, which was already setting.
Thus, matching my steps with the faithful steps
of my master, I came out of that cloud
into rays already dead on the low shores.
O imagination, which steals us
sometimes so far from outward things that we do not notice,
though a thousand trumpets sound around us,
who moves you, if sense supplies you nothing?
A light that takes form in heaven moves you,
of itself, or through a will that guides it downward.
Of the impiety of her who changed form
into the bird that delights most in singing,
the imprint appeared in my imagination;
and here my mind was so confined
within itself that from outside there did not come
anything that was then received by it.
Then there rained into the lofty fantasy
one crucified, scornful and fierce
in his appearance, and thus he was dying;
around him were the great Ahasuerus,
Esther his wife, and the just Mordecai,
who was so upright in word and deed.
And as this image broke apart
of itself, like a bubble
beneath which the water on which it formed gives way,
there arose in my vision a maiden
weeping bitterly, and she said: “O queen,
why in anger have you wished to be nothing?
You have slain yourself so as not to lose Lavinia;
now you have lost me! I am she who mourns,
mother, at your ruin before another's”.
As sleep is broken when suddenly
a new light strikes the closed eyes,
and, broken, flickers before it dies completely;
so my imagining fell away
as soon as a light struck my face,
far greater than the light we are accustomed to.
I was turning to see where I was,
when a voice said “Here one ascends”,
which drew me away from every other thought;
and made my desire so eager
to look upon the one who was speaking,
that it never rests until it meets its object.
But as before the sun, which burdens our sight
and by its excess veils its own form,
so my power of sight failed me there.
“This is a divine spirit, who onto the
upward path directs us without being asked,
and with his light conceals his very self.
He deals with us as a man deals with himself;
for whoever waits for a request and sees the need,
is already maliciously inclining toward refusal.
Now let us match our steps to so great an invitation;
let us strive to climb before it grows dark,
for afterward we could not, until day returns”.
So spoke my guide, and I with him
turned our steps toward a stairway;
and as soon as I reached the first step,
I felt near me something like a movement of a wing
and a fanning of my face, and the saying: ‘Blessed
the peacemakers, who are without evil wrath!’.
Already so high above us were raised
the last rays that night follows,
that stars were appearing on many sides.
‘O strength of mine, why do you fade away so?’,
I said within myself, for I felt in myself
the power of the legs put in abeyance.
We were where no farther climbed
the stairway upward, and we had come to a halt,
just like a ship that reaches the shore.
And I waited a little, in case I might hear
anything upon the new terrace;
then I turned to my master, and said:
“My sweet father, tell me, what offense
is purged here on the terrace where we are?
If our feet stand still, let not your speech stand still”.
And he to me: “Love of the good, falling short
of its duty, is restored here;
here the sinfully slackened oar is plied anew.
But so that you may understand still more clearly,
turn your mind to me, and you will gather
some good fruit from our delay”.
“Neither creator nor creature ever”,
he began, “my son, was without love,
either natural or of the mind; and you know this.
Natural love is always without error,
but the other can err through an evil object
or through too much or too little vigor.
As long as it is directed toward the highest good,
and measures itself in loving lesser goods,
it cannot be the cause of sinful pleasure;
but when it turns toward evil, or with more zeal
or less than it should pursues the good,
the creature works against its maker.
From this you can understand that it must be
love that is the seed in you of every virtue
and of every action that deserves punishment.
Now, because never can, from the well-being
of its subject, love turn its face,
all things are safe from hatred of themselves;
and because one cannot conceive as divided,
and self-subsisting, any being from the First,
every effect is barred from hating him.
It remains, if I judge rightly in making these distinctions,
that the evil loved is one's neighbor's; and this
love arises in three ways in your clay.
There is one who, through his neighbor's being brought low,
hopes for eminence, and for this alone desires
that he be cast down from his greatness;
there is one who power, favor, honor, and fame
fears to lose because another rises,
and therefore grieves so much that he loves the opposite;
and there is one who seems to take offense at an injury,
so that he becomes greedy for revenge,
and such a person must press for another's harm.
This threefold love down here below
is lamented: now I wish you to understand the other,
which pursues the good in a disordered way.
Everyone dimly apprehends a good
in which the soul may rest, and desires it;
therefore everyone strives to attain it.
If sluggish love draws you to see it
or to attain it, this terrace,
after just repentance, torments you for it.
There is another good that does not make man happy;
it is not happiness, it is not the good
essence, the fruit and root of every good.
The love that abandons itself too much to it,
is lamented above us in three circles;
but how its division into three is explained,
I leave unsaid, so that you may seek it out for yourself”.
