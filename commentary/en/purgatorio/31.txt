“O you who are beyond the sacred river”,
turning her speech upon me with its point,
which even edgewise had seemed sharp to me,
she began again, continuing without delay,
“say, say if this is true: to so great an accusation
your confession must be joined”.
My power was so confounded,
that my voice stirred, and sooner died away
than it could be released from its organs.
She waited little; then said: “What are you thinking?
Answer me; for the sorrowful memories
in you have not yet been impaired by the water”.
Confusion and fear mingled together
drove such a “yes” out of my mouth,
that to understand it eyes were needed.
As a crossbow breaks, when it is shot
with too much tension, both its cord and bow,
and with less force the shaft strikes the mark,
so I broke beneath that heavy burden,
pouring forth tears and sighs,
and my voice weakened along its passage.
Then she to me: “Amid desires for me,
which led you to love the good
beyond which there is nothing to aspire to,
what ditches laid across your way or what chains
did you find, that of going onward
you should thus strip yourself of hope?
And what ease or what advantages
appeared upon the face of other things,
that you should court them instead?”.
After drawing a bitter sigh,
I scarcely had the voice that answered,
and my lips formed it with difficulty.
Weeping, I said: “Present things
with their false pleasure turned my steps,
as soon as your face was hidden”.
And she: “If you kept silent or denied
what you confess, no less known would be
your fault: by such a judge is it known!
But when from one's own cheek bursts forth
the accusation of sin, in our court
the wheel turns itself against the cutting edge.
Nevertheless, so that you may now bear shame
for your error, and so that another time,
hearing the sirens, you may be stronger,
lay down the seed of weeping and listen:
thus you will hear how in the opposite direction
my buried flesh should have moved you.
Never did nature or art present to you
pleasure as great as the lovely limbs in which I
was enclosed, and which are scattered in the earth;
and if the highest pleasure thus failed you
through my death, what mortal thing
should then have drawn you into desire for it?
Indeed you ought, at the first arrow
of deceptive things, to have risen upward
behind me, who was no longer such.
Neither should your wings have been weighed downward,
to await another blow, by some young girl
or another novelty of such brief enjoyment.
A young little bird waits for two or three;
but before the eyes of feathered birds
a net is spread in vain, or an arrow is shot”.
As children, silent in their shame,
stand with their eyes upon the ground, listening
and acknowledging their fault and repentant,
so I stood; and she said: “Since
through hearing you are sorrowful, lift your beard,
and you will take on greater sorrow by looking”.
With less resistance is uprooted
a sturdy oak, whether by our own wind
or by that from the land of Iarbas,
than I raised my chin at her command;
and when by the beard she asked for my face,
well did I recognize the sting of her meaning.
And as my face lifted,
those first-created beings resting
from their scattering my eye perceived;
and my eyes, still unsure,
saw Beatrice turned toward the beast
that is a single person in two natures.
Beneath her veil and beyond the river
she seemed to me to surpass her former self more
than she surpassed the others here, when she was here.
The nettle of repentance so stung me there,
that of all other things, whichever had turned me
most toward loving it became most hateful to me.
Such recognition bit into my heart,
that I fell overcome; and what I then became,
she knows who gave me the cause.
Then, when my heart restored strength to my limbs,
the woman whom I had found alone
I saw above me, and she said: “Hold me, hold me!”.
She had drawn me into the river up to my throat,
and pulling me behind her she went
over the water, light as a skiff.
When I was near the blessed bank,
‘You will sprinkle me’ was heard so sweetly,
that I cannot recall it, much less write it.
The beautiful woman opened her arms;
she clasped my head and submerged me
where I had to swallow the water.
Then she lifted me out, and, wet, presented me
within the dance of the four lovely women;
and each covered me with her arm.
“We are nymphs here and stars in heaven;
before Beatrice descended into the world,
we were appointed to her as her handmaids.
We shall lead you to her eyes; but in the joyful
light that is within, yours will be sharpened by
the three over there, who gaze more deeply”.
Thus singing they began; and then
they led me with them to the griffin's breast,
where Beatrice stood turned toward us.
They said: “See that you do not spare your eyes;
we have placed you before the emeralds
from which Love once shot his weapons at you”.
A thousand desires hotter than flame
bound my eyes to the shining eyes,
which remained fixed upon the griffin.
As the sun in a mirror, in no other way
the double beast shone within them,
now with one, now with another aspect.
Think, reader, whether I marveled,
when I saw the thing itself remain still,
and in its image it was changing.
While, full of wonder and joyful,
my soul was tasting that food
which, satisfying with itself, makes one thirst for itself,
showing themselves to be of a higher order
in their bearing, the other three came forward,
dancing to their angelic song.
“Turn, Beatrice, turn your holy eyes”,
was their song, “toward your faithful one
who, to see you, has taken so many steps!
By grace grant us the grace that you unveil
your mouth to him, so that he may discern
the second beauty that you conceal”.
O splendor of living eternal light,
who grew so pale beneath the shade
of Parnassus, or drank so deeply from its well,
that he would not seem to have his mind obstructed,
attempting to render you as you appeared
there where heaven in harmony overshadows you,
when in the open air you unveiled yourself?
