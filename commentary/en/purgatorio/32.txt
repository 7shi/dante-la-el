So fixed and intent were my eyes
on satisfying their ten-year thirst,
that all my other senses were extinguished.
And on either side they had a wall
of indifference—so the holy smile
drew them to itself with the ancient net!—;
when my face was forcibly turned
toward my left by those goddesses,
because I heard from them a “Too fixed!”;
and the condition for seeing that exists
in eyes just struck by the sun
left me for a while without sight.
But after my sight readjusted to the lesser light
(and I say ‘the lesser’ in relation to the great
splendor from which I was forcibly turned),
I saw that to the right had turned
the glorious army, and was returning
with the sun and the seven flames before its face.
As beneath their shields, to save themselves,
a troop turns, and wheels with its standard,
before the whole of it can change direction;
that army of the heavenly kingdom
which went before us had all passed by
before the chariot turned its foremost beam.
Then the women returned to the wheels,
and the griffin moved the blessed burden
so that not a feather of his stirred thereby.
The lovely woman who drew me across the ford
and Statius and I followed the wheel
that traced its track in the smaller arc.
As we walked thus through the lofty forest, empty
through the fault of her who believed the serpent,
an angelic song kept time for our steps.
Perhaps in three flights as much space was covered
by a released arrow as we had
moved away, when Beatrice descended.
I heard everyone murmur “Adam”;
then they encircled a tree stripped
of leaves and other foliage on every branch.
Its crown, which spreads out so much
the more the higher it rises, would by the Indians
in their forests be admired for its height.
“Blessed are you, griffin, who do not tear
with your beak from this wood, sweet to the taste,
since the belly twists painfully from it.”
Thus around the mighty tree
the others cried; and the two-natured creature:
“Thus is preserved the seed of every righteous person.”
And turning to the pole that he had drawn,
he drew it to the foot of the widowed tree,
and left that which came from her bound to her.
As our plants, when falls
down the great light mingled with that
which shines behind the heavenly Fish,
swell, and then is renewed
in its own color each one, before the sun
yokes his coursers beneath another star;
less than that of roses and more than that of violets
unfolding its color, the tree renewed itself,
which before had its branches so bare.
I did not understand it, nor is sung here
the hymn that those people sang then,
nor did I endure its melody to the end.
If I could portray how they fell asleep,
the merciless eyes, hearing of Syrinx,
the eyes whose constant watchfulness cost them so dear;
like a painter who paints from a model,
I would depict how I fell asleep;
but let whoever will portray falling asleep well.
Therefore I pass on to when I awoke,
and say that a radiance tore apart for me the veil
of sleep, and a call: “Rise: what are you doing?”.
Just as, to see some blossoms of the apple tree
that makes the angels hunger for its fruit
and makes perpetual marriage feasts in heaven,
Peter and John and James, led
and overcome, came back to themselves at the word
by which greater sleeps had been broken,
and saw their company diminished
by Moses as well as by Elijah,
and their master's robe changed;
so I came to myself, and saw that kindly woman
standing over me who had been the guide
of my steps along the river before.
And full of uncertainty I said: “Where is Beatrice?”.
At which she said: “See her beneath the foliage
newly grown, sitting upon its root.
See the company that surrounds her:
the others are going upward after the griffin
with a sweeter and more profound song.”
And if her speech went on further,
I do not know, because already in my eyes was
she who had closed me to all other attention.
Alone she sat upon the bare earth,
like a guard left there over the chariot
that I saw the two-formed beast bind.
In a circle they made of themselves an enclosure around her,
the seven nymphs, with those lights in their hands
that are safe from the north wind and the south.
“Here you will be a forest-dweller a little while;
and you will be with me forever a citizen
of that Rome of which Christ is Roman.
Therefore, for the benefit of the world that lives badly,
keep your eyes now on the chariot, and what you see,
having returned to the world beyond, make sure you write.”
Thus Beatrice; and I, who wholly at the feet
of her commands was devoted,
directed my mind and eyes where she wished.
Never did there descend with motion so swift
fire from a dense cloud, when it rains
from that region which lies most remote,
as I saw the bird of Jove descend
down through the tree, breaking its bark,
as well as its flowers and its new leaves;
and it struck the chariot with all its strength;
so that it lurched like a ship in a storm,
overcome by the waves, now to starboard, now to port.
Then I saw leap into the body
of the triumphal vehicle a fox
that seemed starved of every good food;
but, rebuking it for its foul sins,
my lady drove it into flight as swift
as its fleshless bones allowed.
Then, by the way along which it had first come,
I saw the eagle descend into the body
of the chariot and leave it feathered with itself;
And like one that comes from a grieving heart,
such a voice came from heaven and spoke thus:
“O my little ship, how badly you are laden!”.
Then it seemed to me that the earth opened
between the two wheels, and I saw a dragon emerge from it
that drove its tail upward through the chariot;
and like a wasp that withdraws its sting,
drawing its malignant tail toward itself,
it tore away part of the floor, and went wandering off.
What remained, as with couch grass
fertile earth, with the feathers, offered
perhaps with sound and kindly intent,
became covered, and covered with them were
both wheels and the pole, in so brief a time
that a sigh keeps the mouth open longer.
The holy structure, transformed in this way,
put forth heads from its different parts,
three above the pole and one at each corner.
The first were horned like an ox,
but the four had only one horn upon each forehead:
such a monster had never yet been seen.
Secure, like a fortress on a high mountain,
sitting upon it, an unrestrained prostitute
appeared to me, with her eyes alert on every side;
And as though to keep her from being taken from him,
I saw a giant standing upright beside her;
and from time to time they kissed each other.
But because her lustful and wandering eye
she turned toward me, that fierce lover
scourged her from her head down to her soles;
then, full of suspicion and fierce with anger,
he untied the monster and dragged it through the forest,
so far that the forest alone became a screen for me
against the prostitute and the new beast.
