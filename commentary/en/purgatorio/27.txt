Just as when it casts its first rays
there where its Maker shed his blood,
with the Ebro falling beneath lofty Libra,
and the waves in the Ganges scorched by noonday,
so stood the sun; hence the day was departing,
when the angel of God appeared to us, glad.
Outside the flame he stood upon the bank,
and was singing ‘Blessed are the pure in heart!’
in a voice far more alive than ours.
Then “One goes no farther unless first bites,
holy souls, the fire: enter into it,
and to the singing beyond do not be deaf”,
he told us as we came near him;
at which I became such, when I heard him,
as is one who is laid in the grave.
Over my clasped hands I leaned forward,
looking at the fire and vividly imagining
human bodies I had already seen aflame.
My good guides turned toward me;
and Virgil said to me: “My son,
here there may be torment, but not death.
Remember, remember! And if I
upon Geryon guided you safely,
what shall I do now nearer to God?
Believe for certain that if within the belly
of this flame you stood a full thousand years,
it could not rob you of a single hair.
And if perhaps you think that I deceive you,
draw near to it, and have proof made
with your own hands upon the hem of your clothes.
Put down now, put down every fear;
turn this way and come: enter safely!”.
And I still motionless, and against conscience.
When he saw me still standing firm and stubborn,
a little troubled, he said: “Now see, son:
between Beatrice and you is this wall”.
As at the name of Thisbe he opened his eyes,
Pyramus, at the point of death, and looked at her again,
when the mulberry became crimson;
so, my hardness having become soft,
I turned to my wise leader, hearing the name
that ever wells up in my mind.
At which he shook his head and said: “What!
Do we wish to stay on this side?”; then he smiled
as one does at a child won over by an apple.
Then into the fire he went before me,
asking Statius to come behind,
who before, for a long stretch of road, had separated us.
As soon as I was inside, into boiling glass
I would have thrown myself to cool down,
so measureless was the burning there.
My sweet father, to comfort me,
kept speaking only of Beatrice as he went,
saying: “Already I seem to see her eyes”.
A voice was guiding us that sang
from beyond; and we, intent on it alone,
came out where the ascent began.
‘Come, you blessed of my Father’,
sounded within a light that was there,
such that it overwhelmed me and I could not look at it.
“The sun is departing”, it added, “and evening comes;
do not stop, but hasten your steps,
while the west has not yet turned black”.
Straight upward ran the path within the rock
toward such a quarter that I blocked the rays
before me of the sun that was already low.
And we had tried but a few steps,
when the sun's setting, by the shadow that faded,
we sensed behind us, both I and my wise guides.
And before in all its immense regions
the horizon had become of one aspect,
and night had all its allotted realms,
each of us made a bed of a step;
for the nature of the mountain had broken in us
the power to climb farther and the delight in it.
As they remain meekly ruminating,
the goats, having been swift and unruly
upon the peaks before they have fed,
silent in the shade, while the sun blazes,
watched by the shepherd, who upon his staff
has leaned and tends them in their rest;
and as the herdsman who lodges outdoors
spends the night quietly beside his flock,
watching lest a wild beast scatter it;
such were all three of us then,
I like a goat, and they like shepherds,
enclosed on either side by high rock.
Little of the outside could be seen there;
but, through that little, I saw the stars
both brighter and larger than their wont.
Thus ruminating and thus gazing at them,
sleep took me; the sleep that often,
before the event occurs, knows the news.
At the hour, I believe, when from the east
Cytherea first shone upon the mountain,
who seems ever burning with the fire of love,
young and beautiful, in a dream I seemed
to see a lady walking through a meadow,
gathering flowers; and singing she said:
“Let whoever asks my name know
that I am Leah, and go moving about
my beautiful hands to make myself a garland.
To please myself in the mirror, here I adorn myself;
but my sister Rachel never turns away
from her mirror, and sits all day.
She is as eager to see her beautiful eyes
as I to adorn myself with my hands;
seeing satisfies her, and working satisfies me”.
And already before the splendors preceding dawn,
which rise the more welcome to pilgrims,
the less far from home they lodge on their return,
the darkness was fleeing on every side,
and my sleep with it; hence I rose,
seeing the great masters already risen.
“That sweet fruit which through so many branches
the striving of mortals goes seeking,
today will bring your hungers to peace”.
Virgil toward me these very
words spoke; and never were there gifts
that equaled these in delight.
So much desire upon desire came over me
to be above, that then at every step
I felt my feathers growing for flight.
When the stairway wholly beneath us
had been traversed and we were upon the highest step,
Virgil fixed his eyes upon me,
and said: “The temporal fire and the eternal
you have seen, son; and you have come to a place
where by myself I discern no farther.
I have brought you here with intelligence and skill;
now take your own pleasure as your guide;
you are beyond the steep paths, beyond the narrow ones.
See the sun that shines upon your forehead;
see the grasses, the flowers, and the little shrubs
that here the earth produces entirely of itself.
Until those beautiful eyes come, glad,
which, weeping, made me come to you,
you may sit and you may walk among them.
Expect no more my word or my signal;
free, upright, and sound is your will,
and it would be an error not to act according to its judgment:
therefore over yourself I crown and mitre you”.
