When the seven stars of the highest heaven,
which never knew either setting or rising
nor a veil of any mist other than guilt,
and which there made each one mindful
of his duty, as the lower one does
for whoever turns the helm to come to port,
stood fixed: the truthful company,
which had come first between the griffin and it,
turned toward the chariot as toward its peace;
and one of them, as though sent from heaven,
singing ‘Come, bride, from Lebanon’
cried out three times, and all the others after him.
As the blessed at the final summons
will rise swiftly, each from his cavern,
singing alleluia with reclothed voices,
so upon the divine carriage
rose a hundred, at the voice of so great an elder,
ministers and messengers of eternal life.
All were saying: ‘Blessed are you who come!’,
and throwing flowers above and all around,
‘Oh, give lilies with full hands!’.
I have seen before, at the beginning of day,
the eastern region all rosy,
and the rest of heaven adorned with fair serenity;
and the face of the sun rise shaded,
so that through the tempering of vapors
the eye could bear it for a long time:
so within a cloud of flowers
that rose from angelic hands
and fell back down within and without,
crowned with olive over a white veil,
a lady appeared to me, beneath a green mantle
clothed in the color of living flame.
And my spirit, for which already so much
time had passed since, in her presence,
it had last been overwhelmed, trembling, by awe,
without having further recognition through the eyes,
through a hidden power that came from her,
felt the great power of ancient love.
As soon as there struck me in my sight
the lofty power that had already pierced me
before I was out of childhood,
I turned to the left with the trust
with which a little child runs to his mother
when he is afraid or when he is distressed,
to say to Virgil: ‘Less than a dram
of blood remains in me that does not tremble:
I recognize the signs of the ancient flame’.
But Virgil had left us bereft
of himself, Virgil, sweetest father,
Virgil, to whom I entrusted myself for my salvation;
nor did all that the ancient mother lost
avail to keep my cheeks, washed with dew,
from growing dark again as I wept.
“Dante, because Virgil goes away,
do not weep yet, do not weep yet;
for you must weep for another sword”.
Like an admiral who at stern and prow
comes to see the people serving
aboard the other ships, and heartens them to do well;
upon the left side of the chariot,
when I turned at the sound of my name,
which of necessity is recorded here,
I saw the lady who had first appeared to me
veiled beneath the angelic celebration,
direct her eyes toward me on this side of the stream.
Although the veil that fell from her head,
encircled with the leaves of Minerva,
did not allow her to appear clearly,
still regally stern in her bearing,
she continued like one who speaks
and reserves the more burning words for later:
“Look well here! Indeed I am, indeed I am Beatrice.
How did you deign to ascend the mountain?
Did you not know that here humankind is happy?”.
My eyes fell down upon the clear spring;
but seeing myself in it, I drew them toward the grass,
so much shame weighed upon my brow.
So stern does a mother seem to her child,
as she seemed to me; for of bitterness
tastes the flavor of stern compassion.
She fell silent; and the angels sang
at once ‘In you, Lord, I have hoped’;
but beyond ‘my feet’ they did not go.
As snow among the living beams
along the spine of Italy freezes,
blown and packed by the Slavonian winds,
then, melted, trickles through itself,
if only the land that loses shadow breathes,
so that it seems like fire melting a candle;
so I was without tears and sighs
before the singing of those who always sing
following the notes of the eternal spheres;
but after I heard in their sweet harmonies
their compassion for me, as though they had said
‘Lady, why do you so torment him?’,
the ice that had gathered tightly around my heart,
became breath and water, and with anguish
through mouth and eyes came out from my breast.
She, still standing firm upon that same side
of the chariot, to the compassionate beings
then turned her words thus:
“You keep watch in the eternal day,
so that neither night nor sleep steals from you
any step the world takes along its ways;
therefore my reply is made with greater care
that he who weeps beyond may understand me,
so that guilt and grief may be of one measure.
Not only through the working of the great spheres,
which direct each seed toward some end
according to the stars that are its companions,
but through the bounty of divine graces,
which have such lofty vapors for their rain
that our sight does not come near them,
this man was such in his early life
in potential, that every good disposition
would have achieved a wonderful result in him.
But so much more harmful and more wild
does the soil become with bad seed and left untilled,
the more it possesses good earthly vigor.
For a time I sustained him with my face:
showing my youthful eyes to him,
I led him with me, turned in the right direction.
As soon as I was upon the threshold
of my second age and changed life,
this man withdrew from me and gave himself to another.
When I had risen from flesh to spirit,
and beauty and virtue had increased in me,
I was less dear and less pleasing to him;
and he turned his steps along an untrue way,
following false images of good,
which fulfill no promise completely.
Nor did it avail me to obtain inspirations,
with which both in dreams and otherwise
I called him back: so little did he care for them!
So low he fell that all means
for his salvation were already insufficient,
except showing him the lost people.
For this I visited the doorway of the dead,
and to him who has led him up here,
my prayers, weeping, were offered.
The high decree of God would be broken,
if Lethe were crossed and such nourishment
were tasted without any payment
of repentance that sheds tears”.
