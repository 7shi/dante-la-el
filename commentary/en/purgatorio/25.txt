It was an hour when the ascent allowed no hindrance;
for the sun had the meridian circle
left to Taurus, and the night to Scorpio:
therefore, like a man who does not stop
but goes his way, whatever may appear to him,
if the spur of need pierces him,
so we entered through the narrow passage,
one before the other taking the stair
that by its narrowness separates those who climb.
And like the young stork that raises its wing
from a desire to fly, and does not dare
to abandon the nest, and lowers it again;
so was I, with desire kindled and extinguished
to ask, coming as far as the gesture
made by someone preparing to speak.
Nor did he refrain, though our pace was swift,
my gentle father, but said: “Let fly
the bow of speech, which you have drawn to the iron tip”.
Then confidently I opened my mouth
and began: “How can one grow thin
where the need for nourishment does not apply?”.
“If you remembered how Meleager
was consumed as a firebrand was consumed,
this would not be”, he said, “so difficult for you;
and if you considered how, at your sudden movement,
your image moves suddenly within the mirror,
what seems hard would seem soft to you.
But so that you may rest content in your desire,
here is Statius; and I call on him and beg
that he now be the healer of your wounds”.
“If I unfold to him the eternal vision”,
replied Statius, “here where you are,
let my inability to deny you excuse me”.
Then he began: “If my words,
son, your mind preserves and receives,
they will shed light for you on the how that you ask.
Perfect blood, which then is not drunk
by the thirsty veins, and remains
like food that you remove from the table,
acquires in the heart, for all the human limbs,
formative power, like that blood
which goes through the veins to become those limbs.
Further digested, it descends where it is more seemly
to be silent than to speak; and from there then trickles
upon another's blood in the natural vessel.
There the one and the other come together,
the one disposed to undergo, and the other to act
because of the perfect place from which it is pressed;
and, joined to it, begins to work,
first coagulating, and then gives life to
what it caused to solidify as its material.
The active power having become a soul
like that of a plant, differing in this respect,
that this is on its way and that already at the shore,
it then works so much that it already moves and feels,
like a sea sponge; and then undertakes
to furnish with organs the powers of which it is the seed.
Now unfolds, my son, now extends
the power that comes from the begetter's heart,
where nature attends to all the limbs.
But how from an animal it becomes a speaking being,
you do not yet see: this is such a point
that it once led one wiser than you astray,
so that in his teaching he made separate
from the soul the possible intellect,
because he saw no organ employed by it.
Open your breast to the truth that is coming;
and know that, as soon as in the fetus
the articulation of the brain is complete,
the First Mover turns joyfully toward it
over so great a work of nature, and breathes
a new spirit, filled with power,
which draws whatever it finds active there
into its own substance, and becomes a single soul,
which lives and feels and turns itself upon itself.
And so that you may marvel less at these words,
consider the sun's heat, which becomes wine,
joined to the moisture that flows from the vine.
When Lachesis has no more flax,
it is loosed from the flesh, and potentially
carries away with it both the human and the divine:
all the other powers entirely mute;
memory, intelligence, and will
in action far keener than before.
Without stopping, of itself it falls
wondrously to one of the shores;
there it first knows its paths.
As soon as a place there circumscribes it,
the formative power radiates around
in the same manner and measure as in the living limbs.
And as the air, when it is heavy with moisture,
through another's ray reflected within it,
becomes adorned with various colors;
so the neighboring air there arranges itself
and in that form which upon it impresses
by its power the soul that has halted there;
and then, like the little flame
that follows the fire wherever it moves,
its new form follows the spirit.
Because from this it thereafter has its appearance,
it is called a shade; and from this it then gives organs to
every sense, even that of sight.
Through this we speak and through this we laugh;
through this we make the tears and sighs
that you may have heard along the mountain.
According as desires afflict us
and the other emotions, the shade takes shape;
and this is the cause of what you marvel at”.
And already to the last bend
we had come, and turned to the right,
and we were intent upon another concern.
There the cliff shoots flame outward,
and the terrace breathes a wind upward
that turns it back and keeps it away from the edge;
so that we had to go along the open side
one by one; and I feared the fire
on this side, and on that feared falling down.
My guide was saying: “Through this place
one must keep a tight rein upon the eyes,
because one could go astray through a small lapse”.
‘God of highest clemency’ within the heart
of the great burning I then heard sung,
which made me no less concerned to turn;
and I saw spirits walking through the flame;
so that I looked at them and at my steps,
dividing my gaze from time to time.
After the ending that is made to that hymn,
they cried aloud: ‘I know not a man’;
then they began the hymn again in low voices.
Having finished it, they also cried: “To the woods
Diana kept, and drove Elice out of them
who had felt the poison of Venus”.
Then they returned to singing; then women
they called out, and husbands who were chaste
as virtue and marriage enjoined.
And I believe this practice continues for them
throughout the time the fire burns them:
with such treatment and with such nourishment it is necessary
that the last wound should be sewn closed.
