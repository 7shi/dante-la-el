I had already departed from those shades,
and was following the footsteps of my guide,
when behind me, pointing a finger,
one cried: “Look how there seems not to shine
the ray from the left upon the lower one,
and how he seems to move like one alive!”
I turned my eyes at the sound of these words,
and I saw them looking in wonder
only at me, only at me, and at the light that was broken.
“Why does your mind become so entangled,”
said the master, “that you slacken your pace?
what does it matter to you what is whispered here?
Come behind me, and let the people talk:
stand like a firm tower, which does not shake
its summit ever at the blowing of winds;
for always the man in whom thought springs up
upon thought, distances the goal from himself,
because the force of one weakens the other.”
What could I answer, except “I am coming”?
I said it, somewhat suffused with the color
that sometimes makes a man worthy of forgiveness.
And meanwhile across the slope
people were coming a little ahead of us,
singing ‘Have mercy’ verse by verse.
When they realized that I gave no passage
through my body to the passing rays,
they changed their song into a long and hoarse “oh!”;
and two of them, acting as messengers,
ran to meet us and asked us:
“Make us acquainted with your condition.”
And my master: “You may go
and report to those who sent you
that this man's body is true flesh.
If they stopped because they saw his shadow,
as I suppose, they have been answered enough:
let them honor him, and it may prove dear to them.”
I did not see fiery vapors so swiftly
ever cleave the clear sky at nightfall,
nor, as the sun was setting, the clouds of August,
that these did not return above in less time;
and, arriving there, with the others they turned toward us,
like a troop that rushes without restraint.
“These people pressing toward us are many,
and they come to entreat you,” said the poet:
“but keep going, and listen as you go.”
“O soul who go your way to blessedness
with those limbs with which you were born,”
they came crying, “a little slow your step.
Look whether you have ever seen any of us,
so that you may carry news of him back there:
ah, why do you go? ah, why do you not stop?
We were all once put to death by violence,
and sinners until the final hour;
then the light of heaven made us aware,
so that, repenting and forgiving, out
of life we went, reconciled to God,
who pains our hearts with the desire to see him.”
And I: “Though I gaze upon your faces,
I recognize no one; but if you desire
anything within my power, spirits born to blessedness,
tell me, and I will do it, by that peace
which, behind the footsteps of such a guide,
I am made to seek from world to world.”
And one began: “Each of us trusts
in your kindness without your swearing it,
provided inability does not cut short your will.
Therefore I, who alone speak before the others,
entreat you, if ever you see that land
which lies between Romagna and the land of Charles,
that you be generous to me with your entreaties
in Fano, so that prayer may duly be offered for me
so that I may purge my grave offenses.
From there I came; but the deep wounds
from which flowed the blood in which I dwelt,
were dealt me in the land of the Antenori,
there where I thought myself most secure:
the man of Este had it done, who held me in hatred
far beyond what justice allowed.
But had I fled toward Mira,
when I was overtaken at Oriaco,
I would still be there where people breathe.
I ran to the marsh, and the reeds and mud
entangled me so that I fell; and there I saw
from my veins a lake forming on the ground.”
Then another said: “Ah, so may that desire
be fulfilled which draws you to the lofty mountain,
with kindly pity help fulfill mine!
I was of Montefeltro; I am Bonconte;
Giovanna or anyone else takes no care of me;
therefore I go among these with lowered brow.”
And I to him: “What force or what chance
led you so far astray from Campaldino,
that your burial place was never known?”
“Oh!” he replied, “at the foot of Casentino
a stream runs across, called the Archiano,
which rises above the Hermitage in the Apennines.
There where its name becomes in vain,
I arrived with my throat pierced,
fleeing on foot and bloodying the plain.
There I lost sight and speech;
in the name of Mary I ended, and there
I fell, and my flesh remained alone.
I shall tell the truth, and you repeat it among the living:
God's angel took me, and the one from hell
cried: “O you from heaven, why do you rob me?
You carry away this man's eternal part
for one little tear that takes him from me;
but I shall deal otherwise with the other!”
You know well how in the air there gathers
that moist vapor which turns back into water,
as soon as it rises where the cold seizes it.
He joined that evil will which seeks only evil
with his intellect, and stirred the mist and the wind
through the power that his nature gave.
Then, when the day was spent, the valley
from Pratomagno to the great ridge he covered
with mist; and he made the sky above overcast,
so that the laden air turned into water;
the rain fell, and into the ditches came
all of it that the earth could not absorb;
and as it joined together in the larger streams,
toward the royal river so swiftly
it rushed that nothing held it back.
My ice-cold body at the river's mouth
the raging Archiano found; and it thrust that
into the Arno, and loosened upon my breast the cross
that I made of myself when pain overcame me;
it rolled me along the banks and over the bottom,
then covered and encircled me with its spoils.”
“Ah, when you have returned to the world
and rested from the long journey,”
the third spirit continued after the second,
“remember me, who am Pia;
Siena made me; Maremma unmade me:
he knows it who, having first ringed me,
had married me with his jewel.”
