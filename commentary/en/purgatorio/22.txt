Already the angel had remained behind us,
the angel who had turned us toward the sixth circle,
having erased a stroke from my face;
and those who set their desire on justice
he had told us were blessed, and his words
with ‘sitiunt’, and nothing else, completed this.
And I, lighter than at the other passageways,
went onward, so that without any effort
I followed upward the swift spirits;
when Virgil began: “Love,
kindled by virtue, always kindled another,
provided that its flame appeared outwardly;
therefore, from the hour when among us descended
into the limbo of Hell Juvenal,
who made your affection known to me,
my goodwill toward you was such as
ever bound one most closely to a person unseen,
so that now these stairs will seem short to me.
But tell me, and as a friend forgive me
if too much confidence loosens my rein,
and as a friend now speak with me:
how could there find within your breast
a place for avarice, amid so much wisdom
as through your care you were filled with?”.
These words made Statius break
into a slight smile at first; then he replied:
“Every word of yours is a dear sign of love to me.
Yet often things appear
that give false grounds for doubt
because the true reasons are hidden.
Your question assures me that your belief
is that I was avaricious in the other life,
perhaps because of that circle where I was.
Now know that avarice was separated
too far from me, and this lack of measure
thousands of lunar cycles have punished.
And had I not directed my attention,
when I understood that passage where you cry,
almost angered at human nature:
‘Why do you not govern, O sacred hunger
for gold, the appetite of mortals?’,
I would suffer the wretched jousts, rolling burdens.
Then I perceived that too widely spreading their wings
the hands could be in spending, and I repented
of that as well as of my other evils.
How many will rise again with shorn hair
through ignorance, which of this fault
prevents repentance in life and at the last!
And know that the fault which counters
through direct opposition any sin,
together with it dries up its greenery here;
therefore, if I have been among those people
who weep for avarice, to purge myself,
it has happened to me for its opposite.”
“Now when you sang of the cruel warfare
of Jocasta's twofold sorrow,”
said the singer of the pastoral songs,
“from what Clio plays with you there,
it does not seem you had yet been made faithful
by the faith, without which doing good is not enough.
If that is so, what sun or what candles
dispelled your darkness so that you set
your sails thereafter behind the fisherman?”
And he to him: “You first sent me
toward Parnassus to drink in its caves,
and first enlightened me in following God.
You acted like someone who walks at night,
who carries the light behind and does not benefit himself,
but makes the people behind him knowledgeable,
when you said: ‘The age is renewed;
justice returns, and humanity's first age,
and a new offspring descends from heaven’.
Through you I became a poet, through you a Christian:
but so that you may better see what I sketch,
I will stretch out my hand to color it.
Already the whole world was pregnant
with the true belief, sown
by the messengers of the eternal kingdom;
and your word touched upon above
was in harmony with the new preachers;
therefore I adopted the practice of visiting them.
They then came to seem so holy to me,
that, when Domitian persecuted them,
their weeping was not without my tears;
and for as long as I remained over there,
I aided them, and their upright ways
made me despise all other sects.
And before I led the Greeks to the rivers
of Thebes in poetry, I received baptism;
but through fear I was a hidden Christian,
for a long time displaying paganism;
and this lukewarmness the fourth circle
made me circle for more than four hundred years.
You, therefore, who have lifted the covering
that hid from me all the good I speak of,
while we still have some climbing left,
tell me where our ancient Terence is,
Caecilius and Plautus and Varro, if you know:
tell me whether they are damned, and in what district.”
“These and Persius and I and many others”,
my guide replied, “are with that Greek
whom the Muses suckled more than any other,
in the first circle of the blind prison;
often we speak of the mountain
that always has our nurses with it.
Euripides is there with us, and Antiphon,
Simonides, Agathon, and many other
Greeks who once adorned their brows with laurel.
There among your people are seen
Antigone, Deiphyle and Argia,
and Ismene, as sorrowful as she was.
There is seen the woman who showed Langia;
there are the daughter of Tiresias, and Thetis,
and with her sisters Deidamia.”
Both poets were already silent,
once more intent on looking around,
free of stairs and walls;
and already four handmaids of the day had
remained behind, and the fifth was at the chariot's pole,
directing the burning horn ever upward,
when my guide said: “I believe that toward the edge
we must turn our right shoulders,
circling the mountain as we usually do.”
Thus custom was our guide there,
and we took the path with less uncertainty
because of that worthy soul's assent.
They went ahead, and I alone
behind, and I listened to their conversations,
which gave me understanding for composing poetry.
But soon the sweet conversation was interrupted by
a tree that we found in the middle of the path,
with fruit sweet and pleasant to smell;
and as a fir narrows upward
from branch to branch, so that one downward,
I believe, so that no person may climb up.
On the side where our path was blocked,
a clear liquid fell from the high rock
and spread over the leaves above.
The two poets approached the tree;
and a voice from within the foliage
cried: “Of this food you will have scarcity”.
Then it said: “Mary thought more about how
the wedding might be honorable and complete,
than about her mouth, which now intercedes for you.
And the ancient Roman women, for their drink,
were content with water; and Daniel
scorned food and gained wisdom.
The first age, which was as beautiful as gold,
made acorns savory through hunger,
and every stream nectar through thirst.
Honey and locusts were the foods
that nourished the Baptist in the wilderness;
for which he is glorious and as great
as is revealed to you through the Gospel.”
