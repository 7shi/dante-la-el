The natural thirst that never finds satisfaction
except with the water for which the humble woman
of Samaria asked the gift,
tormented me, and haste spurred me on
along the encumbered way behind my leader,
and I grieved at the just retribution.
And behold, just as Luke writes of it
that Christ appeared to the two who were on the road,
already risen out of the sepulchral hollow,
a shade appeared to us, and came behind us,
guarding from his feet the throng that lies there;
nor did we notice him until he first spoke,
saying: “O my brothers, may God give you peace”.
We turned at once, and Virgil
returned to him the gesture that befits such words.
Then he began: “In the blessed assembly
may the true court establish you in peace
which confines me to eternal exile”.
“How!”, he said, as meanwhile we walked swiftly:
“if you are shades whom God does not deem worthy above,
who has guided you so far up his stairway?”.
And my teacher: “If you look at the marks
that this man bears and that the angel traces,
you will clearly see that he must reign with the good.
But because she who spins day and night
had not yet drawn out for him the distaff
that Clotho sets for each and loads,
his soul, which is sister to yours and mine,
coming upward, could not come alone,
because it does not see in our manner.
Therefore I was drawn out of the vast throat
of Hell to show him, and I shall show him
farther, as far as my teaching can lead him.
But tell me, if you know, why such tremors
shook the mountain just now, and why all of it at once
it seemed to cry out down to its wave-washed feet”.
Thus, in asking, he struck me through the eye of the needle
of my desire, so that with hope alone
my thirst became less starved.
He began: “There is nothing that without
order the sacred rule experiences
of the mountain, or that falls outside its custom.
This place is free from every alteration:
what heaven receives into itself from itself
can be the cause here, and nothing else.
Therefore neither rain, nor hail, nor snow,
nor dew, nor frost falls any higher
than the short stairway of three steps;
clouds neither thick nor thin appear,
nor lightning, nor the daughter of Thaumas,
who over there often changes regions;
dry vapour rises no farther
than the top of the three steps of which I spoke,
where Peter's vicar has his feet.
Perhaps it trembles farther down, a little or much;
but because of wind that hides within the earth,
I know not how, up here it never trembled.
It trembles here when some soul cleansed
feels itself to be, so that it rises or moves
to ascend above; and such a cry follows.
Of cleansing, the will alone gives proof,
which, wholly free to change community,
takes hold of the soul, and willing delights it.
Before that it wills indeed, but the desire does not permit it
which divine justice, against the will,
sets upon torment, as once it was upon sin.
And I, who have lain in this suffering
five hundred years and more, only now felt
my will free for a better threshold:
therefore you felt the earthquake and the pious
spirits throughout the mountain render praise
to that Lord, that he may soon send them above”.
Thus he spoke to us; and because one enjoys
drinking as much as one's thirst is great,
I could not say how much good he did me.
And the wise leader: “Now I see the net
that entangles you here and how it is undone,
why it trembles here and in what you rejoice together.
Now who you were, may it please you that I know,
and why for so many centuries you have lain
here, let me gather from your words”.
“In the time when good Titus, with the help
of the highest king, avenged the wounds
from which flowed the blood sold by Judas,
Under the name that endures longest and most honours
I was over there”, replied that spirit,
“quite famous, but not yet possessed of faith.
So sweet was my singing spirit,
that, though from Toulouse, Rome drew me to herself,
where I earned the right to adorn my temples with myrtle.
Statius the people over there still call me:
I sang of Thebes, and then of great Achilles;
but I fell on the way with the second burden.
The sparks were seed for my ardour,
which warmed me, from the divine flame
by which more than a thousand are illuminated;
I speak of the Aeneid, which a mother
was to me, and was my nurse, in composing poetry:
without it I established nothing of a dram's weight.
And to have lived over there when
Virgil lived, I would consent to one sun
more than I owe before my departure from exile”.
These words turned Virgil toward me
with a look that, in silence, said ‘Be silent’;
but the power that wills cannot do everything;
for laughter and tears follow so closely
the emotion from which each springs,
that they obey the will less in the most truthful.
Yet I smiled like a man who gives a wink;
at which the shade fell silent, and looked at me
in the eyes, where expression fixes itself most;
and “So may you bring such labour to a happy end”,
he said, “why did your face just now
show me a flash of laughter?”.
Now I am caught on one side and the other:
one makes me silent, the other implores
me to speak; so I sigh, and am understood
by my master, and “Have no fear”,
he tells me, “of speaking; but speak and tell him
what he asks with such concern”.
And I: “Perhaps you marvel,
ancient spirit, at the smile I gave;
but I would have greater wonder seize you.
This one who guides my eyes on high,
is that Virgil from whom you drew
strength to sing of men and gods.
If you believed some other cause lay behind my smile,
dismiss it as untrue, and believe it to be
those words that you spoke of him”.
Already he was bending to embrace the feet
of my teacher, but he said to him: “Brother,
do not, for you are a shade and a shade you see”.
And he, rising: “Now you can the measure
understand of the love that warms me toward you,
when I forget our insubstantiality,
treating shades as a solid thing”.
