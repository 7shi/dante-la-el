While through the green foliage my eyes
I was peering, as is wont to do
one who wastes his life pursuing little birds,
he who was more than a father was saying to me: “My son,
come away now, for the time allotted to us
must be apportioned more usefully.”
I turned my face, and my steps no less promptly,
after the sages, who spoke in such a way
that they made walking cost me no effort.
And suddenly weeping and singing were heard
‘My lips, Lord’ in such a manner
that it brought forth delight and sorrow.
“O sweet father, what is this I hear?”
I began; and he: “Shades who go
perhaps untying the knot of their debt.”
Just as thoughtful pilgrims do,
overtaking strangers on the road,
who turn toward them and do not stop,
so, moving more swiftly, from behind us,
coming up and passing, gazed at us in wonder
a crowd of shades, silent and devout.
Each was dark and hollow about the eyes,
pale in the face, and so wasted
that the skin took its shape from the bones.
I do not believe that to such withered skin
Erysichthon was shriveled,
by fasting, when he feared it most.
I said within myself, pondering: ‘Behold
the people who lost Jerusalem,
when Mary sank her teeth into her son!’
The eye sockets seemed rings without gems:
whoever reads ‘omo’ in human faces
would clearly have recognized the M here.
Who would believe that the scent of an apple
could so reduce them, by arousing desire,
and that of water, without knowing how?
I was already wondering what so starves them,
since the cause was not yet clear
of their thinness and their wretched skin,
when suddenly, from the depths of its head,
a shade turned its eyes toward me and stared;
then cried aloud: “What grace is this for me?”
I would never have recognized him by his face;
but in his voice was revealed to me
what his appearance had effaced.
This spark fully rekindled in me
my recognition of the altered countenance,
and I recognized Forese's face.
“Ah, do not dwell on the dry scab
that discolors,” he pleaded, “my skin,
nor on whatever lack of flesh I have;
but tell me the truth about yourself, say who are those
two souls who escort you there;
do not hold back from speaking to me!”
“Your face, which I once wept over dead,
gives me no less sorrow now that makes me weep,”
I answered him, “seeing it so disfigured.
Therefore tell me, for God's sake, what strips you so;
do not make me speak while I am wondering,
for one full of another desire can scarcely speak.”
And he to me: “From the eternal counsel
power descends into the water and into the tree
left behind, through which I grow so thin.
All these people who sing while weeping
for indulging the appetite beyond measure,
in hunger and thirst here become holy again.
The longing to drink and eat is kindled in us
by the scent that rises from the fruit and from the spray
that spreads over its greenery.
And not just once, as this terrace
we circle, is our pain renewed:
I say pain, and I ought to say solace,
for that desire leads us to the trees
which led Christ willingly to say ‘Eli’,
when he freed us with his blood.”
And I to him: “Forese, since that day
on which you exchanged worlds for a better life,
five years have not yet passed.
If the power in you had first run out
to sin further, before the hour came
of the good sorrow that reunites us with God,
how have you already come up here?
I thought I would find you down below,
where time is made good by time.”
Then he to me: “So swiftly has brought me
to drink the sweet wormwood of these torments
my Nella with her abundant weeping.
With her devout prayers and with sighs
she has drawn me from the slope where one waits,
and has freed me from the other circles.
So much dearer to God and more beloved
is my little widow, whom I loved so much,
as she is more alone in doing good;
for the Barbagia of Sardinia is far
more modest in its women
than the Barbagia where I left her.
O sweet brother, what would you have me say?
A future time is already before my eyes,
to which this hour will not be very ancient,
in which from the pulpit it will be forbidden
to the shameless Florentine women
to go about displaying their breasts and bosoms.
What barbarian women, what Saracen women were there ever,
who needed, to make them go covered,
either spiritual or other discipline?
But if the shameless women were certain
of what swift heaven prepares for them,
they would already have their mouths open to howl;
for, if foresight here does not deceive me,
they will be grieving before down clothes the cheeks
of one who now is soothed with lullabies.
Ah, brother, now hide yourself from me no longer!
see that not only I, but all these people
gaze there where you veil the sun.”
Therefore I to him: “If you bring back to mind
what you were with me, and what I was with you,
remembering now will still be painful.
He turned me from that life,
who goes before me, the other day, when full
the sister of that one appeared to you,”
and I pointed to the sun; “he through the deep
night of the truly dead has led me
with this true flesh that follows him.
From there his encouragement has drawn me up,
climbing and circling the mountain
that straightens you whom the world made crooked.
He says he will keep me in his company so far
that I shall be where Beatrice will be;
there I must remain without him.
This is Virgil, who tells me so,”
and I pointed to him; “and this other is that shade
for whom every slope was lately shaken
by your kingdom, which sends him forth from itself.”
