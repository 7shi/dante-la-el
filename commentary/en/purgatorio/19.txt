At the hour when the heat of day can no longer
temper the cold of the moon,
overcome by the earth, and sometimes by Saturn
—when the geomancers their Greater Fortune
see in the east, before dawn,
rise along a path that remains dark for it only briefly—,
there came to me in a dream a stammering woman,
cross-eyed, and crooked upon her feet,
with maimed hands, and pale in complexion.
I gazed at her; and as the sun revives
the cold limbs that the night weighs down,
so my gaze made nimble for her
the tongue, and then straightened all of her
in a short while, and her faded face,
as love wills, so it gave color to.
When she had her speech thus loosened,
she began to sing so that with difficulty
would I have turned my attention from her.
“I am,” she sang, “I am a sweet siren,
who lead sailors astray in the midst of the sea;
so full of pleasure to hear am I!
I turned Ulysses, eager for his journey,
toward my song; and whoever grows accustomed to me,
rarely departs from me; so completely do I satisfy him!”.
Her mouth had not yet closed again,
when a woman appeared, holy and ready,
beside me, to put that other woman to shame.
“O Virgil, Virgil, who is this?”,
she said fiercely; and he came
with his eyes fixed only on that noble woman.
He seized the other, and opened her in front,
tearing her garments, and showed me her belly;
that woke me with the stench that came from it.
I opened my eyes, and the good master: “At least three
calls have I given you!”, he said, “Rise and come;
let us find the opening through which you may enter”.
I rose up, and already all were filled
with the broad daylight, the terraces of the holy mountain,
and we walked with the new sun at our backs.
Following him, I carried my brow
like one who has it laden with thought,
who makes of himself half an arch of a bridge;
when I heard “Come; here is the passage”
spoken in a manner gentle and kind,
such as is not heard in this mortal realm.
With wings outspread, which seemed a swan's,
he who had spoken thus to us directed us upward
between two walls of solid rock.
Then he moved his feathers and fanned us,
declaring “those who mourn” to be blessed,
for they shall have their souls endowed with consolation.
“What ails you, that you keep gazing toward the earth?”,
my guide began to say to me,
when we had both climbed a little above the angel.
And I: “With such misgiving does it make me go,
a recent vision that bends me toward itself,
so that I cannot withdraw from thinking of it”.
“You saw,” he said, “that ancient witch
who alone is now lamented above us;
you saw how a person frees himself from her.
Let that suffice, and strike your heels upon the ground;
turn your eyes toward the lure that
the eternal King swings with the mighty wheels”.
Like the falcon, which first looks at its feet,
then turns at the call and stretches forward
through desire for the food that draws it there,
so did I become; and so, through all the cleft
in the rock that gives passage to those going upward,
I went as far as where the circling path begins.
When I had emerged upon the fifth terrace,
I saw people upon it who were weeping,
lying on the ground, all turned face downward.
“My soul has clung to the pavement”
I heard them say with such deep sighs,
that the words could scarcely be understood.
“O chosen ones of God, whose sufferings
both justice and hope make less hard,
direct us toward the lofty ascents”.
“If you come exempt from lying,
and wish to find the way more quickly,
let your right sides always face outward”.
Thus the poet asked, and thus an answer
was given us a little ahead of us; at which I
by his speech marked the speaker otherwise hidden,
and turned my eyes to the eyes of my lord:
whereupon he granted me with a cheerful nod
what the look of my desire was asking.
When I could do with myself as I wished,
I drew up over that creature
whose words had first made me take notice,
saying: “Spirit in whom weeping ripens
that without which one cannot return to God,
suspend a little for me your greater care.
Who you were, and why you have your backs turned
upward, tell me, and whether you wish me to obtain for you
anything back there, whence I came still living”.
And he to me: “Why our backs
heaven turns toward itself, you shall learn; but first
know that I was Peter's successor.
Between Sestri and Chiavari descends
a lovely river, and from its name
the title of my lineage takes its proudest claim.
For a month and a little more I experienced how
the great mantle weighs on one who keeps it from the mud,
so that all other burdens seem like feathers.
My conversion, alas, was late;
but, as soon as I was made Roman shepherd,
so I discovered the deceitful life.
I saw that there the heart did not find rest,
nor could one rise higher in that life;
therefore love for this life was kindled in me.
Until that moment a wretched soul, estranged
from God, was I, wholly avaricious;
now, as you see, here I am punished for it.
What avarice does is here made clear
in the purgation of converted souls;
and the mountain has no punishment more bitter.
Just as our eye did not raise itself
on high, fixed upon earthly things,
so justice here plunged it toward the earth.
As avarice extinguished toward every good
our love, whereby the power to act was lost,
so justice here holds us fast,
bound and confined in feet and hands;
and for as long as it shall please the just Lord,
so long shall we remain motionless and outstretched”.
I had knelt down and wished to speak;
but as I began and he became aware,
merely by listening, of my reverence,
“What cause”, he said, “has bent you down so?”.
And I to him: “Because of your dignity
my conscience reproached me for standing upright”.
“Straighten your legs, rise up, brother!”,
he replied; “do not err: a fellow servant am I
with you and with the others under one authority.
If ever that holy Gospel utterance
which says “they shall not marry” you understood,
you can clearly see why I reason thus.
Go now: I do not wish you to linger longer;
for your staying hinders my weeping,
with which I ripen what you spoke of.
I have a niece over there whose name is Alagia,
good in herself, provided that our house
does not make her wicked by example;
and she alone remains to me over there”.
