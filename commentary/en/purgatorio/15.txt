As much as between the end of the third hour
and the beginning of day appears of the sphere
which always plays like a child,
so much, toward evening, there already seemed
to remain to the sun of its course;
it was afternoon there, and here it was midnight.
And the rays struck us in the middle of the nose,
because we had so circled the mountain,
that now we were walking straight toward the sunset,
when I felt my brow weighed down
by the brightness far more than before,
and the unknown things filled me with wonder;
so I raised my hands toward the top
of my brows, and made myself a shade,
which lessens the excess of visible light.
As when from water or a mirror
a ray leaps toward the opposite side,
rising upward in a manner equal
to that by which it descends, and departs as far
from the fall of a stone over an equal distance,
as experience and science show;
so I seemed by reflected light
there before me to be struck;
therefore my sight was quick to withdraw.
“What is that, sweet father, from which I cannot
shield my sight enough to help me,”
I said, “and which seems to be moving toward us?”
“Do not wonder if you are still dazzled
by the household of heaven,” he replied to me:
“it is a messenger who comes to invite us to ascend.
Soon it will be that seeing these things
will not be burdensome to you, but will be a delight
as great as nature fitted you to feel”.
Then, when we had reached the blessed angel,
with a glad voice he said: “Enter here
upon a stairway far less steep than the others.”
We were climbing, having already departed from there,
and ‘Blessed are the merciful!’ was
sung behind us, and ‘Rejoice, you who overcome!’.
My master and I, both alone,
were going upward; and I thought, as we went,
to gain profit from his words;
and I turned to him, asking thus:
“What did the spirit from Romagna mean,
when mentioning ‘exclusion’ and ‘sharing’?”
Therefore he said to me: “Of his greatest failing
he knows the harm; and so let no one wonder
if he rebukes us for it, so there may be less weeping.
Because your desires fix themselves
where a share is diminished by sharing,
envy works the bellows of your sighs.
But if love of the highest sphere
turned your desire upward,
that fear would not be in your breast;
for the more there are who say ‘ours’ there,
the more good each one possesses,
and burns with more charity in that cloister.”
“I am hungrier for satisfaction,”
I said, “than if nothing had been said to me before,
and I gather more doubt in my mind.
How can it be that one good, distributed
among more possessors, makes them richer
in itself than if it is possessed by few?”
And he to me: “Because you fix
your mind still upon earthly things,
you draw darkness from true light.
That infinite and ineffable good
which is up there, so hastens toward love
as a ray comes to a shining body.
It gives itself in proportion to the ardor it finds;
so that, however far charity extends,
the eternal goodness grows upon it.
And the more people there are who love up there,
the more good there is to love, and the more love there is,
and like a mirror each reflects it to the other.
And if my reasoning does not satisfy your hunger,
you will see Beatrice, and she fully
will take away this and every other longing from you.
Strive only that soon there may be erased,
as the two already are, the five wounds,
which close through sorrow”.
Just as I was about to say ‘You satisfy me’,
I found myself arrived upon the next terrace,
so that my eager eyes made me fall silent.
There I seemed into a vision
of ecstasy suddenly to be drawn,
and to see several people in a temple;
and a woman, at the entrance, with the manner
gentle of a mother, saying: “My son,
why have you acted this way toward us?
Behold, sorrowing, your father and I
were seeking you.” And as she fell silent here,
what had appeared before vanished.
Then another woman appeared to me, with those waters
down her cheeks which sorrow distills
when it arises from great anger at another,
and saying: “If you are lord of the city
over whose name there was such strife among the gods,
and from which every science sparkles forth,
avenge yourself upon those daring arms
which embraced our daughter, O Pisistratus.”
And the lord seemed to me, kindly and gentle,
to answer her with a composed face:
“What shall we do to one who wishes us harm,
if one who loves us is condemned by us?”,
Then I saw people kindled with the fire of wrath
killing a young man with stones, loudly
crying only to one another: “Kill him, kill him!”
And I saw him bowing down, because of the death
which already weighed upon him, toward the earth,
but of his eyes he continually made gates toward heaven,
praying to the high Lord, amid such violence,
that he would forgive his persecutors,
with that expression which unlocks compassion.
When my soul returned outward
to the things that are real outside it,
I recognized my not-false errors.
My guide, who could see me
acting like a man who frees himself from sleep,
said: “What ails you, that you cannot steady yourself,
but have come more than half a league
with your eyes veiled and your legs entangled,
like one whom wine or sleep bends down?”
“O my sweet father, if you listen to me,
I will tell you,” I said, “what appeared to me
when my legs were so taken from me.”
And he: “If you had a hundred masks
upon your face, they would not hide from me
your thoughts, however small.
What you saw was so that you do not excuse yourself
from opening your heart to the waters of peace
which are poured forth from the eternal fountain.
I did not ask “What ails you?” for the reason that prompts
one who looks only with the eye that does not see,
when the body lies bereft of soul;
but I asked to give strength to your feet:
thus must one spur the idle, slow
to use their wakefulness when it returns.”
We went on through the evening, intent
ahead as far as our eyes could reach
against the bright rays of the setting sun.
And behold, little by little, a smoke forming
toward us, dark as night;
nor was there any place to escape from it.
This deprived us of sight and pure air.
