“Who is this who circles our mountain
before death has given him flight,
and opens his eyes at will and closes them?”.
“I do not know who he is, but I know he is not alone;
ask him yourself, since you are nearer to him,
and gently, so that he may speak, welcome him”.
Thus two spirits, leaning toward one another,
were speaking of me there on the right;
then they raised their faces to address me;
and one said: “O soul who, still fixed
within the body, are making your way toward heaven,
for charity's sake console us and tell us
whence you come and who you are; for you make us
marvel so greatly at your grace,
as a thing that never was before must do”.
And I: “Through the midst of Tuscany stretches
a little river that rises on Falterona,
and a hundred miles of course do not satisfy it.
From its banks I bring this body:
to tell you who I am would be to speak in vain,
for my name does not yet resound very far”.
“If I rightly grasp your meaning
with my intellect,” then answered me
he who spoke before, “you speak of the Arno”.
And the other said to him: “Why did
this man conceal the name of that river,
just as one does with horrible things?”.
And the shade who had been asked this
thus discharged his debt: “I do not know; but fitting
indeed it is that the name of such a valley perish;
for from its source, where so swollen is
the rugged mountain chain from which Pelorus is severed,
that in few places does it rise beyond that height,
as far as where it yields itself to replenish
what heaven dries up from the sea,
whence rivers have what flows with them,
virtue is thus driven away as an enemy
by everyone, like a snake, either through the misfortune
of the place, or through evil custom that goads them:
therefore they have so changed their nature,
the inhabitants of the wretched valley,
that it seems Circe had them at pasture.
Among filthy pigs, more deserving of acorns
than of other food prepared for human use,
it first directs its impoverished course.
Then it finds curs, coming downstream,
more snarling than their strength warrants,
and disdainfully turns its muzzle away from them.
It goes descending; and the more it swells,
the more it finds dogs becoming wolves,
the accursed and ill-fated ditch.
Having then descended through many deep pools,
it finds the foxes so full of fraud,
that they fear no cunning that might catch them.
Nor will I refrain from speaking because another hears me;
and it will be well for this man, if he still remembers
what a truthful spirit unfolds to me.
I see your nephew becoming
a hunter of those wolves upon the bank
of the savage river, and he terrifies them all.
He sells their flesh while it is alive;
then he kills them like an old beast;
he deprives many of life and himself of honor.
Bloodstained he comes out of the dismal forest;
he leaves it such that a thousand years from now
it will not regrow to its former state”.
As at the announcement of grievous losses
the face of the one who listens is troubled,
from whatever quarter danger sinks its teeth into him,
so I saw the other soul, who, having turned,
stood to listen, grow troubled and sad,
after he had taken those words to heart.
The speech of one and the appearance of the other
made me eager to know their names,
and I asked them, mingling my question with entreaties;
whereupon the spirit who first spoke to me
began again: “You wish me to bring myself
to do for you what you will not do for me.
But since God wills that in you should shine
so much of his grace, I will not be sparing with you;
therefore know that I was Guido del Duca.
My blood was so scorched by envy,
that if it had seen a man becoming happy,
you would have seen me suffused with lividness.
From my own seed I reap such straw;
O human race, why do you set your heart
where exclusion of a fellow sharer is necessary?
This is Rinieri; this is the glory and the honor
of the house of Calboli, where no one
has since become heir to his worth.
And not his bloodline alone has been stripped,
between the Po and the mountain and the sea and the Reno,
of the good required for earnestness and for delight;
for within these boundaries it is filled
with poisonous thickets, so that slowly
would they now diminish through cultivation.
Where is the good Lizio, and Arrigo Mainardi?
Pier Traversaro and Guido di Carpigna?
O people of Romagna, turned into bastards!
When will a Fabbro take root again in Bologna?
when in Faenza a Bernardino di Fosco,
a noble shoot from humble grass?
Do not marvel if I weep, Tuscan,
when I remember, with Guido da Prata,
Ugolino d'Azzo, who lived among us,
Federigo Tignoso and his company,
the house of Traversara and the Anastagi
(and both the one line and the other are without heirs),
the ladies and the knights, the hardships and the ease
to which love and courtesy made us aspire
there where hearts have become so wicked.
O Bertinoro, why do you not flee away,
since your family has departed,
and many people, so as not to be wicked?
Bagnacavallo does well, for it produces no more offspring;
and Castrocaro does badly, and Conio worse,
which troubles itself further with begetting such counts.
The Pagani will do well, once the demon
of theirs departs; but not so that a pure
testimony of them may ever remain.
O Ugolino de' Fantolini, secure
is your name, since no longer is anyone expected
who could make it dark by degenerating.
But go your way now, Tuscan; for now it pleases me
far more to weep than to speak,
so tightly has our discourse gripped my mind”.
We knew that those dear souls
could hear us walking; therefore, by keeping silent,
they made us confident of the way.
Then, as we went on, we were left alone,
like lightning when it cleaves the air seemed
a voice that came toward us, saying:
‘Whoever finds me will kill me’;
and it fled like thunder that fades away,
if suddenly it bursts the cloud apart.
As soon as our hearing had respite from it,
and behold another with so great a crash,
that it resembled thunder following close upon it:
“I am Aglauros, who became stone”;
and then, to draw closer to the poet,
to the right, and not forward, I took a step.
Already the air was quiet on every side;
and he said to me: “That was the hard bit
that should keep a person within due bounds.
But you take the bait, so that the hook
of the ancient adversary draws you to itself;
and therefore restraint or summons is of little use.
Heaven calls you and revolves around you,
showing you its eternal beauties,
and your eye still looks only toward the earth;
therefore he who discerns everything chastises you”.
