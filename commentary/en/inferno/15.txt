Now one of the hard banks bears us along;
and the vapor of the stream casts shade above,
so that it shields the water and the banks from the fire.
As the Flemings between Wissant and Bruges,
fearing the tide that rushes toward them,
build their barrier to drive the sea back;
and as the Paduans along the Brenta,
to defend their villages and their castles,
before Carinthia feels the heat:
in that likeness these were made,
though neither so high nor so thick,
whoever he was, the master who made them.
Already we had drawn away from the wood
so far that I could not have seen where it was,
even if I had turned back,
when we met a troop of souls
coming along the bank, and each one
looked at us as is usual at evening
for one to gaze at another under a new moon;
and they so sharpened their gaze toward us
as an old tailor does at the eye of a needle.
Thus scrutinized by such a company,
I was recognized by one who took me
by the hem and cried: “What a marvel!”
And I, when he stretched his arm toward me,
fixed my eyes upon his scorched features,
so that his burned face did not prevent
my mind from recognizing him;
and lowering my hand toward his face,
I answered: “Are you here, ser Brunetto?”
And he: “O my son, let it not displease you
if Brunetto Latino a little way with you
turns back and lets the procession go on.”
I said to him: “I beg you for it as earnestly as I can;
and if you wish me to sit with you,
I will, if it pleases this one with whom I go.”
“O son,” he said, “whoever of this flock
stops for a moment must then lie a hundred years
without fending off the fire when it strikes him.
Therefore go on: I will follow at your skirts;
and then I will rejoin my band,
which goes lamenting its eternal woes.”
I did not dare descend from the path
to walk beside him; but my head bowed
I kept, like one who walks in reverence.
He began: “What fortune or destiny
brings you down here before your final day?
and who is this who shows you the way?”
“Up there above, in the serene life,”
I answered him, “I lost my way in a valley,
before my age had reached its fullness.
Only yesterday morning I turned my back on it:
this one appeared to me as I was returning to it,
and leads me home by this path.”
And he to me: “If you follow your star,
you cannot fail to reach a glorious harbor,
if I discerned rightly in the lovely life;
and if I had not died so early,
seeing heaven so favorable to you,
I would have given you encouragement in your work.
But that ungrateful, malicious people
that descended from Fiesole long ago,
and still retains something of the mountain and the rock,
will become your enemy because of your good deeds;
and rightly so, for among the bitter sorb trees
it is unfitting for the sweet fig to bear fruit.
An ancient report in the world calls them blind;
they are a people greedy, envious, and proud:
see that you keep yourself free of their ways.
Your fortune reserves so much honor for you,
that both the one party and the other will hunger
for you; but far from the beak shall the grass be.
Let the beasts of Fiesole make fodder
of themselves, and not touch the plant,
if any still springs up in their dung,
in which the holy seed lives again
of those Romans who remained there when
the nest of so much malice was made.”
“If my wish were wholly fulfilled,”
I answered him, “you would not yet be
banished from human life;
for fixed in my mind, and now grieving my heart, is
the dear and good paternal image
of you when in the world from time to time
you taught me how a man makes himself eternal:
and how grateful I am for it, as long as I live,
must be discerned in my words.
What you tell me of my course I write down,
and keep it to be explained with another text
by a lady who will know, if I reach her.
This much I wish to make clear to you,
provided that my conscience does not rebuke me,
that I am ready for Fortune, whatever she wills.
Such a pledge is not new to my ears:
therefore let Fortune turn her wheel
as she pleases, and the peasant his mattock.”
My master then over his cheek
on the right turned back and looked at me;
then said: “He listens well who takes note of it.”
Yet no less do I go on speaking
with ser Brunetto, and ask who are
his most renowned and eminent companions.
And he to me: “It is good to know of some;
of the others it will be praiseworthy to keep silent,
for time would be too short for so much telling.
In short, know that all were clerics
and great men of letters and of great fame,
stained in the world by one and the same sin.
Priscian goes with that sorrowful crowd,
and Francesco d'Accorso too; and seen there,
had you had a craving for such scurf,
you could have the man who by the servant of servants
was transferred from the Arno to the Bacchiglione,
where he left his sinfully strained sinews.
I would say more; but my walking and my speech
cannot be longer, because I see
new smoke rising there from the sand.
People are coming with whom I must not be.
Let my Treasure be commended to your care,
in which I still live, and I ask no more.”
Then he turned, and seemed one of those
who run at Verona for the green cloth
across the countryside; and among these he seemed
the one who wins, not the one who loses.
