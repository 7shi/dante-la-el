Silent, alone, without company
we went on, one in front and the other behind,
as Friars Minor go along the road.
Turned toward the fable of Aesop was
my thought because of the recent brawl,
where he spoke of the frog and the mouse;
for ‘now’ and ‘this instant’ are no more alike
than the one is to the other, if one rightly matches
beginning and end with an attentive mind.
And as one thought springs from another,
so from that one another then was born,
which doubled my first fear.
I was thinking thus: ‘These, because of us,
have been mocked with harm and with humiliation
so great that I believe it pains them much.
If anger is added to their ill will,
they will come after us more cruelly
than the dog after that hare which it seizes’.
Already I felt all my hairs bristling
with fear, and I kept my attention behind me,
when I said: “Master, if you do not hide
yourself and me quickly, I am afraid
of the Malebranche. We have them behind us already;
I imagine them so vividly that I already hear them.”
And he: “If I were made of lead-backed glass,
your outward image I would not draw
more quickly to myself than I receive the one within.
Just now your thoughts were coming among mine,
with like bearing and with like appearance,
so that from both I made a single plan.
If it is that the right bank slopes so
that we can descend into the next ditch,
we shall escape the imagined chase.”
He had not yet finished giving this advice,
when I saw them coming with wings outstretched
not very far away, intent on seizing us.
My guide suddenly seized me,
like the mother who is awakened by the noise
and sees the flames blazing close beside her,
who takes her child and flees and does not stop,
having more concern for him than for herself,
even long enough to put on a single shift;
and down from the crest of the hard bank
he gave himself, on his back, to the sloping rock,
which closes off one side of the next ditch.
Never did water run so swiftly through a channel
to turn the wheel of a land mill,
when it draws closest to the paddles,
as my master down that bank,
carrying me away upon his chest,
like his son, not like a companion.
Scarcely had his feet reached the bed
of the bottom below, when they were on the crest
directly above us; but there was no cause for fear:
for the high providence that willed them
to be placed as ministers of the fifth ditch,
takes from them all the power to leave it.
Down there we found a painted people
who went around with very slow steps,
weeping and in appearance weary and overcome.
They had cloaks with hoods pulled low
before their eyes, made in the fashion
that is used for the monks at Cluny.
On the outside they are gilded, so that it dazzles;
but within entirely lead, and so heavy,
that Frederick put them on made of straw.
Oh, mantle wearisome for eternity!
We turned once more to the left
together with them, intent on their sorrowful weeping;
but because of the weight those weary people
came so slowly that we had new
companions at every movement of the hip.
Therefore I to my guide: “See that you find
someone known by deed or by name,
and as you go, move your eyes about.”
And one who understood the Tuscan speech,
from behind us cried: “Stay your feet,
you who run so through the murky air!
Perhaps you will have from me what you ask.”
At this my guide turned and said: “Wait,
and then proceed according to his pace.”
I stopped, and saw two showing great eagerness
of spirit, in their faces, to be with me;
but the burden and the narrow way delayed them.
When they had reached us, with a very sidelong look
they gazed at me without saying a word;
then they turned to each other, and said among themselves:
“This man seems alive from the movement of his throat;
and if they are dead, by what privilege
do they go uncovered by the heavy robe?”
Then they said to me: “O Tuscan, who to the company
of wretched hypocrites have come,
do not disdain to say who you are.”
And I to them: “I was born and grew up
beside the fair river Arno, in the great city,
and I am with the body I have always had.
But who are you, from whom there trickles so much
grief, as I see, down your cheeks?
and what punishment is upon you that sparkles so?”
And one replied to me: “The yellow cloaks
are of lead so thick that the weights
make their scales creak so.
We were Jovial Friars, and Bolognese;
I Catalano and this man Loderingo
named, and chosen together by your city
as one solitary man is usually chosen,
to preserve its peace; and we were such,
that it still shows around the Gardingo.”
I began: “O friars, your sufferings . . . ”;
but I said no more, for my eye was caught by
one crucified to the ground with three stakes.
When he saw me, he writhed all over,
blowing into his beard with sighs;
and Friar Catalano, who noticed this,
said to me: “That one nailed down whom you gaze at,
advised the Pharisees that it was expedient
to subject one man to torment for the people.
He lies crosswise, naked, in the road,
as you see, and it is necessary that he feel
whoever passes, how much he weighs, first.
And in this fashion his father-in-law is tormented
in this ditch, and the others of the council
that was an evil seed for the Jews.”
Then I saw Virgil marvel
over him who was stretched out on a cross
so shamefully in eternal exile.
Then he addressed these words to the friar:
“May it not displease you, if you are permitted, to tell us
whether on the right there lies some passage
by which we both may get out,
without compelling any of the black angels
to come and take us out of this depth.”
He then replied: “More than you expect
a rock draws near that from the great circle
sets out and crosses all the savage valleys,
except that over this one it is broken and does not cover it;
you will be able to climb up by the wreckage,
which lies on the slope and rises above the bottom.”
My guide stood a while with his head bowed;
then said: “He gave a false account of the matter,
the one who hooks the sinners over there.”
And the friar: “I once heard it said in Bologna
of many vices of the devil, among which I heard
that he is a liar, and the father of falsehood.”
Then my guide went off with great strides,
his appearance somewhat troubled by anger;
so I departed from those burdened souls
following the tracks of those dear feet.
