“The banners of the king of Hell advance
toward us; therefore look ahead,”
said my master, “if you can make him out.”
As when a thick fog blows,
or when our hemisphere grows dark,
a windmill turned by the wind appears from afar,
such a structure I seemed to see then;
then because of the wind I drew back behind
my guide, for there was no other shelter there.
Already I was, and with fear I put it into verse,
where the shades were entirely covered,
and showed through like a straw in glass.
Some lie flat; others stand upright,
one on its head and another on its feet;
another, like a bow, bends its face back toward its feet.
When we had gone so far forward
that my master thought fit to show me
the creature that once had the lovely countenance,
he moved from before me and made me stop,
“Behold Dis,” he said, “and behold the place
where you must arm yourself with fortitude.”
How frozen and faint I then became,
do not ask, reader, for I do not write it,
because all speech would fall short.
I did not die, and I did not remain alive;
think now for yourself, if you have any spark of understanding,
what I became, deprived of both.
The emperor of the sorrowful kingdom
rose from the ice from mid-chest upward;
and I am closer in size to a giant
than giants are to his arms:
see now how great that whole must be
that corresponds to such a part.
If he was as beautiful as he is now ugly,
and raised his brows against his maker,
surely every sorrow must proceed from him.
Oh how great a wonder it seemed to me
when I saw three faces on his head!
One was in front, and that one was red;
the others were two, which joined this one
above the middle of each shoulder,
and joined one another at the place of the crest:
and the right one appeared between white and yellow;
the left, to look upon, was like those who
come from where the Nile flows down.
Beneath each came forth two great wings,
of a size befitting so great a bird:
I never saw such sails at sea.
They had no feathers, but of a bat
was their form; and he kept flapping them,
so that three winds arose from him:
therefore Cocytus froze entirely.
With six eyes he wept, and down three chins
dripped his tears and bloody foam.
From each mouth he was tearing with his teeth
a sinner, in the manner of a flax brake,
so that he thus made three of them suffer.
For the one in front the biting was nothing
compared with the clawing, for sometimes his back
was left entirely stripped of skin.
“That soul up there who has the greatest suffering,”
said my master, “is Judas Iscariot,
who has his head inside and flails his legs outside.
Of the other two who have their heads below,
the one hanging from the black muzzle is Brutus:
see how he writhes, and says not a word!
and the other is Cassius, who appears so powerfully built.
But night rises again, and now
it is time to depart, for we have seen everything.”
As he wished, I clasped his neck;
and he chose the time and place,
and when the wings were opened wide,
he fastened himself to the shaggy sides;
then from tuft to tuft he descended downward
between the thick hair and the frozen crusts.
When we were where the thigh
turns, right at the thick of the haunches,
my guide, with effort and with anguish,
he turned his head where he had had his legs,
and gripped the hair like a man climbing,
so that I thought I was returning again into Hell.
“Hold fast, for by such stairs,”
said my master, panting like a weary man,
“we must depart from so much evil.”
Then he came out through an opening in a rock
and set me down to sit upon its edge;
then he stepped cautiously toward me.
I raised my eyes and thought to see
Lucifer as I had left him,
and saw him holding his legs upward;
and if I then became bewildered,
let the ignorant judge it, who do not see
what point it was that I had passed.
“Rise up,” said my master, “to your feet:
the way is long and the path is rough,
and already the sun returns halfway to the third hour.”
It was no palace hall
where we were, but a natural cavern
that had rough ground and little light.
“Before I tear myself away from the abyss,
my master,” I said when I stood upright,
“speak to me a little to free me from error:
where is the ice? and how is this one fixed
so upside down? and how, in so short a time,
has the sun made its journey from evening to morning?”
And he to me: “You still imagine
yourself beyond the center, where I took hold
of the hair of the evil worm that pierces the world.
You were on that side as long as I descended;
when I turned, you passed the point
to which weights are drawn from every side.
And now you have come beneath the hemisphere
opposite to that which the great dry land
covers, and beneath whose highest point slain
was the man who was born and lived without sin;
you have your feet upon a small sphere
that forms the other face of Judecca.
Here it is morning when there it is evening;
and this one, who made himself a ladder for us with his hair,
is still fixed just as he was before.
From this side he fell down from heaven;
and the land, which formerly extended on this side,
for fear of him made a veil of the sea,
and came to our hemisphere; and perhaps
to flee from him left an empty place here
that land which appears on this side, and rushed upward.”
There is a place down there, remote from Beelzebub
as far as the tomb extends,
known not by sight, but by the sound
of a little stream that descends there
through the opening of a rock, which it has worn away,
with the course it winds along, and which slopes gently.
The guide and I by that hidden path
entered to return to the bright world;
and without taking thought for any rest,
we climbed upward, he first and I second,
until I saw some of the beautiful things
that heaven bears, through a round opening.
And from there we came out to see the stars again.
