In the time when Juno was angered
on Semele's account against the Theban blood,
as she showed on one occasion and another,
Athamas became so insane,
that, seeing his wife with two children
go burdened on either hand,
cried: “Let us spread the nets, so that I may catch
the lioness and the lion cubs at the pass”;
and then stretched out his pitiless claws,
seizing the one whose name was Learchus,
and swung him around and dashed him against a rock;
and she drowned herself with the other burden.
And when fortune cast down
the pride of the Trojans, which dared all things,
so that together with the kingdom the king was destroyed,
Hecuba, sorrowful, wretched, and captive,
after she saw Polyxena dead,
and of her Polydorus upon the shore
of the sea the sorrowful woman became aware,
out of her mind, she barked like a dog;
so greatly did grief twist her mind.
But neither Theban nor Trojan furies
were ever seen in anyone so cruel,
not even in goading beasts, let alone human limbs,
as I saw in two shades, pale and naked,
who ran about biting in the manner
of a pig when it is let out of the sty.
One reached Capocchio, and at the joint
of his neck sank its fangs into him, so that, dragging,
it made his belly scrape along the hard ground.
And the Aretine who remained, trembling,
said to me: “That goblin is Gianni Schicchi,
and he goes about rabid, treating others thus”.
“Oh”, I said to him, “may the other not sink
its teeth into you; let it not trouble you
to say who it is, before it darts away from here”.
And he to me: “That is the ancient soul
of wicked Myrrha, who became
to her father, outside rightful love, a lover.
She came thus to sin with him,
counterfeiting herself in another's form,
as the other who goes off there undertook,
to gain the lady of the herd,
to counterfeit Buoso Donati in himself,
making a will and giving the testament its form.”
And after the two rabid shades had passed,
on whom I had kept my eye,
I turned it back to look at the other ill-born souls.
I saw one, shaped like a lute,
if only he had had the groin
cut off from the other part that a man has forked.
The grievous dropsy, which so misshapes
the limbs with the fluid it ill converts,
that the face does not match the belly,
made him hold his lips open
as the consumptive does, who from thirst
turns one toward his chin and the other upward.
“O you who without any punishment are,
and I know not why, in this sorrowful world”,
he said to us, “look and attend
to the misery of Master Adam;
alive, I had enough of what I wished,
and now, alas, I crave a drop of water.
The little streams that from the green hills
of Casentino descend down into the Arno,
making their channels cool and moist,
they are always before me, and not in vain,
for their image dries me out far more
than the illness through which my face grows wasted.
The stern justice that torments me
draws cause from the place where I sinned
to send my sighs flying all the more.
There is Romena, there where I falsified
the alloy stamped with the Baptist;
for which I left my body burned above.
But if I saw here the wretched soul
of Guido or Alessandro or their brother,
I would not give up the sight for Fonte Branda.
One is already within, if the rabid
shades that go about speak truly;
but what good is it to me, whose limbs are bound?
If only I were still light enough
that I could move an inch in a hundred years,
I would already have set out along the path,
seeking him among this hideous company,
although it runs eleven miles around,
and is no less than half a mile across.
Because of them I am among such a household;
they induced me to strike the florins
that contained three carats of base metal.”
And I to him: “Who are the two wretches
who steam like wet hands in winter,
lying close together at your right side?”.
“I found them here—and since then they have not turned—”,
he answered, “when I rained down into this hollow,
and I do not believe they will for all eternity.
One is the false woman who accused Joseph;
the other is false Sinon, the Greek of Troy:
from acute fever they give off such a stench”.
And one of them, who took offense,
perhaps at being named so basely,
struck his hardened belly with his fist.
It sounded as though it were a drum;
and Master Adam struck his face
with his arm, which seemed no less hard,
saying to him: “Although I am deprived
of movement by my heavy limbs,
I have my arm free for such a task”.
At which he answered: “When you were going
to the fire, you did not have it so ready;
but you had it as ready and more when you were coining”.
And the dropsical one: “You speak truly of this:
but you were not so truthful a witness
there where at Troy you were asked for the truth”.
“If I spoke falsely, you too falsified the coinage,”
said Sinon; “and I am here for one offense,
and you for more than any other demon!”
“Remember, perjurer, the horse”,
answered the one who had the swollen belly;
“and may it torment you that all the world knows it!”.
“And may the thirst torment you that cracks”,
said the Greek, “your tongue, and the putrid water
that so hedges your belly before your eyes!”.
Then the coiner: “Thus gapes
your mouth to your own harm, as it is accustomed;
for if I have thirst and fluid stuffs me,
you have the burning fever and the head that aches,
and to lick the mirror of Narcissus,
you would not need many words of invitation”.
I was wholly intent on listening to them,
when the master said to me: “Now keep looking,
for I am very close to quarreling with you!”.
When I heard him speak to me in anger,
I turned toward him with such shame,
that even now it turns within my memory.
As one who dreams of his own harm,
who, dreaming, wishes he were dreaming,
so that he longs for what is to be as though it were not,
so I became, unable to speak,
for I desired to excuse myself, and was excusing
myself all the while, and did not believe I was doing so.
“Less shame washes away a greater fault”,
said the master, “than yours has been;
therefore unburden yourself of all sorrow.
And reckon that I am always at your side,
if ever again fortune brings you
where people are in a like dispute:
for wishing to hear such things is a base desire.”
