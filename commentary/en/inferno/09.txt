That color which cowardice painted on my face,
on seeing my guide turn back,
the sooner drove his new color back within.
He stopped intent, like a man who listens;
for his eye could not lead him far
through the black air and the thick mist.
“Yet we must win the struggle,”
he began, “unless . . . Such a one pledged us aid.
Oh, how long it seems to me before another comes here!”
I saw clearly how he covered
his opening words with those that followed,
which were words different from the first;
but nonetheless his speech gave me fear,
because I drew from the unfinished phrase
perhaps a worse meaning than it held.
“Into this bottom of the sorrowful hollow
does anyone ever descend from the first circle,
whose only punishment is hope cut off?”
This question I asked; and he: “Rarely
does it happen,” he answered me, “that any of us
makes the journey along which I go.
It is true that another time I was down here,
conjured by that cruel Erichtho
who recalled the shades to their bodies.
My flesh had been stripped of me only a short time,
when she made me enter within that wall,
to draw a spirit from the circle of Judas.
That is the lowest place and the darkest,
and the farthest from the heaven that turns all things:
well do I know the way; therefore be reassured.
This marsh that breathes the great stench
encircles the sorrowful city,
where now we cannot enter without wrath.”
And he said more, but I do not remember it;
because my eye had drawn me wholly
toward the high tower with its glowing summit,
where in an instant there suddenly stood upright
three infernal Furies stained with blood,
who had female limbs and bearing,
and were girdled with intensely green hydras;
little snakes and horned serpents they had for hair,
with which their savage temples were entwined.
And he, who well knew the handmaids
of the queen of eternal lamentation,
“Look,” he said to me, “at the fierce Erinyes.
This is Megaera on the left side;
she who weeps on the right is Alecto;
Tisiphone is in the middle”; and with that he fell silent.
With her nails each tore her breast;
they beat themselves with their palms and cried so loudly,
that I pressed close to the poet out of fear.
“Let Medusa come: then we shall turn him to stone,”
they all were saying, looking downward;
“we did ill not to avenge Theseus's assault.”
“Turn backward and keep your eyes shut;
for if the Gorgon shows herself and you should see her,
there would be no returning ever above.”
So spoke the master; and he himself
turned me, and did not so trust my hands
as not to cover my eyes with his as well.
O you who have sound intellects,
consider the teaching that conceals itself
beneath the veil of the strange verses.
And already there was coming over the turbid waves
a crashing sound, full of terror,
at which both shores trembled,
no different from that of a wind
made violent by opposing heats,
which strikes the forest and without any check
shatters the branches, beats them down and carries them away;
dust-laden, it advances proudly,
and makes the beasts and shepherds flee.
He uncovered my eyes and said: “Now direct the keenness
of your sight across that ancient foam,
toward where that smoke is most bitter.”
As the frogs before their enemy
the snake all scatter through the water,
until each huddles on the bottom,
I saw more than a thousand ruined souls
flee thus before one who, at a walking pace,
was crossing Styx with the soles of his feet dry.
From his face he brushed away that heavy air,
moving his left hand often before him;
and only with that discomfort did he seem weary.
Well did I perceive that he was sent from heaven,
and I turned to the master; and he made a sign
that I should remain quiet and bow to him.
Ah, how full of disdain he seemed to me!
He came to the gate and with a little wand
opened it, for there was no resistance.
“O you cast out of heaven, despised people,”
he began upon the horrible threshold,
“why does this insolence lodge in you?
Why do you kick against that will
whose end can never be cut short,
and which has many times increased your pain?
What good is it to butt against the fates?
Your Cerberus, if you remember well,
still bears his chin and throat stripped bare for it.”
Then he turned back along the filthy road,
and spoke no word to us, but had the look
of a man whom another care grips and gnaws
than that of the one who stands before him;
and we moved our feet toward the city,
reassured after the holy words.
We entered within it without any conflict;
and I, who had a desire to observe
the condition that such a fortress encloses,
as soon as I was within, cast my eye around:
and I see on every side a great plain,
full of grief and cruel torment.
Just as at Arles, where the Rhône forms stagnant waters,
just as at Pola, near the Quarnero,
which bounds Italy and bathes its borders,
the tombs make the whole place uneven,
so they did here on every side,
except that the manner here was more bitter;
for among the tombs flames were scattered,
by which they were so thoroughly heated,
that no craft requires iron hotter.
All their lids were raised,
and from them came such harsh laments,
that they clearly seemed those of the wretched and afflicted.
And I: “Master, who are those people
who, buried within those tombs,
make themselves heard with sorrowful sighs?”
And he to me: “Here are the founders of heresies
with their followers, of every sect, and much
more than you believe are the tombs laden.
Like is buried here with like,
and the tombs are hotter and less hot.”
And after he had turned to the right,
we passed between the torments and the high ramparts.
