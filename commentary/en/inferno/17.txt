“Behold the beast with the pointed tail,
that crosses mountains and breaks through walls and weapons!
Behold her who makes the whole world stink!”
Thus my guide began to speak to me;
and he beckoned her to come to the bank,
near the end of the stones we had walked upon.
And that foul image of fraud
came on, and brought her head and chest ashore,
but did not draw her tail onto the bank.
His face was the face of a righteous man,
so kindly was the skin he had on the outside,
and all the rest of his trunk was a serpent's;
he had two paws, hairy up to the armpits;
his back and chest and both his flanks
he had patterned with knots and little circles.
With more colors, in weave and overlay,
neither Tartars nor Turks ever made cloth,
nor were such webs ever laid on the loom by Arachne.
As sometimes barges lie at the shore,
partly in water and partly on land,
and as there among the gluttonous Germans
the beaver settles himself to wage his war,
so that most wicked beast lay
upon the rim that is of stone and bounds the sand.
In the void his whole tail was darting about,
twisting upward the venomous fork
that armed its tip like a scorpion's.
My guide said: “Now we must turn
our way a little toward that
wicked beast that lies over there.”
So we descended on the right-hand side,
and took ten steps along the edge,
to keep well clear of the sand and the flame.
And when we had come to her,
a little farther on I see upon the sand
people sitting near the edge of the abyss.
There my master, “So that a fully complete
experience of this round you may carry away,”
said to me, “go, and see their condition.
Let your conversations there be brief;
until you return, I will speak with this beast,
so that she may grant us her strong shoulders.”
Thus, still along the outermost edge
of that seventh circle, all alone
I went, where the sorrowful people sat.
Their anguish was bursting out through their eyes;
this way and that they sought relief with their hands
now against the fiery vapors, now against the hot ground:
No differently do dogs behave in summer
now with their muzzle, now with their paw, when they are bitten
by fleas or flies or horseflies.
When I turned my eyes upon the faces of some,
upon whom the painful fire falls,
I recognized none of them; but I noticed
that from each one's neck hung a pouch
that had a particular color and a particular emblem,
and on this their eyes seem to feed.
And as I come among them, looking about,
on a yellow purse I saw something blue
that had the face and bearing of a lion.
Then, as the chariot of my gaze moved on,
I saw another, red as blood,
displaying a goose whiter than butter.
And one who with a large blue sow
had his little white bag marked,
said to me: “What are you doing in this pit?
Now go away; and since you are still alive,
know that my neighbor Vitaliano
will sit here at my left side.
Among these Florentines I am a Paduan:
often they deafen my ears,
shouting: ‘Let the sovereign knight come,
who will bring the pouch with three goats!’”
Here he twisted his mouth and stuck out
his tongue, like an ox licking its nose.
And I, fearing that staying longer might anger
him who had warned me to stay but briefly,
turned back from those weary souls.
I found my guide, who had climbed
already onto the back of the savage animal,
and he said to me: “Now be strong and bold.
From now on we descend by stairs like these;
mount in front, for I wish to be between,
so that the tail cannot do harm.”
Like one who is so near the shivering fit
of quartan fever that his nails are already pale,
and he trembles all over merely looking at the shade,
so I became at the words spoken;
but shame made its threats to me,
which makes a servant brave before a good master.
I settled myself upon those great shoulders;
I did wish to speak, but my voice did not come
as I intended: ‘Be sure to hold me in your arms.’
But he, who had helped me once before
in another peril, as soon as I mounted
with his arms he encircled me and supported me;
and said: “Geryon, move now:
let the turns be wide, and the descent be slight;
consider the unusual burden that you bear.”
As a little boat moves out from its place
backward, backward, so he withdrew from there;
and when he felt himself entirely free,
he turned his tail to where his chest had been,
and, stretching it out, moved it like an eel,
and with his paws gathered the air toward himself.
Greater fear I do not believe there was
when Phaethon let go of the reins,
because of which the sky, as still appears, was scorched;
nor when wretched Icarus his back
felt losing its feathers because of the heated wax,
his father crying to him, “You are taking the wrong course!”,
than mine was, when I saw that I was
surrounded by air on every side, and saw extinguished
every sight except that of the beast.
He goes swimming slowly, slowly;
he wheels and descends, but I do not perceive it
except that the wind blows on my face and from below.
Already I could hear on my right the torrent
making a horrible roar beneath us,
so I stretch my head out, looking down.
Then I grew more fearful of the plunge,
because I saw fires and heard weeping;
so, trembling all over, I clench my thighs.
And then I saw, for I had not seen it before,
our descent and wheeling, by the great torments
that drew near from different sides.
As the falcon that has long been on the wing,
that, without seeing lure or bird,
makes the falconer say, “Alas, you are coming down!”,
descends weary to where it set out swiftly,
through a hundred turns, and settles far away
from its master, disdainful and sullen;
so Geryon set us down at the bottom,
at the very foot of the sheer rock,
and, having unloaded our persons,
he vanished like an arrow from a bowstring.
