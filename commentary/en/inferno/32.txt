If I had harsh and rasping rhymes,
as would be fitting for the dismal hole
upon which all the other rocks press,
I would press out the juice of my conception
more fully; but because I do not have them,
not without fear do I bring myself to speak;
for it is no undertaking to be taken lightly
to describe the bottom of the whole universe,
nor one for a tongue that says mamma or babbo.
But may those ladies help my verse
who helped Amphion to enclose Thebes,
so that the telling may not differ from the fact.
Oh rabble, ill-created beyond all others,
who dwell in the place of which it is hard to speak,
better had you been sheep or goats here!
When we were down in the dark well,
far lower than the giant's feet,
and I was still gazing at the high wall,
I heard someone say to me: “Watch how you step:
go so that you do not tread with your soles
upon the heads of the wretched, weary brothers.”
At this I turned, and saw before me
and beneath my feet a lake that through freezing
had the appearance of glass and not of water.
Never made so thick a veil upon its course
in winter the Danube in Austria,
nor the Don beneath the cold sky there,
as there was here; for if Tambernicchi
had fallen upon it, or Pietrapana,
it would not have made even the edge creak.
And as the frog sits croaking
with its snout out of the water, when dreams are dreamed
of gleaning often by the peasant woman,
livid, as far as where shame shows,
were the sorrowing shades within the ice,
setting their teeth to the note of a stork.
Each kept its face turned downward;
from the mouth the cold, and from the eyes the sorrowing heart
each obtains its testimony among them.
When I had looked about me a little,
I turned toward my feet, and saw two so close
that the hair of their heads was mingled together.
“Tell me, you who press your breasts so close,”
I said, “who are you?” And they bent their necks;
and when they had raised their faces toward me,
their eyes, which before were wet only within,
dripped over their lips, and the frost bound
the tears between them and sealed them shut again.
No bar ever bound wood to wood
so tightly; whereupon they, like two he-goats,
butted against each other, so greatly did anger overcome them.
And one who had lost both ears
to the cold, still with his face turned downward,
said: “Why do you gaze so intently at us?
If you wish to know who those two are,
the valley from which the Bisenzio descends
belonged to their father Alberto and to them.
From one body they came forth; and all Caina
you may search, and you will find no shade
more deserving of being fixed in jelly:
not he whose breast and shadow were pierced
by the selfsame blow from Arthur's hand;
not Focaccia; not this one who obstructs me
with his head so that I can see no farther,
and was named Sassol Mascheroni;
if you are Tuscan, you now know well who he was.
And so that you do not draw me into further talk,
know that I was Camiscion de' Pazzi;
and I await Carlin to exonerate me.”
Then I saw a thousand doglike faces
made so by the cold; whence a shudder comes over me,
and always will, at frozen pools.
And while we were going toward the center
at which all weight gathers,
and I was trembling in the eternal shade;
whether it was will or destiny or chance,
I do not know; but, walking among the heads,
I struck my foot hard against the face of one.
Weeping, he shouted at me: “Why do you trample me?
if you do not come to increase the vengeance
for Montaperti, why do you torment me?”
And I: “My master, wait for me here now,
so that I may resolve a doubt through this man;
then you may hurry me as much as you wish.”
My leader stopped, and I said to the one
who was still cursing fiercely:
“Who are you who so reproach others?”
“Now who are you who go through Antenora,
striking,” he replied, “the cheeks of others,
so that, if you were alive, it would be too much?”
“Alive I am, and it may please you,”
was my reply, “if you desire fame,
that I set your name among my other notes.”
And he to me: “The opposite is what I crave.
Get away from here and give me no more trouble,
for you know little how to flatter in this hollow!”
Then I seized him by the hair at the nape
and said: “You will have to name yourself,
or not a hair will remain on you up here.”
Then he to me: “Though you tear out my hair,
I will neither tell you who I am nor show you,
though you fall upon my head a thousand times.”
I already had his hair wound about my hand,
and had pulled out more than one tuft of it,
while he barked with his eyes held downward,
when another cried: “What is wrong with you, Bocca?
is it not enough for you to rattle your jaws,
unless you bark as well? what devil touches you?”
“Now,” I said, “I do not want you to speak further,
wicked traitor; for to your shame
I shall carry true reports of you.”
“Go away,” he replied, “and tell whatever you wish;
but do not keep silent, if you get out of here,
about him whose tongue was just now so ready.
He weeps here for the silver of the French:
‘I saw,’ you will be able to say, ‘the man from Duera
there where the sinners stay cold.’
If you should be asked ‘Who else was there?’,
you have beside you him of Beccheria
whose throat Florence cut.
Gianni de' Soldanieri I believe to be
farther on with Ganelon and Tebaldello,
who opened Faenza while people slept.”
We had already parted from him,
when I saw two frozen in a single hole,
so that one head was a cap for the other;
and as bread is eaten out of hunger,
so the upper one set his teeth into the other
there where the brain joins with the nape:
not otherwise did Tydeus gnaw
the temples of Menalippus in fury,
than this one did the skull and the other parts.
“O you who show by so bestial a sign
hatred toward him whom you devour,
tell me why,” I said, “on this condition,
that if you have just cause to complain of him,
knowing who you both are and his offense,
in the world above I may yet repay you for it,
if that with which I speak does not dry up.”
