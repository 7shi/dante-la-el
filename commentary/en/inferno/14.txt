Since love for my native place
constrained me, I gathered the scattered leaves
and returned them to him who was already hoarse.
Then we came to the boundary where is divided
the second ring from the third, and where
the dreadful art of justice is seen.
To make the strange new things quite clear,
I say that we arrived upon a plain
that excludes every plant from its bed.
The sorrowful wood is a garland about it
all around, as the dismal moat is about the wood;
here we halted our steps at the very edge.
The expanse was a dry and thick sand,
formed no differently from that
which once was trodden by Cato's feet.
O vengeance of God, how much you ought
to be feared by everyone who reads
what was revealed before my eyes!
I saw many flocks of naked souls
who all were weeping most miserably,
and different laws seemed laid upon them.
Some people lay supine upon the ground,
some sat all drawn together,
and others walked continually.
Those who went about were more numerous,
and those who lay in torment fewer,
but their tongues were looser in lament.
Over all the sandy waste, with a slow descent,
broad flakes of fire were raining,
like snow in the mountains without wind.
Just as Alexander in those hot regions
of India saw above his army
flames fall intact all the way to the ground,
so he took care to trample the ground
with his troops, so that the flame
might be better quenched while still alone:
so descended the eternal burning;
whereby the sand caught fire, like tinder
beneath a fire-steel, to double the pain.
Never at rest was the dance
of the wretched hands, now here, now there,
shaking off the freshly fallen fire.
I began: “Master, you who overcome
all things, except the stubborn demons
who came out against us at the entrance to the gate,
who is that great one who seems not to heed
the burning and lies scornful and grim,
so that the rain seems not to torment him?”
And that same man, who had noticed
that I was asking my guide about him,
cried: “As I was alive, so am I dead.
Though Jupiter wear out his smith, from whom
in anger he took the sharp thunderbolt
with which I was struck on my last day;
or though he wear out the others, shift by shift,
in Mongibello at the black forge,
crying ‘Good Vulcan, help, help!’,
just as he did at the battle of Phlegra,
and strike me with thunderbolts with all his strength:
he could have no joy of his vengeance.”
Then my guide spoke with such force
that I had never heard him speak so loudly:
“O Capaneus, in that there is not quenched
your pride, you are punished the more;
no torment, except your rage,
would be pain sufficient for your fury.”
Then he turned to me with a gentler countenance,
saying: “He was one of the seven kings
who besieged Thebes; and he held, and seems still to hold,
God in disdain, and seems to prize him little;
but, as I told him, his scorn
is a fitting ornament for his breast.
Now follow me, and take care not to set,
as yet, your feet upon the burning sand;
but always keep your feet close to the wood.”
In silence we came to where there gushes
out of the wood a little stream,
whose redness still makes me shudder.
As from the Bulicame a little stream issues
which the sinful women then divide among themselves,
so that one went down across the sand.
Its bed and both its sloping banks
were made of stone, and the margins alongside;
whereby I realized that the passage lay there.
“Among all the other things I have shown you,
since we entered through the gate
whose threshold is denied to no one,
no thing has been perceived by your eyes
as remarkable as the present stream,
which quenches every little flame above it.”
These were my guide's words;
wherefore I begged him to grant me the food
for which he had granted me the desire.
“In the midst of the sea lies a wasted land,”
he said then, “which is called Crete,
under whose king the world was once innocent.
There is a mountain there once glad
with water and foliage, which was called Ida;
now it is deserted like a thing outworn.
Rhea once chose it as a secure cradle
for her son, and to conceal him better,
when he cried, she had shouting raised there.
Within the mountain a great old man stands upright,
who keeps his shoulders turned toward Damietta
and looks at Rome as his mirror.
His head is formed of fine gold,
and his arms and breast are pure silver,
then he is of copper down to the fork;
from there downward he is all choice iron,
except that his right foot is baked clay;
and he stands upright on that one more than on the other.
Every part, except the gold, is broken
by a fissure that drips tears,
which, gathered together, pierce that cavern.
Their course plunges down into this valley;
they form Acheron, Styx, and Phlegethon;
then they go down through this narrow channel,
until, where there is no farther descent,
they form Cocytus; and what that pool is like
you will see, so it is not told here.”
And I to him: “If the present stream
flows down thus from our world,
why does it appear to us only at this edge?”
And he to me: “You know the place is round;
and although you have come a long way,
always to the left, descending toward the bottom,
you have not yet turned through the whole circle;
therefore, if something new appears to us,
it should not bring wonder to your face.”
And I again: “Master, where are found
Phlegethon and Lethe? For of one you are silent,
and the other you say is formed from this rain.”
“In all your questions you certainly please me,”
he replied, “but the boiling of the red water
should surely have answered one that you ask.
You will see Lethe, but outside this pit,
where souls go to wash themselves
when the sin repented of is removed.”
Then he said: “Now it is time to move away
from the wood; see that you follow behind me:
the margins make a path, for they are not burned,
and above them every flame is quenched.”
