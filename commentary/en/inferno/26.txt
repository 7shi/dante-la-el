Rejoice, Florence, since you are so great
that over sea and land you beat your wings,
and through Hell your name spreads!
Among the thieves I found five such
citizens of yours that shame comes over me,
and you rise to no great honor through them.
But if near morning one dreams the truth,
you shall feel, within a little time,
what Prato, not to mention others, wishes upon you.
And if it were already here, it would not be too soon.
Would that it were so, since it must indeed be!
for it will weigh on me the more, the more I age.
We departed, and up the stairways
which the crags had made for us to descend before,
my guide climbed back and drew me after him;
and continuing along the solitary way,
among the splinters and the masses of the rock,
the foot could not advance without the hand.
Then I grieved, and now I grieve again
when I turn my mind to what I saw,
and I rein in my intellect more than I usually do,
so that it may not run without virtue guiding it;
so that, if a good star or something better
has given me the good, I myself may not deprive myself of it.
As many as the peasant who rests on the hill,
in the season when he who lights the world
keeps his face less hidden from us,
as the fly gives way to the mosquito,
sees fireflies down through the valley,
perhaps there where he harvests grapes and ploughs:
with so many flames all was shining
the eighth bolgia, as I perceived
as soon as I was where the bottom could be seen.
And as the one who avenged himself with the bears
saw Elijah's chariot at its departure,
when the horses rose upright toward heaven,
who could not so follow it with his eyes
that he saw anything but the flame alone,
like a little cloud, rising upward:
so each one moves through the gorge
of the ditch, for none reveals its stolen contents,
and every flame steals a sinner from sight.
I stood upon the bridge, risen up to look,
so that, had I not grasped a jutting rock,
I would have fallen down without being pushed.
And my guide, who saw me so intent,
said: “Within the fires are the spirits;
each wraps himself in that by which he is burned.”
“My master,” I replied, “from hearing you
I am more certain; but already it seemed to me
that it was so, and already I wished to ask you:
who is in that fire which comes so divided
above that it seems to rise from the pyre
where Eteocles was placed with his brother?”
He answered me: “Within there is tormented
Ulysses and Diomedes, and thus together
they go to punishment as they went to wrath;
and within their flame is lamented
the ambush of the horse that made the gateway
through which the noble seed of the Romans came forth.
Within it is mourned the trick through which, though dead,
Deidamia still grieves for Achilles,
and there for the Palladium punishment is borne.”
“If they can from within those sparks
speak,” I said, “master, I earnestly pray you
and pray again, that my prayer may count as a thousand,
that you not deny me the chance to wait
until the horned flame comes here;
see how with desire I bend toward it!”
And he to me: “Your prayer is worthy
of much praise, and therefore I accept it;
but see that your tongue restrains itself.
Let me speak, for I have understood
what you wish; for they would be reluctant,
because they were Greeks, perhaps toward your speech.”
After the flame had come to the place
where time and place seemed fitting to my guide,
I heard him speak in this manner:
“O you who are two within one fire,
if I earned your favor while I lived,
if I earned your favor much or little
when in the world I wrote the lofty verses,
do not move; but let one of you tell
where, lost, he went to meet his death.”
The greater horn of the ancient flame
began to sway, murmuring,
just like a flame that the wind torments;
then, moving its tip this way and that,
as though it were the tongue that spoke,
it cast forth a voice and said: “When
I departed from Circe, who kept
me more than a year there near Gaeta,
before Aeneas so named it,
neither the sweetness of a son, nor the reverence
for the aged father, nor the owed love
which ought to have made Penelope glad,
could conquer within me the ardor
that I had to become experienced in the world
and in human vices and worth;
but I set myself upon the high open sea
with but one ship and with that company
so small, by which I was not deserted.
The one shore and the other I saw as far as Spain,
as far as Morocco, and the island of the Sardinians,
and the others that that sea bathes round about.
I and my companions were old and slow
when we came to that narrow passage
where Hercules marked out his boundary signs
so that humanity should not venture farther;
on my right I left Seville,
on the other side I had already left Ceuta.
‘O brothers,’ I said, ‘who through a hundred thousand
perils have reached the west,
to this so little vigil
of our senses that remains
do not wish to deny the experience,
following the sun, of the world without people.
Consider your origin:
you were not made to live like beasts,
but to pursue virtue and knowledge.’
I made my companions so eager,
with this little speech, for the journey,
that afterward I could scarcely have held them back;
and with our stern turned toward the morning,
we made wings of our oars for the mad flight,
always gaining ground toward the left.
All the stars now of the other pole
I saw at night, and ours so low
that it did not rise above the ocean floor.
Five times rekindled and as many extinguished
was the light beneath the moon,
since we had entered on the high passage,
when there appeared to us a mountain, dark
because of the distance, and it seemed to me so high
as I had never seen any.
We rejoiced, and soon it turned to weeping;
for from the new land a whirlwind arose
and struck the foremost part of the ship.
Three times it made it whirl with all the waters;
at the fourth, to raise the stern upward
and the prow to go downward, as another willed,
until the sea had closed again over us.”
