Nessus had not yet arrived on the other side,
when we made our way into a wood
that was marked by no path.
Not green foliage, but of a dark color;
not straight branches, but knotted and twisted;
there were no fruits, but thorns with poison.
No thickets so harsh or so dense have
those wild beasts that hold in hatred
the cultivated lands between Cecina and Corneto.
Here the foul Harpies make their nests,
who drove the Trojans from the Strophades
with a sorrowful announcement of future harm.
They have broad wings, and human necks and faces,
feet with claws, and their great bellies feathered;
they make lamentations upon the strange trees.
And the good master: “Before you enter farther,
know that you are in the second ring”,
he began to tell me, “and will remain there up to the time
when you come to the dreadful stretch of sand.
Therefore look carefully; thus you will see
things that would rob my words of belief”.
I heard cries of grief rising from every side
and saw no person making them;
so that, utterly bewildered, I stopped.
I believe he believed that I believed
that so many voices came, among those tangled trunks,
from people hiding themselves from us.
Therefore the master said: “If you break off
some little twig from one of these plants,
the thoughts you have will all be cut short”.
Then I stretched my hand a little forward
and I plucked a little branch from a great thorn tree;
and its trunk cried: “Why do you break me?”.
After it had then become stained with dark blood,
it began again to say: “Why do you tear me?
have you no spirit of pity at all?
We were human beings, and now we are made brushwood:
surely your hand ought to be more merciful,
even if we had been souls of serpents”.
As from a green log that is burning
at one end, which at the other drips
and hisses from the wind escaping,
so from the broken splinter came together
words and blood; so I let the tip
fall, and stood like a man afraid.
“If he could have believed beforehand”,
my wise guide replied, “wounded soul,
what he has seen through my verse alone,
he would not have stretched his hand against you;
but the incredible nature of the thing made me
urge him to an act that weighs upon me too.
But tell him who you were, so that, by way
of some amends, he may refresh your fame
in the world above, where he is allowed to return”.
And the trunk: “So you entice me with your sweet words,
that I cannot keep silent; and may it not burden you
if I become a little entangled in my speech.
I am the one who held both keys
of Frederick's heart, and who turned them,
locking and unlocking, so gently,
that I removed almost everyone from his confidence;
I kept faith with the glorious office,
so much that I lost sleep and life for it.
The prostitute who never from the household
of Caesar turned her harlot eyes,
the death of all and the vice of courts,
inflamed every mind against me;
and those inflamed so inflamed Augustus,
that joyful honors turned into sorrowful griefs.
My spirit, through a disdainful impulse,
believing through death to escape contempt,
made me unjust against my just self.
By the new roots of this tree
I swear to you that I never broke faith
with my lord, who was so worthy of honor.
And if either of you returns to the world,
let him restore my memory, which lies
still beneath the blow that envy dealt it”.
He waited a little, and then: “Since he is silent”,
the poet said to me, “do not waste the time;
but speak, and question him, if you wish to know more”.
So I said to him: “You ask him further
about whatever you think would satisfy me;
for I could not, such pity grips my heart”.
Therefore he began again: “So may this man do for you
freely what your words request,
imprisoned spirit, may it also please you
to tell us how the soul is bound
within these knots; and tell us, if you can,
whether any ever frees itself from such limbs”.
Then the trunk blew forcefully, and afterward
that wind changed into this voice:
“You shall be answered briefly.
When the savage soul departs
from the body from which it has torn itself,
Minos sends it to the seventh gulf.
It falls into the wood, and no place is chosen for it;
but wherever chance hurls it,
there it sprouts like a grain of spelt.
It rises into a shoot and a wild plant:
the Harpies, feeding then upon its leaves,
make pain, and a window for the pain.
Like the others, we shall come for our bodily remains,
but not so that any of us may clothe herself in them again,
for it is not just to have what one takes from oneself.
Here we shall drag them, and throughout the mournful
wood our bodies will be hung,
each upon the thorn tree of its suffering shade”.
We were still intent upon the trunk,
believing it wished to tell us more,
when we were startled by a noise,
like a man who, as they approach,
hears the boar and the hunt at his post,
who hears the beasts, and the branches rustling.
And behold, two on the left side,
naked and scratched, fleeing so fast,
that they broke through every barrier of the wood.
The one in front: “Now come, come, death!”.
And the other, who thought he was too slow,
cried: “Lano, not so nimble were
your legs at the jousts of Toppo!”.
And since perhaps his breath was failing him,
he made one knot of himself and a bush.
Behind them the wood was full
of black bitches, ravenous and running
like greyhounds released from their chains.
Into the one who crouched in hiding they sank their teeth,
and they tore him apart piece by piece;
then they carried away those suffering limbs.
Then my guide took me by the hand,
and led me to the bush that wept
in vain through its bleeding wounds.
“O Iacopo”, it said, “of Santo Andrea,
what has it profited you to make a shield of me?
what fault have I in your wicked life?”.
When the master had stopped beside it,
he said: “Who were you, who through so many broken tips
breathe out with blood your sorrowful speech?”.
And it said to us: “O souls who have come
to see the shameful mangling
that has so torn my leaves from me,
gather them at the foot of the mournful bush.
I was from the city that for the Baptist
exchanged its first patron; therefore he, for this,
will always make it sorrowful through his art;
and were it not that at the crossing of the Arno
some likeness of him still remains,
those citizens who afterward rebuilt it
upon the ashes left by Attila,
would have had their labor done in vain.
I made a gallows for myself out of my own home”.
