The multitude of people and the various wounds
had so intoxicated my eyes,
that they longed to remain and weep.
But Virgil said to me: “Why do you keep staring?
why does your gaze keep lingering
down there among the sad, mutilated shades?
You have not done so in the other bolgias;
consider, if you think to count them,
that the valley circles twenty-two miles.
And already the moon is beneath our feet;
little time now remains that is granted to us,
and there is more to see that you do not see.”
“If you had”, I answered then,
“heeded the reason why I was looking,
perhaps you would have allowed me to stay longer.”
Meanwhile he was going on, and I followed behind him,
my guide, already making my reply,
and adding: “Within that hollow
where I just held my eyes so fixed,
I believe a spirit of my own blood weeps for
the guilt that costs so dearly down there.”
Then the master said: “Let not
your thoughts henceforth be troubled over him.
Attend to something else, and let him remain there;
for I saw him at the foot of the little bridge
pointing at you and threatening fiercely with his finger,
and I heard him named Geri del Bello.
You were then so wholly absorbed
in him who once held Hautefort,
that you did not look that way before he had gone.”
“O my guide, the violent death
that has not yet been avenged for him”, I said,
“by anyone who shares in the disgrace,
made him indignant; therefore he went away
without speaking to me, as I suppose:
and in this he has made me more compassionate toward him.”
Thus we spoke as far as the first place
on the rocky bridge that shows the next valley,
if there were more light there, right to the bottom.
When we were above the last cloister
of Malebolge, so that its lay brothers
could appear within our sight,
various laments shot arrows at me,
which had their shafts tipped with pity;
so I covered my ears with my hands.
What suffering there would be, if from the hospitals
of Valdichiana between July and September
and of Maremma and Sardinia the ailments
were all together in a single ditch,
such was it here, and such a stench came out of it
as usually comes from rotting limbs.
We descended onto the last bank
of the long rocky bridge, still keeping to the left;
and then my sight was clearer
down toward the bottom, where the minister
of the high Lord, infallible justice,
punishes the falsifiers whom it registers here.
I do not believe it was a greater sorrow to see
in Aegina the whole people sick,
when the air was so full of pestilence,
that the animals, down to the little worm,
all fell, and afterward the ancient peoples,
according to what the poets hold as certain,
were replenished from the seed of ants;
than it was to see through that dark valley
the spirits languishing in scattered heaps.
Some upon the belly and some upon the shoulders
of one another lay, and some on all fours
moved along the sorrowful path.
Step by step we went without speaking,
watching and listening to the sick,
who could not raise their bodies.
I saw two sitting propped against each other,
as pan is propped against pan for warming,
stained from head to foot with scabs;
and never have I seen a currycomb plied
by a stableboy awaited by his master,
nor by one who unwillingly stays awake,
as rapidly as each plied the bite
of his nails upon himself, for the great fury
of the itching, which has no other relief;
and so their nails scraped off the scabs
as a knife does the scales of a bream
or of another fish that has them larger.
“O you who strip off your mail with your fingers”,
my guide began to one of them,
“and who sometimes make pincers of them,
tell us whether any Italian is among those
who are in here, so may your nails suffice you
eternally for that work.”
“Italians are we, whom you see so ravaged
here, both of us”, one answered weeping;
“but who are you who asked about us?”
And the guide said: “I am one who descends
with this living man down from ledge to ledge,
and I intend to show Hell to him.”
Then their mutual support broke apart;
and trembling, each turned toward me,
with others who had overheard him.
The good master drew fully close beside me,
saying: “Say to them what you wish”;
and I began, since he so wished:
“May your memory not steal away
in the first world from human minds,
but may it live beneath many suns,
tell me who you are and of what people;
let not your disfiguring and repulsive punishment
frighten you from revealing yourselves to me.”
“I was from Arezzo, and Albero of Siena”,
one replied, “had me put to the fire;
but that for which I died does not bring me here.
It is true that I said to him, speaking in jest:
‘I would know how to rise through the air in flight’;
and he, who had desire and little sense,
wanted me to show him the art; and only
because I did not make him Daedalus, he had me
burned by one who regarded him as a son.
But into the last bolgia of the ten
me, for the alchemy that I practiced in the world,
Minos condemned, who is not permitted to err.”
And I said to the poet: “Now was there ever
a people as vain as the Sienese?
Certainly not the French by far!”
At this the other leper, who heard me,
replied to my words: “Except Stricca for me
who knew how to make moderate expenditures,
and Niccolò who the costly custom
of the clove first discovered
in the garden where such seed takes root;
and except the company in which squandered
Caccia of Asciano his vineyard and his great fortune,
and Abbagliato displayed his wit.
But so that you may know who thus seconds you
against the Sienese, sharpen your gaze toward me,
so that my face may answer you clearly:
thus you will see that I am the shade of Capocchio,
who falsified metals through alchemy;
and you should remember, if I recognize you rightly,
how I was a good ape of nature.”
