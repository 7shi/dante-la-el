In that part of the youthful year
when the sun tempers its locks beneath Aquarius
and already the nights draw toward half the day,
when the frost upon the ground copies
the likeness of its white sister,
but the temper of its pen lasts only a little.
the little peasant whose provisions fail,
rises, and looks, and sees the countryside
gleaming white all over; whereupon he strikes his thigh,
returns indoors, and laments here and there,
like a poor wretch who knows not what to do;
then comes back out, and gathers hope anew,
seeing that the world has changed its face
in a little while, and takes his shepherd's crook
and drives the little sheep outside to graze.
So did the master cause me to lose heart
when I saw his brow grow so troubled,
and just as quickly the plaster came to the wound;
for, when we came to the broken bridge,
the guide turned toward me with that expression
so gentle that I first saw at the mountain's foot.
He opened his arms, after some plan
chosen within himself, first having surveyed
the ruins well, and took hold of me.
And like one who acts and weighs his actions,
who always seems to provide beforehand,
so, lifting me up toward the summit
of a crag, he marked another jutting rock,
saying: “Then take hold of that one;
but first test whether it will bear your weight.”
It was no path for someone dressed in a cloak,
for we scarcely, he light and I pushed,
could climb up from rock to rock.
And had it not been that on that encircling bank
the slope was shorter than upon the other,
I know not about him, but I would surely have been overcome.
But because Malebolge toward the mouth
of the deepest pit slopes all the way,
the position of each valley entails
that one bank rises and the other falls;
we came at last, nevertheless, upon the crest
from which the last stone breaks away.
The breath was so drained from my lungs
when I was up, that I could go no farther,
but sat down as soon as I arrived.
“Now you must thus shake off your sloth,”
said the master; “for, sitting upon feathers,
one does not attain fame, nor beneath a coverlet;
without which whoever spends his life,
leaves such a trace of himself on earth,
as smoke in air and foam upon water.
And therefore rise; overcome your breathlessness
with the spirit that conquers every battle,
if it does not sink beneath its heavy body.
A longer stair must be climbed;
it is not enough to have departed from these people.
If you understand me, now make it serve you.”
Then I rose, showing myself supplied
with breath better than I felt myself to be,
and said: “Go on, for I am strong and bold.”
We took our way up along the rocky bridge,
which was craggy, narrow, and difficult,
and far steeper than the one before.
I went along speaking so as not to seem faint;
whereupon a voice came from the other ditch,
ill-suited to forming words.
I know not what it said, although upon the crown
of the arch that crosses there I already stood;
but the speaker seemed moved to anger.
I was turned downward, but my keen eyes
could not reach the bottom through the darkness;
wherefore I said: “Master, see that you reach
the other encircling bank, and let us descend the wall;
for, just as I hear from here and do not understand,
so I look down and make out nothing.”
“No other answer,” he said, “do I give you
except the deed; for an honest request
should be followed by action in silence.”
We descended the bridge at the end
where it joins the eighth bank,
and then the ditch lay open to my sight:
and within it I saw a terrible mass
of serpents, and of such varied kinds
that the memory still ravages my blood.
Let Libya boast no more of its sands;
for though chelydri, jaculi, and phareae
it breeds, and cenchres with the amphisbaena,
neither so many plagues nor such cruel ones
did it ever show, even with all Ethiopia
and with all that lies above the Red Sea.
Amid this cruel and most dreadful throng
ran people naked and terrified,
without hope of a hiding hole or heliotrope:
with serpents they had their hands bound behind them;
those thrust through the loins their tail
and head, and were knotted together in front.
And behold, at one who was near our bank,
a serpent darted and pierced him
there where the neck is joined to the shoulders.
Neither O nor I was ever written so swiftly,
as he caught fire and burned, and wholly ash
he had to become as he fell;
and after he was thus destroyed upon the ground,
the dust gathered itself together
and returned at once into that same person.
Thus it is acknowledged by the great sages
that the phoenix dies and then is reborn,
when it approaches its five-hundredth year;
it feeds on neither grass nor grain throughout its life,
but only on tears of incense and amomum,
and nard and myrrh are its last swaddling bands.
And like one who falls, and does not know how,
through the force of a demon that drags him to the ground,
or of another obstruction that binds a person,
when he rises, looking about him,
all bewildered by the great anguish
that he has suffered, and sighing as he looks:
such was the sinner after he had risen.
O power of God, how severe it is,
that rains down such blows in retribution!
The guide then asked him who he was;
whereupon he replied: “I rained down from Tuscany,
a little while ago, into this savage gorge.
A bestial life pleased me, and not a human one,
as befitted the mule that I was; I am Vanni Fucci
the beast, and Pistoia was a worthy den for me.”
And I to the guide: “Tell him not to slip away,
and ask what guilt thrust him down here;
for I saw him as a man of blood and wrath.”
And the sinner, who heard, did not dissemble,
but turned toward me his mind and his face,
and was suffused with dismal shame;
then he said: “It pains me more that you have caught me
in the misery in which you see me,
than when I was taken from the other life.
I cannot deny what you ask;
I am placed so far down because I was
a thief of the beautiful furnishings in the sacristy,
and it was once falsely charged to another.
But so that you may take no joy in such a sight,
if ever you are outside these dark places,
open your ears to my announcement, and listen.
Pistoia first grows thin of the Blacks;
then Florence renews its people and its ways.
Mars draws a vapor from the Val di Magra
which is wrapped in murky clouds;
and with an impetuous and bitter storm
over Campo Piceno battle will be fought;
whereupon it will suddenly break apart the mist,
so that every White will be wounded by it.
And I have said it so that it may grieve you!”
