His mouth he raised from the savage meal,
that sinner, wiping it on the hair
of the head that he had ravaged at the back.
Then he began: “You wish me to renew
the desperate grief that weighs upon my heart
even in thought, before I speak of it.
But if my words are to be seed
that bears infamy for the traitor whom I gnaw,
you will see speaking and weeping together.
I do not know who you are, nor by what means
you have come down here; but Florentine
you truly seem to me when I hear you.
You must know that I was Count Ugolino,
and this is Archbishop Ruggieri:
now I will tell you why I am such a neighbor to him.
That through the effect of his evil designs,
trusting in him, I was taken prisoner
and afterward killed, there is no need to tell;
but what you cannot have heard,
that is, how cruel my death was,
you shall hear, and know whether he has wronged me.
A narrow opening within the Muda,
which because of me bears the name of hunger,
and which must yet be shut upon others,
had shown me through its aperture
several moons already, when I had the evil dream
that tore away for me the veil of the future.
This man appeared to me as master and lord,
hunting the wolf and the wolf cubs toward the mountain
because of which the Pisans cannot see Lucca.
With lean hounds, eager and well trained,
the Gualandi with the Sismondi and the Lanfranchi
he had placed ahead of him at the front.
After a short run they seemed to me exhausted,
the father and the sons, and with sharp fangs
I seemed to see their flanks being torn open.
When I awoke before the dawn,
I heard my children weeping in their sleep,
who were with me, and asking for bread.
Cruel indeed are you, if you do not already grieve
thinking of what my heart foreboded;
and if you do not weep, what do you weep for?
They were already awake, and the hour was approaching
when food used to be brought to us,
and each was fearful because of his dream;
and I heard the door below being nailed shut
in the horrible tower; therefore I looked
into the faces of my children without saying a word.
I did not weep, so completely had I turned to stone within:
they were weeping; and my little Anselmo
said: “You look so, father! What is wrong?”
For all that, I neither wept nor answered
all that day nor the night that followed,
until another sun rose upon the world.
When a little ray of light had made its way
into the sorrowful prison, and I discerned
in four faces my own very likeness,
I bit both my hands in grief;
and they, thinking that I did it from a desire
to eat, suddenly rose
and they said: “Father, it will be much less pain for us
if you eat of us: you clothed us
in this miserable flesh, and you strip it away.”
I quieted myself then, so as not to make them sadder;
that day and the next we all remained silent;
ah, hard earth, why did you not open?
When we had reached the fourth day,
Gaddo threw himself outstretched at my feet,
saying: “My father, why do you not help me?”
There he died; and just as you see me,
I saw the three fall one by one
between the fifth day and the sixth; so I began,
already blind, to grope over each one,
and for two days I called them, after they were dead.
Then fasting had more power than grief.”
When he had said this, with eyes askew
he seized the wretched skull again with his teeth,
which were strong upon the bone, like a dog's.
Ah, Pisa, disgrace of the peoples
of the fair country where ‘sì’ is heard,
since your neighbors are slow to punish you,
let Capraia and Gorgona move,
and make a barrier across the Arno at its mouth,
so that it may drown every person within you!
For if Count Ugolino had the reputation
of having betrayed you in the matter of your castles,
you should not have put the children to such torment.
Their youthful age made innocent,
new Thebes, Uguiccione and Brigata
and the other two whom the canto names above.
We passed on, to where the ice
roughly encases another people,
not turned downward, but all lying on their backs.
Weeping itself there does not allow weeping,
and the grief that finds an obstruction at the eyes,
turns inward to make the anguish grow;
for the first tears form a knot,
and like visors of crystal,
they fill the whole hollow beneath the eyebrow.
And although, as in a callus,
because of the cold all feeling
had ceased to dwell in my face,
already I seemed to feel some wind;
so I said: “My master, what moves this?
is not every vapor extinguished down here?”
And he to me: “Soon you will be where
your eye will give you the answer to this,
seeing the cause that rains down the blast.”
And one of the wretched souls in the frozen crust
cried out to us: “O souls so cruel
that the lowest station has been assigned to you,
lift from my face the hardened veils,
so that I may vent the grief that fills my heart,
a little, before the tears freeze again.”
So I said to him: “If you wish me to help you,
tell me who you are, and if I do not free you,
may I have to go to the bottom of the ice.”
Then he answered: “I am Friar Alberigo;
I am the one of the fruit from the evil orchard,
who here receives a date for a fig.”
“Oh,” I said to him, “are you already dead, then?”
And he to me: “How my body fares
in the world above, I have no knowledge.
Such a privilege has this Tolomea,
that often the soul falls here
before Atropos gives it movement.
And so that you may more willingly scrape
the glassy tears from my face,
know that, as soon as the soul betrays
as I did, its body is taken from it
by a demon, which afterward governs it
until its allotted time has entirely run its course.
It plunges into such a cistern as this;
and perhaps the body still appears above
of the shade that winters here behind me.
You must know, if you have only just come down:
he is Ser Branca Doria, and several years
have passed since he was enclosed here.”
“I believe,” I said to him, “that you are deceiving me;
for Branca Doria has not yet died,
and eats and drinks and sleeps and wears clothes.”
“In the ditch above,” he said, “of the Malebranche,
where the sticky pitch boils,
Michel Zanche had not yet arrived,
when this man left the devil in his place
in his body, as did a kinsman of his
who committed the betrayal together with him.
But stretch your hand this way now;
open my eyes.” And I did not open them for him;
and it was courtesy to be rude to him.
Ah, Genoese, men estranged
from every civilized custom and full of every vice,
why are you not scattered from the world?
For with the worst spirit of Romagna
I found one of you who, for his deeds,
already bathes in Cocytus in his soul,
and in his body still seems alive above.
