At the return of consciousness, which shut itself
before the pitiable condition of the two relatives by marriage,
which wholly overwhelmed me with sadness,
new torments and new tormented souls
I see around me, however I move
and turn, and however I look.
I am in the third circle, of the rain
eternal, accursed, cold and heavy;
its pattern and nature are never new.
Large hailstones, darkened water, and snow
pour down through the darkened air;
the earth that receives this stinks.
Cerberus, a cruel and monstrous beast,
with three throats barks like a dog
over the people who are submerged here.
His eyes are crimson, his beard greasy and black,
and his belly broad, and his hands clawed;
he scratches the spirits and flays and quarters them.
The rain makes them howl like dogs;
they make one side a shield for the other;
the wretched profane ones turn themselves often.
When Cerberus, the great worm, caught sight of us,
he opened his mouths and showed us his fangs;
he had no limb that he held still.
And my guide spread out his hands,
took the earth, and with his fists full
threw it into the ravenous throats.
As is that dog that barks in hunger,
and then quiets down when it bites its food,
for it attends and strives only to devour it,
so became those filthy faces
of the demon Cerberus, who thunders at
the souls so loudly that they would wish to be deaf.
We were passing over the shades that are beaten down by
the heavy rain, and we were setting our feet
upon their emptiness that seems a human body.
They were all lying on the ground,
except one that rose to sit, as soon
as it saw us passing before it.
“O you who are led through this hell,”
it said to me, “recognize me, if you can:
you were made before I was unmade.”
And I to him: “The anguish that you have
perhaps draws you out of my memory,
so that it seems I never saw you.
But tell me who you are, who in so sorrowful
a place are set, and have such punishment,
that, if another is greater, none is so unpleasant.”
And he to me: “Your city, which is full
of envy so that already the sack overflows,
held me within it in the serene life.
You citizens called me Ciacco:
for the harmful sin of gluttony,
as you see, beneath the rain I collapse.
And I, a sorrowful soul, am not alone,
for all these endure a similar punishment
for a similar sin.” And he spoke no more.
I answered him: “Ciacco, your suffering
weighs on me so that it invites me to weep;
but tell me, if you know, what will become of
the citizens of the divided city;
whether anyone there is just; and tell me the cause
for which so much discord has assailed it.”
And he to me: “After a long struggle
they will come to bloodshed, and the rustic faction
will drive out the other with great injury.
Then afterward this one must fall
within three suns, and the other rise
through the power of one who now temporizes.
It will hold its heads high for a long time,
keeping the other beneath heavy burdens,
however it may weep at this or resent it.
Two are just, and they are not heeded there;
pride, envy, and avarice are
the three sparks that have set hearts ablaze.”
Here he put an end to his mournful words.
And I to him: “I still wish you to instruct me
and to grant me the gift of further speech.
Farinata and Tegghiaio, who were so worthy,
Iacopo Rusticucci, Arrigo, and Mosca
and the others who devoted their talents to doing good,
tell me where they are and let me recognize them;
for a great desire presses me to know
whether heaven sweetens them or hell poisons them.”
And he: “They are among the blackest souls;
different sins weigh them down toward the bottom;
if you descend so far, there you will be able to see them.
But when you are in the sweet world,
I beg you to bring me to the minds of others:
I tell you no more and answer you no more.”
His straight gaze he then turned askance;
he looked at me a little and then bowed his head:
he fell with it level with the other blind ones.
And my guide said to me: “He wakes no more
before the sound of the angelic trumpet,
when the hostile power will come:
each will see again the sorrowful tomb,
will take back their flesh and their form,
will hear what resounds through eternity.”
Thus we passed through the filthy mixture
of the shades and the rain, with slow steps,
touching a little on the future life;
at which I said: “Master, these torments,
will they increase after the great sentence,
or be less, or be as burning?”
And he to me: “Return to your learning,
which holds that, the more perfect a thing is,
the more it feels good, and likewise pain.
Although these accursed people
never attain true perfection,
expect to be more complete then than now.”
We circled around along that path,
speaking much more than I recount;
we came to the point where the descent begins:
there we found Pluto, the great enemy.
