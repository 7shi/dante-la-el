Already I was where one could hear the thunder
of the water falling into the next circle,
like the humming sound that beehives make,
when three shades together broke away,
running, from a troop that was passing
beneath the rain of bitter torment.
They came towards us, and each cried:
“Stop, you who by your dress seem to us
to be someone from our wicked city.”
Alas, what wounds I saw upon their limbs,
fresh and old, burned into them by the flames!
It pains me still whenever I remember them.
To their cries my teacher gave heed;
he turned his face towards me, and “Now wait,”
he said, “to these men we must be courteous.
And were it not for the fire that rains down,
by the nature of this place, I would say
that haste better befitted you than them.”
They began again, as we stopped,
their old lament; and when they reached us,
all three made a wheel of themselves.
As champions are wont to do, naked and oiled,
watching for their hold and their advantage,
before they strike and wound each other,
thus turning, each his face
directed towards me, so that his neck, contrary
made a continual journey to the feet.
And “If the misery of this loose-sanded place
brings us and our prayers into contempt,”
one began, “and our blackened and bare appearance,
let our fame incline your spirit
to tell us who you are, who your living feet
so fearlessly rub along through Hell.
This man, whose footsteps you see me tread,
though he goes naked and stripped of hair,
was of higher rank than you believe:
he was the grandson of the good Gualdrada;
Guido Guerra was his name, and in his life
he accomplished much with judgment and with the sword.
The other, who behind me treads the sand,
is Tegghiaio Aldobrandi, whose voice
in the world above ought to be prized.
And I, who am placed in torment with them,
I was Iacopo Rusticucci, and certainly
my fierce wife harms me more than anything.”
Had I been sheltered from the fire,
I would have thrown myself down among them,
and I believe my teacher would have allowed it;
but because I would have been burned and scorched,
fear overcame my good intention
that made me eager to embrace them.
Then I began: “Not contempt, but grief
your condition fixed within me,
so great that only slowly is it wholly shed,
as soon as this lord of mine spoke to me
words from which I thought
that people such as you were coming.
I am from your city, and always
your deeds and your honoured names
I have recounted and heard with affection.
I leave the gall and go for sweet fruits
promised to me by the truthful guide;
but first I must descend as far as the centre.”
“So may your soul long guide
your limbs,” that man replied again,
“and so may your fame shine after you,
courtesy and valour, say whether they remain
in our city as they used to,
or whether they have wholly departed;
for Guiglielmo Borsiere, who has suffered
with us but a little while and goes there with our companions,
greatly troubles us with his words.”
“The new people and sudden gains
have generated pride and excess,
Florence, in you, so that already you lament it.”
Thus I cried with my face raised;
and the three, who took this as an answer,
looked at one another as men look upon the truth.
“If at other times it costs you so little,”
they all replied, “to satisfy others,
happy are you if you speak thus at will!
Therefore, if you escape these dark places
and return to see again the beautiful stars,
when it will delight you to say ‘I was there’,
see that you speak of us to the people.”
Then they broke the wheel, and as they fled
their nimble legs seemed wings.
An amen could not have been said
so quickly as they disappeared;
therefore the master thought it time to leave.
I followed him, and we had gone but a little way,
when the sound of the water was so near us
that, had we spoken, we would scarcely have been heard.
As that river which has its own course,
the first from Monte Viso towards the east,
on the left slope of the Apennines,
which is called Acquacheta above, before
it flows down into its lower bed,
and at Forlì is stripped of that name,
thunders there above San Benedetto
dell'Alpe, falling in a single descent
where it should have been received by a thousand;
so, down a precipitous bank,
we found that dark-stained water resounding,
so that in a short while it would have hurt the ear.
I had a cord girded about me,
and with it I once thought
to catch the leopard with the spotted skin.
After I had wholly loosened it from myself,
just as my guide had commanded me,
I handed it to him knotted and coiled.
Then he turned towards the right,
and a little way away from the edge
threw it down into that deep chasm.
‘Surely some strange thing must answer’,
I said within myself, ‘the strange signal
that the master follows so intently with his eye’.
Ah, how cautious men should be
near those who see not only the deed,
but with their understanding gaze into our thoughts!
He said to me: “Soon there will come up
what I await and what your thought dreams of;
soon it must reveal itself to your sight.”
Always to that truth which has the face of falsehood
a man should close his lips as long as he can,
because without fault it brings shame;
but here I cannot keep it silent; and by the verses
of this comedy, reader, I swear to you,
so may they not be bereft of lasting favour,
that I saw through that thick and dark air
a figure come swimming upwards,
astonishing to every steadfast heart,
just as he returns who goes down
sometimes to free an anchor caught
on a rock or something else hidden in the sea,
stretching upwards and drawing in his feet.
