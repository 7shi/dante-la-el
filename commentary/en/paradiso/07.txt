“Hosanna, holy God of hosts,
illuminating beyond measure with your brightness
the blessed fires of these kingdoms!”
Thus, turning to its own melody,
that substance was seen by me to sing,
upon which a double light is joined;
and it and the others moved to its dance,
and like the swiftest sparks
were veiled from me by sudden distance.
I was in doubt and was saying, ‘Speak, speak!’
within myself; ‘speak,’ I said, ‘to my lady
who quenches my thirst with sweet drops’.
But that reverence which becomes mistress
of all of me, merely at Be and at ice,
made me bow like a man falling asleep.
Beatrice suffered me thus but little
and began, shining upon me with a smile
such that it would make a man happy in the fire:
“According to my unerring perception,
how just vengeance justly
was punished has set you thinking;
but I shall soon unbind your mind;
and listen, for my words
will make you a gift of great meaning.
For not allowing to the faculty that wills
a restraint for its own good, that man who was not born,
condemning himself, condemned all his offspring;
hence the human race lay sick
below for many centuries in great error,
until it pleased the Word of God to descend
where the nature that from its maker
had estranged itself, he united to himself in person
through the sole act of his eternal love.
Now turn your gaze to what is now explained:
this nature, united to its maker,
was pure and good as it was created;
but through itself alone it was banished
from paradise, because it turned
from the way of truth and from its life.
The punishment, then, that the cross inflicted,
if measured against the nature assumed,
none ever struck so justly;
and likewise none was so great an injustice,
considering the person who suffered,
in whom that nature was joined.
Thus from one act came different things:
for one death pleased both God and the Jews;
through it the earth trembled and heaven opened.
It should no longer seem difficult to you,
when it is said that just vengeance
was afterward avenged by a just court.
But now I see your mind entangled,
from thought to thought, within a knot,
from which it waits with great desire to be freed.
You say: “I clearly discern what I hear;
but why God should have wished, is hidden from me,
precisely this way for our redemption”.
This decree, brother, lies buried
from the eyes of anyone whose intellect
has not matured within the flame of love.
Nevertheless, because at this mark
many aim and few discern,
I shall tell you why such a way was worthiest.
The divine goodness, which spurns from itself
all envy, burning within itself, sparkles
so that it unfolds the eternal beauties.
What distils from it without an intermediary
thereafter has no end, because it does not shift,
its imprint, when it sets its seal.
What rains from it without an intermediary
is wholly free, because it is not subject
to the power of newly created things.
It is more like it, and therefore pleases it more;
for the holy ardour that irradiates everything
is most lively in what most resembles it.
With all these gifts is endowed
the human creature, and if one is lacking,
it must fall from its nobility.
Only sin is what deprives it of freedom
and makes it unlike the highest good,
so that it is little brightened by his light;
and it never returns to its dignity,
unless it fills again where guilt empties,
with just penalties against wrongful pleasure.
Your nature, when it sinned wholly
in its seed, from these dignities,
as from paradise, was removed;
nor could they be recovered, if you consider
very closely, by any way,
without crossing one of these fords:
either that God alone, through his generosity,
should have forgiven, or that man by himself
should have made satisfaction for his folly.
Now fix your eye within the abyss
of the eternal counsel, as closely as you can
intent upon my speech.
Humanity could not, within its own limits,
ever make satisfaction, being unable to descend
in humility through subsequent obedience,
as far as through disobedience it intended to rise;
and this is the reason why humanity was
excluded from being able to make satisfaction by itself.
Therefore it was fitting for God through his ways
to restore humanity to its entire life,
I mean through one, or else through both.
But because a work is the more appreciated
from its maker, the more it displays
of the goodness of the heart from which it issued,
the divine goodness that imprints the world,
to proceed by all its ways,
to raise you up again, was pleased.
Nor between the last night and the first day
was or will there be so lofty or so magnificent a course,
through either the one or the other:
for God was more generous in giving himself
to make humanity capable of raising itself again,
than if he had merely forgiven on his own;
and all the other ways fell short
of justice, if the Son of God
had not humbled himself to become incarnate.
Now, to fulfil your every desire,
I return to clarify a certain point for you,
so that you may see there as I do.
You say: “I see water, I see fire,
air and earth and all their mixtures
come to corruption and endure but briefly;
and yet these things were created;
therefore, if what has been said was true,
they ought to be safe from corruption”.
The angels, brother, and the pure country
in which you are, may be said to have been created,
just as they are, in their entire being;
but the elements that you have named
and those things that are made from them
are given form by a created power.
The matter that they possess was created;
created was the form-giving power
in these stars that move around them.
The soul of every beast and of the plants
from a compound endowed with potential is drawn by
the ray and the motion of the holy lights;
but your life, without an intermediary, is breathed forth by
the highest goodness, and it makes it fall in love
with itself, so that thereafter it always desires it.
And from this you can also infer
your resurrection, if you consider again
how human flesh was made at that time
when the first parents were both made.”
