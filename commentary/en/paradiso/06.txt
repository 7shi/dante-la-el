“After Constantine turned the eagle
against the course of heaven, which it followed
behind the ancient man who took Lavinia as wife,
for two hundred years and more the bird of God
remained at the far edge of Europe,
near the mountains from which it first came forth;
and beneath the shadow of the sacred feathers
governed the world there from hand to hand,
and, changing thus, came into mine.
Caesar I was, and Justinian I am,
who, by the will of the primal love that I feel,
from within the laws removed the excessive and the useless.
And before I gave my attention to the work,
that there was one nature in Christ, no more,
I believed, and with that faith was content;
but the blessed Agapetus, who was
supreme shepherd, toward the true faith
directed me with his words.
I believed him; and what was in his faith,
I now see as clearly as you see
the false and the true in every contradiction.
As soon as I walked in step with the Church,
it pleased God through grace to inspire in me
the lofty work, and I gave myself wholly to it;
and to my Belisarius I entrusted the arms,
to whom heaven's right hand was so closely joined,
that it was a sign that I should rest.
Now here, at your first question, ends
my answer; but its nature
compels me to follow it with an addition,
so that you may see with how much reason
moves against the most sacred standard
both he who claims it as his own and he who opposes it.
See how much valor has made it worthy
of reverence; and it began from the hour
when Pallas died to give it a kingdom.
You know that it made its dwelling in Alba
for three hundred years and more, until the end
when the three fought the three for it once more.
And you know what it did from the wrong of the Sabine women
to Lucretia's sorrow, under seven kings,
conquering the neighboring peoples all around.
You know what it did, borne by the illustrious
Romans against Brennus, against Pyrrhus,
against the other princes and republics;
whence Torquatus and Quinctius, who from his lock
unkempt was named, the Decii and the Fabii
gained the fame that I gladly embalm with myrrh.
It cast down the pride of the Arabs
who behind Hannibal crossed
the Alpine crags, O Po, from which you flow.
Beneath it, while still young, triumphed
Scipio and Pompey; and to that hill
beneath which you were born it proved bitter.
Then, near the time when all heaven willed
to bring the world to its own serene condition,
Caesar, by Rome's will, takes it up.
And what it did from the Var as far as the Rhine,
the Isère and the Saône saw, and the Seine saw,
and every valley from which the Rhône is filled.
What it did after he came out of Ravenna
and crossed the Rubicon, was of such flight,
that neither tongue nor pen could follow it.
Toward Spain it turned the host,
then toward Durazzo, and struck Pharsalia
so that the grief was felt by the warm Nile.
Antandros and Simois, from where it set forth,
it saw again, and the place where Hector lies;
and then, to Ptolemy's harm, it roused itself.
From there it descended like lightning upon Juba;
whence it turned toward your west,
where it heard the Pompeian trumpet.
Of what it did with the next bearer,
Brutus with Cassius howls in hell,
and Modena and Perugia were sorrowful.
For it sad Cleopatra still weeps,
who, fleeing before it, from the serpent
took death, sudden and dark.
With this man it ran as far as the Red Sea shore;
with this man it placed the world in such peace,
that Janus's temple was closed.
But what the standard that makes me speak
had done before and was to do afterward
for the mortal realm that lies beneath it,
becomes in appearance small and dim,
if in the hand of the third Caesar it is viewed
with a clear eye and with pure affection;
for the living justice that inspires me,
granted it, in the hand of the one I mean,
the glory of executing vengeance for its wrath.
Now marvel here at what I add to you:
afterward with Titus it ran to take vengeance
for the vengeance of the ancient sin.
And when the Lombard tooth bit
the Holy Church, beneath its wings
Charlemagne, conquering, came to its aid.
Now you can judge those men
whom I accused above, and their faults,
which are the cause of all your woes.
One, against the public standard, the yellow lilies
sets up, and the other appropriates that standard for a faction,
so that it is hard to see who errs more.
Let the Ghibellines practice, let them practice their schemes
under another standard, for badly follows that one
always whoever separates justice from it;
and let not this new Charles strike it down
with his Guelfs, but let him fear the talons
that stripped the hide from a mightier lion.
Many times already have the children wept
for the father's guilt, and let him not believe
that God will change his arms for his lilies!
This little star is adorned
with the good spirits who were active
so that honor and fame might follow them:
and when desires rise toward this,
thus straying, the rays must surely
of true love rise upward less brightly.
But in the measuring of our rewards
against our merit lies part of our joy,
because we see them neither less nor greater.
Thus the living justice sweetens
affection in us so much that it cannot
ever be twisted toward any wickedness.
Different voices make sweet notes;
thus different seats in our life
render sweet harmony among these spheres.
And within this present pearl
shines the light of Romeo, whose
great and beautiful work was ill received.
But the Provençals who acted against him
have not laughed; and thus an evil path is walked
by whoever takes another's good deeds as his own harm.
Four daughters he had, and each a queen,
Raimond Berenger, and this was achieved for him by
Romeo, a humble person and a stranger.
And then the crooked words moved him
to demand an accounting from this just man,
who rendered him seven and five for ten,
then departed poor and old;
and if the world knew the heart he had
begging for his livelihood morsel by morsel,
it praises him greatly, and would praise him more.”
