O you who are in a very little boat,
eager to listen, having followed
behind my vessel that passes onward singing,
turn back to see your shores again:
do not venture onto the open sea, for perhaps,
losing me, you would remain astray.
The water I embark upon has never before been sailed;
Minerva breathes, and Apollo guides me,
and nine Muses show me the Bears.
You other few who raised your necks
early toward the bread of angels, on which
one lives here, but never comes away sated,
you may indeed set upon the high salt sea
your vessel, keeping to my furrow
ahead of the water that grows level again.
Those glorious men who crossed to Colchis
did not marvel as you will,
when they saw Jason made a ploughman.
The innate and perpetual thirst
for the godlike kingdom bore us onward
swift almost as you see the heavens move.
Beatrice gazed upward, and I gazed at her;
and perhaps in as much time as a bolt comes to rest
and flies and is released from the catch,
I found myself arrived where a wondrous thing
turned my gaze toward itself; and so she
from whom my concern could not be hidden,
turned toward me, as joyful as she was beautiful,
“Direct your mind in gratitude to God,” she told me,
“who has joined us with the first star.”
It seemed to me that a cloud enveloped us,
luminous, thick, solid, and polished,
like a diamond struck by the sun.
Within itself the eternal pearl
received us, as water receives
a ray of light while remaining whole.
If I was a body, and here it cannot be conceived
how one dimension admitted another,
as must happen if body enters into body,
this should kindle in us still more desire
to see that essence in which one sees
how our nature and God were united.
There will be seen what we hold by faith,
not demonstrated, but it will be known in itself
like the first truth that a person accepts.
I replied: “Lady, as devoutly
as I possibly can, I thank him
who has removed me from the mortal world.
But tell me: what are the dark marks
of this body, which down there on earth
make people tell tales of Cain?”
She smiled a little, and then “If it errs,
the opinion,” she told me, “of mortals
where the key of sense does not unlock,
surely the arrows should not pierce you
of wonder now, since following the senses
you see that reason has short wings.
But tell me what you yourself think of it.”
And I: “What appears different to us up here
I believe is caused by rare and dense bodies.”
And she: “Certainly you will see deeply submerged
in falsehood your belief, if you listen well
to the argument I shall make against it.
The eighth sphere shows you many
lights, which in both quality and quantity
can be observed to have different aspects.
If rarity and density alone produced this,
one single power would be in them all,
distributed more, less, or equally.
Different powers must be fruits
of formal principles, and those, except for one,
would consequently be destroyed by your reasoning.
Further, if rarity were of that darkness
the cause you ask about, either through and through in places
would be so lacking in its matter
this planet, or, just as there are distributed
the fat and the lean by a body, so this one
would change pages within its volume.
If the first were so, it would be evident
during an eclipse of the sun, through the shining through
of the light, as when introduced into another rare medium.
This is not so: therefore we must consider
the other case; and if I should refute that other,
your opinion will have been proved false.
If this rare matter does not pass right through,
there must be a boundary beyond which
its contrary allows it to pass no farther;
and from there another's ray is reflected
just as color returns through glass
which conceals lead behind itself.
Now you will say that it appears dark
there, the ray, more than in other places,
because it is reflected there from farther back.
From this objection you can be freed
by experiment, if ever you try it,
which is wont to be the source of the streams of your arts.
Take three mirrors; and place two
at equal distances from you, and the other, farther off,
let your eyes find between the first two.
Facing them, arrange that behind the back
there stands for you a light that lights the three mirrors
and returns to you reflected from them all.
Although in size it does not extend as much,
the more distant image, there you will see
how it must shine equally brightly.
Now, as beneath the blows of the warm rays
the underlying substance of the snow is left bare
of both its former color and its cold,
so, with you left thus in your intellect,
I wish to shape you with light so living
that it will shimmer in your sight.
Within the heaven of divine peace
revolves a body in whose power
lies the being of everything it contains.
The next heaven, which has so many lights,
distributes that being among diverse essences,
distinct from it and contained by it.
The other spheres, through various differences,
the distinctions they have within themselves
dispose toward their ends and their seeds.
These organs of the world thus proceed,
as you now see, from grade to grade,
receiving from above and acting below.
Now watch closely how I proceed
through this passage toward the truth you desire,
so that afterward you may know how to keep to the ford alone.
The movement and power of the holy spheres,
as from the smith comes the hammer's craft,
must proceed from the blessed movers;
and the heaven which so many lights make beautiful,
from the profound mind that turns it
takes its image and makes itself its seal.
And as the soul within your dust
through different members, fitted
to different powers, distributes itself,
so the intelligence its goodness
unfolds, multiplied through the stars,
revolving upon its own unity.
Different power makes a different alloy
with the precious body that it quickens,
within which, like life in you, it binds itself.
Because of the joyful nature from which it derives,
the power mingled through the body shines
like gladness through a living pupil.
From it comes what from light to light
appears different, not from density and rarity;
it is the formal principle that produces,
according to its goodness, the dim and the bright.”
