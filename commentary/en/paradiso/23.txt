Like the little bird, among the beloved boughs,
resting at the nest of its sweet young
through the night that hides things from us,
which, to see their longed-for faces
and to find the food with which to feed them,
in which its heavy labors are welcome to it,
anticipates the hour upon an open bough,
and with burning affection awaits the sun,
gazing fixedly, intent only that dawn should break;
so my lady stood upright
and attentive, turned toward the region
beneath which the sun shows least haste:
so that, as I saw her expectant and eager,
I became like one who, desiring,
would have more, and is content with hoping.
But little time lay between the one and the other moment,
of my waiting, I mean, and of my seeing
the heaven grow brighter and brighter;
and Beatrice said: “Behold the hosts
of Christ's triumph and all the fruit
gathered from the turning of these spheres!”
It seemed to me that all her face was aflame,
and her eyes were so full of gladness
that I must pass on without describing it.
As in the clear nights of the full moon
Trivia smiles among the eternal nymphs
that paint the heaven through all its depths,
I saw above thousands of lamps
a sun that kindled every one of them,
as ours does the lights above;
and through the living light shone forth
the radiant substance, so bright
to my sight that I could not bear it.
O Beatrice, sweet guide and dear!
She said to me: “What overwhelms you
is a power from which nothing can shield itself.
There are the wisdom and the power
that opened the roads between heaven and earth,
for which there once was such long yearning.”
As fire bursts forth from a cloud
through expanding till it finds no room there,
and, against its nature, falls down to earth,
my mind likewise, amid those feasts
made greater, went out of itself,
and what it became it cannot remember.
“Open your eyes and look upon what I am;
you have seen things such that capable
you have been made of sustaining my smile.”
I was like one who comes to himself
from a forgotten vision and strives
in vain to bring it back to mind,
when I heard this offer, worthy
of such gratitude that it is never erased
from the book that records the past.
If now all those tongues should sound
that Polyhymnia and her sisters made
richer with their sweetest milk,
to help me, a thousandth of the truth
would not be reached, in singing the holy smile
and how radiant it made the holy countenance;
and so, in depicting paradise,
the sacred poem must make a leap,
like someone who finds his path cut off.
But whoever considered the weighty theme
and the mortal shoulder that takes it on
would not blame it if beneath that weight it trembles:
it is no stretch of sea for a little boat
that which the daring prow goes cleaving,
nor for a helmsman who spares himself.
“Why does my face so enamor you
that you do not turn toward the lovely garden
that blossoms beneath the rays of Christ?
There is the rose in which the divine Word
became flesh; there are the lilies
at whose fragrance the good path was taken.”
Thus Beatrice; and I, who to her counsels
was wholly ready, once more gave myself
to the struggle of my feeble eyes.
As, in a ray of sun that streams pure
through a broken cloud, sometimes a meadow of flowers
my eyes, themselves in shadow, have seen;
so I saw many throngs of splendors,
illumined from above by burning rays,
without seeing the source of that radiance.
O gracious power that so stamps them with your impress,
you raised yourself on high, to grant me room
for my eyes there, which were not strong enough for you.
The name of the lovely flower that I always invoke
both morning and evening wholly drew
my mind to gaze upon the greatest fire;
and when both my eyes were imprinted with
the nature and the greatness of the living star
that triumphs there above as here below it triumphed,
through the heaven descended a little flame,
formed into a circle like a crown,
and it encircled her and revolved around her.
Whatever melody sounds sweetest
here below and most draws the soul toward itself
would seem a cloud that, rent apart, thunders,
compared with the sound of that lyre
with which was crowned the lovely sapphire
with which the brightest heaven is ensapphired.
“I am angelic love, who circle
the lofty joy that breathes from the womb
that was the dwelling of our desire;
and I shall circle, lady of heaven, until the time
when you follow your son, and make radiant
still more the highest sphere by entering there.”
Thus the circling melody
came to its close, and all the other lights
made the name of Mary resound.
The royal mantle of all the revolving spheres
of the world, which burns more fiercely and grows more alive
in the breath of God and in his ways,
had its inner shore above us
so far away that its appearance,
there where I was, was not yet visible:
therefore my eyes did not have the power
to follow the crowned flame
that rose after her offspring.
And like a little child who toward its mother
stretches its arms, after taking milk,
because of the feeling that flames even outward;
each of those radiances stretched upward
with its summit, so that the deep affection
that they bore toward Mary became clear to me.
Then they remained there in my sight,
singing ‘Regina celi’ so sweetly
that the delight has never left me.
Oh how great is the abundance stored
in those richest chests that were
good fields for sowing here below!
There one lives and rejoices in the treasure
that was acquired weeping in the exile
of Babylon, where the gold was left behind.
There triumphs, beneath the exalted Son
of God and of Mary, in his victory,
and with the ancient and the new assembly,
he who holds the keys of such glory.
