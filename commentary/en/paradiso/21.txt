Already my eyes were fixed again upon the face
of my lady, and my mind with them,
and it had withdrawn from every other concern.
And she was not smiling; but, “If I were to smile,”
she began to me, “you would become as
Semele was when she was turned to ashes:
for my beauty, which upon the stairs
of the eternal palace burns more brightly,
as you have seen, the higher one ascends,
if it were not tempered, shines so greatly,
that your mortal power, before its splendor,
would be a branch that a thunderbolt shatters.
We have been raised to the seventh radiance,
which beneath the breast of the burning Lion
now beams downward mingled with its power.
Fix your mind behind your eyes,
and make of them mirrors for the figure
that in this mirror will appear to you.”
Whoever knew what was the nourishment
of my sight in that blessed countenance
when I turned to another concern,
would know how pleasing it was to me
to obey my heavenly guide,
weighing the one side against the other.
Within the crystal that bears the name,
encircling the world, of its beloved ruler
under whom all malice lay dead,
of the color of gold through which a ray shines
I saw a ladder raised upward
so far that my sight could not follow it.
I also saw descending down the steps
so many splendors, that I thought every light
that appears in heaven had poured forth from there.
And as, according to their natural habit,
the jackdaws together, at the beginning of the day,
move to warm their cold feathers;
then some go away without returning,
others turn back to where they started,
and others, wheeling, remain;
such a manner seemed to me to be there
in that sparkling host that came together,
as soon as it struck a certain step.
And the one that stayed nearest to us
grew so bright that I said in thought:
‘I see well the love you signal to me.
But she from whom I await the how and when
of speech and silence remains still; therefore I,
against my desire, do well not to ask.’
Whereupon she, who saw my silence
in the sight of him who sees all,
she said to me: “Release your ardent desire.”
And I began: “My merit
does not make me worthy of your answer;
but for her sake who grants me leave to ask,
blessed life that stays hidden
within your joy, make known to me
the reason that has placed you so near me;
and say why in this sphere is silent
the sweet symphony of paradise,
which sounds so devoutly through the others below.”
“Your hearing is mortal just as your sight is,”
he answered me; “therefore here there is no singing
for the same reason that Beatrice has not smiled.
Down the steps of the holy ladder
I descended so far only to welcome you
with speech and with the light that cloaks me;
nor did greater love make me more eager,
for as much and greater love burns above here,
as the flaming makes plain to you.
But the exalted charity, which makes us servants
ready for the counsel that governs the world,
allots here as you observe.”
“I see well,” I said, “sacred lamp,
how freely given love in this court
suffices to follow eternal providence;
but this is what seems hard for me to discern,
why you alone were predestined
to this office among your companions.”
No sooner had I reached the final word,
than the light made its own middle its center,
spinning like a swiftly turning millstone;
then the love that was within it answered:
“Divine light focuses upon me,
penetrating through this light in which I am enclosed,
whose power, joined with my vision,
raises me so far above myself that I see
the supreme essence from which it is drawn.
From this comes the joy with which I flame;
and thus to my vision, in all its clarity,
I match the brightness of my flame.
But that soul in heaven which shines most brightly,
that seraph whose eye is most fixed on God,
will not satisfy your question,
because so far penetrates into the abyss
of the eternal decree that which you ask,
that it is cut off from every created sight.
And to the mortal world, when you return,
report this, so that it may not presume
to move its feet toward so high a goal again.
The mind, which shines here, on earth smokes;
therefore consider how it can down below
do what it cannot, even though heaven raises it up.”
So did his words set bounds for me,
that I left the question and confined myself
to asking him humbly who he was.
“Between the two shores of Italy rise rocks,
and not very distant from your homeland,
so high that the thunder sounds far below,
and form a hump that is called Catria,
beneath which a hermitage is consecrated,
which is wont to be devoted to worship alone.”
Thus he began his third discourse to me;
and then, continuing, he said: “There
in the service of God I became so steadfast,
so that simply with foods dressed in olive oil
I passed lightly through heat and cold,
content in contemplative thoughts.
That cloister used to yield to these heavens
abundantly; and now it has become barren,
so that soon this must be revealed.
In that place I was Peter Damian,
and Peter the Sinner I was in the house
of Our Lady upon the Adriatic shore.
Little mortal life remained to me,
when I was sought and drawn to that hat,
which is passed on only from bad to worse.
Cephas came, and the great vessel came
of the Holy Spirit, lean and barefoot,
taking food from any lodging.
Now they want on either side someone to prop up
the modern shepherds, and someone to lead them,
so heavy are they, and someone behind to lift them.
They cover their palfreys with their cloaks,
so that two beasts go beneath one skin:
O patience that endures so much!”
At these words I saw more little flames
descend from step to step and wheel about,
and every turn made them more beautiful.
Around this one they came and halted,
and uttered a cry of such mighty sound,
that here it could not be matched;
nor did I understand it, so greatly the thunder overcame me.
