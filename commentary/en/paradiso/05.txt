“If I flame before you in the heat of love
beyond the measure that is seen on earth,
so that I overcome the power of your sight,
do not wonder, for this proceeds
from perfect seeing, which, as it apprehends,
so moves its foot toward the good apprehended.
I see clearly how already shines
within your intellect the eternal light,
which, once seen, alone and always kindles love;
and if some other thing entices your love,
it is nothing but some trace of that light,
poorly understood, that shines through there.
You wish to know whether with other service,
for an unfulfilled vow, so much can be repaid
as to secure the soul against dispute”.
Thus Beatrice began this canto;
and like one who does not interrupt his speech,
she thus continued her holy discourse:
“The greatest gift that God in his generosity
made in creating, and to his goodness
most conformed, and that which he most prizes,
was the freedom of the will;
with which intelligent creatures,
all of them and they alone, were and are endowed.
Now you will see, if you reason from this,
the high worth of a vow, if it is so made
that God consents when you consent;
for, in establishing the pact between God and man,
a sacrificial offering is made of this treasure,
such as I describe; and it is made by its own act.
Therefore what can be given in compensation?
If you think to make good use of what you have offered,
you wish to make a good work out of stolen goods.
You are now certain of the greater point;
but because Holy Church grants dispensations in this,
which seems contrary to the truth I have revealed to you,
you must still sit a little at the table,
because the tough food you have taken
still requires help for your digestion.
Open your mind to what I reveal to you
and fix it within; for it does not make knowledge,
without retaining it, to have understood.
Two things belong to the essence
of this sacrifice: one is that
of which it is made; the other is the agreement.
This last is never canceled
unless fulfilled; and concerning it
I spoke above in such absolute terms:
therefore the Hebrews were obliged
to make offerings nevertheless, though some offering
might be exchanged, as you should know.
The other, which is explained to you as the substance,
may indeed be such that no wrong is done
if it is exchanged for other substance.
But let no one shift the burden upon his shoulder
by his own discretion, without the turning
of both the white key and the yellow;
and consider every substitution foolish,
unless the thing relinquished within the thing taken up
is contained as four is within six.
Therefore whatever weighs so heavily
in its worth that it tips every balance,
cannot be paid for with another expenditure.
Let mortals not take a vow lightly;
be faithful, and in doing so not misguided,
as Jephthah was with his first offering;
for whom it would have been more fitting to say ‘I did wrong’,
than, by keeping it, to do worse; and just as foolish
you may find the great leader of the Greeks,
for whom Iphigenia mourned her fair face,
and made both fools and wise men weep for her
who heard tell of such an act of worship.
Be more deliberate, Christians, in your actions:
do not be like a feather in every wind,
and do not believe that every water washes you clean.
You have the New and the Old Testament,
and the shepherd of the Church who guides you;
let this suffice for your salvation.
If evil greed cries something else to you,
be human beings, and not mad sheep,
so that the Jew among you may not laugh at you!
Do not act like a lamb that leaves the milk
of its mother, and simple and frisky,
fights with itself for its own pleasure!”
Thus Beatrice spoke to me, just as I write;
then she turned, all full of longing,
toward that part where the world is most alive.
Her silence and her change of countenance
imposed silence on my eager mind,
which already had new questions before it;
and just as an arrow that in the target
strikes before the bowstring is still,
so we sped into the second realm.
There I saw my lady so joyful,
as she entered the light of that heaven,
that the planet became more luminous because of it.
And if the star was changed and smiled,
what did I become, who by my very nature
am subject to change in every way!
As in a fishpond that is calm and clear
the fish draw toward what comes from outside
in such a way that they judge it to be their food,
so I saw well over a thousand radiances
draw toward us, and in each was heard:
“Here is one who will increase our loves”.
And as each one came toward us,
the shade could be seen full of gladness
in the bright radiance that issued from it.
Think, reader, if what begins here
were not to continue, how you would feel
an anguished craving to know more;
and through yourself you will see how from these
I longed to hear of their conditions,
as soon as they became apparent to my eyes.
“O you well born, to whom to see the thrones
of eternal triumph is granted by grace
before warfare is abandoned,
by the light that spreads through all of heaven
we are kindled; and therefore, if you desire
to learn of us, satisfy yourself at your pleasure”.
Thus by one of those devout spirits
I was addressed; and by Beatrice: “Speak, speak
with confidence, and believe them as you would gods”.
“I see clearly how you nest
within your own light, and that you draw it from your eyes,
because it flashes as you smile;
but I do not know who you are, nor why you have,
worthy soul, the rank of the sphere
that veils itself from mortals with another's rays”.
This I said directly to the light
that had first spoken to me; whereupon it became
far more radiant than it had been.
Just as the sun conceals itself
through excess of light, when the heat has eaten away
the tempering effects of the dense vapors,
through greater joy, so hid itself from me
within its own radiance the holy figure;
and thus wholly enclosed it answered me
in the manner that the following canto sings.
