There appeared before me with wings spread
the beautiful image that in sweet fruition
the joyful souls joined together were forming;
each appeared a little ruby in which
a ray of sunlight burned so brightly,
that it reflected the sun into my eyes.
And what I must now recount,
no voice ever conveyed, nor ink ever wrote,
nor was it ever grasped by imagination;
for I saw and also heard the beak speak,
and in the voice both “I” and “my” resound,
when in the meaning there were both ‘we’ and ‘our’.
And it began: “For being just and pious
I am here exalted to that glory
which does not let itself be surpassed by desire;
and on earth I left my memory
such that the wicked people there
praise it, but do not follow its example”.
Thus a single heat from many embers
makes itself felt, just as from many loves
a single sound issued from that image.
Then I replied: “O everlasting flowers
of eternal gladness, who but one
make all your fragrances seem to me,
relieve for me, by breathing forth, the great fast
that has long kept me in hunger,
finding no food for it anywhere on earth.
Well I know that, if in heaven another realm
divine justice makes its mirror,
yours does not apprehend it through a veil.
You know how attentively I prepare myself
to listen; you know what is that
doubt that has been so long a fast to me”.
Like a falcon that emerges from its hood,
moves its head and claps its wings,
showing eagerness and making itself beautiful,
I saw that sign behave, which of praises
of divine grace was woven,
with songs such as those who rejoice above know.
Then it began: “He who turned the compass
at the outermost edge of the world, and within it
distinguished so much hidden and manifest,
could not so imprint his power
throughout the universe, that his Word
would not remain infinitely beyond it.
And this is made certain by the fact that the first proud one,
who was the highest of all creatures,
through not awaiting light, fell before his time;
and hence it appears that every lesser nature
is too small a receptacle for that good
which has no end and measures itself by itself.
Thus your sight, which must
be one of the rays of the mind
with which all things are filled,
cannot by its nature be powerful
enough to discern its source
far beyond what is apparent to it.
Therefore into everlasting justice
the sight that your world receives,
like an eye through the sea, penetrates within;
for, although from the shore it sees the bottom,
in the open sea it does not see it; and nonetheless
it is there, but its depth conceals it.
There is no light unless it comes from the clear sky
that is never troubled; otherwise it is darkness
or a shadow of the flesh or its poison.
Now sufficiently laid open to you is the hiding place
that concealed from you the living justice
about which you questioned so frequently;
for you were saying: “A man is born on the bank
of the Indus, and there is no one there who speaks
of Christ, nor anyone who reads or writes;
and all his wishes and deeds good
are, as far as human reason sees,
without sin in life or in speech.
He dies unbaptized and without faith:
where is this justice that condemns him?
where is his fault, if he does not believe?”.
Now who are you, who wish to sit upon the judgment seat,
to judge from a thousand miles away
with sight as short as a handspan?
Certainly, for one who reasons subtly with me,
if Scripture were not set above you,
there would be astonishing cause for doubt.
O earthly creatures! O dull minds!
The primal will, which is good of itself,
from itself, which is the highest good, has never departed.
Only so much is just as accords with it:
no created good draws it toward itself,
but it, radiating, causes that good”.
Just as above the nest wheels round,
after she has fed her young, the stork,
and as the one she has fed looks up at her;
so did it act, and so I raised my eyes,
the blessed image, which its wings
moved, impelled by so many counsels.
Wheeling, it sang, and said: “As
my notes are to you, who do not understand them,
so is eternal judgment to you mortals”.
After those shining flames had grown still
of the Holy Spirit, still in the sign
that made the Romans revered throughout the world,
it began again: “To this kingdom
no one ever ascended who did not believe in Christ,
either before or after he was nailed to the wood.
But see: many cry “Christ, Christ!”,
who at the judgment will be much less near
to him than someone who does not know Christ;
and such Christians the Ethiopian will condemn,
when the two companies are separated,
the one forever rich and the other destitute.
What will the Persians be able to say to your kings,
when they see that volume opened
in which all their contemptible deeds are written?
There will be seen, among Albert's deeds,
the one that soon will set the pen in motion,
through which the kingdom of Prague will be laid waste.
There will be seen the suffering that upon the Seine
he brings about, by debasing the coinage,
the one who will die from a blow of boar's hide.
There will be seen the pride that arouses thirst,
that makes the Scot and the Englishman mad,
so that neither can abide within his borders.
There will be seen the lust and the soft living
of the one of Spain and the one of Bohemia,
who never knew nor wished for virtue.
There will be seen against the Lame One of Jerusalem
his goodness marked with an I,
while an M will mark its opposite.
There will be seen the avarice and the baseness
of the one who guards the island of fire,
where Anchises ended his long life;
and to make understood how little he is worth,
his record will be in shortened letters,
which will note much in little space.
And to everyone will appear the foul deeds
of the uncle and the brother, who so illustrious
a nation and two crowns have rendered bastard.
And the one of Portugal and of Norway
will be known there, and the one of Rascia
who has looked upon the coinage of Venice to his own harm.
O blessed Hungary, if she does not let herself
be mistreated any longer! And blessed Navarre,
if she would arm herself with the mountain that girds her!
And everyone should believe that already, as a pledge
of this, Nicosia and Famagusta
lament and cry out because of their beast,
which does not depart from the flank of the others”.
