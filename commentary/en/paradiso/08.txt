The world used to believe, to its peril,
that the beautiful Cyprian the mad love
beamed forth, revolving in the third epicycle;
wherefore not to her alone paid honor
with sacrifice and votive cries
the ancient peoples in their ancient error;
but they honored Dione and Cupid,
the former as her mother, the latter as her son,
and they said that he sat in Dido's lap;
and from her with whom I make my beginning
they took the name of the star
that woos the sun now from behind, now from before.
I did not notice my ascent into it;
but that I was within it was made certain enough to me by
my lady, whom I saw grow more beautiful.
And as a spark is seen within a flame,
and as one voice is distinguished within another,
when one is steady and the other comes and goes,
I saw within that light other lamps
moving in a circle, more and less swiftly,
according, I believe, to their inward visions.
No winds descended from a cold cloud,
whether visible or not, so swiftly
that they would not have seemed hindered and slow
to anyone who had those divine lights
seen coming toward us, leaving the circling
first begun among the lofty Seraphim;
and within those that appeared furthest forward
‘Hosanna’ sounded so that ever since
I have not been without desire to hear it again.
Then one of them drew nearer to us
and alone began: “We all are ready
at your pleasure, that you may have joy of us.
We revolve with the celestial princes
in one circle, one revolving, and one thirst,
to whom you, from the world, once said:
‘You who through understanding move the third heaven’;
and we are so full of love that, to please you,
a little rest will be no less sweet”.
After my eyes had been offered
reverently to my lady, and she
had made them content and assured of her,
they turned back to the light that had promised
so much, and “Pray, who are you?” was
my voice, impressed with great affection.
And how much greater and brighter I saw it grow
through the new gladness that was added,
when I spoke, to its gladness!
Thus transformed, it said to me: “The world had me
below for a short time; and if it had been longer,
much evil will occur that would not occur.
My joy keeps me hidden from you,
radiating about me and concealing me
like a creature wrapped in its own silk.
You loved me greatly, and you had good reason;
for if I had remained below, I would have shown you
of my love more than the leaves.
That left bank which is washed
by the Rhone after it mingles with the Sorgue,
awaited me in due time as its lord,
and that horn of Ausonia which has for its towns
Bari and Gaeta and Catona,
from where the Tronto and Verde flow into the sea.
Already upon my brow shone the crown
of that land which the Danube waters
after it leaves the German banks.
And beautiful Trinacria, which grows dark with smoke
between Pachino and Peloro, above the gulf
that receives the greatest buffeting from Eurus,
not because of Typhoeus but of rising sulfur,
would still have awaited its kings,
born through me of Charles and Rudolf,
if bad lordship, which always afflicts
subject peoples, had not
moved Palermo to cry: “Death, death!”.
And if my brother foresaw this,
the avaricious poverty of Catalonia
he would already shun, so that it might not harm him;
for truly provision must be made
by him, or by another, so that upon his boat,
already laden, no further load is placed.
His nature, which miserly from a generous one
descended, would have need of such retainers
as would not care to put money into a chest”.
“Because I believe that the deep joy
which your speech pours into me, my lord,
there where every good ends and begins,
is seen by you as I see it,
it is more welcome to me; and this too I hold dear
because you discern it gazing into God.
You have made me glad, and now make clear to me,
since, by speaking, you have moved me to question
how there can be, from sweet seed, bitter fruit”.
This I said to him; and he to me: “If I can
show you a truth, toward what you ask
you will turn your face as now you turn your back.
The Good that all the kingdom you ascend
turns and satisfies, makes a power
of its providence within these great bodies.
And not only are the natures foreseen
within the mind that is perfect of itself,
but they together with their well-being:
therefore whatever this bow shoots
falls disposed toward a foreseen end,
like something directed toward its target.
If this were not so, the heaven you traverse
would so produce its effects
that they would be not works of art, but ruins;
and this cannot be, if the intellects
that move these stars are not defective,
and defective the first, which has not perfected them.
Do you wish this truth made clearer to you?”.
And I: “No indeed; for I see it impossible
that nature should fail in what is necessary”.
Then he continued: “Now tell me: would it be worse
for man on earth if he were not a citizen?”.
“Yes”, I answered; “and here I ask for no proof”.
“And can that be, if below people do not live
differently through different offices?
No, if your master writes rightly for you”.
Thus he proceeded in his reasoning up to this point;
then he concluded: “Therefore to be different
is necessary for the roots of your effects:
hence one is born Solon and another Xerxes,
another Melchizedek and another he
who, flying through the air, lost his son.
The circling nature, which is a seal
upon mortal wax, performs its art well,
but does not distinguish one dwelling from another.
Hence it comes that Esau differs
in his seed from Jacob; and Quirinus comes
from so base a father that he is ascribed to Mars.
By begotten nature its course
would always be made like that of its begetters,
if divine providence did not prevail.
Now what was behind you is before you:
but so that you may know that I delight in you,
I would have a corollary cloak you.
Always nature, if it finds fortune
at variance with itself, like every other seed
outside its proper region, fares badly.
And if the world below paid heed
to the foundation that nature lays,
following it, it would have worthy people.
But you twist toward religious life
one who will have been born to gird on the sword,
and you make a king of one fit for preaching;
thus your course is off the road”.
