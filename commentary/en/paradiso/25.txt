If it should ever happen that the sacred poem
to which both heaven and earth have set their hand,
so that it has made me lean for many years,
should overcome the cruelty that keeps me outside
the beautiful sheepfold where I slept as a lamb,
an enemy to the wolves that wage war on it;
with another voice by then, with another fleece
I shall return as a poet, and upon the font
of my baptism I shall receive the crown;
because into the faith, which makes known
souls to God, I entered there, and afterward
Peter for its sake thus encircled my brow.
Then a light moved toward us
from that sphere whence came forth the first
of his vicars whom Christ left behind;
and my lady, full of joy,
said to me: “Look, look: here is the baron
for whose sake Galicia is visited down below.”
Just as when a dove settles
beside its companion, each reveals to the other,
circling and murmuring, its affection;
so I saw the one great
glorious prince welcomed by the other,
praising the food that feeds them there above.
But after their greeting was completed,
each stood fixed and silent before me,
so fiery that it overcame my sight.
Then, smiling, Beatrice said:
“Illustrious life through whom the bounty
of our basilica was written,
make hope resound in this height:
you know, who figure it as many times
as Jesus showed greater favor to the three.”
“Raise your head and take courage:
for whatever comes up here from the mortal world,
must ripen in our rays.”
This encouragement from the second fire
came to me; so I raised my eyes to the mountains
that had bent them down before with too great a weight.
“Since by grace he wills that you should meet,
our Emperor, before your death,
in his most secret hall with his counts,
so that, having seen the truth of this court,
the hope that rightly inspires love down below
you may thereby strengthen in yourself and others,
say what it is, say how there flowers with it
your mind, and say whence it came to you.”
Thus the second light continued further.
And that gracious lady who guided the feathers
of my wings to so high a flight,
thus anticipated me in answering:
“The Church militant has no child
with greater hope, as is written
in the Sun that irradiates all our company:
therefore it is granted to him that from Egypt
he should come into Jerusalem to see,
before his term of warfare is completed.
The other two points, which not for knowledge
are asked, but so that he may report
how much this virtue pleases you,
I leave to him, for they will not be difficult for him
nor cause for boasting; and let him answer this,
and may the grace of God enable him to do so.”
Like a student who follows his teacher,
ready and willing in what he knows well,
so that his ability may be revealed,
“Hope,” I said, “is a certain expectation
of future glory, which is produced by
divine grace and preceding merit.
From many stars this light comes to me;
but he first distilled it into my heart
who was the highest singer of the highest leader.
‘Let them hope in you’, in his song to God
he says, ‘those who know your name’:
and who does not know it, if he has my faith?
You instilled it in me, together with his instilling,
in the epistle afterward; so that I am full,
and upon others I rain down your rain again.”
While I was speaking, within the living heart
of that blaze a gleam was quivering,
sudden and frequent, like lightning.
Then it breathed: “The love with which I burn
still toward the virtue that followed me
to the palm of victory and my departure from the field,
wills that I breathe to you who take delight
in it; and it pleases me that you should say
what hope promises you.”
And I: “The new and the ancient scriptures
set forth the goal, and it points this out to me,
of the souls whom God has made his friends.
Isaiah says that each clothed
in its own land shall be in a double garment:
and its own land is this sweet life;
and your brother, much more fully elaborated,
where he treats of the white robes,
makes this revelation manifest to us.”
And at once, after the end of these words,
‘Let them hope in you’ was heard above us;
to which all the circling dancers responded.
Then among them a light grew bright,
so that, if Cancer had such a crystal,
winter would have a month of a single day.
And as a maiden rises and goes and enters the dance,
joyful, solely to do honor
to the bride, not from any unworthy motive,
so I saw the brightened splendor
come to the two who were turning to a melody
such as befitted their ardent love.
It joined there in the song and in the circling;
and my lady kept her gaze upon them,
just like a bride, silent and motionless.
“This is he who reclined upon the breast
of our pelican, and this is he who was
from upon the cross chosen for the great office.”
Thus my lady; yet no more
was her gaze moved from its attentive watching
after her words than before them.
Like one who looks intently and endeavors
to see the sun become a little eclipsed,
who, through trying to see, becomes unable to see;
so did I become before that last fire
while it was said: “Why do you dazzle yourself
to see a thing that has no place here?
On earth my body is earth, and there it will remain
with the others until our number
accords with the eternal purpose.
With the two robes in the blessed cloister
are only the two lights that ascended;
and this you shall carry back to your world.”
At this voice the flaming circle
grew still, together with the sweet mingling
made in the sound of the threefold breath,
just as, to avoid fatigue or danger,
the oars, before repeatedly striking the water,
all come to rest at the sound of a whistle.
Ah, how deeply I was troubled in my mind,
when I turned to see Beatrice,
because I could not see, although I was
near her, and in the happy world!
