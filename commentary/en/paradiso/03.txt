That sun which first warmed my breast with love,
of beautiful truth had uncovered for me,
by proving and disproving, the sweet aspect;
and I, to confess corrected and certain
myself, as far as was fitting,
raised my head higher to speak;
but a vision appeared that held
me so closely to itself, to be seen,
that I forgot my confession.
As through transparent and polished glass,
or through clear and tranquil waters,
not so deep that their bottoms are lost,
the traces of our faces return
so faint that a pearl upon a white forehead
comes no less strongly to our pupils;
such I saw several faces ready to speak;
so that I rushed into the opposite error
to that which kindled love between the man and the spring.
As soon as I became aware of them,
judging them to be reflected images,
I turned my eyes to see whose they were;
and saw nothing, and turned them forward again,
straight into the light of my sweet guide,
who, smiling, glowed in her holy eyes.
“Do not wonder that I smile,”
she told me, “at your childish thought,
since it does not yet trust its footing on the truth,
but turns you, as usual, toward emptiness:
what you see are real substances,
assigned here for a deficiency in their vows.
Therefore speak with them, and hear and believe;
for the true light that satisfies them
does not let them turn their feet away from itself.”
And I toward the shade that seemed most eager
to converse turned, and began,
almost like a man whom excessive longing bewilders:
“O spirit created for blessedness, who in the rays
of eternal life feel that sweetness
which, untasted, can never be understood,
it will be gracious to me if you satisfy me
concerning your name and your allotted state.”
At which she, readily and with smiling eyes:
“Our charity closes no doors
to a just desire, any more than does that love
which wills its whole court to be like itself.
I was a virgin sister in the world;
and if your mind looks carefully within itself,
my being more beautiful will not hide me from you,
but you will recognize that I am Piccarda,
who, placed here with these other blessed souls,
am blessed in the slowest sphere.
Our affections, whose only kindling
is in the pleasure of the Holy Spirit,
rejoice, shaped by his order.
And this allotted place, which seems so low,
is given us because neglected were
our vows, and void in some respect.”
Then I to her: “In the wondrous countenances
of yours there shines something divine I cannot name,
which transforms you from my first conceptions:
therefore I was not quick to remember;
but now what you tell me helps me,
so that recognizing you is easier for me.
But tell me: you who are happy here,
do you desire a higher place
to see more and to make yourselves more beloved?”
With those other shades she first smiled a little;
then answered me so joyfully,
that she seemed to burn in love's first fire:
“Brother, our will is quieted by
the power of charity, which makes us will
only what we have, and makes us thirst for nothing else.
If we desired to be higher,
our desires would be at variance
with the will of him who assigns us here;
which you will see can find no place within these spheres,
if dwelling in charity is necessary here,
and if you consider its nature well.
Rather, it is essential to this blessed being
to remain within the divine will,
through which our very wills become one;
so that our being from threshold to threshold
throughout this kingdom pleases all the kingdom,
as it does the king who makes us will his will.
And in his will is our peace:
it is that sea toward which everything moves
that it creates or that nature makes.”
Then it was clear to me how every place
in heaven is paradise, although the grace
of the highest good does not rain there in one measure.
But just as it happens, if one food satisfies
and an appetite for another still remains,
that one is asked for and the other thanked for,
so did I in gesture and in word,
to learn from her what was the web
through which she did not draw the shuttle to the end.
“Perfect life and lofty merit place in heaven
a lady higher up,” she told me, “according to whose rule
in your world below one takes the habit and veil,
so that until death one may wake and sleep
with that bridegroom who accepts every vow
that charity conforms to his pleasure.
From the world, to follow her, as a young girl
I fled, and enclosed myself in her habit
and pledged myself to the path of her order.
Then men, more accustomed to evil than to good,
abducted me from the sweet cloister:
God knows what my life was afterward.
And this other radiance that appears to you
on my right side and that is kindled
with all the light of our sphere,
understands of herself what I say of myself;
she was a sister, and likewise from her head was taken
the shelter of the sacred veil.
But after she was nevertheless turned back to the world
against her will and against right custom,
she was never released from the veil of the heart.
This is the light of the great Constance
who by the second wind of Swabia
bore the third and the final power.”
Thus she spoke to me, and then began ‘Hail,
Mary’ singing, and singing vanished
like a heavy thing through deep water.
My gaze, which followed her as long
as was possible, once it lost her,
turned toward the object of greater desire,
and directed itself wholly to Beatrice;
but she flashed like lightning into my gaze
so that at first my sight could not endure it;
and this made me slower to ask.
