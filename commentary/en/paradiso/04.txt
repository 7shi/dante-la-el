Between two foods, distant and enticing
in equal measure, one would sooner die of hunger,
than a free man would bring one to his teeth;
so would a lamb stand between two hungers
of fierce wolves, fearing equally;
so would a dog stand between two does:
therefore, if I kept silent, I do not blame myself,
driven in equal measure by my doubts,
since it was necessary, nor do I praise myself.
I kept silent, but my desire was painted
upon my face, and my question with it,
far more ardently than through distinct speech.
Beatrice did as Daniel did,
lifting Nebuchadnezzar out of anger,
which had made him unjustly cruel;
and said: “I see well how you are drawn
by one desire and the other, so that your concern
binds itself so that it finds no outward breath.
You reason: ‘If good will endures,
for what reason does another's violence
diminish the measure of my merit?’.
Further cause for doubt is given you
by the souls' seeming to return to the stars,
according to the judgment of Plato.
These are the questions that within your will
press equally; and therefore first
I shall treat the one that has more gall.
The one among the Seraphim who most enters into God,
Moses, Samuel, and whichever John
you choose, I say, not to speak of Mary,
have their seats in no other heaven
than these spirits who just now appeared to you,
nor have they more or fewer years of being;
but all make beautiful the first circuit,
and differently possess sweet life
through feeling more and less the eternal breath.
Here they showed themselves, not because allotted
this sphere is to them, but to give a sign
of the heavenly degree that has the least height.
Thus must one speak to your understanding,
because only from sense-perception does it apprehend
what it afterward makes worthy of the intellect.
For this reason Scripture condescends
to your capacity, and feet and hand
attributes to God, and means something else;
and Holy Church in human likeness
represents Gabriel and Michael to you,
and the other who made Tobias well again.
What Timaeus argues concerning souls
is not like what is seen here,
because, as he speaks, he seems to mean.
He says that the soul returns to its own star,
believing it to have been severed from there
when nature gave it as a form;
and perhaps his meaning is of another kind
than his words sound, and may be
of an intention not to be derided.
If he means to return to these wheels
the honor of influence and the blame, perhaps
his bow strikes upon some truth.
This principle, misunderstood, once led astray
almost the whole world, so that into naming Jupiter,
Mercury, and Mars it went astray.
The other doubt that troubles you
has less poison, because its evil
could not lead you elsewhere, away from me.
That our justice should seem unjust
in the eyes of mortals, is a matter
of faith and not of heretical wickedness.
But because your understanding can
indeed penetrate this truth,
I shall satisfy you, as you desire.
If violence is when the one who suffers
contributes nothing to the one who compels,
these souls were not excused by it:
for will, unless it wills, is not quenched,
but acts as nature acts in fire,
though violence twist it a thousand times.
Therefore, if it bends much or little,
it follows force; and so these souls did,
though able to flee back to the holy place.
If their will had been whole,
as it held Lawrence upon the gridiron,
and made Mucius severe toward his hand,
so would it have driven them back along the road
by which they had been dragged, as soon as they were freed;
but so steadfast a will is all too rare.
And by these words, if you have gathered
them as you should, the argument is annulled
that would have troubled you many times again.
But now another difficult passage crosses
before your eyes, such that by yourself
you could not emerge: you would first be weary.
I have put it in your mind as certain
that a blessed soul could not lie,
because it is always close to the primal truth;
and then you were able to hear from Piccarda
that Constance kept her affection for the veil;
so that she seems here to contradict me.
Many times already, brother, it has happened
that, to escape danger, against one's liking
one did what ought not to have been done;
as Alcmaeon, who, entreated to do this
by his father, slew his own mother,
not to lose piety, made himself pitiless.
On this point I wish you to consider
that force mingles with will, and they bring it about
that the offenses cannot be excused.
Absolute will does not consent to the wrong;
but it consents to it insofar as it fears,
if it draws back, falling into greater distress.
Therefore, when Piccarda expresses that,
she means the absolute will, and I
the other; so that we both speak truth.”
Such was the flowing of the holy stream
that issued from the fountain whence all truth derives;
thus it brought both one desire and the other to peace.
“O beloved of the primal lover, O divine lady,”
I said then, “whose speech floods me
and warms me so that it quickens me more and more,
my affection is not so profound
that it suffices to render you grace for grace;
but may he who sees and has the power answer for this.
I see clearly that never is satisfied
our intellect, unless the truth illuminates it
outside which no truth extends.
It rests in that, like a beast in its lair,
as soon as it has reached it; and it can reach it:
if not, every desire would be in vain.
Through that, in the manner of a young shoot, is born
at the foot of truth, doubt; and it is nature
that drives us toward the summit from hill to hill.
This invites me, this gives me confidence
reverently, lady, to ask you
about another truth that is obscure to me.
I wish to know whether a person can make satisfaction to you
for unfulfilled vows with other good deeds, so
that in your scales they may not be insufficient.”
Beatrice looked at me with her eyes full
of sparks of love so divine,
that, overcome, my power turned in flight,
and I almost lost myself with my eyes lowered.
