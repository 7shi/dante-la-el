From the center to the circle, and likewise from the circle to the center
the water moves in a round vessel,
according to whether it is struck outside or within:
in my mind there suddenly occurred
this that I tell, as soon as fell silent
the glorious life of Thomas,
through the likeness that arose
between his speech and that of Beatrice,
who was pleased, after him, to begin thus:
“This man has need, and does not tell it to you
either with his voice or even in thought as yet,
to reach the root of another truth.
Tell him whether the light with which is adorned
your substance, will remain with you
eternally just as it is now;
and if it remains, say how, afterward,
when you have been made visible again,
it will be possible for it not to harm your sight.”
As, impelled and drawn by greater gladness,
sometimes those who dance in a ring
raise their voices and enliven their movements,
so, at the ready and devout entreaty,
the holy circles showed new joy
in their wheeling and in their wondrous song.
Whoever laments because here one dies
to live up there, did not see here
the refreshment of the eternal rain.
That one and two and three who lives forever
and reigns forever in three and in two and in one,
not circumscribed, and circumscribing everything,
was sung three times by each
of those spirits with such melody,
that it would be a just reward for every merit.
And I heard within the most divine light
of the smaller circle a gentle voice,
perhaps such as came from the angel to Mary,
reply: “As long as shall last the feast
of paradise, so long our love
shall radiate such a garment around itself.
Its brightness follows the ardor;
the ardor the vision, and that is as great,
as the grace it has beyond its own capacity.
When the glorious and holy flesh
has been put on again, our person
will be more pleasing through being complete;
whereby there will increase what is bestowed on us
of freely given light by the highest good,
light that fits us to behold him;
hence our vision must increase,
the ardor kindled by it increase,
the radiance that comes from that increase.
But just as a coal that gives forth flame,
and surpasses it in living brightness,
so that its own appearance is preserved;
so this radiance that already surrounds us
will be surpassed in visibility by the flesh
which the earth still covers;
nor will so much light be able to weary us:
for the organs of the body will be strong
for everything that will be able to delight us.”
So prompt and eager did they seem to me,
both the one choir and the other, in saying “Amen!”,
that they clearly showed desire for their dead bodies:
perhaps not only for themselves, but for their mothers,
for their fathers and for the others who were dear
before they became everlasting flames.
And behold, all around, of equal brightness,
a radiance arose beyond what was there,
like a horizon growing bright.
And just as at the coming of early evening
new appearances begin throughout the sky,
so that the sight seems and does not seem real,
it seemed to me that there new subsistences
I began to see, and that they formed a ring
outside the other two circumferences.
O true sparkling of the Holy Spirit!
how it became sudden and incandescent
to my eyes, which, overcome, could not endure it!
But Beatrice, so beautiful and smiling,
appeared to me, that among those sights
which my mind could not follow she must be left.
From this my eyes regained strength
to raise themselves; and I saw myself transported
alone with my lady into higher blessedness.
I clearly perceived that I had risen higher,
through the fiery smile of the star,
which seemed to me redder than usual.
With all my heart and with that speech
which is one in everyone, I made a whole burnt offering to God,
such as befitted the new grace.
And not yet was exhausted from my breast
the ardor of the sacrifice, when I knew
that offering to have been accepted and auspicious;
for with such brilliance and such redness
splendors appeared to me within two rays,
that I said: “O Helios, who so adorns them!”
As, marked out by lesser and greater
lights, between the poles of the world gleams white
the Milky Way, so that it makes even wise men doubt;
so, studded with stars, in the depths of
Mars those rays made the venerable sign
that the joining of quadrants makes within a circle.
Here my memory surpasses my intellect;
for that cross flashed forth Christ,
so that I cannot find a worthy comparison;
but whoever takes up his cross and follows Christ,
will yet excuse me for what I leave out,
seeing Christ flash in that radiance.
From arm to arm and between the top and bottom
lights were moving, sparkling brightly
as they joined together and passed one another:
so here are seen, straight and crooked,
swift and slow, changing their appearance,
the particles of bodies, long and short,
moving through the ray that streaks
sometimes the shade which, for their protection,
people contrive with ingenuity and skill.
And as fiddle and harp, with the tuned tension
of many strings, make a sweet chiming
to one by whom the tune is not understood,
so from the lights that appeared to me there
a melody gathered along the cross
that enraptured me, without my understanding the hymn.
I clearly perceived that it was of exalted praises,
because to me came “Arise” and “Conquer”
as to someone who does not understand and hears.
I was so enamored by this,
that until then there had been nothing
that bound me with such sweet bonds.
Perhaps my words seem too bold,
placing below this the delight of those lovely eyes,
gazing into which my desire finds rest;
but whoever perceives that the living seals
of every beauty work more powerfully the higher they are,
and that I had not turned toward those eyes there,
can excuse me for what I accuse myself of
to excuse myself, and see that I speak the truth:
for holy delight is not excluded here,
because, in ascending, it becomes purer.
