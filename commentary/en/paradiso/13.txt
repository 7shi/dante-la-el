Let whoever longs to understand well imagine
what I just saw—and let him retain the image,
while I speak, like a steadfast rock—,
fifteen stars that in different regions
enliven heaven with so serene a brightness
that it surpasses every density of the air;
imagine that Wain for which the bosom
of our sky suffices both night and day,
so that at the turning of its pole it does not vanish;
imagine the mouth of that horn
that begins at the tip of the axis
around which the first wheel turns,
to have made of themselves two signs in heaven,
such as the daughter of Minos made
when she felt the chill of death;
and the one to have its rays within the other,
and both to turn in such a manner
that one should go forward and the other backward;
and he will have almost the shadow of the true
constellation and of the double dance
that circled the point where I was:
since it is as far beyond our experience,
as beyond the motion of the Chiana
moves the heaven that outstrips all the others.
There they sang not Bacchus, not Paean,
but three persons in divine nature,
and in one person that nature and the human.
The singing and the turning completed their measure;
and those holy lights turned their attention to us,
making themselves happy from care to care.
The silence among the harmonious divinities was broken
then by the light in which the wondrous life
of God's poor man had been told to me,
and it said: “When the one sheaf has been threshed,
when its seed has already been stored,
sweet love invites me to thresh the other.
You believe that into the breast from which the rib
was drawn to form the beautiful cheek
whose palate costs the whole world,
and into that which, pierced by the lance,
both before and after made such satisfaction,
that it outweighs every sin in the balance,
as much as human nature is permitted
to have of light, was all infused
by that power which made the one and the other;
and therefore you wonder at what I said above,
when I declared that no second had
the good enclosed within the fifth light.
Now open your eyes to what I answer you,
and you will see your belief and my words
become in truth like the center in a circle.
What does not die and what can die
is nothing but radiance of that Idea
which our Lord begets, loving;
for that living light which so streams
from its radiant source that it does not separate itself
from him nor from the love that makes a third with them,
through its goodness it concentrates its radiance,
as though reflected, in nine subsistences,
eternally remaining one.
From there it descends to the lowest powers
down from act to act, becoming such
that it makes only brief contingencies;
and by these contingencies I mean
the generated things, which are produced
with seed and without seed by heaven in motion.
The wax of these things and what shapes it
do not remain in one condition; and therefore beneath the stamp
of the Idea it then shines through more and less.
Hence it happens that one and the same tree,
in respect of species, bears better or worse fruit;
and you are born with differing talents.
If the wax were brought to perfect readiness
and heaven were at its highest power,
all the light of the seal would appear;
but nature always gives it diminished,
working in a way similar to the artist
who, possessing artistic skill, has a trembling hand.
Therefore if ardent love the clear vision
of the primal power disposes and stamps,
all perfection is acquired there.
Thus the earth was once made worthy
of all the perfection of animate life;
thus the Virgin was made pregnant;
so that I commend your opinion,
that human nature never was
nor will be what it was in those two persons.
Now if I were to go no further,
‘Then how was this man without equal?’
your words would begin.
But so that what is not clear may be quite clear,
think who he was, and what cause moved him,
when he was told “Ask”, to make his request.
I have not spoken so that you cannot
clearly see that he was a king who asked for wisdom
so that he might be an adequate king;
not to know the number in which exist
the movers here above, or whether the necessary
with the contingent ever made the necessary;
not whether a first motion can be granted,
or whether within a semicircle one can make
a triangle such that it has no right angle.
Therefore, if you note what I said and this,
kingly prudence is that unequalled insight
which the arrow of my intention strikes;
and if you turn your clear eyes to “rose”,
you will see that it refers only
to kings, who are many, while the good are rare.
Take my statement with this distinction;
and thus it can stand with what you believe
of the first father and of our Beloved.
And let this always be lead at your feet,
to make you move slowly like a weary man
toward both the yes and the no that you do not see:
for he ranks very low among the fools,
who without distinction affirms and denies
in the one case as in the other;
because it happens that often inclines
hasty opinion toward the false side,
and then attachment binds the intellect.
Far worse than in vain does he leave the shore,
since he does not return as he sets out,
who fishes for the truth and lacks the art.
And of this there are clear proofs in the world:
Parmenides, Melissus, and Bryson, and many others,
who went onward and did not know where;
so did Sabellius and Arius and those fools
who were like swords to the Scriptures
in making their straight faces crooked.
Nor should people be too confident
in judging, like one who estimates
the crops in the field before they are ripe;
for I have seen throughout the winter first
the thorn show itself stiff and fierce,
then bear the rose upon its summit;
and I once saw a ship, straight and swift,
run across the sea throughout its course,
perish at last at the entrance to the harbor.
Let Dame Berta and Master Martin not believe,
because they see one stealing, another making an offering,
that they see them within the divine counsel;
for the former may rise, and the latter may fall.”
