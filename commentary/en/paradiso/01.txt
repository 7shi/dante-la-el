The glory of the one who moves all things
penetrates through the universe, and shines
in one part more, and less elsewhere.
In the heaven that receives most of his light
was I, and saw things that to recount
one who descends from up there neither knows how nor has the power;
because, as it draws near to its desire,
our intellect plunges so deep,
that memory cannot follow behind.
Yet whatever of the holy kingdom
I could treasure up in my mind
shall now be the matter of my song.
O good Apollo, for the final labor
make me such a vessel of your power,
as you require for granting the beloved laurel.
Until now one peak of Parnassus
sufficed for me; but now with both
I must enter the arena that remains.
Enter my breast, and breathe, you,
just as when you drew Marsyas
out of the sheath of his limbs.
O divine power, if you lend yourself to me
so much that the shadow of the blessed kingdom
imprinted in my head I may reveal,
you will see me at the foot of your beloved tree
come, and crown myself with the leaves
of which the subject and you will make me worthy.
So rarely, father, is any of it gathered
for the triumph of either emperor or poet,
the fault and shame of human desires,
that to bring forth joy in the joyful
Delphic deity should the bough
of Peneus, when it makes someone thirst for it.
A great flame follows a little spark:
perhaps after me, with better voices,
prayers will be offered so that Cirrha may answer.
There rises for mortals through different openings
the lamp of the world; but from that one
which joins four circles with three crosses,
with a better course and with a better star
it comes forth conjoined, and the worldly wax
it tempers and stamps more after its own fashion.
It had made morning there and evening here,
that opening, and almost all was white there,
that hemisphere, and the other part black,
when Beatrice, toward the left side,
I saw turned and gazing into the sun:
never did an eagle fix its gaze upon it so.
And just as a second ray is wont
to issue from the first and rise back upward,
just like a pilgrim who wishes to return,
so from her action, infused through my eyes
into my imagination, mine arose,
and I fixed my eyes upon the sun beyond our wont.
Much is permitted there that here is not permitted
to our powers, by virtue of the place
made as the proper home of the human species.
I did not endure it long, nor so briefly,
that I did not see it sparkle all around,
like iron that comes glowing from the fire;
and suddenly day seemed to day
to be added, as if he who has the power
had adorned heaven with another sun.
Beatrice wholly upon the eternal wheels
stood with her eyes fixed; and I upon her
fixed my eyes, withdrawn from up above.
In gazing at her I became such within
as Glaucus became on tasting the herb
that made him a companion in the sea of the other gods.
To signify transhumanizing through words
would not be possible; therefore let the example suffice
for the one for whom grace reserves the experience.
If I was only that part of me which you created
newly, love that governs heaven,
you know it, who raised me with your light.
When the revolution that you make everlasting
by being desired, made me attentive to itself
with the harmony that you tune and distinguish,
so much of heaven then seemed to me aflame
with the sun's flame, that rain or river
never made a lake so wide.
The newness of the sound and the great light
kindled in me a desire for their cause
never felt with such keenness.
Whereupon she, who saw me as I saw myself,
to quiet my troubled mind,
before I could ask, opened her mouth
and began: “You yourself make yourself dull
with false imagining, so that you do not see
what you would see if you had shaken it off.
You are not on earth, as you believe;
but lightning, fleeing its proper place,
did not run as you do, who return to it.”
If I was stripped of my first doubt
by her brief words spoken with a smile,
within a new one I was more ensnared,
and said: “Already, content, I rested
from great wonder; but now I wonder
how I rise beyond these light bodies.”
Whereupon she, after a tender sigh,
directed her eyes toward me with that look
a mother gives her delirious child,
and she began: “All things whatsoever
have order among themselves, and this is the form
that makes the universe resemble God.
Here the higher creatures see the trace
of the eternal power, which is the end
for which the aforesaid order is made.
In the order of which I speak are inclined
all natures, through diverse lots,
nearer to their source and less near;
whence they move toward different ports
over the great sea of being, and each
with an instinct given to it to carry it.
This carries fire toward the moon;
this is the mover in mortal hearts;
this binds the earth together and gathers it into itself;
nor only the creatures that are outside
intelligence does this bow shoot,
but those that have intellect and love.
Providence, which orders so much,
with its light makes the heaven ever still
within which turns the one that has the greatest haste;
and now there, as to an appointed place,
the power of that bowstring carries us,
which directs whatever it shoots toward a joyful target.
It is true that, as form does not accord
many times with the intention of the art,
because matter is deaf to responding,
so from this course departs
sometimes the creature, which has the power
to turn, though thus impelled, in another direction;
and just as one may see falling
fire from a cloud, so the primal impulse
casts it down, twisted by false pleasure.
You should no more wonder, if I judge rightly,
at your ascent, than at a stream
if from a high mountain it descends to the lowest point.
It would be a marvel in you if, free
of impediment, you had settled below,
like stillness on earth in living fire.”
Then she turned her face toward heaven.
