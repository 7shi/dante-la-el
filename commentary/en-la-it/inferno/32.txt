If I had rhymes harsh and hoarse,
As would befit the dismal hole
Upon which all the other rocks press,
I would squeeze from my conception the juice
More fully; but because I have them not,
Not without fear do I bring myself to speak.
For it is no undertaking to take in jest,
To describe the bottom of all the universe,
Nor one for a tongue that calls mamma and papa.
But may those ladies aid my verse
Who aided Amphion to wall in Thebes,
So that from the fact the telling may not differ.
O rabble ill-created above all others,
Who are in a place of which it is hard to speak,
Better had you been sheep or goats here.
When we were down in the dark well
Beneath the giant's feet, much lower down,
And I was still looking at the high wall,
I heard it said to me: Watch how you step;
Go so that you do not trample with your soles
The heads of the wretched, weary brothers.
At this I turned, and saw before me
And beneath my feet a lake that, from the frost,
Had the look of glass and not of water.
Never made for its course so hard a veil
In winter the Danube in Austria,
Nor the Don there beneath the cold sky,
As there was here: for if Tambernic
Had fallen on it, or Pietrapana,
It would not have given a creak at the edge.
And as the frog sits croaking
With its snout out of the water, when dreams
Often of gleaning the peasant woman;
Livid up to where shame shows,
Were the grieving shades in the ice,
Setting their teeth to the stork's note.
Each kept its face turned down:
From the mouth the cold, and from the eyes the sad heart,
Each draws its witness among them.
When I had looked about me a little,
I turned to my feet and saw two so close together
That the hair of their heads was mingled together.
Tell me, you who press your breasts so close,
I said, who are you? And they bent their necks;
And after they had raised their faces toward me,
Their eyes, which before were wet only within,
Dripped onto their lips, and the frost bound
The tears between the lids and shut the eyes again.
Timber to timber no clamp ever bound
So tightly; whereupon they, like two he-goats,
Butted each other: such anger overcame them.
And one who had lost both ears
To the cold, still with his face turned down,
Said: Why do you gaze at us as in a mirror for so long?
If you wish to know who those two are,
The valley from which the Bisenzio descends
Belonged to their father Alberto and to them.
From one womb they came; and all Caina
You may search, and you will not find a shade
More worthy of being set in jelly:
Not the one whose breast and shadow were pierced
With one and the same blow by Arthur's hand;
Nor was it Caccia; nor this one who blocks me,
And the head so that I can see no farther,
And was named Sassol Mascheroni:
If you are Tuscan, you know well by now who he was.
And so that you do not draw me into further talk,
Know that I was Camiscion de' Pazzi
And await Carlin to clear me of blame.
Then I saw a thousand faces dark,
Made so by the cold: hence a shudder comes over me,
And always will, at frozen pools.
And while we were going toward the center,
To which all weight gathers,
And I was trembling in the eternal shade;
Whether it was will, or destiny, or chance,
I do not know: but walking among the heads,
I struck my foot hard against the face of one.
Weeping, he cried out at me: Why do you trample me?
If you do not come to increase the vengeance
Of Montaperti, why do you molest me?
And I: My master, now wait for me here,
So that through this man I may get free of a doubt:
Then you may hurry me as much as you wish.
My guide stood still; and I said to him
Who was still cursing bitterly:
Tell me who you are, who so revile others.
Now who are you, who go through Antenora
Striking, he replied, the cheeks of others
So that, were you alive, it would be too much?
Alive I am, and dear it may be to you,
Was my reply, if you seek fame,
That I set your name among the other notes.
And he to me: For the contrary I long:
Get away from here, and give me no more grief;
For you know little of how to flatter in this hollow.
Then I took him by the hair at the nape,
And said: You will have to name yourself,
Or not a hair will remain on you up here.
Whereupon he to me: Though you strip me of my hair,
I will not tell you who I am, nor show you,
Though a thousand times you fall upon my head.
I already had his hair wound round my hand,
And had pulled more than one lock of it out of him,
He barking with his eyes drawn down;
When another cried: What ails you, Bocca?
Is it not enough for you to rattle your jaws
Unless you bark? What devil is touching you?
Now, I said, I do not want you to speak,
Wicked traitor, for to your shame
I shall bear true tidings of you.
Go away, he replied, and tell what you will;
But do not be silent, if you get out of here,
About him whose tongue was so ready just now.
He weeps here for the silver of the French:
I saw, you may say, the man of Duera
There where the sinners are kept cool.
If you should be asked who else was there,
You have beside you the man of Beccaria,
Whose throat Florence cut through.
Gianni del Soldanier I believe to be
Farther on with Ganelon and Tribaldello,
Who opened Faenza when people were asleep.
We had already gone away from him
When I saw two frozen in one hole,
So that one head was a cap to the other:
And as bread is eaten out of hunger,
So the upper one set his teeth in the other
Where the brain joins the nape.
No differently did Tydeus thus gnaw
The temples of Melanippus in his fury
Than that one did the skull and the other parts.
O you who show by so bestial a sign
Hatred for him whom you are eating,
Tell me why, I said, on this agreement:
That if with reason you complain of him,
Knowing who you are and what his sin is,
In the world above I may yet repay you for it,
If this, with which I speak, does not dry up.
