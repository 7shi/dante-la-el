Thus from bridge to bridge, speaking of other things
of which my Comedy does not care to speak,
we came, and kept to the crest, when
we stopped to see another cleft
of Malebolge, and the other vain laments;
and I saw it wondrously dark.
As in the arsenal of the Venetians
boils in winter the tenacious pitch,
to coat anew those ships of theirs that are unsound,
which cannot sail, and in the meantime
one builds his ship anew, and another reca ulks
the ribs of one that made more voyages;
one hammers at the prow, and another at the stern;
others make oars, and others twist shrouds;
one patches foresail and mainsail:
so, not by fire but by divine art,
there boiled down there a thick pitch,
which smeared the bank on every side.
I saw it, but I saw in it
nothing but the bubbles that the boiling raised,
swelling, and it settling back all flattened.
While I was gazing fixedly down there,
my guide, saying: Look out, look out,
drew me to him from the place where I stood.
Then I turned like the man who is eager
to see what he must flee,
and whom sudden fear unnerves,
who, for the sake of seeing, does not delay his departure:
and I saw behind us a black devil
coming at a run up the crag.
Ah, how fierce he was in aspect!
And how harsh he seemed to me in bearing,
with his wings spread, light on his feet!
His shoulder, which was sharp and high,
a sinner weighed down with both haunches,
and the sinew of his feet the devil held gripped.
From our bridge he said: O Malebranche,
behold one of the elders of Santa Zita:
put him under, for I go back for more
to that town which is well supplied with them:
everyone there takes bribes, except Buonturo;
of no, for money, they make a yes there.
Down there he flung him, and along the hard crag
he turned, and never was a mastiff loosed
in such haste to pursue the thief.
That one plunged in, and came up again face first;
but the demons, who had the bridge for cover,
cried: Here the Holy Face has no place;
here one swims otherwise than in the Serchio;
so if you want none of our claws,
do not stick out above the pitch.
Then they sank more than a hundred hooks into him;
they said: Under cover you must dance,
so that, if you can, you may snatch in secret.
No differently do cooks have their scullions
plunge into the middle of the cauldron
the meat with their hooks, so that it does not float.
The good Master: So that it may not be seen
that you are here, he said to me, crouch down
behind a crag that may afford you some shelter.
And at no assault that may be made on me
need you fear, for I know these things well,
because at other times I was in such a fray.
Then he passed beyond the head of the bridge,
but when he came upon the sixth bank,
he had need of a steadfast countenance.
With that fury and with that storm
with which dogs rush out at the poor man
who suddenly begs where he stops,
those devils came out from under the little bridge,
and turned all their hooks toward him.
But he cried: Let none of you be vicious.
Before your hook takes me,
let one of you come forward to hear me;
and then let him consider hooking me.
All cried: Let Malacoda go;
whereat one moved, and the others stood still;
and he came to him, saying: Now what good does it do him?
Do you think, Malacoda, that you see me here,
having come, said my master,
safe already from all your resistance,
without divine will and favoring fate?
Let us go, for in heaven it is willed
that I show another this wild way.
Then his pride so fell away
that he let his hook fall at his feet,
and said to the others: Now let him not be struck.
And my master to me: O you who sit
among the crags of the bridge, well hidden,
safely now come back to me.
Whereupon I stirred, and came swiftly to him;
and the devils all came forward,
so that I feared they might not keep the pact.
Thus I once saw the foot soldiers fear
who came out of Caprona under terms,
seeing themselves among so many enemies.
I pressed close with my whole body
alongside my guide and did not turn my eyes
from their look, which was not good.
And lowering their hooks: Shall I touch him,
one was saying to the other, on the rump?
And they answered: Yes, see that you stick it in him.
But that demon who was conversing
with my guide turned very quickly,
saying: Hold, hold, Scarmiglione.
Then he said to us: To go farther along this
crag is no longer possible, because there lies
all shattered at the bottom the sixth arch:
and if it still pleases you to go on,
go on up along this rocky bank;
close by is another crag that makes a path.
Yesterday, five hours later than this hour,
a thousand two hundred and sixty-six
years were completed since the way was broken here.
I am sending some of these men of mine that way,
to look whether anyone is airing himself:
go with them, for they will not be wicked.
Come forward, Alichino and Calcabrina,
he began to say, and you, Cagnazzo;
and let Barbariccia lead the ten.
Let Libicocco come forward, and Draghignazzo,
tusked Ciriatto, and Graffiacane,
and Farfarello, and mad Rubicante.
Search all around the boiling birdlime;
let these men be safe as far as the other crag,
which runs all unbroken over the dens.
Ah me! Master, what is it that I see?
I said, pray, without escort let us go alone,
if you know the way, for I for my part do not ask for one.
If you are as watchful as you usually are,
do you not see that they gnash their teeth,
and with their brows threaten us with pain?
And he to me: I do not wish you to fear:
let them gnash away to their hearts' content,
for they do this for the tormented wretches.
Along the left embankment they turned;
but first each had pressed his tongue
between his teeth, toward my guide as a signal,
and he had made a trumpet of his arse.
