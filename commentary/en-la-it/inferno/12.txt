The place where, to descend the bank,
we came was rugged, and, for what was there besides,
such that every eye would shrink from it.
As is that landslide which in the flank
struck the Adige on this side of Trent,
either through earthquake or through failed support;
so that from the top of the mountain, whence it moved,
to the plain the rock has tumbled down in such a way
that it would afford some path to one who was above;
such was the descent of that ravine.
And on the very edge of the broken hollow
the infamy of Crete lay stretched,
which was conceived in the false cow:
and when it saw us, it bit itself,
like one whom anger breaks within.
My master cried out toward it: Perhaps
you believe that here is the duke of Athens,
who in the world above dealt you death?
Get you gone, beast, for this one does not come
instructed by your sister,
but goes to see your punishments.
As is that bull that breaks away the moment
it has received the mortal blow,
that cannot walk, and leaps here and there;
I saw the Minotaur do likewise:
and that discerning one cried: Run to the passage;
while it is in a fury, it is well that you descend.
Thus we made our way down over the heap
of those stones, which often shifted
beneath my feet under the unaccustomed load.
I was already pondering; and he said: You are thinking
perhaps of that landslide, which is guarded
by that bestial rage which I have now quenched.
Now I wish you to know that the other time
that I descended down here into lower Hell,
this rock had not yet fallen.
But certainly a little before, if I discern aright,
he came who the great spoil
took from Dis out of the uppermost circle,
on all sides the deep foul valley
trembled so that I thought the universe
felt love, through which I believe
the world has several times been turned to chaos:
and at that moment this ancient rock
here and elsewhere suffered such a fall.
But fix your eyes; for down in the valley there draws near
the river of blood, in which boils
whoever by violence does harm to another.
O blind cupidity, wicked and mad,
that so spurs us on in our brief life,
and in the eternal one then steeps us so cruelly!
I saw a broad ditch bent in an arc,
as one that embraces the whole plain,
just as my escort had said:
and between the foot of the bank and it, in file
ran Centaurs armed with arrows,
as they used to go hunting in the world.
Seeing us descend, each halted,
and from the troop three departed
with bows and shafts first chosen.
And one cried from afar: To what torment
are you coming, you who descend the slope?
Say it from where you are; if not, I draw the bow.
My master said: Our answer
we shall give to Chiron there, at close quarters:
ill for you that your will was always so hasty.
Then he touched me and said: That is Nessus,
who died for the beautiful Deianira,
and himself took vengeance for his own death:
and the one in the middle, who gazes at his breast,
is the great Chiron, who reared Achilles:
that other is Pholus, who was so full of wrath.
Around the ditch they go by thousands upon thousands,
shooting whatever soul raises itself
out of the blood farther than its guilt allotted it.
We drew near to those fleet beasts:
Chiron took an arrow, and with the nock
pushed his beard back to his jaws.
When he had uncovered his great mouth,
he said to his companions: Have you noticed
that the one behind moves what he touches?
The feet of the dead are not wont to do so.
And my good guide, who was already at his breast,
where the two natures are joined,
replied: He is indeed alive, and thus alone
I must show him the dark valley:
necessity brings him, and not delight.
One left off singing Alleluia
who entrusted me with this new office;
he is no robber, nor I a thieving soul.
But by that power through which I move
my steps along so wild a road,
give us one of yours, that we may keep close to him,
so that he may show us where one fords,
and that he may carry this man on his rump;
for he is no spirit that goes through the air.
Chiron turned toward his right breast,
and said to Nessus: Turn back, and guide them,
and make way if another troop is met.
Now we set out with the faithful escort
along the bank of the vermilion flood that boiled,
where those being boiled uttered loud shrieks.
I saw people submerged up to the brow;
and the great Centaur said: They are tyrants,
who laid hands on blood and on possessions.
Here they weep for the merciless harm they did:
here is Alexander, and fierce Dionysius,
who made Sicily endure sorrowful years:
and that forehead with the hair so black
is Azzolino; and that other who is fair-haired
is Obizzo d'Este, who in truth
was slain by his stepson in the world above.
Then I turned to the Poet; and he said:
This one shall now be first for you, and I second.
A little farther on the Centaur stopped
over some people who, as far as the throat,
seemed to emerge from the boiling flood.
He showed us a shade alone to one side,
saying: That one cleft in the bosom of God
the heart that on the Thames is still revered.
Then I saw people who out of the stream
held their heads and even their whole chests:
and of these I recognized many.
Thus, little by little, there grew shallow
that blood, so that it scalded only the feet:
and there was our passage across the ditch.
Just as you see on this side
the boiling flood that keeps diminishing,
said the Centaur, I wish you to believe
that on this other side deeper and deeper sinks
its bed, until it rejoins the place
where tyranny must groan.
Divine justice on this side goads
that Attila who was a scourge on earth,
and Pyrrhus, and Sextus; forever it milks
the tears that with the boiling it draws forth
from Rinier da Corneto, from Rinier Pazzo,
who made such war upon the roads.
Then he turned and recrossed the ford.
