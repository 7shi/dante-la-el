The multitude and the diverse wounds
had so inebriated my eyes
that they were desirous of staying to weep.
But Virgil said to me: Why do you keep gazing?
Why does your sight still linger
down there among the sad and mutilated shades?
You have not done so at the other ditches.
Think, if you mean to number them,
that the valley runs round for twenty-two miles;
and already the moon is beneath our feet.
Little now is the time that is granted us,
and there is more to see that you do not see.
If you had, I then replied,
attended to the cause for which I was looking,
perhaps you would have allowed me to stay still longer.
Meanwhile he went on, and I went behind him,
the guide, I already making my reply
and adding: Within that hollow,
where I held my eyes just now so fixedly,
I believe that a spirit of my blood bewails
the fault that costs so much down there.
Then said the master: Let not break
your thought henceforth upon him;
attend to something else, and let him remain;
for I saw someone beneath the little bridge
point you out and threaten fiercely with his finger,
and I heard him named Geri del Bello.
You were then so wholly intent
upon him who once held Altaforte
that you did not look that way; so he departed.
O my guide, the violent death
that has not yet been avenged for him, I said,
by anyone who shares in the disgrace,
made him disdainful; hence he went away
without speaking to me, as I suppose;
and thereby he has made me more compassionate toward him.
Thus we spoke as far as the first place
on the ridge that shows the other valley,
were there more light, right to the bottom.
When we were above the last cloister
of Malebolge, so that its lay brothers
could appear to our sight,
diverse laments shot into me,
which had their arrows tipped with pity;
hence I covered my ears with my hands.
Such pain as there would be if from the hospitals
of Val di Chiana, between July and September,
and of Maremma and of Sardinia the ills
were all together in one ditch,
such was there, and such a stench came out of it
as is wont to come from rotting limbs.
We descended onto the last bank
of the long ridge, still on the left hand,
and then my sight was keener
here toward the bottom, where the minister
of the high Lord, infallible justice,
punishes the falsifier whom it registers here.
I do not believe that a sadder sight
was the whole people sick in Aegina,
when the air was so full of pestilence
that the animals down to the little worm
all fell, and afterward the ancient people,
according to what the poets hold for certain,
were restored from the seed of ants,
than it was to see through that dark valley
the spirits languishing in separate heaps.
One upon the belly, and one upon the shoulders,
one lay on another, and one on all fours
shifted his place along the sad path.
Step by step we went without speech,
looking at and listening to the sick,
who could not raise their bodies up.
I saw two sitting propped against each other,
as to heat them pan is set against pan,
from head to foot spotted with scabs;
and never did I see a currycomb wielded
by a lad awaited by his master,
nor by one who unwillingly stays awake,
as rapidly as each wielded the bite
of his nails upon himself for the great fury
of the itching, which has no other relief.
Thus the nail pulled down the scab,
as a knife does the scales from a bream
or from another fish that has them broader.
O you who with your fingers strip off your mail,
my guide began to one of them,
and who sometimes make pincers of them,
tell me if any Italian is among those
who are in here, so may your nail suffice you
eternally for that work.
Italians are we whom you see so ravaged,
both of us here, one replied, weeping;
but who are you, who asked about us?
And the guide said: I am one who descends
with this living man down from ledge to ledge,
and to show Hell to him is my intent.
Then their mutual support broke off;
and trembling, each turned to me,
with others who heard him at second hand.
The good master drew quite close to me,
saying: Say to them what you will.
And I began, since he so willed:
So may the memory of you not be stolen
in the first world from the minds of men,
but may it live after many suns,
tell me who you are and of what people;
your disfiguring and loathsome punishment
must not frighten you from revealing yourselves to me.
I was of Arezzo, and Albero of Siena,
one replied, had me put to the fire;
but that for which I died does not bring me here.
It is true that I said to him, speaking in jest,
I could raise myself in flight through the air.
And he, who had the desire and little sense,
wanted me to show him the art; and solely
because I did not make him Daedalus, he had me
burned by one who held him as a son.
But to the last ditch of the ten
me, for the alchemy I practiced in the world,
Minos condemned, to whom it is not given to err.
And I said to the master: Now was there ever
a people so vain as the Sienese?
Certainly not the French by a long way.
At this the other leper, who heard me,
replied to my words: Take out for me Stricca,
who knew how to spend in moderation;
and Niccolò, who the costly custom
of the clove first discovered
in the garden where such seed takes root;
and take out the company in which squandered
Caccia d'Asciano the vineyard and the great woodland,
and the Abbagliato displayed his wit.
But that you may know who so seconds you
against the Sienese, sharpen your eye toward me,
so that my face may answer you well;
thus you will see that I am the shade of Capocchio,
who falsified metals with alchemy,
and you should remember this, if I discern you rightly,
how good an ape of nature I was.
