On the very edge of a high bank,
Which great broken stones formed in a circle,
We came above a more cruel throng:
And there, because of the horrible excess
Of the stench that the deep abyss throws up,
We drew close behind the lid
Of a great tomb, where I saw an inscription
That said: Pope Anastasius I guard,
Whom Photinus drew from the straight way.
Our descent must be slow,
So that the sense may first grow a little accustomed
To the foul breath, and then there will be no need for heed.
Thus the master. And I: Some recompense,
I said to him, find, that the time may not pass
Wasted. And he: See that I am thinking of that.
My son, behind those rocks,
He then began to say, are three little circles,
From step to step, like those you leave behind.
All are full of accursed spirits:
But that afterward the sight may better suffice you,
Understand how and why they are confined.
Of every malice that earns hatred in heaven,
Injury is the end, and every such end
Either by force or by fraud grieves another.
But because fraud is an evil peculiar to man,
It displeases God more; and therefore below are
The fraudulent, and greater pain assails them.
To the violent the first circle belongs entire:
But because violence is done to three persons,
Into three rings it is divided and constructed.
To God, to oneself, to one's neighbor one can
Do violence, I mean to them and to their things,
As you will hear with clear reasoning.
Death by force and painful wounds
Are inflicted on one's neighbor, and on his possessions
Ruin, fires, and harmful levies.
Hear, murderers, and whoever wrongfully wounds,
Ravagers, robbers, all are tormented by
The first ring in separate bands.
A man can lay upon himself a violent hand
And upon his goods: and therefore in the second
Ring must repent without avail
Whoever deprives himself of your world,
Gambles away and dissipates his wealth,
And weeps where he ought to be glad.
Violence can be done to the Deity,
By denying and blaspheming it in one's heart,
And scorning nature and its goodness:
And therefore the smallest ring seals
With its mark Sodom and Cahors
And whoever, scorning God, speaks with his heart.
The fraud by which every conscience is bitten
A man may use against one who trusts in him,
And against one who pockets no trust.
This latter mode seems to kill
Only the bond of love that nature makes;
Hence in the second circle nest
Hypocrisy, flatteries, and whoever casts spells,
Falsehood, thievery, and simony,
Pimps, corrupt dealings, and like filth.
Through this love the other love is forgotten
That nature makes, and that which is afterward added,
From which special trust is created:
Hence in the smallest circle, where is the point
Of the universe upon which Dis sits,
Whoever betrays is eternally consumed.
And I: Master, very clearly proceeds
Your reasoning, and very well distinguishes
This pit and the people it holds.
But tell me: of those whom the marsh besmears,
Whom the wind drives and whom the rain beats,
And who clash with such harsh tongues,
Why not within the ruddy city
Are they punished, if God holds them in wrath?
And if he does not, why are they in such a state?
And he to me: Why does it stray so far,
He said, your wit, from its wont?
Or where else is your mind looking?
Do you not remember those words
With which your Ethics treats of
The three dispositions that heaven does not want,
Incontinence, malice, and mad
Bestiality? And how incontinence
Offends God less and earns less blame?
If you consider well this judgment,
And bring to mind who they are,
Who up there outside endure penance,
You will see well why from these wicked ones
They are parted, and why with less wrath
Divine justice hammers them.
O sun that heals every troubled sight,
You so content me when you resolve,
That no less than knowing, doubting pleases me.
Turn back a little once more,
I said, to where you say that usury offends
Divine goodness, and untie the knot.
Philosophy, he said to me, to one who understands it,
Points out, not in just one place,
How nature takes its course
From the divine intellect and from its art;
And if you mark well your Physics,
You will find, after not many pages,
That your art and that one, as far as it can,
Follows, as the learner does the master,
So that your art is, as it were, God's grandchild.
From these things, if you call to mind
Genesis from the beginning, must
Mankind take its livelihood and advance.
But because the usurer keeps to another way,
Nature in itself and in its follower
He scorns, since in something else he places his hope.
But follow me now, for it pleases me to go:
For the Fishes dart up over the horizon,
And the Wain lies wholly above the northwest wind,
And the ledge is descended much farther on.
