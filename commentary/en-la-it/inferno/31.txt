One and the same tongue first bit me,
So that it tinged both the one and the other cheek,
And then held out the remedy to me.
So I hear that the lance was wont,
Achilles' and his father's, to be the cause
First of a sad and then of a good gift.
We turned our backs on the wretched valley,
Up on the bank that girds it round,
Crossing over without any speech.
There it was less than night and less than day,
So that my sight went but a little way before me;
But I heard a loud horn sound,
So loud that it would have made any thunder faint,
Which, as I followed its course back toward it,
Directed my eyes wholly to one place.
After the grievous rout, when
Charlemagne lost his holy host,
Roland did not sound so terribly.
Only a little while did I keep my head turned that way
Before I seemed to see very tall towers;
Whereupon I: Master, tell me, what city is this?
And he to me: Because you range
Through the darkness too far off,
It comes about that then in imagining you err.
You will see well, if you draw near there,
How much the sense deceives itself from afar;
Therefore spur yourself on a little more.
Then lovingly he took me by the hand,
And said: Before we are farther on,
So that the fact may not seem strange to you,
Know that they are not towers, but giants;
And they are in the well all around its rim,
From the navel downward, every one of them.
As, when the mist disperses,
The eye little by little makes out
What the vapor that crowds the air conceals;
So, piercing the thick and dark air,
Drawing nearer and nearer toward the brink,
My error flees me, and my fear grows.
For as upon its round circuit
Monteriggioni crowns itself with towers,
So above the rim that encircles the well
Towered with half their bodies
The horrible giants, whom threatens
Jupiter from heaven still, when he thunders.
And I could already discern the face of one,
The shoulders and the belly, and of the chest a great part,
And down along the ribs both arms.
Nature surely, when she forsook the art
Of making such creatures, did very well
To take such agents away from Mars;
And if of elephants and whales
She does not repent, whoever looks closely
Holds her the more just and more discerning for it;
For where the resourcefulness of the mind
Is joined to ill will and to might,
No defense against it can people make.
His face seemed to me as long and bulky
As the pine cone of Saint Peter's in Rome;
And in proportion to it were the other bones;
So that the bank, which was a loincloth
From the middle downward, showed quite so much of him
Above, that of reaching his hair
Three Frisians would have boasted in vain;
For I saw thirty great spans of him
Down from the place where a man buckles his cloak.
Raphel hay hameth zabi et almi,
The savage mouth began to shout,
For which no sweeter psalms were fitting.
And my guide to him: Foolish soul,
Keep to your horn, and with that vent yourself,
When anger or some other passion touches you.
Feel about your neck, and you will find the cord
That keeps it tied, O bewildered soul,
And see it where it drapes your great chest.
Then he said to me: He himself accuses himself;
This is Nimrod, through whose evil wish
One language alone is not in use in the world.
Let us leave him be, and not speak in vain;
For so is every language to him
As his to others, which is known to none.
We made our way farther, then,
Turned to the left; and at a crossbow shot's distance
We found the other, much fiercer and greater.
Who the master was that girded him,
I cannot say; but he had bound close
In front the other arm, and behind the right,
With a chain that kept him fettered
From the neck downward, so that on the part exposed
It wound about as far as the fifth turn.
This proud one wished to make trial
Of his power against supreme Jupiter,
Said my guide, whence he has such a reward.
Ephialtes is his name; and he did great deeds
When the giants put fear into the gods:
The arms he wielded he never moves.
And I to him: If it may be, I would
That of the measureless Briareus
My eyes might have experience.
Whereupon he replied: You will see Antaeus
Near here, who speaks and is unbound,
Who will set us in the depths of all evil.
The one you wish to see is much farther on,
And is bound and shaped like this one,
Except that he looks more ferocious in the face.
Never was an earthquake so violent
That it shook a tower so mightily
As Ephialtes was quick to shake himself.
Then more than ever I feared death,
And nothing more than fear would have been needed,
Had I not seen the bonds.
We proceeded farther then,
And came to Antaeus, who a full five ells,
Not counting the head, rose out of the pit.
O you, who in the fortunate valley
That made Scipio heir to glory,
When Hannibal with his men turned his back,
Once carried off a thousand lions as prey,
And of whom, had you been at the great war
Of your brothers, it seems still to be believed
That the sons of Earth would have won;
Set us down (and let it not be distasteful to you)
Where the cold locks Cocytus fast.
Do not make us go to Tityus or to Typhon:
This man can give of what is longed for here;
Therefore bend down, and do not twist your snout.
He can yet bring you fame in the world;
For he lives, and a long life still awaits him,
If grace does not call him to itself before his time.
Thus spoke the master; and the other in haste
Stretched out his hand and took my guide,
From which Hercules once felt great squeezes.
Virgil, when he felt himself being taken,
Said to me: Come here, so that I may take you.
Then he so contrived that he and I were one bundle.
As the Garisenda appears when one looks at it
From beneath its leaning side, when a cloud passes
Over it, so that it seems to lean to meet it,
Such Antaeus seemed to me as I waited
To see him bend, and it was a moment
When I would have wished to go by another road;
But gently on the bottom that devours
Lucifer with Judas, he set us down;
Nor did he linger there thus bent,
But like a mast on a ship he rose.
