Rejoice, Florence, since you are so great,
that over sea and over land you beat your wings,
and through Hell your name spreads.
Among the thieves I found five such
citizens of yours, whence shame comes to me,
and you rise not to great honor by it.
But if near morning one dreams the truth,
you shall feel, within a little time,
something of what Prato, to say nothing of others, craves for you.
And if it had already come, it would not be too soon:
would that it were so, since it must indeed be;
for the more it will weigh on me, the more I age.
We departed thence, and up by the stairs
that the darkness had made us descend before,
my master climbed again, and drew me along.
And pursuing the solitary way
among the splinters and among the crags of the cliff,
the foot without the hand made no headway.
Then I grieved, and now I grieve again,
when I turn my mind to what I saw;
and I rein in my wit more than is my wont,
lest it run without virtue to guide it;
so that, if a good star, or a better thing,
has given me the good, I myself may not begrudge it to myself.
As the peasant who rests on the hillside,
in the season when he who lights the world
keeps his face less hidden from us,
when the fly gives way to the mosquito,
already sees fireflies down the valley,
perhaps there where he gathers grapes or plows;
with so many flames all aglow was
the eighth ditch, as I perceived
as soon as I was where the bottom came into view.
And as he who avenged himself with the bears
saw the chariot of Elijah at his departure,
when the horses rose erect toward heaven;
for he could not so follow it with his eyes
as to see anything but the flame alone,
like a little cloud, rising upward;
so moved each flame through the throat
of the ditch, for none shows what it has stolen,
and every flame steals a sinner from sight.
I stood up on the bridge to see,
so that, if I had not grasped a crag,
I would have fallen down without being pushed.
And the guide, who saw me so intent,
said: Within the fire are the spirits:
each wraps himself in that with which he is aflame.
My master, I replied, from hearing you
I am more certain; but already it had seemed to me
that it was so, and already I wished to ask you:
Who is in that fire which seems so divided
at the top that it seems to rise from the pyre
where Eteocles was placed with his brother?
And he to me: There within are tormented
Ulysses and Diomedes, and thus together
they go to vengeance as they went to wrath:
and within their flame is bewailed
the ambush of the horse, which made the gate
through which went forth the noble seed of the Romans.
Wept for within is the trick through which, though dead,
and Deidamia still grieves for Achilles,
and for the Palladium punishment is borne there.
If they can, within those sparks,
speak, I said, master, I earnestly beg you,
and beg again that my plea may count for a thousand,
that you not refuse me leave to wait
until the horned flame comes here:
see how with desire I bend toward it.
And he to me: Your prayer is worthy
of much praise, and I therefore accept it;
but see that your tongue holds itself in check,
just leave the speaking to me, for I have understood
what you wish: for they would be disdainful,
because they were Greeks, perhaps of your words.
When the flame had come there
where time and place seemed right to my guide,
I heard him speak in this fashion.
O you who are two within one fire,
if I deserved well of you while I lived,
if I deserved well of you, much or little,
when in the world I wrote the lofty verses,
do not move on: but let one of you say
where, lost, he went to die.
The greater horn of the ancient flame
began to sway, murmuring,
just like one that the wind buffets.
Then, moving its tip this way and that,
as if it were the tongue that spoke,
it cast forth its voice, and said: When
I departed from Circe, who detained
me more than a year there near Gaeta,
before Aeneas so named the place;
neither the sweetness of a son, nor piety
toward my old father, nor the love owed,
which should have made Penelope glad,
could overcome within me the ardor
that I had to become versed in the world,
and in human vices and worth:
but I put out upon the deep open sea
with only one ship and with that company,
small, with which I was afterward lost.
The one shore and the other I saw as far as Spain,
as far as Morocco, and the island of the Sardinians,
and the others that that sea washes all around.
I and my companions were old and slow,
when we came to that narrow mouth
where Hercules set the mark with his boundary stones,
so that man should not venture farther:
on the right hand I left Seville behind me,
on the other I had already left Ceuta behind me.
O brothers, I said, who through a hundred thousand
perils have reached the west,
to this so brief vigil
of your senses that still remains,
do not choose to deny the experience,
following the sun, of the world without people.
Consider your seed:
you were not made to live like brutes,
but to pursue virtue and knowledge.
My companions I made so keen,
with this little speech, for the journey,
that scarcely could I afterward have held them back.
And, our stern turned toward the morning,
of our oars we made wings for the mad flight,
always gaining ground on the left-hand side.
Already all the stars of the other pole
we saw at night, and ours so low
that it did not rise above the surface of the sea.
Five times rekindled and as many times blotted out
had been the light on the underside of the moon,
since we had entered upon the deep passage,
when there appeared to us a mountain, dark
from the distance, and it seemed to me as high
as any I had ever seen.
We rejoiced, and soon it turned to weeping;
for from the new land a whirlwind arose
and struck the forepart of the ship:
three times it made her turn with all the waters,
at the fourth it made the prow rise up
and the stern go down, as pleased another,
until the sea had closed again over us.
