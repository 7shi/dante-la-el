Broke the deep sleep in my head
a heavy thunderclap, so that I started up,
like a person who is forcibly awakened:
and my rested eye I moved about,
risen upright, and steadily I gazed
to know the place where I was.
True it is that I found myself upon the brink
of the abysmal valley of sorrow,
which gathers the thunder of infinite woes.
Dark it was, deep and murky,
so much so that, though I fixed my gaze on the bottom,
I could not discern anything there.
Now let us descend down here into the blind world,
began the poet, all pale;
I shall be the first, and you shall be the second.
And I, who had noticed his color,
said: How shall I come, if you are afraid,
you who are wont to be a comfort in my doubt?
And he to me: The anguish of the people
who are down here paints upon my face
that pity which you take for fear.
Let us go, for the long way urges us on.
Thus he set out, and thus he made me enter
the first circle that girds the abyss.
There, as far as I could tell by listening,
there was no weeping, save only sighs
that made the eternal air tremble:
this came of grief without torments,
which the throngs felt, throngs very great,
of infants and of women and of men.
The good master to me: You do not ask
what spirits these are that you see?
Now I want you to know, before you go farther,
that they did not sin, and if they have merits,
it is not enough, because they did not have baptism,
which is part of the faith that you believe:
and if they were before Christianity,
they did not duly worship God;
and of such as these am I myself.
For such a lack, and not for any other evil,
we are damned, and afflicted only in this,
that without hope we live in longing.
Great grief seized my heart when I heard him;
because people of great worth
I knew were suspended in that Limbo.
Tell me, my master, tell me, my lord,
I began to say, to be certain
of that faith which conquers every error.
Did anyone ever go out from here, either through his own merit
or through another's, who afterward was blessed?
And he, who understood my veiled speech,
replied: I was new to this state
when I saw a mighty man come here,
crowned with a sign of victory.
He drew from here the shade of the first parent,
of Abel his son, and that of Noah,
of Moses, lawgiver and obedient;
Abraham the patriarch, and David the king,
Israel with his father and with his children,
and with Rachel, for whom he did so much,
and many others; and he made them blessed:
but I want you to know that, before them,
human spirits had not been saved.
We did not cease walking because he spoke;
rather we passed through the forest all the while,
the forest, I mean, of thickly crowded spirits.
Not long yet had our way been
on this side of sleep, when I saw a fire
that overcame a hemisphere of darkness.
We were still a little way from it,
but not so far that I did not discern in part
that honorable people possessed that place.
O you who honor every science and art,
who are these who have such honor
that it sets them apart from the condition of the others?
And he to me: The honored name
that resounds of them up above, in your life,
gains grace in heaven that so exalts them.
Meanwhile a voice was heard by me:
Honor the most exalted poet;
his shade returns, which had departed.
After the voice had ceased and fallen silent,
I saw four great shades coming toward us;
their looks were neither sad nor glad.
The good master began to say to me:
Look at him who holds the sword in his hand
and comes before the three, as their lord.
That is Homer, sovereign poet,
the other who comes is Horace the satirist,
Ovid the third, and the last is Lucan.
Because each shares with me
the name that the lone voice sounded,
they do me honor; and in this they do well.
Thus I saw the fair school gather
of that lord of the most exalted song,
who above the others, like an eagle, flies.
After they had talked together awhile,
they turned to me with a sign of greeting;
and my master smiled at this.
And far more honor still they did me,
for they made me one of their company:
so that I was sixth amid such wisdom.
Thus we went on as far as the light,
speaking of things of which silence is fitting,
just as speech was, there where I was.
We came to the foot of a noble castle,
seven times encircled by high walls,
defended all around by a fair little stream.
This we crossed as if it were firm ground:
through seven gates I entered with these sages;
we came to a meadow of fresh greenery.
People were there with eyes slow and grave,
of great authority in their looks;
they spoke seldom, with gentle voices.
Thus we drew aside to one of the corners,
to a place open, bright and high,
so that they could all be seen.
There, upright upon the green enamel,
I was shown the great spirits,
so that at the sight I feel myself exalted.
I saw Electra with many companions,
among whom I recognized Hector and Aeneas,
Caesar armed, with his hawklike eyes.
I saw Camilla and Penthesilea
on the other side; I saw King Latinus,
who sat with Lavinia his daughter.
I saw that Brutus who drove out Tarquin,
Lucretia, Marcia, Julia and Cornelia,
and alone, apart, I saw Saladin.
After I had raised my brows a little higher,
I saw the master of those who know
sitting amid a family of philosophers.
All gaze at him, all do him honor.
There I saw Socrates and Plato,
who before the others stand nearest him:
Democritus, who ascribes the world to chance,
Diogenes, Anaxagoras and Thales,
Empedocles, Heraclitus and Zeno:
and I saw the good collector of qualities,
Dioscorides, I mean; and I saw Orpheus,
Tullius, and Linus, and Seneca the moralist:
Euclid the geometer and Ptolemy,
Hippocrates, Avicenna and Galen,
Averroes, who made the great commentary.
I cannot treat of all in full;
because the long theme so drives me on
that many times the telling falls short of the fact.
The company of six is diminished by two;
by another way the wise guide leads me,
out of the quiet air, into the air that trembles;
and I come to a place where nothing shines.
