A place there is in Hell, called Malebolge,
All of stone and of an iron hue,
Like the ring that runs around it.
In the very middle of the evil field
A well circles round, very wide and deep,
Whose structure I shall tell of in its place.
That belt, then, which remains, round,
Between the well and the foot of the high, hard bank,
Had its floor divided into ten valleys.
Such as, where for guard of the walls
Moat after moat encircles castles,
Is the part that they make safe;
Such an image did those present there:
And as at such strongholds from their thresholds
To the outer bank there are little bridges,
So from the foot of the cliffs rocky spurs
Ran, which cut across embankments and ditches
As far as the well, which cuts them off and gathers them in.
In this place, shaken off the back
Of Geryon, we found ourselves: and the Poet
Kept to the left, and I moved behind him.
On my right hand I saw a new grief:
New scourged men and new scourgers,
Of whom the first ditch was full.
On the bottom the sinners were naked:
From the middle to this side they came facing us;
On the farther side, along with us, but with longer strides.
As the Romans, on account of the great host,
In the year of the Jubilee, over the bridge
Have adopted a way of making the people pass:
So that on one side all have their faces
Toward the Castle, and go to Saint Peter's;
On the other side they go toward the mount.
On this side, on that, upon the gloomy rock,
I saw horned demons with great lashes,
Who beat them cruelly from behind.
Ah, how they made them lift their heels
At the first blows! Indeed no one
Waited for the second, or the third.
As I walked, my eyes by one man
Were met; whereupon I straightway said:
Of having seen this man before I am not unfed.
Therefore, to make him out, I planted my feet:
And my sweet guide likewise stopped,
And consented that I should go back a little.
And that scourged man thought to hide himself
By lowering his face, but little did it avail him;
For I said: O you who cast your eye to the ground,
If the features you bear are not false,
Venetico Caccianemico you are;
But what brings you to such stinging Salse?
And he to me: Unwillingly I tell it;
But your clear speech compels me,
Which makes me remember the world of old.
I am the one who Ghisolabella
Led to do the will of the marquis,
As up above the foul tale runs.
And not I alone weep here a Bolognese:
Rather this place is so full of us
That not so many tongues are now taught
To say sipa between Savona and Reno:
If of this you want proof or testimony,
Call to mind our greedy bosom.
As he spoke thus, a demon struck him
With his lash, and said: Away,
Pander, here there are no women to coin money from.
I rejoined my escort:
Then in a few steps we came
Where a rocky spur jutted from the bank.
Quite easily we climbed it,
And turning right along its jagged ridge,
From those eternal rings we departed.
When we were where it gapes open
Beneath, to give passage to the scourged,
The guide said: Hold fast, and let strike
Upon you the face of these other ill-born men,
Whose faces you have not yet seen,
Because they went along with us.
From the old bridge we watched the file
That was coming toward us on the other side,
And that the lash likewise drives on.
The good Master, without my asking,
Said to me: Look at that great one who comes,
Who for all his pain seems to shed no tears:
What a royal aspect he still retains!
That is Jason, who by courage and by wit
Left the Colchians bereft of the ram.
This man passed through the island of Lemnos,
After the bold and pitiless women
Had put all their males to death.
There with signs and with ornate words
He deceived Hypsipyle, the young girl
Who earlier had deceived all the others.
He left her there pregnant and all alone:
Such guilt condemns him to such torment;
And for Medea too vengeance is taken.
With him goes whoever deceives in such a way:
And let this suffice of the first valley
To know, and of those whom it torments within it.
Already we were where the narrow path
Crosses the second embankment,
And makes of it a support for another arch.
There we heard people whimpering
In the other ditch, snorting through their snouts,
And beating themselves with their palms.
The banks were crusted with a mold
From the breath from below that cakes upon them,
Which did battle with the eyes and with the nose.
The bottom is so deep that there suffices not
The eye to see without climbing to the crown
Of the arch, where the rocky spur rises highest.
There we came, and there below in the ditch
I saw people plunged in excrement
That seemed to have come from human privies.
And while I search down there with my eye,
I saw one with his head so fouled with shit
That it did not show whether he was layman or cleric.
He shouted at me: Why are you so eager
To look at me more than at the other filthy ones?
And I to him: Because, if I remember well,
I have seen you before with your hair dry,
And you are Alexo Interminel of Lucca:
Therefore I eye you more than all the others.
Whereupon he then, beating his pate:
Down here have the flatteries submerged me,
Of which my tongue was never sated.
After this the guide: See that you thrust,
He said to me, your face a little farther forward,
So that with your eyes you may clearly grasp the face
Of that filthy, disheveled wench,
Who there scratches herself with shitty nails,
And now squats, and now stands on her feet.
Thais is the whore who replied
To her lover, when he said: Have I favor
Great with you? Nay, marvelous.
And with this let our eyes be sated.
