The banners of the King of Hell advance
Toward us: therefore look ahead,
Said my master, to see if you discern him.
As when a thick fog blows,
Or when our hemisphere grows dark,
A mill that turns in the wind appears from afar:
Such a structure I seemed to see then:
Now on account of the wind I pressed close behind
My guide, for there was no other shelter there.
Already I was (and with fear I put it into verse)
There where the shades were all covered,
And showed through like a straw in glass.
Some lie flat, others stand upright,
One on its head, another on the soles of its feet;
Another, like a bow, bends back at neck and feet.
When we had come so far forward
That it pleased my master to show me
The creature that had the fair countenance,
He moved from before me and made me stop:
Behold Dis, saying, and behold the place
Where you must arm yourself with fortitude.
How frozen and faint I then became,
Do not ask it, reader, for I do not write it,
Because all speech would be too little.
I did not die and did not remain alive:
Think now for yourself, if you have a jot of wit,
What I became, deprived of both the one and the other.
The emperor of the sorrowful kingdom
From mid-breast rose out of the ice;
And I am closer in size to a giant
Than giants are to his arms:
See now how great that whole must be
Which matches such a part.
If he was as fair as he is now ugly,
And against his Maker raised his brows,
Well may all sorrow proceed from him.
O how great a marvel it seemed to me
When I saw three faces on his head!
One in front, and that was vermilion;
Of the other two, which joined this one
Just above the middle of each shoulder,
And joined at the top of the crown,
The right seemed to me between white and yellow;
The left to look at was such as those
Who come from where the Nile flows down.
Beneath each came forth two great wings,
As large as befitted so great a bird;
Sails at sea I never saw the like of.
They had no feathers, but a bat's
Was their fashion; and he raised them up,
So that three winds went forth from him.
From this all Cocytus froze:
With six eyes he wept, and down three chins
Dripped the tears and bloody slaver.
In each mouth he crushed with his teeth
A sinner, in the manner of a flax brake,
So that he made three thus suffer.
To the one in front the biting was nothing
Beside the clawing, for at times his back
Was left entirely bare of skin.
That soul up there who has the greatest pain,
Said the master, is Judas Iscariot,
Who has his head inside and outside flails his legs.
Of the other two who have their heads below,
The one who hangs from the black muzzle is Brutus;
See how he writhes and utters not a word:
That other is Cassius, who seems so stout of limb.
But night rises again; and now
We must depart, for we have seen everything.
As he wished, I twined my arms about his neck;
And he watched for the time and place:
And when the wings were spread wide enough,
He took hold of the shaggy sides;
From tuft to tuft he then descended
Between the thick hair and the frozen crusts.
When we were there where the thigh
Turns just at the thick of the haunches,
The guide, with toil and with anguish,
Turned his head to where he had had his shanks,
And clutched the hair like a man who climbs,
So that I thought I was returning to Hell again.
Hold fast, for by such stairs,
Said the master, panting like a weary man,
One must depart from all evil.
Then he came out through a hole in a rock,
And set me on the edge to sit:
Afterward he stepped warily toward me.
I raised my eyes and thought to see
Lucifer as I had left him,
And saw him holding his legs up.
How bewildered I then became,
Let the dull-witted think, who do not see
What point it was that I had passed.
Rise up, said the master, to your feet:
The way is long and the road is bad,
And already the sun returns to the middle of terce.
It was no palace hall
Where we were, but a natural dungeon
That had rough ground and a lack of light.
Before I tear myself from the abyss,
My master, said I when I was upright,
To draw me out of error speak to me a little.
Where is the ice? And how is this one fixed
Thus upside down? And how in so short a time
From morning to evening has the sun made its passage?
And he to me: You imagine yourself still
To be beyond the center, where I took hold
Of the hair of the evil worm that pierces the world.
On that side you were for as long as I descended:
When I turned, then you passed the point
Toward which from every side all weights are drawn:
And you have now come beneath the hemisphere
That is opposite to the one that the great dry land
Covers, and beneath whose summit put to death
Was the man who was born and lived without sin:
You have your feet upon a little sphere
That forms the other face of Judecca.
Here it is morning when on that side it is evening:
And this one who made us a ladder with his hair
Is fixed still, just as he was fixed.
On this side he fell down from heaven:
And the land that before stood out on this side,
For fear of him made of the sea a veil,
And came to our hemisphere; and perhaps,
To flee him, left this place empty
That land which appears on this side, and rushed upward.
There is a place down there, distant from Beelzebub
As far as the tomb extends,
That is known not by sight but by the sound
Of a little stream that descends there
Through the mouth of a rock that it has gnawed
With its course that winds and slopes but little.
The guide and I upon that hidden way
Entered to return to the bright world:
And without taking thought for any rest
We climbed up, he first and I second,
Until I saw some of the beautiful things
That heaven bears, through a round opening,
And thence we came out to see the stars again.
