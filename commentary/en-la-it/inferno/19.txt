O Simon Magus, O wretched followers,
Who the things of God, which to goodness
Should be brides, and you, rapacious ones,
For gold and for silver adulterate;
Now must the trumpet sound for you,
Because in the third ditch you are.
Already to the next tomb we had
Climbed, to that part of the crag
Which hangs plumb just over the middle of the bottom.
O highest wisdom, how great is the art
You show in heaven, on earth and in the evil world,
And how justly your power apportions!
I saw along the sides and along the bottom
The livid stone full of holes,
All of one width, and each was round.
They seemed to me no less wide nor larger
Than those that are in my fair San Giovanni
Made as a place for the baptizers;
One of which, not many years ago now,
I broke for one who was drowning within:
And let this be a seal to undeceive every man.
Out of the mouth of each there protruded
The feet of a sinner, and of the legs
Up to the calf, and the rest remained within.
The soles of all were both aflame;
Wherefore so hard did the joints twitch
That they would have snapped withes and ropes.
As, at the flaming of oily things, it is wont
To move up along the very outer skin,
So it was there from the heels to the toes.
Who is that one, master, who torments himself,
Twitching more than his other companions,
Said I, and whom a redder flame sucks?
And he to me: If you wish me to carry you
Down there by that bank which slopes more gently,
From him you shall learn of himself and of his wrongs.
And I: It is as fair to me as it pleases you:
You are lord, and know that I do not depart
From your will, and know what is left unsaid.
We came then onto the fourth embankment,
Turning; and we descended to the left
Down there to the bottom, pierced with holes and narrow.
My master still from his hip
Did not set me down until he reached the hole
Of him who so wept with his shank.
O whoever you are, who keep your upper part below,
Sad soul, driven in like a stake,
I began to say, if you can, speak a word.
I stood like the friar who hears the confession
Of the perfidious assassin, who, after he is planted,
Calls him back, whereby he puts off death.
And he cried: Are you already standing there,
Are you already standing there, Boniface?
By several years the writing lied to me.
Are you so soon sated with that wealth
For which you did not fear to take by guile
The fair lady, and then to make havoc of her?
I became like those who stand,
Not understanding what is answered them,
As if put to shame, and know not how to answer.
And Virgil said to me: Tell him at once,
I am not he, I am not he whom you think:
And I answered as I was bidden.
Whereupon the spirit twisted both his feet;
Then sighing, with a voice of weeping,
He said to me: What then do you ask of me?
If to know who I am matters so much to you
That you have therefore run down the bank,
Know that I was clothed in the great mantle:
And truly I was a son of the She-bear,
So greedy to advance the cubs
That above I put wealth, and here myself, in a purse.
Beneath my head the others are dragged,
Who preceded me in practicing simony,
Flattened in the fissure of the stone.
Down there I too shall fall, when
He comes whom I believed you to be
When I put the sudden question.
But longer already is the time I have roasted my feet,
And have been thus upside down,
Than he shall remain planted with red feet:
For after him shall come, of fouler deeds,
From the west a lawless shepherd,
Who must cover both me and him.
A new Jason shall he be, of whom one reads
In Maccabees: and as to him was pliant
His king, so shall he who rules France be to this one.
I know not if here I was too rash,
For I did answer him in this measure:
Pray, tell me now how much treasure wished
Our Lord first from Saint Peter
Before he placed the keys in his power?
Surely he said nothing save: Follow me.
Neither Peter nor the others took from Matthias
Gold or silver, when he was chosen by lot
For the place that the wicked soul lost.
Pray, stay there now, for you are well punished;
And guard well the ill-gotten money
That made you bold against Charles.
And were it not that it is still forbidden me by
Reverence for the supreme keys,
Which you held in the happy life,
I would use words far weightier;
For your avarice brings grief to the world,
Trampling the good and raising up the wicked.
You shepherds the Evangelist discerned,
When she who sits upon the waters
Was seen by him whoring with kings:
She who with the seven heads was born,
And from the ten horns had support,
So long as virtue pleased her husband.
You have made yourselves a god of gold and silver;
And what difference is there between you and the idolater,
Save that he worships one, and you a hundred of them?
Ah, Constantine, of how much evil was the mother
Not his conversion, but that dowry
Which the first rich father took from him!
And while I sang such notes to him,
Whether it was anger or conscience that bit him,
He kicked hard with both soles.
I do believe it pleased my guide,
With such contented will did he attend throughout
To the sound of the words, true and close upon each other.
Therefore with both arms he took me,
And after he had me wholly up against his breast,
He climbed back by the way he had descended;
Nor did he tire of holding me clasped to him,
But bore me thus away to the crown of the arch
That makes a crossing from the fourth to the fifth embankment.
There he gently set down the burden;
We climbed along the crag, rough and steep,
Which would be a hard passage for goats:
From there another valley was revealed to me.
