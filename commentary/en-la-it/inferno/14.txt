Since love of my native place
constrained me, I gathered the scattered leaves,
and gave them back to him who was already hoarse.
Thence we came to the boundary, where is divided
the second ring from the third, and where
is seen the dreadful art of justice.
To make well plain the new things,
I say that we came to a waste
that keeps every plant from its bed.
The sorrowful wood garlands it
all around, as the dismal ditch does the wood:
here we stayed our steps at the very edge.
The ground was a sand dry and thick,
fashioned no otherwise than that
which once was trodden by Cato's feet.
O vengeance of God, how greatly you ought
to be feared by everyone who reads
what was made manifest to my eyes!
Of naked souls I saw many a flock,
who all wept most miserably,
and a different law seemed laid upon them.
Supine upon the ground lay some;
some sat all drawn up,
and others walked continually.
Those who went about were more numerous,
fewer those who lay in torment,
but their tongues were looser in their pain.
Over all the sand, with a slow fall,
broad flakes of fire rained down,
like flakes of snow in the Alps without wind.
Just as Alexander in those hot parts
of India saw upon his host
flames fall, intact all the way to the ground;
wherefore he took care to trample the soil
with his ranks, so that the fiery vapor
might better be quenched while still alone:
such was the eternal heat that descended;
whereby the sand caught fire, like tinder
under the striking steel, to double their pain.
Never at rest was the dance
of the wretched hands, now there, now here
shaking from themselves that fresh burning.
I began: Master, you who overcome
all things, except the stubborn demons
who came out against us at the entrance to the gate,
who is that great one who seems not to heed
the blaze, and lies scornful and twisted,
so that the rain seems not to ripen him?
And that very one, who had perceived
that I was asking my guide about him,
cried: As I was alive, so am I dead.
Though Jove weary his smith, from whom
in anger he took the sharp thunderbolt
with which on my last day I was struck;
and though he weary the others, shift by shift,
in Mongibello at the black forge,
crying: O good Vulcan, help, help;
just as he did at the battle of Phlegra,
and strike me with his bolts with all his might,
he could get no joy of his vengeance from it.
Then my guide spoke with force
so great that I had not heard him speak so strongly:
O Capaneus, in that there is no quenching
of your pride, you are well punished:
no torment, save your own rage,
would be for your pain a fury complete.
Then he turned to me with a gentler countenance,
and said: That is one of the seven kings
who besieged Thebes, and he held, and seems still to hold,
God in disdain, and little does he seem to esteem him.
But, as I said to him, his acts of scorn
are ornaments well suited to his breast.
Now come behind me, and see that you do not set
your feet yet in the scorched sand:
but always keep your feet close to the wood.
In silence we came to where there gushes
out of the sand a little stream,
whose redness still makes me shudder.
As from the Bulicame issues the brook
which the sinful women then divide among themselves,
so down through the sand went this one.
Its bed and both its sloping sides
were made of stone, and the margins alongside;
whereby I perceived that the passage was there.
Among all else that I have shown you,
since we entered through the gate
whose threshold is denied to no one,
nothing has been seen by your eyes
so notable as this present stream,
which quenches all the little flames above it.
These were the words of my guide:
wherefore I begged him to grant me the food
for which he had granted me the desire.
In mid-sea lies a wasted land,
he said then, which is called Crete,
under whose king the world was once chaste.
A mountain is there which once was glad
with water and foliage, which is called Ida;
now it is deserted like a thing grown old.
Rhea once chose it as a trusty cradle
for her son, and, to hide him better
when he wept, she had cries raised there.
Within the mountain stands upright a great old man,
who keeps his shoulders turned toward Damietta,
and looks at Rome as at his mirror.
His head is formed of fine gold,
and pure silver are his arms and breast,
then he is of copper down to the fork:
from there down he is all choice iron,
save that the right foot is baked clay,
and he stands erect more on that one than on the other.
Each part, except the gold, is split
by a fissure that drips tears,
which, gathered together, pierce this cavern.
Their course plunges down into this valley:
they form Acheron, Styx and Phlegethon;
then they go down through this narrow channel
as far as where one descends no farther:
they form Cocytus; and what that pool is like
you shall see it, so it is not told here.
And I to him: If this present rivulet
is thus drawn from our world,
why does it appear to us only at this edge?
And he to me: You know that the place is round,
and although you have come a long way,
always to the left, descending toward the bottom,
you have not yet turned through the whole circle;
therefore, if anything new should appear to us,
your face ought not to show wonder.
And I again: Master, where are to be found
Phlegethon and Lethe, since of the one you are silent?
And the other, which is made of this rain?
In all your questions you surely please me,
he replied; but the boiling of the red water
should surely have solved one that you ask.
Lethe you shall see, but outside this pit,
there where the souls go to wash themselves,
when the guilt repented of is removed.
Then he said: Now it is time to draw away
from the wood: see that you come behind me:
the margins make a way, for they are not burned,
and above them every fiery vapor is quenched.
