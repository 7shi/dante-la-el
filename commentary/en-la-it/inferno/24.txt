In that part of the youthful year
when the sun tempers his locks beneath Aquarius,
and already the nights draw toward half the day;
when the hoarfrost upon the earth copies
the image of her white sister,
but little lasts the temper of her pen;
the little peasant, whose provisions are lacking,
rises and looks, and sees the countryside
whiten all over, whereupon he strikes his hip:
he returns indoors, and here and there laments,
like the poor wretch who knows not what to do;
then he goes out again, and hope he baskets anew,
seeing the world have changed its face
in a short time, and takes his crook,
and out he drives the little sheep to graze:
thus did my master make me lose heart,
when I saw his brow so troubled,
and thus swiftly did the plaster come to the hurt:
for as we came to the ruined bridge,
my guide turned to me with that look
so sweet, which I first saw at the foot of the mountain.
His arms he opened, after some course of action
chosen within himself, first surveying
the ruin well, and took hold of me.
And like one who acts and reckons,
who always seems to provide beforehand,
so, lifting me up toward the top
of a crag, he eyed another splinter of rock,
saying: Onto that one next take hold;
but test first whether it is such as will bear you.
There was no path there for one clad in a cloak,
for barely could we, he light and I pushed,
make our way up from slab to slab.
And were it not that on that enclosing bank,
more than on the other, the slope was short,
I know not about him, but I should indeed have been beaten.
But because Malebolge toward the mouth
of the deepest well slopes all the way,
the lie of each valley entails
that one slope rises and the other descends:
we came nevertheless at last onto the crest
where the last slope breaks steeply off.
The breath was so drained from my lungs
when I was up that I could go no farther,
but sat down the instant I arrived.
Now you must indeed shake off sloth like this,
said the Master, for, lying on feathers,
one does not come to fame, nor under a quilt:
without which whoever spends his life
leaves such a trace of himself on earth
as smoke in air or on water the foam:
and therefore rise up, conquer the breathlessness
with the spirit that conquers every battle,
if with its heavy body it does not sink down.
A longer stair must be climbed;
it is not enough to have parted from these:
if you understand me, see that it avails you.
I rose then, showing myself furnished
better with breath than I felt myself to be;
and said: Go, for I am strong and bold.
Up along the rock we took the way
that was craggy, narrow and difficult,
and far steeper than the one before.
Speaking I went, so as not to seem weak,
when a voice came out of the next ditch,
that formed words ill-suited to speech.
I know not what it said, although upon the back
I was already of the arch that crosses there;
but whoever spoke seemed moved by anger.
I was turned downward; but my living eyes
could not reach the bottom through the dark:
wherefore I: Master, see that you reach
the other girdling bank, and let us go down the wall;
for just as I hear from here and do not understand,
so I look down and make out nothing.
No other answer, he said, do I give you
save the deed; for an honest request
should be followed by action in silence.
Then we descended the bridge at the end
where it joins the eighth bank,
and then the pit was plain to me:
and I saw within it a terrible throng
of serpents, and of such diverse kinds,
that the memory still spoils my blood.
Let Libya boast no more of her sand;
chelydri of her own, javelin-snakes and phareans
she produces, and cenchres with the amphisbaena.
So many plagues, nor ones so wicked,
she never showed, with all Ethiopia,
nor with what lies above the Red Sea.
Amid this cruel and most woeful multitude
people ran naked and terrified,
without hope of a hole or a heliotrope.
With snakes their hands behind them were bound:
these thrust through their loins the tail
and the head, and were knotted in front.
And behold, at one who was by our bank
a serpent lunged, which pierced him
there where the neck is joined to the shoulders.
Never was O so quickly written, nor I,
as he caught fire and burned, and wholly ash
he had perforce to become as he fell:
and after he was thus destroyed upon the ground,
the dust gathered itself together of its own accord,
and that very man returned in an instant.
Thus by the great sages it is affirmed
that the phoenix dies and then is born again
when it draws near its five hundredth year.
On neither grass nor grain in its life does it feed,
but solely on tears of incense and amomum,
and nard and myrrh are its last swaddling bands.
And as is one who falls and knows not how,
by force of a demon that pulls him to the ground,
or of some other obstruction that binds a man,
when he rises, looking about him
all bewildered by the great anguish
that he has suffered, and looking, sighs;
such was the sinner when afterward he rose.
O power of God! How severe it is,
that crashes down such blows in vengeance!
My guide then asked who he was:
whereupon he answered: I rained down from Tuscany,
a short time ago, into this savage gorge.
A bestial life pleased me, and not a human one,
like the mule I was: I am Vanni Fucci
the Beast, and Pistoia was a worthy den for me.
And I to my guide: Tell him not to slip away,
and ask what guilt thrust him down here,
for I saw him once a man of blood and rage.
And the sinner, who heard, did not dissemble,
but directed toward me his mind and his face,
and colored himself with dismal shame;
then he said: It pains me more that you have caught me
in the misery in which you see me
than when I was taken from the other life.
I cannot deny what you ask:
so far down am I put because I was
a thief of the fair furnishings in the sacristy;
and falsely was it once laid to another's charge.
But so that you may not rejoice at such a sight,
if ever you are outside the dark places,
open your ears to my announcement, and hear:
Pistoia first grows lean of Blacks,
then Florence renews her people and her ways.
Mars draws a vapor from the Val di Magra
that is wrapped in troubled clouds,
and with a tempest impetuous and bitter
over Campo Piceno shall battle be joined:
whereupon suddenly it will rend the mist,
so that every White shall be wounded by it.
And I have said it so that it must grieve you.
