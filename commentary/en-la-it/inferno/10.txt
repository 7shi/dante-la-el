Now he goes along a hidden path
Between the wall of the city and the torments,
My master, and I behind his shoulders.
O highest virtue, who through the impious circles
Lead me about, I began, as it pleases you,
Speak to me, and satisfy my desires.
The people who lie in the tombs,
Could they be seen? Already lifted are
All the lids, and no one keeps guard.
And he to me: All will be shut
When from Jehoshaphat they shall return here
With the bodies that they have left above.
Their cemetery on this side have
With Epicurus all his followers,
Who hold the soul to be dead with the body.
Therefore as to the question that you put to me
Here within you will be satisfied soon,
And as to the desire too that you keep from me.
And I: Good guide, I do not keep hidden
From you my heart, except to say little;
And you have not only now inclined me to this.
O Tuscan, who through the city of fire
Go alive, thus speaking courteously,
May it please you to stop in this place.
Your speech makes you known
As a native of that noble homeland
To which perhaps I was too troublesome.
Suddenly this sound came forth
From one of the tombs: therefore I drew near
A little more, fearing, to my guide.
And he said to me: Turn around: what are you doing?
See there Farinata, who has drawn himself erect:
From the waist up you will see all of him.
I had already fixed my gaze on his;
And he rose with chest and brow
As if he held Hell in great disdain:
And the bold hands of my guide, and ready,
Pushed me among the tombs toward him,
Saying: Let your words be well chosen.
When I was at the foot of his tomb,
He looked at me a little, and almost disdainful
Asked me: Who were your ancestors?
I, who was eager to obey,
Did not conceal it from him, but disclosed it all to him:
Whereupon he raised his eyebrows a little upward;
Then he said: Fiercely were they opposed
To me and to my forebears and to my party,
So that twice I scattered them.
If they were driven out, they returned from every quarter,
I answered him, both the one time and the other;
But yours did not learn that art well.
Then there rose at the uncovered opening
A shade alongside this one, as far as the chin:
I believe it had risen on its knees.
It looked around me, as though a desire
It had to see whether another was with me;
But after its surmise was quite extinguished,
Weeping it said: If through this blind
Prison you go by loftiness of intellect,
My son, where is he? Why is he not with you?
And I to him: Of myself I do not come:
He who waits there leads me through here,
Whom perhaps your Guido held in disdain.
His words and the manner of his punishment
Had already told me this man's name:
Therefore my answer was so full.
Suddenly drawing himself erect, he cried: How
Did you say: He held? Does he not still live?
Does the sweet light not strike his eyes?
When he noticed a certain delay
That I made before my answer,
He fell back supine, and appeared no more outside.
But that other great-hearted one, at whose bidding
I had stopped, did not change his look,
Nor move his neck, nor bend his side.
And if, continuing his earlier speech,
If they have learned that art, he said, poorly,
That torments me more than this bed.
But not fifty times will it be rekindled,
The face of the lady who rules here,
Before you will know how heavily that art weighs.
And so may you ever return to the sweet world,
Tell me: Why is that people so pitiless
Against mine in its every law?
Whereupon I to him: The slaughter and the great carnage
That made the Arbia red in color
Cause such a prayer to be said in our temple.
After he had, sighing, shaken his head,
In that I was not alone, he said, nor surely
Without cause would I have moved with the others:
But I alone was, there where suffered
It was by each one to do away with Florence,
The one who defended her openly.
Ah! so may our seed ever rest,
I begged him, untie for me that knot
That here has entangled my understanding.
It seems that you see, if I hear aright,
What the present time brings with it,
And in the future you hold another manner.
We see, like one who has poor sight,
The things, he said, that are far from us;
So much light does the supreme leader still shed on us:
When they draw near, or are present, all in vain is
Our understanding; and if another does not bring to us,
Nothing do we know of our human state.
Therefore you can understand that wholly dead
Will be our knowledge, from that moment
When the door of the future shall be closed.
Then, as if stung by my fault,
I said: Now tell that fallen one
That his son is still joined with the living.
And if just now I was mute in answering,
Tell him that I did so because I was pondering
Already the error you have cleared up for me.
And already my master was calling me back:
Wherefore I begged the spirit all the more hastily
To tell me who was staying with him.
He said to me: Here with more than a thousand I lie:
In here is the second Frederick,
And the cardinal, and of the others I keep silent.
Then he hid himself: and I toward the ancient
Poet turned my steps, thinking again
Of that speech which seemed hostile to me.
And he set out; and then, walking thus,
Said to me: Why are you so bewildered?
And I answered his question to his satisfaction.
Let your mind preserve what you have heard
Against you, that sage began to us,
And now attend to this: and he raised his finger.
When you are before the sweet radiance
Of her whose fair eye sees all,
From her you will learn the journey of your life.
Then toward the left he moved his foot,
We left the wall, and went toward the middle
Along a path that strikes into a valley
Whose stench gave offense even up there.
