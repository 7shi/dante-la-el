Behold the beast with the pointed tail,
that crosses mountains and breaks walls and weapons;
behold her who befouls the whole world with her stench:
thus began my guide to show her to me;
and he beckoned to her to come to the edge,
near the end of the marble stones we had walked.
And that foul image of fraud
came on, and brought her head and bust ashore;
but onto the bank she did not draw her tail.
Her face was the face of a just man;
so kindly was the skin she showed outside,
and all the rest of her trunk was a serpent's.
Two paws she had, hairy up to the armpits:
her back and breast and both her flanks
she had painted with knots and little circles.
With more colors interwoven and overlaid
neither Tartars nor Turks ever made cloths,
nor were such webs laid on the loom by Arachne.
As sometimes barges lie at the bank,
which are partly in the water and partly on land,
and as there among the gluttonous Germans
the beaver settles himself to make his war;
so that most evil beast lay
on the rim of stone that bounds the plain of sand.
In the void her whole tail was flicking,
twisting upward the venomous fork
that armed the tip in the manner of a scorpion.
My guide said: Now it is needful to turn
our way a little, as far as that
wicked beast that lies down over there.
So we went down on the right-hand side,
and ten paces we took along the very edge,
to keep well clear of the sand and the little flame:
and when we had come to her,
a little farther on I see upon the sand
people sitting close to the place where the ground falls away.
There my master: So that quite full
experience of this ring you may carry away,
he said to me, go and see how they fare.
And let your speeches there be short:
until you return I shall speak with this one,
that she may grant us her strong shoulders.
So, farther along the outermost edge
of that seventh circle, all alone,
I went to where the sorrowful people sat.
Through their eyes their grief was bursting forth:
here and there they came to their own aid with their hands,
now against the vapors, now against the hot ground.
No otherwise do dogs in summer,
now with the muzzle, now with the paw, when they are bitten
by fleas or by blowflies or by gadflies.
After I had fixed my eyes on the faces of some
on whom the tormenting fire falls,
I recognized not one; but I noticed
that from the neck of each hangs a purse,
which had a certain color and a certain sign,
and on that their eyes seem to feed.
And as I come along looking among them,
on a yellow purse I saw something blue
that had the face and bearing of a lion.
Then, the chariot of my gaze moving on,
I saw another, red as blood,
on which was a goose whiter than butter.
And one who with a fat blue sow
had his little white sack marked
said to me: What are you doing in this pit?
Now go away: since you are still alive,
know that my neighbor Vitaliano
will sit here at my left flank.
Among these Florentines I am a Paduan;
often they thunder in my ears,
crying: Let the sovereign knight come,
who will bring the purse with three beaks:
here he twisted his face and thrust out
his tongue, like an ox licking its nose.
And I, fearing that staying longer would vex
him who had warned me to stay but a little,
turned back from the weary souls.
I found my guide, who had climbed
already onto the rump of the fierce animal;
he said to me: Now be strong and bold.
From now on one descends by stairs such as these:
mount in front, for I wish to be in the middle,
so that the tail can do no harm.
As is he who is so close to the chill
of the quartan fever that his nails are already pale,
and trembles all over just looking at the shade,
such I became at the words spoken;
but shame uttered its threats to me,
which before a good lord make a servant brave.
I seated myself on those great ugly shoulders:
I did wish to say, but my voice did not come
as I thought it would: Be sure to hold me in your arms.
But he who at other times had come to my aid
in other straits, as soon as I mounted,
encircled me with his arms and held me up:
and he said: Geryon, move now;
let the circles be wide and the descent slight:
think of the strange burden you bear.
As the little boat leaves its place
backward, backward, so she drew away from there;
and after she felt herself wholly free to move,
to where her breast had been she turned her tail,
and, stretching it out, moved it like an eel,
and with her paws gathered the air toward herself.
Greater fear I do not think there was
when Phaethon let go the reins,
whereby the sky, as still appears, was scorched:
nor when wretched Icarus his back
felt losing its feathers from the heated wax,
his father crying to him: You take a bad course;
than mine was when I saw that I was
in the air, and on every side saw blotted out
every sight except that of the beast.
She goes on swimming slowly, slowly;
she circles and descends, but I do not perceive it,
save that the wind blows on my face and from below.
I could already hear on my right the torrent
making a horrible roar beneath us;
wherefore I thrust my head out, my eyes cast down.
Then I grew more fearful of the plunge:
for I saw fires and heard lamentations;
whereupon, trembling all over, I grip tighter with my thighs.
And then I saw, for I had not seen it before,
the descent and the circling, by the great torments
that drew near from different sides.
As the falcon that has been long on the wing,
that, without seeing lure or bird,
makes the falconer say: Alas! You are coming down:
descends weary whence it moves swiftly,
through a hundred circles, and settles far
from its master, disdainful and sullen:
so Geryon set us down at the bottom,
at the very foot of the sheer rock,
and, having unloaded our persons,
vanished like a notched arrow from the bowstring.
