I say, continuing, that much earlier
than we were at the foot of the high tower,
our eyes went all the way up to the summit,
because of two little flames that we saw set there,
and another from afar return a signal,
so far that the eye could scarcely catch it.
I turned to the sea of all wisdom:
This, I said, what does it say? And what answers
that other fire? And who are they that made it?
And he to me: Out upon the filthy waves
already you can well discern what is awaited,
if the smoke of the marsh does not hide it from you.
Never did bowstring send from itself an arrow
that sped away through the air so swift
as I saw a tiny boat
come through the water toward us at that moment,
under the command of a single boatman,
who shouted: Now you have arrived, wicked soul!
Phlegyas, Phlegyas, you shout now in vain,
said my lord, for this time
you will have us no more than just in passing through the mire.
As is one who hears of a great deception
that has been practiced on him, and then grieves over it,
so did Phlegyas in his pent-up wrath.
My guide went down into the boat,
and then made me enter after him;
and only when I was inside did it seem laden.
As soon as my guide and I were in the craft,
With the current goes the ancient prow,
deeper into the water than it is wont to go with others.
While we sped across the dead pool,
before me came one all covered in mud,
and said: Who are you who come before your time?
And I to him: Though I come, I do not stay;
but who are you, who have grown so foul?
He answered: You see that I am one who weeps.
Whereupon I to him: With weeping and with mourning,
accursed spirit, stay;
for I know you, though you are all filthy.
Then he stretched out both hands to the craft:
whereupon my watchful master shoved him away,
saying: Off with you there among the other dogs.
My neck he then encircled with his arms,
kissed my face, and said: Indignant soul,
blessed is she who bore you in her womb.
That one was in the world a proud person:
no goodness is there to adorn his memory:
so is his shade here furious.
Many hold themselves up there now to be great kings
who here will lie like pigs in mire,
leaving behind them horrible contempt for themselves!
Whereupon I: Master, I would be most eager
to see him dunked in this broth
before we came out of the lake.
And he to me: Before the shore
lets itself be seen by you, you will be sated;
it is fitting that you should enjoy such a desire.
Shortly after that, I saw such torment
inflicted on him by the muddy folk
that I still give praise and thanks for it.
All were shouting: At Filippo Argenti!
Ah, furious Florentine spirit!
He bit his very self with his teeth.
There we left him, for I tell no more of him;
but a lament struck my ears,
whereupon I open my eyes wide, intent on what lies ahead.
And the good master said: Now, my son,
the city that has the name of Dis draws near,
with its grave citizens, with its great host.
And I: Master, already its mosques
there within I clearly discern across the valley,
vermilion, as if out of fire come forth
they had. And he said to me: The eternal fire
that sets them aglow within shows them red,
as you see in this lower Hell.
We at last came within the other moats
that fortify that disconsolate city:
the walls seemed to me to be iron.
Not without first making a great circuit,
we came to a place where the boatman loudly
cried to us: Get out; here is the entrance.
I saw more than a thousand upon the gates,
rained down from heaven, who peevishly
said: Who is this who without death
goes through the kingdom of the dead folk?
And my wise master made a sign
of wishing to speak to them in secret.
Then they held their great disdain in check a little,
and said: Come you alone, and let that one go away
who so boldly entered this kingdom.
Alone let him return by the mad road:
let him try if he knows how; for you will remain here,
who have guided him through so dark a region.
Think, reader, whether I lost heart
at the sound of the accursed words:
for I did not believe I should ever return here.
O my dear guide, who more than seven
times have restored my confidence and drawn me
out of other peril that stood against us,
do not leave me, I said, so undone:
and if going farther is denied us,
let us retrace our steps together quickly.
And that lord who had led me there
said to me: Do not fear, for our passage
no one can take from us: by such a one is it granted us.
But wait for me here; and your weary spirit
comfort and feed with good hope,
for I will not leave you in the lower world.
Thus he goes away, and there he leaves me,
my sweet father, and I remain in doubt;
and yes and no contend in my head.
I could not hear what he put to them;
but he did not stay there with them long,
for each vied with the others in turning back.
Those adversaries of ours shut the gates
against the breast of my lord, who remained outside
and returned to me with slow steps.
And his eyes were on the ground, and his brows shorn
of all confidence, and he said amid his sighs:
Who has denied me the dwellings of sorrow?
And to me he said: You, because I grow angry,
do not be dismayed, for I will win the contest,
whoever moves about within in its defense.
This presumption of theirs is not new,
for they once used it at a less secret gate,
which is still to be found without a lock.
Above it you saw the inscription of death;
and already on this side of it the steep slope descends,
passing through the circles without escort,
one through whom the city will be opened to us.
