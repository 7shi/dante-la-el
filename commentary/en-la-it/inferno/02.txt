The day was going away, and the darkening air
was releasing the creatures that are on earth
from their labors; and I alone, just one,
was preparing myself to sustain the war
both of the journey and of pity,
which the mind that does not err will recount.
O Muses, O lofty genius, now help me;
O mind, who wrote down what I saw,
here shall your nobility be shown.
I began: Poet who guide me,
look at my strength, whether it is mighty enough,
before you entrust me to the deep passage.
You say that the father of Silvius,
still corruptible, to the immortal
world went, and was there in the flesh.
Therefore, if the adversary of all evil
was gracious, considering the lofty effect
that was to come forth from him, and who he was, and of what sort,
it does not seem unfitting to a man of understanding:
for he was, of fostering Rome and of her Empire,
in the Empyrean heaven elected as father.
The one and the other, to tell the truth,
were established for the holy place
where sits the successor of the great Peter.
Through this going, for which you give him praise,
he learned things that were the cause
of his victory and of the papal mantle.
There went afterward the Vessel of Election,
to bring back from there comfort to that faith
which is the beginning of the way of salvation.
But I, why go there? Or who grants it?
I am not Aeneas, I am not Paul;
that I am worthy of this neither I nor anyone else believes.
Therefore, if I give myself up to coming,
I fear that the coming may be folly:
you are wise; you understand better than I speak.
And as is he who no longer wills what he willed,
and through new thoughts changes his purpose,
so that he withdraws altogether from beginning;
such I became on that dark slope:
so that, thinking, I brought to nothing the undertaking
that was at its beginning so swift.
If I have well understood your words,
replied that shade of the great-souled man,
your soul is wounded by cowardice:
which many times hampers a man
so that it turns him back from an honorable undertaking,
as a false sight does a beast when it shies.
That you may free yourself from this fear,
I will tell you why I came and what I heard
at the first moment that I grieved for you.
I was among those who are suspended,
and a lady called me, blessed and beautiful,
such that I asked her to command me.
Her eyes shone brighter than the star;
and she began to say to me, gentle and quiet,
with an angelic voice in her own speech:
O courteous Mantuan soul,
whose fame still endures in the world,
and will endure as long as the world lasts:
my friend, and not fortune's,
on the deserted slope is hindered
so in his way that he has turned back for fear:
and I fear that he is already so lost
that I have risen too late to his aid,
from what I have heard of him in heaven.
Move, with your ornate speech,
and with whatever is needed to save him,
help him so that I may be comforted by it.
I am Beatrice, who bid you go:
I come from the place where I desire to return;
love moved me, which makes me speak.
When I shall be before my Lord,
I shall often praise you to him.
She fell silent then, and afterward I began:
O lady of virtue, through whom alone
the human race surpasses all that is contained
by that heaven which has the smallest of its circles:
so pleasing to me is your command
that to obey, even were it already done, is late to me;
you need no further disclose your wish to me.
But tell me the reason why you do not shrink
from descending down here into this center
from the spacious place where you burn to return.
Since you wish to know so deeply,
I shall tell you briefly, she replied to me,
why I do not fear to come in here.
One should fear only those things
that have the power to do one harm:
not the others, for they are not fearful.
I am made by God, through his grace, such
that your misery does not touch me,
nor does a flame of this fire assail me.
There is a noble lady in heaven who grieves
over this hindrance, to which I send you,
so that she breaks stern judgment up there.
She called for Lucia in her plea,
and said: Now your faithful one has need
of you, and I commend him to you.
Lucia, enemy of every cruel one,
set out and came to the place where I was,
where I sat with Rachel of old.
And she said: Beatrice, true praise of God,
why do you not help him who loved you so much
that for your sake he left the common throng?
Do you not hear the anguish of his weeping?
Do you not see the death that battles him
upon the flood over which the sea has no boast?
In the world there were never people so swift
to seek their own good or to flee their own harm
as I, after such words were spoken,
descended here from my blessed seat,
trusting in your honorable speech,
which honors you and those who have heard it.
After she had spoken thus to me,
she turned her shining eyes away, weeping;
thereby she made me the swifter to come:
and I came to you just as she wished;
from before the beast I took you away,
which denied you the short way up the fair mountain.
What is it, then? Why, why do you hold back?
Why do you invite such cowardice into your heart?
Why have you not daring and boldness,
since three such blessed ladies
care for you in the court of heaven,
and my speech promises you so much good?
As little flowers, by the frost of night
bent and closed, once the sun whitens them,
stand upright, all open, on their stems;
so I became in my wearied strength;
and so much good courage ran to my heart
that I began like one grown bold:
O compassionate she who came to my aid,
and you courteous, who obeyed at once
the true words that she offered you.
You have disposed my heart with desire
so to come, with your words,
that I have returned to my first purpose.
Now go, for a single will is in us both:
you my guide, you my lord and you my master.
Thus I spoke to him; and when he had set out,
I entered upon the deep and wooded way.
