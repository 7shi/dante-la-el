Nessus had not yet reached the other side
when we entered a wood
that was marked by no path.
Not green leaves, but dark in color,
not straight branches, but knotty and twisted,
no fruits were there, but thorns with poison.
No thickets so rough or so dense have
those wild beasts that hold in hatred
between Cecina and Corneto the cultivated places.
Here the foul Harpies make their nests,
who drove from the Strophades the Trojans
with sad tidings of harm to come.
Wide wings they have, and human necks and faces,
feet with talons, and feathered is their great belly:
they make laments up in the strange trees.
And the good master: Before you enter farther,
know that you are in the second ring,
he began to say, and will be, until such time
as you come to the horrible waste of sand.
Therefore look well, and so you will see
things that would rob my words of credence.
I heard on every side cries of woe go up,
and saw no one who uttered them;
wherefore, all bewildered, I stopped.
I believe he believed that I believed
that so many voices came out among those brambles
from people who were hiding from us.
Therefore, said the master, if you break off
some little twig from one of these plants,
the thoughts you have will all be cut short.
Then I stretched my hand a little forward
and plucked a sprig from a great thornbush:
and its trunk cried: Why do you break me?
After it had then grown dark with blood,
it began to cry: Why do you tear me?
Have you no spirit of pity at all?
Men we were, and now we are made thickets:
your hand ought indeed to be more merciful
even had we been souls of serpents.
As with a green brand that is burning
on one side and oozes at the other,
and squeals from the wind that escapes;
so from the broken splinter came out together
words and blood: wherefore I let the tip
fall, and stood like a man who fears.
If he could have believed beforehand,
my sage replied, wounded soul,
what he has seen, on my verse alone,
he would not have stretched out his hand against you;
but the incredible thing made me
induce him to a deed that weighs on me myself.
But tell him who you are, so that, by way
of some amends, he may renew your fame
in the world above, where he is allowed to return.
And the trunk: So do you lure me with sweet speech
that I cannot keep silent; and may it not burden you
if I get caught up a little in speaking.
I am he who held both keys
to Frederick's heart, and who turned them,
locking and unlocking, so gently
that from his confidence I kept almost everyone:
I bore faith to the glorious office,
so much that through it I lost my senses and pulse.
The harlot who ever from the dwelling
of Caesar turned not her whorish eyes,
the common death and vice of courts,
inflamed all hearts against me;
and those inflamed so inflamed Augustus
that glad honors turned to sad mourning.
My spirit, in a taste of disdain,
believing by dying to flee disdain,
made me unjust against my just self.
By the new roots of this wood
I swear to you that I never broke faith
with my lord, who was so worthy of honor.
And if any of you returns to the world,
let him strengthen my memory, which lies low
still from the blow that envy dealt it.
He waited a little, and then: Since he is silent,
the Poet said to me, do not lose the hour;
but speak, and ask him if more would please you.
Whereupon I to him: Ask him yourself again
of what you believe would satisfy me;
for I could not: such pity wrings my heart.
Therefore he began again: So may this man do for you
freely what your speech requests,
imprisoned spirit, may it please you further
to tell us how the soul is bound
in these knots; and tell us, if you can,
whether any ever frees itself from such limbs.
Then the trunk blew hard, and then
that wind was turned into such a voice:
Briefly shall you be answered.
When the fierce soul departs
from the body from which it has torn itself,
Minos sends it to the seventh mouth.
It falls into the wood, and no spot is chosen for it;
but wherever fortune flings it,
there it sprouts like a grain of spelt;
it rises into a shoot and into a woodland tree:
the Harpies, feeding then upon its leaves,
make pain, and for the pain a window.
With the others, we shall return for our cast-off bodies,
but not so that any may clothe itself in them again:
for it is not just to have what one takes from oneself.
Here we shall drag them, and through the mournful
wood our bodies will be hung,
each on the thornbush of the shade that harmed it.
We were still intent upon the trunk,
believing it wished to tell us more,
when we were surprised by a noise,
just like one who, coming,
hears the boar in the hunt toward his post,
who hears the beasts and the branches rustle.
And behold, two from the left side,
naked and scratched, fleeing so hard
that they broke through every barrier of the wood.
And the one in front: Come running, come running, death.
And the other, who seemed to himself to lag too far,
cried: Lano, not so nimble were
your legs at the jousts of the Toppo.
But because perhaps his breath was failing him,
of himself and a bush he made a knot.
Behind them the wood was full
of black bitches, ravenous and running,
like greyhounds let off the chain.
Into the one who crouched down they set their teeth,
and him they tore to pieces, shred by shred;
then they carried off those suffering limbs.
My guide then took me by the hand
and led me to the bush that wept,
through its bleeding breaks, in vain.
O Jacomo, it said, da Santo Andrea,
what did it profit you to make a shield of me?
What blame have I for your wicked life?
When the master had come to a stop over it,
he said: Who were you, who through so many points
breathe out with blood sorrowful speech?
And he to us: O souls, who have come
to see the shameful rending
that has so torn my leaves from me,
gather them at the foot of the sad bush;
I was of the city that for the Baptist
exchanged its first patron: wherefore for this
he will always with his art make it sorrowful:
were it not that at the crossing of the Arno
some likeness of him still remains,
those citizens who afterward founded it anew
upon the ashes that remained from Attila
would have had the work done in vain.
I made a gibbet for myself of my own house.
