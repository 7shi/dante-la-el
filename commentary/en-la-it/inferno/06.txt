At the return of my mind, which had closed
before the pity of the two in-laws,
which wholly confounded me with sorrow,
new torments and new tormented souls
I see about me, however I move,
and however I turn, and however I look about me.
I am in the third circle, that of the rain
eternal, accursed, cold and heavy;
its course and nature are never new to it.
Great hailstones, dark water and snow
pour down through the murky air;
the earth that receives this stinks.
Cerberus, a beast cruel and strange,
with three throats barks like a dog
over the people who are submerged there.
His eyes are vermilion, his beard greasy and black,
his belly broad, and his hands clawed;
he scratches the spirits, swallows them and tears them apart.
The rain makes them howl like dogs:
with one of their sides they shield the other;
the wretched profane ones turn often.
When Cerberus, the great worm, caught sight of us,
he opened his mouths and showed us his fangs:
he had no limb that he held still.
And my guide spread his hands;
he took earth, and with his fists full
threw it into the ravenous gullets.
As is that dog that craves while barking,
and then quiets down when it bites its food,
for it attends only to devouring it and strives to do so;
so became those filthy faces
of the demon Cerberus, who so thunders at
the souls that they would wish to be deaf.
We were passing among the shades that are beaten down
by the heavy rain, and were setting the soles of our feet
upon their emptiness that seems a person.
They were lying on the ground, every one,
except for one that rose to sit, straightway
when it saw us passing before it.
O you who are led through this Hell,
it said to me, recognize me, if you can:
you were made before I was unmade.
And I to it: The anguish that you have
perhaps draws you out of my mind,
for it does not seem to me that I ever saw you.
But tell me who you are, who in so grievous
a place are set, and have such a punishment
that if another is greater, it is not so repugnant?
And he to me: Your city, which is full
of envy, so that already the sack overflows,
held me within it in the serene life.
You citizens called me Ciacco:
for the ruinous sin of gluttony,
as you see, I break beneath the rain;
and I, a sorrowful soul, am not alone,
for all these are under a like punishment
for a like sin. And he said no more.
I answered him: Ciacco, your distress
weighs on me so that it bids me weep:
but tell me, if you know, what will become of
the citizens of the divided city?
Is anyone there just? And tell me the cause
for which it has come to such discord.
And he to me: After long contention
they will come to bloodshed, and the woodland faction
will drive the other out with much injury.
Then afterward this one must fall
within three suns, and the other rise above it
with the strength of one who now plays both sides.
High will it hold its brows for a long time,
keeping the others under heavy weights,
however the other weeps for this or takes offense at it.
Two are just, and there they are not heeded:
pride, envy and avarice are
the three sparks that have set hearts ablaze.
Here he put an end to the mournful sound.
And I to him: I still wish you to instruct me,
and to make me a gift of further speech.
Farinata and Tegghiaio, who were so worthy,
Iacopo Rusticucci, Arrigo and Mosca,
and the others who set their minds to doing good,
tell me where they are, and let me know them;
for great desire presses me to know
whether heaven sweetens them or Hell poisons them.
And he: They are among the blacker souls;
different sins weigh them down to the bottom:
if you descend so far, you will be able to see them.
But when you have returned to the world,
I pray you to bring me to the minds of others:
no more do I tell you and no more do I answer you.
His eyes, till then looking straight, he turned askew:
he looked at me a little, and then bowed his head:
with it he fell level with the other blind ones.
And the guide said to me: He wakes no more
on this side of the sound of the angelic trumpet,
when he will see the power hostile to them,
for each will see again his sorrowful tomb,
will take up again his flesh and his form,
will hear that which resounds for eternity.
Thus we passed through the sorrowful mixture
of the shades and the rain, with slow steps,
touching a little on the life to come:
then I said: Master, these torments,
will they increase after the great sentence,
or will they be less? Or will they be as searing?
Whereupon he to me: Return to your science,
which holds that the more perfect a thing is,
the more it feels the good, and likewise the pain.
Although these accursed people
never attain true perfection,
they expect to be more so on that side than on this.
We went round along that road,
speaking far more than I retell:
we came to the point where the way goes down:
there we found Plutus, the great enemy.
