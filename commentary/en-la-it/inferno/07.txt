Pape Satan, pape Satan aleppe,
began Plutus with his hoarse voice.
And that gentle sage who knew all
said to comfort me: Let it not harm you,
your fear, for whatever power he has
will not keep you from descending this rock.
Then he turned to those swollen lips
and said: Be silent, accursed wolf,
consume yourself within, with your own rage.
Not without cause is our going into the deep:
it is willed on high, there where Michael
took vengeance for the proud outrage.
As the sails swollen by the wind
fall in folds when the mast snaps,
so fell to earth the cruel beast.
Thus we descended into the fourth hollow,
taking in more of the doleful slope
that holds all the evil of the universe in its sack.
Ah, justice of God, who heaps up so many
strange torments and pains as I saw?
And why does our guilt so lay us waste?
As does the wave there over Charybdis,
which breaks against the one it meets,
so must the people here dance their round.
Here I saw a greater throng than elsewhere,
on one side and the other, with great howls,
rolling weights by force of breast:
they struck against one another, and then right there
each turned about, rolling backward,
crying: Why do you hoard? and Why do you squander?
Thus they ran along the dark circle,
on either hand to the opposite point,
crying out again their taunting verse;
then each turned, when he had arrived,
along his own half-circle to the other joust.
Whereupon I, whose heart was all but pierced,
said: My master, now show me
what people these are, and whether all were clerics,
these tonsured ones on our left.
And he to me: Every one of them was blind
so in mind, in the first life,
that they made no expenditure there with measure.
Their voice barks it out clearly enough
when they come to the two points of the circle
where opposite guilt sets them apart.
These tonsured ones, who have no covering
of hair on their heads, were both popes and cardinals,
in whom avarice practices its excess.
Whereupon I: Master, among such as these
I ought surely to recognize some
who were defiled by these evils.
And he to me: A vain thought you entertain:
the undiscerning life that made them foul
now makes them dark to all recognition;
forever will they come to the two collisions;
these will rise again from the grave
with fist clenched, and these with hair shorn.
The fair world ill giving and ill keeping
have taken from them, and set them to this brawl:
what it is like, I embellish with no words.
Now you can see, my son, the brief mockery
of the goods that are entrusted to Fortune,
over which humankind comes to blows.
For all the gold that is beneath the moon
and that once belonged to these weary souls
could not give even one of them rest.
My master, I said, now tell me also:
this Fortune of whom you speak to me,
what is she, who holds the world's goods so in her claws?
And he to me: O foolish creatures,
how great is the ignorance that harms you!
Now I wish you to take my teaching into your mouth:
he whose wisdom transcends all
made the heavens and gave them those who guide them,
so that every part shines upon every part,
distributing the light equally:
likewise over the splendors of the world
he appointed a general minister and guide,
who should transfer in due time the vain goods
from people to people and from one bloodline to another,
beyond the power of human wits to prevent:
hence one people rules and another languishes,
following the judgment of this lady,
which lies hidden, like the snake in the grass.
Your wisdom has no power to withstand her:
she foresees, judges, and pursues
her rule, as the other gods do theirs.
Her changes have no respite:
necessity makes her swift;
so often comes one whose turn it is.
This is she who is so often put on the cross
even by those who ought to give her praise,
giving her wrongful blame and ill repute.
But she is blessed and hears none of it:
with the other first-created beings, joyful,
she turns her sphere, and in bliss rejoices.
Now let us descend at last to greater woe.
Already every star is setting that was rising
when I set out, and too long a stay is forbidden.
We cut across the circle to the other bank
above a valley that boils and spills over
through a ditch that runs from it.
The water was far darker than deepest purple:
and we, in company with the gray waves,
went down along a strange path.
There is a marsh there that has the name of Styx,
this sad brook, when it has descended
to the foot of the evil gray slopes.
And I, who stood intent on looking,
saw muddy people in that mire,
and all naked, with looks of injury.
These struck one another, not with hands alone,
but with head, with breast, and with feet,
tearing one another with their teeth, shred by shred.
The good master said: Son, see
the souls of those whom anger overcame:
and I wish you also to believe for certain
that beneath the water are people who sigh,
and make this water bubble at the surface,
as your eye tells you wherever it turns.
Fixed in the mire, they say: Sullen we were
in the sweet air that is gladdened by the sun,
bearing within us a slothful smoke.
Now we grow sullen in the black slime.
This hymn they gurgle in their throats,
for they cannot say it with a whole word.
Thus, skirting the filthy pool
in a great arc, between the dry bank and the mire,
with eyes turned toward those who gulp down mud,
we came to the foot of a tower at last.
