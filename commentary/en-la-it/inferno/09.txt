That color which cowardice painted on my face,
On seeing my guide turn back,
The sooner checked within him his own new color.
Attentive he stopped, like a man who listens;
For his eye could not lead him far
Through the black air and through the thick fog.
Yet we shall have to win the fight,
He began: if not... such a one offered herself to us.
Oh, how long it seems to me till another comes here!
I saw well how he covered
The one beginning with the other that came after,
Which were words different from the first.
But nonetheless his speech gave me fear,
Because I drew the broken-off words
Perhaps to a worse meaning than they held.
Into this bottom of the dismal hollow
Does anyone ever descend from the first degree,
Which has for its sole punishment hope cut short?
This question I asked; and he: Seldom
Has it happened, he answered me, that of us
Any should take the road by which I go.
True it is that once before I was down here,
Conjured by that cruel Erichtho,
Who called the shades back to their bodies.
Only a little while had my flesh been bare of me
When she made me enter within that wall,
To draw from it a spirit of the circle of Judas.
That is the lowest place and the darkest,
And the farthest from the heaven that circles all:
Well do I know the way: therefore be assured.
This marsh, which breathes the great stench,
Girds round about the grieving city,
Where we cannot now enter without wrath.
And more he said, but I have it not in mind;
Because my eye had drawn me wholly
Toward the high tower with its red-hot summit,
Where in an instant there stood erect all at once
Three infernal Furies stained with blood,
Who had the limbs of women, and their bearing;
And with vivid green hydras they were girdled:
Little snakes and horned vipers they had for hair,
With which their fierce temples were entwined.
And he, who well recognized the handmaids
Of the queen of eternal weeping:
Look, he said to me, at the fierce Erinyes.
That is Megaera on the left side:
The one who weeps on the right is Alecto:
Tisiphone in the middle. And at that he fell silent.
With her nails each one was rending her breast;
They beat themselves with their palms and cried so loud
That I pressed close to the poet out of fear.
Let Medusa come: and so we shall make him stone;
They all cried, looking down:
Ill did we do not to avenge the assault on Theseus.
Turn back, and keep your eyes shut:
For if the Gorgon shows herself, and you should see her,
There would be no returning ever above.
Thus spoke the master, and he himself
Turned me, and did not so trust my hands
As not to close my eyes with his as well.
O you who have sound intellects,
Behold the teaching that hides itself
Beneath the veil of the strange verses.
And already there came up over the filthy waves
A crash of sound full of terror,
At which both banks trembled;
Made no differently than by a wind
Impetuous from opposing heats,
Which strikes the forest without any check,
The branches it shatters, strikes down, and carries away:
Dust-laden it goes proudly on ahead,
And makes the beasts and the shepherds flee.
He freed my eyes, and said: Now direct the full force
Of your sight out over that ancient foam
Toward where that smoke is most acrid.
As frogs before their enemy
The snake all vanish through the water,
Until on the bottom each one crouches;
I saw more than a thousand ruined souls
Flee before one who with his stride
Was crossing Styx with dry soles.
From his face he brushed away that thick air,
Waving his left hand before him often;
And only of that distress did he seem weary.
Well did I perceive that he was sent from heaven,
And my wise master made a sign
That I should keep still and bow to him.
Ah, how full of disdain he seemed to me!
He came to the gate, and with a little wand
Opened it, for it offered no resistance.
O you cast out of heaven, despised people,
He began upon the horrible threshold,
Whence does this insolence lodge in you?
Why do you kick against that will
Whose end can never be cut short,
And which more than once has increased your sorrow?
What good is it to butt against the fates?
Your Cerberus, if you remember well,
Still bears for it his chin and throat stripped of hair.
Then he turned back along the filthy road,
And said not a word to us, but had the look
Of a man whom another care grips and bites
Than that of him who is before him.
And we moved our feet toward the city,
Secure after the holy words.
Within it we entered without any battle:
And I, who had a desire to look upon
The conditions that such a fortress encloses,
As soon as I was inside, cast my eye about;
And saw on every hand a great company
Full of grief and grievous torment.
Just as at Arles, where the Rhône lies still,
And as at Pola, near the Quarnero
Which bounds Italy and bathes its borders,
The tombs make all the place uneven;
So did they here on every side,
Save that the manner of it there was more bitter;
For among the tombs flames were scattered,
By which they were so wholly kindled
That no craft asks for iron hotter.
All their lids were suspended,
And out of them came such harsh laments
That they seemed plainly those of the wretched and afflicted.
And I: Master, who are those people
Who, buried within those chests,
Make themselves heard with their sorrowful sighs?
And he to me: Here are the heresiarchs
With their followers of every sect, and much
More than you believe are the tombs laden.
Like here with like is buried:
And the tombs are more and less hot.
And after he had turned to the right hand,
We passed between the torments and the high ramparts.
