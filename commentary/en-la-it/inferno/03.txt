Through me one goes into the sorrowful city,
Through me one goes into eternal sorrow,
Through me one goes among the lost people.
Justice moved my high Maker;
Divine power made me,
The highest wisdom and the first love.
Before me no things were created,
Save eternal ones, and I endure eternal;
Abandon every hope, you who enter.
These words of dark color
I saw written at the top of a gate;
Whereupon I: Master, their meaning is hard for me.
And he to me, as a discerning person:
Here one must leave behind every misgiving;
All cowardice must here be dead;
We have come to the place where I have told you
That you will see the sorrowful people,
Who have lost the good of the intellect.
And after he laid his hand on mine,
With a glad face, from which I took comfort,
He led me in among the hidden things.
There sighs, weeping and loud laments
Resounded through the starless air,
So that at first I wept at them.
Diverse tongues, horrible speech,
Words kindled with sorrow and with anger,
Voices loud and faint, and the sound of hands with them,
Made a tumult, which circles
Ever in that air stained beyond time,
Like sand when a whirlwind blows.
And I, who had my head girt with confusion,
Said: Master, what is it that I hear?
And what is it that seems so overcome by sorrow?
And he to me: This wretched manner
The sad souls keep of those
Who lived without fame and without praise.
They are mingled with that cowardly choir
Of the angels who neither were rebels
Nor were faithful to God, but were for themselves.
The heavens cast them out so as not to be less fair;
Nor does deep Hell receive them,
For the wicked would have some glory from them.
And I: Master, what is so grievous
To them that it makes them weep so hard?
He answered: I shall tell it to you very briefly:
These have no hope of death,
And their blind life is so base
That they are envious of every other lot.
The world lets no fame of them remain;
Mercy and justice disdain them;
Do not speak of them, but look and pass on.
And I, who looked, saw a banner
That, circling, ran so swiftly
That it seemed to me unworthy of any rest;
And behind it came so long a train
Of people that I would not have believed
That death had undone so many of them.
After I had recognized some of them,
I saw and knew the shade of him
Who made, through cowardice, the great refusal.
Immediately I understood, and was certain,
That this was the sect of the cowards,
Displeasing to God and to his enemies.
These wretches, who never were alive,
Were naked and much stung
By flies and by wasps that were there.
They streaked their faces with blood,
Which, mingled with tears, at their feet
Was gathered up by loathsome worms.
And after I turned to look farther,
I saw people on the bank of a great river;
Whereupon I: My master, now grant me
That I may know who they are and what custom
Makes them seem so ready to cross,
As I discern by the faint light.
And he to me: These things will be made known to you
When we halt our steps
On the sad bank of Acheron.
Then with eyes ashamed and downcast,
Fearing that my speech might be burdensome to him,
As far as the bank I held back from speaking.
And behold, coming toward us by boat,
An old man white with ancient hair,
Crying: Woe to you, wicked souls.
Never hope to see heaven,
I come to lead you to the other bank,
Into the eternal darkness, into heat and ice.
And you who are there, living soul,
Depart from these who are dead.
But after he saw that I was not departing,
He said: By other ways, by other ports,
You will come to shore, not here, to cross:
A lighter craft must carry you.
And the guide to him: Charon, do not be angry:
It is so willed there where one can do
What one wills, and ask no more.
Thereupon quiet were the woolly cheeks
Of the boatman of the livid marsh,
Who around his eyes had wheels of flame.
But those souls, who were weary and naked,
Changed color, and their teeth chattered,
As soon as they heard the harsh words.
They cursed God and their parents,
The human race, the place, the time, the seed
Of their begetting and of their births.
Then they gathered all together,
Weeping bitterly, on the evil bank
That awaits everyone who does not fear God.
Charon the demon, with eyes of glowing coals,
Beckoning to them, gathers them all;
He beats with his oar whoever lingers.
As in autumn the leaves come off,
One after the other, until the branch
Sees on the ground all its spoils;
Likewise the evil seed of Adam:
They cast themselves from that shore one by one,
At signs, as a bird at its call.
Thus they go off over the dark water,
And before they have disembarked over there,
Here again a new throng gathers.
My son, said the courteous master,
Those who die in the wrath of God
All come together here from every land:
And ready are they to cross the stream,
For divine justice spurs them on,
So that fear turns into desire.
No good soul ever crosses from here;
And therefore if Charon complains of you,
You may well know by now what his speech means.
This finished, the dark plain
Trembled so strongly that at the fright
The memory still bathes me in sweat.
The tearful earth gave forth wind,
And a vermilion light flashed,
Such that it overcame my every sense:
And I fell like a man whom sleep takes.
