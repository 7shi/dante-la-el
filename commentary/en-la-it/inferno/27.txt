Already the flame was upright and still,
To speak no more, and already it was going away from us
With the sweet Poet's leave;
When another, which was coming behind it,
Made us turn our eyes to its tip,
At a confused sound that came out of it.
As the Sicilian bull that first bellowed
With the lament of him (and this was just)
Who had fashioned it with his file,
Bellowed with the voice of the afflicted one,
So that, for all that it was of copper,
Yet it seemed pierced by pain;
So, for lack of a way or opening
At first out of the fire, into its language
The woeful words were changed.
But after they had found their way
Up through the tip, giving it that flick
Which the tongue had given in their passage,
We heard it say: O you, to whom I direct
My voice, and who just now spoke Lombard,
Saying: Now go your way, I urge you no further;
Though I may perhaps have come somewhat late,
Let it not irk you to stay to speak with me:
See that it does not irk me, who burn.
If only just now into this blind world
You have fallen from that sweet
Latin land, whence I bring all my guilt;
Tell me if the Romagnols have peace or war;
For I was from the mountains there in Urbino's lands,
On the ridge from which the Tiber breaks forth.
I was still intent and bending down,
When my guide nudged me in the side,
Saying: You speak, this one is Latin.
And I, who had my answer ready already,
Without delay began to speak:
O soul, who are hidden down there,
Your Romagna is not, and never was,
Without war in the hearts of its tyrants,
But in the land I left none just now.
Ravenna stands as it has stood for many years:
The Eagle of Polenta broods there,
So that it covers Cervia with its wings.
The city that once stood the long trial,
And made of the French a bloody heap,
Finds itself beneath the green claws.
And the old mastiff and the new one of Verucchio,
Who dealt so ill with Montagna,
Bore with their teeth where they are wont to.
The cities of the Lamone and of the Santerno
The young lion of the white lair rules,
Who changes sides from summer to winter.
And she whose flank the Savio bathes,
Just as she lies between the plain and the mountain,
Among tyrants lives in a free state.
Now who you are, I pray you tell us:
Be no harder than another has been,
So may your name hold its head high in the world.
After the fire had roared awhile
In its own fashion, it moved its sharp tip
This way, that way, and then gave forth this breath:
If I believed that my reply were
To a person who would ever return to the world,
This flame would stay without further stirrings:
But because never from this depth
Has anyone returned alive, if I hear the truth,
Without fear of infamy I answer you.
I was a man of arms, and then I was a corded friar,
Believing that, thus girded, I might make amends:
And surely my belief would have come fully true,
Had it not been for the great Priest, may ill befall him,
Who put me back into my former sins;
And how and why, I want you to hear me tell.
While I was the form of bones and flesh
That my mother gave me, my deeds
Were not a lion's, but a fox's.
The stratagems and the hidden ways
I knew them all; and so I plied their art
That to the ends of the earth the sound of it went out.
When I saw myself come to that part
Of my life where everyone ought
To lower the sails and gather in the ropes,
What had pleased me then grieved me;
And repentant and confessed I gave myself up,
Alas, wretched me! And it would have availed.
But the prince of the new Pharisees,
Having a war on near the Lateran,
And not with Saracens, nor with Jews;
For every enemy of his was Christian;
And none had been at the taking of Acre,
Nor a merchant in the Sultan's land;
Neither his supreme office nor his holy orders
Did he regard in himself, nor in me that cord
Which used to make those it girded leaner.
But as Constantine sought out Sylvester
Within Soracte to be healed of leprosy,
So this one sought me out as his master
To be healed of his proud fever:
He asked me for counsel; and I kept silent,
Because his words seemed drunken.
And then he said to me: Let your heart not fear;
Even now I absolve you, and you teach me to act
So that I may cast Palestrina to the ground.
Heaven I can lock and unlock,
As you know; for that there are two keys,
Which my predecessor did not hold dear.
Then the weighty arguments pressed me,
So that silence seemed to me the worse course,
And I said: Father, since you cleanse me
Of that sin into which I must now fall,
Long promise with short fulfillment
Will make you triumph on the high seat.
Francis came then, as soon as I was dead,
For me; but one of the black Cherubim
Said to him: Do not take him; do me no wrong:
He must come down here among my wretches,
Because he gave the fraudulent counsel,
Since which I have been at his hair;
For one cannot absolve him who does not repent;
Nor can one repent and will at once,
Because of the contradiction that does not allow it.
O wretched me! How I started
When he seized me, saying to me: Perhaps
You did not believe that I was a logician!
To Minos he carried me; and Minos wound
Eight times his tail about his hard back,
And then in his great rage he bit it,
Saying: This is one of the guilty of the thieving fire;
Wherefore I am lost there where you see,
And going thus clothed I lament.
When he had thus finished his speech,
The flame went away lamenting,
Twisting and tossing its sharp horn.
We passed on, my guide and I,
Up along the crag as far as the next arch,
Which reveals the ditch in which the penalty is paid
By those who, by sundering, earned a burden.
