Silent, alone, and without company,
We went on, one in front and the other behind,
As the Friars Minor go along the way.
Toward the fable of Aesop was turned
My thought by the present brawl,
Where he spoke of the frog and the mouse:
For no more alike are mo and issa
Than the one is to the other, if one well compares
Beginning and end with mind intent.
And as one thought bursts from another,
So from that one another then was born,
Which made my first fear double.
I was already thinking: If these on our account
Are made fools of, with hurt and mockery
Such as I believe much vexes them:
If anger is heaped upon ill will,
They will come after us more cruel
Than a dog to that hare it seizes in its jaws.
Already I felt all my hairs bristling
From fear, and was watchful of what lay behind,
When I said: Master, unless you hide
Yourself and me swiftly, I am in dread
Of the Malebranche; we have them behind us already:
I imagine them so vividly that already I hear them.
And he: If I were of lead-backed glass,
Your outward image I would not draw
More quickly to me than I take in the one within.
Just now your thoughts were coming among mine
With like gesture and with like face,
So that of both I made a single plan.
If it is the case that the right bank slopes so
That we can descend into the other ditch,
We shall escape the imagined chase.
He had not yet finished giving this counsel
When I saw them coming with wings outstretched,
Not very far off, intent on seizing us.
My guide at once then took hold of me,
Like the mother who is wakened by the noise,
Who sees close to her the flames alight,
Who takes her child and flees without a pause,
Having more care for him than for herself,
So much so that she wears only a shift:
And down from the crest of the hard bank
On his back he gave himself to the sloping rock
That closes off one of the sides of the other ditch.
Never did water run so swiftly through a chute
To turn the wheel of a land mill,
When it draws closest to the paddles,
As my master down that edge,
Carrying me away upon his breast,
Like his child, and not like a companion.
At the foot his feet had reached the bed
Of the bottom below when they reached the crest
Right above us: but there was no fear of them;
For the high Providence that willed them
To be set as ministers of the fifth ditch
Takes from them all the power to depart from there.
Down there we found a painted people
Who went round with very slow steps,
Weeping, and in appearance weary and overcome.
They had cloaks with hoods low
Before their eyes, made in the cut
That in Cologne is used for the monks:
On the outside they are gilded, so that it dazzles them;
But within all lead, and so heavy
That Frederick put cloaks of straw on.
O mantle wearisome for all eternity!
We turned once more, still to the left,
Together with them, intent on the sad weeping;
But under the weight that weary people
Came so slowly that we were new
In company at every movement of the hip.
Wherefore I to my guide: See that you find
Someone whose deed and name are known,
And move your eyes about as you go.
And one who understood the Tuscan speech
Cried out behind us: Stay your feet,
You who run so through the dark air:
Perhaps you will have from me what you ask.
Whereupon the guide turned and said: Wait,
And then according to his pace proceed.
I stopped, and saw two showing great haste,
In the expression of their faces, to be with me;
But the load and the narrow way delayed them.
When they had arrived, long with sidelong eye
They looked me over without saying a word:
Then they turned to each other and said between themselves:
This one seems alive from the movement of his throat:
And if they are dead, by what privilege
Do they go uncovered by the heavy stole?
Then one said to me: O Tuscan, who to the college
Of the sad hypocrites have come,
Do not disdain to say who you are.
And I then: I was born and raised
Upon the fair river Arno in the great city,
And I am with the body I have always had.
But who are you, for whom so much drips,
As I see, of sorrow down your cheeks,
And what punishment is upon you that so sparkles?
And one answered: Alas! the orange cloaks
Are of lead so thick that the weights
Make their scales creak so.
Jovial Friars we were, and Bolognese,
I Catalano and this one Loderingo
By name, and by your city taken together,
As one man alone is wont to be taken
To preserve its peace, and we were such
That it still shows round the Guardingo.
I began: O Friars, your sufferings...
But no more did I say: for before my eyes came
One crucified on the ground with three stakes.
When he saw me, he writhed all over,
Blowing into his beard with sighs;
And Friar Catalano, who noticed this,
Said to me: That one nailed down whom you gaze at
Advised the Pharisees that it was fitting
To put one man to torments for the people.
Crosswise and naked he lies in the way,
As you see, and he must feel,
Whoever passes, how much he weighs first:
And in such a manner his father-in-law suffers
In this ditch, and the others of the council
That was an evil seed for the Jews.
Then I saw Virgil marvel
At him who was stretched out in a cross
So vilely in eternal exile.
Then he addressed these words to the Friar:
May it not displease you, if you may, to tell us
Whether on the right hand there lies some opening
By which we both may make our way out of here
Without compelling any of the black angels
To come and take us out of this depth.
He answered then: More than you hope
There draws near a ridge of rock that from the great circle
Sets out and crosses all the savage valleys,
Except that at this one it is broken and does not span it:
You will be able to climb up over the wreckage
That lies on the slope and heaps up on the bottom.
The guide stood a little while with head bowed,
Then said: Falsely sang of the matter
He who hooks the sinners hereabouts.
And the Friar: Once I heard tell in Bologna
Of many vices of the devil, among which I heard
That he is a liar and the father of lies.
After that the guide went off with great strides,
A little troubled with anger in his look:
Whereupon I departed from the burdened ones,
Following the tracks of the dear feet.
