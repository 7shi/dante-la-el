We were at the top of the stair,
where for the second time is girdled
the mountain that, as one climbs, rids one of evil:
there a ledge so binds itself
about the hill, as does the first,
except that its arc bends more sharply.
No shadows are there, nor marks that show;
so bare appears the bank, and so bare appears the way,
with the livid color of the rock.
If here we wait for people to ask,
the Poet was saying, I rather fear
that our choice will suffer too much delay.
Then steadily he turned his eyes toward the sun;
he made his right side the pivot of his movement,
and turned the left part of himself.
O sweet light, trusting in which I enter
upon the new path, do you conduct us,
he was saying, as one ought to be conducted here within.
You warm the world, you shine upon it;
if no other reason urges the contrary,
your rays ought always to be guides.
As much as on this side is reckoned a mile,
so far on that side had we already gone,
in little time, through our ready will.
And toward us were heard flying,
yet not seen, spirits, speaking
courteous invitations to the table of love.
The first voice that passed flying
said loudly, They have no wine,
and behind us went on repeating it.
And before it could no longer be heard at all
for growing distant, another, I am Orestes,
passed crying, and likewise did not stop.
O, said I, Father, what voices are these?
And as I asked, behold the third,
saying: Love those from whom you have received harm.
And the good master: This circle scourges
the sin of envy, and therefore are
drawn from love the cords of the scourge.
The bridle must be of the contrary sound;
I believe you will hear it, as I judge,
before you reach the pass of pardon.
But fix your gaze through the air most steadily,
and you will see people sitting before us,
and each is seated along the cliff.
Then wider than before I opened my eyes;
I looked before me and saw shades in cloaks
no different from the color of the stone.
And after we were a little farther on,
I heard them cry: Mary, pray for us,
cry: Michael, Peter, and all the Saints.
I do not believe there walks the earth today
a man so hard that he would not have been pierced
with compassion at what I next saw:
for when I had come so close to them
that their gestures grew clear to me,
through my eyes was grievous sorrow wrung from me.
With coarse haircloth they seemed to me covered,
and one supported the other with his shoulder,
and all by the bank were supported.
Thus the blind, who lack means,
stand at the pardons to ask for what they need,
and one lowers his head upon the other,
so that pity may soon be planted in others,
not only through the sounding of the words,
but through the sight that begs no less.
And as the sun does not avail the blind,
so to the shades, there whereof I now speak,
the light of heaven will not give of itself;
for in all of them an iron wire pierces the eyelid,
and sews it up thus, as to a wild sparrowhawk
is done, because it does not stay still.
It seemed to me, as I went, that I did a wrong,
seeing others, not being seen:
therefore I turned to my wise counselor.
Well did he know what the silent one wished to say;
and therefore he did not await my question,
but said: Speak, and be brief and pointed.
Virgil was coming beside me on that side
of the ledge from which one may fall,
because it is ringed by no parapet:
on the other side of me were the devout
shades, who through the horrible seam
pressed so hard that they wet their cheeks.
I turned to them, and: O people sure,
I began, of seeing the high light
which alone your desire holds dear;
so may grace soon dissolve the scum
of your conscience, so that clear
through it may flow the river of the mind,
tell me, for it will be pleasing and dear to me,
if any soul is here among you that is Italian;
and perhaps it will be good for that soul if I learn it.
O my brother, each is a citizen
of one true city; but you mean
one who lived in Italy as a pilgrim.
This I seemed to hear in answer
a little farther on than where I stood;
therefore I made myself heard still farther on.
Among the others I saw a shade that was waiting
to all appearance; and should anyone ask: How?
Its chin, in the manner of a blind person, it raised upward.
Spirit, I said, who to ascend subdue yourself,
if you are the one who answered me,
make yourself known to me by place or by name.
I was Sienese, she answered, and with these
others I mend here my wicked life,
weeping to him that he may lend himself to us.
Wise I was not, although Sapia
I was called, and at the losses of others I was
far gladder than at my own good fortune.
And so that you may not believe that I deceive you,
hear whether I was, as I tell you, foolish.
As I was already descending the arc of my years,
my fellow citizens were near Colle
met on the field with their adversaries,
and I was praying to God for what he willed.
Routed were they there, and turned to the bitter
steps of flight, and seeing the chase,
I took a joy unlike any other;
so much so that I turned upward my bold face,
crying to God: Now I fear you no more;
as does the blackbird at a little fair weather.
Peace I desired with God at the very end
of my life; and still would not be
my debt by penance lessened,
were it not that I was held in remembrance by
Pier Pettinaio in his holy prayers,
who out of charity took pity on me.
But who are you, who about our condition
go asking, and keep your eyes unbound,
as I believe, and speak while breathing?
My eyes, said I, will yet be taken from me here;
but for a short time, for small is the offense
committed by their having been turned in envy.
Far greater is the fear in which is held in suspense
my soul by the torment below,
for already the burden down there weighs on me.
And she to me: Who then has brought you
up here among us, if you expect to return below?
And I: This one who is with me and says not a word:
I am alive; and therefore ask me,
chosen spirit, if you wish me to move
somewhere on the other side my still mortal feet.
Oh, this is so new a thing to hear
that it gives great sign that God loves you;
therefore with your prayer help me at times.
I ask you, by that which you most desire,
if ever you tread the earth of Tuscany,
that among my kinsfolk you restore my good name.
You will see them among that vain people
who set their hope on Talamone, and will lose there
more hope than in seeking Diana;
but more will the admirals lose there.
