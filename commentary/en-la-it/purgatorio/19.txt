At the hour when the day's heat can no
longer warm the cold of the moon,
overcome by the earth, or at times by autumn;
when geomancers their Greater Fortune
see in the east, before dawn,
rising by a way that stays dark for it but little;
there came to me in a dream a stammering woman,
cross-eyed, and crooked on her feet,
with maimed hands and pale in color.
I gazed at her; and as the sun revives
the cold limbs that the night weighs down,
so my gaze made nimble for her
her tongue, and then straightened her whole body
in little time, and her wan face,
as love wills, so it colored.
And after she had her speech thus loosened,
she began to sing so that with difficulty
would I have turned my attention from her.
I am, she sang, I am a sweet siren,
who lead sailors astray in mid-sea;
so full am I of pleasure to the ear.
I turned Ulysses, eager on his way,
to my song; and whoever grows used to me
seldom departs from me, so wholly do I satisfy him.
Not yet was her mouth closed again,
when a lady appeared, holy and swift,
beside me to confound that woman.
O Virgil, Virgil, who is this?
she said fiercely; and he came
with eyes fixed only on that noble lady.
He took the other and laid her open in front,
tearing the clothes and showing me her belly;
that woke me with the stench that came from it.
I turned my eyes to the good master; and meanwhile
I heard him as though he were saying: Rise and come,
let us find the gate by which you may enter.
Up I rose, and all were already full
of the high daylight, the circles of the holy mountain,
and we went with the new sun at our backs.
Following him, I bore my brow
like one who has it laden with thought,
who makes of himself half an arch of a bridge;
when I heard: Come, here one crosses;
spoken in a manner sweet and kindly,
such as is not heard in this mortal realm.
With wings spread that seemed a swan's,
he who thus spoke to us turned us upward,
between two walls of hard rock.
He moved his feathers then and fanned us,
affirming those who mourn to be blessed,
for their souls shall be mistresses of consolation.
What ails you, that you keep looking toward the earth?
my guide began to say to me,
when we had both climbed a little above the angel.
And I: With such misgiving does it make me go,
a new vision that bends me toward itself,
so that I cannot break away from thinking.
Did you see, he said, that ancient witch,
who alone above us is lamented henceforth?
Did you see how a man frees himself from her?
Let that suffice you, and strike the ground with your heels,
turn your eyes to the lure that is spun
by the eternal king with the great wheels.
Like the falcon that first looks at its feet,
then turns at the cry and stretches forward,
for desire of the food that draws it there;
such did I become, and thus, where it is cleft,
the rock, to make a way for him who goes up,
I went on as far as where the circling begins.
When I emerged upon the fifth circle,
I saw people along it who were weeping,
lying on the ground, all turned face down.
My soul has clung to the pavement,
I heard them say with sighs so deep
that the words could scarcely be understood.
O chosen of God, whose sufferings
both justice and hope make less hard,
direct us toward the ascents on high.
If you come exempt from lying down,
and wish to find the way more quickly,
let your right sides be always to the outside.
Thus the poet prayed, and thus an answer
was given us a little ahead of us; whereupon I
discerned in the speech the rest that was hidden;
and I turned my eyes to my lord's eyes:
upon which he granted me with a cheerful nod
what the look of my desire was asking.
After I could do with myself as I saw fit,
I drew up over that creature
whose words had first made me take note,
saying: Spirit, in whom weeping ripens
that without which one cannot return to God,
pause a little for me in your greater care.
Who you were, and why you have your backs turned
upward, tell me, and whether you wish me to obtain for you
anything from there, whence I set out alive.
And he to me: Why our backs
heaven turns toward itself, you shall know; but first,
know that I was a successor of Peter.
Between Sestri and Chiavari flows down
a fair river, and of its name
the title of my bloodline makes its crown.
For a month or little more I learned how
the great mantle weighs on him who keeps it from the mud,
so that all other burdens seem a feather.
My conversion, alas, was late;
but as soon as I was made the Roman shepherd,
so did I discover the deceitful life.
I saw that there the heart did not find rest,
nor could one climb higher in that life;
hence love of this one was kindled in me.
Until that point a wretched and parted
soul from God was I, wholly avaricious:
now, as you see, here I am punished for it.
What avarice does is here made manifest
in the purgation of converted souls,
and no punishment has the mountain more bitter.
Just as our eye did not open
on high, fixed on earthly things,
so justice here has sunk it to the ground.
As avarice quenched for every good
our love, whereby the power to act was lost,
so justice here holds us fast,
bound and captive in feet and hands;
and as long as it shall please the supreme lord,
so long shall we stay motionless and stretched out.
I had knelt down, and wished to speak;
but as I began, he became aware,
by listening alone, of my reverence:
What cause, he said, bent you down so?
And I to him: Because of your dignity
my conscience reproached me for standing upright.
Straighten your legs, stand upright, brother,
he answered, do not err; a fellow servant am I
with you and with the others, under one power.
If ever that holy voice of the Gospel,
which says, Neither shall they marry, you have heeded,
well can you know why I speak thus.
Go now; I do not wish you to stay longer,
for your staying hinders my weeping,
with which I ripen what you spoke of.
A niece I have over there whose name is Lagia,
good of herself, provided our house
does not by example make her wicked;
and she alone is left to me over there.
