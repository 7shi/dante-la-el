When the Seven Stars of the first heaven,
which never knew either setting or rising,
nor a veil of any mist save that of guilt,
which there made each one aware
of his duty, as the lower one does
whoever turns a helm to come to port,
stood fixed and still, the truthful folk,
who had come first between the griffin and it,
turned themselves to where the chariot was, as to their peace:
and one of them, as if sent from heaven,
Come, bride, from Lebanon, singing,
cried out three times, and all the others after him.
As the blessed at the last summons
will rise promptly, each from his cavern,
singing alleluia with the voice regained,
so, upon the divine litter,
rose folk, at the voice of so great an elder,
ministers and messengers of eternal life.
All were saying: Blessed are you who come;
and, scattering flowers above and all around,
O give lilies by the handful.
I have seen before, at the beginning of the day,
the eastern quarter all rosy,
and the rest of the sky adorned with fair clear blue,
and the face of the sun rise shaded,
so that, through the tempering of vapors,
the eye could bear it for a long while:
so within a cloud of flowers,
which from angelic hands was rising,
and falling down again within and without,
over a white veil girt with olive
a lady appeared to me, beneath a green mantle,
clothed in the color of living flame.
My spirit, when already for so long
a time it had been in her presence,
was not, trembling, overcome with awe,
without having further knowledge through the eyes,
through a hidden power that went forth from her,
felt the great power of ancient love.
As soon as there struck me in the sight
the lofty power that had already pierced me
before I was out of childhood,
I turned to the left with the look
with which the little child runs to its mother,
when it is afraid or when it is distressed,
to say to Virgil: Less than a dram
of blood is left in me that does not tremble;
I recognize the signs of the ancient flame.
But Virgil had left us bereft;
I said: Virgil, sweetest father,
Virgil, to whom for my salvation I gave myself:
and nothing that the ancient mother lost
availed to keep my cheeks, cleansed with dew,
from turning dark again with weeping.
Dante, because Virgil goes away,
do not weep yet, do not weep yet,
for you must weep for another sword.
Like an admiral who, to stern and to prow,
comes to see the people who serve
aboard the tall ships, and heartens them to do well,
upon the left side of the chariot,
when I turned at the sound of my name,
which of necessity is recorded here,
I saw the lady, who there appeared to me
veiled beneath the angelic celebration,
direct her eyes toward me on this side of the stream.
Although the veil that descended from her head,
encircled with the foliage of Minerva,
did not let her appear distinctly,
regally, in her bearing still imperious,
she began again, like one who speaks
and keeps the more ardent speech in reserve:
Look at us well; I am indeed Beatrice;
how did you deign to approach the mountain?
Did you not know that here man is happy?
My eyes fell down to the clear spring;
but, seeing myself in it, I drew them to the grass,
so much shame weighed down my brow.
So does a mother seem haughty to her child,
as she seemed to me; for as bitter
I felt the taste of stern pity.
She fell silent, and the angels sang
at once: In you, O Lord, have I hoped:
but beyond My feet they did not pass.
Just as snow among the living timbers
along the spine of Italy freezes,
blown and packed by the Slavonian winds,
then, melted, trickles through itself,
if only the land that loses its shadow breathes,
so that it seems like fire melting the candle;
so without tears or sighs was I
before the singing of those who ever sing
following the notes of the eternal spheres.
But after I heard in the sweet harmonies
their compassion, it seemed as if they had said:
Lady, why do you so unmake him?
The ice that had drawn tight around my heart
became breath and water, and with anguish
through mouth and eyes came forth from my breast.
She, still fixed upon the right flank
of the chariot, standing, to the pious beings
then turned her words thus:
You keep vigil in the eternal day,
so that neither night nor sleep steals from you
a step the world takes along its ways;
hence my answer is given with greater care
that he who weeps over there may understand me,
so that guilt and grief may be of one measure.
Not only through the working of the great wheels,
which direct each seed to some end,
according as the wheels are joined together;
but through the bounty of divine graces,
which have such vapors for their rain
that our sight does not come near them,
this man was such in his young life
in potential that every good disposition
would have given wondrous proof in him.
But so much the more rank and the wilder
grows the soil with bad seed and left untilled,
the more good vigor of earth it has.
For a time I sustained him with my face;
showing my youthful eyes to him,
I led him with me, turned in the right direction.
As soon as upon the threshold I was
of my second age and changed my life,
this man withdrew from me and gave himself to others.
When from flesh to spirit I had risen,
and beauty and virtue had grown in me,
I was to him less dear and less pleasing;
and he turned his steps to a way not true,
following false images of good,
which fulfill no promise in full.
Nor did it avail me to obtain inspirations by prayer,
with which in dreams and otherwise
I called him back; so little did he care for them.
So far down he fell that all means
for his salvation already fell short,
except to show him the lost people.
For this I visited the doorway of the dead,
and to him who has led him up here,
my prayers, as I wept, were offered.
A high deed of God would be broken,
if Lethe were crossed, and such fare
were tasted without any payment
of repentance that sheds tears.
