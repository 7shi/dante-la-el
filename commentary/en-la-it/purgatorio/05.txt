I had already departed from those shades,
And was following the footprints of my guide,
When behind me a finger was pointed
By one, who cried: Look, it does not seem to shine,
The ray from the left, on the lower one,
And like a living man he seems to bear himself.
And I turned my eyes at the sound of these words,
And saw them looking in wonder
At me alone, at me alone, and at the light that was broken.
Why does your mind become so entangled,
Said the master, that you slacken your pace?
What is it to you what is whispered here?
Come after me, and let the people talk;
Stand firm, like a tower that does not shake
Its summit ever, however the winds may blow.
For always the man in whom thought springs
Upon thought puts the mark farther from himself,
Because the force of one slackens that of the other.
What could I say, except: I am coming?
I said it, somewhat tinged with the color
That makes a man at times worthy of pardon.
Meanwhile across the slope
People were coming a little way ahead of us,
Singing Miserere verse by verse.
When they perceived that I gave no room,
Because of my body, for the passage of the rays,
They changed their song to a long and hoarse O;
And two of them, in the guise of messengers,
Ran toward us and asked us:
Of your condition make us aware.
And my master: You may go back,
And report to those who sent you
That this man's body is true flesh.
If at seeing his shadow they stopped,
As I suppose, they are answered enough:
Let them do him honor, and it may be dear to them.
I did not see fiery vapors so swiftly
At midnight ever cleave the clear sky,
Nor, as the sun sank, the clouds of August,
But those two returned above in less time,
And having arrived there, with the others turned toward us,
Like a troop that races without restraint.
These people who come to us are many,
And they come to entreat you, said the poet;
Therefore keep going, and as they ask, listen.
O soul, who go on your way to be blessed
With those limbs with which you were born,
They came crying, slow your step a little.
Look whether you ever saw any one of us,
So that you may carry news of him back there:
Ah, why do you go? Ah, why do you not stop?
We were all once put to death by violence,
And sinners until the last hour:
Then light from heaven made us aware,
So that, repenting and forgiving, out
Of life we passed, reconciled to God,
Who makes our hearts ache with longing to see him.
And I: Though I gaze into your faces,
I recognize no one; but if you desire
A thing within my power, O spirits born to good,
Tell me; and I shall do it, by that peace
Which, following the feet of such a guide,
From world to world I am made to seek.
And one began: Each of us trusts
In your good offices without your swearing to them,
Provided inability does not cut short your will.
Therefore I, who alone speak before the others,
Beg you, if ever you see that land
Which lies between Romagna and Charles's land,
That you be generous to me with your entreaties
In Fano, so that prayers may well be said for me,
If only I may purge my grave offenses.
From there I came; but the deep holes
Whence came the blood on which my life rested
Were made in me in the lap of Antenor's people,
There where I believed myself most secure:
The man of Este had it done, who held me in hatred
Far beyond what justice would allow.
But if I had fled toward Mira,
When I was overtaken at Oriaco,
I would still be over there where people breathe.
I ran to the marsh; and the reeds and the mire
Caught me so that I fell, and there I saw
From my veins a lake form on the ground.
Then said another: Ah, so may that longing
Be fulfilled which draws you to the high mountain,
With kindly pity help mine too.
I was of Montefeltro, I was Buonconte:
Giovanna and the others take no thought for me;
Therefore I go among these with a cheerful countenance.
And I to him: What force, or what chance,
Led you so far astray from Campaldino
That your burial place was never known?
Oh, he answered, at the foot of the Casentino
There flows across a stream named the Archiano,
Which above the Hermitage rises in the Apennines.
Where its name becomes void
I arrived, pierced in the throat,
Fleeing on foot and bloodying the plain.
There I lost my sight, and my speech
I ended with the name of Mary, and there
I fell, and my flesh remained alone.
I shall tell the truth, and you tell it again among the living;
God's angel took me, and the one from Hell
Cried: O you from heaven, why do you rob me?
You carry away the eternal part of this man
For one little tear that takes him from me:
But the other part I shall treat otherwise.
You well know how in the air there gathers
That moist vapor which turns back to water
As soon as it rises where the cold seizes it.
He joined that evil will, which seeks only evil,
With intellect, and set mist and wind in motion
By the power that his nature gave him.
There the valley, as soon as day was quenched,
From Pratomagno to the great ridge he covered
With fog, and made the cold above intense
So that the laden air turned to water:
The rain fell, and into the ditches came
As much of it as the earth could not bear:
And as it gathered in the great streams,
Toward the royal river so swiftly
It hurtled that nothing held it back.
My frozen body at its mouth
The raging Archiano found; and it thrust it
Into the other, and undid on my breast the cross,
I made of myself when pain overcame me:
It rolled me along the banks and along the bottom,
Then with its spoils it covered and encircled me.
Ah, when you have returned to the world,
And rested after the long journey,
The third spirit continued after the second,
Remember me, who am Pia:
Siena made me, and Maremma unmade me;
He knows it who had first ringed me,
In wedding me, with his gem.
