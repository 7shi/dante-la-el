Eager now to search within and round about
the divine forest, dense and living,
which tempered the new day to my eyes,
without waiting longer I left the edge,
taking to the open ground slowly, slowly,
over the soil that on every side breathed fragrance.
A sweet breeze, without any change
in itself, struck me on the brow
with no more force than a gentle wind;
at which the leaves, readily trembling,
all bent to the side
where first the holy mountain casts its shadow:
yet not so far from their upright stance were they bent
that the little birds among the treetops
ceased to ply all their art;
but with full gladness the first hours,
singing, they welcomed among the leaves,
which held the drone to their rhymes.
Just such a sound gathers from branch to branch
through the pinewood, on the shore of Chiassi,
when Aeolus lets the sirocco loose.
So far had my slow steps already carried me
within the wood that I
could not see again where I had entered:
and behold, a stream kept me from going farther,
which toward the left with its little waves
bent the grass that sprang up on its bank.
All the waters that are purest here on earth
would seem to have in them some admixture
beside that one, which hides nothing;
although it flows dark, dark
beneath the perpetual shade, which never
lets sun or moon shine there.
With my feet I stood still and with my eyes I passed
beyond the little river, to admire
the great variety of fresh May boughs:
and there appeared to me, just as there appears
suddenly something that turns aside
through wonder every other thought,
a woman all alone, who went along
singing and choosing flower from flower,
with which all her path was painted.
Ah, fair lady, who in the rays of love
warm yourself, if I am to believe those looks
that are wont to bear witness to the heart,
may you be moved to come forward,
I said to her, toward this bank,
far enough that I may understand what you sing.
You make me remember where and how
Proserpina was at the time when her mother lost
her, and she the spring.
As a dancing woman turns, with the soles of her feet close
to the ground and to each other,
and scarcely sets foot before foot,
she turned upon the crimson and upon the yellow
little flowers toward me, no otherwise
than a maiden who lowers her modest eyes;
and she brought my prayers to fulfillment,
drawing so near that the sweet sound
came to me with its meanings.
As soon as she was there where the grasses are
already bathed by the waves of the fair river,
she gave me the gift of raising her eyes.
Nor do I believe so much light shone
beneath the brows of Venus pierced
by her son, quite against his custom.
She smiled, standing on the other bank,
plucking with her hands the many colors
that the high land puts forth without seed.
Three paces apart the river kept us;
but the Hellespont, where Xerxes crossed it,
still a curb on all human pride,
suffered no greater hatred from Leander
for surging between Sestos and Abydos
than that stream from me because it did not then part.
You are newcomers, and perhaps because I smile,
she began, in this place chosen
for human nature as its nest,
some doubt holds you wondering;
but the psalm Delectasti gives light
that can clear the mist from your understanding.
And you who are in front and made your plea to me,
say if you wish to hear more, for I came ready
for every question of yours, as fully as need be.
The water, I said, and the sound of the forest
challenge within me a newfound belief
in something I heard contrary to this.
Whereupon she: I shall tell how there comes about
through its own cause what makes you marvel,
and I shall clear away the mist that afflicts you.
The highest good, which alone is pleasing to itself,
made man good, and for good this place
it gave him as a pledge of eternal peace.
Through his own fault he dwelt here but a little;
through his own fault into weeping and into distress
he changed innocent laughter and sweet play.
So that the turmoil that beneath them is made by
the exhalations of the water and the earth,
which, as far as they can, follow after heat,
should wage no war on man,
this mountain rose so far toward heaven,
and is free of it from where it is gated.
Now, because in a circuit all
the air turns with the first revolving sphere,
unless its circle is broken at some point,
against this height, which stands wholly free
in the living air, that motion strikes
and makes the forest sound because it is dense;
and the plant thus struck has such power
that with its virtue it impregnates the air,
and the air then, circling round, shakes it out:
and the other earth, according as it is fitted
by itself and by its sky, conceives and gives birth
from diverse virtues to diverse trees.
It would then seem no marvel over there,
once this is heard, when some plant
without apparent seed takes root there.
And you must know that the holy field
where you are is full of every seed,
and has in it fruit that over there is not plucked.
The water you see does not rise from a vein
replenished by vapor that cold condenses,
like a river that gains and loses strength;
but it issues from a fountain steady and sure,
which takes in again by the will of God as much
as it pours forth, open on two sides.
On this side it descends with a power
that takes from one the memory of sin;
on the other, of every good deed it restores the memory.
Here it is called Lethe, and on the other side
Eunoe; and it does not work
unless here and there it is first tasted.
Above all other savors is this one:
and although well satisfied may be
your thirst, even if I reveal no more to you,
I shall give you a corollary too, as a favor;
nor do I believe my speech will be less dear to you
if beyond my promise it ranges thus with you.
Those who in ancient times sang in verse
of the age of gold and its happy state
perhaps on Parnassus dreamed of this place.
Here the first root was innocent;
here is spring always, and every fruit;
nectar is this of which each poet speaks.
I turned then quite around
to my poets, and saw that with a smile
they had heard the last words:
then to the fair lady I turned my face again.
