Side by side, like oxen that go under the yoke,
I went along with that burdened soul,
as long as the gentle tutor allowed it.
But when he said: Leave him, and pass on,
for here it is good with sail and with oars,
as much as each one can, to drive his boat:
upright, as one ought to walk, I made myself again
in body, although the thoughts
within me remained bowed and lowly.
I had moved on, and was gladly following
my master's steps, and we both
already showed how light we were;
and he said to me: Turn your eyes downward:
it will be good for you, to ease the way,
to see the bed beneath the soles of your feet.
Just as, that there may be remembrance of them,
above the buried the tombs set in the ground
bear engraved what each was before;
whence there one often weeps over it
for the prick of remembrance,
which sets spurs only to the compassionate;
so I saw there, but of finer appearance
in point of workmanship, figured
all that projects from the mountain to form the way.
I saw him who was created noble
more than any other creature, down from heaven
flashing like lightning, descend on one side.
And I saw Briareus, pierced by the bolt
of heaven, lying on the other side,
heavy upon the earth with the chill of death.
I saw Thymbraeus, I saw Pallas and Mars,
still armed, around their father,
gazing at the scattered limbs of the Giants.
I saw Nimrod at the foot of the great work,
as if bewildered, looking at the peoples
who in Shinar had been proud with him.
O Niobe, with what grieving eyes
I saw you figured on the road
amid seven and seven dead children of yours!
O Saul, how upon your own sword
there you appeared dead on Gilboa,
which thereafter felt neither rain nor dew!
O foolish Arachne, so I saw you
already half spider, sorrowing over the shreds
of the work that was made to your harm.
O Rehoboam, no longer threatening seems
your image there; but full of terror
a chariot bears it away before anyone drives it off.
The hard pavement showed also
how Alcmaeon made dear to his mother
seem the ill-fated ornament.
It showed how the sons threw themselves
upon Sennacherib inside the temple,
and how they left him dead there.
It showed the ruin and the cruel slaughter
that Tomyris wrought, when she said to Cyrus:
For blood you thirsted, and with blood I fill you.
It showed how in rout fled
the Assyrians, after Holofernes was dead,
and also the remains of the slaughter.
I saw Troy in ashes and hollows:
O Ilium, how you low and vile
the image discerned there showed!
What master was there of brush or stylus
who could render the shadows and the gestures, there
would make every subtle mind marvel?
Dead seemed the dead, and the living seemed alive:
no better than I did he see who saw the reality
of all I trod on as long as I went bowed.
Now wax proud, and on with haughty face,
children of Eve, and do not bow your faces
so that you may see your evil path.
More of the mountain was already turned toward us,
and much more of the sun's course spent,
than my engrossed mind reckoned;
when he who, always intent on what lay ahead,
went on, began: Raise your head;
it is no longer time to go so absorbed.
See over there an angel who makes ready
to come toward us: see how there returns
from the service of the day the sixth handmaid.
With reverence adorn your face and gestures,
so that it may delight him to send us upward:
think that this day never dawns again.
I was well used to his admonishing me
always not to lose time, so that on that
matter he could not speak obscurely to me.
Toward us came the beautiful creature,
clad in white, and in the face such
as the morning star appears, trembling.
He opened his arms, and then he opened his wings;
he said: Come; the steps are near here,
and easily now one ascends.
To this announcement very few come:
O humankind, born to fly upward,
why at a little wind do you so fall?
He led us where the rock was cut;
there he brushed my forehead with his wings,
then promised me safe passage.
As on the right hand, to climb the hill
where sits the church that looks down upon
the well-guided city above Rubaconte,
the bold sweep of the ascent is broken
by the stairs which were made in an age
when the ledger and the stave were safe;
so the slope is eased that falls
there very steeply from the next terrace:
but on this side and on that the high rock grazes one.
Then we turned our bodies there,
Blessed are the poor in spirit, voices
sang in such a way that speech could not tell it.
Ah! How different are those voices
from those of Hell; for here to songs
one enters, and down there to fierce laments.
Already we were climbing up the holy steps,
and I seemed to be far lighter
than on the level I had seemed before;
whence I: Master, say, what heavy thing
has been lifted from me, that almost no
weariness is felt by me as I go?
He answered: When the P's that have remained
still on your face, all but effaced,
shall be, like the one, wholly erased,
your feet will be so conquered by good will
that not only will they feel no weariness,
but it will be their delight to be driven upward.
Then I did as those do who go
with something on their head, unknown to them,
save that others' signs make them suspect it;
wherefore the hand lends aid by feeling round,
and seeks and finds, and performs that office
which cannot be fulfilled by sight;
and with the fingers of my right hand spread apart
I found but six of the letters that engraved
he of the keys upon me above my temples.
At which, looking on, my guide smiled.
