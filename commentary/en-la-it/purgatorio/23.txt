While my eyes through the green leaves
I kept fixing thus, as is the wont
of one who after the little birds wastes his life;
he who was more than a father said to me: My son,
come away now, for the time allotted us
must be more usefully apportioned.
I turned my face and my step no less promptly
after the sages, who spoke in such a way
that they made the going cost me nothing.
And behold, weeping and singing were heard:
My lips, O Lord, in a manner
such that it brought forth delight and grief.
O sweet Father, what is it that I hear?
I began; and he: Shades that go
perhaps untying the knot of their debt.
Just as pensive pilgrims do,
overtaking on the road people unknown to them,
turning toward them and not stopping;
so too, behind us, moving more swiftly,
coming up and passing on, gazed at us in wonder
a throng of souls, silent and devout.
About the eyes each was dark and hollow,
pale in the face, and so wasted
that from the bones the skin took its shape.
I do not believe that to so bare a rind
Erysichthon was dried up
by fasting, when he had most fear of it.
I was saying to myself, thinking: Behold
the people who lost Jerusalem,
when Mary set her beak to her son.
Their eye sockets seemed rings without gems.
Whoever in the face of men reads HOMO
would well have recognized the M there.
Who would believe that the scent of a fruit,
so disposing them, could engender craving,
or that of water, without knowing how?
Already I was wondering what so famishes them,
for the cause was not yet manifest
of their leanness and of their sorry scaly skin;
and behold, from the depths of its head
a shade turned its eyes to me and gazed hard;
then cried aloud: What grace is this to me?
Never would I have recognized his face;
but in his face he made plain to me
what his appearance had effaced in itself.
This speech wholly rekindled in me
my recognition of the changed countenance,
and I recognized the face of Forese.
Ah! Pay no heed to the dry scab
that discolors, he entreated, my skin,
nor to any lack of flesh I may have;
but tell me the truth about yourself, and who are those
two souls who escort you there:
do not hold back from speaking to me.
Your face, which I once wept over in death,
gives me now no less grief to weep for,
I answered him, seeing it so distorted.
Therefore tell me, for God's sake, what so strips you;
do not make me speak while I wonder,
for ill can he speak who is full of another desire.
And he to me: From the eternal counsel
power descends into the water and into the tree
left behind, whereby I grow so thin.
All these people, who weeping are so many,
for following their appetite beyond measure,
in hunger and thirst here make themselves holy again.
A longing to drink and to eat is kindled in us by
the scent that comes from the fruit and from the spray
that spreads over its greenery.
And not only once in this space,
as we circle, is our pain renewed;
I say pain, and I ought to say solace;
for that same desire leads us to the trees
that led Christ gladly to say Eli,
when he set us free with his lifeblood.
And I to him: Forese, since that day
on which you exchanged the world for a better life,
five years have not gone by even now.
If the power in you had ended sooner
to sin further, than there came the hour
of the good sorrow that weds us again to God,
how have you come up here already?
I thought to find you down below,
there where time for time is made good.
Whereupon he to me: So swiftly has brought me
to drink the sweet wormwood of torments
my Nella with her unrestrained weeping.
With her devout prayers and with her sighs
she has drawn me from the slope where one waits,
and has freed me from these circles.
So much dearer and more beloved to God is
my little widow, whom I loved much,
as in doing good she is the more alone;
for the Barbagia of Sardinia by far
is more modest in its women
than the Barbagia where I left her.
O sweet brother, what would you have me say?
A time to come is already in my sight,
to which this hour will not be very ancient,
in which it will be forbidden from the pulpit
to the shameless Florentine women
to go about showing their bosoms with their breasts.
What barbarian women were there ever, what Saracen women,
for whom there was need, to make them go covered,
of either spiritual or other discipline?
But if the shameless women were certain
of what swift heaven is preparing for them,
already they would have their mouths open to howl.
For, if foresight here does not deceive me,
they will be sorrowful before hair grows on the cheeks
of him who now is comforted with a lullaby.
Ah! Brother, now see that you hide yourself no longer from me;
see that not I alone, but these people
all gaze there where you veil the sun.
Wherefore I to him: If you call to mind
what you were with me and what I was with you,
even now the remembering will be grievous.
From that life this one turned me
who goes before me, the other day, when round
there appeared to you the sister of that one.
And I pointed to the sun. This one through the deep
night of the truly dead has led me,
with this true flesh that follows him.
Thence his encouragements have drawn me up,
climbing and looking upon the mountain
that straightens you whom the world made crooked.
So long, he says, will he keep me company
until I am there where Beatrice will be;
there I must remain without him.
Virgil is this one who speaks thus to me,
and I pointed to him, and this other is that shade
for whom just now every slope was shaken by
your kingdom, which sends him forth from itself.
