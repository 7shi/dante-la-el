It was an hour when the ascent would brook no delay,
for the sun had the circle of noon
left to Taurus, and night to Scorpio.
Wherefore, as does the man who does not stop,
but goes his way, whatever may appear to him,
if the spur of need pierces him;
so we entered by the narrow passage,
one before the other, taking the stair
that by its steepness sets climbers apart.
And like the young stork that lifts its wing
for desire to fly, and does not venture
to leave the nest, and lowers it;
such was I, with desire kindled and quenched
to ask, coming so far as to make the gesture
that he makes who prepares to speak.
He did not hold back, though our going was swift,
my sweet father, but said: Let fly
the bow of speech, which you have drawn to the iron tip.
Then confidently I opened my mouth,
and began: How can one grow lean
there where the need for nourishment does not touch one?
If you recalled how Meleager
wasted away as a firebrand wasted away,
this would not be, he said, so hard for you:
and if you thought how at your darting movement
your image darts within the mirror,
what seems hard would seem soft to you:
but that you may be at ease in your desire,
behold Statius here, and I call on him and pray
that he be now the healer of your wounds.
If I unfold the eternal view to him,
replied Statius, where you are present,
let my inability to refuse you excuse me.
Then he began: If my words,
son, your mind guards and receives,
they will be a light to you on the how of which you speak.
Perfect blood, which is not then drunk
by the thirsty veins, and so remains
like food that you take from the table,
takes on in the heart, for all the human limbs,
formative power, like that blood
which goes through the veins to become those limbs.
Further digested, it descends where it is more seemly
to be silent than to speak; and thence it afterwards drips
upon other blood in a natural vessel.
There the one and the other gather together,
the one disposed to undergo, and the other to act,
by virtue of the perfect place from which it is pressed;
and, joined to the other, it begins to work,
first coagulating, and then it brings to life
what it made hold together as its matter.
The active power, having become a soul
like that of a plant, differs in this much,
that this one is on the way, and that one already at shore:
it then works so much that already it moves and feels,
like a sea sponge; and then it undertakes
to give organs to the powers of which it is the seed.
Now unfolds, son, now extends itself
the power that comes from the heart of the begetter,
where nature has all the limbs in view:
but how from an animal it becomes an infant,
you do not yet see: this is such a point
that it once led one wiser than you astray;
so that in his teaching he made separate
from the soul the possible intellect,
because he saw no organ assumed by it.
Open your breast to the truth that comes,
and know that, as soon as in the fetus
the articulation of the brain is complete,
the First Mover turns to it, rejoicing
over so much art of nature, and breathes
a new spirit full of power,
which draws what it finds active there
into its substance, and becomes a single soul,
which lives and feels, and turns back upon itself.
And that you may marvel less at my words,
behold the heat of the sun, which becomes wine,
joined to the moisture that flows from the vine.
When Lachesis has no more flax,
the soul is loosed from the flesh, and in potency
carries away with it the human and the divine.
The other powers are every one mute;
memory, intelligence, and will,
in act much keener than before.
Without pausing, of itself it falls
wondrously to one of the shores;
there it first knows its paths.
As soon as the place there circumscribes it,
the formative power radiates round about,
in the same way and as fully as in the living limbs;
and as the air, when it is well laden with rain,
through another's ray that is reflected in it,
becomes adorned with diverse colors;
so the neighboring air there takes on
that form which is stamped upon it,
by its power, by the soul that has stopped:
then, like the little flame
that follows the fire wherever it moves,
its new form follows the spirit.
Because from this it thereafter has its appearance,
it is called a shade; and there it then gives organs
to every sense, even to sight.
By this we speak, and by this we laugh,
by this we make the tears and sighs
that about the mountain you may have heard.
According as desires press upon us,
and the other affections, the shade changes shape;
and this is the cause of what you marvel at.
And already come to the last bend
we had, and turned to the right hand,
and we were intent on another care.
Here the cliff shoots flame outward;
and the ledge breathes a blast upward,
which turns it back, and keeps a path apart from it;
whence we had to go along the open side
one by one, and I feared the fire
on this side, and on that side feared to fall down.
My guide said: Through this place
one must keep a tight rein on the eyes,
for one might go astray for very little.
O highest God of clemency, in the bosom
of the great blaze I then heard sung,
which made me no less eager to turn:
and I saw spirits going through the flame;
wherefore I looked at them and at my steps,
dividing my sight from time to time.
After the end that is made to that hymn,
they cried aloud: I know not a man;
then they began the hymn again in low voices.
Having finished it, again they cried: To the wood
Diana kept, and drove Helice from it,
who had felt the poison of Venus.
Then to the singing they returned; then wives
they cried out, and husbands who were chaste,
as virtue and marriage enjoin upon us.
And this manner I believe lasts them
for all the time that the fire burns them.
With such care it is needful, with such fare,
that the wound at last be sewn up.
