O you who are beyond the sacred river,
Turning her speech upon me with its point,
Which even with its edge had seemed sharp to me,
She began again, continuing without delay:
Say, say if this is true; to so grave a charge
Your confession must be joined.
My strength was so confounded
That my voice stirred, and sooner died
Than it was released from its organs.
She waited a little, then said: What are you thinking?
Answer me; for the sad memories
In you have not yet been impaired by the waters.
Confusion and fear mingled together
Forced such a yes out of my mouth
That to understand it eyes were needed.
As a crossbow breaks, when it shoots,
From too much tension, its string and bow,
And with less force the bolt touches the mark;
So I burst beneath that very heavy load,
Pouring forth tears and sighs,
And my voice slackened in its passage.
Whereupon she to me: Amid desires for me,
Which were leading you to love the good
Short of which there is nothing to aspire to,
What ditches laid across your way or what chains
Did you find, that of passing onward
You should thus strip yourself of hope?
And what advantages and what gains
Showed themselves on the brow of the others,
That you should walk before them?
After drawing a bitter sigh,
I scarcely had the voice that answered,
And my lips with difficulty formed it.
Weeping I said: Things present
With their false pleasure turned my steps,
As soon as your face was hidden.
And she: If you were silent or if you denied
What you confess, no less well known would be
Your fault; to such a judge it is known.
But when from one's own cheek bursts forth
The accusation of sin, in our court
The wheel turns back against the edge.
Nevertheless, that you may better bear the shame
Of your error, and that another time,
Hearing the sirens, you may be stronger,
Put down the seed of weeping, and listen;
Thus you will hear how in the opposite direction
My buried flesh ought to have moved you.
Never did nature or art present
Such delight as the fair limbs in which I
Was enclosed, which are scattered in the earth.
And if the highest delight failed you
Through my death, what mortal thing
Ought then to have drawn you to desire it?
Surely you ought, at the first arrow
Of things that deceive, to have risen up
Behind me, who was no longer such a thing.
They ought not to have weighed your wings downward,
To await more blows, either a young girl
Or any other vanity of such brief use.
A fledgling bird awaits two or three;
But before the eyes of the full-fledged
A net is spread in vain, or an arrow shot.
As children, mute with shame,
With eyes on the ground, stand listening,
Both owning their fault and repentant;
So I stood. And she said: Since
Hearing makes you sorrowful, lift your beard,
And you will feel more sorrow looking.
With less resistance is uprooted
A sturdy oak, whether by our wind
Or by that from the land of Iarbas,
Than I raised my chin at her command;
And when by the beard she called for the face,
Well did I recognize the venom of the argument.
And when my face was lifted,
That those first creatures had ceased
From their activity my eye perceived.
And my eyes, still unsure,
Saw Beatrice turned toward the beast
That alone is one person in two natures.
Beneath her veil and beyond the river,
She seemed to me to surpass, her former self more
To surpass than she did the others here when she was here.
There the nettle of repentance so stung me
That of all things, whichever had drawn me aside
Most into love of it, I made most my enemy.
Such acknowledgment bit at my heart
That I fell overcome, and what I then became,
She knows who gave me cause.
Then, when my heart restored my outward powers,
The lady whom I had left alone
I saw above me, and she was saying: Hold me, hold me.
She had drawn me into the river up to my throat,
And, pulling me behind her, she went
Over the very water, light as a shuttle.
When I was near the blessed bank,
You shall sprinkle me was heard so sweetly
That I cannot recall it, much less write it.
The fair lady opened her arms,
Clasped my head, and submerged me
Where I had to swallow the water.
From there she lifted me, and wet, presented me
Within the dance of the four fair ones,
And each covered me with her arm.
We are nymphs here, and in heaven we are stars;
Before Beatrice descended into the world,
We were appointed to her as her handmaids.
We will lead you to her eyes; but for the joyous
Light that is within, yours will be sharpened by
The three over there, who gaze more deeply.
Thus singing they began; and then
To the griffin's breast they led me with them,
Where Beatrice stood turned toward us.
They said: See that you do not spare your eyes;
We have set you before the emeralds
From which Love once shot his weapons at you.
A thousand desires hotter than flame
Held my eyes fast to the shining eyes
That still stayed fixed upon the griffin.
As a mirror in the sun, no otherwise
The twofold beast shone forth within them,
Now in one manner, now in another.
Think, reader, if I marveled
When I saw the thing itself stay still,
And in its image it was changing.
While, full of wonder and glad,
My soul was tasting of that food
Which, even as it sates itself, makes one thirst for it,
Showing themselves to be of the higher tribe
In their movements, the other three came forward,
Dancing to their angelic roundelay.
Turn, Beatrice, turn your holy eyes,
Was their song, upon your faithful one,
Who, to see you, has taken so many steps.
Of your grace, grant us the grace that you unveil
For him your mouth, so that he may discern
The second beauty that you hide.
O divine splendor, O eternal light,
Who grew pale beneath the shade
Of Parnassus to such a degree, or drank from its cistern,
That he would not seem to have his mind encumbered,
Trying to render you as you appeared
There where, making harmony, heaven overshadows you,
When in the open air you unveiled yourself?
