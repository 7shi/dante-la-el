After the courteous and glad greetings
had been repeated three and four times,
Sordello drew back and said: You, who are you?
Before toward this mountain were turned
the souls worthy to ascend to God,
my bones were buried by Octavian.
I am Virgil; and for no other wrong
did I lose Heaven than for not having faith.
Thus answered then my guide.
As is one who before him something
sudden sees, at which he marvels,
and believes and does not, saying: It is, it is not;
such did he seem, and then he lowered his brows,
and humbly turned back toward him,
and embraced him where the lesser takes hold.
O glory of the Latins, he said, through whom
our tongue showed what it could do,
O eternal honor of the place whence I came,
what merit, or what grace, shows you to me?
If I am worthy to hear your words,
tell me if you come from Hell, and from which cloister.
Through all the circles of the sorrowful realm,
he answered him, I have come here:
power from Heaven moved me, and with it I come.
Not for doing, but for not doing, have I lost
the sight of the high Sun that you desire,
and that too late was known by me.
A place there is down there, not sad from torments,
but from darkness alone, where laments
do not sound like wails, but are sighs.
There I dwell with the innocent little children,
bitten by the teeth of death before
that they were freed from human guilt.
There I dwell with those who in the three holy
virtues did not clothe themselves, and without vice
knew the others and followed them all.
But if you know and can, some indication
give us, so that we may come sooner
there where Purgatory has its true beginning.
He answered: No fixed place is appointed us;
it is lawful for me to go up and about;
as far as I can go, I join you as guide.
But see already how the day declines,
and to go up by night is not possible;
therefore it is well to think of a pleasant resting place.
Souls there are to the right, apart here:
if you consent to me, I shall lead you to them,
and not without delight will they become known to you.
When this had been answered: Whoever wished
to ascend by night, would he be hindered
by another? Or would it be that he could not?
And good Sordello drew his finger along the ground,
saying: See, even this line
you would not cross after the sun had gone;
nor because anything else would give trouble,
other than the darkness of night, in going up:
that darkness entangles the will with inability.
One might indeed in it turn back downward,
and walk the slope, wandering about,
while the horizon keeps the day shut in.
Then my lord, as if marveling:
Lead us, he said, then, there where you say
that one may have delight in staying.
Little distance had we gone from there,
when I perceived that the mountain was hollowed out,
in the way that valleys form hollows here.
Thither, said that shade, we shall go,
where the slope makes of itself a lap,
and there we shall await the new day.
Between steep and level there was a slanting path,
which led us to the side of the hollow,
there where the rim dies away by more than half.
Gold and fine silver, scarlet and white lead,
Indian wood, lustrous and clear,
fresh emerald at the moment it is split,
by the grass and flowers within that hollow
set, each would be surpassed in color,
as by its greater the lesser is surpassed.
Nature had not only painted there,
but from the sweetness of a thousand scents
she made there one unknown and indistinguishable.
Hail, Queen, upon the green and upon the flowers
from there I saw souls sitting and singing,
who, in the valley, could not be seen from outside.
Before what little sun remains now goes to its nest,
began the Mantuan who had turned us aside,
do not wish me to guide you among them.
From this ledge the gestures and faces the better
will you discern of every one of them
than in the hollow below, gathered among them.
He who sits highest, and gives signs
of having neglected what he ought to have done,
and who does not move his mouth to the others' songs,
was Emperor Rudolf, who could have
healed the wounds that have killed Italy,
so that only late is she revived by another.
The other, who by his look comforts him,
ruled the land where the water rises
that the Moldau carries away into the Elbe, and the Elbe into the sea:
Ottokar was his name, and in swaddling clothes
he was far better than Wenceslaus his son
full-bearded, whom lust and idleness feed.
And that little-nosed one, who deep in counsel
seems with him who has so kindly an aspect,
died fleeing and stripping the lily of its bloom:
look there, how he beats his breast.
See the other, who has made for his cheek
of his palm, sighing, a bed.
Father and father-in-law they are of the bane of France:
they know his life, vicious and foul,
and thence comes the grief that so pierces them.
He who seems so strong of limb, and who is in tune
singing with the one of the manly nose,
wore the cord of every virtue girded about him.
And if as king after him had remained
the youth who sits behind him,
well would worth have passed from vessel to vessel;
which cannot be said of the other heirs:
James and Frederick have the kingdoms;
of the better inheritance neither has any part.
Seldom does there rise again through the branches
human goodness: and this is willed
by Him who gives it, that it be sought from Him.
To the large-nosed one too my words apply,
no less than to the other, Peter, who sings with him,
whence Apulia and Provence already grieve.
As much is the plant inferior to its seed
as, more than Beatrice and Margaret,
Constance still boasts of her husband.
See the king of the simple life
sitting there alone, Henry of England:
this one has in his realms better issue.
He who sits lowest on the ground among these,
looking up, is William the marquis,
on whose account Alessandria and its war
make Montferrat and the Canavese weep.
