Just as when he sends forth his first rays
there where his Maker shed his blood,
the Ebro falling beneath lofty Libra,
and the waves in the Ganges scorched by noon,
so stood the sun, whence the day was departing,
when the angel of God appeared to us, glad.
Outside the flame he stood upon the bank,
and sang: Blessed are the pure of heart,
in a voice far more living than ours.
Then: One goes no farther unless first it bites,
holy souls, the fire: enter into it,
and to the singing beyond do not be deaf.
So he said, when we were near him:
whereupon I became such, when I heard him,
as is one who is put into the grave.
Over my clasped hands I stretched upward,
looking at the fire, imagining vividly
human bodies I had seen burning before.
My good guides turned toward me,
and Virgil said to me: My son,
here there can be torment, but not death.
Remember, remember, that if I
upon Geryon himself guided you safe,
what shall I do now, nearer to God?
Believe for certain that if within the belly
of this flame you stayed a full thousand years,
it could not strip you of a single hair.
And if perhaps you believe I deceive you,
draw near it, and have proof given you
with your own hands by the hem of your clothes.
Lay down now, lay down every fear;
turn this way, and come, enter without fear.
And I still motionless, and against my conscience.
When he saw me stand still motionless and unyielding,
a little troubled, he said: Now see, son,
between Beatrice and you is this wall.
As at the name of Thisbe he opened his eyes,
Pyramus, at the point of death, and looked at her,
at the time when the mulberry became vermilion;
so, my hardness made soft,
I turned to my wise guide, hearing the name
that in my mind is always welling up.
At that he shook his head and said: What?
Do we want to stay on this side? Then he smiled,
as one does at a child who is won over by an apple.
Then into the fire he went before me,
asking Statius to come behind,
who earlier for a long way had been between us.
When I was inside, into boiling glass
I would have thrown myself to cool myself,
so beyond measure was the blaze there.
My sweet father, to comfort me,
kept going on speaking of Beatrice alone,
saying: Her eyes I seem already to see.
A voice was guiding us that sang
on the other side; and we, intent on it alone,
came out there where one ascended.
Come, you blessed of my Father,
sounded from within a light that was there,
such that it overcame me, and I could not look.
The sun is departing, it added, and evening comes;
do not stop, but hasten your step,
while the west does not yet grow dark for us.
Straight the way went up through the rock,
in such a direction that I cut off the rays
in front of me of the sun, which was already low.
And of only a few stairs had we made trial
when the sun's setting, by the shadow that went out,
we perceived behind us, I and my sages.
And before in all its immense reaches
the horizon had become of one aspect,
and night had all its dispensations,
each of us made a bed of a step;
for the nature of the mountain broke in us
the strength to climb more than the delight.
As they stand ruminating, tame,
the goats, that were swift and unruly
upon the peaks before they had fed,
silent in the shade while the sun burns,
watched by the shepherd who upon his staff
has leaned, and leaning tends them;
and as the herdsman who lodges in the open
beside his flock quietly spends the night,
watching lest a wild beast scatter it;
such were we all three then,
I like a goat and they like shepherds,
hemmed in on this side and that by the rock.
Little could be seen there of the day outside;
but through that little I saw the stars,
both brighter and larger than was their wont.
Thus ruminating, and thus gazing at them,
sleep took me; sleep that often,
before the event has come to pass, knows the news.
At the hour, I believe, when from the east
Cytherea first shone upon the mountain,
who with the fire of love seems always aflame,
young and beautiful, in a dream I seemed
to see a lady walking through a meadow,
gathering flowers; and singing she said:
Let whoever asks my name know
that I am Leah, and I go moving about
my fair hands to make myself a garland.
Here to please myself in the mirror I adorn myself;
but my sister Rachel never turns away
from her mirror, and sits all day.
She is as eager to see with her fair eyes
as I am to adorn myself with my hands;
her the seeing, and me the adorning, satisfies.
And already, before the splendors that precede the dawn,
which rise the more welcome to pilgrims
the less far off, returning, they lodge,
the shadows were fleeing on every side,
and my sleep with them: whereupon I rose,
seeing the great masters already risen.
That sweet fruit which through so many branches
the care of mortals goes seeking,
today will set your hungers at peace:
Virgil to me these very
words addressed; and never were there gifts
that in the pleasure they gave were equal to those.
Such desire upon desire came to me
to be above, that at every step thereafter
I felt the feathers for my flight grow.
When the stairway all beneath us
had been traversed, and we were on the highest step,
Virgil fixed his eyes on me,
and said: The temporal fire and the eternal
you have seen, son, and have come to a place
where of myself I discern no farther.
I have drawn you here with wit and with art;
your own pleasure henceforth take for guide:
you are out of the steep ways, you are out of the narrow.
See how the sun shines upon your brow;
see the tender grass and the flowers and the little trees,
which that earth produces of itself alone.
Until the glad and beautiful eyes come
which, weeping, made me come to you,
you may sit and you may walk among them.
Look no more for word or sign from me:
free, upright, and sound is your will;
and it would be an error not to do as it wills:
therefore over yourself I crown and mitre you.
