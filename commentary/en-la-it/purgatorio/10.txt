Once we were inside the threshold of the gate
which the soul's evil love leaves unused,
because it makes the crooked way seem straight,
with a clang I heard it close again:
and if I had turned my eyes toward it,
what would have been a worthy excuse for the fault?
We were climbing through a cleft rock,
which bent to one side and the other,
just like the wave that flees and draws near.
Here one must use a little skill,
my guide began, in drawing close,
now here, now there, to the side that recedes.
And this made our steps short,
so much so that the waning moon sooner
reached its bed again to lie back down
than we were out of that needle's eye.
But when we were free and in the open
up where the mountain draws back,
I weary, and both of us uncertain
of our way, we stopped on a level place
more solitary than roads through deserts.
From its edge, where the void borders it,
to the foot of the high bank, which rises still,
a human body would measure it in three lengths;
and as far as my eye could take wing
now on the left and now on the right flank,
this ledge seemed to me just so.
Up there our feet had not yet stirred
when I perceived that the bank all around,
which was less steep behind,
was of white marble, and adorned
with carvings such that not only Polyclitus,
but nature too would there be put to shame.
The angel who came to earth with the decree
of the peace wept for over many years,
which opened heaven after its long ban,
before us appeared so true to life,
carved there in a gentle pose,
that he did not seem an image that keeps silent.
One would have sworn that he was saying: Hail;
for there was portrayed she
who turned the key to open love on high.
She had these words impressed in her bearing:
Behold the handmaid of God; precisely
as a figure is stamped in wax.
Do not keep your mind on just one place,
said the gentle master, who had me
on that side where people have their heart:
whereupon I turned my gaze; and I saw,
behind Mary, on that side
where he who moved me stood in relation to me,
another story set into the rock:
whereupon I passed Virgil and drew near,
so that it might lie open to my eyes.
There was carved there in the marble itself
the cart and the oxen drawing the holy Ark,
on whose account an office not entrusted is feared.
In front appeared people; and all of them,
divided into seven choirs, of two of my senses
made the one say no, the other yes, they sing.
Likewise over the smoke of the incense
that was portrayed there, the eyes and the nose
found themselves at odds over yes and no.
There proceeded the blessed vessel,
dancing with robe raised, the humble Psalmist,
and more and less than king was he on that occasion.
Depicted opposite, at a window
of a great palace, Michal looked on,
like a woman scornful and sad.
I moved my feet from the place where I stood,
to look closely at another story
that gleamed white for me behind Michal.
There was depicted the high glory
of Roman rule, whose worth
moved Gregory to his great victory:
I speak of Trajan the emperor;
and a poor widow was at his bridle,
her bearing full of tears and grief.
Around him it seemed thronged and full
of horsemen, and the eagles on the gold
above them visibly moved in the wind.
The poor woman amid all these
seemed to say: Lord, grant me vengeance
for my son who is dead, for whom my heart grieves.
And he to answer her: Now wait for me
until I return. And she: O my lord,
like one in whom grief presses on,
what if you do not return? And he: Whoever takes my place
will grant it to you. And she: Another's good deed,
what will it be to you, if you consign yours to oblivion?
Whereupon he: Now take comfort, for it is fitting
that I discharge my duty before I stir:
justice wills it, and pity holds me back.
He who never saw a new thing
produced this visible speech,
new to us, because here it is not found.
While I delighted in looking at
the images of so many acts of humility,
dear to behold also for their maker;
Look, over here, though they take infrequent steps,
the Poet murmured, many people:
these will direct us to the next steps.
My eyes, which were content to gaze,
to see new things, of which they are fond,
were not slow in turning toward him.
Yet I do not wish, reader, that you should waver
in your good resolve, on hearing
how God wills that the debt be paid.
Do not heed the form of the torment:
think of what follows; think that, at worst,
beyond the great sentence it cannot go.
I began: Master, what I see
moving toward us do not seem to me to be people,
and I know not what they are, so bewildered am I in seeing.
And he to me: The heavy condition
of their torment hunches them down to the ground
so that my eyes at first were in doubt about them.
But look steadily there, and disentangle
with your sight what comes beneath those stones:
already you can discern how each beats himself.
O proud Christians, weary wretches,
who, infirm in the sight of the mind,
put your trust in backward steps;
do you not perceive that we are worms
born to form the angelic butterfly
that flies to justice without defenses?
What makes your spirit float on high?
You are as it were insects not fully formed,
like a worm in which formation fails.
As, to support a floor or roof,
in place of a corbel sometimes a figure
is seen to draw its knees to its chest,
which makes, from what is not real, real distress
arise in whoever sees them; thus shaped
I saw those, when I paid close heed.
It is true that they were more or less bent,
according as they had more or less upon their backs.
And he who showed most patience in his bearing,
weeping, seemed to say: I can bear no more.
