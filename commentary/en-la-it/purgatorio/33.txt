O God, the nations have come, alternating,
Now three, now four, a sweet psalmody
The ladies began, and weeping:
And Beatrice, sighing and devout,
Listened to them in such a state that little
More was Mary changed at the cross.
But after the other virgins gave place
To her to speak, risen upright on her feet,
She answered, colored like fire:
A little while, and now you shall not see me;
And again, my beloved sisters,
A little while, and now you shall see me.
Then she set them before her, all seven,
And after her, merely beckoning, set in motion
Me and the lady and the sage who remained.
Thus she went on, and I do not believe
Her tenth step had been set on the ground
When with her eyes she struck my eyes;
And as I faced that gaze: Come faster,
She told me, so much so that if I speak with you,
To listen to me you may be well placed.
As soon as I was, as I ought to be, beside her,
She said to me: Brother, why do you not venture
To ask, now that you are coming with me?
As with those who are too reverent
When speaking before their betters,
So that they bring no living voice to their teeth,
It happened to me that without full sound
I began: My lady, my need
You know, and what is good for me.
And she to me: From fear and from shame
I wish you now to disentangle yourself,
So that you speak no more like a man who dreams.
Know that the vessel that the serpent broke
Was, and is not; but let whoever bears the blame believe
That God's vengeance does not fear sops.
Not for all time will it be without an heir,
The eagle that left its feathers on the chariot,
Whereby it becomes a monster and then prey;
For I see with certainty, and therefore tell it,
A time drawing on, stars already near,
Safe from every hindrance and every bar;
In which a five hundred, ten and five,
Sent by God, will slay the she-thief,
Together with that giant who sins with her.
And perhaps my dark account,
Like Themis and the Sphinx, persuades you less
Because in their manner it clouds the intellect;
But soon there shall be the Fates and the Naiads,
Who will solve this hard enigma,
Without harm to sheep or crops.
Take note; and just as they are delivered by me,
So teach these words to those who live
The life that is a running toward death;
And bear in mind, when you write them,
Not to conceal how you have seen the tree,
Which has now twice been robbed here.
Whoever robs it, or breaks it,
With blasphemy of deed offends God,
Who for his use alone created it holy.
For biting it, in pain and in longing,
Five thousand years and more the first soul
Yearned for Him who in himself punished the bite.
Your understanding sleeps if it does not judge
That for a singular cause it is exalted
So high, and so inverted at its crown.
And had they not been water of the Elsa,
The vain thoughts round about your mind,
And the pleasure of them a Pyramus to the mulberry,
By so many circumstances alone
The justice of God in the prohibition
You would recognize, and the tree in its moral sense.
But because I see you in your intellect
Turned to stone and, once petrified, stained,
So that the light of my saying dazzles you,
I wish even so, if not written, at least painted,
That you carry it away within you, for the reason
That one carries a staff girdled with palm.
And I: Like wax beneath a seal,
Which does not alter the figure impressed on it,
My brain is now marked by you.
But why so far above my sight
Does your longed-for speech fly
That the more my sight strives, the more it loses it?
So that you may know, she said, that school
Which you have followed, and may see its teaching,
How far it can follow my word;
And may see your way from the divine
Stand as far apart as the distance
From earth to the heaven that hastens highest.
Whereupon I answered her: I do not remember
That I ever estranged myself from you,
Nor have I a conscience that reproaches me for it.
And if you cannot remember it,
She answered, smiling, now recall
How you drank of Lethe today;
And if from smoke fire is inferred,
That forgetfulness clearly proves
Guilt in your will, intent elsewhere.
Yet from now on they shall be naked,
My words, as far as it shall be fitting
To uncover them to your untrained sight.
And more brilliant, and with slower steps,
The sun held the circle of noon,
Which forms here and there, according to the viewpoint,
When they halted, just as one halts
Who goes ahead of people as a guide
If he finds something new in his path.
The seven ladies, at the edge of a dim shade,
Such as beneath green leaves and dark branches
The Alps cast over their cold streams.
Before them the Euphrates and the Tigris
I seemed to see issue from one spring
And, like friends, part reluctantly.
O light, O glory of the human race,
What water is this that here unfolds
From one source, and draws itself away from itself?
To such a prayer it was said to me: Ask
Matelda to tell you. And here answered,
As one does who clears herself of blame,
The fair lady: This and other things
Have been told him by me, and I am sure
That the river Lethe did not hide them from him.
And Beatrice: Perhaps a greater care,
Which often robs one of memory,
Has made his mind dark of sight.
But see Eunoe, how it flows there:
Lead him to it, and, as you are accustomed,
Revive his power that has fallen into a swoon.
Like a noble soul that offers no excuse
But makes another's will her own
As soon as by a sign it is disclosed;
So, after I had been taken by her,
The fair lady set out, and to Statius
In ladylike fashion said: Come with him.
If I had, reader, more space
To write, I would yet sing in part
The sweet drinking that never would have sated me:
But because all the pages are full
That were laid out for this second canticle,
The bridle of this art lets me go no further.
I returned from the most holy water
Remade, just like young plants
Renewed with new leaves,
Pure and disposed to rise to the stars.
