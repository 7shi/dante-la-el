When the game of zara breaks up,
he who loses stays behind grieving,
repeating the throws, and sadly learns:
with the other all the people go away;
one from in front and one from behind takes hold of him,
and one at his side brings himself to his mind;
he does not stop, and listens to this one and that;
he to whom he extends his hand draws no nearer;
and thus he defends himself from the press.
Such was I in that dense throng,
turning my face to them here and there,
and by promising I freed myself from it.
There was the Aretine who from the arms
so fierce of Ghin di Tacco met his death,
and the other who drowned fleeing in the chase.
There prayed with hands outstretched
Federico Novello, and he of Pisa
who made good Marzucco show himself strong.
I saw Count Orso, and the soul parted
from its body through spite and through envy,
as it said, not for a fault committed;
Pier de la Broccia I mean: and here let her take heed,
while she is on this side, the lady of Brabant,
lest for that she be of a worse flock.
As soon as I was free of all
those shades, who begged only for others' prayers,
that their becoming holy might be hastened,
I began: It seems that you deny to me,
O my light, expressly in a certain text,
that prayer bends a decree of heaven;
and these people pray for this very thing.
Would their hope then be vain?
Or is your saying not clear to me?
And he to me: My writing is plain,
and the hope of these does not fail,
if one looks well with a sound mind.
For the height of judgment is not lowered
because the fire of love fulfills in an instant
what he who tarries here must pay in full:
and there where I established that point,
no defect was made good by praying,
because the prayer was severed from God.
Yet on so high a doubt
do not settle, unless she tells you,
who will be a light between the truth and the intellect.
I do not know if you understand; I speak of Beatrice:
you will see her above, upon the summit
of this mountain, smiling and happy.
And I to my guide: Let us go with greater haste;
for I no longer tire as I did before;
and see already how the hill casts its shadow.
We shall go forward with this daylight,
he answered, as far as we now can;
but the matter is otherwise than you suppose.
Before you are up there, you will see return
him who is already hiding behind the slope,
so that you do not break his rays.
But see there a soul who, stationed
all alone, looks toward us;
that soul will teach us the quickest way.
We came to it: O Lombard soul,
how proud and disdainful you stood,
and in the movement of your eyes how dignified and slow!
It said nothing to us;
but let us go on, only looking
in the manner of a lion when it rests.
Still Virgil drew near to it, asking
that it show us the best ascent;
and it did not answer his question:
but about our country and our life
it asked us. And the gentle guide began:
Mantua... And the shade, all withdrawn into itself,
rose toward him from the place where it had stood before,
saying: O Mantuan, I am Sordello
of your own land. And each embraced the other.
Ah, enslaved Italy, and lodging of sorrow,
ship without a helmsman in a great tempest,
not mistress of provinces, but a brothel!
That noble soul was so quick,
just for the sweet sound of its native land,
to welcome its fellow citizen there;
and now within you they do not dwell without war,
your living, and each gnaws at the other
of those whom one wall and one moat enclose.
Search, wretched one, all around your shores,
along your coasts, and then look into your bosom,
to see if any part within you enjoys peace.
What use is it that your bridle was mended
by Justinian, if the saddle is empty?
Without it the shame would be less.
Ah, people, who ought to be devout,
and let Caesar sit in the saddle,
if you rightly understand what God points out to you!
Look how vicious this beast has grown,
for lack of correction by the spurs,
since you laid hand upon the bridle.
O Albert the German, who abandon
her who has grown untamed and wild,
you who ought to bestride her saddlebows,
may just judgment fall from the stars
upon your blood, and be unheard-of and manifest,
so that your successor may take fright at it:
for you and your father have suffered,
held fast by greed over there,
the garden of the Empire to be laid waste.
Come see the Montecchi and Cappelletti,
the Monaldi and Filippeschi, heedless man:
the former already grieving, and the latter full of fears.
Come, cruel one, come, and see the distress
of your nobles, and tend their wounds,
and you will see how Santa Fior is tended.
Come see your Rome, who weeps,
widowed, alone, and day and night calls:
My Caesar, why do you not keep me company?
Come see the people, how they love one another;
and if no pity for us moves you,
come to be ashamed of your renown.
And if I may, O highest Jove,
who were on earth for us crucified,
are your just eyes turned elsewhere?
Or is it a preparation that in the abyss
of your counsel you make for some good
wholly cut off from our perception?
For the cities of Italy all full
are of tyrants, and a Marcellus becomes
every peasant who comes taking sides.
My Florence, well may you be content
with this digression which does not touch you,
thanks to your people who take such pains.
Many have justice in their hearts, but it is slow to fly,
lest it come without counsel to the bow;
but your people have it on the tip of their tongues.
Many refuse the public burden;
but your people promptly answer
without being called, and say: I shoulder it.
Now rejoice, for you have good reason:
you rich, you at peace, you wise.
If I speak the truth, the outcome does not hide it.
Athens and Lacedaemon, which made
the ancient laws, and were so civilized,
gave but a slight hint of living well
compared with you, who make such fine-spun
provisions that to mid-November
what you spin in October does not reach.
How many times within the time you remember,
laws, and offices, and coinage, and custom
have you changed, and renewed your members!
And if you remember well, and see clearly,
you will see yourself like that sick woman
who can find no rest upon the feathers,
but by turning over wards off her pain.
