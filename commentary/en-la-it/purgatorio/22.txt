Already the angel had remained behind us,
The angel who had turned us toward the sixth circle,
Having shaved a stroke from my face:
And those who have their desire set on justice
He had called blessed to us, and his words
With I thirst, without more, ended it.
And I, lighter than through other passes,
Went along so that without any labor
I followed upward the swift spirits:
When Virgil began: Love,
Kindled by virtue, always kindled another,
Provided that its flame showed outwardly.
Thus, from the hour when there descended among us
Into the limbo of Hell Juvenal,
Who made your affection manifest to me,
My goodwill toward you was such as
Ever bound one most closely to a person unseen,
So that now these stairs will seem short to me.
But tell me, and as a friend forgive me,
If too much confidence loosens my rein,
The pure faith in all excuses me:
How could there find within your breast
A place for avarice, amid such wisdom
As, through your care, you were full of?
These words moved Statius
A little to laughter at first; then he answered:
Every word of yours is to me a dear sign of love.
Truly, often things appear
That give false grounds for supposition,
Because the true causes are hidden.
Your question confirms to me your belief
To be that I was avaricious in your lifetime,
Perhaps because of that circle where I was:
Now know that avarice was removed
Too far from me; this excess
Thousands of moons have punished.
And had it not been that I set my care aright,
When I understood the passage where you cry out,
As if angered at human nature:
To what do you not bring, O accursed hunger
For gold, the appetite of mortals?
Rolling, I would feel the dismal jousts.
Then I perceived that too wide to open their wings
Could hands in spending, and I repented
As much of that as of the other evils.
How many will rise again with their hair shorn,
Through ignorance, which for this fault
Draws thought, in life, to the extremes!
And know that the fault that counters
By direct opposition any sin
Committed, together with it here dries up its sight.
Therefore, if I have been among those people
Who weep for avarice, to purge myself,
It befell me for its contrary.
Now when you sang of the cruel arms
Of the twofold sorrow of Jocasta,
Said the singer of bucolic songs,
From what Clio there plays with you,
It does not seem that you had yet been made faithful by
The faith without which doing good does not suffice.
If that is so, what sun and what candles
So dispelled your darkness that you set
Thereafter behind the Fisherman your sails?
And he to him: You first sent me
Toward Parnassus to drink in its caves,
And first, toward God, you gave me light.
You did as one who walks by night,
Who carries the light behind him and does himself no good,
But makes the people after him wise,
When you said: The age is renewed;
Justice returns, and the first age of man;
And a new progeny descends from heaven.
Through you I was a poet, through you a Christian;
But that you may see well what I sketch,
To color it I shall stretch out my hand.
Already the whole world was pregnant
With the true belief, sown
By the messengers of the eternal kingdom;
And your word touched on above
Was in such accord with the new preachers
That I took to visiting them often.
They came then to seem to me so holy
That, when Domitian persecuted them,
Their tears were not without my weeping.
And for as long as I remained over there,
I aided them, and their upright ways
Made me despise all the other sects:
And before I led the Greeks to the rivers
Of Thebes in verse, I received baptism:
But out of fear I was a Christian in secret,
Long making a show of paganism:
And this lukewarmness the fourth circle
Made me circle for more than four hundred years.
You then who have lifted from me the covering
That hid from me so great a good as I speak of,
While we have some climbing left,
Tell me where Terence our friend is,
Caecilius, Plautus, Varro, if you know,
Tell me if they are damned, and in what quarter.
These, and Persius, and I, and many others,
Answered my guide, are, with that Greek
Whom the Muses suckled more than any other ever,
In the first circle of the blind prison.
Often we speak of the mountain
That has our nurses always with it.
Euripides is there with us, and Antiphon,
Simonides, Agathon, and many other
Greeks who once adorned their brows with laurel.
There are seen, among your people,
Antigone, Deiphyle and Argia,
And Ismene, as sorrowful as she was.
There is seen she who showed Langia;
There is the daughter of Tiresias, and Thetis,
And with her sisters Deidamia.
Both poets were silent now,
Attentive anew to looking about,
Free of the climb and of the walls;
And already the four handmaids of the day had
Remained behind, and to the fifth fell the chariot pole,
Directing ever upward the burning horn;
When my guide: I believe that toward the edge
We must turn our right shoulders,
Circling the mountain as we are accustomed to do.
Thus custom was our banner there,
And we took the way with less misgiving
Through the assent of that worthy soul.
They went ahead, and I all alone
Behind, and I listened to their discourses,
Which gave me understanding for making verse.
But soon the sweet discourse was broken by
A tree that we found in the middle of the way,
With fruits sweet and good to smell.
And as a fir tapers upward
From branch to branch, so that one downward,
I believe so that no one may climb up.
On the side on which our path was shut in,
There fell from the other rock a clear liquid,
And it spread over the leaves above.
The two poets drew near the tree;
And a voice from within the foliage
Cried: Of this food you shall have want.
Then it said: More thought did Mary give to how
The wedding might be honorable and complete,
Than to her own mouth, which answers for you.
And the Roman women of old, for their drink,
Were content with water; and Daniel
Disdained food, and gained knowledge.
The first age was as beautiful as gold;
It made acorns savory with hunger,
And with thirst every brook nectar.
Honey and locusts were the foods
That nourished the Baptist in the desert;
Wherefore he is glorious, and as great
As is revealed to us through the Gospel.
