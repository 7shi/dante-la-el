As much as, between the ending of the third hour
And the beginning of the day, appears of the sphere
That always plays in the manner of a child,
So much already, toward evening, seemed
To be left to the sun of its course;
It was vespers there, and here midnight.
And the rays struck us full on the nose,
Because by us the mountain had been so rounded
That straight now we were going toward the sunset;
When I felt my brow grow heavy
At the splendor far more than before,
And things unknown were a wonder to me:
Whereupon I raised my hands toward the tops
Of my brows, and made myself the shade
That pares away the excess of what is seen.
As when from water or from the mirror
The ray leaps to the opposite side,
Rising upward in a manner equal
To that of the ray that descends, and departs as far
From the fall of the stone over an equal stretch,
As experience and art show;
So I seemed by reflected light
There before me to be struck,
Wherefore my sight was swift to flee.
What is that, sweet Father, against which I cannot
Shield my sight enough for it to avail me,
Said I, and which seems to be moving toward us?
Do not marvel if still it dazzles you,
The household of heaven, he answered me:
It is a messenger who comes to invite one to ascend.
Soon it will be that to see these things
Will not be hard for you, but will give you delight
As great as nature fitted you to feel.
When we had come to the blessed angel,
With glad voice he said: Enter by this way
Upon a stairway far less steep than the others.
We were climbing, having now departed thence,
And Blessed are the merciful was
Sung behind us, and Rejoice, you who conquer.
My master and I, alone, the two of us,
Were going upward, and I thought, as I went,
To gain profit from his words;
And I turned to him, asking thus:
What did the spirit of Romagna mean,
Mentioning both exclusion and sharer?
Whereupon he to me: Of his greatest fault
He knows the harm; and therefore let none marvel
If he rebukes it, that there may be less weeping for it.
Because your desires have set their sights
Where by sharing a portion is diminished,
Envy works the bellows for your sighs.
But if love of the highest sphere
Were to turn your desire upward,
That fear would not be in your breast;
For the more that ours is said there,
The more of good each one possesses,
And the more of charity burns in that cloister.
I am hungrier for satisfaction,
Said I, than if I had kept silent before,
And more doubt do I gather in my mind.
How can it be that a good distributed
Makes the greater number of possessors richer,
Said I, than if it is held by few?
And he to me: Because you keep fixing
Your mind on earthly things alone,
From true light you pluck darkness.
That infinite and ineffable good
Which is on high runs to love just
As to a shining body a ray comes.
It gives itself in such measure as it finds ardor:
So that however far charity extends,
The eternal worth increases upon it.
And the more people there are on high who love,
The more there is to love well, and the more love there is,
And like a mirror each reflects the other.
And if my reasoning does not sate your hunger,
You will see Beatrice, and she fully
Will take from you this and every other longing.
Only strive that soon may be effaced,
As the two already are, the five wounds
That close again through being sorrowful.
Just as I wished to say: You satisfy me,
So I saw myself arrived upon the next terrace,
So that my eager eyes made me fall silent.
Here I seemed into a vision
Of ecstasy suddenly to be drawn,
And to see in a temple several people;
And a woman at the entrance, with the bearing
Sweet of a mother, saying: My son,
Why have you done thus to us?
Behold, sorrowing, your father and I
Were seeking you. And as here she fell silent,
That which appeared before disappeared.
Then another woman appeared to me, with those waters
Down her cheeks which grief distills
When from great scorn for another it has sprung,
And saying: If you are lord of the city
Over whose name there was such strife among gods,
And whence all knowledge sends forth sparks,
Avenge yourself upon those daring arms
That embraced our daughter, O Pisistratus.
And the lord seemed to me, kindly and mild,
To answer her with countenance composed:
What shall we do to one who wishes us ill,
If he who loves us is by us condemned?
Then I saw people kindled in the fire of wrath,
Killing a young man with stones, loudly
Crying always to one another: Martyr him, martyr him.
And I saw him bow beneath the death
That already weighed him down toward earth,
But of his eyes he still made gates to heaven;
Praying the high Lord amid such strife
That he forgive his persecutors,
With that look which unlocks pity.
When my soul turned outward again
To the things that are real outside it,
I recognized my errors that were not false.
My guide, who could see me
Act like a man who frees himself from sleep,
Said: What ails you, that you cannot steady yourself,
But have come more than half a league,
Veiling your eyes and with your legs entangled,
In the manner of one whom wine and sleep bend down?
O sweet father of mine, if you will hear me,
I will tell you, said I, what appeared to me
When my legs were so taken from me.
And he: If you had a hundred masks
Upon your face, they would not be hidden from me,
Your thoughts, however small.
What you saw was so that you should not excuse yourself
From opening your heart to the waters of peace
That from the eternal fountain are poured forth.
I did not ask, What ails you? for the reason he does
Who looks only with the eye that does not see
When the body lies bereft of soul;
But I asked to give strength to your foot:
Thus must one spur the sluggish and the slow
To use their wakefulness when it returns.
We were going through the evening, intent
Ahead, as far as our eyes could reach,
Against the late and shining rays.
And behold, little by little, a smoke drawing
Toward us, dark as the night,
Nor was there any place to turn aside from it:
This took from us our sight and the pure air.
