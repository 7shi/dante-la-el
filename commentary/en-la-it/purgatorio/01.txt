To course over better waters spreads its sails
now the little bark of my wit,
which leaves behind it a sea so cruel.
And I shall sing of that second realm
where the human spirit purges itself
and of ascending to heaven becomes worthy.
But here let dead poetry rise again,
O holy Muses, since I am yours,
and here let Calliope rise a little,
accompanying my song with that sound
of which the wretched Magpies felt
the blow so keenly that they despaired of pardon.
The sweet color of oriental sapphire,
which gathered in the clear aspect
of the pure air as far as the first circle,
to my eyes renewed delight,
as soon as I came out of the dead air
that had saddened my eyes and breast.
The fair planet that encourages love
made all the East smile,
veiling the Fishes that were in its train.
I turned to the right hand and gave heed
to the other pole, and saw four stars
never seen save by the first people.
Heaven seemed to rejoice in their little flames.
O northern, widowed region,
since you are deprived of seeing them!
When I had parted from the sight of them,
turning a little toward the other pole,
there whence the Wain had already vanished,
I saw near me an old man alone,
worthy in appearance of so much reverence
that no son owes more to a father.
Long was the beard, and mingled with white hairs,
that he wore, like the hair of his head,
of which a double strand fell to his breast.
The rays of the four holy lights
so adorned his face with light
that I saw him as if the sun were before him.
Who are you who, against the blind river's current,
have fled the eternal prison?
he said, moving those venerable plumes.
Who has guided you? Or what was your lamp
as you came out of the deep night
that makes the infernal valley ever black?
Are the laws of the abyss so broken?
Or has counsel in heaven changed to something new,
that, damned, you come to my rocks?
My guide then took hold of me,
and with words and with hands and with signs
made my knees and brow reverent.
Then he answered him: Of myself I did not come.
A lady descended from heaven, at whose prayers
I aided this man with my company.
But since it is your will that more be unfolded
of our condition as it truly is,
it cannot be that my will should be denied you.
This man has never seen his last evening,
but through his folly he was so near it
that very little time remained to run.
Just as I said, I was sent to him
to save his life, and there was no other way
than this on which I have set out.
I have shown him all the wicked people;
and now I intend to show him the spirits
who purge themselves under your sway.
How I have brought him would be long to tell you:
from on high descends a power that helps me
lead him to see you and to hear you.
Now may it please you to welcome his coming:
liberty he goes seeking, which is so dear,
as one knows who for it renounces life.
You know, for for its sake no bitter thing to you
was death in Utica, where you left
the garment that on the great day will be so bright.
The eternal edicts are not broken by us:
for this man lives, and Minos does not bind me;
but I am of the circle where are the chaste eyes
of your Marcia, who in her look still begs you,
O holy breast, to hold her as your own:
for love of her, then, incline toward us.
Let us go through your seven realms:
thanks for you I shall carry back to her,
if you deign to be mentioned down there.
Marcia so pleased my eyes,
while I was on the other side, he said then,
that whatever favors she wished of me, I granted.
Now that beyond the evil river I dwell,
she can move me no more, by that law
which was made when I came out from there.
But if a lady of heaven moves and governs you
as you say, there is no need of flatteries here:
it is quite enough that you ask me on her behalf.
Go then, and see that you gird this man anew
with a plain rush, and that you wash his face,
so that you wipe all grime from it:
for it would not be fitting for an eye overcast
with any mist to go before the first
minister who is of those of Paradise.
This little island, all around its lowest edge,
down there where the wave beats it,
bears rushes upon the soft mud.
No other plant that put forth leaf
or hardened could have life there,
because it does not yield to the blows.
Afterward let your return not be this way;
the sun will show you, which is rising now,
how to take the mountain at an easier ascent.
Thus he vanished; and I rose up
without speaking, and drew quite back
to my guide, and turned my eyes to him.
He began: Follow my steps:
let us turn back, for on this side slopes
this plain to its lower bounds.
The dawn was overcoming the morning hour
that shone before it, so that from afar
I discerned the trembling of the sea.
We went along the solitary plain
like one who returns to the road he has lost,
who until he reaches it seems to himself to go in vain.
When we were there where the dew
struggles with the sun and, being in a place
where there is shade, thins but little,
both hands, outspread upon the tender grass,
gently my master laid;
whereupon I, perceiving his intent,
held out toward him my tearful cheeks;
there he laid quite bare in me
that color which Hell had hidden from me.
We came then upon the deserted shore
that never saw sail upon its waters
a man who afterward knew what it was to return.
There he girded me just as another had pleased:
O marvel! For such as he chose
the humble plant, such did it spring up again
suddenly there whence he plucked it.
