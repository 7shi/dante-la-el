Remember, reader, if ever in the mountains
fog caught you, through which you saw
no otherwise than moles through skin,
how, when the vapors, damp and thick,
begin to thin, the sphere
of the sun feebly enters through them;
and your imagination will be nimble
in coming to see how I saw again
the sun at first, which was already setting.
Thus, matching my steps with the trusty steps
of my master, I came out of such a cloud
into the rays already dead on the low shores.
O imagination, that steals us
sometimes so far from what is outside that one takes no notice,
though a thousand trumpets sound around one,
who moves you, if the senses offer you nothing?
Does a light move you that takes form in heaven
of itself, or by a will that guides it down?
Of the wrath of her who changed form
into the bird that most delights in singing,
in my imagination appeared the trace;
and there my mind was so closely drawn
within itself that from outside there came
nothing that was then received by it.
Then there rained down into my deep imagination
a crucified man, scornful and fierce
in his look, and so he was dying.
Around him was the great Ahasuerus,
Esther his wife and the just Mordecai,
who was in deed and in word so upright.
And as this image burst
of its own accord, like a bubble
beneath which the water fails, and so it came to nothing,
there arose in my vision a maiden,
weeping loudly, and she said: O queen,
why out of wrath have you willed to be nothing?
You have killed yourself so as not to lose Lavinia;
now you have lost me; I am she who mourns,
mother, at your ruin before another's.
As sleep is broken when suddenly
a new light strikes the closed eyes,
so that, broken, it quivers before it dies quite away,
so my imagining fell away
as soon as the light struck my face,
far greater than that to which we are accustomed.
I was turning to see where I was,
when a voice said: Here one ascends;
which drew me away from every other concern
and made my desire so eager
to look on who it was that spoke
that it never rests unless it comes face to face.
But as before the sun, which weighs upon our sight
and by excess veils its own shape,
so the power of sight failed there.
This is an upright spirit, who on the
way to ascend directs us without asking
and with his light hides his very self.
He does with us as a man does with himself;
for whoever waits to be asked, and sees the need,
is already maliciously setting himself to refuse.
Now let us suit our step to so great an invitation:
let us strive to ascend before it grows dark,
for afterward it would not be possible unless day returned.
Thus spoke my guide, and I with him
turned our steps toward a stairway;
and as soon as I was on the first step,
I felt close by me as it were a movement of a wing,
and a fanning in my face, and heard: Blessed
are the peacemakers, who are without evil wrath.
Already so high above us had risen
the last rays that night follows
that the stars were appearing on several sides.
O strength of mine, why do you so melt away?
I was saying to myself, for I felt
the power of my legs brought to a truce.
We were where no farther did the stairway
rise upward, and we were fixed in place,
just like a ship that comes to shore;
and I waited a little to hear whether I might catch
anything in the new circle;
then I turned to my master and said:
My sweet father, say, what offense
is purged here in the circle where we are?
If our feet stand still, let not your speech stand still.
And he to me: Love of the good, falling short
of what it owes, is restored right here;
here the ill-delayed oar strikes again.
But that you may understand yet more clearly,
turn your mind to me, and you will gather
some good fruit from our delay.
Neither creator nor creature ever,
he began, son, was without love,
either natural or of the mind; you know it.
The natural is always without error;
but the other can err: through an evil object,
or through too much or too little vigor.
While it is directed toward the first good
and in the second keeps its measure,
it cannot be the cause of evil delight;
but when it turns to evil, or with more zeal,
or with less than it ought, runs toward the good,
against its maker works his creature.
From this you can understand that it must be
love that is the seed in you of every virtue
and of every deed that merits punishment.
And because never can love turn from the welfare
of its own subject to look away,
from hatred of self all things are safe;
and because one cannot conceive as divided
and standing by itself any being apart from the first,
from hating that first being every affection is cut off.
It remains, if in distinguishing I judge rightly,
that the evil one loves is in one's neighbor, and this
love is born in three ways in your clay.
There is one who, through his neighbor's being brought low,
hopes for preeminence, and for this alone longs
that he be cast down from his greatness;
there is one who power, favor, honor, and fame
fears to lose because another rises,
at which he grieves because he loves the contrary;
and there is one who at an injury seems to take offense
so deeply that he grows greedy for revenge;
and such a one must needs stamp evil upon another.
This threefold love down here below
is wept for; now I want you to understand the other,
which runs toward the good in a disordered way.
Each dimly apprehends a good
in which the soul may rest, and desires it;
therefore each strives to reach it.
If sluggish love draws you to see it,
or to attain it, this terrace,
after due repentance, torments you for it.
Another good, which does not make man happy,
is not happiness, is not the good
essence, of every good the fruit and root.
The love that abandons itself too much to it
above us is wept for on three circles;
but how it is reckoned threefold,
I leave unsaid, so that you may seek it out for yourself.
