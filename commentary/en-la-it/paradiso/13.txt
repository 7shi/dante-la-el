Let him imagine, who desires to understand well
what I now saw, and let him hold the image,
while I speak, like a steadfast rock:
fifteen stars that in diverse regions
enliven the sky with such radiance
that it overcomes every thickness of the air;
let him imagine that Wain for which the bosom
of our sky suffices both night and day,
so that at the turning of the pole it does not vanish;
let him imagine the mouth of that horn
which begins at the tip of the axis
around which the first wheel turns,
to have made of themselves two signs in heaven,
such as the daughter of Minos made
when she felt the chill of death;
and the one to have its rays toward the other,
and both to turn in such a manner
that the one went forward and the other backward:
and he will have almost the shadow of the true
constellation and double dance
that circled the point where I was;
since it is as far beyond our experience
as beyond the motion of the Chiana
moves the heaven that outstrips all the others.
There they sang not Bacchus, not Paean,
but three persons in divine nature,
and in one person that nature and the human.
The singing and the turning fulfilled their measure,
and those holy lights gave heed to us,
finding their bliss from care to care.
The silence among the concordant divinities was broken
then by the light in which the wondrous life
of God's Little Poor Man was recounted to me,
and it said: When the one straw is threshed,
when its seed is already stored away,
to beat the other sweet love invites me.
You believe that into the breast from which the rib
was drawn to form the fair cheek
whose palate costs the whole world dear,
and into that which, pierced by the lance,
both afterward and beforehand made such amends
that it outweighs every fault in the balance,
as much as human nature is permitted
to have of light was all infused
by that power which made the one and the other:
and therefore you marvel at what I said above,
when I recounted that no second had
the good that is enclosed in the fifth light.
Now open your eyes to what I answer you,
and you will see your belief and my words
meet in the truth, like the center in a circle.
That which does not die and that which can die
are nothing but the splendor of that Idea
which our Lord, loving, brings forth;
for that true light which streams
from its shining source, which is not parted
from it, nor from the love that makes a third in them,
of its goodness gathers its radiance,
as if mirrored, in nine subsistences,
eternally remaining one.
Thence it descends to the last potentialities,
down from act to act, so diminishing
that it makes no more than brief contingencies.
And by these contingencies I mean
the generated things that are produced,
with seed and without seed, by heaven in motion.
The wax of these, and that which shapes it,
do not remain the same, and so beneath the stamp
of the Idea it shines through now more, now less:
whence it comes about that one and the same tree,
as to species, bears fruit both better and worse;
and you are born with differing gifts.
If the wax were prepared to perfection,
and heaven were at its highest power,
the light of the seal would appear entire.
But nature always renders it imperfect,
working in like manner to the artist
who has the skill of the art but a hand that trembles.
Thus if fervent Love the clear vision
of the primal Power disposes and imprints,
all perfection is attained there.
Thus was the earth once made worthy
of all the perfection of living beings;
thus was the Virgin made pregnant.
So I commend your opinion
that human nature never was,
nor will be, such as it was in those two persons.
Now, if I proceeded no further,
How then was this man without peer?
your words would begin.
But, that what does not appear may appear clearly,
think who he was and of the cause that moved him,
when he was told, Ask, to make his request.
I have not spoken so that you cannot
see well that he was a king who asked for wisdom
in order that he might be an able king;
not to know the number in which exist
the movers up here, or whether a necessary premise
with a contingent one ever yielded a necessary conclusion;
not whether one may grant a first motion to exist,
or whether in a semicircle one can make
a triangle such that it would not have a right angle.
Hence, if you note what I said, and this,
royal prudence is that peerless vision
at which the arrow of my intent strikes.
And if to arose you turn your clear eyes,
you will see that it has regard only
to kings, who are many, and the good ones are rare.
With this distinction take my saying,
and so it can stand with what you believe
of the first father and of our Beloved.
And let this always be lead at your feet,
to make you move slowly, like a weary man,
toward both yes and no in what you do not see;
for low indeed among fools is he
who without distinction affirms or denies,
in the one case just as in the other;
for it happens that many times inclines
to the false side an opinion formed in haste,
and then affection binds the intellect.
Far worse than in vain does he leave the shore,
because he does not return such as he sets out,
who fishes for the truth and does not know the art.
And of this there are in the world manifest proofs:
Parmenides, Melissus, Bryson, and many
who went on and did not know where.
So did Sabellius and Arius, and those fools
who were like swords to the Scriptures
in making crooked the straight faces.
Nor let people be too sure, either,
in judging, like one who reckons
the crops in the field before they are ripe:
for I have seen, all winter long at first,
the thorn show itself stiff and fierce,
then bear the rose upon its tip;
and a ship I once saw, straight and swift,
run the sea through all its course,
to perish at last at the entrance to the harbor.
Let not Lady Berta and Master Martin believe,
from seeing one steal, the other make an offering,
that they see them within the divine counsel;
for the former may rise, the other may fall.
