O fellowship chosen for the great supper
of the blessed Lamb, who feeds you
so that your desire is always full;
if by the grace of God this man foretastes
something of what falls from your table,
before death prescribes a time for him,
give heed to his immense affection,
and sprinkle him a little: you drink
always at the fountain whence comes what he thinks.
Thus Beatrice. And those happy souls
made themselves spheres upon fixed poles,
flaming brightly like comets.
And as wheels in the mechanism of clocks
turn so that the first, to one who pays heed,
seems still, and the last seems to fly;
so those round dances, in differing
ways dancing, of their riches
gave me the measure, swift and slow.
From the one in which I noted the most beauty
I saw come forth a fire so happy
that it left none there of greater brightness;
and three times around Beatrice
it turned with a song so divine
that my imagination cannot retell it to me;
therefore my pen skips it, and I do not write it,
for our imagining, for folds such as these,
to say nothing of speech, is too vivid a color.
O holy sister of mine, who so entreat us,
devout as you are, by your ardent affection
you loose me from that fair sphere.
Having then come to rest, the blessed fire
toward my lady directed its breath,
which spoke just as I have said.
And she: O eternal light of the great man
to whom our Lord left the keys
that he brought down, of this wondrous joy,
test this man on points light and weighty,
as you please, concerning the faith
by which you walked upon the sea.
Whether he loves well, hopes well and believes,
is not hidden from you, for you have your sight there
where everything is seen depicted.
But because this kingdom has made citizens
through the true faith, to glorify it,
it is good that it should fall to him to speak of it.
And as the bachelor arms himself and does not speak
until the master puts the question,
to prove it and not to settle it;
so I armed myself with every argument,
while she spoke, to be ready
for such a questioner and such a profession.
Say, good Christian, make plain to us
what faith is. At that I raised my brow
toward that light from which this was breathed.
Then I turned to Beatrice, and she, with ready
looks, signaled to me that I should pour
the water forth from my inward fountain.
The grace that grants me leave to profess my faith,
I began, before the high first champion,
may it cause my thoughts to be expressed.
And I continued: As the truthful pen
of your dear brother wrote of it, father,
who with you set Rome on the right course,
faith is the substance of things hoped for,
and the evidence of things not seen;
and this seems to me its quiddity.
Then I heard: You judge rightly,
if you well understand why it was placed
among substances, and then among arguments.
And I next: The profound things
that here grant me the sight of them
are so hidden from eyes down there
that their being there is in belief alone,
upon which high hope is founded,
and therefore faith takes the meaning of substance.
And from this belief we must
reason by syllogism, having no other sight;
therefore faith bears the meaning of argument.
Then I heard: If all that is acquired
below through teaching were thus understood,
no sophist's ingenuity would have a place there.
Thus came the breath from that kindled love;
then he added: Quite well examined are
this coin's alloy and weight already.
But tell me whether you have it in your purse.
And I: Yes, I have it, so bright and so round
that nothing in its stamp raises a doubt in me.
Next there came forth from the deep light
that shone there: This precious jewel,
upon which every virtue is founded,
whence came it to you? And I: The abundant rain
of the Holy Spirit, which is poured
upon the old and upon the new parchments,
is the syllogism that proved it to me
so sharply that beside it
every demonstration seems to me blunt.
I then heard: The ancient and the new
proposition that brings you thus to your conclusion,
why do you take it for divine speech?
And I: The proof that lays the truth open to me
is the works that followed, for which nature
never heats iron nor strikes an anvil.
I was answered: Say, who assures you
that those works took place? The very thing
that needs proving, and nothing else, swears it to you.
If the world turned to Christianity,
I said, without miracles, this one
is such that the others are not a hundredth of it;
for you went poor and fasting
into the field to sow the good plant
that once was a vine and now has become a thornbush.
This ended, the high holy court
resounded through the spheres: One God we praise,
in the melody that is sung up there.
And that baron who thus from branch to branch,
examining me, had already led me
so that we were nearing the outermost leaves,
began again: The grace that holds converse
with your mind has opened your mouth
thus far just as it ought to have opened it;
so that I approve what came forth;
but now you must express what you believe,
and whence it offered itself to your belief.
O holy father and spirit, who see
what you so believed that you outstripped
younger feet on the way to the Sepulcher,
I began, you wish me to make manifest
the form here of my ready belief,
and you also asked its cause.
And I answer: That I believe in one God,
sole and eternal, who moves all heaven,
himself unmoved, with love and with desire;
and for such belief I have not only proofs
physical and metaphysical, but it is given me
also by the truth that rains down from here
through Moses, through the Prophets and through the Psalms,
through the Gospel, and through you who wrote
after the burning Spirit made you holy;
and I believe in three eternal Persons, and these
I believe to be one essence so one and so three
that it admits both are and is together.
Of the profound divine condition,
which I touch on now, my mind receives the seal
many times from the teaching of the Gospel.
This is the beginning, this is the spark
that then spreads into a living flame
and, like a star in heaven, sparkles in me.
As the lord who hears what pleases him
then embraces the servant, rejoicing
at the news, as soon as he falls silent;
so, blessing me in song,
three times it encircled me, as I fell silent,
the apostolic light, at whose command
I had spoken; so did I please him in my speaking.
