Virgin Mother, daughter of your Son,
Humble and high beyond any creature,
Fixed term of eternal counsel,
You are she who human nature
Ennobled so that its Maker
Did not disdain to make himself its creature.
In your womb love was rekindled,
Through whose warmth, in the eternal peace,
This flower has thus sprung forth.
Here you are to us the noonday torch
Of charity, and below, among mortals,
You are of hope the living fountain.
Lady, you are so great and have such power
That whoever desires grace and does not turn to you,
His desire would fly without wings.
Your kindness not only gives aid
To him who asks, but many times
Freely goes before the asking.
In you is mercy, in you pity,
In you magnificence, in you is gathered
Whatever goodness there is in any creature.
Now this man, who from the lowest pit
Of the universe as far as here has seen
The spiritual lives one by one,
Beseeches you for such a gift of strength,
So great that he may with his eyes rise
Higher toward the ultimate salvation.
And I, who never for my own sight burned
More than I do for his, all my prayers
I offer you, and pray that they may not fall short,
That you may dispel for him every cloud
Of his mortality with your prayers,
So that the highest delight may unfold itself to him.
Again I pray to you, O Queen, who can do
What you will, that you preserve sound,
After so great a vision, his affections.
May your guardianship overcome human impulses:
See Beatrice, with how many of the blessed
Clasping their hands to you in support of my prayers.
The eyes beloved and venerated by God,
Fixed on the suppliant, showed us
How pleasing devout prayers are to her.
Then toward the eternal light they turned,
Into which one must not believe there is directed
By any creature an eye so clear.
And I, who to the end of all desires
Was drawing near, as I was bound to do,
Brought the ardor of desire in me to its end.
Bernard was making a sign to me and smiling,
That I should look upward; but I was
Already of my own accord such as he wished;
For my sight, becoming clear,
Was entering more and more deeply into the ray
Of the lofty light that of itself is true.
From this point onward my seeing was greater
Than speech can show, for it yields to such a sight,
And memory yields to such excess.
As is he who sees while dreaming,
And after the dream the feeling imprinted
Remains, and the rest does not return to mind;
Such am I, for almost wholly gone is
My vision, and still there falls drop by drop
Into my heart the sweetness that was born of it.
Thus the snow in the sun loses its imprint,
Thus in the wind, amid the light leaves,
The meaning of the Sibyl was lost.
O highest light, that raise yourself so far
Above mortal thoughts, to my mind
Lend again a little of what you appeared to be;
And make my tongue so powerful
That a single spark of your glory
I may leave to people yet to come;
For, by returning somewhat to my memory,
And by sounding a little in these verses,
More of your victory will be conceived.
I believe, from the sharpness I endured
Of the living ray, that I would have been lost
If my eyes had turned away from it.
And I remember that I was the bolder
For this to endure so much that I joined
My gaze to the infinite power.
O abounding grace, through which I presumed
To fix my gaze deep within the eternal light,
So far that I spent my sight upon it!
In its depth I saw that there is contained,
Bound by love in a single volume,
What through the universe is spread in separate leaves;
Substances and accidents and their relations,
As if fused together in such a manner
That what I say is a mere glimmer.
The universal form of this knot
I believe I saw; for more abundantly,
In saying this, I feel myself rejoice.
A single instant is greater oblivion to me
Than twenty-five centuries are to the enterprise
That made Neptune gaze at the shadow of the Argo.
Thus my mind, wholly in suspense,
Gazed, fixed, motionless and attentive,
And ever grew more ardent to gaze.
At that light one becomes such
That to turn from it for another sight
It is impossible ever to consent;
Because the good, which is the object of the will,
Is wholly gathered in it, and outside it
What is perfect there is deficient.
From now on my speech will fall further short,
Even of what I remember, than that of an infant
Who still wets his tongue at the breast.
Not because more than one simple aspect
Was in the living light at which I gazed,
For it is always such as it was before;
But through the sight that was growing stronger
In me as I gazed, one single appearance,
As I changed, was changing for me:
In the deep and clear subsistence
Of the lofty light there appeared to me three circles
Of three colors and of one extent;
And one by the other, as rainbow by rainbow,
Seemed reflected, and the third seemed fire
That from the one and the other is breathed forth equally.
O how short is speech, and how faint
Beside my conception! And this, beside what I saw,
Is such that to say little does not suffice.
O eternal light, that alone dwell in yourself,
Alone understand yourself, and, understood by yourself
And understanding yourself, smile upon me!
That circling which, thus conceived,
Appeared in you as a reflected light,
When my eyes had gazed about it a while,
Within itself, in its very own color,
Seemed to me painted with our likeness:
Wherefore my gaze was wholly set on it.
Like the geometer who wholly applies himself
To measure the circle, and does not find,
For all his thinking, the principle he needs;
Such was I at that new sight:
I wished to see how there fitted
The image to the circle, and how it finds its place there;
But my own wings were not equal to this:
Except that my mind was struck
By a flash in which its wish came to fulfillment.
Here power failed the lofty imagination:
But already my desire and my will were being turned,
Like a wheel that is moved evenly,
By love that moves the heaven and the other stars.
