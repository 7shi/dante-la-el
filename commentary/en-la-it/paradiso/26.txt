While I was in doubt because of my extinguished sight,
From the radiant flame that extinguished it
Came forth a breath that made me attentive,
Saying: While you are recovering
The sight that you have spent on me,
It is well that by speaking you make up for it.
Begin then, and say where it is aimed,
Your soul, and reckon that it is
Lost to you, your sight, and not dead;
For the lady who through this divine
Region leads you has in her gaze
The power that the hand of Ananias had.
I said: At her pleasure, whether soon or late,
May a remedy come to the eyes that were gates
When she entered with the fire with which I always burn.
The Good that makes this court content
Is Alpha and O of all the writing
That Love reads to me both softly and loudly.
That very voice, which fear
Had taken from me of the sudden dazzling,
Set me once more to the task of speaking;
And said: Surely through a finer sieve
You must sift your thought; you must say
What directed your bow at such a target.
And I: Through philosophical arguments
And through authority that descends from here,
Such love must be impressed on me;
For the good, as good, as it is understood,
So kindles love, and love the greater,
The more of goodness it comprises in itself.
Therefore toward the essence in which there is such preeminence
That every good that is found outside it
Is nothing other than a light from its ray,
More than toward any other must move
The mind, in loving, of everyone who discerns
The truth on which this proof is founded.
Such truth makes clear to my intellect
He who shows me the first love
Of all the everlasting substances.
It is made clear by the voice of the truthful author,
Who says to Moses, speaking of himself:
I will make you see all worth.
You too make it clear to me, in beginning
The lofty proclamation that cries out the mystery
From here to the world below, and above every other herald's cry.
And I heard: Through human understanding
And not through authorities in accord with it,
Of your loves the sovereign looks to God.
But say further whether you feel other cords
Drawing you toward him, so that you sound forth
With how many teeth this love bites you.
Not hidden was the holy intention
Of the eagle of Christ; rather, I perceived
Where he wished to lead my profession.
Therefore I began again: All those bites
That can make the heart turn to God
Have contributed to my charity;
For the world's being, and my own being,
The death that he endured that I might live,
And that for which every faithful soul hopes, as I do,
Together with the aforesaid living knowledge,
Have drawn me out of the sea of crooked love,
And on the shore of upright love have set me.
The leaves with which all the garden is leafed
Of the eternal gardener, I love just so much
As good is bestowed on them by him.
As soon as I fell silent, a most sweet song
Resounded through heaven; and my lady
Was saying with the others: Holy, Holy, Holy.
And as at a piercing light one wakes
Through the visual spirit that runs back
To the brightness that passes from coat to coat,
And the awakened man recoils from what he sees,
So unknowing is his sudden wakefulness,
Until the power of judgment comes to his aid;
So from my eyes every speck
Beatrice drove with the rays of her own,
Which shone brighter than a thousand miles;
Wherefore, better than before, I then saw,
And, almost stupefied, I asked
About a fourth light that I saw with us.
And she said: Within those rays
Gazes lovingly upon its Maker the first soul
That the first Power ever created.
As the bough that bends its tip
At the passing of the wind, and then rises
Through its own strength that lifts it up,
So I did while she was speaking,
Marveling; and then what made me confident again was
A desire to speak, with which I burned;
And I began: O fruit, who ripe
Alone were brought forth, O ancient father,
To whom every bride is daughter and daughter-in-law;
As devoutly as I can, I beg you
To speak to me: you see my desire;
And to hear you soon, I do not utter it.
Sometimes a covered animal stirs
So that its feeling must show itself,
Through the covering's following its impulse;
And likewise the first soul
Made shine through its covering for me
How gladly it came to please me.
Then it breathed: Without its being uttered to me,
Dante, your desire I discern better
Than you do whatever is most certain to you;
For I see it in the truthful mirror,
Which makes the other things reflections of itself,
And nothing makes it a reflection of itself.
You wish to know how long it is since God placed me
In the lofty garden where this lady
Prepared you for so high a stair;
And how long it was a delight to my eyes,
And the precise cause of the great wrath,
And the tongue that I used and that I made.
Now, my son, not the tasting of the tree
In itself was the cause of so great an exile,
But solely the passing beyond the mark.
There, whence your lady sent Virgil forth,
For four thousand three hundred and two revolutions
Of the sun I longed for this assembly;
And I saw it return to all the lights
Of its path nine hundred and thirty
Times, while I was on earth.
The tongue that I spoke was wholly extinct
Before ever to the work that could not be finished
The people of Nimrod gave their attention;
For no product of reason ever,
Because of human pleasure, which renews itself
Following the heavens, endured forever.
A work of nature it is that man speaks;
But this way or that, nature leaves
You then to do, according as it pleases you.
Before I descended to infernal anguish,
I was the name on earth of the highest Good,
Whence comes the gladness that enfolds me;
Hely it was called afterward: and this is fitting;
For the usage of mortals is like a leaf
On a branch, which goes, and another comes.
On the mountain that rises highest from the waves,
I was, with life both pure and dishonored,
From the first hour to that which is second,
As the sun changes quadrant, to the sixth hour.
