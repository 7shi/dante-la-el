After, against the present life
of wretched mortals, the truth was laid bare
by her who in Paradise holds my mind;
as in the mirror the flame of a double taper
is seen by one who is lit by it from behind,
before he has it in sight or in thought,
and he turns himself about, to see if the glass
tells him the truth, and sees that it agrees
with it, as a note with its measure;
so my memory recalls
that I did, looking into the beautiful eyes
from which, to take me, Love made the cord.
And as I turned myself about, and were touched
my own eyes by what appears in that sphere,
whenever one looks well into its circling,
a point I saw that radiated light
so sharp that the eyes it sets aflame
one must close, for its piercing sharpness:
and whichever star appears smallest from here
would seem a moon set beside it,
as star beside star is set.
Perhaps as far as appears near
to the halo the light that paints it,
when the vapor that bears it is thickest,
distant around the point a circle of fire
revolved so swiftly that it would have outstripped
that motion which most swiftly girds the world;
and this one was by another encircled,
and that by the third, and the third then by the fourth,
by the fifth the fourth, and then by the sixth the fifth.
Beyond followed the seventh, so far spread
already in length, that the messenger of Juno
entire would be too narrow to contain it.
So too the eighth and the ninth; and each
moved more slowly, according as it was
in number more distant from the first:
and that one had the purest flame
from which the pure spark was least distant;
I believe because it partakes more of its truth.
My lady, who saw me in thought
deeply suspended, said: On that point
depend heaven and all nature.
Look at that circle which is most closely joined to it;
and know that its motion is so swift
through the burning love by which it is spurred.
And I to her: If the world were arranged
in the order that I see in those wheels,
what is set before me would have sated me.
But in the world of sense one can
see things so much the more divine,
the more remote they are from the center.
Hence, if my desire is to have an end
in this wondrous and angelic temple,
which has only love and light for its bounds,
I must hear further how the copy
and the model do not go in one manner;
for by myself I contemplate this in vain.
If your fingers are not for such a knot
sufficient, it is no marvel,
so hard has it grown for want of trying.
Thus my lady; then she said: Take
what I shall tell you, if you wish to be sated,
and about it sharpen your wits.
The bodily circles are broad and narrow
according to the more and less of the power
that extends throughout all their parts.
Greater goodness wills to work greater good;
greater good a greater body holds,
if it has its parts equally perfect.
Therefore this one, which sweeps all
the rest of the universe along with it, corresponds
to the circle that loves most and knows most.
Wherefore, if about the power you wrap
your measure, not about the appearance
of the substances that appear round to you,
you will see a wondrous correspondence,
of greater to more, and of smaller to less,
in each heaven to its intelligence.
As there remains splendid and serene
the hemisphere of air, when blows
Boreas from that cheek from which he is gentlest,
because the murk is cleared and dissolved
that troubled it before, so that the sky smiles on that account
with the beauties of its every quarter;
so did I, after I was furnished
by my lady with her clear reply;
and like a star in heaven, the truth was seen.
And after her words had ceased,
no differently does iron cast off sparks
when it seethes, than did the circles sparkle.
Their blaze each spark followed;
and they were so many that their number
mounts into thousands beyond the doubling on the chessboard.
I heard hosannas sung from choir to choir
to the fixed point that holds them in the place,
and ever will hold them, in which they ever were.
And she who saw the doubtful thoughts
in my mind said: The first circles
have shown you Seraphim and Cherubim.
So swiftly they follow their bonds
to make themselves like the point as far as they can,
and they can in proportion as they are exalted in seeing.
Those other loves that go around them
are called Thrones of the divine countenance,
because they brought the first triad to its end.
And you must know that all have delight
in proportion as their vision plunges deep
into the Truth in which every intellect finds rest.
From this one can see how there is grounded
the state of blessedness in the act that sees,
not in that which loves, which follows after;
and of seeing the measure is merit,
which grace brings forth, and good will;
thus from degree to degree one proceeds.
The other triad that thus puts forth shoots
in this everlasting spring,
which the nocturnal Ram does not strip bare,
warbles a perpetual and sweet hosanna
in three melodies that sound in the three
orders of gladness of which it is made threefold.
In this hierarchy are the other goddesses:
first Dominions, then Virtues;
the third order is of Powers.
Then in the two next-to-last dances
Principalities and Archangels revolve;
the last is wholly of angelic sports.
These orders all gaze upward in wonder,
and so prevail below that toward God
all are drawn, and all draw.
And Dionysius with such desire
set himself to contemplate these orders
that he named and distinguished them as I do.
But Gregory afterward differed from him;
wherefore, as soon as he opened his eyes
in this heaven, he laughed at himself.
And if so secret a truth was uttered
by a mortal on earth, I would not have you marvel;
for he who saw it up here disclosed it to him,
with much else of the truth of these circles.
