Refreshed to his pleasure, that contemplative
freely took up the office of teacher,
and began these holy words:
The wound that Mary closed again and anointed,
she who is so fair at her feet
is the one who opened it and so deeply pierced.
In the row that the third seats make
sits Rachel beneath this woman,
with Beatrice, as you see.
Sarah, Rebecca, Judith, and her
who was great-grandmother to the singer who, for sorrow
at his fault, said: Have mercy on me,
you can see thus from tier to tier
descending, as I, by their proper names,
go down through the rose from petal to petal.
And from the seventh step down, just as
down to it, Hebrew women follow one another,
dividing all the tresses of the flower;
for, according to the direction in which
faith looked toward Christ, these women are the wall
at which the sacred stairways part.
On this side, where the flower is mature
in all its petals, are seated
those who believed in Christ to come.
On the other side, where there are gaps
of empty seats in the semicircle, are
those who had their faces turned to Christ already come.
And as on this side the glorious seat
of the Lady of Heaven, and the other seats
beneath it, make so great a division,
so opposite does that of the great John,
who, ever holy, the desert and martyrdom
endured, and afterward Hell for some two years;
and beneath him thus to divide was the lot of
Francis, Benedict, and Augustine,
and others down to here, from circle to circle.
Now behold the lofty divine foresight,
for the one and the other aspect of faith
will equally fill this garden.
And know that from the step down that cuts
midway across the expanse and the two divisions,
by no merit of one's own does one sit,
but by another's, with certain conditions;
for all these are spirits released
before they had true choices.
Well can you discern it by their faces,
and also by their childish voices,
if you look well at them or if you listen to them.
Now you doubt, and doubting keep silent;
but I will loosen the tight bond
in which subtle thoughts bind you.
Within the breadth of this realm
no point of chance can have a place,
any more than sadness, or thirst, or hunger;
for by eternal law is established
all that you see, so that exactly
the ring here fits the finger.
And therefore these people, hastened
to true life, are not without cause
among themselves here more and less excellent.
The King through whom this kingdom rests
in so much love and in so much delight
that no will has dared desire more,
all minds, in his glad sight,
creating, at his pleasure endows with grace
diversely: and here let the effect suffice.
And this is expressly and clearly marked for you
in Holy Scripture, in those twins
who in their mother had their anger stirred.
Therefore, according to the color of the hair
of such grace, the highest light
must worthily crown the head.
Thus, without merit from their conduct,
they are placed on different steps,
differing only in their first keenness of sight.
It sufficed thus in the ages when the world was new,
together with innocence, to have salvation,
to have the faith of the parents alone.
After the first ages were completed,
for males it was necessary that their innocent wings,
through circumcision, acquire strength.
But after the time of grace came,
without the perfect baptism of Christ,
such innocence was kept below.
Look now upon the face that Christ's
most resembles, for its brightness
alone can prepare you to see Christ.
I saw upon her so much gladness
rain down, borne in the holy minds
created to fly across that height,
that all I had seen before
did not hold me in so much wonder,
nor show me so great a likeness of God.
And that love which first descended there,
singing: Hail, Mary, full of grace,
before her spread its wings.
To the divine song responded
from every side the blessed court,
so that every countenance grew more serene thereby.
O holy Father, who for my sake endure
being down here, leaving the sweet place
in which you sit by eternal lot,
who is that angel who with so much joy
looks into the eyes of our Queen,
so enamored that he seems of fire?
Thus I had recourse again to the teaching
of him who drew beauty from Mary,
as from the sun the morning star.
And he to me: Boldness and grace,
as much as can be in angel or in soul,
are all in him, and so we will it to be;
for he is the one who bore the palm
down to Mary, when the Son of God
willed to burden himself with our load.
But come now with your eyes, just as I
go on speaking, and mark the great patricians
of this most just and pious empire.
Those two who sit up there, happier
for being nearest to the Empress,
are, as it were, two roots of this rose.
He who on the left is close beside her
is the father through whose daring taste
the human race tastes so much bitterness.
On the right you see that ancient Father
of Holy Church to whom Christ the keys
entrusted of this lovely flower.
And he who saw all the grievous times,
before he died, of the fair bride
who was won with the lance and with the nails,
sits beside him; and beside the other rests
that leader under whom there lived on manna
the ungrateful people, fickle and refractory.
Opposite Peter you see seated Anne,
so content to gaze at her daughter
that she does not move an eye for singing Hosanna.
And opposite the greatest father of a family
sits Lucy, who moved your lady
when you bent your brows toward ruin.
But because the time flies that holds you asleep,
here we shall stop, like the good tailor
who, according to the cloth he has, makes the gown;
and we shall direct our eyes to the first Love,
so that, looking toward him, you may penetrate,
as far as is possible, through his radiance.
Yet, lest perhaps you draw back,
moving your wings, believing you advance,
by praying it is necessary to obtain grace,
grace from her who well can help you;
and do you follow me with your affection,
so that from my words you do not part your heart.
And he began this holy prayer.
