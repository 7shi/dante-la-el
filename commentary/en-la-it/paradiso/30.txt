Perhaps six thousand miles away
From here the sixth hour burns, and this world
Already inclines its shadow almost to the level bed,
When the middle of the sky, deep to us,
Begins to become such that some star
Loses its appearance down here in this depth;
And as there comes the most radiant handmaid
Of the sun farther on, so the sky closes
From sight to sight, down to the fairest;
Not otherwise the triumph that plays
Ever around the point that overcame me,
Seeming enclosed by that which it encloses,
Little by little faded from my sight;
Wherefore to turn my eyes again to Beatrice
Seeing nothing and love constrained me.
If all that up to here is said of her
Were gathered all into one praise,
It would be little to suffice this time.
The beauty that I saw goes beyond measure,
Not only beyond us, but surely I believe
That only her Maker enjoys it all.
By this passage I own myself vanquished,
More than ever by a point of his theme
Was comic or tragic poet overcome.
And as the sun does to the sight that trembles most,
So the remembering of the sweet smile
Deprives my mind of its very self.
From the first day that I saw her face
In this life, down to this vision,
The following has not been cut short for my song;
But now my following must desist
Farther after her beauty in verse,
As every artist must at his utmost.
Such as I leave her to a greater proclamation
Than that of my trumpet, which brings
Its arduous matter to a close,
With voice and gesture of a ready guide
She began: We have passed out
Of the greatest body into the heaven that has pure light;
Intellectual light full of love,
Love of true good full of gladness,
Gladness that transcends every sweetness.
Here you will see the one and the other host
Of Paradise, and the one in those forms
That you will see at the final judgment.
Like a sudden flash that scatters
The visual spirits, so that it deprives
The eye of the power to see the strongest objects;
So a living light flashed all around me,
And left me swathed in such a veil
Of its brilliance that nothing appeared to me.
Ever the love that stills this heaven
Welcomes into itself with such a greeting,
To make the candle ready for its flame.
No sooner had there entered into me
These few words than I perceived
Myself rising above my power;
And I was rekindled with new sight
Such that no light is so pure
That my eyes could not have withstood it.
And I saw light in the form of a river
Resplendent with splendors, between two banks
Painted with wondrous spring.
From that great stream living sparks came forth,
And on every side they settled in the flowers,
Like a ruby that gold encircles.
Then, as if drunken with the scents,
They plunged again into the wondrous flood;
And if one entered, another came forth.
The lofty desire that now inflames and urges you
To have knowledge of what you see
Pleases me the more, the more it swells.
But of this water you must drink
Before so great a thirst in you is quenched.
Thus said to me the sun of my eyes.
She further added: The river and the topazes
That enter and go out, and the smiling of the grasses,
Are shadowy prefaces to their truth:
Not that in themselves these things are unripe;
But the defect is on your part,
For you do not yet have sight so exalted.
There is no babe that rushes so quickly
With its face toward the milk, if it wakes
Much later than is its wont,
As I did, to make better mirrors
Still of my eyes, bending down to the wave
That flows forth so that one may be made better in it.
And as soon as there drank of it the rims
Of my eyelids, so it seemed to me
From its long shape to have become round.
Then, like people who have been under masks,
Who seem other than before if they put off
The semblance not their own in which they disappeared,
So were changed for me into greater rejoicings
The flowers and the sparks, so that I saw
Both courts of heaven made manifest.
O divine splendor, by which I saw
The lofty triumph of the true kingdom,
Give me power to tell it as I saw it.
Light is there above that makes visible
The Creator to that creature
That only in seeing him has its peace;
And it extends itself in a circular figure
So far that its circumference
Would be too broad a girdle for the sun.
All its appearance is made of a ray
Reflected at the summit of the First Moved,
Which takes from it life and power.
And as a hillside in the water at its foot
Mirrors itself, as if to see itself adorned,
When in the green every little flower is luxuriant;
So, standing above the light all round,
I saw mirrored more than a thousand petals,
All those of us who have returned above.
And if the lowest tier gathers into itself
So great a light, how great is the breadth
Of this rose in its outermost petals?
My sight in the breadth and in the height
Did not lose itself, but took in all
The measure and the nature of that gladness.
Near or far, there, neither adds nor takes away;
For where God governs without intermediary,
Natural law counts for nothing.
Into the yellow of the everlasting rose,
Which widens, descends by degrees, and breathes
An odor of praise to the Sun that makes spring forever,
Like one who is silent and would speak,
Beatrice drew me, and said: Behold
How great is the assembly of the white robes!
See how wide the circuit of our city is!
See our seats so filled
That few people more are wanted here.
And on that great seat, on which you keep your eyes
Because of the crown already set upon it,
Before you dine at this wedding feast,
Will sit the soul, which shall be imperial below,
Of lofty Henry, who to set Italy straight
Will come before she is disposed to it.
The blind cupidity that bewitches you
Has made you like the little child
That dies of hunger and drives away the nurse.
And there shall be prefect in the divine court
Then such a one that openly and in secret
He will not walk one path with him.
But little longer will he be borne with by God
In the holy office; for he will be thrust down
There where Simon Magus is for his deserts,
And will make him of Alagna go farther down.
