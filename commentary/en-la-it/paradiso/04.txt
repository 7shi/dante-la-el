Between two foods, distant and enticing
in equal measure, one would sooner die of hunger
than a free man would bring one to his teeth.
So would a lamb stand between two ravenous mouths
of fierce wolves, equally fearing them;
so would a hound stand between two does.
Wherefore, if I kept silent, I do not blame myself,
by my doubts equally impelled,
since it was necessary; nor do I praise myself.
I kept silent, and my desire was painted
on my face, and my question with it,
far more clearly than in distinct speech.
Beatrice made herself such as Daniel made himself,
lifting Nebuchadnezzar out of the wrath
that had made him unjustly fierce.
And she said: I see well how you are drawn
by one desire and the other, so that your concern
binds itself so that it cannot breathe out.
You reason: If the good will endures,
for what cause does another's violence
diminish for me the measure of my merit?
Again, cause for doubt is given you
by the souls' seeming to return to the stars,
according to the opinion of Plato.
These are the questions that upon your will
press equally; and therefore first
I shall treat the one that has more gall.
Of the Seraphim, he who is most absorbed in God,
Moses, Samuel, and that John,
whichever you choose, I say, not even Mary,
do not have their seats in any other heaven
than those spirits who just now appeared to you,
nor have they more or fewer years to their being;
but all make fair the first circle,
and in differing measure have sweet life,
through feeling more or less the eternal breath.
Here they showed themselves, not because allotted
to them is this sphere, but to signify
the heavenly sphere that has the least ascent.
Thus must one speak to your understanding,
because only from what is sensed does it learn
what it afterward makes worthy of the intellect.
For this reason Scripture stoops
to your capacity, and foot and hand
it attributes to God, and means something else;
and Holy Church in human form
represents Gabriel and Michael to you,
and the other who made Tobias well again.
What Timaeus argues concerning souls
is not like what is seen here,
because, as he speaks, so he seems to think.
He says that the soul returns to its star,
believing it to have been severed from there
when nature gave it as a form.
And perhaps his meaning is of another sort
than his words sound, and it may be
with an intention not to be derided.
If he means that to these wheels return
the honor of their influence and the blame, perhaps
his bow strikes upon some truth.
This principle, ill understood, led astray
once nearly all the world, so that Jupiter,
Mercury and Mars it went so far as to name.
The other doubt that stirs you
has less poison, because its malice
could not lead you away from me elsewhere.
That our justice should appear unjust
in the eyes of mortals is an argument
for faith, and not for heretical wickedness.
But because your discernment can
well penetrate to this truth,
as you desire, I shall content you.
If it is violence when he who suffers
contributes nothing to him who uses force,
the souls would not on that account be excused;
for the will, if it does not will it, is not quenched,
but acts as nature acts in fire,
though violence twist it a thousand times;
for if it bends much or little,
it follows the force; and so these souls did,
being able to return to the holy place.
If their will had been whole,
as was the will that held Lawrence on the gridiron
and made Mucius stern toward his own hand,
so it would have driven them back along the way
from which they were dragged, as soon as they were freed;
but so steadfast a will is all too rare.
And by these words, if gathered
you have them as you ought, the argument is annulled
that would have troubled you many times more.
But now another obstacle sets itself across your path
before your eyes, such that by yourself
you would not get past it; sooner would you be weary.
I have set it in your mind as certain
that a blessed soul could not lie,
because it always has the primal truth close by;
and then you could hear from Piccarda
that Constance kept her affection for the veil;
so that she seems here to contradict me.
Many times already, brother, it has happened
that, to flee danger, against one's will
one did what it was not fitting to do;
as Alcmaeon, who, entreated to do this
by his father, slew his own mother;
not to lose piety, he made himself pitiless.
At this point I would have you consider
that force mingles with the will, and they bring it about
that the offenses cannot be excused.
Absolute will does not consent to the harm,
but it consents to it insofar as it fears,
if it draws back, to fall into greater distress.
Therefore, when Piccarda expresses this,
she means the absolute will, and I
the other, so that together we speak the truth.
Such was the rippling of the holy stream
that issued from the fount whence every truth flows;
thus it set both one desire and the other at peace.
O beloved of the first lover, O divine lady,
I said next, whose speech floods me
and warms me so that more and more it quickens me,
my affection is not so deep
that it suffices to render you grace for grace;
but may he who sees and can do so answer for it.
I see well that never is it sated,
our intellect, unless the truth illumines it,
outside of which no truth has scope.
It rests in it as a beast in its lair,
as soon as it has reached it; and it can reach it;
if not, every desire would be in vain.
For that reason there springs, in the manner of a shoot,
at the foot of truth, doubt; and it is nature
that drives us to the summit from hill to hill.
This invites me, this gives me courage,
with reverence, lady, to ask you
about another truth that is obscure to me.
I wish to know whether a man can make amends to you
for unfulfilled vows with other goods such
that in your scales they will not be too small.
Beatrice looked at me with eyes full
of sparks of love, with eyes so divine
that, overcome, my power turned its back,
and I nearly lost myself, my eyes cast down.
