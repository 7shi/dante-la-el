That sun which first with love warmed my breast,
had of fair truth uncovered for me,
proving and disproving, the sweet aspect;
and I, to confess corrected and certain
myself, as much as was fitting,
raised my head higher to speak.
But a vision appeared, which held
me to itself so tightly to be seen,
that my confession did not come to mind.
As through clear and polished glass,
or through waters clear and tranquil,
not so deep that the bottoms are lost,
there return the outlines of our faces
so faint that a pearl on a white brow
comes no less strongly to our pupils;
such did I see several faces ready to speak:
wherefore I fell into the error opposite
to that which kindled love between the man and the spring.
As soon as I became aware of them,
taking them for mirrored likenesses,
to see whose they were, I turned my eyes;
and nothing did I see, and turned them forward again,
straight into the light of my sweet guide,
who, smiling, burned in her holy eyes.
Do not marvel that I smile,
she said to me, at your childish thought,
which on the truth does not yet trust its foot,
but turns you, as is its wont, toward emptiness.
True substances are what you see,
relegated here for failure in a vow.
Therefore speak with them, and hear, and believe
that the truthful light, which satisfies them,
does not let them turn their feet from it.
And I toward the shade that seemed most eager
to converse turned myself, and began,
almost as one whom too great a longing bewilders:
O spirit created for good, who in the rays
of eternal life feel the sweetness
which, untasted, is never understood;
it will be pleasing to me if you satisfy me
as to your name and the lot of you all.
Whereupon she, readily and with smiling eyes:
Our charity does not shut doors
to a just prayer, any more than does that charity
which wills its whole court to be like itself.
I was in the world a virgin sister:
and if your memory looks well into itself,
my being more beautiful will not hide me from you;
but you will recognize that I am Piccarda,
who, placed here with these other blessed ones,
am blessed in the slowest sphere.
Our affections, which kindled only
are in the pleasure of the Holy Spirit,
rejoice, being formed by his ordinance.
And this lot, which seems so low,
is given us for this reason, that neglected were
our vows, and void in some part.
Whereupon I to her: In the wondrous faces
of you all there shines I know not what divine,
which transforms you from my first conceptions.
Therefore I was not quick to remember;
but now what you tell me helps me,
so that recognizing you is easier for me.
But tell me: you who are happy here,
do you desire a higher place
to see more, or to make yourselves closer friends?
With those other shades she first smiled a little;
then she answered me so joyously
that she seemed to burn with love in the primal fire:
Brother, our will is quieted
by the power of charity, which makes us will
only what we have, and makes us thirst for nothing else.
If we desired to be higher,
our desires would be at odds
with the will of him who assigns us here;
which you will see has no place in these circles,
if to be in charity is necessary here,
and if you consider its nature well;
rather it is of the essence of this blessed being
to keep within the divine will,
whereby our very wills are made one.
So that, as we are from threshold to threshold
throughout this kingdom, the kingdom pleases all,
as it does the king who moves us to will his will;
and his will is our peace;
it is that sea toward which moves all
that it creates or that nature makes.
Clear to me then was how every place
in heaven is paradise, and yet the grace
of the highest good does not rain there in one measure.
But just as it happens, if one food satisfies,
and for another the appetite still remains,
that the one is asked for, and for the other thanks are given;
so did I with gesture and with word,
to learn from her what was the web
through which she did not draw the shuttle to the end.
A perfect life and high merit set in heaven
a lady higher up, she told me, according to whose rule
in your world below one takes the habit and veil;
so that until death one may wake and sleep
with that spouse who accepts every vow
that charity conforms to his pleasure.
From the world, to follow her, as a young girl
I fled, and in her habit enclosed myself,
and pledged myself to the way of her order.
Men then, more accustomed to evil than to good,
snatched me out of the sweet cloister;
God knows what my life then was.
And that other splendor which shows itself to you
on my right side, and which is kindled
with all the light of our sphere,
understands of herself what I say of myself:
a sister she was, and likewise from her was taken
off the head the shade of the sacred bands.
After she was indeed turned back to the world,
against her will and against good custom,
she was never loosed from the veil of the heart.
This is the light of the great Constance,
who by the second wind of Swabia
begot the third and the last power.
Thus she spoke to me, and then began: Hail,
Mary, singing; and singing she vanished,
as through deep water a heavy thing.
My sight, which followed her as far
as was possible, after it lost her,
turned toward the mark of greater desire,
and to Beatrice it turned entirely;
but she flashed upon my gaze
so that at first my sight could not bear it;
and this made me slower to ask.
