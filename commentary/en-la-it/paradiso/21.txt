Already were my eyes fixed again upon the face
Of my lady, and my mind with them
From every other purpose had withdrawn;
And she was not smiling: But if I smiled,
She began to me, you would become as
Semele was, when she was turned to ashes;
For my beauty, which, upon the stairs
Of the eternal palace, kindles the more,
As you have seen, the higher one climbs,
If it were not tempered, shines so brightly
That your mortal power, at my fulgor,
Would be a bough that a thunderbolt rends.
We have been raised to the seventh splendor,
Which beneath the breast of the burning Lion
Has now beamed down its power mingled with his.
Fix your mind behind your eyes,
And make of them a mirror for the figure
That in this mirror will be visible to you.
Whoever knew what was the pasture
Of my sight in that blessed countenance,
When I turned from my other care,
Would know how pleasing it was to me
To obey my heavenly guide,
Weighing the one side against the other.
Within the crystal that bears the name,
As it circles the world, of its dear ruler,
Under whom all warfare lay dead,
Of the color of gold through which a ray shines,
I saw a ladder raised upward
So far that my sight could not follow it.
I saw also, down the steps, descending
So many splendors that I thought every light
That appears in heaven had been shed from there.
And as, by their natural habit,
The jackdaws together, at the beginning of day,
Set out to warm their cold feathers;
Then some go away without returning,
Others turn back to where they started,
And others, wheeling, linger;
Such a manner it seemed to me there was
In that sparkling that came together,
As soon as it struck a certain step;
And the one that stayed nearest us
Grew so bright that I said in thought:
I see well the love you show me.
But she from whom I await the how and the when
Of speaking and of keeping silent holds still; hence I,
Against my desire, do well if I do not ask.
Wherefore she, who saw my silence
In the sight of Him who sees all,
Said to me: Give vent to your ardent desire.
And I began: My merit
Does not make me worthy of your answer;
But for her sake who grants me leave to ask,
Blessed life, who remain hidden
Within your gladness, make known to me
The cause that brings you so close to me;
And say why there is silent in this sphere
The sweet symphony of Paradise,
Which down through the others sounds so devoutly.
You have mortal hearing, as you have mortal sight,
It answered me; hence here there is no singing
For the same reason that Beatrice has not smiled.
Down the steps of the holy ladder
I descended so far solely to welcome you
With speech and with the light that mantles me:
Nor did greater love make me readier,
For as much love and more burns from here upward,
As the flaming makes plain to you.
But the high charity that makes us servants
Ready for the counsel that governs the world
Assigns by lot here, as you observe.
I see well, said I, sacred lamp,
How freely given love in this court
Suffices to follow eternal providence:
But this is what seems hard to me to discern:
Why you alone were predestined
To this office among your companions.
No sooner had I reached the last word
Than the light made its own middle its center,
Spinning like a swift millstone.
Then answered the love that was within it:
Divine light is focused upon me,
Penetrating through this light in which I am enclosed;
Its power, joined with my sight,
Raises me so far above myself that I see
The supreme essence from which it is drawn.
From this comes the joy with which I flame,
For to my sight, in the measure of its clarity,
I match the brightness of the flame.
But that soul in heaven that shines most brightly,
That seraph whose eye is most fixed on God,
Would not satisfy your question;
For so enfolded is in the abyss
Of the eternal decree what you ask
That from all created sight it is cut off.
And to the mortal world, when you return,
Report this, so that it may not presume
To move its feet toward so high a goal again.
The mind that here shines gives off smoke on earth;
Hence consider how it can do down there
What it cannot do once heaven takes it up.
So did his words set bounds for me
That I left the question and confined myself
To asking him humbly who he was.
Between the two shores of Italy rise crags,
And not very distant from my homeland,
So high that the thunder sounds far lower,
And they form a hump that is called Catria,
Beneath which a hermitage is consecrated,
Which is wont to be given over to worship alone.
Thus he began again his third speech to me;
And then, continuing, he said: There
In God's service I made myself so steadfast
That, with no food but fare dressed with olive oil,
I passed easily through heats and frosts,
Content in contemplative thoughts.
That cloister used to yield to these heavens
Fruitfully, and now it has become barren,
So that soon this must be revealed.
In that place I was Peter Damian;
And Peter the Sinner I was in the house
Of Our Lady on the Adriatic shore.
Little mortal life remained to me
When I was called and drawn to that hat
Which ever passes from bad to worse.
Cephas came, and the great Vessel came
Of the Holy Spirit, lean and barefoot,
Taking their food from any lodging.
Now they want, on this side and that, someone to prop them up,
The modern shepherds, and someone to lead them,
So heavy are they, and someone behind to lift them.
They cover their palfreys with their mantles,
So that two beasts go beneath one hide:
O patience, that endures so much!
At this voice I saw more little flames
From step to step descend and turn,
And every turn made them more beautiful.
Around this one they came and halted,
And gave a cry of so lofty a sound
That it could be likened to nothing here:
Nor did I understand it, so did the thunder overcome me.
