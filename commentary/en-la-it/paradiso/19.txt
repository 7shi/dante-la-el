There appeared before me with wings spread
the fair image, which in sweet fruition
made glad the souls woven together.
Each appeared a little ruby, in which
a ray of sun burned so brightly
that it reflected it into my eyes.
And what I must portray just now
no voice ever bore, nor ink wrote,
nor was it ever conceived by imagination;
for I saw, and also heard, the beak speak,
and in the voice sound both I and My,
when in the thought it was both We and Our.
And it began: For being just and pious
I am here exalted to that glory
which does not let itself be surpassed by desire;
and on earth I left my memory
such that the wicked peoples there
commend it, but do not follow its history.
Thus a single heat from many embers
makes itself felt, as from many loves
a single sound issued from that image.
Whereupon I next: O everlasting flowers
of eternal gladness, who yet as one
make all your fragrances seem to me,
end for me, breathing forth, the great fast
that has long held me in hunger,
finding for it on earth no food at all.
Well do I know, if in heaven a high realm
divine justice makes its mirror,
that yours does not perceive it through a veil.
You know how attentively I prepare myself
to listen; you know what it is, that
doubt which is to me so old a fast.
Like a falcon that, coming out of its hood,
moves its head and with its wings applauds itself,
showing eagerness and making itself fair,
I saw that emblem become, which of praises
of divine grace was woven,
with songs such as he knows who rejoices up there.
Then it began: He who turns the compass
at the world's outer edge, and within it
marked out so much hidden and manifest,
could not make his power so imprinted
upon the whole universe that his Word
should not remain infinitely in excess.
And this is made certain by the fact that the first Proud One,
who was the highest of all creatures,
for not awaiting light, fell unripe:
and hence it appears that every lesser nature
is too small a vessel for that good
which has no end and measures itself by itself.
Therefore your sight, which must
be one of the rays of the mind
with which all things are filled,
cannot by its nature be powerful
so much that its source does not discern
far beyond what is apparent to it.
Therefore into everlasting justice
the sight that your world receives
penetrates as does the eye into the sea;
for although from the shore it sees the bottom,
in the open sea it does not see it; and nonetheless
it is there, but its depth conceals it.
It is not light unless it comes from the clear sky
that is never troubled; rather it is darkness,
either the shadow of the flesh or its poison.
Sufficiently laid open to you now is the hiding place
that concealed from you the living justice
of which you made such frequent question;
for you used to say: A man is born on the bank
of the Nile, and there is no one there who speaks
of Christ, nor anyone who reads, nor anyone who writes;
and all his desires and acts good
are, as far as human reason sees,
without sin in life or in speech.
He dies unbaptized and without faith:
where is this justice that condemns him?
Where is his fault if he does not believe?
Now who are you, who would sit upon the bench
to judge a thousand miles away
with sight no longer than a span?
Surely for him who reasons subtly with me,
if Scripture were not above you,
there would be wondrous cause for doubt.
O earthly animals, O dull minds!
The primal will, which is good of itself,
from itself, which is the highest good, never moved.
Just is so much as accords with it;
no created good draws it to itself,
but it, shining forth, brings that good into being.
As over her nest she circles,
after the stork has fed her young,
and as the one that has been fed gazes at her,
so it became, and lifted up its brows,
the blessed image, which its wings
moved, impelled by so many counsels.
Wheeling, it sang and said: As
my notes are to you who do not understand them,
so is eternal judgment to you mortals.
Then moved on those bright fires
of the Holy Spirit, still in the emblem
that made the Romans revered by the world.
It began again: To this kingdom
none ever ascended who did not believe in Christ,
either before or after he was nailed to the wood.
But see, many cry Christ, Christ,
who at the judgment will be far less near
to him than one who does not know Christ:
and such Christians will the Ethiopian condemn
when the two companies shall part,
the one forever rich, the other destitute.
What will the Persians be able to say to your kings
when they see that volume opened
in which all their acts of contempt are written?
There will be seen, among the deeds of Albert,
the one that soon will move the pen,
through which the kingdom of Prague will be laid waste:
there will be seen the grief that upon the Seine
brings, by falsifying the coinage,
he who will die from the blow of a boar's hide.
There will be seen the pride that causes thirst,
that drives the Scot and the Englishman mad
so that neither can bear to stay within his bounds.
There will be seen the lust and the soft living
of him of Spain and of him of Bohemia,
who never knew worth nor desired it.
There will be seen, for the Lame One of Jerusalem,
his goodness marked with an I,
while an M will mark the contrary.
There will be seen the avarice and the baseness
of him who guards the island of fire,
where Anchises ended his long life;
and, to make it understood how little he is,
his record will be in shortened letters,
which will note much in little space.
And there will appear to everyone the foul deeds
of the uncle and the brother, who so illustrious
a nation and two crowns have made bastards.
And he of Portugal and he of Norway
will there be known, and he of Rascia,
who to his hurt has seen the coin die of Venice.
O happy Hungary, if she does not let herself
be ill-treated any longer! And happy Navarre,
if she would arm herself with the mountain that girds her!
And everyone must believe that already, as a pledge
of this, Nicosia and Famagusta
lament and cry out on account of their beast,
which does not draw away from the others' side.
