If ever it should happen that the sacred poem,
To which both heaven and earth have set their hand,
So that it has for many years made me lean,
Should overcome the cruelty that shuts me out
Of the fair sheepfold where I slept a lamb,
An enemy to the wolves that wage war on it;
With another voice now, with another fleece,
I shall return a poet, and at the font
Of my baptism I shall take the crown;
Because into the Faith that makes known
Souls to God, there I entered, and afterward
Peter for its sake circled my brow.
Then a light moved toward us
From that sphere whence came forth the first fruit
That Christ left among his vicars.
And my lady, full of gladness,
Said to me: Look, look, behold the Baron
For whose sake down there Galicia is visited.
Just as when the dove settles
Beside its companion, the one to the other reveals,
Circling and murmuring, its intent;
So I saw the one by the other great
Glorious prince welcomed,
Praising the food on which one dines up there.
But after the greeting had come to an end,
Silent before me each stood fixed,
So aflame that he overpowered my sight.
Smiling then Beatrice said:
Illustrious life, by whom the gladness
Of our basilica was written of,
Make hope resound in this height;
You know that as often you figure it
As Jesus showed greater brightness to the three.
Lift your head, and take courage;
For what comes up here from the mortal world
Must ripen in our rays.
This encouragement from the second fire
Came to me; whereupon I lifted my eyes to the mountains
That had bent them down before with too great a weight.
Since, by grace, it is his will that you come face to face,
Our Emperor's, before your death,
In the most secret hall of his counts;
So that, having seen the truth of this court,
Hope, which down there rightly makes you fall in love,
You may strengthen thereby in yourself and in others;
Say what it is? and how is adorned with its flowers
Your mind? and say whence it came to you?
Thus the second light continued further.
And that pious lady who guided the feathers
Of my wings to so high a flight
Thus forestalled me in answering.
A son the Church militant
Does not have with greater hope, as is written
In the Sun that sheds its rays on all our host;
Therefore it is granted him that out of Egypt
He should come into Jerusalem to see,
Before his soldiering is brought to an end.
The other two points, which not for knowledge
Are asked, but that he may report
How much this virtue pleases you,
To him I leave them, to whom they will not be difficult,
Nor matter for boasting; and let him too answer,
And may the grace of God hearten him in this.
Like the pupil who responds to the teacher,
Prompt and willing, in that in which he is versed,
That his worth may be revealed:
Hope, I said, is a certain expectation
Of future glory, which is brought forth by
Divine grace and preceding merit.
From many stars this light comes to me;
But he first distilled it into my heart
Who was the supreme singer of the foremost Leader.
Let them hope in you, in his song to God
He says to those who know your name:
And who does not know it, if he has my faith?
You let it fall drop by drop into me with your own distilling
In the epistle afterward, so that I am full,
And upon others I rain down again the rain of you both.
While I was speaking, within the living breast
Of that blaze there quivered a flash,
Sudden and frequent, like lightning.
Then it breathed: The love with which I burn
Still for the virtue that followed me
Even to the palm and to my leaving the field
Wills that I breathe again to you, who take delight
In it, and it pleases me that you should say
What Hope promises you.
And I: The new and the ancient Scriptures
Set the goal, and it points it out to me,
Of the souls that God has made his friends.
Isaiah says that each one clothed
In its own land shall be in a double garment,
And its own land is this sweet life.
And your brother, far more fully set forth,
There where he treats of the white robes,
Makes this revelation manifest to us.
And first, near the end of these words,
Let them hope in you was heard above us,
To which all the dancing choirs responded;
Then among them a light grew bright,
Such that if Cancer had such a crystal,
Winter would have a month of a single day.
And as there rises and goes and enters the dance
A glad maiden, solely to do honor
To the bride, not through any fault;
So I saw the brightened splendor
Come to the two who were wheeling round,
As befitted their ardent love.
It joined there in the song and in the measure,
And my lady kept her gaze on them,
Just like a bride silent and still.
This is he who lay upon the breast
Of our Pelican, and this is he who was
From upon the Cross chosen for the great office.
My lady spoke thus; nor for that did she any more
Shift her gaze from its intent watch
After than before her words.
As is one who peers and endeavors
To see the sun eclipsed a little,
Who through looking becomes sightless;
So I became before that last fire,
While it was said: Why do you dazzle yourself
To see a thing that has no place here?
On earth my body is earth, and there it shall be
So long with the others that our number
Shall match the eternal purpose.
With their robes in the blessed cloister
Are the two lights alone that ascended;
And this you shall bear back to your world.
At this voice the flaming round
Grew still, and with it the sweet mingling
That was made in the sound of the threefold breath,
Just as, to avert peril or risk,
The oars, till then striking the water again and again,
All come to rest at the sound of a whistle.
Ah! how shaken I was in mind,
When I turned to see Beatrice,
At not being able to see her, although I was
Beside her, and in the happy world!
