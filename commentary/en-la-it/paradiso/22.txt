Overcome by amazement, to my guide
I turned, like a little child who runs back
Always to where he has most trust.
And she, like a mother who comes to the aid
At once of her pale and panting son
With her voice that is wont to set him right,
Said to me: Do you not know that you are in heaven?
And do you not know that heaven is all holy,
And what is done here comes from good zeal?
How the singing would have transformed you,
And I smiling, you can now imagine,
Since the shout has moved you so much;
In which, had you understood its prayers,
Already the vengeance would be known to you
That you will see before you die.
The sword from up here does not cut in haste,
Nor late, save in the judgment of him
Who, desiring or fearing it, awaits it.
But turn now toward the others,
For many illustrious spirits you will see,
If, as I say, you redirect your gaze.
As it pleased her, I directed my eyes,
And saw a hundred little spheres, which together
Grew more beautiful with one another's rays.
I stood like one who represses within himself
The point of desire, and does not venture
To ask, so much does he fear excess.
And the largest and the most luminous
Of those pearls came forward,
To satisfy with itself my desire.
Then within it I heard: If you could see,
As I do, the love that burns among us,
Your thoughts would be expressed;
But so that you, by waiting, may not be late
For the lofty end, I will give you an answer
Even to the thought of which you are so wary.
That mountain on whose slope Cassino stands
Was frequented once upon its summit
By the people deceived and ill-disposed.
And I am he who first carried up there
The name of Him who brought to earth
The truth that so exalts us;
And so much grace shone upon me
That I drew the surrounding villages away
From the impious worship that seduced the world.
These other fires, all contemplative,
Were men, kindled by that heat
Which makes holy flowers and fruits spring forth.
Here is Macarius, here is Romuald,
Here are my brothers who within the cloisters
Planted their feet and held their hearts steadfast.
And I to him: The affection you show
In speaking with me, and the kindly look
That I see and mark in all your flames,
Have so expanded my confidence
As the sun does the rose, when open
So much it becomes as it has power.
Therefore I pray you, and you, father, assure me,
Whether I may receive so great a grace that I
May see you with your form unveiled.
Whereupon he: Brother, your ardent desire
Will be fulfilled in the last sphere,
Where all the others are fulfilled, and mine.
Here is perfect nature, and whole
Each longing; in this sphere alone
And every part there where it is, always was:
For it is not in space, and has no poles;
And our ladder reaches all the way to it,
Whence it thus steals away from your sight.
As far as up there the patriarch saw it,
Jacob, stretch its uppermost part,
When it appeared to him so laden with angels.
But to climb it now no one lifts
His feet from earth, and my rule
Has remained below to the waste of paper.
The walls that used to be an abbey
Have become dens, and the cowls
Are sacks full of bad flour.
But no usury so grievous rises
Against God's pleasure as does that fruit
Which makes the monks' hearts so mad.
For whatever the Church holds, all
Belongs to the people who ask for God's sake,
Not to kin, nor to anything fouler.
The flesh of mortals is so yielding
That down below a good beginning does not last
From the oak's birth to its bearing the acorn.
Peter began without gold and without silver,
And I with prayers and with fasting,
And Francis humbly, his brotherhood.
And if you look at the beginning of each,
Then look again at where it has strayed,
You will see the white there turned dark.
Truly, for Jordan to turn backward
Was more, and for the sea to flee, when God willed it,
A marvel to see than the succor here.
Thus he spoke to me, and then he withdrew
To his company; and the company drew close,
Then, like a whirlwind, gathered wholly into itself.
The sweet lady then urged me after them,
With a single sign, up that ladder;
So did her power overcome my nature:
Nor ever down here, where one rises and falls,
Was there by nature motion so swift
That it could be equaled to my wing.
So may I ever return, reader, to that devout
Triumph, for the sake of which I often weep for
My sins, and beat my breast,
You could not in that time have drawn out and put
Into the fire your finger, in which I saw the sign
That follows Taurus, and was within it.
O glorious stars, O light pregnant
With great power, from which I acknowledge
All, whatever it may be, of my talent;
With you was rising and with you was setting
He who is father of all mortal life,
When first I felt the Tuscan air;
And then, when grace was granted me
To enter the high wheel that turns you,
Your region was allotted me.
To you devoutly now sighs
My soul, to gain strength
For the difficult passage that draws it to itself.
You are so close to the ultimate salvation,
Beatrice began, that you must
Have your eyes clear and keen.
And therefore, before you enter further into it,
Look down again, and see how much of the world
Beneath your feet I have already set;
So that your heart, as joyful as it can be,
May present itself to the triumphant throng
That comes rejoicing through this rounded ether.
With my gaze I returned through all
The seven spheres, and saw this globe
Such that I smiled at its paltry appearance;
And that judgment I approve as best
Which holds it cheapest; and he who thinks of other things
May be called truly worthy.
I saw Latona's daughter all aflame
Without that shadow which was the cause
Why once I believed her rare and dense.
The sight of your son, Hyperion,
I bore there, and saw how there move
Around and near him Maia and Dione.
From there appeared to me Jove's tempering
Between his father and his son, and from there was clear to me
The variation they make in their positions;
And all seven showed themselves to me,
How great they are, and how swift they are,
And how far apart are their abodes.
The little plot that makes us so fierce,
As I turned with the eternal Twins,
Appeared to me whole, from the hills to the river mouths:
Then I turned my eyes to the beautiful eyes.
