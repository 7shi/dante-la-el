O senseless care of mortals,
How faulty are the syllogisms,
Those that make you beat your wings down low!
One after laws, and another after aphorisms
Went his way, and another pursuing priesthood,
And another to reign by force or by sophisms,
And another to rob, and another in civic business,
Another, in the delight of the flesh enwrapped,
Toiled, and another gave himself to idleness;
When I, from all these things set free,
With Beatrice was up in heaven
So gloriously received.
After each had returned to the
Point of the circle where he had been before,
He stood still like a candle in a candlestick.
And I heard within that light
Which earlier had spoken to me, smiling,
Begin, as it grew purer:
Just as I shine with his ray,
So, gazing into the eternal light,
I discern your thoughts and whence they spring.
You are in doubt, and you wish to have sifted anew,
In language so open and so full,
My saying, that your understanding may discern it,
Where before I said: Where one fattens well,
And there where I said: No second arose;
And here there is need to distinguish well.
Providence, which governs the world
With that counsel by which every gaze
Created is overcome before it reaches the bottom,
In order that toward her beloved might go
The bride of him who, with loud cries,
Wedded her with his blessed blood,
Secure in herself and still more faithful to him,
Ordained two princes in her favor,
Who on this side and on that should be her guides.
The one was wholly seraphic in ardor,
The other, through wisdom, was on earth
A radiance of cherubic light.
Of one I shall speak, because of both
One speaks in praising one, whichever one takes,
Because to one end were their works directed.
Between the Tupino and the water that descends
From the hill chosen by blessed Ubaldo,
A fertile slope of a high mountain hangs,
From which Perugia feels cold and heat
At Porta Sole, and behind it weep
Under a heavy yoke Nocera and Gualdo.
From this slope, there where it breaks
Its steepness most, a sun was born to the world,
As this one rises at times from the Ganges.
Therefore whoever speaks of that place
Should not say Ascesi, for he would say too little,
But Orient, if he wishes to speak precisely.
He was not yet very far from his rising
When he began to make the earth feel
Some comfort from his great virtue;
For, for such a lady, as a youth, into war
With his father he rushed, to whom, as to death,
No one unlocks the door of pleasure;
And before his spiritual court,
And in his father's presence, he was united to her,
Then day by day he loved her more strongly.
She, bereft of her first husband,
For eleven hundred years and more, despised and obscure,
Until this man, remained unwooed;
Nor did it avail to hear that he found her unafraid
With Amyclas, at the sound of his voice,
He who made the whole world afraid;
Nor did it avail her to be steadfast and fierce,
So that, whereas Mary remained below,
She with Christ wept upon the cross.
But so that I may not proceed so obscurely,
Francis and Poverty for these lovers
Take from now on in my lengthy discourse.
Their gladness and their glad countenances,
Love and wonder and sweet looks
Made to be the cause of holy thoughts;
So much so that the venerable Bernard
First went barefoot, and after so great a peace
Ran, and running he seemed to himself slow.
O unknown riches, O fruitful good!
Giles goes barefoot, Sylvester goes barefoot,
After the bridegroom; so pleasing is the bride.
Thence goes that father and that master
With his lady and with that family
Which the humble cord already bound;
Nor did faintness of heart weigh down his brows,
For being the son of Pietro Bernardone,
Nor for appearing so wondrously despised:
But in kingly fashion his austere intent
He laid open to Innocent, and from him received
The first seal upon his religious order.
After the poor little band had grown
In the footsteps of this man, whose wondrous life
Would better be sung in the glory of heaven,
With a second crown wreathed
Was through Honorius by the eternal Spirit
The holy will of this archimandrite.
And after, through thirst for martyrdom,
In the proud presence of the Sultan
He preached Christ and the others who followed him;
And finding for conversion unripe
Too much the people, so as not to remain in vain,
He returned to the fruit of the Italian field;
On the rugged rock between Tiber and Arno
From Christ he received the last seal,
Which his limbs bore for two years.
When to him who destined him for so great a good
It pleased to draw him up to the reward
Which he earned in making himself small,
To his friars, as to rightful heirs,
He commended his dearest lady,
And commanded that they love her faithfully;
And from her lap his illustrious soul
Wished to depart, returning to its kingdom,
And for his body he wished no other bier.
Consider now what manner of man he was who a worthy
Colleague was in keeping the bark
Of Peter on the high sea on a true course!
And this was our Patriarch;
Wherefore whoever follows him, as he commands,
You can discern that he loads good merchandise.
But his flock for new food
Has grown so greedy that it cannot be
That it should not scatter through diverse pastures;
And by how much his sheep, remote
And wandering, go farther from him,
The emptier of milk they return to the fold.
There are indeed some that fear the harm
And draw close to the shepherd; but they are so few
That little cloth furnishes their cloaks.
Now, if my words are not faint,
And if your hearing has been attentive,
If you recall to mind what I have said,
In part your wish will be satisfied;
For you will see the tree from which the splinter comes,
You will see what the qualification means:
Where one fattens well, if one does not stray.
