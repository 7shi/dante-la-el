The world used to believe, to its peril,
that the fair Cyprian mad love
beamed forth, turning in the third epicycle;
for not to her alone did they pay honor
of sacrifice and of votive cry,
the peoples of old in their ancient error;
but Dione they honored, and Cupid,
the one as her mother, the other as her son,
and they said that he sat in Dido's lap;
and from her from whom I take my beginning
they took the name of the star
that the sun woos now from behind, now from in front.
I did not perceive my ascent into it;
but that I was within it was sufficiently attested to me
by my lady, whom I saw grow more beautiful.
And as in a flame a spark is seen,
and as in a voice a voice is discerned,
when one is steady and the other comes and goes,
I saw in that light other lamps
moving in a circle, some swifter, some slower,
according, I believe, to their eternal visions.
From a cold cloud no winds descended,
whether visible or not, so swiftly
that they would not have seemed hampered and slow
to one who had those divine lights
seen coming toward us, leaving the round
first begun among the high Seraphim.
And behind those who appeared farthest in front,
Hosanna resounded so that ever thereafter
I was not without desire to hear it again.
Then one drew nearer to us,
and alone began: We are all ready
at your pleasure, that you may have joy of us.
We turn with the celestial princes
in one circle, with one turning and one thirst,
to whom you once said in the world:
You who by understanding move the third heaven;
and we are so full of love that, to please you,
no less sweet will be a little rest.
After my eyes had been offered
to my lady in reverence, and she
had made them content with her and sure,
they turned again to the light that had promised
itself so freely, and in saying: Who are you? was
my voice stamped with great affection.
And how much greater and how changed I saw it grow
through the new gladness that was added,
when I spoke, to its beauties!
Thus made (it told me), the world had me
below a little while; and had I stayed longer,
much evil will be that would not be.
My gladness keeps me hidden from you,
for it rays out around me and conceals me
like a creature wrapped in its own silk.
Much you loved me, and well you had cause;
for had I stayed below, I would have shown you
of my love more than the leaves.
That left bank which is washed
by the Rhône after it has mingled with the Sorgue
awaited me in due time as its lord;
and that horn of Ausonia which is studded with the towns
of Bari, of Gaeta and of Crotone,
there where the Tronto and the Verde pour into the sea.
Already on my brow shone the crown
of that land which the Danube waters
after it leaves the German banks.
And fair Trinacria, which grows dim with smoke
between Pachynus and Pelorus, above the gulf
that receives from Eurus the greatest buffeting,
not through Typhoeus, but through rising sulfur,
would still have awaited its kings,
sprung through me from Charles and from Rudolf,
if bad lordship, which always grieves
the peoples subject to it, had not
moved Palermo to cry: Let him die, let him die.
And if my brother foresaw this,
the greedy poverty of Catalonia
he would already shun, lest it harm him;
for truly provision must be made
by him or by another, so that on his boat,
laden, no more load be laid.
His nature, which, stingy, from a generous one
descended, would have need of such soldiery
as would not care to lay up treasure in a chest.
Because I believe that the high gladness
which your speech pours into me, O my lord,
there where every good ends and begins,
is seen by you as I see it,
more grateful is it to me; and this too I hold dear,
because you discern it gazing into God.
You have made me glad, and likewise make clear to me,
since, in speaking, you have moved me to doubt,
how from sweet seed bitter can spring.
This I said to him. And he to me: If I can
show you a truth, toward that of which you ask
you will turn your face as you turn your back.
The Good that all the realm you climb
turns and contents makes a power
of its providence in these great bodies;
and not only natures foreseen
are in the Mind that is of itself perfect,
but they together with their well-being.
Wherefore whatever this bow shoots
falls disposed to a foreseen end,
just as a thing directed at its mark.
Were this not so, the heaven you traverse
would so produce its effects
that they would be not works of art but ruins;
and this cannot be if the intellects
that move these stars are not defective,
and defective the First, who has not perfected them.
Would you have this truth become clearer to you?
And I: Not so, for I see it is impossible
that nature, in what is needful, should fail.
Whereupon he again: Now say, would it be worse
for man on earth if he were not a citizen?
Yes, I replied; and here I ask no reason.
And can he be one, if below men do not live
in different ways for different offices?
No, if your master writes rightly for you.
Thus he came reasoning down to this point;
then he concluded: Therefore to be diverse
behooves the roots of your effects:
hence one is born a Solon and another a Xerxes,
another a Melchizedek, and another the one
who, flying through the air, lost his son.
The circling nature, which is a seal
upon mortal wax, does its work well,
but does not distinguish one dwelling from another.
Hence it comes about that Esau differs
in seed from Jacob, and Quirinus comes
from so base a father that he is ascribed to Mars.
Begotten nature its course
would always make like that of those who begot it,
if divine providence did not prevail.
Now what was behind you is before you;
but that you may know that I delight in you,
I wish a corollary to mantle you.
Always nature, if it finds fortune
at variance with itself, like any other seed
outside its own region, fares ill.
And if the world down there gave heed
to the foundation that nature lays,
following it, it would have good people.
But you bend to the religious life
one who will have been born to gird on the sword,
and make a king of one who is fit for preaching;
hence your track is off the road.
