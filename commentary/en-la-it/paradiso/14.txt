From the center to the circle, and from the circle to the center,
the water moves in a round vessel,
according as it is struck without or within.
Into my mind suddenly fell
this that I say, as soon as there fell silent
the glorious life of Thomas,
through the likeness that arose
from his speaking and from that of Beatrice,
whom it pleased, after him, thus to begin:
This man has need, and he does not tell you so
either with his voice or even yet in thought,
to go to the root of another truth.
Tell him whether the light with which is bedecked
your substance will remain with you
eternally, just as it is now;
and, if it remains, say how, once
you are made visible again,
it can be that it does not trouble you in seeing.
As, impelled and drawn by greater gladness,
at times those who go in a ring
raise their voices and make their movements merrier,
so at the ready and devout prayer
the holy circles showed fresh joy
in their wheeling and in their wondrous song.
Whoever laments that here one must die
to live up there has not seen there
the refreshment of the eternal rain.
That one and two and three who ever lives,
and ever reigns in three and two and one,
uncircumscribed, and circumscribes all,
three times was sung by each
of those spirits with such melody
that for any merit it would be a just reward.
And I heard in the divinest voice
of the smaller circle a modest voice,
perhaps such as was the angel's to Mary,
answer: As long as shall last the long feast
of Paradise, so long shall our love
radiate thus about us in such a garment.
Its brightness will follow the ardor,
the ardor the vision; and that is as great
as the grace it has beyond its own worth.
As soon as the glorious and holy flesh
is put on again, our person
will be more pleasing for being whole.
Wherefore there will increase what is given us
of freely granted light by the highest Good;
light that fits us to see him:
whence the vision must increase,
increase the ardor that is kindled by it,
increase the radiance that comes from that ardor.
But just as a coal that gives forth flame,
and by its vivid whiteness outshines it
so that it preserves its own appearance,
so this splendor that already encircles us
will be surpassed in visibility by the flesh
that the earth still covers;
nor will so great a light be able to weary us,
for the organs of the body will be strong
enough for all that can delight us.
So quick and alert did they seem to me,
both the one choir and the other, to say Amen,
that they plainly showed desire for their dead bodies;
perhaps not for themselves alone, but for their mothers,
for their fathers, and for the others who were dear
before they became everlasting flames.
And behold, all around, of equal brightness,
a radiance arising over what was there,
like a horizon that is brightening.
And just as at the rising of early evening
new appearances begin across the sky,
so that the thing seems and seems not real,
it seemed to me that there new beings
I began to see, and to see them make a ring
outside the other two circumferences.
O true sparkling of the Holy Spirit,
how sudden and incandescent it became
to my eyes, which, overcome, could not bear it!
But Beatrice, so beautiful and smiling,
showed herself to me that among those sights
she must be left which the mind did not follow.
Thence my eyes regained the power
to raise themselves again, and I saw myself translated
alone with my lady into higher blessedness.
Well did I perceive that I was raised higher,
by the fiery smile of the star,
which seemed to me redder than was its wont.
With all my heart, and with that speech
which is one in all, to God I made a burnt offering,
such as befitted the new grace;
and not yet was spent from my breast
the ardor of the sacrifice when I knew
that very offering to have been accepted and auspicious;
for with such brilliance and so ruddy
did splendors appear to me within two rays
that I said: O Helios, who so adorn them!
As, picked out with lesser and greater
lights, there gleams white between the poles of the world
the Galaxy, such that it makes even sages doubt,
so, studded with stars, did those rays form in the depths
of Mars the venerable sign
that the joinings of quadrants make in a circle.
Here my memory surpasses my wit;
for that cross flashed forth Christ,
so that I cannot find a worthy likeness.
But whoever takes up his cross and follows Christ
will yet excuse me for what I leave out,
seeing that whiteness flash forth Christ.
From arm to arm, and between the top and the foot,
lights moved, sparkling brightly
in joining together and in passing one another.
Thus are seen here, straight and crooked,
swift and slow, changing their appearance,
the minute particles of bodies, long and short,
moving through the ray with which is streaked
at times the shade that for their protection
people procure by ingenuity and art.
And as a fiddle and a harp, with the tuned tension
of sweet strings, make a sweet ringing
for one by whom the tune is not understood,
so from the lights that appeared to me there
there gathered along the cross a melody
that enraptured me without my understanding the hymn.
Well did I perceive that it was of lofty praise,
because there came to me: Rise again and conquer;
as to one who does not understand and hears.
I grew so enamored of this
that until then there had been nothing
that bound me with such sweet bonds.
Perhaps my word seems too bold,
setting second the delight of the beautiful eyes,
gazing into which my desire has rest.
But whoever perceives that the living seals
of all beauty work the more the higher they are,
and that I had not yet turned back to those eyes,
can excuse me for that of which I accuse myself
to excuse myself, and see that I speak the truth:
for the holy delight is not here excluded,
since it grows, as one ascends, purer.
