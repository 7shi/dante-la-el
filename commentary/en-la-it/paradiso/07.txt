Hosanna, holy God of hosts,
Illumining from above with your brightness
The happy fires of these realms!
Thus, turning to its wheel,
That substance seemed to me to sing,
Upon which a double light is doubled:
And it and the others moved to their dance,
And, like the swiftest sparks,
They veiled themselves from me by sudden distance.
I was in doubt, and was saying: Tell her, tell her,
To myself; tell her, I was saying, to my lady,
Who quenches my thirst with sweet drops;
But that reverence which takes possession
Of all of me, just at Be and at Ice,
Bowed me down like a man growing drowsy.
Therefore Beatrice bore with me thus,
And began, beaming on me with a smile
Such as would make a man happy in the fire:
According to my infallible judgment,
How just vengeance justly
Could have been punished, you have set yourself to ponder;
But I shall soon unbind your mind:
And you listen, for my words
Will make you a gift of great import.
For not suffering his willing faculty
A bridle for its good, that man who was not born,
Damning himself, damned all his offspring;
Whence the human race on earth lay
Low for many centuries in great error,
Until it pleased the divine Word to descend,
Where the nature that from its Maker
Had grown distant he united to himself in person
By the act alone of his eternal love.
Now turn your gaze to what is now being reasoned:
This nature, united to its Maker,
As it was created, was pure and good;
But by its own doing it was afterward banished
From Paradise, because it turned aside
From the way of truth and from its life.
The punishment, then, that the Cross inflicted,
If it is measured against the nature assumed,
None ever bit so justly;
And likewise none was so great a wrong,
Looking to the person who suffered,
In whom that nature was bound up.
Thus from one act came different things;
For one death pleased God and the Jews:
At it the earth trembled and heaven opened.
It should no longer seem hard to you,
When it is said that just vengeance
Was afterward avenged by a just court.
But now I see your mind drawn tight
From thought to thought within a knot,
From which with great desire it waits to be loosed.
You say: I discern well what I hear;
But why God should have willed, is hidden from me,
For our redemption this very way.
This decree, brother, lies buried
From the eyes of everyone whose understanding
Has not grown to maturity in the flame of love.
Yet, because at this mark
Much is aimed, and little is discerned,
I shall say why such a way was more worthy.
Divine goodness, which spurns from itself
All envy, burning in itself sparkles
So that it unfolds the eternal beauties.
That which distils from it without intermediary
Has thereafter no end, because there is no shifting
Of its imprint when it sets its seal.
That which rains down from it without intermediary
Is wholly free, because it is not subject
To the power of things newly made.
It is more like it, and therefore pleases it more;
For the holy ardor, which irradiates all things,
Is more alive in that which is more like it.
In all these things excels
The human creature; and if one is lacking,
From its nobility it must fall.
Sin alone is what unfre es it,
And makes it unlike the highest good,
So that it is little whitened by its light;
And to its dignity it never returns,
Unless it fills what guilt empties,
Countering evil pleasure with just pains.
Your nature, when it sinned entire
In its seed, from these dignities,
As from Paradise, was removed;
Nor could it be recovered, if you consider
Very closely, by any way,
Without passing by one of these steps:
That God alone, by his graciousness,
Should have forgiven, or that man by himself
Should have made satisfaction for his folly.
Fix now your eye deep within the abyss
Of the eternal counsel, as far as you can,
Closely intent upon my words.
Man could not within his limits
Ever make satisfaction, being unable to go down
In humility, by obeying afterward,
As far as by disobeying he meant to go up;
And this is the reason why man was
Shut out from being able to make satisfaction by himself.
Therefore it fell to God, by his ways,
To restore man to his full life,
By one, I mean, or else by both.
But because the work is so much the more esteemed
In the worker, the more it shows
Of the goodness of the heart whence it came;
Divine goodness, which imprints the world,
To proceed by all its ways,
To raise you up again, was pleased.
Nor between the last night and the first day
So lofty or so magnificent a course,
Either by the one or by the other, has there been or shall there be.
For God was more generous in giving himself,
To make man sufficient to raise himself again,
Than if he had merely forgiven of himself:
And all the other ways fell short
Of justice, if the Son of God
Had not humbled himself to become incarnate.
Now, to fulfill entirely your every desire,
I return to make clear a certain point,
So that you may see there as I do.
You say: I see the air, and I see the fire,
The water, and the earth and all their mixtures
Come to corruption and last but a little;
And yet these things were creatures:
Wherefore, if what I have said was true,
They ought to be safe from corruption.
The angels, brother, and the pure realm
In which you are, may be called created,
Just as they are, in their entire being;
But the elements which you have named,
And those things which are made of them,
By created power are given form.
Created was the matter that they have,
Created was the power that gives form
In these stars that go around them.
And the soul of every beast and of the plants
From a mixture endowed with potential draw
The ray and the motion of the holy lights.
Your life without intermediary is breathed forth by
The highest goodness, and it makes that life enamored
Of itself, so that thereafter it always desires it.
And from this you may infer also
Your resurrection, if you consider again
How human flesh was made at the time
When the first parents were both made.
