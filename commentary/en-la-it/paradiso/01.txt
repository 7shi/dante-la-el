The glory of him who moves all things
penetrates through the universe, and shines
in one part more, and less elsewhere.
In the heaven that takes most of his light
I was, and saw a thing that to tell again
one who descends from up there neither knows how nor is able;
for, drawing near to its desire,
our intellect plunges so deep
that memory cannot go after it.
Yet as much as I of the holy kingdom
in my mind could treasure up
will now be the matter of my song.
O good Apollo, for the last labor
make me such a vessel of your power
as the bestowal of the beloved laurel demands.
Until now the one peak of Parnassus
was enough for me, but now with both
I must enter the arena that remains.
Enter into my breast, and breathe, you,
just as when you drew Marsyas
from the sheath of his limbs.
O divine power, if you lend yourself to me
so far that the shadow of the blessed kingdom
imprinted in my head I may make manifest,
you will see me at the foot of your cherished tree
come, and crown myself with the leaves
of which the matter and you will make me worthy.
So rarely, father, are any gathered
for the triumph of either Caesar or poet,
the fault and shame of human desires,
that to bring forth joy in the joyful
Delphic deity ought the bough
of Peneus, when it makes anyone thirst for it.
A little spark a great flame follows:
perhaps after me with better voices
prayer will be made that Cirrha may answer.
There rises for mortals from different mouths
the lamp of the world; but from that one
which joins four circles with three crosses,
with a better course and with a better star
it comes forth conjoined, and the worldly wax
it tempers and seals more after its own fashion.
It had made morning there, and evening here,
almost that very mouth; and all white there was
that hemisphere, and the other part black;
when Beatrice toward her left side
I saw turned, and gazing into the sun:
no eagle ever fixed its gaze on it so.
And just as a second ray is wont
to issue from the first, and rise back upward,
just like a pilgrim who wishes to return,
so from her act, through my eyes infused
into my imagination, my own was formed,
and I fixed my eyes on the sun beyond our wont.
Much is permitted there that here is not permitted
to our powers, by virtue of the place
made as the proper home of the human race.
I did not endure it long, nor yet so briefly
that I did not see it spark on every side,
as glowing iron comes out of the fire.
And suddenly it seemed to me that day to day
was added, as if he who can
had adorned the sky with another sun.
Beatrice, wholly on the eternal wheels
fixed with her eyes, remained; and I on her
fixed my eyes, withdrawn from up there.
In looking at her I became such within
as Glaucus became on tasting the herb
that made him a companion in the sea of the other gods.
To pass beyond the human, to signify in words
one could not; therefore let the example suffice
for one for whom grace reserves the experience.
Whether I was only that of me which you created
last, O Love who govern heaven,
you know, who with your light raised me.
When the wheel which you keep forever turning,
yourself desired, made me attentive to it
with the harmony you tune and distinguish,
so much of the sky then seemed to me kindled
with the flame of the sun that rain or river
never made any lake so broad.
The newness of the sound and the great light
kindled in me a desire to know their cause
never felt with such keenness.
Whereupon she, who saw me as I saw myself,
to quiet my troubled spirit,
before I could ask, opened her mouth.
And she began: You yourself make yourself dull
by false imagining, so that you do not see
what you would see if you had shaken it off.
You are not on earth, as you believe;
never does lightning, fleeing its first place,
does not run as you do who now return.
If of my first doubt I was stripped
by the brief little words spoken with a smile,
in a new one I was the more ensnared;
and I said: Already, content, I have found rest
from great wonder; but now I wonder
how I pass beyond these light bodies.
Whereupon she, after a gentle sigh,
turned her eyes toward me, with that look
a mother gives her delirious child;
and she began: All things whatsoever
have order among themselves; and this is the form
that makes the universe like God.
Here the higher creatures see the imprint
of the eternal power, which is the end
for which the rule just mentioned is made.
In the order of which I speak are inclined
all natures, by differing lots,
more or less near to their origin;
whence they move to different ports
over the great sea of being, and each
with an instinct given it to carry it.
This carries fire away toward the moon;
this in mortal hearts is the mover;
this binds and gathers the earth into itself.
Nor only the creatures that are without
intelligence does this bow shoot forth,
but those that have intellect and love.
Providence, which orders so much,
makes with its light the heaven forever still
within which turns the one that has the greatest haste.
And now there, as to a place decreed,
the power of that bowstring carries us away
which directs what it shoots to a joyful mark.
It is true that, as form does not accord
many times with the intention of art,
because the matter is deaf to its bidding;
so from this course there departs
at times the creature that has the power
to bend, though thus impelled, in another direction,
and so, as one may see falling
fire from a cloud, if the first impulse
is twisted toward earth by false pleasure.
You should marvel no more, if I judge well,
at your ascent than at a stream
if from a high mountain it descends to the depths.
The marvel in you would be if, free
of impediment, you had sat down below,
as on earth a living fire at rest would be.
Then she turned her face toward heaven.
