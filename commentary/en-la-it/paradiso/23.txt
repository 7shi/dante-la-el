As the bird, among the beloved boughs,
settled at the nest of its sweet young,
in the night that hides things from us,
which, to see the longed-for faces,
and to find the food with which to feed them,
wherein heavy labors are ease to it,
forestalls the hour upon the open branch,
and with ardent affection awaits the sun,
gazing steadfastly, if only dawn would break;
so my Lady stood erect
and intent, turned toward the region
beneath which the sun makes less haste;
so that, seeing her expectant and eager,
I became like one who, desiring,
would have something more, and, hoping, is content.
But little time lay between the one moment and the other,
that of my waiting, I mean, and that of seeing
the sky grow clearer and clearer.
And Beatrice said: Behold the hosts
of the triumph of Christ, and all the fruit
gathered from the turning of these spheres.
It seemed to me that her face was all aflame,
and her eyes she had so full of joy
that I must pass on without telling it.
As at the full moons and in clear skies
Trivia smiles among the eternal nymphs
that paint the sky in all its recesses,
I saw, above thousands of lamps,
a Sun that kindled every one of them,
as ours does the lights on high;
and through the living light there shone
the shining substance, so bright
that my sight could not sustain it.
O Beatrice, guide sweet and dear!
She said to me: That which overmasters you
is power from which nothing can shield itself.
Here are the wisdom and the might
that opened the roads from heaven to earth,
for which there once was such long yearning.
As fire breaks free of a cloud
through expanding, so that it no longer fits within,
and, contrary to its nature, falls earthward;
my mind thus, among those feasts
made greater, went out of itself,
and what it became it cannot remember.
Open your eyes, and look upon me as I am;
you have seen things such that able
you have become to endure my smile.
I was like one who comes to himself
from a forgotten vision, and who endeavors
in vain to bring it back to mind,
when I heard this offer, worthy
of such gratitude that it is never erased
from the book that records the past.
If now there sounded all those tongues
that Polyhymnia with her sisters made
richer with their sweetest milk,
to help me, a thousandth of the truth
would not be reached in singing the holy smile
and how radiant the holy countenance made it.
And thus, in depicting Paradise,
the sacred poem must leap,
like one who finds his path cut off.
But whoever thought of the weighty theme
and the mortal shoulder that loads itself with it
would not blame it if beneath it it trembles.
It is no passage for a little boat,
this that the daring prow goes cleaving,
nor for a helmsman who would spare himself.
Why does my face so enamor you
that you do not turn to the fair garden
that beneath the rays of Christ breaks into flower?
There is the rose in which the divine Word
became flesh; and there are the lilies
at whose fragrance the fair path was taken.
Thus Beatrice. And I, who to her counsels
was wholly ready, once more gave myself
to the battle of my feeble eyes.
As by a ray of sun that streams pure
through a broken cloud, a meadow of flowers once
my eyes saw, themselves covered in shade;
so I saw many throngs of splendors
lit from above by burning rays,
without seeing the source of the flashes.
O benign power, who so imprints them,
so high did you raise yourself to grant room
to my eyes there, which lacked the strength.
The name of the fair flower that I always invoke
both morning and evening wholly drew
my mind to gaze upon the greater fire.
And as soon as upon both my eyes were painted
the quality and the magnitude of the living star
that conquers up there as it conquered down here,
down through the sky came a little flame,
formed into a circle in the manner of a crown,
and it girdled her and turned about her.
Whatever melody sounds sweetest
down here, and most draws the soul to itself,
would seem a cloud that, rent, thunders,
compared to the sound of that lyre
with which was crowned the fair sapphire
with which the brightest heaven is ensapphired.
I am angelic love, who circle
the lofty joy that breathes from the womb
that was the dwelling of our desire;
and I shall circle, Lady of heaven, while
you follow your Son and make more divine
the highest sphere because you enter there.
Thus the circling melody
sealed itself, and all the other lights
it made resound with the name of Mary.
The royal mantle of all the revolving spheres
of the world, which burns most and is most quickened
in the breath of God and in his ways,
had above us its inner shore
so distant that its appearance,
there where I was, was not yet visible to me.
Therefore my eyes had not the power
to follow the crowned flame
that rose after her offspring.
And as the little child who toward its mother
stretches its arms after it has taken milk,
through the feeling that flames up even outwardly,
each of those white radiances stretched upward
with its tip, so that the deep affection
they bore for Mary was plain to me.
Then they remained there in my sight,
singing Queen of Heaven so sweetly
that the delight never departs from me.
O how great is the abundance that is stored
in those richest chests, which were,
for sowing down here, good plowmen!
There one lives and rejoices in the treasure
that was gained weeping in the exile
of Babylon, where the gold was left behind.
There triumphs, beneath the exalted Son
of God and of Mary, in his victory,
with both the old and the new assembly,
he who holds the keys of such glory.
