A kindly will, in which melts
always the love that breathes aright,
as cupidity does in the wicked will,
put to silence that sweet lyre,
and made the holy strings grow still,
which the right hand of heaven slackens and draws taut.
How shall they be deaf to just prayers,
those substances which, to give me the desire
that I should pray to them, were of one accord in falling silent?
It is right that he should grieve without end
who, for love of a thing that does not last
eternally, strips himself of that love.
As through the tranquil and pure clear skies
there courses from time to time a sudden fire,
stirring the eyes that are at rest,
and it seems a star that changes place,
save that from the part where it is kindled
nothing is lost, and it lasts but a little;
so, from the arm that stretches to the right,
to the foot of that Cross ran a star
of the constellation that shines there;
nor did the gem leave its ribbon,
but along the radiant strip it sped,
so that it seemed fire behind alabaster.
So lovingly did the shade of Anchises come forward,
if our greater muse merits belief,
when in Elysium he caught sight of his son.
O blood of mine! O poured from above
grace of God, as upon thee, to whom
was heaven's gate ever opened twice!
Thus that light; whereupon I gave heed to him,
then turned my face to my lady,
and on this side and on that I was astonished;
for within her eyes burned a smile
such that I thought with mine to touch the depths
of my grace and of my paradise.
Then, delightful to hear and to see,
the spirit added to his opening words things
that I did not understand him, so profoundly did he speak.
Nor by choice did he hide himself from me,
but of necessity, for his conception
rose above the mark of mortals.
And when the bow of ardent affection
was so far discharged that his speech descended
toward the mark of our intellect,
the first thing that was understood by me
was: Blessed be thou, three and one,
who to my seed art so gracious.
And he continued: A welcome and long-standing fast,
drawn out by reading in the greater volume
where neither white nor dark ever changes,
thou hast broken, son, within this light
in which I speak to thee, thanks to her
who clothed thee in feathers for the lofty flight.
Thou believest that to me thy thought flows
from Him who is first, just as there ray forth
from one, if it be known, five and six.
And therefore who I am, or why I seem
more joyous to thee, thou dost not ask me,
than any other in this joyous throng.
Thou believest the truth, for lesser and greater
in this life gaze into the mirror
in which, before thou thinkest, thou dost disclose thy thought.
But that the sacred love, in which I keep watch
with perpetual sight, and which makes me thirst
with sweet desire, may be more fully fulfilled,
thy voice, sure, bold, and glad,
let sound forth the will, let sound forth the desire,
to which my answer is already decreed.
I turned to Beatrice, and she heard
before I spoke, and smiled me a sign
that made wings grow upon my will.
Then I began thus: Affection and understanding,
as soon as the primal Equality appeared to you,
became of one weight for each of you;
for in the Sun that lit and kindled you
with heat and with light, these are so equal
that all likenesses fall short.
But will and the power to give it utterance in mortals,
for the reason that is manifest to you,
are differently feathered in their wings.
Hence I, who am mortal, feel myself in this
inequality, and therefore do not give thanks
save with my heart for the fatherly welcome.
Yet I do beseech thee, living topaz,
who dost begem this precious jewel,
that thou make me satisfied as to thy name.
O leaf of mine, in whom I took delight
even in waiting, I was thy root:
such a beginning, in answering, he made to me.
Then he said to me: He from whom is named
thy kindred, and who for a hundred years and more
has circled the mountain on the first ledge,
was my son, and was thy great-grandfather;
it is indeed fitting that the long toil
thou shouldst shorten for him with thy works.
Florence, within the ancient ring of walls
whence she still takes tierce and none,
dwelt in peace, sober and chaste.
There was no little chain, no coronet,
no ladies in finery, no girdle
that was more to look at than the person.
Not yet did she, at birth, bring fear,
the daughter to the father, for the time and the dowry
did not depart, this way and that, from due measure.
There were no houses empty of family;
Sardanapalus had not yet come there
to show what may be done in the chamber.
Not yet was Montemalo surpassed
by your Uccellatoio, which, as Montemalo is surpassed
in the ascent, so shall surpass it in the decline.
Bellincion Berti I saw go girt
with leather and bone, and come to the mirror
his lady with her face unpainted;
and I saw him of the Nerli and him of the Vecchio
be content with plain hide,
and their ladies with the spindle and the flax.
O fortunate women! Each was certain
of her burial place, and as yet none
was for France's sake deserted in her bed.
One kept watch in care of the cradle,
and, soothing, used the speech
that first delights mothers and fathers;
another, drawing the tresses from the distaff,
told tales with her household
of the Trojans, of Fiesole, and of Rome.
As great a marvel would have been held then
a Cianghella, a Lapo Saltarello,
as now would be a Cincinnatus or a Cornelia.
To so tranquil, to so fair
a life of citizens, to so faithful
a citizenry, to so sweet a dwelling,
Mary gave me, invoked with loud cries,
and in your ancient Baptistery
at once I became a Christian and Cacciaguida.
Moronto was my brother, and Eliseo;
my lady came to me from the valley of the Po,
and thence thy surname was formed.
Then I followed the emperor Conrad,
and he girded me with his knighthood,
so greatly through good deeds did I come into his favor.
Behind him I went against the wickedness
of that folk whose people usurps,
through the fault of the shepherds, what is ours by right.
There was I by that foul folk
disentangled from the deceitful world,
the love of which disfigures many souls,
and I came from martyrdom to this peace.
