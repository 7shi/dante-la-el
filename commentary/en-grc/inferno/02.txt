The day was passing away, and all
The living things upon the earth the dusky air
Was withdrawing from their toils; while I alone
Was preparing to endure the burden
Both of the journey and of the soul's suffering,
Which now my faithful memory shall describe.
O Muses, O divine spirit, now give aid;
And O memory, in which is written all that I saw,
Now shall your noble eloquence be shown.
„Poet, my guide,“ I began to say,
„See whether my worth is strong enough,
„Before I proceed upon the dread journey.
„You say that the grandfather of Sylvius went,
„While still mortal, into the immortals'
„ Realm, having the senses of a living man.
„ Yet if the adversary of all wickedness
„ Was gracious to him, having in mind the great benefits
„ To issue from him, both who and what they were,
„ This does not seem unseemly to a prudent man.
„ For he of august Rome and of the Empire
„ Was chosen father in the ether of the Heavens;
„ And Rome and the Empire, to speak aright,
„ Were foreordained as a holy place,
„ Where the successor of Peter has his seat.
„ From this journey, which you celebrated in song,
„ He learned beforehand all that was the cause
„ Of his victory and of the Papal mantle.
„ Thither came afterwards the vessel of Election,
„ To strengthen that faith,
„ Which is the beginning of the saving path.
„ But I, how do I go? and who permits it?
„ For I am not Æneas, nor indeed am I Paul;
„ Neither another, nor I myself, judges me worthy.
„ If therefore I consent to come,
„I fear lest I dare a foolish deed.
„You are wise and discern whether I speak well.”
And as one who no longer wills what he willed,
And changing his mind through new purposes
Abandons the undertaking he had begun,
Such was I upon that gloomy ridge;
For on reflecting I turned away from that which I had begun,
The enterprise, with such eagerness.
“If I have rightly understood what you have spoken,”
Replied the shade of the great-souled one,
“Fear has rendered your soul worthless,
„Which so often presses hard upon a man
„That it turns him away from every noble deed,
„Like a false sight making a horse shy.
„To rid you of this so great fear of yours,
„I will tell you whence and on learning what I came hither,
„When first I was grieved for your troubles.
„While I was among those who are in suspense here,
„A fair and blessed lady called me,
„Whose commands I eagerly asked for.
„With eyes more radiant than the stars,
„She began to speak, gentle and sweet,
„Addressing me with angelic voice.”—
—“O gracious soul of Mantua,
„Whose renown still endures upon the earth,
„And shall endure as long as the universe moves:
„A certain friend of mine, though not a friend of fortune,
„Is so hindered in the desert places
„Along his way, that out of fear he turns back;
„And there is fear lest he has already gone astray,
„And I have come too late to his succour,
„From what I heard in the heavens concerning him.
„Come now, bestir yourself, and with your fair speech,
„And with all the means whereby he may be saved,
„Assist him, and become a helper to me.
„I am Beatrice, and I send you forth;
„I come from a place whither I long to return.
„Love moved me, and inspires my words.
„When I shall stand before my Lord,
„I will often speak well of you to him.”—
—“She fell silent, and I began.”
“O lady, possessor of the sole gift,
„Through which the human race surpasses
„All things in the lesser-wheeled heaven:
„So pleasing to me is your command,
„That even in obeying at once I seem to linger.
„You need not express your wishes further;
„But tell me, how did you descend hither
„Into this lowly centre, leaving
„The spacious realm, whither you wish to return.”—
—“Since you wish to know all things deeply,
„I will tell you briefly,” she said,
“Why I do not fear to descend hither.
„Those things alone ought every man to fear,
„Which have power to bring him harm;
„But not the others; for these are not to be feared.
„Such have I been made by divine grace,
„That your wretchedness harms me not at all,
„Nor does any flame of this fire touch me.
„In Heaven there is a gracious Lady,
„ Who pities the man to whom I send you,
„ So as to bend the stern judgments on high.
„ She, having summoned Lucia,
„ Said: ‘“Now your faithful one has need of you;
„ And I also warmly commend him to you.”’
“Lucia, foe of all the hard-hearted,
„ Immediately arose, and came to the place where
„ I was sitting with ancient Rachel;
„ She said: ‘“Beatrice, true praise of God,
„ Why do you not help him who loved you,
„ And for your sake came out from the vulgar crowd?
„ Do you not hear now his piteous laments?
„ Do you not see him battling with death
„ Upon a river more terrible than the sea?”’
“No one ever in the world, hunting for gain,
„ Or fleeing from harm, ran so swiftly
„ As I did when I heard such words.
„ Hither I came down from the seat of the blessed,
„ Putting my trust in your noble speech,
„ Which honours both you and those who have heard you.”—
—“Having said these things, her eyes with tears
„ Shining she turned toward me, and this
„ Hastened my coming hither.
„ And coming to you, as she urged,
„ I saved you from the beast, which, to the mountain
„ That is fair, hindered your approach.
„ What then? why, why do you tarry?
„ Why are you so faint-hearted?
„ Why have you neither daring nor boldness,
„ While three such blessed Ladies
„ Care for you in the heavens,
„ And my words promise you such good things?”
Just as the flower droops from the nightly frost,
And remains closed; then when the sun rises,
Opening upon its stem it stands upright;
So I felt myself refreshed;
And so much courage came upon me,
That I answered like a brave man.
“O compassionate soul, my protectress,
„ And you, kindly one, who swiftly obeyed
„The faithful words with which she urged you on!
„ So with your exhortations into the heart
„ You have instilled in me longing to go forward,
„ That I have returned to my first purpose.
„ Come now, let one will be ours;
„ You my guide, you my lord, and you my master.“
These things I said to him; and then he went forward,
And I entered upon the savage way.
