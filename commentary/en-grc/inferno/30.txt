At the time when Juno was wroth
Because of Semele against the Thebans'
Race, as she proved it many times,
Athamas became so maddened,
That, seeing his wife coming forth,
And leading with her hands the two children,
He cried out: “Let us set the nets, so that
„ I may catch the lioness and the lion-cubs.”
Then he stretched out his pitiless hands,
And seizing Learchus, one of the boys,
And whirling him round, dashed him against a rock;
And the mother drowned herself with the other.
And at the time when Fortune humbled
The daring Trojan height, and there vanished
Both the king and his kingdom together,
Wretched, miserable, captive Hecuba,
Seeing her daughter Polyxena dead,
And her son Polydorus along the sea's
Shore dead having sorrowfully discovered,
Wholly frenzied, barked like a dog:
So out of her wits through grief did she go!
But neither Furies of Thebes nor Trojan ones
Ever showed such hardness of heart,
As not even to prick beasts, much less human beings,
Such as when I saw two pale, naked shades,
Who biting ran in such a manner,
As a hog, when it rushes out from the sty.
The one seized Capocchio by the knot
Of the neck, and biting dragged him so,
That he scraped the ground with his belly.
And the Aretine, who remained trembling,
Said: “Gianni Schicchi is the madman
„ That one there; and he runs in frenzy mangling others.”
And I to him: “May the other spirit
„ Not fix its teeth into you! yet tell me,
„ If it pleases you, who it is, before it departs.”
And he to me: “This is foul
„ Myrrha's ancient shade, who became
„ Impiously the paramour of her own father.
„ This abomination she wrought with him, the form
„ Of another woman having deceitfully assumed,
„ Even as he, who runs down yonder,
„ In order to gain the fair mare,
„ Having feigned himself as Buoso Donati,
„ Made a will, giving validity to the testament.”
Then when the two frenzied ones had passed by,
Upon whom I had my eyes fixed,
I turned them, so that I might see other wretches.
Then I saw one, who would have had the shape of a lute,
If he had had his groin cut off on the side,
Where the body of man is cleft in two.
The tympanic dropsy, which alters,
Perverting the humours, the limbs in such wise,
That the face does not correspond to the belly,
Forced him to hold his lips parted wide,
As the consumptive suffering from thirst turns
One of his lips upward, the other downward.
—“You, who come without punishment,
„ And I know not how, into this piteous world,
„ Look,” said he to us, “cast a glance
„ Of pity toward the unfortunate Master Adam.
„ While living I had enough of all that I desired;
„ And now, alas! I fiercely crave a drop of water.
„ The rivulets, which from the green hills
„ Of Casentino descend into the Arno,
„ Making cool and soft watercourses,
„ I have ever before my eyes, and suffer.
„ For the longed-for image dries me up more
„ Than the malady, which wastes away my face.
„ And stern Justice chastises me,
„ Drawing means from the place where I sinned,
„ To increase my groans yet the more.
„ For I debased, there where Romena lies,
„ The coin that bears the stamp of the Baptist;
„ Wherefore I left my body burned above.
„ But if I saw here Guido's,
„ And Alessandro's soul, or their brother's,
„ I would prefer that to the fountain of Branda.
„ One of them is within, if the raging
„ Shades that go round about have spoken the truth.
„ Yet what is the profit to one whose limbs are bound?
„ But if I were so light as to walk
„ One finger-breadth in a hundred years,
„ I would set out on the road, and for him round about
„ In this piteous valley search,
„ Although it has in length stadia
„ A hundred, and in breadth five stadia.
„ On their account I am now in such a place,
„ Who persuaded me to counterfeit the staters
„ Having an alloy of three carats.”—
And I to him: “Who are the two,
„ Who are smoking like a wet hand in winter,
„ And lying pressed close together on your right?”
—“I found them thus; and they lie motionless,”
He said, “since I was cast into this pit.
„ And I think they will remain so forever.
„ This is the false accuser of Joseph;
„ And the other is the liar Sinon, the Trojan Greek;
„ From intense heat they send forth a foul-smelling steam.”—
And one of them provoked, having heard perhaps
Himself named so reproachfully,
Struck with his fist his swollen belly;
And it resounded just like a drum; then
Master Adam smote his face
With his arm with no less violence,
Saying to him: “Although wholly deprived
„ Of the movement of my heavy limbs,
„ Yet I have my hand free for this.”
And he retorted: “Yet when you were going
„ To the pyre, you did not have it so ready,
„ As above all when you were coining money.”
And the dropsical one: “Truly you have spoken this;
„ But you were not a true witness in Troy,
„ When you were called upon to speak the truth.”—
“If I spoke falsehood, you too falsified coin,”
Said Sinon; “yet I am here for one offence,
„ While you for more than any other demon.”—
—“And you, perjurer, remember your horse,”
Retorted he who had the swollen belly;
“Receive then a punishment known to all the world.”—
—“May thirst be your punishment; may it burst,”
Said the Greek, “your tongue, and the putrid
„ Water, which with your belly hedges up your face.”—
And the counterfeiter: “So would it gape,
„ Your wide mouth speaking evil, as is your wont;
„ For if I thirst and humour swells me up,
„ You indeed burn with fever and have a fierce headache,
„ So that, to lick the mirror of Narcissus,
„ You will have no need of many urgings.”—
I was wholly intent listening to them,
When my Master said to me: “Now look to yourself;
„ A little more and I should be angry with you.”
And seeing that he addressed me angrily,
With such shame toward him I turned,
That it is still kept in my memory.
And like him who sees an evil dream in his sleep,
And being in a dream hopes he is dreaming,
Longing for what he sees as though it were not,
Such was I, having no power to speak.
For I longed to ask pardon, and altogether
Was asking it, yet thought I was not doing so.
“Less shame washes away a greater fault
„ Than yours was,” said my Master;
“Wherefore let all your distress now cease.
„ And deem that I ever stand beside you,
„ If ever by chance you find yourself among men
„ Wrangling with one another in such strife;
„ For it is ignoble to wish to hear such things.”
