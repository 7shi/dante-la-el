There shook from my head the deep sleep
A dreadful thunder bursting forth, and I rose up
Suddenly, like one who was awakened by force.
Moving my rested eyes all around,
Wholly upright, I gazed intently
To discern the place where I was standing.
And indeed I found myself upon the brink
Of the sorrowful valley of the abyss,
Where resounds a din of endless wailings.
Dark it was, deep, and misty
So much so that, fixing my gaze into the depths,
I was not able to distinguish anything.
“Come, let us descend into the world of darkness,”
The poet says to me, wholly pale:
“I shall be first, and you shall be second.”
And when I saw him turning so pale,
I said: “How shall I come, when you are terrified,
„Who bring me aid in my fears?”
And he to me: “The anguish of those who suffer here
„Paints upon my countenance
„That very pity which you suppose to be fear.
„Let us go; for the long journey urges us on.”
So going forward he led me into the first
Circle that encompasses the abyss.
There, so far as one could gather from hearing,
Were no lamentations, save sighs that made
The eternal air tremble;
And these came from sorrow without torments,
Which great multitudes felt there,
Of countless men, women, and children.
And my Master to me: “Do you not ask me
„What spirits these are whom you see here?
„Now I wish you to know, before you go farther,
„These did not sin; yet their goodness
„ Did not suffice them, since they did not obtain
„ Baptism, the portal of your worship.
„ For having been born before the religion of Christ,
„ They did not worship God as was fitting;
„ And I myself am one here among these.
„ Through such defects, not through any other
„ Fault, are we lost; and our only punishment
„ Is to live in longing without hope.“
Grievous sorrow I felt on hearing this;
For many men of surpassing worth
I knew to be in suspense within this circle.
“My Master and Lord, tell me, tell me,”
I said, wishing to be assured concerning
That faith which overcomes every error,
“Did anyone ever go forth from here through his own
„ Merit or another's, and become blessed?”
And he, perceiving the covert meaning of my words,
Answered me: “I was a newcomer here,
„ When I saw a mighty One descending,
„ Who, crowned with the sign of victory,
„Led forth the shade of the first-formed man,
„And of his son Abel, and Noah, and Moses,
„Obedient lawgiver, and the patriarch
„Abraham, and David the king,
„And Israel with his father and with his children,
„And with Rachel, for whom he laboured so much,
„And many others, making them blessed.
„And I wish you to know that before these
„No human spirit was ever saved.”
While he was speaking thus, we walked onward,
Passing continually through the wood—
The wood, I mean, of densely crowded spirits.
Not far as yet had our journey gone
Thence to the summit, when I saw a fire
Piercing through a hemisphere of darkness.
We were still at a little distance from there,
Yet not so far as not to discern
That this was a place of eminent men.
“O you who honour all wisdom and art,
„Who are these who possess such great honour,
„That they sit together apart from the rest?”
And he to me: “Their great renown,
„Which shines forth even down to your days,
„Has given them from God this grace.”
Then a voice was heard in my ears:
“Come, honour the pre-eminent poet,
„Whose shade, that was absent, now returns.”
When the voice grew silent, I saw four
Great shades coming towards us,
Having an aspect neither of sorrow nor of joy.
My Master began to speak to me thus:
“Behold him who, bearing a sword in hand,
„Comes before the three like a king.
„This is Homer, the sovereign poet;
„The next is Horace, the satirist;
„The third is Ovid, and this one is Lucan.
„And since each of these shares with me
„In the title which one voice just sounded,
„They do me honour, and therein do well.”
Thus I saw assembled the great school
Of the poetry of the foremost master,
Who like an eagle soars above all others.
After conversing a little with one another,
They turned to me with a sign of greeting;
And my Master smiled upon them.
Yet still greater honour they bestowed on me,
Taking me into their own company,
So that I was sixth among such great men.
Thus we walked onward as far as the light,
Discoursing of things that must be passed in silence,
Even as it was fitting there.
We arrived at the foot of a noble castle,
Seven times encircled by lofty walls,
And surrounded by a pellucid stream;
This we crossed over as though it were dry land.
Through seven gates I entered with the sages;
And we came to a meadow of fresh greenery.
Souls stately and grave in their looks
Were present there, majestic in appearance,
Speaking seldom, with a gentle voice.
We withdrew, standing apart by ourselves,
To a lofty place, open and full of light,
Whence we could behold all persons and all things.
There upon the flower-strewn turf
The most eminent spirits appeared to me,
At sight of whom I deem myself honoured.
I saw Electra with many companions,
Among whom I knew Hector and Æneas,
And Cæsar in arms, with keen gaze.
I saw Penthesilea and Camilla
On the other side; I saw King Latinus,
Sitting beside his daughter Lavinia.
I saw Brutus, who drove out Tarquin,
Lucretia, Martia, and Cornelia,
And Saladin standing apart by himself.
Then raising my eyes a little higher,
I saw the master of knowledge
Seated amid the family of the wise.
All admire him, all do him honour.
There indeed I saw Socrates and Plato
Standing foremost before the others there,
Democritus, who ascribed the world to Chance,
Thales, Anaxagoras, and Diogenes,
Empedocles, Zeno with Heraclitus.
I saw the profound investigator of quality,
Dioscorides, I mean, after whom Orpheus,
Tully, Linus, and moral Seneca,
Euclid the geometer, and Ptolemæus,
Hippocrates, Galen, and with Avicenna
Averrhoës the commentator.
Of all of them I cannot make mention;
For the length of the theme presses me on,
And words often fall short of the reality.
The company of six poets turns into two;
And my wise guide leads me by another road
Out of the tranquil into the trembling air;
And I come where there is no light to be seen.
