“The banners of the King of Hell go forth ahead
„ Toward us; look now before you,”
Said my Master, “if you can discern.”
And as when a thick mist covers the earth,
Or night descends upon our sky,
There appears afar, turned by the wind,
A windmill, so then I saw something moving,
And because of the wind I stood behind
My Master, having no other shelter.
I came indeed (and with fear I write in verse),
Where all the shades were covered over
And transparent, like embryos in glass.
Some remain lying on their backs, and others upright,
Some upon their feet, others upon their head,
And one bending its face toward its feet, like a bow.
And when we had advanced far enough forward,
Where it pleased my Master to show me
The creature that was once exceedingly fair,
He stepped aside from before me, and making me
Stop, he said: “Behold Dis, behold the place,
„ Where you must arm yourself with courage.”
How chilled and terrified I then became,
Reader, do not ask me to write for you now;
For all my speech would be inadequate.
I was neither dead nor alive; consider now
For yourself, if you have even a little wit,
What state I was in without death and life.
The Emperor of the realm of Lamentations
Emerged from mid-breast out of the ice.
And I compared better with a giant
Than a giant with one of his arms.
Judge now from that part, how great
His whole body by comparison had to be.
And if he was as fair as he is hideous now,
And raised his horn against his Maker,
Truly from him must spring every woe.
Oh, how great a marvel I felt then,
Seeing three faces upon his head,
The one in front being crimson-coloured,
And the other two joined onto this one
Above the middle of each shoulder,
And joined together around the crown.
And the right one was of a yellowish-white colour;
And the left in appearance was like
Those who come from whence the Nile flows,
Each having two great wings,
Such as befitted such a winged creature;
Never did I see ships' sails so huge.
Feathers they had not; but they were after the fashion
Of a bat's; and as these were flapped,
From them three winds rushed forth together,
By which all Cocytus was frozen solid.
With six eyes he wept, and down his chins—
All three—trickled the tears with slaver
Stained with blood. With its teeth each mouth
Crushed one sinner like a mortar,
And at once mangled three wretches.
As for the one in front, the bitings were nothing
Beside the claws; for at times his back
Remained stripped bare of its skin.
“The soul suffering more above,” said
My Master, “is Judas Iscariot,
„ Having his head inside, and his legs outside.
„ And of the other two, who have their head downward,
„ The swarthy one is Brutus;
„ See how he writhes, uttering not a word;
„ And the other is Cassius, seeming so stout.
„ But night is returning; and now for us it is time
„ To depart; for we have seen everything.”
Upon his neck, as he bade me, I clung;
And he, choosing a fitting time and manner,
As soon as the wings were opened wide enough,
Clung fast to the shaggy flanks;
Then from tuft to tuft descended downward
Between the thick hair and the slabs of ice.
And when we came down to where at the top
Of the haunches the thigh turns as it were,
My Guide, with toil and struggle,
Placing his head where he had had his feet,
Grasped the hair, like one leaping upon a horse,
So that I thought we were turning back into Hell.
“Hold fast now,” said the Master, panting,
Like a man exhausted, “by ladders
„ Such as these we must escape from these horrors.”
Then coming out through an opening of a rock,
And seating me gently upon its rim,
He set beside me his watchful foot.
I lifted up my eyes, and thought I saw
Lucifer just as I had left him at first;
And I saw him with his feet upward.
And how utterly bewildered I then became,
Let the common folk reckon, who do not understand
Through what point I had just passed.
“Rise up upon your feet,” said the Master,
“The path is long, and rough is the journey;
„ And the sun is returning to half-tierce.”
It was no promenade of a palace,
Where we were, but a natural cleft
Of rough ground and deprived of light.
“Before I depart from this abyss,” I said,
When I had stood upright, “tell me just a little,
„ To lead me out of error, O Master:
„ Where is the ice? and how has he been set
„ Thus upside down? and how from evening has passed
„ The sun in a few hours to morning?”
And he to me: “You perhaps still imagine
„ You are beyond the centre, where I grasped
„ The hairs of the worm that bores through the earth.
„ There indeed you were, while I descended; yet
„ When I turned round, you crossed the point
„ Toward which weights incline from every side.
„ And now you are in the lower hemisphere,
„ Which is opposite to that which covers
„ All the dry land, where the cross was endured by
„ Him who was born and lived without sin.
„ You have your feet upon a tiny sphere
„ That lies opposite to Judecca.
„ Here it is morning, while there it is evening.
„ And he whose hair was a ladder for us
„ Is still sunk fast, just as he was at first.
„ Upon this side he fell down from heaven;
„ And the land that formerly rose here, then
„ In fear of him covered itself with sea,
„ And came into our hemisphere.
„ And perhaps fleeing him it left a space
„ Empty here, and thence rose up on high.” —
Below there is a place, distant from Beelzebub
As far as the abyss extends in length;
So that it is not visible to eyes, but by the sound
Of a streamlet, which descends from there
Through a fissure of a crag, which flowing round
It has hollowed out, being gently inclined.
My Guide and I into this hidden path
Entered, returning to the bright world.
Taking no thought of rest we climbed upward,
He first, and I behind him,
Until I beheld the beauties of heaven
Through a round opening, and from there
We came forth, to see the stars again.
